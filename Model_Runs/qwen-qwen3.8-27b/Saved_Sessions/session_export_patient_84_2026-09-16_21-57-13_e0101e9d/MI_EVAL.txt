# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming Malik for his cannabis use. Acceptance is somewhat procedural, with fewer explicit affirmations of Malik’s strengths or emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, especially in capturing Malik’s ambivalence between his mother’s concerns and his need to connect with friends. The main limitation is repetitiveness, with less depth in exploring feelings such as shame, control, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays generally relevant but becomes focused on “fairness” and the mother’s reaction rather than a clear behavioral target. More deliberate guidance toward a specific change focus or next step would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative questions and inclusive language to invite Malik to shape the conversation. Collaboration would be stronger if Malik’s contributions were more clearly integrated into a shared agenda or plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids being directive and respects Malik’s choice to some degree. However, autonomy support is mostly implicit rather than explicitly reinforced through statements like “This is your decision.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes Malik’s values around trust, control, and not wanting to hide. However, the evocation is not consistently directed toward motivation to change cannabis use itself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-authoritarian stance and invites Malik to co-construct the conversation. Partnership is not fully developed into an explicitly equal decision-making process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Malik provides meaningful change talk about control, grades, future, and honesty, and the psychiatrist reflects some of it. The therapist could more consistently deepen and expand change talk with evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Malik’s minimizing statements but also does not skillfully soften or redirect them toward change. More effective responses would use double-sided reflections or discrepancy to address his ambivalence.
