# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth and nonjudgment, normalizing nervousness and validating feelings without criticism. However, affirmations are frequent and sometimes feel slightly formulaic rather than emerging organically from the moment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate reflections that capture nuance, such as untangling fear from lack of planning. Occasional moments where reflections land slightly before the full emotional weight of Marcus's vulnerability is fully acknowledged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical progression from overwhelm to a concrete, manageable plan with each question building on the previous one. The final commitment step could be more explicitly tied to a measurable target behavior (e.g., starting checks after dinner this week).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a joint exploration rather than an expert consultation, with all plan elements (sequencing, kitchen counter, wife conversation) emerging from Marcus's own ideas. The psychiatrist explicitly acknowledges Marcus's contributions, maintaining a genuinely equal power dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary reinforcement of Marcus's ownership and choice throughout, from the opening statement to the closing question about making the plan feel natural. The psychiatrist never prescribes, directs, or pressures, consistently ceding decisions back to Marcus.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful evocation—every question is designed to draw out Marcus's own thinking, and he generates all key insights (three-step breakdown, one-step-at-a-time approach, written plan, kitchen counter, telling his wife). The final reflection explicitly reinforces that the motivation is internally generated rather than externally imposed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as a partnership where Marcus is the expert on his own life, using language like 'what do you think?' and 'your own version.' The psychiatrist serves as a facilitator rather than an authority, with Marcus's ideas shaping the session at every turn.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes desire, ability, and commitment through strategic questions, with Marcus producing numerous instances of change talk. Could have more explicitly evoked confidence scaling or pushed commitment one step further at the close.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating and redirecting rather than pushing against, such as reframing the wife concern into a collaboration question. A couple of moments could have more actively separated sustain talk from the change direction to pivot more decisively.
