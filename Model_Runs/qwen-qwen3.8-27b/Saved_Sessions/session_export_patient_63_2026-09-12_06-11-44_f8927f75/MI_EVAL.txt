# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming. The tone is supportive but somewhat procedural, with less explicit affirmation of worth and a missed final emotional moment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is strong reflective listening and accurate naming of triggers and feelings. The responses could go deeper into underlying shame, fear of weakness, belonging, guilt, and legal fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior, legal risk, and practical planning. Direction becomes somewhat rigid around scripts and does not fully refocus when ambivalence returns at the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and includes Jordan in choosing strategies. The interaction often shifts into expert problem-solving and scripted advice, reducing equal-partner collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan is given choices and opportunities to select options. Autonomy is weakened by frequent specific scripts and directive behavioral instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses scaling and questions to elicit motivation. Evocation is limited because the clinician often supplies strategies instead of deeply exploring Jordan's own reasons, values, and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early collaboration is present through questions and choice-making. The session becomes increasingly directive as the psychiatrist provides detailed scripts and contingency plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Readiness and confidence scaling help generate concrete change-oriented plans. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify and deepen Jordan's own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged without argument. There is limited use of double-sided reflections, and the final sustain talk is not effectively softened or redirected.
