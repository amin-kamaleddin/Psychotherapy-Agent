# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing avoidance and validating the client's feelings. However, the tone is sometimes clinical and the early directive push slightly reduces unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the client's frustration and ambivalence accurately, but the reflections remain surface-level and repetitive. There is limited exploration of the deeper emotional meaning behind the avoidance and overwhelm.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior of charging and using the medical monitor. However, the direction is narrow and operational, with little connection to broader health values or personal goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and offers choices, but often guides the client toward immediate practical action. The client's expertise is not consistently central to the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy support improves later in the session with explicit statements respecting the client's choice. Early in the session, the psychiatrist is more directive and prescriptive, making autonomy support uneven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited opportunities for open questioning, and much of the session focuses on practical options rather than eliciting the client's own motivations. The psychiatrist misses repeated chances to explore values, concerns, readiness, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some collaborative intent by asking preferences and offering options. However, the session is often driven by the counselor's problem-solving agenda rather than shared exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist misses several client statements that lean toward change and does not reflect, amplify, or build on them. Instead of exploring importance, confidence, or values, the focus returns to practical steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without overt reinforcement, but the psychiatrist frequently redirects to action options. The introduction of risk information and limited use of double-sided reflections can feel pressuring.
