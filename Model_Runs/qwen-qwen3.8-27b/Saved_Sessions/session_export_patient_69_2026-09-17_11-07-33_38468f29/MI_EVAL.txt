# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, consistently removing self-critical labels and validating the client's experience as understandable. Never uses judgmental language; warmth is specific and individualized rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Provides highly accurate complex reflections that add emotional depth and nuance beyond simple restatement. Each reflection is tailored to the client's specific language and experience, capturing subtle emotional weight without overstepping.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical progression focused on the core issue of disengagement and lack of ownership. Could be slightly more strategic in connecting the client's emerging insights to a concrete next step or collaborative plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates a genuine collaborative dynamic with shared power; the session reads as joint exploration rather than clinical interrogation. The client's responses clearly shape the direction of conversation, and the psychiatrist adapts in real-time.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support; never imposes decisions, timelines, or directive language. Consistently removes external pressure and affirms the client's right to his own experience and choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** All evocation is client-centered with no external reasons for change offered. Could press slightly further to ground the client's abstract motivation ('if I had a say, maybe I'd care more') in concrete, actionable experience.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the authority on his own experience, explicitly crediting his insights and inviting his own perspective rather than offering interpretations. Never offers unsolicited advice or positions himself as the expert with answers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Makes effective moves to elicit change talk through well-crafted open questions and explicit affirmation of the client's own insights. Could be more sustained — the emerging change talk could be explored more deeply to strengthen the client's sense of agency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully acknowledges valid parts of sustain talk without arguing or pushing back, explicitly resisting the urge to correct the client. Could incorporate more double-sided reflections to more explicitly honor both sides of the client's ambivalence.
