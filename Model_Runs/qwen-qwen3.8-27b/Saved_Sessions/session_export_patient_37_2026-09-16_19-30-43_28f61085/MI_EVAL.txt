# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the patient's doubts. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, with fewer explicit affirmations of the patient as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows good reflective listening and accurately captures the patient's ambivalence and need for trustworthy information. A stronger empathic response would name the underlying fear, pressure, and emotional load more directly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session keeps clear focus on the vaccination decision and gradually narrows broad hesitancy into specific concerns and next steps. It would be stronger with more explicit early statements of the target decision and brief consolidating summaries.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by building on the patient's ideas, such as the take-home chart and discussion with her partner. Collaboration could be stronger by more explicitly co-creating the agenda and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a major strength: the psychiatrist consistently supports the patient's pace, judgment, and right to decide without pressure. The counselor avoids pushing immediate vaccination and validates the need to review information at home.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions to elicit the patient's values, concerns, and decision-making preferences. To reach a higher score, the counselor could more directly evoke internal motivation and deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that the patient has important expertise in her child's care and invites her perspective to guide the conversation. The session remains fairly structured, so more explicit shared agenda-setting would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient produces some change talk, especially toward the end, and the psychiatrist notices it. However, the counselor does not consistently elicit or expand Desire, Ability, Reason, Need, and Commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by using double-sided reflections and not arguing with the patient's hesitancy. It would be stronger if the counselor more actively reframed sustain talk into change language while preserving autonomy.
