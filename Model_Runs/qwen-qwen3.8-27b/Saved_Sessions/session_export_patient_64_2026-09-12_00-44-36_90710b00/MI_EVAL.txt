# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, with frequent affirmations of Jordan’s perspective. Acceptance could be warmer and more individualized, while consistently affirming Jordan beyond the vaccination decision.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Jordan’s ambivalence about personal risk and protecting his grandparents. However, the session shifts quickly into facts and planning, limiting deeper exploration of Jordan’s emotional concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the flu vaccine and protecting Jordan’s grandparents, with a clear progression from concerns to planning. The main weakness is drifting toward precautions and scripts rather than deepening vaccine-related change talk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language and incorporates Jordan’s preference for precautions. However, the interaction remains expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying most of the information, options, and plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Jordan’s right to choose and avoids coercion or pressure. This is slightly weakened by providing a detailed plan before fully exploring what Jordan wants to decide or how he wants to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak because the session relies heavily on factual information rather than drawing out Jordan’s own motivations, values, and confidence. Key change talk about not wanting to harm his grandparents is not fully explored or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some partnership language and acknowledges Jordan’s control over the choice. However, the frequent provision of facts, options, and scripts creates a more expert-driven dynamic than a fully collaborative one.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to evoke or strengthen change talk, and Jordan’s important statements about protecting his grandparents are not strategically reflected or deepened. The session lacks rulers, scaling, or consistent effort to draw out Jordan’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Jordan’s sustain talk and keeps the interaction safe. However, it does not consistently use double-sided reflections or skillfully shift Jordan’s reasons for not vaccinating toward protection-based change talk.
