# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrated a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance, normalizing Amina's ambivalence and reframing potential failure as useful data. The overall warmth was strong, though occasionally formulaic and more solution-focused than deeply relational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening effectively captured Amina's ambivalence and practical difficulties, such as the tension between sleep and staying connected. A missed opportunity to explore the emotional weight behind her statement about not knowing how to sit without the screen kept empathy from reaching a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remained tightly focused on the target behavior of nighttime social media use and its impact on sleep. It successfully guided Amina toward a concrete, measurable 3-night experiment with clear tracking outcomes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amina's contributions actively shaped the final plan, including her choices for the tea substitute, shortened timer, and personalized voice note. The psychiatrist occasionally drove the structure by introducing multiple predefined options, which limited the interaction from being fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported Amina's sense of choice by offering options, avoiding commands, and validating her decision to try without promising perfection. Minor prescriptive phrasing in the recommendations slightly reduced the level of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** While the use of readiness and confidence rulers drew out some of Amina's motivations and preferences, the session often moved quickly into advice-giving and plan-building. More evocative questions exploring her values and internal reasons for change would have strengthened this criterion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was generally collaborative, with the psychiatrist frequently inviting Amina's input and incorporating her preferences into the plan. The frequent use of structured lists and direct recommendations made the session somewhat guided rather than fully equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made reasonable efforts to elicit change talk, and Amina produced some positive change-related language. However, a quicker transition to concrete options limited the depth and amplification of her self-motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well through reflection and normalization rather than argument or persuasion. The score was not a 5 because the psychiatrist did not consistently use advanced MI maneuvers like double-sided reflections to pivot Amina's ambivalence toward change.
