# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with multiple validating statements throughout. Not a 5 because the warmth is brief and formulaic, quickly shifting to psychoeducation or directives.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Acknowledgments of worry and overwhelm are supportive but surface-level, lacking complex reflection of Linda's ambivalence. Missed opportunities to reflect statements like "fix one thing and break another" or "waste their time."

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on asthma management, inhaler use, tracking, and safety thresholds without wandering. Rated below 5 because the direction is delivered in a fairly directive, preconstructed way rather than collaboratively clarified with Linda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Conversation largely structured by the psychiatrist's clinical plan with limited co-construction of tools or next steps. Linda is invited to accept or reject options but does not clearly shape the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Occasional choices offered (list format, reminder text) but the overall stance remains prescriptive with many direct recommendations. Linda is not consistently positioned as the decision-maker about whether or how to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Weakest area; the psychiatrist primarily provides external reasons, risk/benefit information, and action steps rather than drawing out Linda's own motivations or values. No substantive effort to expand her statements of desire, ability, need, or plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Frequent informational and directive statements keep the psychiatrist firmly in the expert role. Some collaborative offers appear, but the session direction is not clearly shaped by Linda's preferences or ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only rare and weak efforts to elicit change talk, with no DARN-style questions about desires, needs, or confidence. The session largely supplies a plan rather than helping Linda articulate her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Linda's concerns about side effects, dependence, and task overload are met primarily with factual reassurance and additional problem-solving. Little use of double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to soften barriers and reframe them as motivation.
