# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and accepted Sofia’s ambivalence without pushback. Positive regard could be stronger with more individualized affirmation of her strengths and insights.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Sofia’s experience, ambivalence, cost concerns, and social pressure. Empathy could deepen by exploring underlying feelings such as embarrassment, fear of judgment, or belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on marijuana use, stress, anxiety, cost, and social pressure. It could be more strategic by consolidating a clearer target behavior or change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a collaborative stance and built on Sofia’s ideas, such as music, wallet checking, and a social “out.” Collaboration would improve if Sofia more consistently led the generation of options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used low-pressure language and respected Sofia’s choice to make no promises. Autonomy support could be more explicit by affirming that all decisions about use remain entirely her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions drew out practical ideas and some motivation, especially around cost and coping. Evocation was limited by not deeper exploration of values, discrepancies, confidence, or readiness for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist framed the work as a shared effort and invited Sofia to help set priorities. Partnership would be stronger with more explicit co-negotiation of goals and less therapist-led option framing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia expressed willingness to try small alternatives, and the psychiatrist asked questions that invited change-oriented ideas. However, the psychiatrist did not consistently reinforce, expand, or amplify her change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without argument, helping preserve the alliance. The psychiatrist could more strategically soften it by using double-sided reflections that connect staying the same with her stated concerns.
