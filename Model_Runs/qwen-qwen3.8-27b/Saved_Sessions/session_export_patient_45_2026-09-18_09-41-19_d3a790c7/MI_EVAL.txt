# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, explicitly validating Erik's boundaries and right to his own space. Could be deeper by affirming his twenty-year coping strategy or normalizing his ambivalence more strongly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates excellent reflective listening with accurate, progressively deeper reflections that capture multiple emotional threads simultaneously. The 'it's not the beer, it's the end of the day' reframe is a particularly powerful moment of empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent focus on target behaviors throughout, with every question and reflection circling back to the core issue. The session progresses logically from establishing the problem to exploring function to evoking a specific commitment, with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates fully collaborative behavior with inclusive language and a genuinely balanced power dynamic. Erik's ideas (the dog, the shower, the one-beer plan) directly shape the session's direction rather than following a pre-planned agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Erik's autonomy and ownership, from explicit statements like 'I'm not here to tell you what to do' to attributing the final plan as 'you taking the wheel.' Never imposes, pressures, or minimizes Erik's right to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within Erik rather than providing external reasons for change. All change talk originates from Erik himself, whether through questions about what a good answer looks like or what making a real difference means to him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance using inclusive language and explicitly inviting Erik's input. Occasional directive moments, such as 'I'd suggest we keep it simple' and the somewhat leading commitment question, slightly tilt toward the counselor's agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits change talk particularly in the latter half of the session, drawing out concrete goals and commitment language. Could be more consistent in the early portion and could more explicitly amplify and reinforce Erik's change talk as it emerges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk effectively by acknowledging tension without arguing or dismissing, and validates past coping before gently exploring function. Could use more explicit double-sided reflections to further soften moments of resistance, such as when Erik says 'I'm not sure what the big deal is.'
