# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the client’s experience without becoming defensive. Acceptance is sometimes diluted by quickly moving into practical problem-solving and lists of options.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several reflections accurately capture practical concerns, but empathy often stays on the surface and misses deeper emotional material. The use of question clusters and vague reflections limits more precise attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on reducing relapse risk and building a stable, local, hands-on employment plan. The task focus could be strengthened by more attention to values, identity change, and internal motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s preferences meaningfully shape the plan, but the psychiatrist often supplies multiple options and practical solutions before the client fully explores them. The role still tilts toward a more knowledgeable helper rather than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently asks for choice, respects the client’s decisions, and avoids pressure or moralizing. It would be stronger with more explicit reinforcement of the client’s ownership and self-determination.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some good open questions and scaling attempts, but the psychiatrist frequently shifts to external advice and practical planning. More consistent eliciting of the client’s own reasons, values, and change motivations is needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and offers choices, but often assumes a resource-expert role by generating lists, agencies, and steps. The client’s expertise is used, but not consistently positioned as the primary source of direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some relevant change talk and affirms progress, but does not consistently deepen or follow up on it. The quick shift to external options limits amplification of the client’s own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or moralizing and often acknowledges the client’s sustain talk. More active MI strategies, such as double-sided reflections and gentle redirection toward change, are needed.
