# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful language and accepts the client’s refusal of naloxone. However, repeated harm-education can feel admonishing rather than warmly accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges some practical concerns but rarely reflects the emotional meaning behind the client’s desire for independence. Empathic response is mostly factual agreement rather than deeper perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior, infection, and safety risks. Direction is clear but becomes overly logistical, centered on checklist creation rather than goal clarification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for goals and preferences but remains largely expert-driven. The client’s stated priorities, such as saline use and avoiding another infection, do not fully shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms the client’s right to decline naloxone and offers choices at times. However, repeated suggestions of precautions and checklists can feel directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few initial questions but then shifts to psychoeducation and advice. The client’s expressed values are not followed up with evocative questions to develop his own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some collaborative questions and inclusive language, but the psychiatrist leads the session with repeated recommendations. The client’s contributions do not fully determine the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes limited efforts to expand the client’s change talk, such as avoiding another infection and reducing pain. Change-related statements are not reflected, amplified, or developed further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often responds to the client’s sustain talk by adding more recommendations, which can prolong resistance. A stronger MI response would have reflected ambivalence and helped reconcile independence with safety.
