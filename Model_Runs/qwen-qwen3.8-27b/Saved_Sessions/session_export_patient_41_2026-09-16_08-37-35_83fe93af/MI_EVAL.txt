# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client's anxiety and avoidance. Slightly reduced by occasional inferential labels and positive assumptions that feel prescriptive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures the client's cycle, ambivalence, and burden of check-ins. The emotional nuance behind self-critical statements such as fear of failure is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing avoidance and increasing follow-through for a specific anxiety task. Each turn moves toward a concrete, actionable experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed by incorporating the client's ideas about the reward, placement, backup, and no check-in. Collaboration is limited by the psychiatrist's role in introducing much of the structure and problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy through choices, low pressure, and permission to stop. Repeated confidence ratings and requests for commitment can subtly pressure the client to agree.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and menu-style questions evoke useful client ideas and confidence increases. Deeper intrinsic motivation and values are not sufficiently explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's choices visibly shape the plan, and questions invite collaboration. The interaction remains somewhat expert-driven because the psychiatrist presents a fairly complete intervention structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and planning questions elicit concrete client-generated ideas and raise confidence from 4 to 6. The client's change talk is present but not consistently amplified, summarized, or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through normalization, permission, and double-sided reflections without argument. Some self-critical sustain talk, especially fear of failure and disappointment, is left underprocessed.
