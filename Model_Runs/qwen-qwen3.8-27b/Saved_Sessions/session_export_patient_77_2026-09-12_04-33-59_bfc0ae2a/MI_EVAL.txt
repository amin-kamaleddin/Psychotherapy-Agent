# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client’s distress, including slips. Minor evaluative or clinically directive language prevents a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is accurate reflective listening to the client’s exhaustion, loneliness, and ambivalence. Deeper emotional themes around self-worth and body image are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session moves clearly from presenting distress to a concrete, manageable behavior plan. The early assessment is somewhat broad and expands into multiple possible directions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client actively chooses parts of the plan, and the psychiatrist summarizes and honors her preferences. The structure remains somewhat therapist-led, especially with numbered options and rapid questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently asks for preferences and affirms the client’s choices, reducing pressure. However, much of the plan is still shaped through therapist-generated options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some eliciting questions help the client identify a target and preference. There is not enough sustained evocation of the client’s own values, reasons, and confidence before moving into planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choice and uses the client’s language, supporting a collaborative stance. The session would benefit from more explicit space for the client to generate ideas before options are offered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change talk, such as wanting to be less miserable and trying the experiment. The psychiatrist could have more consistently deepened and amplified this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Self-doubt and fear of failure are normalized rather than argued with, and slips are addressed kindly. More active MI reframing could further separate identity from behavior.
