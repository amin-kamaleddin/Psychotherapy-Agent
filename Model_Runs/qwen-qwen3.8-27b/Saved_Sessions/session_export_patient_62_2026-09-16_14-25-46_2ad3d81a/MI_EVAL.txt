# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance without pressure or pathologizing. Jordan’s struggle is validated unconditionally, even when he is discouraged or avoids choosing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects both the content and emotional meaning of Jordan’s statements, including ambivalence, exhaustion, and fear of failure. The reflections are strong, though they could occasionally name deeper underlying emotions more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session moves from broad financial and work stress toward a more focused target around work schedule, first shift, sleep, and worry. The direction is generally appropriate, but the final target behavior is not fully consolidated or crisply confirmed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the work as shared, following Jordan’s concerns and building on his own ideas. The client actively shapes the session, such as by proposing the schedule, phone alarm, and tomorrow list.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Jordan’s choice, ownership, and self-direction without imposing advice or a plan. Statements such as “you’re allowed to stop trying when you’re tired” clearly affirm autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often draws out Jordan’s own ideas, meanings, and possible solutions rather than prescribing interventions. However, some prompts are mildly leading, and deeper values, confidence, or identity language is not fully elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly positions Jordan as the expert on his own life and repeatedly invites his perspective. The interaction feels collaborative rather than expert-driven or prescriptive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist notices and invites Jordan’s change-oriented language, including wanting to feel less stuck and identifying small workable steps. The score is limited by not fully consolidating or amplifying change talk with more explicit MI techniques.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects and acknowledges Jordan’s sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it. However, the session remains heavily focused on helplessness and barriers, with limited use of deliberate techniques to redirect or balance the sustain talk.
