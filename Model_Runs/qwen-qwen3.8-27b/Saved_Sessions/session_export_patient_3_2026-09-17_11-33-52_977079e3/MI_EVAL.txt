# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, affirming tone with validation of the patient's experience. Occasionally feels slightly performative (e.g., 'I want to sit with that') rather than deeply individualized, and lacks explicit normalization of self-critical tendencies beyond reflection.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and nuanced reflections capture the emotional core of the patient's statements, including remarkable perceptual sensitivity to word choices like 'braver' rather than 'stronger.' The tension between wanting the old self and accepting gradual change is reflected with precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear progressive focus on resuming physical activity with logical flow from motivation to small steps to self-tracking. However, occasionally lingers in reflection territory without explicitly tying back to a concrete next action or collaborative closure with a specific commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates full collaboration as equal partners, consistently building on the patient's own ideas (app tracking, yes/no framework, 'braver' language) rather than introducing external frameworks. The dialogue is genuinely bidirectional with language positioning both as co-explorers.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the patient's ownership and agency, never imposing a plan or telling what to do. Language like 'on your own terms,' 'that's yours, not something I'm handing you,' and 'you set the bar yourself' explicitly communicates that all choices are entirely hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within the patient through questions that draw out her own values, past experiences, and self-evaluations. Never offers external reasons for change, resulting in abundant self-generated, deeply personal change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within the patient, never assuming the expert role or prescribing solutions. Questions are open and exploratory, and the session is shaped substantially by the patient's own contributions rather than redirected.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully and consistently evokes abundant change talk across desire, ability, commitment, and taking steps through strategically designed questions. Reinforces and amplifies the patient's own language for change rather than introducing external reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles the minimal sustain talk that appears well by affirming the patient's own resolution ('I set the bar myself') without challenging or dismissing the doubt. Limited opportunity to demonstrate more sophisticated techniques like double-sided reflections given the relative absence of prominent sustain talk.
