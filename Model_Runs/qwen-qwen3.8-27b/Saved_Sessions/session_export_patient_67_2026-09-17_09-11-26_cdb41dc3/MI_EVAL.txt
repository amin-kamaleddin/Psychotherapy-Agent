# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warmth, validation, and nonjudgment toward the client’s drinking and discomfort. There is no shaming, labeling, or corrective language, even when the client describes feeling powerless.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows strong perspective-taking and uses several complex reflections of ambivalence and context. However, a few reflections feel slightly interpretive, and the response to the client’s sense of powerlessness moves toward problem-solving too quickly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use and the client’s awareness of its role. Each topic, including work stress, tiredness, and evening routines, is connected back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on the client’s ideas and uses inclusive language rather than imposing solutions. Collaboration could be strengthened with more explicit co-construction, as the session remains somewhat therapist-paced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with repeated emphasis on the client’s choices, ownership, and freedom to decide. The psychiatrist avoids pressure, advice-giving, and coercive framing throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently uses open-ended questions to elicit the client’s own observations and problem-solving. A 5 would require deeper evocation of the client’s values, confidence, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as the expert and invites his perspective throughout the session. However, some agenda framing and interpretive statements make the collaboration less fully co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk, such as testing a day off and using a visual cue, and the psychiatrist affirms these ideas. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen change talk or elicit stronger reasons, desire, need, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk effectively by normalizing the appeal of the easy path and reframing it as an energy issue rather than failure. A 5 would require more strategic double-sided reflections that explicitly hold and pivot both sides of ambivalence.
