# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance by normalizing hesitation and reducing shame. A few mildly exhortative comments shift the tone toward task performance rather than unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers reasonable reflections of frustration, fear, and tiredness. Important emotional content such as social vulnerability and self-judgment is underexplored before moving to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the conversation clearly focused on the target behavior and a manageable next step. The emphasis becomes tactical and implementation-heavy, reducing collaborative clarification of motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructs a manageable micro-plan and adapts to client preferences. The clinician drives much of the structure rather than centering client-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respects client choice by offering options and explicitly reducing pressure. The repeated provision of specific behavioral instructions subtly limits the emphasis on client decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses evocation techniques such as the readiness ruler and small-step prompting. Many barriers and motivations are addressed superficially and quickly replaced by practical planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites client input and respects preferences in the plan. The stance remains quite clinician-driven, with many strategies supplied rather than client-discovered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evokes some change-related language through the readiness ruler and identification of a small step. The session focuses more on logistics than on eliciting client values, hopes, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoids arguing with reasons for staying home and reframes the task as low-risk. Some sustain talk is bypassed rather than reflected or used to explore ambivalence.
