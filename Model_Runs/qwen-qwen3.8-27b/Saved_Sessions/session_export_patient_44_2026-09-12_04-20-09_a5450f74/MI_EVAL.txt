# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms Erik’s efforts, validates his progress, and avoids blame or shaming. Warmth is strong but slightly structured, limiting fully unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Erik’s progress, frustration, and self-criticism. They are competent but do not always go deep into the emotional meaning behind his discouragement or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear target around gym follow-through through a small, manageable routine. It becomes narrow when focused on message wording and does not consistently reconnect to broader health and identity goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician co-constructs a plan with Erik and builds on his contributions, but frequently introduces ideas before first eliciting his own. Collaboration is functional but not fully client-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses open options, confidence ratings, and non-pressure language while respecting Erik’s preference for a small step. It could be stronger by reinforcing Erik’s full ownership to modify or drop the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions and confidence rulers help, but the session does not consistently draw out Erik’s values, identity, past successes, or internal reasons for change. It leans into practical planning before deeply evoking motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites Erik to choose the focus and allows his contributions to shape the plan, but often provides concrete suggestions and scripts. This makes the interaction collaborative but not fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik offers some change talk, and the clinician supports it with affirmations and confidence ratings. However, the session moves into planning without consistently eliciting or expanding his reasons, needs, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician normalizes Erik’s worries, avoids arguing with sustain talk, and redirects toward self-compassion and low-friction action. It occasionally remains problem-focused rather than consistently pivoting to agency or values.
