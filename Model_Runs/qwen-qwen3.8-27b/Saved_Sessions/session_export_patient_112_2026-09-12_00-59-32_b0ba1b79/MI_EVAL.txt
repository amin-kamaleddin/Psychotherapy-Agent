# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses warm, nonjudgmental language that normalizes worry and reduces shame. However, some praise feels premature and shifts toward approving compliance rather than unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of perspective-taking, but empathic responses are often brief and move quickly into problem-solving. Deeper identity and social embarrassment concerns are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and narrows to a specific target behavior. The main limitation is moving too quickly into practical planning before matching the client’s readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and asks for preferences, but remains largely counselor-led. The client’s own idea of trying water and staying private is not fully used as the basis for the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are clear statements reducing pressure and respecting choice. However, repeated suggestions for plans and scripts create a subtle pull toward action before readiness is fully established.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and explores what smoking means to the client. More of the session relies on giving information and suggestions rather than drawing out the client’s own values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language but often positions themself as the expert providing solutions. A stronger partnership would have invited the client to generate more of the options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk appears, including a tentative intention to skip one cigarette. The psychiatrist does not fully amplify or explore the meaning of this movement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not met with arguing, but much of the response is practical problem-solving. The counselor could better use double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence while opening space for change.
