# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, normalizes embarrassment, and avoids blame or shame. Warmth is sometimes brief and quickly shifts to advice before deeper acceptance of shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are adequate but often brief and move quickly into problem-solving. Deeper reflections of shame, family dynamics, and fear of failure are limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and narrows to a concrete morning delay target. Some drift into broad general cessation recommendations reduces precision.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Anisa’s preferences meaningfully shape the plan, including no quit date and a small delay target. The clinician still sometimes leads with expert recommendations before client-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers options, respects the client’s pace, and adapts the plan to a ten-minute delay. Autonomy is slightly undermined by frequent advice and one prompt to pick for her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes a small plan and self-acknowledgment, but does not deeply explore values, confidence, or future motivations. Much of the session relies on clinician suggestions rather than client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, showing some partnership. The early list of tips and expert-driven suggestions weaken genuine shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is present and reinforced, especially around progress and a small step. The clinician does not consistently elicit or amplify the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively reframes slips and all-or-nothing thinking to reduce shame and keep engagement. The score is limited by infrequent double-sided reflections of ambivalence.
