# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is nonjudgmental, respectful, and consistently honors the client’s requests and boundaries. The stance is somewhat procedural rather than deeply relational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects surface-level emotions but the reflections become somewhat formulaic. Deeper themes such as control, shame, and ambivalence are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on relevant intake concerns and does not wander randomly. However, attention drifts toward administrative details rather than consistently guiding the conversation toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and confirms documentation preferences, and the client’s choices shape the session length. Collaboration remains mostly task-centered rather than deeply collaborative around the client’s values or goals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is strong respect for the client’s choices, preferences, and freedom to decide how to proceed. The autonomy support is strongest around logistics and documentation rather than broader change-related decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** One initial open question invites client motivation, but it is not followed up after the client becomes dismissive. The remainder of the session relies mainly on closed intake questions that do little to elicit internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and seeks client input, creating some degree of partnership. However, the collaboration is limited to administrative aspects and does not fully position the client as the expert on his own life or goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal cultivation of change talk, with only a weak initial invitation that is not developed. The client’s statements remain compliance-oriented and are not reinforced as pro-change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not challenge or reinforce the client’s sustain talk, which is appropriate. However, there is little strategic effort to soften sustain talk or use it to explore discrepancy.
