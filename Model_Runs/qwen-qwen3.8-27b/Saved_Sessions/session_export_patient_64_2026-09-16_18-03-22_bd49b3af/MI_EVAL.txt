# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the patient’s reasons and autonomy. Acceptance is slightly limited by formulaic warmth and broad affirmations rather than deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively reflects the patient’s ambivalence, including both personal reluctance and concern for grandparents. Reflections remain mostly at the content level and do not fully explore the deeper emotional texture of the patient’s statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the flu vaccine decision and moves toward a concrete, manageable action step. Direction is strong but could be tightened with less redundancy and a clearer closing summary of next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds the plan around the patient’s own ideas, preferences, and proposed script. Collaboration is slightly reduced by counselor-led pacing and repeated reframing that can make the session feel more guided than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes that the decision belongs to the patient and avoids pressure, guilt, authority, or risk-based persuasion. The session strongly supports the patient’s sense of ownership, choice, and readiness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits the patient’s own reasons for considering vaccination rather than providing external medical arguments. Evocation is effective but could be deeper by exploring confidence, identity, and personal meaning behind the change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses partner-like language and invites the patient’s perspective in shaping the plan. Partnership is slightly limited by moments of consecutive counselor questions/reflections that create a more directive rhythm.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits and reinforces the patient’s reasons for considering vaccination, culminating in a self-generated pharmacy script. Change talk is strong but could be further developed through confidence, commitment, and deeper values exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the patient’s reasons against vaccination and instead reflects them within the larger ambivalence. Sustain talk is handled well but could be more strategically reframed into autonomy and deeper motivation without keeping the status-quo reasoning prominent.
