# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a nonjudgmental, supportive stance and validated the patient's harm-reduction goal without moralizing. Acceptance was solid but at times procedural and slightly compliance-focused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The provider made adequate reflections about frustration and practical needs, but much of the session became informational. Deeper empathy for stress, being lectured, and ambivalence was limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the patient's stated goal of reducing smoking outside during work shifts. Direction was generally strong but became more procedural than change-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invited input and followed the patient's preference toward lozenges, creating partial collaboration. However, much of the interaction was one-way instruction rather than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported by accepting that quitting was not the goal and framing the work as harm reduction. Repeated direct recommendations and safety directives somewhat reduced choice-focused engagement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The provider relied heavily on options, instructions, and cautions rather than eliciting the patient's own motivations. Few questions helped draw out personal reasons, confidence, or values for reducing smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient was invited to set priorities and his preferences influenced the choice of lozenges. After the opening, the session became more expert-driven, making partnership uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offered useful change talk about staying inside and cutting down during work, but the provider did not consistently reflect or amplify it. Little effort was made to expand the patient's own motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepted the patient's sustain talk without arguing and used autonomy-supportive language. Active softening was limited, with little double-sided reflection to resolve ambivalence.
