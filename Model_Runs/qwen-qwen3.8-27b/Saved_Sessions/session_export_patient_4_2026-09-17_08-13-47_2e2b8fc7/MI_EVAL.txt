# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone while validating Aizada’s fear and need to be taken seriously. The acceptance is strong but remains somewhat agenda-linked rather than explicitly affirming her mixed feelings independent of cooperation or insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections to understand Aizada’s fear of cancer, need for proof, and ambivalence about certainty. Empathy is strong but could go deeper by naming the grief, exhaustion, isolation, and fatigue beneath her biomedical demands.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on Aizada’s central concern and moves toward a concrete, respectful next step. However, the direction could better connect the testing goal to broader change goals, such as reducing hypervigilance and reconnecting with meaningful activities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative stance, invites Aizada’s input, and co-constructs a plan with her and her husband. Collaboration could be further balanced by exploring alternative directions alongside the medical testing plan, such as coping or lifestyle re-engagement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice through open, non-directive questions and avoids pressuring Aizada into testing or minimizing her fear. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming that she has full ownership of the decision, especially in the face of medical uncertainty.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some of Aizada’s values and motivations, including the garden, feeling less tight, and wanting to be taken seriously. However, evocation remains heavily focused on medical proof and does not fully explore her internal resources, confidence, or reasons for broader change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Aizada as an authority on her experience and works with her on a plan that includes her husband. Partnership is strong but could be more explicit by affirming Aizada’s expertise and inviting her to set the agenda or choose among mutually defined goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits meaningful change talk related to hope, values, and a possible action step, such as returning to the garden and feeling less trapped. However, the change talk is not consistently amplified, summarized, or developed using DARN-C language or contrast with the feared outcome.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Aizada and acknowledges the legitimacy of her fear and need for testing. However, repeated reinforcement of the cancer fear and biomedical certainty-seeking may inadvertently maintain the status quo, and double-sided reflections could better soften sustain talk.
