# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental toward the client’s fear, exhaustion, and need for control. Acceptance could be deepened by more explicit normalization of ambivalence and helplessness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures feelings of being stuck, in pain, and seeking relief. A 5 would require deeper emotional exploration, especially around confusion, shame, and helplessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on safety, control, and next-step options. The direction becomes somewhat circular at points, which could be improved by summarizing and moving toward a concrete client-owned plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaves as a collaborator rather than an expert and allows the client’s ideas to shape the session. More explicit joint agenda-setting and co-creation would strengthen this area.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports the client’s autonomy, avoids pressure or advice, and repeatedly frames support as optional. This is one of the strongest aspects of the session, especially given the self-harm context.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses evocative questions to draw out some client-generated safety ideas. However, the evocation remains relatively superficial and does not consistently elicit deeper change-related motivation, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-directive stance and frequently invites the client’s perspective. A 5 would require more explicit shared agenda-setting or co-creation of the session focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers several safety-related ideas, but the therapist does not consistently develop or amplify this change talk. More exploration of desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment language is needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with or challenging the client’s sustain talk, which is appropriate. However, the session remains heavily influenced by sustain talk, and stronger softening through double-sided reflections or pivots to exceptions and values would be more effective.
