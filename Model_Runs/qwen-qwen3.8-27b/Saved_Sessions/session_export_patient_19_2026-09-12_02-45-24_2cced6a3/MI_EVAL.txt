# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental but tends to use generic normalizing language. There are missed opportunities to deepen validation around shame and identity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captures Anya's ambivalence and emotional concerns. Empathy could be stronger with more sustained exploration of shame, fear, and invisibility.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays largely focused on reducing gambling-related financial harm and developing a concrete plan. Focus occasionally broadens into general social-life problem-solving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with the patient to build a client-owned plan, such as 'cash only' and using Sarah as backup. More equal co-creation would reduce the clinician-driven nature of some suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, offers choices, and respects Anya's hesitation. Explicit language reinforcing her ownership and decision-making could be more consistent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions successfully elicit Anya's own reasons, goals, and action ideas. Evocation could be improved by using more scaling questions and less clinician-generated content.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally shares power by asking permission and inviting client-led choices. A higher level would involve more client-generated ideas before the psychiatrist offers options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes change talk, including concrete steps like 'cash only' and using a backup friend. Change talk could be further strengthened by amplifying, summarizing, and scaling Anya's motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without judgment, and ambivalence is reflected. The session could pivot more quickly and consistently away from the positive functions of the current behavior back toward change.
