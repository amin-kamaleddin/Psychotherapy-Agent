# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but the tone is more task-focused than warmly affirming. Acceptance is conditional and centers on managing the conduct meeting rather than affirming Jason as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate, surface-level reflections and understands the practical stakes. However, reflections do not fully explore Jason’s deeper emotions, ambivalence, fear, embarrassment, or social values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The conversation drifts toward meeting strategy, sanctions, and protective wording rather than alcohol-related behavior change. The central MI target of motivating change is not consistently addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers scripts and asks which one fits Jason’s voice, showing some collaboration. However, the psychiatrist remains the primary strategist, and Jason’s role is limited to choosing among provided options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some autonomy-supportive moves, such as offering choices and asking for Jason’s preference. Yet the psychiatrist is also directive about what to say, how to behave, and how to present the message.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jason’s own motivations, values, and personal reasons for change are only lightly drawn out. Much of the response relies on the psychiatrist supplying reasons, strategies, and exact phrases.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports some partnership by offering options and inviting Jason to select wording. Nevertheless, the session is largely guided by the psychiatrist’s strategy rather than Jason’s own agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited elicitation of authentic change talk about alcohol-related behavior. Statements like “I won’t do it again” function more as compliance language than internalized motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jason’s minimization, near-21 framing, and focus on avoiding consequences are not effectively softened or redirected. Some phrasing may subtly reinforce a defensive, sanction-avoidance stance.
