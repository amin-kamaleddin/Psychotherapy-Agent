# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of the client's ambivalence and readiness. Warmth was present but remained somewhat practical and task-oriented rather than deeply relational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provided adequate reflective listening and accurately summarized parts of the client's conflict and concerns. However, several deeper emotional and identity-related statements were underexplored, limiting the overall depth of empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior of moving toward employment and a law-abiding life. The direction became somewhat overly logistical toward the end, which narrowed deeper MI exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was evidence of collaboration, with the client's preferences shaping choices such as agency work and a simple log. The stance remained fairly directive, as the psychiatrist frequently introduced structured options and practical solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported autonomy by inviting client choice, respecting boundaries, and affirming the client's desire to proceed in his own way. Some prescriptive suggestions and planning remained directive enough to limit a higher score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some evocative techniques, especially scaling questions and prompts to explore what would increase readiness. Evocation was present but somewhat superficial, as powerful value-based statements were not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and adapted to the client's choices, showing a partial collaborative stance. However, the session remained more expert-led, with frequent lists and options positioned by the counselor rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited meaningful change talk, including the client's intention to walk into a warehouse the next morning. The opportunity to deepen or amplify this change talk was not consistently pursued.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with resistance and responded in ways that reduced friction, such as normalizing quitting as data. The response was helpful but moved too quickly into practical problem-solving rather than fully processing ambivalence.
