# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized Andrei's ambivalence and preference for a brief visit. Acceptance was supportive but somewhat procedural, with less emotionally personalized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflections of Andrei's ambivalence, concern about energy, and need to unwind. Empathy was solid but remained somewhat surface-level and did not deeply explore the meaning of his concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on blood pressure and alcohol use and moved toward a clear, low-burden action plan. Direction was strong, though the psychiatrist moved quickly into planning and repeated a similar prompt about what matters most.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and invited Andrei to choose doable options, which shaped the plan. Collaboration was not fully equal because the psychiatrist still controlled much of the structure and offered a preselected menu.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked permission, offered choices, and respected Andrei's preference for a short, nonjudgmental check-in. Autonomy support was good, though some of the suggested steps and plan framing were still fairly prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked some useful open questions about doable steps, confidence, priorities, and how the delayed drink felt. Evocation was limited because the psychiatrist often provided reasons or options before fully eliciting Andrei's own personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Andrei as an expert on his own life and invited input on what was realistic and doable. Partnership was clear, but the structured list of options made the session feel somewhat provider-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helped Andrei identify small changes, increase confidence, and commit to a delayed-drink experiment. Change talk was evoked but not consistently deepened or amplified by exploring Andrei's values and motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk about needing the beer to unwind without arguing or pushing abstinence. The response was accepting but not fully strategic, using mainly acknowledgment rather than more nuanced MI techniques to explore tension.
