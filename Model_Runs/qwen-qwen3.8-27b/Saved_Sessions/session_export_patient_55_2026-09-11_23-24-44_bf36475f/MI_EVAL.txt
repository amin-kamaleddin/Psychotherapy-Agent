# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and safety-focused, but remains more informational than genuinely affirming. Repeated risk warnings and brief advice limit the warmth and unconditional positive regard needed for a higher rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician mostly summarizes factual content rather than reflecting the client’s feelings or perspective. There is limited exploration of how the client feels about the referral, being labeled high-risk, or managing IB and band demands.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with the referral concern but drifts into general advice about studying, sleep, hydration, and safety. A clear strategic focus on the target behavior or building motivation for change is not consistently maintained.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative gestures, such as asking for the client’s preference, but the interaction is largely one-sided and clinician-led. The client’s own ideas do not meaningfully shape the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers some choice and acknowledges the client’s preference against a breathing exercise. However, frequent unsolicited tips and reminders reduce the sense that the client is fully in charge of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician asks a few questions that could relate to motivation, but the main focus is information, risk warnings, and harm-reduction advice. The client’s own motivations, values, or internal reasons for change are not effectively elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician offers initial choices between alcohol and stress, showing some surface-level partnership. The interaction quickly becomes expert-driven with repeated advice-giving, limiting true collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are weak attempts to invite change-related thinking, but the clinician does not actively elicit the client’s own reasons, confidence, or values for change. Meaningful client-generated change talk is not produced or strengthened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician does not argue directly, but does not effectively reframe the client’s statements minimizing drinking. Shifting into risk information and harm-reduction advice can reinforce a lecturing dynamic rather than resolving ambivalence.
