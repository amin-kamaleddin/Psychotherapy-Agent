# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone while validating the patient’s need for clarity and safety. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, focusing more on task completion than deeper individualized unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the patient’s frustration and desire to understand the medication. Empathy is adequate but remains mostly cognitive and surface-level, missing opportunities to explore deeper emotional undercurrents.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of understanding and adhering to the new cholesterol medication. The flow is logical and efficient, though the psychiatrist occasionally offers additional suggestions after the patient indicates she feels set.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist and patient jointly develop a practical adherence plan, but the interaction often remains counselor-led. The psychiatrist frequently supplies information and options rather than consistently inviting the patient to generate her own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices, respects the patient’s decisions, and avoids pressuring her to adopt additional supports. Autonomy support is strong, though it is slightly limited by the substantial amount of unsolicited clinical information.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses helpful evocative questions and elicits useful change talk about the patient’s plan and confidence. However, much of the session is driven by clinical explanations and problem-solving rather than deeply eliciting the patient’s own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids a fully authoritarian stance, with the patient’s preferences shaping the final plan. Nevertheless, the psychiatrist often assumes the expert information-provider role, so the power balance still leans toward the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient produces meaningful change talk about her plan, confidence, and intended actions. The psychiatrist supports this with questions and reinforcement but does not consistently deepen, amplify, or summarize the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles mild sustain talk without arguing or becoming defensive, and reframes concerns as reasonable. However, more MI-consistent softening strategies, such as double-sided reflections or strategic redirection toward change, are underused.
