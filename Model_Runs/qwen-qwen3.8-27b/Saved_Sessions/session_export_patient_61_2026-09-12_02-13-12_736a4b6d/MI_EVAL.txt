# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client's overwhelm. Could be stronger with more individualized affirmations rather than relying on generic reassurance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate reflections of the client's ambivalence, overwhelm, and resentment. Misses opportunities for deeper, emotionally nuanced exploration of underlying feelings like fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear strategic focus on diabetes self-management without major topic drift. Moves prematurely toward concrete task development before fully exploring deeper ambivalence and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's preferences successfully co-construct the final, low-pressure plan. However, the therapist still supplies too much of the structure and options, making the interaction partially therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A major strength, repeatedly emphasizing choice, avoiding coercive language, and respecting the client's right to decide. The frequent use of scaling and scheduling questions subtly creates an unintended expectation of performance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses scaling questions but remains too focused on behavior and willingness rather than deeper intrinsic motivation. Misses key opportunities to evoke the client's values or what the change would truly mean to him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites client input and honors his choices in shaping the final plan. However, the therapist is somewhat expert-driven, supplying too many specific micro-options rather than letting the client lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some weak change talk but fails to consistently affirm or amplify it to strengthen personal motivation. Misses opportunities to explore what a successful change would mean to the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoids arguing but lacks strategic double-sided reflections to redirect sustain talk toward change. The repeated focus on tiny tasks inadvertently reinforces the client's frame of care as an imposed burden.
