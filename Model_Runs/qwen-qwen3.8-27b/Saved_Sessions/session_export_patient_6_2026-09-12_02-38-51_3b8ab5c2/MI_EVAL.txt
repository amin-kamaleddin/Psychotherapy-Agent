# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and normalizing, creating a nonjudgmental space. Validation could be more individualized and emotionally specific to reach a higher level.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Alexei’s concerns about his mother, recruitment, and peer pressure. Reflections could be more emotionally nuanced, capturing embarrassment, fear, or identity worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol risk and building refusal/exit skills. It becomes somewhat narrow and script-driven, with a few minor tangents.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Alexei contributes meaningfully by selecting and refining realistic refusal lines. The counselor still leads with options and scripts before the client generates more of his own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor emphasizes choice and avoids pressuring Alexei to change. It is less optimal because specific scripts and behavioral instructions can feel mildly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits Alexei’s own motivation by connecting change to recruitment and risk reduction. There is limited further evocation of internal reasons, confidence, or future self.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and responds to Alexei’s preferences. However, the interaction is less balanced because the counselor frequently provides scripts before Alexei generates his own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits strong importance and readiness related to Alexei’s future and sports goals. It does not consistently deepen his own values, confidence, or desired identity language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early sustain talk is handled without argument, and the need for a low-drama approach is acknowledged. More sophisticated techniques, such as double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance, would strengthen this area.
