# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, normalizes Daniel's reaction, and avoids judgment or pressure. Their responses validate both the concern about drinking and the value of his routine.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures Daniel's ambivalence and underlying tension. However, the reflections remain more cognitive than emotional and do not always name deeper feelings such as exhaustion or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on alcohol use and its possible impact on marathon training, moving from screening concerns to a concrete low-effort experiment. It does not fully reconnect the small Tuesday trial to the broader goal of reducing overall alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is highly collaborative, with the psychiatrist inviting Daniel's perspective and following his ideas rather than imposing a predetermined plan. Daniel's contributions directly shape the session and the resulting plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, with repeated emphasis on Daniel's choice, freedom, and self-direction. The psychiatrist avoids advice-giving, pressure, or implied obligation while respecting Daniel's reservations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits motivation from within Daniel by using open questions and reflections that lead him to articulate his own reasons and plan. Some framing remains therapist-generated, and Daniel's own language could be more consistently expanded into deeper change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates that Daniel is the expert on his own life and choices, positioning themselves as a supportive collaborator rather than an authority. The session is shaped by Daniel's ideas without expert-driven agenda or covert control.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits change talk through open, client-directed questions, leading Daniel to express desire, ability, and a concrete plan. The change talk is not always amplified or developed further, especially in terms of identity- or value-based motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by validating minimization and concerns about punishment while gently redirecting toward exploration. Some sustain talk remains untransformed, particularly statements about low motivation and lack of promises.
