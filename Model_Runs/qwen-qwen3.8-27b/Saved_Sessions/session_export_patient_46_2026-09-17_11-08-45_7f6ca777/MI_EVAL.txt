# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintains a nonjudgmental tone, validates Ethan's age and right to make mistakes, and avoids labeling him as a problem. The warmth is somewhat professional and clinical rather than deeply personal, and there's no explicit statement of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening with complex reflections that capture emotional nuance, such as identifying the tension between genuine enjoyment and social pressure. A 5 would require even more depth in exploring underlying emotions like fear of social rejection or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session follows a logical progression from resistance through social pressures to values-based motivation and potential solutions, with a clear focus on Ethan's alcohol use. A 5 would require tighter strategic guidance with explicit theme summarizing or a clearer collaboratively identified next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-determines the session's content by inviting Ethan's input and framing reflections as invitations rather than assessments. A 5 would require more explicit co-creation of the agenda or visible moments where Ethan's contributions substantially redirect the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Ethan's right to make his own choices and never pressures him to commit to change or imposes a plan. Autonomy support could be slightly stronger with more emphatic affirmations of Ethan's ownership of his choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits motivation from within Ethan through strategic questions that connect his values to his current behavior, avoiding advice or lecturing. A 5 would require more development of the change talk once it appears, such as expanding on Ethan's own alternative solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance with language that positions Ethan as the authority on his own experience and avoids expert-driven advice. The power dynamic remains subtly clinician-led and could be strengthened with more explicit power-sharing language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes change talk through strategic questions, leading Ethan to articulate his own alternative lifestyle and values-based reasons for change. The psychiatrist could have done more to amplify and summarize the change talk Ethan produced more explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by not arguing against it and using strategic reflection to highlight discrepancies. A double-sided reflection that more explicitly tilts toward change when Ethan repeatedly minimizes would be stronger.
