# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, normalizes Marcus’s overwhelm and fear, and avoids shaming his avoidance. Acceptance is not a 5 because it is tied mainly to the task and includes some prescriptive directives.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges needle fear, pressure, and the normalcy of Marcus’s reaction. Reflections are present but brief, often shifting quickly into advice without fully exploring his ambivalence, fear of failure, or need for reassurance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on blood glucose monitoring, meter use, and setting a small next step. It is not a 5 because much of the direction becomes technical instruction rather than MI-focused motivation-building.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works cooperatively by letting Marcus describe the meter, guiding steps without taking control, and following his pace. It is not a 5 because the psychiatrist still leads much of the session with clinical advice and technical routines.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus is offered choices, given the option to stop, and his pace is respected throughout. Some statements such as “Do the test, record the number, and don’t delete it” are more directive and slightly reduce autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist opens with an appropriate question and invites Marcus to set a small goal. Most of the session, however, is driven by psychoeducation and instruction rather than evoking Marcus’s own values, reasons, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus’s input is sought and his choices influence immediate decisions, showing some partnership. However, the psychiatrist remains the main expert providing procedural guidance, so the interaction does not fully convey that Marcus holds the expertise for his own change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus offers change talk, such as “I guess I can try that” and “If the number’s high, I’ll just call the office, not delete it.” The psychiatrist responds supportively but does not consistently amplify this change talk or deepen it with motivational questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Marcus’s concerns and often normalizes them, which helps reduce confrontation. He does not consistently use double-sided reflections or reframing to turn sustain talk into change talk, and some responses maintain the avoidance focus.
