# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, with explicit validation of Natalia's agency and worth (e.g., 'You're not dodging. You're just... not yet'). Never labels, disapproves, or minimizes; language throughout communicates unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections that capture both surface behavior and deeper identity-level concerns (e.g., 'when the thing that quiets me is gone, what's left?'). Tracks emotional nuance—guilt, exhaustion, shame—with precision and depth throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical progression from ambivalence through behavior to the deeper 'pause' need, with each question building on the last. Could be slightly tighter in moments when the conversation broadens into general emotional territory, briefly diluting the behavioral focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructive; the psychiatrist uses Natalia's own language and metaphors ('pause button,' 'survival mode,' 'loop') and positions herself as a thinking partner rather than authority. Session direction is substantially shaped by Natalia's disclosures rather than a predetermined agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary; consistently removes pressure and validates Natalia's right to not have answers ('You don't have to see the whole staircase to take the next step'). Never prescribes or implies she should have a plan; all suggestions are framed as questions back to her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out Natalia's own motivations and change talk through open, non-prescriptive questions. Could be more comprehensive by incorporating scaling questions, confidence ratings, or deeper values exploration beyond her son.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within Natalia, never assuming the answer or prescribing solutions. Language like 'I'd love to hear from you' and 'what would a small shift look like to you?' reinforces a true power balance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits multiple instances of change talk and skillfully amplifies them ('That's not small, Natalia'). Could be more systematic by incorporating scaling questions or prompting Natalia to articulate beliefs about her own capacity to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by reflecting underlying meaning rather than arguing or dismissing (e.g., reframing 'avoiding' as her brain grading the pause). Could benefit from more explicit double-sided reflections to actively hold ambivalence rather than primarily reflecting one side.
