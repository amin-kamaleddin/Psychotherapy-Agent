# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful and validates the student's frustration. However, repeated identical safety checks and directive advice make the stance less consistently warm and MI-consistent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects surface emotions such as annoyance and frustration. The reflections do not fully capture deeper ambivalence, defensiveness, or concern about the student's record and goals.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is organized around practical and legal next steps. However, it does not consistently focus on alcohol use, its risks, or discrepancy between drinking behavior and academic goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and uses the student's information to build a timeline and statement. The conversation remains unevenly collaborative because the psychiatrist largely drives the agenda and recommendations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, offers choices, and avoids harsh blame. Some directive recommendations reduce emphasis on the student's own ownership and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is an initial open question about what the student wants to change, but the session quickly shifts to practical solutions. The psychiatrist does not strongly elicit or expand the student's own motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows partial partnership by asking for input and offering choices. The stance remains more expert-driven and problem-solving than a true collaborative exploration of change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The student offers some change talk, such as wanting to protect his record and not drinking tonight. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on these statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the student's minimization of the incident. However, the sustain talk is not effectively softened or redirected through double-sided reflections or values-based exploration.
