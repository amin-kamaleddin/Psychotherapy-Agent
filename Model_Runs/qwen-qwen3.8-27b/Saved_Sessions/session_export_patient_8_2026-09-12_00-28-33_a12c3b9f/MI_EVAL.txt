# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a polite, nonjudgmental tone and uses validating language. However, persistent pressure to follow a specific plan somewhat undermines acceptance when the client expresses resistance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic reflective listening is present, but empathy remains surface-level and quickly shifts to problem-solving. Deeper ambivalence and the emotional function of the behavior are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior and the 10-minute timer task. However, direction is driven more by the psychiatrist's agenda than by collaborative exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely drives the session by providing solutions, steps, and reminders. The client mostly reacts to the plan rather than co-creating the therapeutic direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some choices are offered, but the approach remains highly prescriptive with added conditions and techniques. The client's reluctance toward certain tools is not fully honored as a right to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is little effort to elicit the client's own reasons, values, or motivation for change. Questions and interventions are mostly directive and focused on task completion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Power is uneven, with the psychiatrist acting more like an expert consultant than a partner. The client's contributions are reactive, modifying the psychiatrist's plan rather than shaping it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effort to draw out the client's own language or desire for change. The session moves quickly into behavioral interventions without evoking intrinsic reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's sustain talk is acknowledged briefly but not deeply explored or reflected back in a way that softens ambivalence. No MI-specific techniques are used to help weigh pros and cons.
