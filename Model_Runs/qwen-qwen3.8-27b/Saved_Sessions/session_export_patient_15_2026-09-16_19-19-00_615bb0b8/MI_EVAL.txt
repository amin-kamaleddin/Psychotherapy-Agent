# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and uses affirming phrases without challenging the patient. Acceptance is slightly limited by repetitive language and fewer explicit affirmations of strengths or values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects key ambivalences, especially the tension between concern about the blood pressure reading and feeling fine overall. Empathy is helpful but remains somewhat surface-level and repetitive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays broadly focused on understanding the blood pressure reading and possible next steps. The target behavior or specific change goal remains unclear, with movement among rechecking, medication, lifestyle, and alcohol.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses questions and invitations to involve the patient, but the interaction remains relatively provider-led. More explicit co-creation would help the patient’s preferences shape the agenda more visibly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the patient’s right to decide and avoids pressure toward medication or specific lifestyle changes. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the patient’s ownership of the process and choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and asks what would help the patient feel confident about trying lifestyle changes. Evocation is limited because the clinician often offers options rather than deeply exploring the patient’s own values, motivations, or hopes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys a collaborative posture and allows the patient’s concerns to influence the session’s direction. Partnership would be stronger if the clinician more explicitly positioned the patient as the expert in his own life and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient offers some change talk, including a preference for lifestyle changes before medication. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or deepen this change talk to build motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and acknowledges the patient’s minimization of blood pressure and alcohol use. Some sustain talk, especially around alcohol, is left underexplored rather than gently reflected or pivoted.
