# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses supportive, nonjudgmental language such as “I hear you” and “thank you for trying.” The final expression of doubt could have received stronger validation of Elias’s frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture discouragement and knee-pain limitations but remain somewhat task-focused. Deeper emotional themes, such as fear of failure and the meaning of past group experiences, were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on increasing physical activity and creates a specific, achievable plan. The main limitation is that the focus is more plan-centered than motivation-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias contributes meaningfully by choosing ankle pumps, commercial breaks, and his wife as a cue. The interaction becomes more therapist-directed later, with repeated reminders and prescriptive details.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choice with questions like “Which feels doable?” and normalizes missed opportunities. However, repeated directive advice and plan repetition reduce the client’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions help Elias identify a tiny goal and cue. Missed opportunities include deeper exploration of his values, confidence, and doubts about whether change can make a difference.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates on a workable plan and allows Elias to shape parts of it. The expert-driven offering of options and medical advice makes the partnership uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias offers change talk such as “I’ll pump when she says pump” and “It’s something, at least.” The therapist does not systematically elicit deeper desire, ability, reasons, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is softened through normalizing missed cues and describing the plan as realistic. Important concerns about knee pain, fear of standing, and doubt about long-term change were not more deeply addressed.
