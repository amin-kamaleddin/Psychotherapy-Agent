# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance of Margaret’s social life and autonomy. Acceptance could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming her worth independent of behavior and normalizing the emotional difficulty of the situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects key themes, including the pub as friendship, safety after the fall, and independence versus hovering. Empathy could be deepened by exploring more emotional nuance, such as fear of falling, embarrassment, or frustration with family concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing alcohol use in a way that supports safety after the fall. There is no drift away from the target behavior, and the session progresses logically toward a practical plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is built from Margaret’s own suggestions, such as the iced tea alternative, not reporting to David, and the kitchen trip. The final plan is clearly co-created rather than imposed by the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with consistent emphasis on Margaret’s freedom, choice, and self-direction. The psychiatrist avoids imposing abstinence, tracking, or surveillance, supporting a self-directed plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits Margaret’s own reasons, preferences, and problem-solving ideas without giving direct advice. Evocation could be deepened by more explicitly exploring values, importance, confidence, and discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally operates from a partnership stance by asking for Margaret’s view and shaping the plan around her ideas. Partnership could be strengthened by reducing therapist-structured prompts and more explicitly acknowledging Margaret as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates opportunities for Margaret to express desire and ability to change, such as trying iced tea and standing up to break the spell. Cultivating change talk could be stronger by more explicitly eliciting reasons, values, importance, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk reasonably well by not arguing with Margaret or pushing for abstinence. This skill could be strengthened by using more explicit double-sided reflections to hold both sides of her ambivalence.
