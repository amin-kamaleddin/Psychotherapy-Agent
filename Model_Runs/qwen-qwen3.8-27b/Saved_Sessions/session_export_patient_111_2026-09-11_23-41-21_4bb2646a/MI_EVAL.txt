# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but the acceptance feels formulaic. The frequent shift into practical menus and scripts can reduce relational warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Richard’s surface-level perspective but remain mostly cognitive and behavioral. Deeper emotions about control, fear, and the meaning of his marriage are missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session quickly drifts away from the target behavior and toward managing the wife’s pressure. There is no clear strategic focus on exploring Richard’s ambivalence about smoking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, but the counselor mostly generates the options and solutions. Richard’s rejections lead to more counselor-driven menus rather than client-led exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and states they will not push, which helps reduce direct pressure. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated suggestions of specific actions and scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak because the session relies heavily on information-giving and practical options. Clear opportunities to elicit internal motivation are not effectively used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some partnership behaviors, such as offering choices and using inclusive language. The counselor still assumes the primary problem-solving role, limiting Richard’s ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes few attempts to evoke or strengthen Richard’s own change talk. Spontaneous change talk about his wife, health, and marriage is not amplified or followed up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and acknowledges resistance, which is better than confrontation. However, sustain talk is not skillfully softened using MI-specific reflection or reconciliation strategies.
