# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes David’s ambivalence without pressuring cessation. It falls slightly short of exemplary because the warmth remains somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses accurate reflections of David’s goals, confidence level, and practical preferences. However, the responses are often more cognitive than emotional and miss deeper feelings such as exhaustion, irritability, and fear of side effects.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing daytime smoking and moves from ambivalence toward a practical one-week experiment. A stronger MI summary could further consolidate the target behavior, plan, confidence, and barriers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist collaboratively builds a realistic plan around David’s routines, partner support, and practical concerns. The score is reduced because detailed product information is sometimes provided before fully inviting David to process or choose.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist consistently respects choice, avoids coercive language, and supports David’s decisions about patch use and backup strategies. Some repeated prompts and planning nudges could feel subtly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence rulers elicit reasons for change and a personal definition of success. The therapist could better develop change talk by exploring values, past successes, and self-efficacy before moving into practical advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist invites input and allows David’s practical details to shape the intervention. The balance is weakened by frequent expert guidance, product options, and dosing information that position the therapist more as an adviser than a partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses helpful questions to elicit change-oriented language and a concrete plan. It does not reach full mastery because David’s change talk is not consistently amplified, reflected, or deepened before shifting into advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing, judging, or pushing cold-turkey cessation. The response could be strengthened by using more complex reflections to explore the meaning of statements like “if it doesn’t work, I’ll just smoke.”
