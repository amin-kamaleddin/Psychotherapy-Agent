# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and generally supportive tone throughout. The warmth is somewhat formulaic and managerial rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate early in the session, especially in summarizing Thomas’s perspective. Empathy becomes superficial and does not consistently explore the emotional or relational meaning behind his minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking, alcohol use, and the follow-up plan without significant drift. However, the direction becomes dominated by fact-giving and logistics rather than MI-oriented change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration in asking for input, offering options, and allowing Thomas to choose monitoring. Overall, the interaction remains more clinician-directed than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, asks what is doable, and avoids explicit pressure. Autonomy support is limited because the plan is still largely clinician-led and framed as the appropriate next step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions invite Thomas to articulate his perspective and readiness. Evocation is inconsistent because the psychiatrist then provides reasons and information rather than continuing to elicit Thomas’s own change-related thoughts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions, uses a scale, and asks permission before sharing findings. Partnership is partial because the psychiatrist still defines the problem, options, and plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few openings that could support change talk, but they are not developed. The psychiatrist does not follow up on scaling questions or amplify Thomas’s limited change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce Thomas’s sustain talk. Active softening is limited, with minimal use of double-sided reflections or redirection toward values and change.
