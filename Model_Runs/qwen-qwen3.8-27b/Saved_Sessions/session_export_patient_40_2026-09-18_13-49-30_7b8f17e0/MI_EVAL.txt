# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent, nonjudgmental stance throughout with effective normalization of the client's anxiety and relapse history. Some moments feel slightly performative or formulaic, and occasional interpretive overlays slightly override the client's own meaning-making.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking on the client's specific experiences and emotional contrasts. Some reflections add the psychiatrist's interpretive layer rather than capturing the pure emotional core, and the fear underlying the 2am 'math' is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical progression from identifying the core concern to exploring the 2am vulnerability, support system, and pride/armor barrier. The session ends somewhat abruptly without a brief collaborative summary or a concrete next step identified by the client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative frame established from the opening question, with the client's insights clearly shaping the session's focus and direction. The tendency to add interpretive meaning before the client has fully articulated it for himself slightly undermines the co-constructed nature of the work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent use of open questions, validation of ambivalence, and avoidance of prescriptive language throughout the session. Interpretive statements that label the client's experience (e.g., 'that's how close the old version of you still is') subtly direct meaning-making rather than leaving it to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of open questions and strategic reflections that elicit the client's own reasons for change tied to personal values. The session could have gone further in evoking ability and commitment language, particularly when the client expressed 'I think I can try that.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative tone with inclusive language and ceding of agenda to the client from the outset. The psychiatrist's interpretive additions occasionally position them as the meaning-maker rather than a partner helping the client discover his own conclusions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully reflects back and amplifies the client's own change talk, particularly around identity and the desire to be present for his daughter. Could have gone further in eliciting commitment and ability dimensions (e.g., confidence in using the reframe at 2am).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without argumentation or dismissal, normalizing uncertainty and using it as a source of information. The reframe of pride as 'protection' could be seen as slightly minimizing the sustain talk rather than using a full double-sided reflection that honors both sides simultaneously.
