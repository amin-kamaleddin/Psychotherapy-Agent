# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive throughout. Acceptance could be warmer when Amira expresses feeling alone and unsupported.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Amira’s ambivalence and pain, especially early in the session. Later, empathy becomes less consistent as the focus quickly shifts to practical planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing drug use and identifying a concrete next step. Direction is strong but sometimes becomes overly task-focused when emotional barriers emerge.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amira helps shape the goal and plan, including choosing to seal the drugs and set an alarm. The collaboration is somewhat counselor-driven because the psychiatrist frequently provides structured options before eliciting Amira’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices, inviting Amira to select a step, and emphasizing that she is in charge. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming her right to do nothing or move at her own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk by asking about goals, smallest changes, and confidence. Evocation is limited because the session relies more on practical options and clinician rationale than on deeper exploration of Amira’s own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Amira to participate in planning. The partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist often positions as the problem-solver by supplying options before fully centering Amira’s expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes several change statements, such as wanting to stop using and trying one sober day. Change talk is present but not deeply amplified through exploration of Amira’s reasons, values, or meaning of recovery.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized without becoming argumentative. The psychiatrist could better soften sustain talk by using more double-sided reflections that hold both Amira’s difficulty and emerging capacity for change.
