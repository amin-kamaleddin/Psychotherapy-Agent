# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, normalizing Derrick’s reluctance and ambivalence. There is no visible pressure, labeling, or moralizing about his drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are nuanced and accurate, capturing Derrick’s values, frustration, and internal self-talk. Complex reflections effectively mirror ambivalence without taking sides.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior of avoiding another DUI by managing drinking, peer pressure, and driving. The progression from values to concrete behavior is clear and logical.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist functions as an equal partner, helping Derrick generate practical steps from his own ideas. The session is co-constructed rather than therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Derrick’s sense of choice and avoids controlling or prescriptive language. Statements such as “You chose it… That’s practical and it’s yours” strongly reinforce ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, evocative questions to help Derrick access his own values, strategies, and self-efficacy. It could be stronger with more targeted questions about readiness, confidence, importance, and identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative language and questions clearly position Derrick as the decision-maker in the process. It could improve by more explicitly naming Derrick as the expert on his own Friday nights, peers, and self-talk.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Derrick to articulate his own reasons, plans, and capabilities and affirms emerging change talk. It could be stronger by more explicitly amplifying change talk using DARN-C language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles ambivalence and sustain talk without arguing or dismissing, especially around not wanting to be “that guy.” It could improve by using more explicit double-sided reflections that link sustain talk directly to change.
