# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates the client's perspective. It falls short of a 5 because the acceptance is somewhat professional and could be warmer and more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures the client's ambivalence and values. It is not a 5 because the reflections sometimes remain cognitive rather than fully accessing deeper emotions such as shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on stress, home dynamics, and communication, but the target behavior of reducing drug use is not consistently foregrounded. A stronger score would require more deliberate return to the behavior itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the session as a joint exploration and builds on the client's own ideas rather than imposing solutions. It is not a 5 because the conversation is still partly guided by the psychiatrist's sequence of questions rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports client autonomy by avoiding pressure, judgment, or the need to decide immediately. Some questions still gently steer toward identifying limits or costs, which keeps this below a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions that bring out some client-driven motivation and self-awareness. However, the evocation is not sustained or deep enough, lacking importance/confidence questions and summaries of change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-hierarchical stance and frequently invites the client's perspective. It is not a 5 because explicit power sharing, such as letting the client set the agenda, is limited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some meaningful change talk related to peace, functionality, and not snapping. This is not fully amplified, summarized, or connected to values, and the session ends with the client reasserting sustain talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and makes reasonable attempts to reframe rigid statements without confrontation. However, the response to minimization and sustain talk is not consistently strategic or effective.
