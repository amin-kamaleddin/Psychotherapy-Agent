# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and validates the patient’s difficulty with cravings. However, the warmth is somewhat procedural and does not fully affirm the patient’s worth or courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate for the patient’s goal, medication interest, and social worries. Empathy remains uneven and often misses deeper emotional cues such as self-doubt, chest tightness, and fear of appearing odd.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on smoking cessation, cravings, and high-risk situations. It moves logically from assessment to medication, planning, scripting, and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by letting the patient choose medication, script, and coping plan. Collaboration is reduced at times by didactic teaching and premature recommendations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy through choices, permissions, and respect for the patient’s decisions. Occasional evaluative statements such as “Good plan” and detailed instructions make the stance less autonomy-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and past-attempt questions help elicit patient input. However, the session is more advice- and problem-solving-driven than evocative of the patient’s own motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient choices meaningfully shape the plan, but the psychiatrist frequently provides answers before fully exploring the patient’s own knowledge. A more equal partnership would elicit patient-generated solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient offers strong change talk, and the psychiatrist affirms parts of the plan. The clinician does not consistently amplify, reflect back, or expand the patient’s own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without arguing or dismissing the patient’s concerns. The psychiatrist could do more MI-style redirection and double-sided reflection instead of relying on reassurance and problem-solving.
