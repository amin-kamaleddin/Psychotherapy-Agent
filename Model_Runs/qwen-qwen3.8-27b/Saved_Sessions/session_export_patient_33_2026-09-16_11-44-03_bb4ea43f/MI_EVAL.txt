# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and warm stance throughout the session. There was no shaming or moralizing, even when the client expressed he was not ready to quit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated accurate reflective listening and acknowledged Diego’s concerns about overdose, stress, and preference for low-drama. However, reflections remained somewhat task-focused and did not consistently explore deeper emotions such as fear, shame, or exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the client’s stated goal of harm reduction and overdose prevention. The psychiatrist avoided drifting into unrelated topics or pushing toward abstinence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist created a genuinely collaborative process by offering options and allowing Diego to shape the plan. Diego’s preferences directly guided the content, including the housemate message, checklist, and decision to stop when tired.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported Diego’s freedom to choose and respected his current readiness. The client explicitly appreciated that he was not pressured to commit to quitting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective evocation occurred early through open questions about concerns and doable next steps. However, much of the session became logistical and information-based, with limited deeper exploration of values, confidence, or meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Diego as the expert in his own life and invited him to choose actions and wording. Partnership could be stronger by eliciting Diego’s own solutions before offering structured scripts and options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively prompted and affirmed change talk related to safer use and concrete safety planning. However, change talk was not consistently deepened with questions about importance, confidence, or personal meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully normalized and accepted sustain talk, especially Diego’s ambivalence about quitting. This response avoided argumentation and kept the session focused on the client’s chosen harm-reduction goal.
