# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance of Nathaniel’s choices. The warmth was strong but somewhat procedural, with less individualized emotional attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captured Nathaniel’s ambivalence between valuing his routine and worrying about blood pressure or jitters. The reflections were accurate but sometimes repetitive and did not fully deepen into underlying feelings or meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed mostly on target, with a practical plan to check blood pressure and keep the current routine. Direction was uneven, shifting repeatedly among options and logistics rather than progressing toward a clear MI focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nathaniel had some voice in the final plan, and the psychiatrist used collaborative language. The session remained largely clinician-guided, with the psychiatrist supplying most options, medical information, and next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly emphasized choice and accepted Nathaniel’s refusals to track intake, use tea, create a script, or schedule a reminder. Some prescriptive advice and detailed instructions slightly limited full client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were limited evocative questions, and much of the session involved providing information rather than eliciting Nathaniel’s own reasons, values, or confidence for change. The psychiatrist missed opportunities to explore what change would mean for his work, energy, or health.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Nathaniel’s preferences and accepted his boundaries, supporting a collaborative tone. The agenda and structure were still largely shaped by the clinician, reducing the sense of shared power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nathaniel offered only small, passive change statements, and the psychiatrist did not actively build on them. The session lacked evocative questions or amplification of his own motivations for possible change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and accepted Nathaniel’s desire to maintain his routine. However, they did not consistently use double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, or other MI-consistent techniques to hold the ambivalence open.
