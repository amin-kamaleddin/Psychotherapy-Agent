# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoided moralizing or arguing with Derrick’s minimization. Acceptance was strong, though affirmations were somewhat concise and occasionally formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used effective reflective listening to capture Derrick’s ambivalence, values, and social concerns. Empathy could be deeper by exploring underlying emotions such as nervousness, shame, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session evoked Derrick’s own reasons for change around his license, work, dignity, and relationships. More systematic scaling or consolidation of his strongest change talk would strengthen this criterion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated by building on Derrick’s ideas rather than imposing a preselected plan. The process could become more explicit through shared agenda-setting or co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported Derrick’s autonomy by using choice-focused questions and avoiding pressure to abstain. A stronger statement reinforcing Derrick’s ownership of the plan would elevate this to a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked Derrick’s own motivations without lecturing about alcohol risks or legal consequences. Some questions were slightly leading, and follow-up on Derrick’s confidence or remaining doubts could have been deeper.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveyed that Derrick was the expert on his own life and used his language throughout the session. The partnership was strong but could be made more explicit through direct acknowledgment of Derrick’s lived experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited and reinforced Derrick’s desire, need, ability, and commitment-like language. More strategic follow-up on his confidence level and final concerns would deepen the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist softened sustain talk by reframing Derrick’s concerns without contradiction or moral pressure. Using more active MI strategies, such as double-sided reflections, would improve this area.
