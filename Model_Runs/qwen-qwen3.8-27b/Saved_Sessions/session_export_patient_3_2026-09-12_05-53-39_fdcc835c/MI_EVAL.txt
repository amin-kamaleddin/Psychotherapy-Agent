# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys respect, warmth, and nonjudgment through validating statements. Acceptance is often tied to Aisha's effort and planning progress rather than consistently affirming her worth independent of performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates adequate understanding of Aisha's practical tensions and fear of failure. However, empathy remains somewhat surface-level, with frequent movement into planning before deeper exploration of shame, discouragement, and body-image emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and logical focus on resuming movement, body comfort, and confidence. The main limitation is that broader values and motivation are not consistently reconnected to the action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha's choices meaningfully shape the plan, including timing, activities, alarm labels, and recovery phrases. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist remains the primary generator of many ideas and structures.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites Aisha to make choices and accepts her adjustments without pressure. The many specific recommendations can subtly reduce the sense that Aisha is fully leading the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses confidence scaling effectively to elicit readiness and self-efficacy. Evocation is limited by the frequent provision of reasons, strategies, and solutions rather than consistently drawing out Aisha's own motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and supports shared decision-making. However, the interaction remains fairly expert-driven because much of the plan and implementation content is generated by the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha expresses meaningful change talk, including desire, intention, and increased confidence. The psychiatrist reinforces some of this language but does not consistently reflect, amplify, or connect Aisha's strongest change statements to deeper values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist addresses Aisha's self-doubt and all-or-nothing thinking with practical problem-solving and a useful backup plan. The approach is helpful but less consistently MI-consistent, with fewer double-sided reflections or techniques that redirect sustain talk toward change talk.
