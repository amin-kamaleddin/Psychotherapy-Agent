# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating tone that acknowledges ambivalence and the client’s small step. The acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic and does not fully demonstrate deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening, especially with double-sided reflections of conflict, anxiety, and self-criticism. The main limitation is that reflections become repetitive and do not fully deepen into the underlying shame, fear, or self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear strategic focus on a specific, manageable behavioral target and moves logically from a broad goal to a concrete implementation step. The direction is mostly procedural rather than consistently linked to the client’s broader values and desired future.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and the client’s ideas clearly shape the content of the session. However, the pacing and direction remain largely clinician-led, limiting full client-led collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by framing the work around the client’s choice and ownership of the step. Some questions still gently steer toward action and do not fully affirm that the client can reject the step without it being a failure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes the client’s own next steps and definitions of success, but the questions are narrow and repetitive. There is limited development of deeper values, past successes, confidence, or readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as the expert on her own experience and invites her perspective to shape the work. The session remains somewhat clinician-led, with the psychiatrist framing most of the questions and progression.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates several change-related statements, such as trying on purpose, showing up, and using a timer. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or consolidate these statements into stronger confidence, readiness, or values-based motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk and uses double-sided reflections to prevent dismissal. However, the session remains heavily focused on the sustain talk rather than effectively redirecting toward future outcomes or broader change.
