# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, patient, and nonjudgmental, without shaming or arguing with the patient. The clinical and task-focused tone slightly limited deeper warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were generally accurate, but often stayed at the surface level of behavior or fatigue. Deeper feelings such as uselessness and fear of falling were not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on creating a small, realistic movement plan with a clear behavioral target. Moments of medical screening and referral discussion slightly diluted the MI focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The final plan was co-created through the patient’s choices, but the psychiatrist supplied most of the options and instructions. The patient was not consistently invited to generate ideas or define what matters most.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choice, avoided coercion, and respected the patient’s decision to try the cue independently. Some detailed directive guidance prevented a fully client-led interaction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicited some initial concerns, but the counselor often shifted into advice and supplied reasons for movement. Deeper values and self-efficacy were not consistently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some partnership through inviting preferences and respecting the patient’s final plan. The frequent expert role and structured options made the interaction more directive than collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offered useful pro-change statements, but these were not consistently reflected or strengthened. The counselor relied more on advice and information than on eliciting the patient’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was accepted without arguing or reinforcing it. The counselor did not consistently use nuanced double-sided reflections to redirect ambivalence toward change.
