# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with effective normalization of Jordan's struggle (e.g., acknowledging the awkwardness of the court-mandated setting). Warmth is professional and appropriate but lacks the depth of personal connection or explicit unconditional acceptance that would warrant a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally accurate and sophisticated complex reflections that track Jordan's evolving emotional landscape from confusion to identity concerns to the specific thrill of the secret. The final reflection capturing both the weight and potential relief in Jordan's insight is particularly masterful.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical arc from surface-level description to deeper understanding, with minimal drift and purposeful pacing throughout. The session ends somewhat abruptly at the 'first step' question without fully developing that thread, though this may reflect session length.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative dialogue where Jordan's contributions directly shape the session's direction and depth. The psychiatrist follows Jordan's leads (e.g., the 'secret' insight) rather than steering toward a pre-planned agenda, modeling true MI partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently non-directive, never prescribing solutions, and explicitly framing change as Jordan's own decision. Could be strengthened with more explicit affirmations of Jordan's capacity to make changes when he expresses doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits Jordan's own insights through well-timed open questions (e.g., 'What's the specific ingredient?'), with all realizations emerging from Jordan himself. Could be stronger by drawing out values, long-term goals, or future self to contrast with current behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with inclusive, curious language and explicit disclaiming of the expert role. The psychiatrist still maintains a somewhat guiding structure and leading questions that steer toward specific realizations, limiting full co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective evocation of change talk (e.g., reflecting back 'that's you looking for a path') and the closing question about a first step. However, the session is predominantly exploratory with no strategic use of summaries, values-based questions, or confidence/ability explorations to amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and validated without reinforcement, which is appropriate, but not strategically redirected or reframed toward change. The psychiatrist holds space for ambivalence without helping resolve it, missing opportunities to gently challenge Jordan's assumptions about peer dependence or explore alternatives.
