# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and responds to resistance without shaming or arguing. The acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, relying on generic validations without consistently affirming strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Marcus’s feelings of being pushed, judged, and skeptical. The empathy is clear and alliance-preserving but remains largely surface-level rather than exploring deeper emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to alcohol use and safety, but it drifts toward managing conflict with Marcus’s mother. The target behavior is never clearly consolidated, making the session feel somewhat scattered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is used and Marcus is offered choices at several points. However, the psychiatrist frequently supplies fully formed scripts, shifting the interaction toward advice-giving rather than co-constructing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Marcus’s right to choose, repeatedly offering options and an exit from the session. Autonomy support is weakened slightly by the repeated provision of exact scripts and solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurs through open questions and invitations to choose a focus. However, the psychiatrist misses clear opportunities to evoke Marcus’s own motivations, confidence, and reasons for safer choices.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reduces power distance by inviting Marcus to direct the session and choose options. Partnership is uneven because the clinician remains the primary source of ideas and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are few and weak attempts to elicit Marcus’s own reasons, desires, abilities, or commitment to change. The psychiatrist does not reflect or build on Marcus’s statements about taking an Uber and knowing the rules.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not confront or reinforce Marcus’s sustain talk and avoids turning the session into a debate. However, there is limited strategic softening, such as using double-sided reflections to explore underlying ambivalence.
