# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, normalizing relocation stress while avoiding shaming around food, body, or activity. This is not a 5 because much of the acceptance is brief and procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s goals, safety concerns, privacy concerns, and need for low pressure. This is not a 5 because reflections are mostly behavioral and miss deeper emotional meaning and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on increasing physical activity and moves efficiently toward a concrete plan. This is not a 5 because it starts broadly and later becomes more logistical planning than strategic motivational direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and meaningfully adopts the client’s sticky-note decision rule. This is not a 5 because specific options and exercise details are introduced early, before the client fully generates her own plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice and ownership are repeatedly emphasized, and the psychiatrist respects flexibility and the client’s preference against scheduled monitoring. This is not a 5 because the volume of suggested plans can still subtly steer the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some action and commitment language, but relies more on problem-solving and suggestion than deep evocation. It does not sufficiently explore the values, ambivalence, or readiness behind the client’s goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally behaves as a partner by asking which options feel easiest and adopting the client’s own rule. This is not a 5 because early concrete suggestions reduce the degree of client-led co-creation from the outset.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers clear change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or explore it. More DARN-CAT-style elicitation would strengthen desire, ability, reasons, need, confidence, action, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles barriers effectively without arguing, reframing pressure, safety, and tracking concerns as client-controlled options. This is not a 5 because some sustain talk is accepted rather than strategically reflected and redirected to values.
