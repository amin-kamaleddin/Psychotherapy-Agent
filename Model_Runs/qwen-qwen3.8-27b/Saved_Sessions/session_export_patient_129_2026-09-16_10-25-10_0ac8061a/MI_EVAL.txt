# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrated unconditional positive regard, validation, and a nonjudgmental stance throughout the interaction. The score was held back from 5 due to a slightly procedural and expert-driven approach, particularly in the opening and when providing information about the client's situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflected the client's feelings of annoyance, embarrassment, and desire to end the session. The response could have been deeper by more fully exploring the underlying emotions, such as shame or fear of loss of autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained the session's focus on the presenting concern and kept the conversation moving forward. However, the direction was often sidetracked by the client's desire to leave and logistical concerns, rather than consistently focusing on a specific target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor fostered a collaborative environment by offering choices and inviting the client's input. The score was limited because the counselor still maintained much of the session's structure, preventing the client's ideas from fully driving the clinical focus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supported the client's autonomy by emphasizing their control over the session's length and content. This could have been improved by more explicitly reinforcing the client's capacity for autonomous decision-making regarding their future behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor used appropriate open questions to elicit the client's perspective on what mattered to them. These moves were not consistently followed up or deepened to draw out the client's own motivations or readiness for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor established a collaborative stance by inviting client input and emphasizing the client's role as the expert in their own life. The partnership was less effective because the counselor's structure and information delivery occasionally felt expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor made limited efforts to evoke language in favor of change, relying primarily on open questions. Most of the session focused on the client's resistance and desire to leave, with little strategic amplification or development of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoided arguing with or reinforcing the client's reasons for maintaining the status quo. However, there was a lack of consistent redirection or strategic use of double-sided reflections to help the client consider the downsides of their current behavior.
