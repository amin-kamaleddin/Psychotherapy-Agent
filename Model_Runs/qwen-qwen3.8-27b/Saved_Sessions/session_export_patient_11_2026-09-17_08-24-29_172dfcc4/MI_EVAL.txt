# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used warm, nonjudgmental language and showed respect for Amira’s experience. Acceptance could be stronger by more explicitly normalizing her past inconsistency and self-doubt as separate from her worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated nuanced understanding by accurately reflecting Amira’s ambivalence, relief, and self-protective meaning. These reflections were precise, therapeutic, and clearly supported Amira’s perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior and progressed from broad concern to a concrete, behavior-specific plan. There was no meaningful drift away from building a small, sustainable activity routine.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Amira’s own ideas shaped the session, including the cue, the action, the success marker, and the language for completion. The interaction functioned as joint problem-solving rather than expert-driven instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided commands and invited Amira to choose and shape the plan. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming her freedom to modify, pause, or abandon the plan without judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited Amira’s own motivations by asking about grounding, low-effort options, and signs of success. Evocation could be deeper by connecting the behavior more explicitly to Amira’s values, confidence, or readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveyed that Amira was the expert on her own life and invited her to lead the work. Partnership could be further strengthened by deferring more consistently to Amira’s own ideas before offering options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amira generated multiple statements of desire, ability, and intention to change, and the psychiatrist supported this with open questions. The work could be stronger by explicitly exploring confidence, commitment, and readiness more directly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reframed barriers and self-doubt in ways that reduced pressure without arguing or reinforcing the concerns. Sustain talk handling could be improved by more explicitly reflecting both sides of Amira’s conflicting parts.
