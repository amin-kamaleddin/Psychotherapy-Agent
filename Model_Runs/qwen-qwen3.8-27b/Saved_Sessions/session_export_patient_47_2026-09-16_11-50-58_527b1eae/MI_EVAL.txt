# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental overall, but the opening statement about drinking being “above recommended limits” introduces an evaluative frame before fully affirming Evelyn’s perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor reflects Evelyn’s ambivalence and discomfort well, yet often stays at a cognitive level rather than deeply exploring underlying values, fears, or identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear target—alcohol-related fall risk and a simple tracking plan—and maintains momentum, though it moves quickly into logistics without a visible closing summary or follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and adapts the plan to Evelyn’s preferences, but still provides much of the structure, templates, and content, so collaboration is strong but not fully client-generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently supported through choices, respectful language, and client-owned decisions without pressure or a pushed agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses some evocative techniques such as open questions and a confidence scale, but does not fully draw out Evelyn’s own values, reasons, or deeper motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction feels collaborative and co-created, though the counselor still leads the structure and offers many concrete options, limiting full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, but the counselor often misses opportunities to amplify, expand, or reflect it back in ways that deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing and accepts minimization well, but occasionally mirrors it and does not consistently use double-sided reflections to gently pivot toward change.
