# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Linda’s frustration and worry. The acceptance is strong but sometimes moves quickly from emotional validation into education or problem-solving, limiting deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflective listening and captures Linda’s ambivalence about needing relief while fearing long-term dependence. Empathy could go deeper when Linda expresses fear of losing independence, identity, or feeling stuck.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on inhaler use, asthma control, tracking, safety, and follow-up planning. Direction is clear and clinically appropriate, though it sometimes leans toward problem-solving and psychoeducation rather than MI-guided change work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds the plan around Linda’s ideas, such as the coffee routine, phone alarm, Notes tracking, and call script. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist remains the main source of medication information and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Permission-seeking, confidence checking, and invitations to choose steps support Linda’s autonomy. The counselor still provides a fairly specific plan and repeated clinical rules, so client ownership could be made more explicitly central.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and the confidence scale help elicit Linda’s goals and motivation. However, the psychiatrist often responds to concerns with information and reassurance instead of continuing to evoke Linda’s own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for permission, invites input, and incorporates Linda’s practical ideas. The session becomes expert-driven at times because of extensive medication education and plan direction, which reduces the sense of equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda expresses useful change talk, such as using the inhaler with coffee, setting an alarm, and wanting to walk the dog. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or consolidate this change talk before shifting to information or planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda’s sustain talk about dependence and long-term use is addressed with reassurance and a clear safety plan. From an MI perspective, some responses feel corrective or challenging rather than rolling with resistance through reflections.
