# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard, meeting every moment of self-doubt with specific, individualized validation (e.g., 'It's not greedy at all,' 'That's a real feeling'). Warmth is never formulaic and never minimizes David's experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond paraphrasing to capture the emotional and existential core of David's experience, exemplified by the masterful reframe 'the cigarette isn't the addiction, it's the permission to stop.' The psychiatrist accurately tracks each shift in David's understanding throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session follows a clear, progressive structure from motivation through history, barriers, insight, solution design, medication decision, and logistics—never drifting from the target behavior. Even when David reframes the problem, the psychiatrist incorporates the new frame while maintaining focus on cessation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a genuine joint construction, with the psychiatrist bringing clinical knowledge and David bringing life expertise. The power dynamic is clearly equal, with the final plan co-constructed and explicitly agreed to by David.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is active and explicit, with every decision point (medication type, timing, order of operations) left to David. The psychiatrist consistently uses language like 'no pressure,' 'you don't have to decide today,' and 'I want you to have a hand in choosing.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All change talk in the session comes from David himself, elicited through questions that draw out his intrinsic motivation, values, and self-efficacy. The psychiatrist never provides external reasons for change, instead creating conditions for David's own language to emerge.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions David as the expert on his own life, returning questions to him and treating his contributions as shaping the session's direction. David's ideas directly drive the trajectory from insight to plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are skillfully designed to deepen and expand David's own change language rather than asking why he should quit. David generates substantial change talk throughout, from 'I'm ready to try' to 'I'd rather start sooner than later.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through double-sided reflections and powerful reframes (e.g., shifting the goal from quitting to getting a break). Slight opportunity for more explicit normalization of the pull before reframing, particularly around the fear of social triggers.
