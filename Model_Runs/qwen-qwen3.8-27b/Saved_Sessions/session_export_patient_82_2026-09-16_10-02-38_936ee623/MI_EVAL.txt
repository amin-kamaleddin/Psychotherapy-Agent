# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance with validation of Lukas’s concerns. Acceptance was solid but somewhat procedural, without deeper affirmation of his courage, self-awareness, or personal worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor used adequate reflective listening to capture Lukas’s ambivalence about friendships, performance, and grades. Empathy was limited by frequent movement toward advice-giving rather than deeper exploration of underlying emotions such as fear, shame, or identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation remained focused on the target behavior and practical next steps, including handling peer pressure and protecting performance. Direction was somewhat repetitive and ended without a clear consolidation or closing that would strengthen the change plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited input and offered joint brainstorming, showing some collaborative effort. However, the session was largely counselor-driven, with the client mainly selecting from options rather than co-creating the agenda and solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-giving language was used, and Lukas was reminded that he did not owe an explanation. Autonomy support was reduced by repeated specific suggestions, scripts, and contingency plans that made the approach more prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor initially asked about Lukas’s perspective and used an importance rating, but evocation was limited overall. The session was frequently interrupted by advice and skill-building, reducing opportunities for Lukas to generate his own values, motivations, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrated some partnership by inviting Lukas’s perspective and asking which options felt most doable. The interaction often shifted into an expert problem-solving role, limiting a truly co-created, client-led process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some meaningful change talk emerged, including Lukas’s goals to protect his season, grades, and performance. The counselor did not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen this change talk before moving into options and scripts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk around belonging, not being the “weirdo,” and staying with friends was acknowledged without judgment. The counselor did not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategically pivot this sustain talk into change language.
