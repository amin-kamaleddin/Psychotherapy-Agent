# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance throughout the interaction. Acceptance is strong but slightly task-oriented rather than deeply emotionally attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the practical content of the client's perspective accurately. Empathy remains limited because underlying emotions such as stress, fatigue, or frustration are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains generally focused on health-related goals and a clear target behavior. Direction is weakened by redundancy, administrative drift, and repeated questions after the client had already answered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with the client in a practical, collaborative manner, allowing the client to shape key decisions. Collaboration is slightly weakened by moments of clinician-driven management and repeated scheduling questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is well supported through invitations to choose, confirm preferences, and commit voluntarily. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist also prescribes specific plan elements.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes the client's own motivations and solutions rather than providing reasons for change. Evocation is somewhat surface-level and does not deeply explore values, confidence, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a partnership stance by inviting the client to choose the focus, plan, follow-up format, and timing. Partnership is not fully advanced because the psychiatrist also takes on an expert role in prescribing structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions effectively elicit meaningful change talk and client-generated ideas for change. The score is limited by occasional clinician introduction of specific plan content rather than fully allowing change talk to emerge.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully by accepting constraints and incorporating them into the plan without arguing or reinforcing resistance. Softening is strong but more practical than deeply relational in processing ambivalence.
