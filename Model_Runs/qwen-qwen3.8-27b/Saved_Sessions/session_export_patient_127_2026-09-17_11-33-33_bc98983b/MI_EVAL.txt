# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, respect, and nonjudgment throughout, with compassionate reframing of self-criticism (e.g., 'lazy' to 'protection'). Could be elevated to a 5 with more explicit statements of unconditional positive regard and deeper normalization of the specific pain of recidivism risk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy with progressive depth, moving from surface ambivalence to the core identity wound ('a day where I wasn't sure who I was'). Complex reflections consistently add meaning rather than simply paraphrasing, and the final reframing of the old life as 'not knowing who you were when you woke up' is masterful.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical progression from initial ambivalence through specific barriers to core identity issues, with no topic drift or drift into advice-giving. Could benefit from a stronger sense of strategic direction, such as a collaborative summary or gentle check on readiness to explore next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with open, exploratory questions and 'I think' / 'I'm curious' language that positions observations as hypotheses. The conversation feels like a genuine dialogue between equals, though it could be elevated with more explicit co-construction of meaning (e.g., 'what am I missing?').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's autonomy and right to choose, never pressuring for commitment or implying a 'right' answer. The validation of 'not yet' as a real-time choice is excellent; could be strengthened with more explicit statements affirming the client's capacity to decide either way.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional evocation with well-timed, well-phrased questions that consistently draw out the client's own motivations, values, and insights. The client generates the majority of the meaningful content, which is the hallmark of skilled evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-hierarchical stance and follows the client's lead throughout without imposing an agenda. Could be strengthened by more explicit acknowledgments of the client's wisdom or by more clearly co-constructing the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and names change talk (e.g., 'something you'd actually fight for,' 'that specific quiet'). Could be elevated to a 5 with more strategic amplification—reflecting back the client's own change statements like 'I want that tea' with greater emphasis to reinforce motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully through reframing ('protection, not laziness') and open questions rather than direct challenge or argumentation. Could be more strategically consistent, particularly with double-sided reflections for statements like 'I'll just not bother' or 'I'll never fit this one.'
