# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids labeling or punishing the client. Warmth is strong but could be more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s ambivalence, fairness concerns, and fear of being singled out. Emotional reflections could be deeper to fully access shame, loyalty, and anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains generally connected to the referral concern by exploring safer monitoring and team-based check-ins. Some focus on logistics could be more explicitly tied back to reducing alcohol risk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor uses joint problem-solving and client-led language throughout. Jamal’s ideas directly shape the session, including the team huddle and no-written-record plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor respects choice, avoids pressure, and validates the need for safety and control. Explicit affirmation of Jamal’s autonomy over his own drinking could strengthen this further.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions draw out useful client ideas about process and safety. However, evocation focuses more on monitoring than on Jamal’s internal motivation, values, or confidence to change drinking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites collaboration and avoids an expert-driven stance. More explicit acknowledgment of Jamal’s expertise in his own life and team culture would enhance partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session evokes some client-generated ideas for safer team processes. Change talk remains limited to rules and monitoring, with missed opportunities to deepen motivation to reduce alcohol use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor reflects sustain talk neutrally without argument or reinforcement. Use of double-sided reflections and strategic redirection toward health, autonomy, or values would be stronger.
