# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and affirming throughout, with powerful reframing of self-critical statements into neutral or positive language (e.g., 'that's not a flaw, that's just how you are'). Never judges, labels, or expresses disapproval at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections that capture both emotional experience and underlying cognitive shifts. Could be slightly deeper in capturing the visceral/physical quality of fear (e.g., the need to 'sit down for a second').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear session arc from emotional processing through values and barriers to practical exploration, with every moment purposefully tied to the target behavior. Pacing is well-managed without rushing or lingering unnecessarily.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conversation structured as genuine joint exploration, with client's contributions visibly shaping direction. Adapted smoothly when client expressed discomfort about the IUD, pivoting without pushback.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding removal of decision pressure throughout, with explicit statements like 'you don't have to pick anything' and 'you don't have to resolve that tension right now.' Reframes hesitation as legitimate autonomy ('that's you honoring your own value of being the one who decides').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within the client using pure evocation questions (e.g., 'what do you think would need to be true...?'). Uses the client's own experience and language as motivational fuel rather than providing external reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative stance maintained with shared exploration language and consistent check-ins on client preferences. A few interpretive leaps could be framed as questions to invite the client's own meaning-making rather than asserting interpretation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully captures and reflects the client's own change-relevant language, which she then adopts (e.g., 'I like that framing'). Could more explicitly amplify and summarize change talk to further consolidate motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates sustain talk while gently redirecting without confrontation or argument. Could use more double-sided reflections that fully validate the sustain talk before opening space for change.
