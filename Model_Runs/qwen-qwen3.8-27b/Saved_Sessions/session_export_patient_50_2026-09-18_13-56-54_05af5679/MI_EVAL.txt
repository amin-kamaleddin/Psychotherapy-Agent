# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizing Hana's experience and gently pushing back on self-blame. There is no instance of judgment, labeling, or disapproval throughout the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates exceptionally accurate and nuanced reflective listening, including a complex reflection that names an emotion Hana hadn't explicitly stated (grief/loss) while using her own metaphor. The empathic attunement is deep, picking up on subtle emotional undercurrents like disappointment and the pain of the 'trustworthy voice' trap.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear, logical arc from identifying the barrier to generating a concrete (if tentative) plan for change, with each question building logically on the previous response. The only minor gap is that the session doesn't close with a concrete next step or commitment, though the open ending is defensible given the depth of work done.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely co-constructed, with Hana's insights and metaphors driving the conversation forward rather than a pre-planned agenda. The power balance is equal — the psychiatrist reflects, amplifies, and asks rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invites Hana to generate her own answers and never prescribes, recommends, or says 'you should.' The closing acknowledgment that Hana chose to notice the psychiatrist wasn't fixing implicitly reinforces her agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key insights — the two voices, the calm voice distinction, the 'data' reframe, and the 'house with no middle room' metaphor — are evoked from Hana herself, not provided by the psychiatrist. This is textbook evocation where motivation and insight come from within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions herself as a curious companion rather than an expert, consistently inviting Hana's perspective and never assuming the answer. When Hana says she doesn't know how to make room for it, the psychiatrist sits with the not-knowing rather than filling in the blank.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes and amplifies substantial change talk, including ability talk, new perspectives, and hope. The minor gap is that she doesn't explicitly ask Hana to rate her confidence or articulate what she values about making this change in her own words at the end.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled with remarkable skill through double-sided reflections that acknowledge the concern while opening a door to change, such as reframing the practice section as something that could be 'in between.' The psychiatrist never argues with or dismisses sustain talk, instead validating its power while helping Hana see the gap between herself and it.
