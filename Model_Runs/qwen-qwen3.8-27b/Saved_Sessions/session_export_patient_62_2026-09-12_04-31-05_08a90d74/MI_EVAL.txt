# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with low-pressure language such as no-pressure and I won't take it personally. However, the warmth is practical rather than deeply affirming of Jordan's worth beyond his coping.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Jordan's exhaustion, stuckness, and frustration, helping him feel heard. The empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore underlying fear, self-blame, isolation, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has direction through a small step and follow-up check-in, but it drifts from work and financial stress to fridge logistics. The connection to Jordan's broader goals of stability, relief, and feeling less stuck is not consistently maintained.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well on practical details by negotiating check-in wording and time. However, much of the session is structured around therapist-generated options rather than Jordan's own ideas, values, and preferred direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice through statements such as it is okay to wait, no-pressure, and the option to skip check-ins. Some repeated prompts toward action and the phrase tiny rule may still feel mildly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a readiness ruler and open-ended prompts to draw out motivation, but the evocation remains shallow and task-focused. The session moves too quickly to options without fully developing Jordan's values, hopes, or sense of agency.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows Jordan to decline action, showing a generally collaborative stance. However, the session is somewhat therapist-led, with menus of options steering the conversation toward a specific micro-task.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks readiness questions and lightly reinforces change-related motivation, but does not consistently reflect, amplify, or summarize Jordan's change talk. More exploration of confidence, ability, values, and reasons for change is needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Jordan's sustain talk, which reduces defensiveness. However, dominant statements such as I can't get a break are not actively reframed with double-sided reflections or discrepancy questions.
