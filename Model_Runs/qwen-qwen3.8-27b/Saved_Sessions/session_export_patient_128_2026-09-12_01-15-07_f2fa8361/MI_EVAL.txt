# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is nonjudgmental and uses affirming phrases, but acceptance is often brief and formulaic. It lacks deeper, individualized affirmation of Tyler's worth during moments of shame or fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects some initial distress and reduces the log to address the client's worry, but empathic responses remain surface-level. It does not consistently capture or validate the deeper ambivalence about friends, safety, and self-image.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays relevant to alcohol-related risk and safety, but the direction is mostly operational rather than MI-focused. It does not consistently guide the conversation toward a clear personal change goal or value-based discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is practical collaboration as the psychiatrist adjusts the plan and offers options, but the structure and content are largely counselor-generated. Tyler refines supplied plans rather than co-creating them from his own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and avoids pressuring Tyler to quit, supporting autonomy at a basic level. Frequent directive advice and detailed scripts, however, reduce the client's sense of being the primary decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions and menu-style choices, but the evocation is not sustained. It misses key opportunities to draw out Tyler's own motivations, values, and willingness to cut back.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shares some power by offering options and adapting to feedback, but the interaction remains expert-led. Tyler does not consistently define goals, values, or the plan in his own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes limited effort to cultivate change talk, noting a menu that includes weighing reasons but not following up strongly. Tyler's change-relevant statements are not reflected, amplified, or deepened with DARN or importance/confidence questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Tyler's sustain talk but does not effectively soften or redirect it. Instead, he largely accommodates it with practical harm-reduction advice rather than exploring the conflict between fun and safety.
