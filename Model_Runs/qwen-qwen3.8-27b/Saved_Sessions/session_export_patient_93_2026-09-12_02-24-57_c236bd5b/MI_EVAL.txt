# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and support for Margaret’s perspective. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural rather than deeply emotionally attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides adequate reflective listening but stays mostly at a cognitive level. Missed opportunities include reflecting concern about the fall, her son’s worry, and the social meaning of the wine.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on alcohol reduction, blood pressure, fall risk, and family concerns. It moves logically toward a practical, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by offering choices and building the plan around Margaret’s preferences. However, the clinician still provides much of the structure and expert guidance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Margaret’s choice and ownership are strongly reinforced throughout. She is encouraged to test the plan on her own terms without pressure toward abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions, but the session remains more advice- and planning-driven. Deeper exploration of Margaret’s values, motivations, and discrepancy is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Margaret’s input substantially shapes the session and the plan. The psychiatrist respects her lead, though he still maintains a fairly expert-led role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret produces useful change talk, and the psychiatrist helps evoke it. There is less amplification of her reasons for change or development of deeper intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without confrontation or pressure. The psychiatrist accepts Margaret’s desire to keep her wine while redirecting toward a realistic experiment.
