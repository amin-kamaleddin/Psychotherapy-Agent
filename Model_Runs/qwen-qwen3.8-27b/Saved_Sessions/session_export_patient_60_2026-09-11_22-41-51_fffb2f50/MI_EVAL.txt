# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was nonjudgmental and respectful, using low-pressure language such as “Totally fair.” However, acceptance remained fairly surface-level and procedural rather than deeply affirming of the person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were a few empathic reflections, but most of the session stayed factual and strategic. The counselor missed opportunities to explore Jason’s underlying frustration, embarrassment, defensiveness, and ambivalence about drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session had a clear practical focus on the conduct meeting and deadlines. However, the agenda shifted away from the target alcohol-related behavior and toward managing consequences.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered options and invited Jason to choose or modify wording, creating some collaboration. Still, the psychiatrist provided most of the structure and strategy, leaving Jason’s perspective underused.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor supported choice by offering multiple response options and avoiding overt pressure. Autonomy support was limited mainly to the conduct process rather than Jason’s ownership of decisions about drinking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor opened with some evocative questions but quickly moved into advice and scripting. There was little follow-up to draw out Jason’s own motivations, values, or reasons for changing drinking behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor showed partial partnership by asking what Jason wanted help with and adapting to his preference for simplicity. The interaction still felt more advisor-led than a true collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were only weak attempts to elicit change talk, with brief mention of drinking frequency and harm reduction. The counselor did not follow up by exploring Jason’s own reasons, benefits, or confidence for reducing or changing use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoided arguing with Jason’s sustain talk, but did not skillfully reframe or redirect it toward change. The conversation stayed focused on avoiding consequences rather than examining the drinking behavior.
