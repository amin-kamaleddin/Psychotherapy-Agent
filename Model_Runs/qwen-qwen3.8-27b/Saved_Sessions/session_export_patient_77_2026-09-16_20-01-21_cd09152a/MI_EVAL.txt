# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warm, nonjudgmental respect and accepts Liyana’s struggles without labeling or pressure. Affirmations such as “you’re trying so hard” and “you deserve a break” clearly demonstrate positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects both cognitive concerns and emotional undertones, including the impact of home silence and the fear of “doing it wrong.” Overall empathy is strong, attuned, and consistently responsive to Liyana’s experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and coherent focus on the connection between habits, confidence, and the scale. Direction is strong and change-oriented, though the target behavior is not fully locked in or summarized before the transcript ends.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the work as a joint process and invites Liyana to shape the session rather than directing it. The interaction avoids lecturing and instead follows Liyana’s concerns and emerging ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids imposing advice or making decisions for Liyana, instead asking what would feel possible, safe, or believable. Autonomy support is strong but could be strengthened with more explicit affirmations of Liyana’s freedom of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Liyana’s own motivation, values, and ideas through open, exploratory questions. Evocation is frequent and useful, but some of Liyana’s internal motivation could have been further developed or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys that Liyana is the expert on her own experience and invites her to guide the conversation. Partnership is strong, though some leading or limited-choice questions slightly reduce the sense of full co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Liyana generates meaningful change talk about stopping the scale as the only measure, tracking feelings, and taking small steps. The psychiatrist could further strengthen this skill by more consistently reflecting, amplifying, and building confidence from those change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Liyana’s fears and instead reflects ambivalence while moving toward safer next steps. Softening is effective, though the session could more consistently pivot sustain talk toward values, hope, or change.
