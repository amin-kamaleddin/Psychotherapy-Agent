### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids reacting to Derrick's minimization. It is not a 5 because the acceptance is more procedural than deeply individualized and lacks explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Derrick's ambivalence and notices a meaningful shift in his self-view. It is not a 5 because deeper emotional material such as shame, isolation, breakup, and fear after a high BAC is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on avoiding another DUI and managing peer pressure. It progresses clearly toward a concrete behavior plan without meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interview is highly collaborative, with Derrick's ideas driving the session. The psychiatrist builds on contributions such as rideshare cash, the car job, keys at home, and texting Marcus rather than imposing an agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Derrick's autonomy by avoiding pressure and helping him generate his own plan. It is not a 5 because it does not explicitly and repeatedly affirm his freedom to choose, adapt, or reject the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Derrick's own reasons, concerns, and solutions, including the "kind of cool" self-view. It is not a 5 because it could more explicitly evoke deeper values and confidence in the plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Derrick as a partner by following his perspective and ideas throughout the session. It is not a 5 because the partnership is mostly implied rather than explicitly stated through unmistakable power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits multiple concrete change statements from Derrick and reinforces them with affirmations. It is not a 5 because it does not fully develop confidence scaling, explicit desire/ability/need statements, or a consolidation commitment question.

#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist softens sustain talk by reframing "monk" and "preacher" concerns while preserving Derrick's need for belonging. It is not a 5 because some sustain talk about folding after alcohol remains underworked.
