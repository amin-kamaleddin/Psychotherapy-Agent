# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoided labeling the client. Acceptance was solid but somewhat task-oriented and formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected the client’s ambivalence and practical concerns accurately. Deeper feelings such as shame, guilt, or fear were not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol use and narrowed to a manageable target: delaying the first drink. The direction was sometimes mechanical and did not deeply connect to broader values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated with the client’s preferences and helped shape a realistic, low-effort plan. The client remained somewhat reactive to the psychiatrist’s structured suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly emphasized that change was the client’s choice and respected informal preferences. Some detailed planning and directive instructions reduced full autonomy emphasis.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Importance and confidence rulers helped elicit some motivation, but evocation was mixed with advice-giving. The psychiatrist did not consistently deepen the client’s own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and invited the client to choose strategies. The psychiatrist supplied much of the structure and content, limiting full partnership at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist encouraged change-related ideas and the client offered some change talk. These statements were not consistently amplified, reflected, or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk without arguing or invalidating. More strategic softening or discrepancy-building was needed to move sustain talk toward change.
