# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, frequently validating Elena’s caregiving and stress. Acceptance is slightly limited by language that still implies her behavior needs correction or more management.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Elena’s anger, exhaustion, protectiveness, and ambivalence about quitting. They are strong but become somewhat repetitive and do not always access deeper emotions such as shame, guilt, or fear of failing as a mother.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on smoking, secondhand smoke, and Elena’s ambivalence about reducing risk. Direction is somewhat limited by an overemphasis on practical risk-reduction tasks rather than her broader values and readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative moves, including inviting Elena’s input and asking what she wants to focus on. However, the psychiatrist often presents a menu of clinical options, making the interaction feel more advisor-driven than genuinely co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms choice, says quitting is not required right now, and apologizes for making Elena feel judged. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated offers of small behavioral steps that may still feel like added tasks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good evocative questions are used, such as asking what would make quitting or reducing exposure feel doable. The psychiatrist often shifts away from evoking Elena’s own motivations and instead provides external options and strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adjusts when Elena becomes resistant and invites her to direct the conversation later in the session. Partnership is uneven because much of the middle is driven by the psychiatrist’s lists of options and clinical suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena’s change-relevant statements are acknowledged, but they are not consistently explored or expanded. The psychiatrist often moves to provider-generated solutions instead of helping Elena develop her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and respectfully acknowledges Elena’s reasons for smoking, such as needing a break. However, sustain talk is not consistently redirected toward change and can inadvertently reinforce the current behavior as acceptable.
