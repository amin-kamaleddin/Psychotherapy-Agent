### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful, and validating stance throughout. Warmth is solid but could deepen with more explicit appreciation for Kenji's willingness to examine a sensitive issue.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate, consistent, and capture both cognitive and emotional experience. The psychiatrist shows strong understanding of Kenji's ambivalence and self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on evening alcohol use, especially the first drink after work. The work progresses clearly from awareness to a small, concrete behavioral experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently builds on Kenji's contributions rather than imposing a plan. Language such as 'we could keep it simpler' reinforces shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist emphasizes choice, experimentation, and lack of pressure. However, some concrete, therapist-initiated suggestions slightly narrow Kenji's decision space.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions help draw out client-generated ideas, but they remain mostly logistical rather than motivational. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to evoke deeper reasons, confidence, or commitment for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kenji is positioned as the expert on his own evenings, and his contributions clearly shape the conversation. The partnership is strong, though some therapist-provided menus feel somewhat advisory.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk appears and is gently reinforced, but the session moves quickly into problem-solving. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen motivation by amplifying importance, confidence, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or judgment, and the psychiatrist redirects toward a smaller testable idea. It could be stronger if more explicitly transformed into change talk.
