# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental. Acceptance is somewhat generic and formulaic rather than deeply individualized, which limits it to a 4.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track the patient’s barriers and ambivalence. However, they remain mostly cognitive and do not deeply explore emotional undercurrents such as fear, shame, or self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of increased community engagement. It occasionally becomes overly logistical, making the direction feel more like problem-solving than MI-guided change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and co-plans small steps using inclusive language. Collaboration is limited by frequent generation of specific scripts, checklists, and logistical solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices and respects hesitation. Stronger autonomy support would require more explicit reinforcement of the patient’s ownership and self-efficacy, especially after she expresses doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some early evocation of what increased connection means to the patient. However, the session shifts into counselor-generated problem-solving rather than consistently eliciting the patient’s own motivations and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and frequently invites the patient’s input. Partnership becomes uneven when the psychiatrist repeatedly generates options and scripts rather than fully restoring the patient’s agency.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some attempts to cultivate change talk, especially early in the session. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen, amplify, or strategically reflect the patient’s reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized without argument. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to pivot the patient’s language toward change.
