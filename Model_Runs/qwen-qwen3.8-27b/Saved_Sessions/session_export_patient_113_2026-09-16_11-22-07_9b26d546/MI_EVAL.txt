# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, with clear language supporting Rowan’s choice. However, the stance is not fully individualized and remains somewhat compliance-focused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Rowan’s frustration, burden, and sense of a catch-22. Empathy is useful but stays more cognitive and task-focused, missing deeper underlying emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a clear focus on device use, but the direction drifts substantially toward stopping clinic calls and drafting messages. This reduces consistent momentum toward the target health-management behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and allows Rowan to choose the final message and reply style. Collaboration is present but more task-oriented, with much of the content generated by the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is repeatedly affirmed and coercive language is avoided. Autonomy support is strong, though repeated tiny-step suggestions still create a subtle sense of pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions explore barriers and worries, but evocation remains superficial. The session does not deeply elicit Rowan’s values, confidence, or reasons for using the device.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Rowan’s preferences and collaborates on the message plan. The interaction can still feel somewhat managed by the clinician rather than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A small amount of change talk is elicited, but it is not consistently amplified or connected to device use. The conversation shifts toward administrative problem-solving rather than building motivation for the target behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is tolerated without argument, but not effectively softened. The clinician does not strategically explore ambivalence or examine what might make device use more acceptable.
