# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming throughout, with explicit normalization of the gap between values and daily behavior. Could extend to explicit statements of unconditional positive regard independent of the client's performance or progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally accurate and emotionally attuned reflections that capture subtle phrases, sensory details, and emotional undercurrents the client hadn't fully named. Consistently goes beyond surface-level paraphrasing to convey felt meaning and emotional texture.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains exceptional focus and logical momentum toward the target concern (evening presence and self-compassion). Each exchange builds on the last with appropriate pacing, moving from exploration to insight to a sense of possibility without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative quality with the client's language and images genuinely shaping the therapeutic narrative. Occasionally positions the psychiatrist as the one identifying significance (e.g., 'You just did something tender there') rather than fully co-constructing meaning with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects the client's self-direction, never prescribes or pressures, and honors her 'I don't want to say it's all fixed' without pushing toward a more positive framing. Could be strengthened by more explicit affirmations of the client's freedom to choose and authority over her own process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All major insights and reframes emerge from the client herself, facilitated by well-designed exploratory questions. The psychiatrist never offers reasons for change or external motivation—every meaningful shift is client-generated through curiosity and reflection.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-directive stance with open-ended questions that follow the client's lead. Could be elevated by more explicit power-sharing language that names the client's expertise and authority over her own process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Produces substantial change talk through well-designed questions, with the client articulating meaningful reframes (practice vs. performance, permission, family happiness includes me). Occasionally misses opportunities to explicitly amplify or deepen powerful change statements when they appear.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk present due to the client's low ambivalence; the primary instance (phone as 'off-ramp') was handled skillfully by validating the need while reframing toward self-understanding. A score of 5 would require more sustained sustain talk to fully demonstrate consistent handling.
