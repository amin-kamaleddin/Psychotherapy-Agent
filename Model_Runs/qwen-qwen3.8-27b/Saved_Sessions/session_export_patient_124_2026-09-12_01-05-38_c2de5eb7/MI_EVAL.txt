# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepts the patient’s refusals without becoming frustrated. Acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and could more fully validate the patient’s specific stressors.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Offers some surface-level reflections, such as normalizing wanting to chill and feeling awkward. Deeper exploration of the patient’s ambivalence, peer pressure, anxiety, and financial strain is limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stays clearly focused on reducing marijuana use and related practical concerns such as frequency, cost, and social use. The direction becomes somewhat behavior-management-focused rather than consistently motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates the patient’s preferred simple strategies, such as a cash limit and casual topic switches. The interaction remains unevenly collaborative, with many clinician-generated options and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supports autonomy by asking for preferences and respecting the patient’s choices not to text, rehearse, or use scripts. It becomes more directive when offering structured plans and specific action steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited evocation of the patient’s own values, motivations, or reasons for change. Change talk emerges more from the psychiatrist’s suggestions than from active evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient’s choices influence parts of the session, and the psychiatrist avoids being pushy. However, the interaction is largely therapist-led with frequent plans, lists, and scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient generates some change-related ideas, such as limiting use to Friday and Saturday and tracking cash. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or follow up on the patient’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with the patient’s reasons for continuing to use marijuana and normalizes her concerns. It does not strategically reflect the ambivalence behind sustain talk or integrate it with reasons for change.
