# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental stance and accepts Tyler’s reluctance without punishment. It is somewhat procedural and could better normalize shame or fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture frustration, privacy concerns, and the double-sided ambivalence about his mother and new peers. It would benefit from deeper validation of vulnerable feelings beneath defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear short-term focus on reducing parental conflict and establishing a privacy boundary. It does not connect this direction to the broader referral target of substance use or peer influence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Tyler’s ideas, such as knocking, texting first, and walking out if his mother escalates. Some therapist-initiated options prevent fully client-led agenda setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** No pressure is placed on Tyler to confess, apologize, stop using, or abandon friends. It could more explicitly affirm his right to choose whether or not to re-engage with his mother.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The questions draw out practical boundary-setting steps and self-protection strategies. It does not consistently evoke deeper values, self-efficacy, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Tyler as the expert on his own experience and follows his contributions. It remains partly structure-driven by suggesting options and frames.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tyler offers some change talk about privacy, normalcy, and wanting his life to be his own. The psychiatrist does not deeply amplify or connect this talk to the core change goal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Tyler’s sustain talk and redirects toward practical steps. It does not use advanced moves such as double-sided reflections to reduce the momentum of resistance.
