# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and validated Jamal’s sense of unfairness. Warmth was present but somewhat procedural and did not fully normalize the broader difficulty of his situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were frequent and generally accurate, especially around practical concerns and consequences. They often stayed surface-level and did not fully explore deeper emotions like shame, fear, or autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stayed focused on the target behavior and referral reasons. Some drift toward logistics and ending slightly weakened strategic direction toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist framed the work as joint and practical, inviting a short plan. Collaboration was uneven at times because planning continued even after Jamal repeatedly signaled he wanted to end.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist strongly emphasized Jamal’s control, responsibility, and expertise about his own life. Some prompts about signing off or making “I’m done” feel safe could come across as subtly gatekeeping.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions elicited some client-led reasons for change, such as avoiding another charge. Evocation remained largely external and compliance-focused rather than drawing out deeper values, fears, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist clearly conveyed that Jamal was the expert on his own life and usage patterns. Some consecutive questions and plan-focused prompts slightly limited a fully shared-agenda feel.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Therapist elicited some change talk about keeping his record clean and avoiding another charge. Change talk was not consistently amplified, summarized, or developed into stronger internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Therapist avoided arguing with Jamal’s minimization and did not directly confront sustain talk. The therapist often accepted minimization without consistently pivoting it into discrepancy or exploring consequences.
