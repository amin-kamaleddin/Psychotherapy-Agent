# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirmed the client’s effort and concerns. Acceptance could become stronger by more explicitly affirming the client’s worth, parenting role, and whole-person experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and often captured complex ambivalence about drinking, guilt, and stress. Empathy could be deepened by more nuanced reflections of underlying feelings such as exhaustion, shame, or fear of losing control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on alcohol use and stress coping, moving logically from exploration to planning and relapse prevention. The psychiatrist guided the conversation purposefully while still respecting the client’s autonomy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated well by inviting the client to choose coping steps, backups, and supports. Collaboration could be strengthened by eliciting more client-generated ideas before offering structured options or scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported choice, permission, and the client’s own decision-making, including normalizing a possible slip. Some of the many counselor-generated resources and scripts slightly shifted the balance toward a more directive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively drew out the client’s own reasons, confidence, and values related to reducing drinking. Evocation could be strengthened by more consistently deepening the client’s change talk and connecting it to future goals before moving into planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed partnership by asking for preferences and building the plan around the client’s realistic options. Partnership could be more balanced by asking the client to generate more solutions before the psychiatrist provides extensive resources.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used useful questions and confidence ratings to elicit desire, ability, and intention for change. Change talk could be cultivated more deeply by explicitly reflecting and amplifying the client’s own DARN-C statements and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk well by normalizing the function of drinking and reducing all-or-nothing thinking around a possible second drink. Softening could be more MI-specific by using double-sided reflections to hold both sides of the client’s ambivalence.
