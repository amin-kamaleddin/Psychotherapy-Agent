# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, normalizes frustration, and avoids judgment. Warmth remains somewhat professional and tied to the client's plan rather than deeper unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective statements accurately capture the client's priorities around efficiency, focus, and practical constraints. Emotional depth is limited, especially around breathlessness, age-related worry, and the tension between health and work performance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear, concrete target behavior and narrows the issue effectively. It could be stronger by more consistently linking the snack/focus plan back to the broader health concerns and by securing a specific follow-up format.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites client input and allows Andrei's preferences to shape the final plan. However, the counselor still supplies much of the solution content, making the collaboration slightly less client-led than ideal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses choice language, respects the client's limits, and avoids pressure. Autonomy support would be stronger if it more explicitly reinforced the client's freedom to modify, pause, or stop the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits useful practical details and some client-generated problem solving. Evocation remains mainly logistical rather than deeply motivational, with limited exploration of the client's own values, reasons, or internal motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes meaningfully to the plan and selects key components. The counselor still retains a problem-solver role by offering options and proposing the main structure rather than first fully elicitating the client's own solution.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks questions that generate change-related detail, such as priority, confidence, and success markers. Change talk is not consistently amplified, explored, or deepened into stronger motivational language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing and acknowledges the client's concerns without reinforcing them as final. However, the counselor does not consistently pivot sustain talk toward change or use double-sided reflections to hold both constraints and the desire for easier breathing.
