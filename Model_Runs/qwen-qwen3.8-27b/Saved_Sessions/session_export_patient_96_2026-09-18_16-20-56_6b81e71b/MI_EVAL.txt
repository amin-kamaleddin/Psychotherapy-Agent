# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates respect and non-judgment through phrases like 'I respect that it was your choice' and 'I want to honor that.' However, acceptance remains somewhat one-dimensional, primarily validating the status quo rather than connecting with the whole person or acknowledging the complexity of her situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes adequate efforts at reflective listening with some accurate perspective-taking, such as recognizing the frustration of having one's judgment questioned. However, reflections tend to be surface-level paraphrases rather than complex reflections that delve into underlying feelings, values, or deeper meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks clear therapeutic direction, moving from validation to more validation without strategic progression toward any specific goal or outcome. Even for a pre-contemplation client, there should be a sense of gentle direction toward exploration or identifying useful information.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some collaborative language and maintains a non-authoritarian tone, but the collaboration is largely superficial in practice. The psychiatrist does not genuinely seek Marisol's input on structuring the conversation or co-create any plan or direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirms Marisol's autonomy with explicit statements like 'You're the one who knows your body and your choices best' and 'No expectations, no pressure.' However, autonomy support is somewhat formulaic and reinforces autonomy only in the direction of maintaining the status quo rather than affirming capacity to explore any option.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Makes minimal effort to elicit motivation from within Marisol, with no evocative questions that draw out her own values, goals, or concerns. The session functions more as a validation exercise than an evocation process, failing to operationalize the core MI principle that motivation resides within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a non-expert stance with language like 'I'm curious' and a general non-directive tone. However, the psychiatrist does not genuinely invite Marisol to co-construct the conversation's direction, and the session agenda is entirely set by the psychiatrist rather than negotiated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The most significant weakness of the session, with virtually no effort to evoke, elicit, or cultivate any change talk from Marisol. When she states 'I don't see a reason to change my mind,' the psychiatrist simply validates rather than gently exploring whether any part of her is even slightly open to consideration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rather than softening or redirecting sustain talk, the psychiatrist actively amplifies it with full validation of her decided position. Final exchanges like 'You made your choice and you're good with it — I respect that completely' effectively close the door on any further exploration.
