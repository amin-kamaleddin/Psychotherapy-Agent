# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance despite Luis's resistance and avoids blaming him for nonadherence. However, the language is sometimes formulaic and slightly framed in the clinician's terms, preventing a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician reflects several surface-level themes accurately, such as frustration, not wanting to be fixed, and feeling overwhelmed. The responses miss deeper emotional undercurrents, such as loss of control, embarrassment, and exhaustion from being managed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays somewhat tied to asthma management and follow-up, but it drifts toward satisfying Oscar and creating a script. A stronger MI-consistent summary would have refocused on a small, meaningful step while honoring autonomy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences, offers options, and allows Luis to set the pace of the session. The approach is generally collaborative but remains somewhat consultant-like because the clinician supplies structured options and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms Luis's choice and avoids pressuring him to commit or argue. Some moments remain problem-solving oriented, especially when providing scripts and a safety note, so autonomy support is strong but not maximal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions to draw out Luis's ideas, such as wanting less hassle and considering phone refills. The evocation is limited because the clinician does not consistently explore Luis's values, confidence, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a generally collaborative, non-authoritarian stance and invites Luis's input throughout. It is not a 5 because the clinician still positions himself as the one offering solutions and scripts rather than fully eliciting Luis's own leadership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some small change talk, including phone refills and a desire to reduce burden. However, the change talk is not strategically amplified, explored, or connected to Luis's values and motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct arguing but does not effectively use double-sided reflections or roll with resistance to soften dismissive statements. Helping Luis craft the script "He's fine, leave him alone" reinforces sustain talk and may reduce follow-up.
