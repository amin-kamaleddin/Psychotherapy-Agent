# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, but warmth and individualized acceptance are inconsistent, often shifting into medical education and risk management.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Nathaniel's ambivalence and conflicting views on coffee, but they remain mostly content-level and miss deeper emotional or meaning-based dimensions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on coffee and blood pressure, but becomes procedural and risk-assessment-driven rather than steering toward broader personal change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nathaniel is invited to participate and does contribute to the final trigger, yet the psychiatrist supplies most of the information, structure, and decision points.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice, asks permission, and respects Nathaniel's right to decide, though prescriptive medical guidance can subtly direct the course.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicit some preferences, but the counselor relies heavily on facts and recommendations rather than drawing out deeper discrepancy and client motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language and invitations are present, but the frequent educator role reduces the sense of a true equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-oriented statements emerge, especially around checking blood pressure, but they are not consistently amplified or connected to deeper reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids confrontation and offers lower-effort alternatives, but occasionally validates the status quo in ways that may reinforce sustain talk.
