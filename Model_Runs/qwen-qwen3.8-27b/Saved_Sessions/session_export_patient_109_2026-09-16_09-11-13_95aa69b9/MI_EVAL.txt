# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance with supportive language. It could be strengthened by more explicitly normalizing ambivalence, fatigue, and repeated non-follow-through.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures practical and emotional barriers such as exhaustion and risk of stalling in the car. The empathy is less deep when exploring underlying discouragement, fear of failure, or the meaning of the struggle.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior and progressively moves from broad barriers to a concrete, testable plan. There is minimal drift from the referral concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates a realistic plan and incorporates the patient’s preferences, such as the duffel, shoes, and two-minute goal. The balance is less equal because the counselor frequently supplies multiple options and structures the next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice, respects boundaries, and reduces pressure with statements such as “no guilt.” Autonomy is somewhat undermined by repeated attempts to raise confidence and continue offering additional supports after the patient declines them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and confidence-related motivation, but often provides possible answers before fully evoking the patient’s own values, reasons, or sources of motivation. The conversation becomes more troubleshooting than motivational evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and asks for the patient’s preference, supporting a collaborative stance. However, the counselor often functions as an options manager, supplying many specific solutions rather than strongly emphasizing the patient as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some elicitation of action-oriented change talk, including trying a short treadmill session and preparing gear. The psychiatrist shifts too quickly into solution generation and does not consistently explore desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the patient and reduces pressure, but the session frequently returns to barriers. It could be stronger with double-sided reflections and strategic reframing that link concerns to the patient’s values.
