# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Marek’s perspective without moralizing or arguing. Acceptance is strong, though it could become a 5 by more explicitly affirming Marek’s worth beyond his drinking behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate, nuanced reflections that track Marek’s meaning and normalize his confusion about being flagged. Empathy could deepen by exploring the emotional and identity stakes beneath his ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and narrows attention from heavy drinking to specific weekend patterns and a possible small adjustment. Direction could improve by consolidating the conversation into a clearer, client-owned next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by using open questions and building on Marek’s preferences rather than lecturing or controlling. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still frequently sets the next question and supplies options before Marek fully generates his own.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, with the psychiatrist consistently emphasizing choice, avoiding pressure, and framing any change as optional and self-directed. The response to Marek’s refusal to make a rule is especially strong.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions that draw out Marek’s own reasoning and possible signals for noticing change. Evocation could be stronger by using more silence, fewer rapid prompts, and deeper expansion of Marek’s own values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally places expertise with Marek and invites him to generate his own answers, which shapes the session meaningfully. Partnership could become a 5 by allowing more shared agenda-setting and less counselor-initiated sequencing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marek offers useful change-oriented statements, and the psychiatrist elicits some small, self-directed possibilities. However, change talk is not consistently amplified, expanded, or systematically developed using DARN-C language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by reflecting it without arguing or reinforcing the status quo. It could be stronger by using double-sided reflections to broaden the “sick or not sick” frame toward earlier, less dramatic signals.
