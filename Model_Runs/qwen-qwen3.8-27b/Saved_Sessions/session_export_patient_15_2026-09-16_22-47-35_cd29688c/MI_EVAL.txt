# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental throughout. However, acceptance sometimes slips into premature approval of the client’s desire to avoid further change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes reasonable surface-level reflections about the client’s need for reassurance and simplicity. The empathy remains shallow because it does not deeply explore the client’s fear, control, burden, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session keeps focus on obtaining a reassuring blood pressure result rather than addressing the stated targets. Alcohol use and other health behaviors are not meaningfully addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and allows the client to shape a low-effort follow-up plan. However, collaboration is narrow and passive, focusing mainly on reassurance logistics rather than shared exploration of health change.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms the client’s right to choose and avoids pressure. This is strong autonomy support, though it may inadvertently reinforce avoidance by endorsing the status quo too strongly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few open questions, but they are not used effectively to draw out the client’s internal motivation. The session does not elicit the client’s reasons, importance, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids a directive stance, but does not clearly position the client as the expert on his own values and priorities. The interaction feels more like administrative planning than true MI partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist occasionally asks what would be helpful or important, but does not skillfully expand on the client’s own statements. The client’s mention of not smoking is not amplified as a source of strength or change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly validates and reinforces the client’s preference to maintain the status quo. There is little use of double-sided reflection, values work, or gentle redirection toward change.
