# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental stance with statements such as “I hear you” and “That makes a lot of sense.” Warmth is somewhat formulaic and often shifts quickly to practical problem-solving rather than deepening support at moments of self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers adequate reflections of effort and ambivalence, but empathy is inconsistent and often surface-level. Important feelings of monitoring, loneliness, fear, and the gap between survival mode and family dreams are not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on a specific behavior, particularly reducing contact with the old crew. The direction becomes overly narrow on micro-level logistics, such as auto-replies and timers, while losing regular connection to broader goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites choices and incorporates some of Mateo’s preferences, creating a collaborative surface. However, the clinician supplies most of the content, so Mateo is often selecting from a menu rather than co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses choice language and acknowledges Mateo’s freedom to decide, including low-pressure framing. Frequent unsolicited advice and structured suggestions undercut autonomy by making the interaction more directive than partner-led.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician begins with useful evocative questions, but the session quickly shifts to scripts, steps, and planning. Mateo’s values, motivations, and reasons for change are not deeply explored or strengthened to build intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo’s preferences shape parts of the session, and the clinician asks for input on small choices. The partnership is uneven because the clinician frequently assumes an expert-planning role before exploring Mateo’s own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mateo offers clear change talk, such as wanting a better life, family, and a stable home, but the clinician does not consistently amplify or expand it. Missed opportunities include exploring the meaning of family goals, confidence, and what past successes reveal about Mateo’s ability to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with sustain talk and sometimes normalizes difficulty, but rarely uses MI strategies to shift focus away from the status quo. Responses often accept the sustain talk and then add practical suggestions rather than using double-sided reflections or discrepancy.
