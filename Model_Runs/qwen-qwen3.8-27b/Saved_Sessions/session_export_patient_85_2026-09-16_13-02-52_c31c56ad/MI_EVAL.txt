# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and avoids judgment, but much of the praise is tied to Marco choosing and following the plan. Acceptance feels more performance-based than unconditional, with limited validation of frustration, past regain, or fear of slipping.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Marco’s goals, triggers, and practical concerns, but the empathy is mainly cognitive and task-oriented. Deeper emotional validation of frustration, disappointment, and mixed feelings about orange juice would strengthen the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on reducing fruit juice intake, especially afternoon dilution, with minimal topic drift. The repeated 7-day check-in and concrete behavior anchors provide clear, action-oriented direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is largely co-constructed, and the psychiatrist builds on Marco’s ideas such as involving his wife, choosing nuts/yogurt, and using a phone alarm. However, the therapist contributes many strategies and scripts, which makes the interaction less fully balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, offers optional choices, and allows Marco to select his own plan, backup, and reminder. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by the high number of therapist-generated suggestions and strong steering toward specific strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions about importance, confidence, barriers, and backup plans help Marco articulate his own motivation and practical ideas. Evocation would be stronger if the therapist elicited more client-generated solutions before offering therapist ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco is allowed to make several key choices, showing a degree of partnership. However, the psychiatrist frequently acts as the primary strategist, offering multiple rapid ideas, which can make the interaction feel coach-like rather than equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively uses importance and confidence scaling to evoke stronger change talk, such as “I know I can do this” and increased confidence scores. More deliberate expansion of values, future self, and ability statements would push this toward a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue against Marco’s ambivalence, but he tends to acknowledge sustain talk briefly and redirect to planning. More skillful softening would use double-sided reflections and normalize cravings or slips to hold ambivalence rather than pivot quickly to problem-solving.
