# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a nonjudgmental, respectful tone that reduces shame. Acceptance is generally strong, though it often remains procedural rather than fully affirming Tyler as a person beyond his choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Tyler's ambivalence and social concerns. Empathy could deepen by more fully addressing the emotional weight behind his fear, identity concerns, and social rejection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on safer alcohol use and preventing another conduct-related incident. The conversation consistently moves toward a concrete harm-reduction plan without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tyler participates by selecting options and adjusting scripts, but the psychiatrist frequently supplies structured choices and tactics. The collaboration is present but uneven, with the psychiatrist often functioning more as the strategist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects Tyler's harm-reduction focus. Autonomy support is weakened by frequent prescriptive tactics and scripts that may reduce Tyler's sense of ownership over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some initial change talk and preferences from Tyler. However, the session often relies on clinician-generated options rather than consistently drawing out Tyler's own motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes Tyler in decisions and offers options, but often leads with detailed lists and scripts. More consistent follow-the-client questioning would strengthen the collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tyler offers several change statements about protecting his degree and avoiding another incident. The psychiatrist affirms these but does not consistently deepen, amplify, or strategically expand Tyler's change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Tyler's sustain talk and normalizes his desire to fit in. More effective softening would involve reflecting both sides and redirecting those concerns toward Tyler's values and change goals.
