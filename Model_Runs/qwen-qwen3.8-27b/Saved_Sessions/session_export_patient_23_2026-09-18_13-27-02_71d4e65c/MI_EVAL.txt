# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment throughout, with genuine validation when the patient expresses self-doubt. Falls just short of a 5 because some warmth moments feel slightly performative or structured rather than fully organic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally accurate and nuanced perspective-taking, capturing the emotional core of the patient's experience (e.g., 'permission to stop being on guard'). Goes beyond surface-level reflection to identify underlying protective strategies and relational dynamics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical progression from understanding the function of drinking to generating a concrete alternative action plan. Deducted because the session has not yet directly addressed the core target behavior of actual drinking reduction, though groundwork is clearly laid.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative behavior with inclusive language and building on patient-generated ideas rather than imposing external solutions. The psychiatrist still retains primary control of question flow and session structure, preventing a full 5.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces patient ownership and right to choose, never saying 'you should' or imposing directives. Patient-generated solutions (two-week trial, shoes by the door) are reflected back with validation, preserving her sense of agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits the patient's own motivations and solutions rather than providing information or reasons for change. The psychiatrist's role is almost entirely to create conditions for the patient to generate her own change talk and problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance with open-ended questions that invite the patient to generate her own solutions. Could benefit from more explicit power-sharing language or co-construction of goals, as the psychiatrist is slightly more guiding than a true equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and skillfully evokes change talk through strategic questions (ideal evening, Tuesday-you, landing on the plan) and by reflecting back the patient's own change language with emphasis. Effectively moves the patient from ambivalence to a concrete, self-owned commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating doubts without arguing or dismissing (e.g., 'You're right that a walk doesn't erase four hours of work'). Skillfully reframes absolute statements into more nuanced expressions without creating a power struggle.
