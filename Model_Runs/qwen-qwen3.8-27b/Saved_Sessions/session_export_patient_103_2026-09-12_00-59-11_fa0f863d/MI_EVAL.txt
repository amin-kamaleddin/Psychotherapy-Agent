# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and validates Nabil’s anger and frustration. However, the interaction often shifts quickly into task-focused problem-solving and directives, which limits deeper warmth and emotional connection.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects some of Nabil’s practical frustrations and perspective-taking. The empathy remains surface-level and does not fully explore underlying fear, exhaustion, powerlessness, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the presenting concern of appointment delays and job impact. However, the direction narrows into administrative action and email drafting rather than broader therapeutic or change-focused goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts to Nabil’s preferences and involves him in choosing tone, focus, and wording. The collaboration remains uneven because the therapist provides most of the structure and solutions, making it feel more like task assignment than equal co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers several choices regarding focus, scripts, and follow-up. Autonomy support is weakened by many prescriptive instructions and a directive tone that may reduce Nabil’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist gives many concrete solutions but offers limited exploration of Nabil’s values, motivations, confidence, or own reasons for change. The session moves quickly from questions into advice, scripts, and problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Nabil’s input and preferences at several points. The session remains largely expert-driven, with the therapist frequently steering the conversation toward concrete tasks and managing the situation for the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nabil offers some change-relevant statements, such as wanting to stop the cycle and agreeing to send the email. The psychiatrist frequently bypasses or does not expand these statements, moving instead into advice and scripting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not challenge Nabil aggressively but also does not effectively soften sustain talk about external control and the costs of acting. Responses remain focused on practical system-oriented plans rather than reflecting ambivalence or redirecting toward Nabil’s agency.
