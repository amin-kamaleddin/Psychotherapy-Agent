# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, non-judgment, and respect throughout the session. Relies somewhat on formulaic affirmations that could be deepened with more individualized expressions of positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that captures underlying meaning and emotional nuance beyond surface-level paraphrasing. Could go deeper emotionally at moments, such as when Kenji mentions chronic fatigue or the grief of realizing his routine may be contributing to it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session follows a logical arc but lacks clear momentum toward change. No strategic summarizing, collaborative goal-setting, or guidance toward concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with the session feeling like a dialogue rather than an interrogation. Could be elevated by more explicit co-construction, such as asking Kenji to help set the agenda or evaluate reflections.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports Kenji's autonomy by allowing him to set the pace and follow his own line of thinking. Could be strengthened by more explicit affirmations of his right to make whatever decision he wants.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some of Kenji's own motivations and insights, but remains superficial by relying primarily on open-ended questions and reflections. Missed opportunities to use scaled questions, decisional balance, or values exploration to deepen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance using curious, non-directive language and avoids an expert-driven approach. Occasionally feels more like a guided interview than a true co-exploration, and could better acknowledge Kenji's expertise in his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some change talk (e.g., 'more than I'd want to admit,' fatigue connection) but does not consistently amplify, reflect back, or deepen it. Lacks use of advanced techniques like scaled questions or decisional balance to build on emerging insights.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk reasonably by not arguing or contradicting directly, and gently redirects in some moments. Does not consistently use double-sided reflections or other strategic softening techniques to work through the client's resistance.
