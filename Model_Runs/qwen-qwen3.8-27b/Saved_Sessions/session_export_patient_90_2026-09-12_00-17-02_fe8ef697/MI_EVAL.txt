# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance, affirming Marcus’s perspective including his concern about the dog. Acceptance is strong but somewhat task-focused, with fewer deeper affirmations of his values or emotional burden.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections acknowledge Marcus’s stress and practical concerns but remain mostly surface-level. It misses deeper emotional meanings such as fear of being judged, attachment to the dog, and life disruption.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on one actionable asthma-management goal, such as reducing appointment burden and securing a refill. It effectively redirects potentially distracting topics toward practical treatment options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus meaningfully chooses the focus, sets boundaries, and creates a fallback, making the interaction collaborative. The collaboration is not fully equal because the psychiatrist provides much of the problem-solving framework and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, respects Marcus’s limits, and supports his ownership of decisions. It is somewhat directive at times by providing scripts, a specific step, and a breathing exercise.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The strategy is mainly practical problem-solving rather than eliciting Marcus’s own reasons for change. It lacks deeper open-ended questions about what asthma control means, what he is protecting, or his confidence and past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marcus to choose the focus, preferences, and role-play, fostering collaboration. However, the session remains more guided than fully co-created because the psychiatrist supplies most of the plan and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is limited and mostly practical compliance rather than intrinsic motivation. The psychiatrist does not systematically elicit importance, confidence, values, or past success related to asthma management.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles the dog-related sustain talk well by validating attachment and avoiding debate. It is effective but could more fully resolve ambivalence through double-sided reflection.
