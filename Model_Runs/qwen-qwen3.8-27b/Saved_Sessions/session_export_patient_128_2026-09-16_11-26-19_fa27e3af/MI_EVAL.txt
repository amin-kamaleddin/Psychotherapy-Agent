# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard through a warm, affirming, and nonjudgmental tone. Statements such as “that took courage” and “that’s progress” validated the client’s efforts without moralizing the alcohol use.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used effective reflective listening, accurately capturing the client’s ambivalence between avoiding consequences and maintaining social life. However, reflections were often brief and practical, with less depth in exploring underlying emotions such as shame or social fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remained highly focused on the target behavior of managing alcohol use at the upcoming party. The conversation moved logically from identifying the problem to assessing readiness and developing a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was highly collaborative, with the psychiatrist and client co-creating the plan. The psychiatrist offered tools and options, while the client actively selected, adapted, and personalized them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported autonomy by asking for permission and offering options rather than directives. The client’s choices, such as setting his own alarm instead of using a roommate reminder, were respected without resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evocative questions such as importance and confidence rulers effectively drew out the client’s motivation and readiness to change. However, some tactical strategies were supplied by the psychiatrist rather than fully evoked from the client’s own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positioned the client as the expert on his own life by inviting input and avoiding an authoritarian stance. The client’s contributions were respected and integrated into the final plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively used scaling questions and affirmations to evoke change talk and reinforce the client’s reasons and abilities for change. However, the session quickly shifted to logistical planning, limiting the depth of evoked motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled effectively by validating the client’s concerns and reframing them as part of problem-solving. The psychiatrist avoided arguing against the client’s views and redirected the conversation toward practical solutions.
