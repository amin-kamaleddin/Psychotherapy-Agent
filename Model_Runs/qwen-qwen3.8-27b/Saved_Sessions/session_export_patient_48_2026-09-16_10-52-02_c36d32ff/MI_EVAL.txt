# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and low-pressure, helping Farid feel safe. The main limitation is fewer affirmations of Farid’s strengths, effort, or values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and helpful around ambivalence, fatigue, and skepticism. The counselor misses deeper emotional themes such as self-doubt, fear of failure, and the meaning of his exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear focus on increasing activity and addressing fatigue, with a logical progression from goals to a small plan. It occasionally becomes more plan-driven than client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Farid’s preferences influence the final plan, such as timing, tone, and reminder method. The conversation remains partly clinician-led because the counselor supplies much of the intervention structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice, emphasizes no pressure, and respects Farid’s concerns about guilt. A minor limitation is that the counselor still frequently initiates suggestions and actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and open questions elicit some of Farid’s own motivations and preferences. Evocation remains limited because the counselor quickly offers explanations and specific solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Farid’s input and allows him to choose options. However, the agenda, content, and intervention details are largely set by the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Farid offers some change talk, such as wanting his energy back and willing to try for a week. The counselor does not consistently amplify, reflect, or deepen these statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges Farid’s skepticism without arguing, which is appropriate. He could more effectively use double-sided reflections to help Farid move from doubt toward change.
