# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and validating, but the tone is often practical and task-oriented. Basic positive regard is shown, though sustained warmth and acceptance are limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are moments of empathy for fear and exhaustion, but reflections remain shallow. The psychiatrist quickly moves from emotional acknowledgment to advice and practical problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on gastrointestinal symptoms and medical follow-up. However, the direction is more practical and task-focused than motivational or values-based.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration, especially in drafting messages and scripts. Nonetheless, the psychiatrist’s medical agenda largely drives the session rather than shared ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and asks whether Aizada wants scripts or revisions. Autonomy support is present but uneven, as the approach also includes prescriptive advice and a structured plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mainly provides information, tests, and scripts rather than drawing out Aizada’s own motivations. There is limited evocation of her values, hopes, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aizada is invited to contribute to scripts and decisions, which supports some partnership. However, the psychiatrist remains in a strong expert role, limiting deeper co-authorship.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Aizada offers change-oriented statements, but the psychiatrist only briefly affirms them. There are missed opportunities to deepen her motivation, agency, and confidence in change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely accommodates Aizada’s emphasis on medical testing and external confirmation. There is little use of reflection or redirection to explore ambivalence or broader coping values.
