# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Uses some nonjudgmental, supportive language, but repeated scripted instructions and coaching make the stance feel controlling rather than consistently affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Acknowledges frustration and time pressure, but reflections remain surface-level and do not capture the client's deeper irritation, loss of autonomy, or resentment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on procedural logistics instead of an MI-appropriate direction toward ambivalence, values, or change-related concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a coach-like, instructional role, and the client's concerns do not meaningfully shape the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive phrases are used, but repeated scripts and specific behavioral instructions undermine genuine choice and self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is little effort to draw out the client's own motivations, values, or reasons for change; the session relies more on supplying statements and instructions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Language such as 'want to try?' appears collaborative, but the interaction remains uneven and driven by the psychiatrist's agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Change talk is not genuinely evoked; the client is given scripted compliance statements rather than being invited to express their own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct confrontation, but minimization and sustain talk are largely ignored rather than reflected, explored, or reframed.
