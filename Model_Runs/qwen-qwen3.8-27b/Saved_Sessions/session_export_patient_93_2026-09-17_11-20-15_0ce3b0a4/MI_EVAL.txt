# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout. No moments of condescension, pressure, or hidden agenda, even when the patient resists or minimizes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and nuanced reflective listening that tracks the emotional undercurrent (ambivalence, loss of ritual, identity) rather than just content. The complex reflection contrasting smoking vs. wine warnings is a standout moment of empathic precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on alcohol reduction with a logical arc from ambivalence through plan design to barrier exploration. Slightly tighter closure would benefit from a brief consolidating summary of the agreed plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session reads as a genuine collaboration between equals, with the plan entirely co-constructed by the patient. Language is consistently inclusive and the psychiatrist serves as a mirror and questioner rather than a director.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is actively woven into the motivational strategy, not just acknowledged. The psychiatrist consistently frames change as the patient's choice and targets her stated values of independence and self-reliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All change talk originates from the patient, elicited by well-timed open questions and reflections. The psychiatrist never provides external reasons, lists consequences, or uses persuasion — textbook evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the patient as the expert on her own life through open questions and reflective invitations. The power dynamic remains egalitarian throughout with no moment of authority-taking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes desire, ability, and commitment language through well-timed questions and reflections. Could have more actively amplified and deepened change talk moments, such as exploring confidence level after the patient expresses commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by redirecting to the patient's own values without arguing or correcting. Could more explicitly use double-sided reflections to hold both dismissive and concerned perspectives simultaneously.
