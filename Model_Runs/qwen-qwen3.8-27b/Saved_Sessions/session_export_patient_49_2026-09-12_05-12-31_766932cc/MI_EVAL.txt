# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects Hana’s ambivalence without pressure, shame, or moralizing. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, with less explicit normalization of her discomfort and fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate reflections of ambivalence and practical concern, but empathy is inconsistent. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to validate fear, social awkwardness, and deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a reasonable focus on smoking but quickly narrows to bad breath, stains, and practical oral-hygiene steps. It does not consistently maintain a strategic focus on smoking cessation or link the plan back to quitting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed, and Hana selects the tongue scraper, reminder, and sticky-note wording. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist still drives much of the content through clinician-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Hana’s right to choose and avoids pressuring a quit decision. Autonomy is well respected, though the role could more explicitly emphasize her ownership of any future smoking decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions and doable-step prompts help Hana articulate a small plan. However, the session does not sufficiently evoke deeper change talk, values, or internal motivation for smoking cessation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative stance and invites Hana’s input throughout the session. The interaction is respectful and client-led in parts, but the psychiatrist still assumes a significant expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is limited and mostly centered on breath, stains, and small oral-hygiene steps. The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out, amplify, or deepen Hana’s own reasons for reducing or quitting smoking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts Hana’s sustain talk without arguing, which is a strength. However, the sustain talk is not actively softened, reframed, or used as a bridge toward change.
