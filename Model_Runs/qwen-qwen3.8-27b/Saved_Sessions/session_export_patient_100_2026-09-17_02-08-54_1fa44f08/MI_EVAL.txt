# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician shows consistent, warm, nonjudgmental regard and validates the client as a person rather than reducing her to her drinking. Pushback is accepted without arguing, labeling, or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses accurate reflections that capture ambivalence, autonomy, and concern about being judged. Emotional intensity, frustration, and fear of being controlled could be reflected more consistently for a deeper empathic response.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains relevant to alcohol use, school, and future goals, but the focus often shifts to privacy, autonomy, and check-in format. It lacks a stronger strategic thread connecting drinking directly to academic risk and behavioral change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client's proposed private check-in is co-constructed from her own language and treated as her plan. The interaction feels genuinely joint rather than clinician-directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supports choice, ownership, and self-direction without pressure or advice. The client is treated as the decision-maker throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids advice and elicits client preferences, but the questions are mostly procedural rather than change-focused. More evocation of the client's values, fears, risks, and future motivation is needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses collaborative language and allows the client's ideas to shape the conversation. However, repeated similar questions make the structure feel somewhat clinician-led rather than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some change talk around confidence, school, and protecting the client's future, but does not consistently amplify or deepen it. Stronger follow-up questions could better build the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing or reinforcing the client's reasons to continue drinking. More double-sided reflections could better honor sustain talk while opening space for change.
