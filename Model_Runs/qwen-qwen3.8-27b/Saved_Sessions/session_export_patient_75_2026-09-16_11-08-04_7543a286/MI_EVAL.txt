# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, normalizing Linda’s concerns and affirming her effort. It falls short of a 5 because the acceptance is sometimes formulaic and does not fully respond to her deeper self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes a good effort to reflect Linda’s ambivalence, especially about wanting control while fearing harm from the inhaler. However, empathy remains surface-level and does not fully explore her embarrassment, dependence worries, or fear of being at fault.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on asthma management and inhaler use, moving logically from barriers to a one-week plan. It is somewhat agenda-driven at times, with multiple structured options and information delivered before fully letting the client set the pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with Linda on decisions, such as technique, tracking, and next steps, resulting in a co-created plan. Collaboration is limited because the psychiatrist still frequently leads as the medical educator and offers options rather than letting the client shape the agenda first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Linda’s autonomy by offering choices, small steps, and avoiding pressure about the spacer. It is not a 5 because the options remain largely framed around adherence tasks rather than affirming her broader self-direction and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk through choice-based questions and a small weekly plan. However, the counselor often moves into education and problem-solving rather than drawing out Linda’s values, confidence, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative stance and invites Linda’s input in planning the inhaler routine and tracking. The session is not fully partner-led because the psychiatrist remains the main agenda-setter and does not explicitly position Linda as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda offers meaningful willingness to try a 3-second hold, rinsing, a sticky note, and tracking dog walks. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect or amplify this change talk, so the client’s own reasons for change are not fully strengthened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Linda’s concerns and often normalizes them, which helps keep the conversation respectful. However, sustain talk is often met with reassurance or information rather than active reflection, double-sided reflection, or redirection toward change.
