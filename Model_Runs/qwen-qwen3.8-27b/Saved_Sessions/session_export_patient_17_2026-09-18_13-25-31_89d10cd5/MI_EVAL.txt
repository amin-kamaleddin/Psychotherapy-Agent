# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming presence throughout. Preemptively normalizes shame and validates the legitimacy of Anisa's experiences without minimizing or pathologizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional complex reflections that capture the emotional meaning behind the client's words (e.g., 'the cigarette isn't really the problem, is it?'). Attuned to specific language and progressively deepens understanding rather than simply restating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation while exploring the underlying emotional landscape that maintains the behavior. Follows a logical therapeutic arc from establishing safety through to building a practical plan, without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language consistently frames the interaction as a joint exploration between equals ('I'd like to work on that with you'). Session direction is shaped by the client's contributions and decisions are co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports the client's right to choose and positions her as the decision-maker. Never prescribes, directs, or pressures; responds to 'what do I do?' with reflective questions rather than answers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never provides external reasons for change; all motivation is elicited from within Anisa through strategic questions and reflections. Skillfully amplifies the client's own insights and values (daughter's gaze, dignity, need for a breath).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly conveys that expertise resides within the client ('since you're the expert on your own life'). Power dynamic is consistently equal — observations are shared but always returned to the client for direction and meaning-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions directly elicit the client's own change language, which is then reflected and amplified. The client produces substantial change talk (the shower plan, the 'not hiding' reframe) entirely through the psychiatrist's evocative questioning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged and validated before being gently reframed (e.g., 'That's a door, not a cliff'). Never argues against or dismisses doubt; uses double-sided reflections to honor ambivalence while pivoting toward change.
