# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard and validation despite Elena's ambivalence. There is no judgment, blame, or shaming, even when she frames the behavior as a penalty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers frequent and generally accurate reflections of Elena's stress, protective motives, and need for safety. Empathy is strong but occasionally slightly leading and does not fully explore deeper emotions such as shame or fear of blame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and practical focus on creating a short smoke-free transition before being around her son. The focus is coherent, but it is narrow and not consistently connected to the broader goal of smoking cessation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Elena's lead and builds on her own ideas, including the car, front steps, entryway pause, and self-phrase. Collaboration is strong but remains mostly implicit rather than explicit shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invites Elena to choose what feels realistic and meaningful, supporting ownership of the plan. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming her right to choose whether or not to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Elena's own insights about calming down, noticing tension, and using the pause for herself. Evocation is useful but limited to the small transition behavior rather than broader smoking cessation motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Elena as the expert on her own life and guides the session based on her ideas. Partnership is strong, though it could be more explicit in agenda-setting and power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk around stress regulation and parenting transitions. However, there is limited development of change talk specifically related to reducing or quitting smoking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging concerns without arguing or dismissing them. The response remains somewhat focused on making the current pattern less burdensome rather than strongly pivoting toward broader change.
