# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and uses validating statements. Acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and does not fully deepen into explicit affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurately reflects anger, delay, and job risk at a surface level. Does not consistently reach deeper feelings such as humiliation, powerlessness, or being devalued.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Focus remains narrow on waiting, leaving, and messages rather than guiding toward a broader change goal. The session feels circular instead of progressively moving toward a target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language but repeatedly offers scripts, menus, and predefined options. This advisory stance undermines equal partnership and patient-led exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frequently affirms the patient’s freedom to choose and avoids forcing action. Autonomy support is inconsistent because menus and suggested lines continue after the patient refuses them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Begins with an open question but then supplies content and options rather than eliciting the patient’s own motivations, values, or confidence. The patient’s statements about respect, time, and job are not used to build internal change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some partnership language is present, but repeated choice sets and scripts reduce the patient’s role in shaping the session. The patient’s request to stop menus is not fully honored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is an initial MI-consistent question, but little sustained evocation of reasons, desire, need, or confidence for change. Patient statements that could support change talk are not expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Avoids direct argument and acknowledges the patient’s desire to leave, but does not use double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance to redirect sustain talk. The session remains stuck in the same anger and logistics loop.
