# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally warm, nonjudgmental, and avoids lecturing or shaming. Acceptance is limited by a subtle agenda-driven focus on harm reduction despite the client’s repeated refusal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the surface content of Marcus’s behavior and priorities. They remain somewhat factual and formulaic, with limited reflection of deeper feelings around belonging, peer approval, and being judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, parties, safety, and avoiding trouble. Direction is somewhat narrow and plan-focused, with less exploration of Marcus’s values, readiness, or personal reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some collaborative invitations for Marcus to choose or adjust suggestions. The psychiatrist remains the main idea generator, and the client’s refusal to plan does not sufficiently reshape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses low-pressure language and acknowledges Marcus’s choice. Autonomy support weakens as the therapist keeps reoffering new plans after repeated refusals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several evocative questions, including the 0–10 importance scale and questions about protecting important parts of his life. The therapist does not sufficiently follow up on Marcus’s low importance rating or expand his own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adjusts suggestions in some ways. Partnership is uneven because the session remains largely therapist-directed rather than client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus offers brief change-related statements, such as keeping his phone charged and knowing when to leave. These statements are acknowledged but not deepened, amplified, or linked to his values or readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and uses statements like “I hear you” and “no judgment.” Softening is inconsistent because the therapist repeatedly returns to harm-reduction options after Marcus rejects them.
