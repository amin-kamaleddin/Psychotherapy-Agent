# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor was respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalized the client's ambivalence without pressure. The stance was professional but not consistently deep enough to affirm values and capacity at a 5 level.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client's core tension, guilt, and cautious optimism. They were largely cognitive and planning-focused rather than deeply exploring values-level emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a specific target behavior and moved logically through actionable steps. The plan remained clear, concrete, and change-oriented without major wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client contributions were respected and shaped the final plan, and multiple options invited participation. The counselor still provided much of the structure, keeping them as the session's architect.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strongly emphasized client choice, flexibility, and the right to stop. The client's limits and preferences were respected without coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited, but the session moved quickly to concrete planning before fully exploring intrinsic reasons. More values-based exploration and amplification of the client's own motivation would improve this criterion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a collaborative stance and allowed client preferences to shape the plan. It could improve by more consistently inviting the client to generate the agenda, language, and options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client change talk was present and elicited through useful questions, but the session shifted quickly into planning. More deliberate evocation and amplification of DARN-C language would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was addressed with normalization, flexibility, and nonpunitive reframing such as 'I tried.' It was often accepted but not consistently used to evoke deeper change reasons.
