# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with markers like hearing, respect, and validation of frustration. Not a 5 because acceptance is somewhat procedural and could more warmly affirm Rowan's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflects surface-level annoyances and burden, maintaining alliance. Does not consistently capture deeper feelings such as being controlled, overwhelmed, or resentful.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps clear focus on charging and uses scaling and implementation planning to maintain momentum. It remains tactical rather than linking the behavior to Rowan's broader values or goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses questions and choices, but often supplies multiple specific tactics for Rowan to select from. It should elicit Rowan's own ideas and expertise before offering options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently affirms choice, respects the stop condition, and avoids pressure or coercion. It could strengthen explicit ownership language and affirm Rowan's capacity to self-direct.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses open questions and the confidence ruler to elicit some intention and confidence. It should more consistently draw out Rowan's own reasons, values, and motivations before advising.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers a collaborative surface and lets Rowan modify the plan, but the clinician remains the main source of solutions. More power-sharing would begin by exploring what Rowan already notices works.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits willingness and a small plan through confidence scaling, but does not consistently deepen or expand Rowan's change talk. It often shifts to practical problem-solving instead of amplifying client-generated motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and acknowledges reasons for maintaining the status quo, preventing confrontation. It could better soften sustain talk through double-sided reflections or reframing toward change.
