# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating without scolding or pressuring the client. However, acceptance becomes procedural and is oriented toward accommodating denial rather than affirming the client’s worth independent of behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides adequate reflections of the client’s perspective and affect, but these remain surface-level and repetitive. He does not deeply explore the underlying ambivalence, fear, shame, or exhaustion behind the client’s defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session quickly shifts from the target issue of drug use to scripting reassurance and avoiding arguments. It lacks a clear focus on the clinically relevant target behavior, its consequences, values, or desired change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input but largely generates the agenda, scripts, and contingency plans. Collaboration is present at a surface level, but the client does not substantially shape the therapeutic direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the client’s freedom to choose and avoids coercive language, which is a strength. The main limitation is that providing multiple scripts can still feel subtly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The initial open questions are appropriate, but the session moves into problem-solving without deepening the client’s reasons, desires, or concerns for change. Evocation remains limited and does not substantially expand change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently offers choices and a collaborative tone, but also takes on an expert role by generating content and plans. Partnership is present but uneven, with the client selecting options rather than co-creating focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effort to cultivate change talk, and missed opportunities are not used to evoke desire, concern, or need for change. As a result, client change talk remains rare and underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk but does not effectively soften or redirect it. By adopting denial-based statements as the working solution, the response reinforces avoidance rather than building discrepancy.
