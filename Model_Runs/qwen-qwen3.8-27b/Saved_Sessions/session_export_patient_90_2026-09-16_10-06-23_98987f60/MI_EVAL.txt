# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and showed positive regard for Marcus’s fatigue, cost concerns, and wish to keep his dog. Warmth was occasionally procedural because the session moved quickly into problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were adequate reflections of frustration and practical limits, but the therapist often stayed at the surface level. Deeper emotional meaning, such as the importance of the dog or fear of burdening his wife, was not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remained focused on improving asthma management, especially nighttime symptoms and inhaler use. There was some drift into logistics, but the overall target stayed clear and practical.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Marcus’s input and allowed some choices, but the interaction often felt expert-led. The therapist frequently supplied scripts, checklists, and structured options rather than fully co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist repeatedly supported Marcus’s choice, ownership, and boundaries, including his decision to skip role-play. Autonomy support was somewhat weakened by the frequent use of directive advice and therapist-generated plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited through scaling and planning questions, but the therapist also provided much of the reasoning and advice. There were missed opportunities to draw out Marcus’s own deeper motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed a partial collaborative stance by asking for preferences and inviting Marcus to choose. The balance of power shifted toward the therapist when offering scripts, options, and structured steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist helped Marcus express small commitments, such as trying the inhaler check and saving the video. However, change talk was not consistently deepened, amplified, or expanded through more evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with Marcus’s reasons for maintaining the status quo, but the session remained heavily focused on barriers. Sustain talk was not consistently softened or redirected toward change using MI-consistent reflections.
