# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates some of Erik’s experience. However, acceptance is often procedural and shifts into practical advice rather than deeper warmth or affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows adequate early reflections of Erik’s concern about lifelong medication and feelings of failure. Empathy becomes less consistent as the session moves into problem-solving and practical planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session well focused on diet, glucose monitoring, and diabetes-related lifestyle change. The main limitation is that alcohol reduction is only briefly addressed and the focus narrows around medication avoidance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some collaborative language and offers choices such as a log or a doctor script. However, the psychiatrist often introduces the solutions and leads the plan rather than consistently eliciting Erik’s ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently affirms Erik’s freedom to choose and avoids pressuring him into medication or specific changes. Autonomy support is somewhat weakened by the amount of prescriptive advice and structured planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Asks some useful initial questions about concerns, past attempts, and confidence. The psychiatrist then largely supplies advice and a plan rather than evoking Erik’s own values, motivations, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates partial partnership through collaborative phrases and inviting input. The session often remains expert-driven, with the counselor supplying much of the plan rather than positioning Erik as the primary source of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Erik offers some change talk, such as trying eggs, monitoring glucose, and wanting proof that lifestyle changes work. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, expand, or connect these statements to Erik’s values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoids arguing with Erik’s resistance to medication and redirects toward practical steps. However, some responses remain focused on avoiding medication rather than fully exploring ambivalence and personal reasons for change.
