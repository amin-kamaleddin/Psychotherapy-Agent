# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates frustration, but acceptance feels somewhat procedural. Repeatedly offering help after rejection can subtly undermine unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture frustration, unfairness, and the importance of hearing his mother’s voice. However, empathy remains surface-level and does not deeply explore underlying fear, vulnerability, or relational hurt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stays focused on making the phone call rather than the broader target behavior of rule compliance and safe release. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to connect family values to prosocial choices over the next three weeks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language, including offering choices and staying with Tariq to listen. However, collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist continues to control the agenda with therapist-selected options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for Tariq’s preferences and eventually accepts his wish to simply be listened to. However, repeated offers of scripts and problem-solving tools can feel mildly pressuring and reduce autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some evocative starters are used, but the session quickly shifts into offering solutions rather than eliciting internal motivation. There is limited exploration of what matters to Tariq about his future, family, record, and self-image.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some partnership by inviting input and offering choices. However, partnership is weakened by continuing to provide interventions after Tariq explicitly rejects them, rather than letting Tariq set the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tariq expresses change talk about keeping his head down, staying out of trouble, and getting home safely. The psychiatrist does not actively cultivate this language through importance, confidence, or values-based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tariq’s sustain talk about blaming the officer and seeing himself as a model resident is acknowledged but not strategically softened. The psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflection or redirect toward choices, consequences, and rule-compliant behavior.
