# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and avoids shaming or labeling the patient as “non-compliant.” Warmth is somewhat formulaic, and repeated safety warnings can begin to feel controlling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician provides accurate content-level reflections about the patient’s concerns about steroids, time pressure, and not being helpless. Deeper emotional themes, such as frustration and feeling monitored, are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on asthma management, inhaler use, and documentation without significant drift. The direction is largely administrative and safety-focused rather than change-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is meaningful collaboration around the note wording and the patient’s preference to avoid being labeled “non-compliant.” The broader MI process remains more clinician-driven than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, respects the patient’s right to decline daily treatment, and avoids forcing the issue. However, repeated safety warnings begin to feel directive and undermine autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening question is MI-friendly, but the session shifts to closed choices and safety checks. There is limited exploration of the patient’s values, motivations, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and co-creates the documentation with the patient. The session is uneven in partnership because it remains driven by the clinician’s safety agenda and expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few reflections of the patient’s capability and agency, such as knowing when to seek care. The psychiatrist does not actively develop or amplify change talk with evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with the patient’s sustain talk but does not effectively redirect it toward change. The sustain talk remains dominant, and no double-sided reflections are used.
