# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated warmth, respect, and non-judgment, validating Daniel’s pride about quitting smoking while addressing alcohol concerns without lecture. Some affirmations felt slightly formulaic and could have been more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Daniel’s mixed feelings, values, and concerns about stress, his son, and perceived monitoring. Deeper emotional attunement to the underlying anxiety and ambivalence would have strengthened this area.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on the target behavior—limiting alcohol use—moving logically from exploration to planning and rehearsal. There was no drift, and every intervention supported the concrete goal of a two-drink trial.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was strongly collaborative, with the plan, phrasing, and role-play co-created with Daniel. Offering several pre-formulated options at times slightly reduced the space for Daniel to generate his own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Daniel was consistently positioned as the decision-maker, with options framed as choices rather than directives. The confidence scale and plans were used to support agency rather than compliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully drew out Daniel’s internal motivations, especially his values around fatherhood and his confidence to change. A missed opportunity to explore his personal identity and reasons for change beyond his son prevented a higher rating.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative, non-expert stance, inviting Daniel’s input and respecting his preferences throughout. A few prescriptive moments with specific scripts and plans slightly reduced the fully partner-led dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited and reinforced change talk through questions about values, past success, and confidence, tracking Daniel’s motivation from 5 to 7. Deeper amplification of Daniel’s existing change talk would have made the skill stronger.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniel’s sustain talk was handled well without argument or judgment, and his language about monitoring was accepted rather than challenged. More strategic use of double-sided reflections could have more actively redirected sustain talk toward change.
