# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and affirmation of the client’s efforts and concerns. A minor growth area is affirming the client’s worth as a person and father beyond his actions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** He accurately reflects the client’s pain, ambivalence, and fear of being monitored or freezing. The score is not a 5 because he could go deeper into the shame, grief, and values beneath the client’s experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing alcohol use, engaging in Stopping Violence, and increasing contact with the children. It purposefully guides vague concerns into concrete, safety-conscious steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** He invites the client to co-create options and adapts when the client rejects formal plans or scripted language. The client’s preferences substantially shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** He consistently honors the client’s pace, offers options rather than directives, and respects the refusal to sign official forms. The client is clearly positioned as the owner of his decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** He effectively elicits the client’s own commitments, support person, and confidence rating. The score is not a 5 because the counselor provides many scripts and practical tips, which can limit further client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as the expert on his own life and repeatedly invites direction. The session is collaborative rather than counselor-led or prescriptive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** He actively asks for and reinforces the client’s progress, commitments, and confidence. The score is not a 5 because he could more consistently deepen and amplify the client’s own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** He validates the client’s concerns about moving in and paperwork without arguing, then offers autonomy-respecting alternatives. The score is not a 5 because he could more explicitly pivot sustain talk into change through double-sided reflections.
