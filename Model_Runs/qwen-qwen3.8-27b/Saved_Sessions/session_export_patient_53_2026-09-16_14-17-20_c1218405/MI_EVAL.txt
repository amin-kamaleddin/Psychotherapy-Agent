# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows warmth, validation, and nonjudgment toward Isabella. Statements normalize her avoidance and affirm her worth independent of action.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures both Isabella’s desire for connection and need for control. Some reflections are leading and do not fully verify her internal experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on low-pressure community engagement. Questions are progressively narrowed into small, concrete, manageable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Isabella’s preferences actively shape the plan, such as checking dates only, avoiding videos, and choosing a quiet event. Collaboration could be more explicit through shared summaries or co-created planning language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly protected through repeated emphasis on choice, exit options, and the right not to proceed. The psychiatrist avoids pressure, persuasion, and obligations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Isabella’s own ideas about what would make a step feel safe. Deeper evocation of values, confidence, or internal motivation would strengthen this criterion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative because Isabella’s answers guide the content of the session. More explicit language about shared power and client ownership would make the partnership clearer.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes small, doable steps like checking a website, viewing dates only, or imagining a quiet event. Change talk could be deepened with more affirmation, confidence, or importance questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and reframed as protection rather than failure. The psychiatrist preserves autonomy while gently creating space for smaller steps.
