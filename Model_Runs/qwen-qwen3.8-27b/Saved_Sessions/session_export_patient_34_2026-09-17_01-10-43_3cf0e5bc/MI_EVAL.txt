# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validates Dylan’s frustration about being singled out and reduced to one incident. A slight limitation is reflecting minimizing language such as 'one night' without fully balancing respect with the behavioral concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Dylan’s concerns about fairness, identity, friendships, and being labeled, but stays more content-focused than emotionally nuanced. It could more deeply explore underlying fear, shame, and ambivalence rather than occasionally imposing interpretations.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains generally on the conduct incident but drifts toward administrative mitigation, transcript, grades, and being perceived fairly. It lacks a clearer, progressive direction toward responsible behavior change or a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and builds on Dylan’s values such as student identity, grades, and friendships. It is limited by a somewhat counselor-driven agenda to help Dylan present the incident favorably rather than fully co-creating the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites Dylan to choose his own framing with open, noncoercive questions and avoids direct advice or pressure. Some phrasing still nudges toward admitting responsibility, so autonomy support is strong but not always fully explicit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Dylan to generate his own language about fairness, being a serious student, and what to say to the conduct team. It remains narrow and administrative, with little use of importance, confidence, scaling, or future-consequence questions to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-authoritarian stance and treats Dylan as the expert on his own life and student identity. The partnership is not fully developed because the counselor appears to steer toward a favorable conduct-team presentation rather than explicitly negotiating goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some evocation of responsibility and student identity, but change talk is not consistently reflected, amplified, or developed. It mostly supports presenting well to the conduct team rather than strengthening intrinsic motivation for behavioral change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct argument and generally reflects defensiveness rather than escalating it. However, frequent validation of minimizing framing such as 'one night' can unintentionally reinforce sustain talk instead of pivoting toward accountability.
