# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Elias’s limits and ambivalence. Some positive but evaluative/formulaic language slightly reduced the purity of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections captured Elias’s practical concerns, ambivalence, and fear of failure. Empathy could be deeper by exploring the emotional meaning of pain, independence, and self-worth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on a clear target behavior: a short, safe standing attempt after breakfast. Guidance consistently moved the plan toward a concrete, actionable step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was mostly co-created, with Elias selecting options and modifying details. At times the psychiatrist acted more directive by introducing several specific plan components.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice, flexibility, and ownership were strongly emphasized throughout. The psychiatrist consistently avoided pressure and supported Elias’s self-directed decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited Elias’s own goals, preferences, and confidence rather than relying on external persuasion. Evocation would strengthen with deeper exploration of values, identity, and meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elias’s input substantially shaped the plan, reflecting a collaborative stance. The partnership was occasionally weakened by a more expert/instructional role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited and amplified Elias’s own change language and confidence. Change talk could be enriched by connecting the goal to deeper personal values and self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers and fear of failure were acknowledged and softened through safe, acceptable planning. More strategic double-sided reflections could better pivot sustain talk into change talk.
