# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental, often affirming the client’s feelings and right to a workable plan. The interaction still feels somewhat structured and task-oriented, limiting deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately capture frustration, anger, and feeling dismissed, but later the counselor misses vulnerable shame and self-blame by moving quickly to scripts. Empathy is helpful but inconsistent and sometimes bypassed by premature problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains connected to asthma, medical burden, and communicating with the doctor, but drifts into script-writing and multiple options. It lacks a consistent client-led focus on values, goals, and a single target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist sometimes uses the client’s preferred language and revises after rejection, but frequently supplies options, scripts, and next steps. The balance is uneven, with the counselor leading more than co-exploring as equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Statements such as 'you do not have to fix everything' support choice, but repeated menus, numbered options, and a practice suggestion can feel pressuring. Autonomy is present but inconsistent, especially when the client rejects being given choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial questions invite the client’s experience, but much of the session shifts to external suggestions, scripts, and problem-solving. The counselor does not consistently draw out the client’s own reasons, values, hopes, or capability for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, yet repeatedly positions himself as the solution generator through multiple-choice prompts and scripts. The client explicitly experiences this as unequal, saying it feels like a test, a menu, or homework.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers useful change talk, such as wanting one simple change and a plan that fits his schedule, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, reinforce, or amplify it. The counselor often moves to offering options instead of developing the client’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and sometimes validates the client’s resistance, but does not consistently soften sustain talk with reflection, double-sided reflections, or values exploration. Resistance often remains focused on practical negotiation rather than being metabolized into change talk.
