### Acceptance

**Comments:** The clinician is respectful and supportive, with multiple affirming statements that avoid judgment. However, acceptance is somewhat generic and does not deeply validate Alessia’s ambivalence or emotional difficulty.

### Empathy

**Comments:** The clinician provides accurate reflective listening and understands the main safety concern. Empathy remains largely cognitive and task-focused, with missed opportunities to explore deeper feelings and values.

### Direction

**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on safely transitioning from phenelzine to citalopram. The interaction is well organized and consistently guides toward concrete, actionable next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician collaborates effectively in creating a brief message and phone script tailored to Alessia’s preferences. Collaboration is strong in method, though the clinician still supplies most of the content and medical guidance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers choices and supports Alessia’s decision-making in practical ways. However, the tone remains somewhat directive, and explicit statements affirming her ownership and final decision are limited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician begins with an appropriate open question and invites participation. Beyond that, the clinician mostly provides information rather than actively drawing out Alessia’s values, motivations, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician includes Alessia’s input and avoids a purely authoritarian stance, but the interaction remains largely expert-driven. Alessia’s lived experience and preferences are not consistently positioned as central to the decision-making process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Alessia expresses some change talk, but the clinician does not consistently evoke, reflect, or amplify it. More strategic follow-up would have helped strengthen her motivation and commitment to the plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges Alessia’s concerns without contradicting them, but does not strategically soften or reframe sustain talk toward agency. The focus on risk and safety precautions may unintentionally reinforce the perceived danger of the transition.
