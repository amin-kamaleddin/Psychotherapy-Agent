# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, respectful, and non-judgmental tone throughout, affirming Marco's efforts and normalizing his confusion. The warmth is consistent but somewhat structured and professional rather than deeply personal, and lacks explicit unconditional acceptance of the yo-yo cycle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening that moves beyond paraphrasing to capture underlying emotional meaning, such as the shift from 'I'm doing healthy things' to 'maybe it's working against me.' Reflections are accurate but sometimes stay at the level of what was just said rather than exploring deeper emotional layers like fear of repeated failure or identity impact.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent focus on the target behavior throughout, with a clear and strategic progression from identifying the barrier through problem-solving to value-based commitment. The session moves with clear momentum from identification to planning without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the session as a joint exploration with language like 'I'd love to hear from you directly' and 'Let's think about what a realistic change might look like.' However, some guiding moments feel slightly counselor-led rather than purely collaborative, and the psychiatrist could more explicitly negotiate the agenda with Marco.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supports Marco's autonomy, never prescribing or imposing a plan, with all solutions generated by the client and affirmed by the psychiatrist. Strong use of ownership language such as 'That's real insight, and it's yours, not mine' and 'What's your instinct?'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Marco's own motivations, reasons, and confidence through strategic open questions, eliciting rich change talk including specific behavioral intentions and value-based language. Missed an opportunity to use a ruler question to explicitly evoke and amplify confidence, which would have elevated this to a 5.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with explicit attribution of insight to the client ('That's real insight, and it's yours, not mine') and avoids giving direct advice. Could be strengthened by more explicit power-sharing language and co-construction of the plan, as some guiding questions are slightly more directional than pure partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategically cultivates change talk through questions eliciting differentiation, ability, and importance, resulting in substantial behavioral plans, confidence statements, and value-based reasons from Marco. Could have more explicitly evoked commitment language (e.g., a ruler question) or further developed confidence talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles Marco's minimal sustain talk ('boy do I love that OJ') well by not reinforcing it, not arguing against it, and gently pivoting toward the plan. A more explicit double-sided reflection could have more fully honored and then softened the attachment to the habit.
