# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remained respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, especially when the patient minimized problems. A few early statements were somewhat assumptive or leading, preventing a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** He demonstrated strong reflective listening and perspective-taking around ambivalence, work, and stress. Several reflections added plausible but slightly ungrounded interpretations, limiting the score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking and moved toward a concrete, behavior-specific step. The opening was somewhat vague before direction improved.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked in a truly collaborative way, building the session around patient-generated ideas. The plan was co-constructed rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This was one of the strongest areas, with clear support for choice, self-determination, and small self-directed experiments. There was no pressure, ultimatums, or advice-giving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively drew out internal motivation, values, and change talk. The main limitation was not always deepening or expanding the change talk after it emerged.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and allowed the patient’s preferences to shape the direction of care. Explicit power-sharing or agenda-negotiation language could have strengthened this further.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicited and reflected useful change talk, including control, autonomy, and reducing use. The score was limited by not consistently amplifying or deepening the change talk after it appeared.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled respectfully without arguing or moralizing. However, the conversation sometimes remained anchored in health-risk framing rather than consistently reframing sustain talk toward the client’s own values.
