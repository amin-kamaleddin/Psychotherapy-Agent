# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and validates Tariq’s frustration, anger, and powerlessness. However, regard feels somewhat conditional and repeated offers of the same problem-solving strategies reduce the sense of unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects surface emotions such as frustration and unfairness but remains at a first-order level. Deeper concerns, including fear of being seen as disrespectful, protecting his record, and the meaning of family connection, are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the presenting problem of getting a phone call or message to his mother. It lacks clearer strategic direction toward the broader change goal, such as managing frustration, protecting his record, or connecting family values to compliant behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, especially when the psychiatrist accepts Tariq’s preferred shorter message and wording. However, much of the session is driven by the psychiatrist’s proposed scripts, notes, and escalation steps rather than Tariq’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist occasionally asks Tariq to choose among options but continues to push scripts, rehearsal, and escalation after Tariq rejects them. Autonomy is undermined by directive language and repeated suggestions that reduce Tariq’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited evocative questions and little effort to draw out Tariq’s own values, goals, self-efficacy, or reasons for change. The psychiatrist supplies much of the motivation and problem-solving rather than evoking Tariq’s internal change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and adapts to Tariq’s preference for a simpler note, showing some collaboration. The interaction is often expert-driven and solution-focused rather than a true co-created partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tariq offers meaningful change talk, such as wanting to protect his record and being “the model resident,” but the psychiatrist does not effectively amplify or build on it. Responses remain practical and do not actively cultivate more change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not effectively redirect Tariq’s sustain talk about the officer “holding the power” or escalation being a trap. Some statements may reinforce the powerless, externally controlled framing rather than gently moving toward agency and change.
