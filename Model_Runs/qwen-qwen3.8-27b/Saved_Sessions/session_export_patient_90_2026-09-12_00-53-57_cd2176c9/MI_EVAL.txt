# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental. Acceptance is slightly weakened by repeated solution-focused responses after the client asks to be heard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some strong reflections of overwhelm and fear. However, empathy is inconsistent because the client’s core ambivalence and grief about the dog are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays partly focused on asthma and breathing through repeated check-ins. However, it frequently drifts into broader stressors without clearly moving toward a concrete asthma-related change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences and offers alternatives, showing some collaboration. Collaboration is uneven because frequent resource lists and problem-solving steps may feel more directive than shared.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids overt pressure. Autonomy support is moderate but weakened by repeated suggestions after the client asks not to be pushed into tasks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open questions but often shifts to options, exercises, and resources. Evocation is limited because the client’s own values and reasons for change are not fully developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist sometimes invites input and avoids a fully directive stance. Partnership is partial because repeated option lists may position the therapist as the problem-solver rather than a true partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk about his asthma, need for relief, and desire to keep both the dog and his health. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen this language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses strong sustain talk about paperwork, embarrassment, and preferring to stay sick rather than be alone. The psychiatrist does not effectively soften or redirect this ambivalence with double-sided reflections.
