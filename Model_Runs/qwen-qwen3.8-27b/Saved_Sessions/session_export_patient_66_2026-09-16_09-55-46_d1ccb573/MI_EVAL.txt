# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and avoids shaming Kareem for smoking or resisting change. The warmth is strong but somewhat procedural, with occasional clinical framing rather than deeply person-centered affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects several of Kareem’s practical and emotional concerns, including workplace pressure and not wanting a quitter label. However, the reflections remain mostly cognitive and miss deeper emotional nuances or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation and moves toward a concrete one-week patch plan with a check-in. The quick shift to logistics reduces the broader motivational direction tied to values or long-term goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates on details, respects preferences, and confirms the plan with Kareem. It is functional rather than fully co-creative because the clinician supplies most options and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports autonomy by asking permission, respecting limits, and framing the patch as reversible and client-controlled. Some language remains mildly directive around completing the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open and scaling questions early but moves quickly into advice and practical planning. There is limited sustained evocation of Kareem’s values, reasons for change, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids authoritarianism, but the session remains fairly clinician-driven. Kareem selects from clinician-generated options rather than substantially shaping the session’s direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, including willingness to try a patch and desk kit. These statements are not consistently reflected, amplified, or deepened with DARN-C or scaling follow-ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing and reframes the patch as low-drama rather than quitting. However, sustain talk remains central, with limited double-sided reflection or redirection toward values and autonomy.
