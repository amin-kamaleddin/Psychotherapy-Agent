# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows respect and normalizes the client's phone use without judgment or blame. Acceptance is strong but could be deeper with explicit affirmations of the client's awareness and emotional effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence, practical details, and conflicting motivations. They sometimes stay cognitive and could more consistently reflect underlying emotions such as loneliness, boredom, or fear of missing out.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays oriented to social media use, sleep, coursework, and boundary-setting. Some questions are broad or repetitive, so clearer milestone summaries could tighten the focus toward a specific next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session uses shared problem-solving and builds on the client's concrete ideas. Collaboration is strong but could be more fully egalitarian by asking the client to prioritize or set the next step in her own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids coercion, accepts limits, and frames trial steps as choices rather than punishments. It could more explicitly affirm the client's ownership of both trying and not trying, and normalize setbacks as information.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks the client to identify her own signals, values, and smallest tests, supporting internally generated motivation. Evocation is solid but more value- and discrepancy-based questions could deepen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly treats the client as the expert and invites input on shaping the plan. Partnership could be stronger by consistently emphasizing that the client's contributions drive the direction rather than the psychiatrist's questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful client-generated options, such as a 9 PM check-in, kitchen phone placement, one study hour, and using a pen. Change talk is present but not consistently reinforced, amplified, or linked to confidence and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not argued against and is generally held within ambivalence. The psychiatrist could more actively use double-sided reflections to link concerns about connection and fear of missing out to change.
