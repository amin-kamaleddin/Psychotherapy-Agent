# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, non-judgmental positive regard throughout the session. The psychiatrist validates Erik's experience as legitimate without minimizing or moralizing, using phrases like 'That's a heavy thing to carry' and directly affirming his worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beyond surface-level paraphrasing to capture the emotional and cognitive core of Erik's experience. Notable examples include capturing the self-arguing dissonance, the scorekeeping weight of the candy bar, and the layered 'double hit' of the loop plus self-blame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on the target behavior with a logical, progressive flow from comparison → scorecard → avoidance loop → insight about not looking back. The session could benefit from a stronger closing that ties insights to a concrete next step or behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong use of Erik's own metaphors (scorecard, video game, consolation prize) as the primary framework, demonstrating active co-construction. Could be elevated with more explicit invitations for Erik to co-create the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** All questions are open and invitational, with phrases like 'if anything' signaling that Erik is not required to have an answer. Could be strengthened with more explicit affirmations of Erik's freedom to choose his own pace and path.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of open questions that elicit Erik's own insights and solutions rather than providing external reasons for change. Falls short of a 5 by staying at the level of problem exploration rather than reaching deeper into values and identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** No lecturing, agenda-imposing, or unilateral direction; Erik's language consistently shapes the session. Could be stronger with more explicit sharing of power, such as asking Erik what he'd like to focus on or inviting him to set the direction more explicitly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questions effectively evoke Erik's own change talk, including insights about separating the gym from the candy bar and recognizing 'okay' as a sign he can still do this. Occasionally misses opportunities to explicitly amplify or deepen change talk when it appears.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce or amplify sustain talk, instead reflecting frustration without endorsing conclusions and redirecting toward Erik's strengths. Could be more proactive in pivoting away from sustain talk, particularly during the 'what's the point' loop.
