# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful validation of frustration is present, but it often shifts quickly into scripts and problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but mostly surface-level, missing deeper themes such as feeling disrespected, invisible, or worried about work.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the delay and resolution, but it is too oriented toward scripts and complaint procedures rather than the client’s motivation and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction feels more advisor-driven than co-constructed, with the client’s preferences not fully shaping the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Early autonomy support is undermined by repeated suggestions to draft emails or use specific lines after the client says he does not want scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial open questions are followed mostly by advice, with few questions that draw out the client’s goals, values, or preferred way forward.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some collaborative choices are offered, but the psychiatrist repeatedly assumes the expert role by supplying scripts and escalation plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful statements about accountability and self-advocacy, but these are rarely reflected or expanded into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client’s sustain talk and resistance are acknowledged, but not effectively softened, and escalation plans can reinforce an adversarial stance.
