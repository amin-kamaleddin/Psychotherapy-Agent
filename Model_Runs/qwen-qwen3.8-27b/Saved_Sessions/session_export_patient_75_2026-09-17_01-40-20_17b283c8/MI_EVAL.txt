# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental about Linda’s side-effect worries. Affirmations are helpful but sometimes shape or evaluate her responses, so warmth could be more unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflects Linda’s ambivalence and practical concerns accurately. Could go deeper into fear, fatigue, and loss of control to better connect with the emotional undercurrent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently focuses on inhaler use, safety, and a workable plan. The questions advance the session toward concrete, client-owned steps without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session feels jointly constructed, with Linda’s ideas shaping the final plan. The psychiatrist integrates her suggestions rather than imposing a preset approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice by emphasizing Linda’s control, a trial period, and self-monitoring. Avoids directive pressure or adherence-based judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits Linda’s own plan, safety checks, and confidence through client-directed questions. Could more explicitly evoke importance and confidence in change using her values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently returns to Linda to define what is workable, safe, and successful. Occasional clinician-initiated suggestions slightly reduce full power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions evoke clear change talk about the trial, logging, and stop conditions. Could further elicit stronger importance, confidence, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validates side-effect fears and pairs them with health and safety values without arguing. Could use more double-sided reflections to fully integrate persistent concerns.
