# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Jason's perspective and avoiding any corrective language. The warmth is professional but measured, lacking deeply personal or emotionally resonant acknowledgment of Jason's courage in being present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening, accurately capturing Jason's sense of unfairness and the 'why me and not them' feeling. Some reflections remain surface-level, not deeply exploring the anxiety or fear beneath Jason's defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear trajectory toward a specific behavioral target, drifting between Jason's values, frustration, and dismissal without narrowing focus. The conversation feels exploratory and circular rather than building toward a concrete change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with open-ended questions and building on Jason's statements without overriding him. However, the session is somewhat therapist-directed in structure, and Jason's ideas don't substantially reshape the conversation's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids directive or pressuring language, framing things as Jason's own choices and values. Could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming Jason's freedom to choose, including the option of not making any changes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Jason's own motivations around his degree, social life, and identity without imposing advice. Could be more systematic with scaling questions or explicit development of the discrepancy between current behavior and stated values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames the conversation as collaborative exploration, following Jason's lead and building on his own words. Could be strengthened by more explicit power-sharing language or negotiating the session agenda with Jason.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits meaningful change talk including 'I need this degree' and 'I could be successful and still have a good time.' Could push further into ability talk, confidence building, or eliciting a concrete commitment statement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with neutral reflection rather than actively softening it, missing opportunities to create discrepancy or use double-sided reflections. The approach is passive-neutral, not strategically redirecting Jason's resistant statements.
