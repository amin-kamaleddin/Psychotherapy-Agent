# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming of Margaret’s values and concerns. It is not a 5 because the warmth becomes slightly formulaic and occasionally shifts into advice-giving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is strong reflective listening and accurate summary of Margaret’s ambivalence. However, empathy remains mostly content-focused and does not deeply explore underlying emotions such as embarrassment, fear, or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing alcohol risk and building a practical plan. The psychiatrist consistently guides the conversation toward safer drinking while preserving independence and pub enjoyment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Margaret’s input and builds the plan around her preferences and proposed strategies. Collaboration is somewhat uneven because the psychiatrist supplies much of the problem-solving content early in the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, avoids coercion, and respects Margaret’s low-pressure preferences. Autonomy support is slightly reduced by offering multiple strategies, a script, and an if-then plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation is elicited through practical questions, but evocation is interrupted by early advice-giving. The psychiatrist does not fully explore Margaret’s deeper values, concerns, or discrepancy with current drinking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is evidence of collaboration, but the psychiatrist also takes on an expert problem-solver role at key moments. Offering a detailed list of strategies and a suggested script makes the session partly advisor-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some useful client-owned change talk around confidence and practical steps. However, change talk is not consistently amplified or connected to deeper reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and validates Margaret’s concerns. The handling is neutral rather than skillful, with limited use of double-sided reflections or deeper ambivalence work.
