# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses nonjudgmental, respectful language and avoids lecturing or pressuring Ethan. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural rather than deeply personalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Ethan’s ambivalence and social concerns. Empathy could be stronger by exploring deeper emotional meanings such as fear of rejection or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on alcohol use in social settings. Each question builds logically toward alternatives, cues, and coping strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ethan contributes his own ideas, and the psychiatrist avoids being directive. Collaboration could be improved by explicitly co-creating the agenda and shared goals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently asks for Ethan’s preferences and self-generated solutions. Choice and ambivalence are respected without pressure to commit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out Ethan’s own motivations, cues, and coping ideas. The session avoids lecturing and helps Ethan articulate his own plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ethan is positioned as the expert on his own experience, and his ideas shape the session. Partnership could be stronger with more explicit agenda negotiation and shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes change talk around drink swaps, social cues, and coping strategies. Confidence, commitment scaling, and a concrete next step could strengthen the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ethan’s reasons to maintain the status quo are reflected without argument. The psychiatrist redirects these concerns toward safer alternatives and self-efficacy.
