# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and validates Erik’s plan without blaming him for lapses. Affirmations are supportive but somewhat formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The reflections accurately capture Erik’s ambivalence, confidence gap, and practical concerns. There is less emotional exploration of his shame, frustration, and self-comparison with younger gym-goers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on concrete gym attendance, reducing all-or-nothing thinking, and a related dietary barrier. It occasionally broadens into multiple tactical options rather than sharply narrowing around the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik’s ideas clearly shape the final plan, including time-based goals, an if-then cue, protein bar, and check-in. The psychiatrist still generates many solutions, making the process somewhat expert-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Erik’s freedom to choose and avoids pressuring him. Erik owns the plan through confidence ratings, self-identified barriers, and his own cue, “Just drive there.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective questions draw out Erik’s prior strategies, confidence level, barriers, and practical solutions. There is less deep exploration of values, identity, or deeper personal motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Erik to choose what is doable and builds on his ideas rather than imposing a fixed plan. The frequent introduction of multiple options can make the interaction feel more therapist-guided than fully peer-like.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik expresses useful ability and action talk, especially around tracking his own progress and using simple cues. The work remains more logistical than deeply exploring desire, need, reasons, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Concerns about tiredness, hunger, and missed days are handled without argument and softened through flexibility and self-compassion. Sustain talk is not always fully transformed into stronger change talk through double-sided reflections or values-based exploration.
