# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Therapist demonstrates warm, non-judgmental stance throughout, with strong moments like 'this isn't a punishment room, this is your space' and affirming Jamal's honesty about feeling like 'the easy target.' Warmth is consistent but somewhat professional rather than deeply individualized, with only a single instance of strong affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate complex reflections, particularly in capturing the social dynamics and the 'setting writes the script' insight. Could be deeper in exploring the emotional pain of feeling singled out and the specific unfairness in the DeShawn comparison, where 'I understand, can you tell me more' missed a chance to validate the precise emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintains clear focus on alcohol use connected to athlete identity, moving logically from rapport to social dynamics to the crossroads between peer connection and drinking. Could benefit from brief summaries to consolidate progress and ensure alignment, particularly after key insights like the 'setting vs. people' distinction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conversation is clearly a joint exploration with Jamal's ideas substantially shaping the session's direction. The therapist builds on and amplifies Jamal's own contributions rather than imposing external frameworks, maintaining an equal power dynamic throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support established from the opening and maintained consistently through open, non-directive questions and refusal to provide directive answers even when asked. The therapist never pressures, lectures, or implies Jamal 'should' stop drinking, respecting his perceived lack of choice in the moment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of evocative questions connecting drinking to Jamal's future goals and the 'if you could change one thing' technique, leading to clear self-identified change talk. Could be stronger with explicit exploration of athlete values/identity and use of confidence or importance rulers to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent partnership established immediately and maintained throughout, with language consistently conveying shared exploration ('I'm curious,' 'I wonder,' 'I get that'). The therapist never assumes the expert role, keeping insights and problem-solving firmly in Jamal's hands.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilful evocation of change talk through well-constructed questions that connect drinking to Jamal's own goals, with effective reflections that amplify and clarify his emerging commitments. Could push further by asking about confidence in making the identified change or exploring what would support follow-through.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective handling of sustain talk through acknowledging reality without arguing or minimizing, particularly the validation of the social cost of change as 'a real bind.' Could be slightly more active in using double-sided reflections to explicitly hold both sides of the ambivalence.
