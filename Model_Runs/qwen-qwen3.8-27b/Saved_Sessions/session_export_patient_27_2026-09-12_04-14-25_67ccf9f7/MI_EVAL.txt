# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes ambivalence without disapproving of secrecy or fear. Warmth is generally strong but sometimes formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures ambivalence, torn feelings, and safety concerns. Some emotional nuances such as shame, vulnerability, and fear of being labeled are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on disclosure to Sarah, safety, and a low-risk next step, with broader suggestions tied to the target concern. A brief collaborative summary could further strengthen strategic momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s choices substantially shape the plan, especially selecting Sarah and co-creating a low-stakes test. The psychiatrist still supplies many structured options, scripts, and safety suggestions, making it somewhat expert-guided.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options, invitations, and validation of the client’s low-risk plan strongly support freedom to choose. Direct behavioral instructions and scripts occasionally reduce the emphasis on the client generating her own choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-driven motivation by asking about fears, safety, and what would make disclosure worthwhile. It moves quickly into pros/cons, scripts, and logistics before deeper values-based or confidence-building evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative questions invite input and let the client’s preferences guide the session, such as choosing to test the water and an exit line. The interaction remains somewhat directive because of frequent concrete scripts and safety planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions like what would need to happen and which step feels doable help the client generate forward-looking, low-risk language. The psychiatrist could more consistently deepen and amplify the client’s own intrinsic motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about liking the easy vibe and not ruining the friendship is acknowledged without argument or pressure to disclose. More active double-sided reflections and pivots could further shift attention toward reasons for change.
