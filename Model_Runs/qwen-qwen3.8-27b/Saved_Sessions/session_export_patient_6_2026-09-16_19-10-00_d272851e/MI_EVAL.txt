# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful tone with validating reflections and no blame or moralizing. The stance is generally warm and supportive, though somewhat clinical and less deeply personal or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is strong reflective listening and accurate understanding of the client’s practical and motivational conflict. The empathy could be deeper by more fully exploring underlying emotions such as fear, shame, and anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing alcohol use and protecting the client’s college soccer and scholarship goals. It moves purposefully from ambivalence to a concrete, practical, client-relevant plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the session as joint problem-solving and consistently builds on the client’s own ideas. The plan is co-created, with the client’s goals and preferences central to the direction of the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client’s right to choose is strongly emphasized through language such as “I'm not here to make that judgment for you” and “moves you own.” The psychiatrist avoids pressure, imposes no formal monitoring, and supports the client’s self-directed approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently draws out the client’s own reasons for change rather than providing external advice. The evocation is effective, though it could be deeper by exploring the client’s underlying values, identity, and emotional meaning more fully.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative stance and asks for the client’s perspective throughout. The session is client-informed, but still somewhat therapist-paced and guided in structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes and reinforces the client’s own reasons for change, especially around soccer, scholarship, and avoiding being “caught.” The change talk could be further developed across desire, ability, reasons, need, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging the client’s desire for independence without arguing or minimizing. It could be more strategic in flipping some sustain talk into stronger change motivation.
