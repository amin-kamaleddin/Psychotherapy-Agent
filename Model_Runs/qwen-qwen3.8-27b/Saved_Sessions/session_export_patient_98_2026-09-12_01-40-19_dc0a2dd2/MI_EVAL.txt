# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Mateo's pain and effort without minimizing or labeling. However, the empathy sometimes shifts quickly into task-focused guidance, limiting a fully individualized acceptance score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate early reflections of exhaustion and discouragement, showing effort to understand the client's perspective. The reflections become brief and problem-solving oriented, with limited deeper or double-sided exploration of ambivalence and identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on rehab participation and pain-related barriers, guiding Mateo toward concrete, practical steps. The main limitation is occasional overemphasis on scripts and procedure rather than motivational development.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adjusts the plan to Mateo's preferences, such as using a simple one-word note. However, the counselor frequently supplies the structure, language, and options, so the session is more partially collaborative than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by validating self-care, simplifying the plan, and allowing Mateo to use or adapt the language as he wishes. The repeated offers of scripts and options create a mildly directive impression rather than a strongly autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions help Mateo identify small practical steps, but the counselor provides many strategies rather than deeply eliciting Mateo's own motivations, values, or confidence. Change talk remains limited and externally focused rather than strongly self-evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a listening stance and responds to Mateo's constraints by simplifying the plan. The session often becomes expert-driven, with the psychiatrist serving as the primary source of solutions and decision points.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps Mateo generate small change-oriented ideas and reinforces realistic commitments. There is limited systematic evocation of change talk around values, importance, confidence, or future outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively reframes several sustaining statements, such as normalizing self-care and reducing blame. Sustain talk is not consistently transformed through double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of ambivalence.
