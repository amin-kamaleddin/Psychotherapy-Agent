# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard throughout, never labeling or disapproving of Milan's drinking. Explicitly validates his perspective and gently normalizes his resistance while maintaining his dignity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are nuanced and capture both content and underlying meaning (e.g., social concern behind 'make a scene,' deeper value behind 'actually pay attention'). Progressively deepens from surface-level to more meaningful self-exploration as the session unfolds.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear progressive arc from social context to values and the film program without wandering off-topic. Could be stronger in bringing the session to a clearer landing point that connects the film program to specific next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Structured as a genuine collaboration with inclusive language ('I'm curious,' 'I'm just wondering') and each question building on Milan's previous response. The session follows Milan's lead, demonstrating true collaborative direction rather than clinician-driven extraction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Milan's perception of choice and ownership, never implying he's making a bad choice or that he should stop drinking. The autonomy support is woven naturally throughout rather than feeling like a deliberate technique.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully draws motivation from within Milan through a clear progression: evoking positives, gently evoking discrepancy, then connecting to values (the film program). Resists the urge to provide advice or information, letting Milan's own words build the case for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within Milan, using open questions that position him as the authority on his own life. The power dynamic is clearly egalitarian — curious, not in charge — with Milan's contributions substantially shaping the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws Milan from minimization to genuine self-expression ('I actually care about stuff,' 'the one thing I actually feel proud of'). Could have gone further in the final moments by explicitly reflecting back the film program as a reason for change or exploring confidence levels to solidify commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively validates sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing the 'it's fine' narrative (e.g., 'I hear you — not a big deal — and I appreciate you being straightforward'). Could have used double-sided reflections or more explicit pivots in a few instances to more clearly redirect toward change.
