# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, validating stance and reduces shame without moralizing. Positive regard is consistent but could be more explicit about the client’s inherent worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence, metaphors, and bodily experience, showing strong perspective-taking. Some reflections could go deeper into shame, fear, and humiliation before moving toward action.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing the risk of returning to crime and exploring prosocial steps. The target behavior could be stated more explicitly and emotional themes tied more directly to action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on the client’s ideas and treats planning as a joint exploration. More explicit co-construction, such as comparing or ranking options, would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids pressure, persuasion, and directive advice, supporting the client’s self-direction. More frequent explicit affirmation of choice and ownership would make autonomy support stronger.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from the client’s own values, goals, and hopes rather than supplied externally. Deeper evocation of confidence, ability, and commitment would enhance change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as the expert on his own life and invites him to define steps. Shared control and agenda could be made more explicit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session effectively elicits reasons for change, including respect, Fiona, hands-on work, and a stable future. Change talk could be developed more systematically across ability, need, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, normalization, or judgment. The counselor could more consistently pivot these moments toward the client’s values and possible change steps.
