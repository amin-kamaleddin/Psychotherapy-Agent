# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while validating the client's tiredness, ambivalence, and urges. A small limitation is that the warmth sometimes shifts quickly into problem-solving rather than deeper emotional affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers accurate and helpful reflections of the client's frustration and ambivalence. However, the empathy remains somewhat surface-level and misses deeper emotions such as shame, fear, and academic pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on excessive social media use and its impact on studying. The main limitation is that direction quickly becomes behavioral rather than MI-process-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client influences several details of the plan, showing moderate collaboration. However, the counselor remains the primary designer of interventions, making the process more clinician-led than truly co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor supports autonomy by normalizing lapses and offering choices. This is partly offset by frequent use of scripts, rules, timers, and structured guidance that feels directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks questions mainly about behavior, tasks, and urge strength. There is limited effort to evoke the client's internal motivation, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites client input and allows the client to choose or tweak options. However, frequent expert-led planning and scripting reduce the sense of equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only limited attempts to build on the client's emerging change talk. The session focuses more on designing behavior than evoking stronger motivation or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor handles sustain talk well by normalizing it and avoiding argument. However, the counselor does not effectively use double-sided reflections or values to soften sustain talk toward change.
