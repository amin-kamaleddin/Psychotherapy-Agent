# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently expresses warm, nonjudgmental validation and normalizes the client's stress. There is no criticism, minimization, or pressure, reflecting strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both the practical and emotional dimensions of the client's experience. The psychiatrist skillfully reflects the client's ambivalence, fear, hope, and desire for a boundary that is both gentle and real.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session has clear focus and momentum, narrowing from the broad childcare stress to a specific arrangement with Sarah. The progression is coherent and purposefully oriented toward a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed with the client rather than prescribed. The text message and childcare safeguards are developed step by step using the client's own language, preferences, and practical knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently supported as the decision-maker, with questions that preserve her control over what to do, what to say, and how to say it. The psychiatrist avoids directive language and respects the client's sense of choice throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client's own ideas, resources, and motivations. The core solution, details, and final wording emerge from the client rather than being introduced by the counselor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is treated as the expert on her own life, and the counselor invites her perspective to guide the work. The client's contributions directly shape the session rather than being overridden by a preselected agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client generates meaningful change talk, and the psychiatrist effectively expands on it to support planning. It could be strengthened by more explicitly evoking confidence, commitment, or values-based reasons to move forward.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk and barrier talk are handled skillfully, with concerns acknowledged without being challenged or reinforced. The session could more consistently redirect fear and risk-focused language toward hope, agency, and actionable confidence.
