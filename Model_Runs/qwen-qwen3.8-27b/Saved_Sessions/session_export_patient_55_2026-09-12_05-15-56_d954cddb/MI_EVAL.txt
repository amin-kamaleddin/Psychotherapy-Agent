# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepts the client’s choices without pressure. However, the acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper emotional attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers accurate reflections of the client’s situation and capability. These reflections are helpful but remain surface-level and miss stronger emotional undercurrents.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The conversation drifts away from the alcohol-use referral into workload, music, and routine. There is limited consistent focus on the target behavior or a collaboratively chosen priority.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites client input and frames suggestions as optional, creating a collaborative tone. However, the clinician repeatedly introduces new ideas rather than fully co-creating a shared agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently supports the client’s freedom of choice and avoids pressuring change. This autonomy support is strong but not clearly connected to a specific change process or target behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits some of the client’s own strengths, preferences, and sources of satisfaction. These efforts are useful but do not effectively draw out motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses a collaborative stance by offering options and inviting the client’s preference. The partnership is limited by repeated optional suggestions rather than a fully shared agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is very little development of change talk related to the referral target or another meaningful goal. After the initial question, the clinician does not further evoke reasons, need, desire, or ability for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client’s sustain talk is mostly reflected and accepted without being effectively softened. The clinician does not use double-sided reflection, reframing, or gentle pivoting toward values or possible change.
