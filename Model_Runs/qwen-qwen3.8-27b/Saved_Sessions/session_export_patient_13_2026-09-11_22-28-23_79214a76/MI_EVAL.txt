# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance throughout the session. However, repeated safety messaging and task-focused options occasionally make the interaction feel more procedural than fully accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective statements are present, but they often remain surface-level. The psychiatrist misses several deeper emotional themes, such as fear of the problem becoming real and conflicting needs for direction and avoidance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of seeking medical evaluation. It sometimes becomes overly task-oriented, emphasizing practical plans and risk warnings over the client’s underlying ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative moments, such as offering options and inviting preference. However, the psychiatrist often supplies the content of the conversation, leaving the client’s expertise and voice underutilized.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms the client’s choice to wait and avoids forcing immediate action. Repeatedly presenting multiple options and task prompts, though, can feel pressuring and may reduce the client’s sense of autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited, as the psychiatrist frequently provides information, scripts, and options instead of drawing out the client’s own motivations. There are several missed opportunities to explore values, confidence, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is used, but the psychiatrist often takes on an expert-driven role by supplying scripts, plans, and safety guidance. The client’s contributions do not consistently shape the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change-oriented language, such as recognizing she should get checked. However, these statements are not consistently reflected, amplified, or used to strengthen the client’s motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses substantial sustain talk, and the psychiatrist acknowledges it without arguing. However, the response is often to add more options and information rather than using MI-consistent strategies to soften resistance.
