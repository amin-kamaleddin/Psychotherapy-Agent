# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, offering clear affirmations of Elias’s effort and plan. Some praise focused more on the plan’s practicality than on normalizing Elias’s shame and emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured some ambivalence and boundary-setting but often summarized content rather than deeper emotional undertones. Loneliness, fear of judgment, shame, and self-compassion were not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on maintaining recovery from alcohol and pill use, especially Friday nights and the next 24–48 hours. Each question moved systematically toward concrete, relevant harm-reduction and relapse-prevention steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist built on Elias’s ideas and integrated his choices into the final plan, including the walk, key hiding, beer disposal, pharmacy drop box, alarm, and texts. The style remained somewhat structured and options-driven rather than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invited choice and avoided pressure, using open questions and revising language to sound like Elias. A few explicit statements that Elias owns the decision and may choose not to act without failing would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently drew out Elias’s internal reasons for sobriety, confidence, and self-efficacy through scaling and open questions. Evocation could be stronger by fully exploring key values and self-compassion before moving to external planning, especially around not being ruined.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elias clearly contributed to the session, choosing steps and reshaping scripts, alarm labels, and texts. The interaction was collaborative but still somewhat counselor-guided because of frequent polished options and structured next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked client-generated reasons for change, such as protecting his children, choosing recovery, and not sleeping next to pills. More amplification and consolidation of the strongest change talk would have moved it to a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing or reinforcing sustain talk and validated some ambivalence, including motivation feeling external and not wanting his sister as caretaker. He missed key opportunities to honor both sides of ambivalence, especially Elias’s fear of judgment and final statement about not being ruined.
