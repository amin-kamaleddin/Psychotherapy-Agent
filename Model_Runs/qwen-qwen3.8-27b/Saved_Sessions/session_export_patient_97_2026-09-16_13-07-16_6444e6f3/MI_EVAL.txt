# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and uses affirming language such as 'That’s a lot to carry.' The warmth is solid but somewhat formulaic, and a few phrases feel mildly leading.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Mateo’s conflict and pressure, such as being 'torn' between stability and his old crew. Empathy is clear but remains more cognitive and behavior-focused than emotionally nuanced.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing risky contact and supporting re-entry goals. The counselor consistently moves the conversation toward concrete, relevant action steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo is invited to choose, modify, and personalize options, which supports collaboration. However, the psychiatrist supplies much of the plan, limiting the degree of joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and asks for input, but also uses repeated directive prompts to select and commit to actions. The style is more directive than fully autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and scaling questions elicit some client-generated motivation and values. The counselor often shifts quickly into planning, which keeps evocation somewhat superficial.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of client input, such as choosing what feels doable and tweaking the text. Overall, the session is heavily guided by the psychiatrist’s structure and agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo expresses desire, capability, and reasons for change through the scaling questions and action choices. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mateo expresses ambivalence and fear of slipping, but the psychiatrist often pivots to the next concrete step rather than exploring the sustain talk. Double-sided reflections and deeper validation of his pull toward old associations would strengthen this criterion.
