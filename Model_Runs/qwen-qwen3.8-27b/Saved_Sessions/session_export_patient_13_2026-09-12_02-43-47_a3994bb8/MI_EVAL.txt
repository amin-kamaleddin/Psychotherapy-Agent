# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect and nonjudgment, validating the client’s fear, avoidance, and self-doubt. However, the acceptance can feel somewhat formulaic rather than deeply individualized or emotionally affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s ambivalence, worry, and preference for safety. Deeper empathy would be needed for shame, self-criticism, and fear of making the problem real.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of contacting a clinician and taking a practical next step. It is more task-oriented than strategically MI-guided, especially near the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates in choosing message options and incorporating the client’s wording. However, the psychiatrist still does much of the cognitive work, such as drafting the message and proposing plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices, low-pressure language, and respect for the client’s refusal to commit are strong. The psychiatrist could more explicitly affirm that the client’s timing, wording, and decision are her own strengths.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are used early, but much of the session relies on menus, scripts, and practical suggestions. More strategic evocation of the client’s own reasons, values, and confidence would strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and uses the client’s own words, which supports partnership. However, frequent expert role-taking, such as drafting solutions, can undermine the client’s expertise on her own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces some change talk, such as seeing the draft as doing something. The psychiatrist does not consistently use strategic MI methods to amplify or build on this motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and responds with understanding. More strategic MI methods are needed to soften avoidance-related sustain talk and connect it to the client’s values.
