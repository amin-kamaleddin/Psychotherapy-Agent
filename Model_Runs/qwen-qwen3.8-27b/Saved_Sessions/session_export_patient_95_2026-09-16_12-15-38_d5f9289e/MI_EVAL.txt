# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a warm, nonjudgmental, and validating tone while respecting Maria's experience. Acceptance is strong, though it is somewhat procedural and focuses more on her pain than explicitly affirming her broader worth, strengths, or capacity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Maria's subjective distress and fear of “snapping.” Empathy is strong but not perfect because some reflections feel repetitive and miss the full emotional complexity of her final ambiguous statement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains focus on Maria's suicidal distress, hopelessness, and safety without wandering into unrelated topics. The direction is generally clear, though the session can feel more like a series of safety checks and coping options than a coherent MI progression.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Maria's input, allowing her choices to shape the session. Collaboration is strong, but it remains somewhat structured by the psychiatrist's crisis-management agenda and safety-focused options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently supports Maria's autonomy by offering choices, avoiding directives, and respecting her resistance to skills or breathing exercises. Autonomy support is solid, but the language could more explicitly reinforce Maria's ownership and expertise in deciding what helps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some effort to evoke Maria's own experience by asking what feels heavy and what has helped before. Evocation is present but remains somewhat surface-level, with limited exploration of her deeper values, internal motivations, or reasons for staying.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by giving Maria choices and honoring her preferences throughout the session. Partnership is strong, though the session still feels somewhat clinician-led and safety-structured rather than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk, including Maria's reluctance to “give up” and her connection to her mom. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or reflect back this change talk to strengthen Maria's sense of agency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing Maria's despair and uses some balanced reflections that pair pain with a reason for staying. Softening sustain talk is present but not consistently skillful, as hopelessness remains the dominant focus without more active pivots toward change.
