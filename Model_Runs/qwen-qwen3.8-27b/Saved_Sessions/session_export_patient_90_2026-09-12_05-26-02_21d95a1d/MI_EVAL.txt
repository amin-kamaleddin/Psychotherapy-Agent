# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment through affirmations and validation of Marcus’s situation. It is not a 5 because acceptance sometimes quickly shifts into problem-solving rather than sustained emotional attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate reflections of Marcus’s overwhelm and the emotional significance of his dog. However, empathy is often followed rapidly by practical suggestions, limiting deeper exploration of his inner experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on asthma management and narrows to a specific, actionable issue around the dog in the bedroom. It is not a 5 because the focus becomes somewhat repetitive without broader pacing or connection to long-term values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and incorporates Marcus’s ideas, such as the hoodie and fan. However, the frequent provision of detailed solution lists shifts the role toward expert advice rather than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes Marcus’s choice, control, and ability to proceed at his own pace. It is not a 5 because the high volume of suggestions may subtly steer Marcus toward preferred solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits practical next steps but does not deeply explore Marcus’s underlying motivations, values, or reasons for change. More evocation of personal meaning and self-efficacy would strengthen this criterion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Inclusive language and invitation of input support a collaborative stance, and Marcus’s ideas are acknowledged. However, the psychiatrist often adopts an advisory role, reducing the sense of true equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk, such as Marcus agreeing to try the shirt first. However, these statements are not consistently reflected, amplified, or developed, leaving change talk underexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus expresses significant sustain talk around his dog, and the psychiatrist acknowledges some of this pain. However, the psychiatrist does not effectively use double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to transform sustain talk into change talk.
