# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a warm, validating, and nonjudgmental stance with frequent normalizing statements. The language is supportive but somewhat formulaic and occasionally agenda-driven, which slightly limits depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is generally accurate and captures the client’s overwhelm, pressure, fear of judgment, and painful “broken” self-message. The clinician sometimes moves quickly from reflection to options and misses opportunities to explore deeper shame and identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on diabetes-related self-management stress without drifting into unrelated topics. A tighter collaborative focus on one specific target behavior or concern would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some responsiveness and shared input, including willingness to stay quiet and follow the client’s lead. The psychiatrist still supplies many options and frames the session in a somewhat counselor-led way rather than fully co-creating direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is frequently honored, especially when the client asks for space and rejects suggestions. Some repeated choice questions subtly reassert control over pacing and can feel pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak, with limited effort to draw out the client’s values, motivations, self-efficacy, or reasons for change. The session stays largely focused on burden and task selection rather than eliciting client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, checks preferences, and follows the client’s lead in some moments. The interaction remains partially counselor-led due to frequent structured options and practical framing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal effort to evoke DARN-C change talk, desire, ability, reasons, or need for change. Questions mainly address difficulty, preference, and task selection rather than motivation or personal values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with resistance and acknowledges the client’s burden. The sustain talk is not consistently softened or redirected toward change through double-sided or exploring reflections.
