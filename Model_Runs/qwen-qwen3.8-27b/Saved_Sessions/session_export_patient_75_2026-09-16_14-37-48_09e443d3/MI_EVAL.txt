# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Linda’s concerns about dependency and being dismissed. The acceptance is strong, though it could be deepened by more explicit normalization of ambivalence and more individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is good reflective listening, especially in capturing Linda’s ambivalence between safety and fear of being “just scared.” Empathy could be stronger by exploring the emotional pain and invalidation behind her self-doubt and her husband’s dismissal.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior of inhaler use and asthma management. Each question progressively narrows toward a concrete, actionable plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with inclusive language and a shared monitoring plan shaped by Linda’s suggestions. It could be stronger if the psychiatrist more explicitly negotiated agenda-setting and invited Linda to prioritize topics.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda is able to create her own plan and rules, showing good client ownership. The psychiatrist could further strengthen autonomy support by more clearly affirming that Linda is free to choose, modify, or reject the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently draws out Linda’s own motivation, goals, and problem-solving ideas rather than lecturing. Evocation could be strengthened by reducing counselor-supplied interpretations and eliciting Linda’s own confidence and evidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and invites Linda’s perspective throughout. Partnership could be elevated by more explicitly affirming Linda as the expert on her own body and experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session effectively elicits concrete change talk, such as using an alarm, creating a rule, and deciding when to call. It could be stronger by more systematically evoking the full range of change talk, including confidence and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk respectfully and reframes concerns in terms of control, data, and safety. Softening could be enhanced by more strategic pivots toward Linda’s values, positive outcomes, or personal evidence for change.
