# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, non-judgmental, and respectful throughout; never expresses disapproval of smoking. Could be deeper in explicitly affirming Natalia's worth as a parent and person independent of her smoking behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy capturing both cognitive and emotional layers (e.g., the cigarette as the 'one thing you can control,' the identity-level weight of change). Each reflection advances understanding rather than simply paraphrasing, without being formulaic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation with a logical progression from disclosure through identity to values-based motivation. Occasionally diffuse (e.g., the mother/identity exploration could have been more quickly tied back to the target behavior) and could use more strategic summarization at transition points.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely collaborative throughout, with the client leading content and the psychiatrist leading process. The power balance is clearly equal, with no moments of assumed expertise or uninvited redirection.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates masterful autonomy support through explicit permission-giving ('You don't have to decide anything right now') and strategic questions that reframe change as the client's own choice. Natalia's final statement about wanting it to be 'hers' is a direct result of this consistent stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never provides a single reason for change, piece of medical information, or suggestion. Every insight in the session is drawn from Natalia, with the progression from ambivalence to 'that's not the real me' being entirely client-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within Natalia, using 'I'm curious' as a collaborative frame and explicitly crediting her self-awareness. The psychiatrist never steps into the expert-advisor role, even when directly asked about next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions directly evoke desire, ability, and planning language (e.g., 'what would wanting even look like to you?'). By the end, Natalia generates substantial change talk on her own, with every insight emerging from the client rather than the clinician.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully using gentle discrepancy-based responses and redirects to underlying needs without arguing. Could be more maximally strategic with explicit double-sided reflections in a few key moments (e.g., 'On one hand, the cigarette is your survival tool, and on the other, it's the thing that doesn't feel like you').
