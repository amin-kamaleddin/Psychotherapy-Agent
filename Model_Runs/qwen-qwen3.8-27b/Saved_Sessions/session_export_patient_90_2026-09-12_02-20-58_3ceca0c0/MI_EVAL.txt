# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally shows respect, validation, and nonjudgment by normalizing Marcus’s exhaustion and worries. Some warmth feels procedural or slightly premature, such as praising the step before the text is sent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor provides accurate reflections of Marcus’s stress, cost fears, and need for relief. These are helpful but often stay at the surface and miss deeper emotions such as humiliation, fear of burdening others, or attachment to the dog.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear, clinically appropriate focus on improving asthma management through albuterol refill access and cost. The direction becomes quite task-driven and does not consistently connect the step to Marcus’s broader values or self-management goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is real collaboration in creating a practical text message using Marcus’s preferred wording, medication, pharmacy, and tone. The collaboration is mostly limited to this one task rather than being fully integrated across the therapeutic agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports choice by offering options and allowing Marcus to decide whether to send, tweak, or delay the message. Autonomy support could be stronger by reducing directive task focus and more explicitly affirming Marcus’s ownership, especially when he expresses doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses some MI-consistent eliciting moves, including an opening question, urgency ranking, and confidence scaling. The session leans heavily into practical problem-solving and does not deeply elicit Marcus’s own reasons, values, or self-efficacy around asthma management.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and uses some collaborative language when narrowing down options. However, the psychiatrist frequently generates options, drafts the message, and carries most of the agenda-setting, making the partnership uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are moments of change talk, such as Marcus saying he needs the inhaler and that taking a small step matters. The counselor does not consistently amplify, reflect, or expand this change talk through stronger MI-style questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing and appropriately normalizes Marcus’s concerns about cost, judgment, and the dog. However, sustain talk is often narrowed into task management rather than being strategically softened through double-sided reflections or exploration of ambivalence.
