# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent respect, warmth, and nonjudgmental regard while validating Alessia’s values. Acceptance is slightly less than optimal because some affirmations are lightly evaluative and uncertainty is not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Alessia’s perspective, emotional meaning, and core need for a trustworthy verbal explanation. A few reflections are interpretive or do not fully explore the depth of her frustration about doing the “detective work.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on medication safety and actionable next steps. It progresses logically from concerns to prioritization, question drafting, and planning how to request clarification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with Alessia’s choices shaping the agenda, question wording, and sequence of actions. The psychiatrist builds on her contributions rather than imposing a standard script.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invites preferences and avoids pressuring Alessia to act or accept a specific interpretation. Autonomy support is limited by the more directive safety recommendation and lack of explicit reaffirmation of her decision-making ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended, client-centered questions effectively elicit Alessia’s values, priorities, and plans. The score is below 5 because the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen intrinsic motivation or explicitly link her values to readiness/confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Alessia’s lead and invites input on pacing, wording, and next steps. Partnership is slightly limited by a brief expert/directive safety recommendation and no explicit statement that she is the expert on her own decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes change talk by inviting Alessia to generate questions, priorities, and phrasing. Change talk could be strengthened by more explicit amplification, consolidation, or confidence/commitment follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally reframes concerns and redirects toward practical next steps rather than reinforcing hesitation. Softening could be stronger with more strategic double-sided reflections or explicit linkage of concerns to Alessia’s capacity to act.
