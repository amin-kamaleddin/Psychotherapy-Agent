# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Mai’s perspective without blame or pressure. The stance is warm and supportive, though it does not fully reach the level of individualized, emotionally attuned acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately captures Mai’s ambivalence and key concerns. Some reflections are accurate but repetitive and do not always deepen into the underlying emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on medication adherence, timing, and the need for a clear explanation. However, the pacing is repetitive and the conversation does not move clearly toward a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Mai as an equal partner and frequently invites her input and preferred wording. The collaboration is client-centered but does not fully integrate Mai’s contributions into a jointly developed plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly frames Mai as the decision-maker and avoids coercive or directive language. Autonomy support is strong, though it would be stronger with clearer emphasis on Mai’s right to choose not to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses several open-ended questions that encourage Mai to articulate her own concerns and conditions for change. The evocation is present but remains somewhat superficial and does not consistently draw out deeper values or motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative, non-authoritarian stance and invites Mai to identify what would make the plan feel like hers. The partnership is respectful but somewhat uneven, with repeated similar questions that can feel circular.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai offers several change-related statements, and the psychiatrist reflects some of this readiness. However, change talk is not consistently amplified, summarized, or developed into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai’s hesitation and concerns are acknowledged without argument or reinforcement. The psychiatrist does not consistently use MI-specific strategies to reframe or move beyond the sustain talk.
