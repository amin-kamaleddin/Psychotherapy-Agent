# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses nonjudgmental, respectful language and accepts the goal of reducing weekday drinking without quitting. It falls short of 5 because the warmth is somewhat procedural and lacks deeper individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s ambivalence and practical concerns, helping her feel heard. However, empathy is mostly content-focused and misses emotional texture such as fear of judgment or feeling fake.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing problematic weekday drinking while preserving social life, work, and study. The conversation consistently moves toward specific, workable goals without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is built around the client’s ideas, such as the pantry rule, one bottle, 90-minute limit, and refusal line. It is not a 5 because the counselor often supplies menus and scripts, making it feel less fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly asks which option feels doable, respects limits, and does not shame the client for possible setbacks. More directive offers of options and scripts prevent a top score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks some open questions and confidence checks, but often moves quickly into practical options before deeply exploring the client’s own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, inviting client input and affirming her choices rather than imposing a fixed plan. It becomes slightly counselor-driven when multiple structured options and scripts are offered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces some commitment language, but confidence remains low and the session is more intervention-focused than evocative. More importance, need, ability, and self-efficacy evocation is needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accepts the client’s desire not to quit but does not consistently use double-sided reflections or gently redirect sustain talk toward change. Responses often pivot to coping strategies rather than exploring ambivalence.
