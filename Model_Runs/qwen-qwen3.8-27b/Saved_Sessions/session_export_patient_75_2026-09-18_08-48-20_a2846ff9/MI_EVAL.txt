# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, with statements like 'You're not being unreasonable at all' directly countering self-blame. Fear and determination are validated simultaneously without judgment or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate perspective-taking with complex reflections that go beyond surface content to underlying needs (e.g., naming the double-bind and the guilt cycle). The final reflection linking her self-insight to hope demonstrates deep attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions consistently evoke DARN-C change talk, and the client's own words are amplified back to her. Session ends with a concrete, client-generated commitment directly elicited by the psychiatrist's questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully co-constructed session where the psychiatrist builds on the client's language and asks for input at every decision point. No top-down moments; the final preparation question exemplifies collaborative problem-solving between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All action steps are framed as the client's own decisions with no 'you should' or 'you need to' language. Genuine choices are offered with no implied right answer, and the client's internal answers are reinforced as her own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within the client through open-ended questions and reflections rather than external rationale. The client articulates her own commitment ('I'll write down my questions and make the appointment'), which is the gold standard of evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly positioned within the client through phrases like 'I want you to really hear yourself' and 'Let's figure this out together.' The psychiatrist consistently points back to the client's own words and insights rather than positioning as the authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well with skillful reframing and redirection toward the client's own goals, but lacks explicit double-sided reflections that would further validate while softening resistance. The redirection could be more active in reducing focus on reasons to maintain the status quo.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Direction is strong with a logical progression from ambivalence to commitment, but the middle section slightly broadens beyond the specific target behavior of consistent inhaler use. The final commitment is well-directed, though tighter focus throughout would strengthen the session.
