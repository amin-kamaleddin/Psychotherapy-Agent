# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the client’s choices throughout. Affirmations were generally supportive but sometimes felt formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflected the client’s goals and practical concerns. However, the emotional sting behind the client’s frustration and fear of failure was not fully addressed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on increasing physical activity. The conversation moved consistently toward a specific, actionable plan without unnecessary drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offered options and invited the client’s input when shaping the plan. The interaction was collaborative, though the counselor still led the structure and sequencing fairly strongly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was repeatedly given choices, and the plan included options to stop early, keep check-ins brief, and avoid pressure. This strongly supported the client’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor drew out some change talk related to energy, strength, and confidence. The exploration remained somewhat surface-level and did not fully deepen the client’s underlying motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treated the client as an expert on her own life and incorporated her preferences into the plan. The pacing and question sequence occasionally made the interaction feel more interview-like than fully co-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked effective evocative questions and the client produced strong change talk. However, the counselor did not consistently amplify or deepen that change talk before moving into logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s concerns about overcommitment, reminders, and pressure were acknowledged and incorporated into the plan. More active reframing of these cautions into change-supportive language could have strengthened this criterion.
