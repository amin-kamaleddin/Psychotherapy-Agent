# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and supportive, validating David’s motivation without judgment. However, some responses feel formulaic and could be more individualized and warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of David’s concerns and ambivalence. These reflections are often surface-level and miss deeper emotional themes such as loneliness, shame, and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation, medication planning, and high-risk triggers. At times, the conversation becomes more medical-task-oriented than MI-process-oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites David’s input and adapts the plan based on his choices, such as medication, quit date, and role-play scenario. However, the psychiatrist still provides a lot of the plan content rather than fully eliciting David’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David is repeatedly given choices and his concerns about feeling trapped are acknowledged. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming that all final decisions remain his.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evoking questions about motivation, confidence, and prior attempts. However, the session shifts into information-giving too quickly and misses opportunities to deepen David’s own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction feels collaborative, with David meaningfully shaping the plan. The psychiatrist could further strengthen partnership by more explicitly positioning David as the expert on his own life and values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David offers clear change talk, such as wanting to quit, feeling tired of waiting, and setting a quit date. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reflect, or expand these statements into stronger motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges David’s concerns about medication, cravings, and relapse without arguing. However, sustain talk is not consistently reframed or redirected toward autonomy, values, and change.
