# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, normalizing Alexei's embarrassment and avoiding moralizing about the alcohol use. The warmth is strong but sometimes becomes tied to performance goals, such as soccer and scholarship, rather than fully unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor reflects Alexei's main concerns about losing his spot, parental trust, and peer pressure. Deeper identity-related feelings, such as shame or fear of being seen as "the kid who got caught," are less explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays strongly focused on avoiding alcohol and protecting Alexei's soccer and scholarship goals. The discussion moves clearly from risk awareness to confidence, strategy, and concrete rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The refusal plan is co-created around Alexei's preferences, including the "bus" text and a short, low-key exit line. The counselor still supplies many options and rationales, making the process somewhat counselor-guided.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites choice, respects Alexei's boundaries, and offers multiple alternatives rather than directing him. Some language still steers him toward the plan, which prevents a fully autonomous stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The importance/confidence scales and open questions help Alexei generate his own strategies and connect them to his values. The counselor could further deepen client-generated motivation by relying less on external reasons and more on Alexei's own change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Alexei's input clearly shapes the session, from rejecting the involvement of his dad or coach to choosing the "bus" signal and preferred wording. The counselor still provides many concrete options and follows a fairly structured path.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some change talk and moves toward a concrete plan, but does not consistently expand Alexei's desire, ability, need, or commitment language. More MI-style reflections would help strengthen his internal motivation before rehearsal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing and normalizes Alexei's hesitation, which keeps sustain talk from escalating. More double-sided reflections would better honor his resistance while gently redirecting him toward change.
