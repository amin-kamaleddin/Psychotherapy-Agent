# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Isabella’s avoidance without blame. The warmth is somewhat procedural, with fewer moments of individualized affirmation or unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers competent but surface-level reflections, especially early in the session. Deeper emotional content, such as fear of being noticed and the cost of avoidance, is underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and follows a clear progression from concern to barrier identification to a small action. The main limitation is a quick shift toward action planning rather than continued MI-style exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers multiple choices, allowing the client to meaningfully shape the plan. However, much of the content is clinician-generated, so the client often selects from supplied options rather than generating her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through repeated emphasis on choice, no pressure, and permission not to promise. The score is limited because the sequence of optional tasks can still subtly imply that some action is expected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening questions elicit relevant concerns and preferences, but the session becomes more didactic and task-focused. The clinician does not consistently draw out Isabella’s own values, motivations, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and respects the client’s input, but the interaction often feels like the clinician is generating options for the client to select. There is limited explicit acknowledgment of Isabella as the expert on her own life and readiness.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes and affirms some tentative change talk, including the desire to feel less alone and small steps like writing a message. These statements are not consistently expanded through deeper importance, confidence, or values-based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing the client’s reasons for staying home, and this is a clear strength. However, sustain talk is not consistently redirected using double-sided reflections or ambivalence-based exploration.
