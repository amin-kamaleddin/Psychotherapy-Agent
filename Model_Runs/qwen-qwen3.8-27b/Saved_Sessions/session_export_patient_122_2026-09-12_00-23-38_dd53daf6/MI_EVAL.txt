# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and offers supportive statements such as “That sounds exhausting and really frustrating.” However, the warmth remains somewhat procedural, and the session often shifts quickly into problem-solving rather than consistently deepening acceptance of Sofia as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful reflections, such as acknowledging that moving can upend routines and that Sofia wants something “tiny, doable, and not performative.” However, the empathy often stays at the behavioral level and does not fully explore the deeper self-criticism, fear of failure, or inner critic she expresses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and organized focus on a specific target behavior, building a concrete plan around a tiny post-meal practice, fallback, and reminder. The main limitation is that the direction becomes heavily implementation-focused and somewhat prescriptive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and uses collaborative language such as “Would you like to co-create a very small weekly plan now?” and “Which fallback feels least awful to try for a week?” However, collaboration is uneven because the therapist frequently supplies the framework, options, and implementation strategies for Sofia to choose from.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several autonomy-supportive moments, including allowing Sofia to choose the fallback, reminder method, and sticky-note wording. However, autonomy support is inconsistent, especially early in the session when the therapist gives more directive guidance about micro-habits, anchors, and preparation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is the weakest area, as the psychiatrist mainly elicits decisions and preferences rather than Sofia’s intrinsic reasons for change. There is little effort to explore her values, importance of change, confidence, or the deeper meaning behind statements like not wanting “another project I’m failing at.”

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some partnership by inviting Sofia to choose among options and incorporating her preference to change “Fork-down reminder” to “Fork down?” However, the therapist often assumes the role of expert planner, offering specific strategies and limiting the experience of true co-construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia does express willingness and commitment, such as agreeing to wait until lunch and trying the sticky note for one week. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently develop or amplify deeper change talk related to values, importance, or confidence in maintaining the plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively handles Sofia’s concerns about failure, alarms, and added work without arguing or reinforcing the fear of failure. Reframes such as “No guilt,” “I’m restarting, not failing,” and “That’s enough” help reduce all-or-nothing thinking and emotional weight.
