# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, consistently avoids lecturing, and normalizes the patient’s stress. Repeated role-play suggestions after the patient says it feels pressuring slightly weaken unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers helpful reflections on stress and the desire for quick relief. Deeper reflections of ambivalence, fear of social rejection, family pressure, and shame are missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on marijuana use, stress, cost, and social situations. The direction is diluted by frequent shifts into practical scripts, tactics, and coping strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and validates the patient’s ideas, showing partial collaboration. The interaction remains uneven because the clinician remains the primary source of content and solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses noncommanding language and affirms the patient’s self-chosen “not buying for a week” plan. Autonomy support is inconsistent because options and scripts continue to be supplied even after the patient says role-play feels pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicit practical stressors and choices from the patient. Evocation remains procedural rather than motivational, with limited exploration of values, goals, self-efficacy, or discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and invites input, creating some shared decision-making. The session remains largely expert-led due to rapid provision of scripts and coping tools.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms the patient’s spontaneous change-related idea to avoid buying for a week. The session does not systematically evoke or expand DARN-C change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk but does not actively reframe it toward values or discrepancy. Treating “cutting back” and “cost-managing” as equivalent endpoints may unintentionally validate maintaining use.
