# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language to create a safe and affirming environment. Acceptance could be slightly deeper by more explicitly normalizing the client’s relapse and cravings.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate and nuanced reflective listening, capturing both the client’s stress-related cravings and emotional ambivalence. Reflections move beyond surface content to validate underlying feelings and values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation, specific triggers, and practical next steps. Questions are strategically guided toward medication preferences, high-risk situations, and confidence in the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the session as joint problem-solving and builds on the client’s ideas. Strategies such as the morning routine and backup plan are co-constructed rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces the client’s ownership of decisions and avoids imposing advice. Phrases like “What do you think?” and “What feels doable?” support the client’s sense of agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client’s own reasons for change by asking them to imagine specific scenarios and desired outcomes. Evocation could be strengthened with more varied techniques, such as scaling questions, to deepen self-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly honors the client as the expert and actively invites their input to shape the session. The power-sharing dynamic is clear, with the client’s ideas meaningfully guiding the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions frequently prompt the client to articulate motivations, goals, and confidence in change. The psychiatrist could more deliberately amplify or summarize the client’s change talk to further strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles barriers effectively by acknowledging concerns without endorsing them and then reframing them into client-centered strategies. Minor improvement is possible by more clearly distancing the response from reinforcing limitations.
