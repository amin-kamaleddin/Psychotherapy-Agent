# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Evelyn’s choices. Acceptance could be strengthened with more explicit, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of ambivalence, safety concerns, and the meaning of the pub. Empathy could improve by directly reflecting Evelyn’s minimization and irritation earlier and avoiding overly interpretive guesses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on alcohol use and safer evening behavior. It moves logically from ambivalence to a small, client-defined plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session feels joint and responsive, with Evelyn shaping the plan. Collaboration is slightly weakened when the therapist introduces plan ideas or repeats a question after Evelyn’s response.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice using language such as “on your terms” and “gentle trial.” Autonomy support could be more explicit by repeatedly affirming that no plan is required.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evelyn is encouraged to generate her own reasons and criteria for change. Evocation could be stronger by relying more consistently on open questions and scaling rather than therapist-supplied plan themes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evelyn is treated as the expert, and her ideas shape the session. Partnership could be enhanced by more explicit power sharing, such as asking her to set the agenda or confirm focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively elicit Evelyn’s own reasons, plans, and confidence statements. The psychiatrist could further strengthen evocation by more consistently amplifying change talk and scaling confidence/importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and uses double-sided reflections to reduce defensiveness. Some sustain talk, such as “it’s not a big deal,” could be softened more explicitly with direct validation.
