# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, respectful, and affirming tone throughout the session. Affirmations were frequent but sometimes formulaic, leaving room for deeper, more individualized validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client’s perspective and planning concerns. However, emotional depth could have been stronger by exploring the feelings beneath statements such as worry and ownership.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on a clear target behavior with a logical, step-by-step progression. There was no drift, and the conversation moved effectively from values exploration to concrete action planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was clearly co-created, with the client selecting, modifying, and personalizing multiple elements. The psychiatrist’s provision of specific scripts and techniques slightly shifted the balance toward the expert role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently framed suggestions as options and supported the client’s decision-making. Offering exact scripts could have further reduced autonomy by limiting the client’s opportunity to generate her own wording.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively drew out the client’s own motivations, values, and solutions rather than providing external reasons for change. Open questions and scaling questions consistently strengthened the client’s internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction conveyed a collaborative, non-prescriptive stance with the client making all key decisions. Slightly greater explicit acknowledgment of the client’s own expertise would strengthen the sense of full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicited change talk through well-designed questions and scaling. Reinforcing and reflecting back the client’s change statements could have been more deliberate and strategic.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal and handled appropriately, with concerns validated without dismissal. The conversation was consistently redirected toward agency, solutions, and action.
