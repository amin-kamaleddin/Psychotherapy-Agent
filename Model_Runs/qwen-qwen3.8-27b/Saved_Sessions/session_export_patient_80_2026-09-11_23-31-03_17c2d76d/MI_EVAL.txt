# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and frequently affirmed the client’s progress. However, some key emotional concerns were acknowledged but not deeply reflected.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was adequate perspective-taking, especially around practical concerns such as fatigue and “earned” drinking. Empathy often remained surface-level and shifted quickly into problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on reducing alcohol use and managing anger, especially in relation to the client’s partner. There was some drift toward skills coaching, but the focus remained clinically relevant.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited the client to choose options and incorporated his preferences. However, much of the structure and content came from the clinician rather than the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client was given choices and asked for preferences, which supported autonomy moderately. However, the psychiatrist also introduced fairly specific plans and scripts that felt directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered advice and techniques rather than consistently eliciting the client’s own motivations and values. The client’s reasons for change were present but not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client was included in decisions and made choices throughout the session. However, the psychiatrist often adopted an expert or coach role, limiting full collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offered meaningful change talk, but the psychiatrist rarely expanded or amplified it. Few evocative questions were used to strengthen the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided challenging the client’s sustain talk and responded supportively. However, there was limited use of active MI strategies to redirect sustain talk toward change.
