# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the harm-reduction goal. However, the warmth is occasionally procedural and could more deeply acknowledge the client's emotional weight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides adequate, practical empathic tracking of the client's immediate concerns. However, reflections remain mostly surface-level, missing opportunities to explore deeper underlying emotional pain and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear focus on practical safety steps and respects the client's boundaries. However, the pacing occasionally relies on repetitive, instructive lists rather than a guided MI process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist and client effectively co-construct and modify the action plan. However, the session lacks consistent, explicit equal-partner language to fully frame them as a two-way expert partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers multiple options and respectfully accepts the client's refusals. However, the inclusion of directive advice and lists slightly detracts from a more fully autonomous, elicitation-first approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions and choices to allow the client to articulate their readiness. However, the session is largely information-focused, with limited use of scaling, values-based questions, or deep elicitation of motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist allows the client's preferences to meaningfully guide the session's direction. However, the psychiatrist still functions somewhat as an information source rather than using explicit language to emphasize the client as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's readiness and plan are affirmed, but the psychiatrist does not consistently expand or cultivate this change talk. More evocative questions and strategic amplification of the client's confidence would have strengthened this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids pushing for abstinence or challenging the client's desire to keep using. However, the approach is passively avoidant rather than actively using double-sided reflections to pivot toward the client's safety values.
