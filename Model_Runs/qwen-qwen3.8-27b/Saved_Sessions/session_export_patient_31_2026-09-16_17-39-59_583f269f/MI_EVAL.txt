# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, nonjudgment, and acknowledgment of David’s concern about being told he is doing something wrong. Warmth is solid, but it would benefit from more individualized affirmation of his strengths, clarity, and self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate, complex reflections that track David’s ambivalence about protecting his break while reducing daytime smoking. Deeper affective empathy could be added around fear of losing control, exhaustion, and self-blame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on managing daytime smoking and exploring practical NRT or reduction options. The progression from broad ambivalence to specific cues, behaviors, and the walk-in moment is strategic and well organized.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor works with David’s practical constraints and adapts suggestions such as keeping the gum optional and stored in his bag. The collaboration is strong, though the agenda remains somewhat counselor-guided rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through language that emphasizes choice, control, and the optionality of the plan. The counselor avoids pressure to quit and repeatedly validates David’s freedom to decide what is workable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicits David’s own reasons, goals, and strategies rather than supplying reasons for change. David articulates his own motivations around protecting the break, reducing daytime smoking, and managing urges.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** David is treated as the expert on his own life, work environment, and practical constraints. His ideas substantially shape the session, and the counselor builds on his contributions without imposing an expert agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively evokes change talk by inviting David to generate his own reasons, ideas, and plans. It would be strengthened by more consistently amplifying, summarizing, and building on that change talk, especially around confidence and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor handles sustain talk well by normalizing and reframing resistant statements without arguing or advising. More strategic use of double-sided reflections and explicit ambivalence exploration would bring this closer to a 5.
