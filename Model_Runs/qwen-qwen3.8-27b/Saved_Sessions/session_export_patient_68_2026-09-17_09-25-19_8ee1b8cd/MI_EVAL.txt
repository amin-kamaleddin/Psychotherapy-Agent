# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Kevin’s social values and pushback. Warmth is sometimes more cognitive than deeply affirming, and the final concern about feeling weak is not directly addressed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence, embarrassment, and identity concerns. However, some reflections remain surface-level and miss the deeper humiliation or fear in the final statement about feeling weak.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol-related injury risk while preserving the social experience. The target behavior could be consolidated more clearly and linked more explicitly to Kevin’s deeper values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a joint problem-solving stance and frequently builds on Kevin’s ideas. The collaboration is strong but could be more client-led, as the therapist still offers many options and menus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kevin’s choice and control are emphasized, including not pushing him to quit. The counselor could better address his uncertainty about making a commitment without feeling weak.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of Kevin’s own warnings, plans, and client-generated cues. Evocation is limited by overreliance on therapist-supplied options and by missing the final opportunity to explore confidence, values, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Kevin’s input and avoids a directive expert role. The offering of multiple planning options can feel somewhat leading and reduce the impression that Kevin is setting the direction alone.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kevin offers several change-relevant statements about staying sharp and avoiding the ER. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk with confidence questions, rulers, or strategic reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully rather than challenged. The counselor could more skillfully use double-sided reflections and value-based discrepancy to reduce the hold of drinking and the social “edge.”
