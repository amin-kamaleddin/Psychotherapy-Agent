# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent warmth, nonjudgmental regard, and affirmation throughout. Statements such as You are not bad at this and You do not owe anyone a perfect system validate the patient without criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers strong reflective listening that captures the patient's tension, mental load, and ambivalence. However, the empathy is slightly limited by repetition and by not consistently exploring deeper emotions such as fear, shame, or broader life stress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on medication adherence and practical daily routine. There is a purposeful movement from general manageability to concrete anchors such as coffee, keys, counter, and morning walk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by building on the patient's ideas and inviting low-effort options. However, the interaction is not fully client-driven because the psychiatrist still provides a strong problem-solving structure and examples.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with repeated emphasis on the patient's choice, ownership, and freedom from pressure. The psychiatrist avoids direct advice and instead invites the patient to identify what fits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some patient-driven ideas, but evocation remains moderate. More consistent MI-specific techniques such as importance or confidence scales, change-talk summaries, and deeper values exploration would strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and positions the patient as the expert on her own daily life. Still, some solution-focused frames and concrete examples make the session somewhat expert-led rather than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the patient's change talk but does not consistently amplify or deepen it. The session would benefit from more skillful reflection, expansion, and connection of change talk to values and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding argument and gently exploring its subtle emotional costs. The main growth area is more consistently redirecting sustain talk toward the patient's own change motivations.
