# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent, warm, and nonjudgmental acceptance throughout the session with no labeling or conditional regard. Strong but not extraordinary in distinctiveness; the acceptance feels like a natural clinician style rather than the most explicitly unconditional form.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that goes beyond paraphrasing, accurately capturing Laura's ambivalence and the emotional weight of her language. Could have sat more fully with significant emotional disclosures (e.g., not feeling 'allowed' to take up space) before moving to the next question.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and consistent focus on the target behavior with a logical arc from identifying the gap to designing a specific first step. Slightly less explicit in pacing and goal-setting than a 5 would require; the direction is natural and organic rather than strategically guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue between equals where Laura contributes the content, solutions, and action plan while the psychiatrist reflects and builds on her language. The collaborative stance feels natural and unforced, never performative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every action step in the session is generated by Laura herself; the psychiatrist never prescribes, suggests, or says 'you should.' Autonomy support is so strong it becomes invisible — Laura is clearly the driver of all content and decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently draws motivation from within Laura using her own values and language as the engine for change, never imposing external reasons. Questions are specifically designed to elicit change talk and consistently succeed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook partnership with expertise and wisdom clearly residing within Laura. The psychiatrist positions themselves as a facilitator, explicitly highlighting Laura's own insights and never directing or prescribing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional skill in eliciting rich, specific change talk through open questions, amplifying Laura's own language, and building on each piece of change talk to deepen it. Exemplary MI practice that a trainee would be well-served to study.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Remarkable use of double-sided reflections that acknowledge sustain talk without reinforcing it, while consistently pivoting toward change. Sustain talk is never dismissed or argued with; instead it is connected to Laura's agency and awareness.
