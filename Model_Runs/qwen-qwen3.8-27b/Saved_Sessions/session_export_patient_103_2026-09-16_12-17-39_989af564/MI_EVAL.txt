# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally communicated respect, nonjudgment, and validation of the client's frustration. Acceptance was slightly limited by occasional corrective coaching and the unaddressed final statement of resentment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made several accurate surface-level reflections but did not consistently explore deeper emotional meanings, such as feeling like a number or professional identity. A premature feedback moment also reduced the accuracy of reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remained well-focused on the target behavior with a clear, practical sequence from exploration to a concrete plan. Direction was strong but occasionally more directive than collaboratively guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's choices clearly shaped the plan, including the 15-minute cutoff, preferred tone, and callback option. Collaboration was not perfect because the psychiatrist initially introduced an expert-driven set of options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered options and allowed the client to edit the script, but also provided direct advice and structured role-play. Autonomy was supported but not consistently reinforced, especially regarding final externalized blame.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evocative questions such as the readiness ruler helped elicit some change talk and client preferences. The psychiatrist often supplied reasons and solutions rather than consistently drawing out the client's own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited client input and allowed the client to influence the session, but frequently shifted into an expert-driven stance with scripts and role-play structure. Partnership was present but uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some intention language through the readiness ruler and option questions. Change talk was not consistently amplified, reflected, or developed across desire, ability, reasons, or need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided arguing and used validation, but often moved quickly into advice or scripting. Some sustain talk, including the final blame statement, was left unresolved.
