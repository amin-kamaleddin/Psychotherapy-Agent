# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and explicitly normalizes Isabelle's experience. However, the warmth occasionally reads as slightly clinical rather than deeply personal, and deeper emotional validation is missed in moments of mild frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening is demonstrated with accurate reflections of Isabelle's specific language and experiences. Some reflections miss the underlying emotional texture, such as the exhaustion behind her avoidance of deeper self-reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session moves from weekly structure to evenings to parties but reads as exploratory drift rather than purposeful, strategic direction toward a specific change goal. The session ends without a clear sense of momentum or next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates some collaborative behaviors and transparency about the therapeutic relationship, but the overall dynamic remains counselor-led with a question-and-answer pattern. There is limited co-construction of meaning or explicit power-sharing throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently affirms Isabelle's right to choose, offers no pressure to commit, and gives her agency over the session's purpose. Could be strengthened by more explicit reinforcement of her capacity for self-direction throughout, not just at key moments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor primarily asks exploratory and informational questions rather than strategically evoking Isabelle's own motivations, values, or reasons for change. No MI-specific evocation techniques (scaling, important-to-me questions) are used, and evocative moments are not pursued when they arise.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses inclusive language, addresses power dynamics transparently, and offers a strong partnership move late in the session. However, the conversation remains largely counselor-driven without explicit co-creation of an agenda or consistent power-sharing throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Very limited attempts are made to elicit change talk; questions focus on understanding life context rather than drawing out motivation for change. The counselor never asks targeted questions designed to evoke Isabelle's own reasons for potential change or confidence in her ability to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoids being adversarial and validates Isabelle's perspective on sustain talk, but does not use MI-specific techniques like double-sided reflections to redirect or open space for change. Sustain talk is largely accepted at face value rather than used as a springboard for further exploration.
