# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respectful, nonjudgmental acceptance throughout. Validation could be slightly deeper to normalize ambivalence as a human experience rather than a problem to manage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and captures the social pull behind Kevin’s ambivalence. The therapist could deepen empathy around shame, fear, and discomfort with needing an explanation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the high-risk drinking context, especially late-night shots. The discussion moves efficiently toward concrete, behavior-specific steps without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly co-created, with Kevin’s social concerns and ideas shaping the plan. The therapist acts as a partner rather than an expert directing the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally supports Kevin’s choice and avoids pressuring a strict abstinence rule. Explicit autonomy support could be added before offering therapist-generated suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist skillfully draws out Kevin’s own motivations, values, and solutions. Kevin’s change talk is repeatedly elicited and expanded into a realistic plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently invites Kevin’s expertise and lets his ideas drive the session. One therapist-initiated suggestion slightly reduces the fully client-led, power-sharing stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open, evocative questions successfully bring out Kevin’s own change-oriented ideas. The therapist reinforces and strengthens these ideas, making them more concrete and believable.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing or exaggerating the pull of drinking. The therapist could use more advanced strategies, such as double-sided reflections, to resolve ambivalence.
