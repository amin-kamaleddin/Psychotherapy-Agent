# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Rosa respectfully and normalizes her mixed feelings without judgment. The acceptance is supportive but often brief or generic rather than deeply validating her specific identity, social, and financial concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Rosa's ambivalence, practical worries, and fear of failure. Empathy could go deeper by addressing the emotional meaning of her identity loss, fear of being a burden, and worry about seeming strange.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on smoking reduction/cessation and craving management. Each interaction guides Rosa toward a concrete, behavior-specific plan without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rosa's preferences meaningfully shape the plan, including seeds, reminder wording, timing, and staying at her desk. However, the therapist still supplies much of the problem-solving content and specific implementation details.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist respects Rosa's choices, asks permission, and offers multiple options. Autonomy support dips when the therapist becomes more directive in prescribing specific reminders, actions, and plan details.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ruler questions and prompts about past successes help draw out Rosa's own motivation and confidence. Evocation is weakened by frequent shifts into therapist-generated advice and specific behavioral instructions before fully eliciting Rosa's values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Rosa's input and avoids a coercive stance. Partnership is limited by the therapist's frequent role as the primary generator of detailed strategies, scripts, and if-then plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist uses rulers and affirmations to strengthen Rosa's emerging commitment and sense of agency. The score is limited because meaningful change statements are not consistently reflected, amplified, or explored for deeper personal meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, and possible slips are framed as learning rather than failure. The softening is less strategic because the therapist often addresses barriers with concrete options rather than using double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence and pivot toward change.
