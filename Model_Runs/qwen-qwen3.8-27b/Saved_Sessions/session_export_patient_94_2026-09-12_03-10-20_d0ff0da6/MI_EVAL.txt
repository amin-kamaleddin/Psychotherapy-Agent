# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates basic respect and a nonjudgmental stance, especially early in the session. However, acceptance becomes inconsistent when repeated safety warnings after the client asks to stop feel paternalistic and undermine unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some accurate reflections of the client's frustration, time pressure, and side-effect concerns. The counselor misses deeper emotions such as fear, need for control, and irritation with medical authority, becoming repetitive and less responsive as the client becomes more resistant.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays within the asthma/inhaler topic but lacks a clear strategic direction toward change. It shifts among breathing exercises, doctor scripts, peak flow, action plans, and safety warnings without a collaboratively chosen target or concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some collaborative offers appear early, but the counselor repeatedly returns to the client despite her clear signals that she wants to leave and does not want a plan. The interaction becomes more counselor-directed than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses autonomy-supportive language, such as respecting the client's choice and offering options. This is undercut by repeated unsolicited safety reminders and directive statements, which can increase resistance and reduce the client's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited because the counselor relies more on providing information and safety guidance than on eliciting the client's own motivations. The client's values, confidence, prior strategies, and personal reasons for change are not sufficiently explored or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids an authoritarian stance early on and offers choices, supporting a basic partnership. However, the client's expertise is not consistently honored, as the counselor continues suggesting scripts, exercises, and safety steps after the client declines them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks a few open questions but does not systematically develop the client's own reasons for change. The client's mild change talk is not reflected, amplified, or built upon, and there is little use of values, ability, confidence, or commitment-based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges the client's sustain talk without arguing, such as concerns about steroids, lack of time, and wanting to reduce inhaler use. However, it is not effectively softened or reframed, with few strong double-sided reflections that honor the client's fear while opening a path toward safety or self-care.
