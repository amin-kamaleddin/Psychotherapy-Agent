# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while validating Hana’s anxiety, avoidance, and progress. Some affirmations are tied to performance or effort, which slightly limits unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate, reflective listening that captures both content and emotion. The reflections are competent but sometimes stay summary-like rather than fully exploring deeper shame, fear of judgment, or self-trust issues.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains an exceptionally clear focus on one actionable goal: taking a small, realistic step toward exam preparation on Saturday. Every question and intervention remains connected to the target behavior, making the pacing focused and clinically useful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites Hana’s input, checks understanding, and co-constructs the plan. Collaboration is strong, though it becomes less equal when the psychiatrist supplies many specific strategies and interventions rather than first eliciting Hana’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana is repeatedly given meaningful choices, and her limits, preferences, and sense of control are respected. Autonomy support is slightly reduced when the psychiatrist provides specific techniques and note wording before fully eliciting Hana’s own coping resources.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative scaling and planning questions that help Hana generate some confidence and commitment. However, the evocation is more procedural than motivational, with missed opportunities to draw out deeper values, past successes, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Hana as the expert on her own experience and lets her choices substantially shape the session. A few expert-driven moments, such as revising Hana’s note, slightly limit the fully collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk through questions about confidence, safety, and the desired step. There are, however, missed opportunities to amplify, summarize, or deepen change talk using DARN-CAT and related MI processes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about fear, failure, and embarrassment is handled respectfully and redirected into a smaller, controllable experiment. The psychiatrist could more explicitly transform this sustain talk into stronger change talk.
