# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warm unconditional positive regard throughout; validates vulnerability and resistance without judgment. Opening line and response to 'I'm not sure the answer is pretty' demonstrate powerful, non-clinical affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy capturing both the relational dimension of the behavior and the emotional weight of ambivalence. Complex reflections identify meta-cognitive patterns (e.g., using 'I need to stop' as a substitute for building alternatives).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Remarkably clear and logical progression: group dynamic → replacement options → escalation loop → social cost → support gaps. Each question builds directly on the previous one with no drift into unrelated territory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine co-investigator dynamic where Jordan provides content and self-observations while the psychiatrist provides structure and MI-specific skills. Shared constructions (conveyor belt metaphor, escalation mapping) feel like a true dialogue between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never prescribes solutions or implies Jordan 'should' make any particular choice; the entire session is framed as Jordan's to define. Explicit attribution of change talk to Jordan ('You're the one who said that, not me') reinforces self-efficacy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The standout skill; every insight, solution, and future image in the session originates from Jordan, elicited by skillful questioning. The psychiatrist never provides reasons for change or arguments—only targeted evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary power balance; the psychiatrist explicitly cedes control in the opening and consistently positions Jordan as the expert on his own experience. No advice, no taking over—content, direction, and conclusions all originate from Jordan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Masterfully identifies and attributes Jordan's own language shift ('I'd like not to be the guy who gets caught again'), then expands it into concrete future imagery. Could be more systematic in evoking specific change talk types (ability, need, desire, commitment) and confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully validates peer influence and material desire without challenging or amplifying, while maintaining direction toward change. Could benefit from more explicit double-sided reflections that simultaneously honor sustain talk and tilt toward change in a more deliberate strategic way.
