# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming Margaret for her drinking or son’s concerns. Acceptance is occasionally weakened by an early, directive problem-solving tone that feels less emotionally attuned than full MI.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are several validating reflections, but they often stay surface-level and miss deeper feelings such as irritation, defensiveness, or fear of falling. Empathy is present but not consistently deep or nuanced.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session frequently drifts toward falls prevention, medication timing, referrals, and practical safety planning rather than alcohol reduction. The MI focus on the target behavior is inconsistent and underdeveloped.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Margaret’s input and modifies the plan based on her preferences, which supports partial collaboration. However, the counselor often supplies the structure, advice, and scripts, making the interaction more counselor-led than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some autonomy-supportive moments, such as offering optional help and accepting Margaret’s refusal of written tracking. Early directive advice and specific recommendations reduce the emphasis on Margaret’s ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly uses medical or screening questions rather than evocative MI questions that draw out Margaret’s own values, reasons for change, or confidence. Change talk appears more from practical concerns than from skilled evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret’s preferences shape parts of the session, but the psychiatrist often assumes the expert or problem-solver role. The interaction shows some partnership but does not consistently feel equal or client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Margaret expresses some willingness to cut back, but the psychiatrist does not consistently evoke, reinforce, or expand this change talk. Few questions are used to strengthen her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding argumentation and validating Margaret’s reasons for keeping her wine. Some responses stop at neutral acceptance rather than actively softening sustain talk with deeper reflective strategies.
