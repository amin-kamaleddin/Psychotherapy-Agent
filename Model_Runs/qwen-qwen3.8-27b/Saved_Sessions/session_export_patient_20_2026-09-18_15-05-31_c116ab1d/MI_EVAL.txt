# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently nonjudgmental, warm tone throughout with zero labeling or criticism of Brandon's drinking. Statements like 'I'm not going to make it a big deal' and 'I hear you' demonstrate genuine, individualized acceptance of his experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently go beyond paraphrasing to capture the emotional and relational nuance beneath Brandon's words (e.g., 'exhausting carrying both,' 'that loss in there'). The therapist accurately names the felt meaning and internal conflict with precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on alcohol-related ambivalence with logical progression from resistance to exploring function and alternatives. However, it lacks stronger progressive momentum toward a concrete next step or specific behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is clearly co-constructed, with Brandon's own material (couch story, walk, 'brake pedal' metaphor) driving direction and content. The therapist uses his language back to him and asks for his perspective before offering any framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly supported from the opening ('not to tell you what to do') and maintained throughout with no 'should' or 'need to' language. All explorations are framed as Brandon's own choices and interpretations rather than therapist directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist consistently elicits motivation from within Brandon rather than providing external reasons for change, using questions that help him articulate his own values and goals. His role is clearly that of a midwife for his own change talk, not a lecturer.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist consistently positions Brandon as the expert on his own life, returning interpretive work to him with questions like 'What do you think that tells me?' There is no instance of the therapist positioning as an authority on what his drinking means.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes multiple important change statements from Brandon (e.g., 'not fun anymore,' 'don't have a replacement for it'). However, he could have more explicitly reinforced and amplified these statements to deepen and lock them in.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through exploration rather than argument, with effective double-sided awareness. However, there are moments where the therapist could have more strategically pivoted or gently tilted toward the change side.
