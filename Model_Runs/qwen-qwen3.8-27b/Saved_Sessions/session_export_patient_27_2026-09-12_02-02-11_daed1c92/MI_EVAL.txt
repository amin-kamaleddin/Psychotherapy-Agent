# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and uses supportive language such as “that hesitation makes sense,” but the acceptance feels somewhat formulaic. There is less deep affirmation of Daniela’s courage, vulnerability, or whole-person experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Daniela’s ambivalence and situation accurately, but does not consistently connect to the deeper emotional weight of stigma, fear of rejection, or secrecy. Stronger empathy would highlight the tension between wanting connection and protecting herself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the disclosure issue and practical wording without significant drift. However, the focus becomes overly narrow on exact scripts rather than broader motivation, ambivalence, or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates with Daniela to refine a message and incorporates her wording, but often provides the main options for her to choose from. This reduces the sense of true co-creation and shared control over the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and acknowledges that Daniela does not have to send the message today, but the repeated focus on practicing or drafting can create subtle pressure. More explicit emphasis on her full control and timing would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is one useful evocative question about what Daniela would hope to gain, but it is not followed up in a way that deepens change talk. Much of the session shifts toward scripting rather than drawing out Daniela’s own motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Daniela’s input, but frequently takes on the role of solution provider. A stronger partnership stance would place more emphasis on Daniela as the expert on her own friendships and readiness.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Daniela offers some change-oriented language, such as not wanting to start from zero, but the psychiatrist does not consistently highlight or amplify it. The session moves too quickly into problem-solving and script-building rather than exploring her reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Daniela’s reasons for keeping the secret and acknowledges that privacy feels safer. However, the sustain talk is not skillfully redirected by exploring the costs of secrecy or using double-sided reflections.
