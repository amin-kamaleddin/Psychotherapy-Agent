# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly normalizes Anisa’s experience. The warmth is strong, though it focuses more on specific behaviors and coping concerns than broader unconditional affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurately captures Anisa’s ambivalence, fear, and sense of stigma. Empathy could be stronger by exploring the deeper shame, fear of failing as a mother, and emotional cost of her concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on smoking cessation and narrows to a concrete after-work pause behavior. However, the questioning becomes repetitive and lacks a summary, agenda check, or clear next-step question to move toward a client-authored plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Anisa’s ideas and frames the work as done together, using co-construction language such as designing the corner-store moment together. The session is collaborative but remains counselor-led, with limited evidence of explicit power sharing or client-led prioritization.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Anisa’s choice, avoids pressure, and emphasizes that the plan should feel like her decision rather than a rule. Autonomy support is strong, though some counselor-proposed options slightly steer the problem-solving frame.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions help draw out Anisa’s own ideas and coping strategies. However, evocation is more practical and solution-focused than deeply motivational, with limited exploration of values, self-efficacy, or stronger DARN-C change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently asks for Anisa’s perspective and co-constructs ideas, allowing her suggestions to shape the conversation. The partnership is strong, though the counselor still maintains a somewhat guiding, agenda-setting role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some practical and identity-related change talk, including ideas about cutting down, taking a walk, and wanting smoking to not be the only thing making the day bearable. However, change talk is not consistently amplified, reflected back, or deepened into stronger commitment or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully without arguing or blaming, such as fear of snapping at her daughter or feeling that her body wants the cigarette. The session could be stronger by more consistently redirecting sustain talk toward Anisa’s values and change motivation.
