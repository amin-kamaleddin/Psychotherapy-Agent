# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming tone throughout the session. Acceptance is strong but somewhat surface-level, with limited deeper attunement to the client’s ambivalence or referral-related concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and capture the client’s schedule, stressors, and practical concerns. However, the empathy remains largely cognitive and does not deeply explore feelings, values, or the meaning behind her statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session quickly shifts from the alcohol-risk referral to study skills and stress management. The target concern is not consistently maintained or meaningfully reconnected to the original reason for referral.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician adapts to the client’s preferences in format and practicality. Collaboration is adequate but limited, as the core plan is largely clinician-generated rather than jointly developed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is given choices and her time constraints are respected. Autonomy support is moderate, but the session remains fairly directive and includes a suggestion that overlooks the client’s stated preference.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some open questions are used early in the session, but the clinician quickly moves to advice-giving. There is limited evocation of the client’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites input and offers options, supporting a collaborative tone. Partnership is partial, as the clinician remains the primary problem-solver rather than drawing out the client’s own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some low-level intention language appears, but it is not skillfully amplified or developed. Change talk is largely procedural and not connected to the underlying alcohol-risk concern.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician acknowledges the client’s preferences and offers smaller, manageable alternatives. Softening is present but not strongly MI-based, and the re-suggestion of breathing later undercuts the response.
