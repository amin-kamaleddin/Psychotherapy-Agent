# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance, often normalizing the client’s experience. Acceptance feels slightly procedural because validation becomes repetitive and the session quickly shifts into problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects surface-level experiences such as feeling hot, shaky, and watched. However, the reflections often miss deeper emotions like shame, fear of failure, or feeling burdened by the task.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays generally focused on the target behavior of managing anxiety during photography work. Direction becomes uneven as the conversation moves into logistical details rather than deeper motivation and commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to choose among options and builds on her preferences. Collaboration is limited because the psychiatrist supplies much of the content and remains the primary source of solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking which option feels safer or more doable. Autonomy is partly undercut by prescriptive advice and adding more details when the client feels overwhelmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some early open questions elicit motivation, but the psychiatrist quickly shifts to providing strategies and implementation details. There is limited exploration of the client’s own values, confidence, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and allows her choices to influence the session. The partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist takes an expert-driven role by rapidly introducing specific scripts and plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few evocative questions that elicit some change talk, especially around keeping clients and doing her job better. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reflect, or build on this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates the client’s doubts but does not consistently soften or redirect sustain talk toward change. Instead, the psychiatrist adds more options and steps, which may reinforce the sense that change is complicated.
