# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, accepting Ryan’s ambivalence and boundaries without pushing. The warmth is strong but somewhat formulaic, with limited explicit affirmation of Ryan’s struggle or effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Ryan’s torn feelings, but the empathy remains more content-focused than emotionally deep. It does not fully explore shame, fear, or the emotional meaning behind the incident.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing the risk of another alcohol-related incident while preserving Ryan’s social life. It is clear and practical, though it could better connect the plan to Ryan’s stated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates on options and rehearsals, especially when using Ryan’s own language for the text. However, much of the session relies on psychiatrist-generated lists, making collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ryan is invited to choose options and modify suggestions, and his boundaries are respected. At times, the prescriptive planning feels more expert-driven than fully supporting Ryan’s ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler and initial questions help Ryan identify some reasons for change. The session often shifts to external strategies rather than continuing to evoke Ryan’s deeper internal motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, but the psychiatrist frequently leads the session by supplying options, scripts, and tools. Ryan’s role is more that of a selector than an equal partner in generating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ryan produces some change talk, especially around safety, school, and the prepaid ride. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen, amplify, or build on this change talk throughout the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Ryan’s reasons for staying well, using double-sided reflections and nonjudgmental redirection. The score is not a 5 because the redirection often becomes practical rather than deeper motivational processing.
