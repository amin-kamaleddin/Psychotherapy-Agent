# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental. Amira’s distress is validated without shame, blame, or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong for helplessness, exhaustion, and fear of rejection. Some reflections are accurate but remain somewhat surface-level and could better capture deeper themes such as feeling burdensome or self-punishing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a relevant and coherent priority: the recent overdose, safety, and immediate support needs. Direction is clear, though it leans more toward crisis management than broader change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration through offering options and adjusting the message based on Amira’s input. However, the psychiatrist largely leads the agenda and provides structured safety steps rather than fully co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently respects Amira’s choice by offering options, asking permission, and avoiding commands. Repeated safety questions and prompts to send the message create some pressure, but overall autonomy is supported.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some open questions and invitations, but evocation is limited. The session shifts quickly to advice and practical steps rather than drawing out Amira’s internal reasons, values, or hopes for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language and invites input, which supports a partnership stance. The interaction becomes more expert-driven, especially around safety planning and message drafting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amira repeatedly expresses regret and change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on it. The session moves quickly to practical safety planning rather than strengthening Amira’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce hopelessness or futility and generally responds supportively. However, MI-consistent softening is limited, as sustain talk is often acknowledged and then redirected to problem-solving rather than explored with reflective or double-sided responses.
