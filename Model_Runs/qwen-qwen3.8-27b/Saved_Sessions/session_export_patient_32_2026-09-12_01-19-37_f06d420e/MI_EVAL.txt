# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the client’s reluctance to be lectured. Some phrases sound mildly evaluative or parent-like, so acceptance is strong but not fully neutral.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges practical concerns about the license and peer pressure. However, empathy stays mostly cognitive, with limited exploration of underlying fear, shame, frustration, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing DUI risk and protecting the client’s license through concrete strategies. Direction is strong, though it emphasizes behavior management more than values or readiness to change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The final plan reflects the client’s preferences, such as keeping cab cash and using a fake beer. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist repeatedly provides structured options and leads the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts refusals without pressure and supports the client’s choice to keep control of his own plan. Autonomy support is strong, though occasional evaluative comments slightly undermine it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out the client’s own motivations, values, confidence, or past successes. Important client statements about license loss and prior sobriety are not explored deeply.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, but the session remains largely expert-driven and advice-focused. The client mostly reacts to suggestions rather than co-creating the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers useful change talk about avoiding another DUI and protecting his license. The psychiatrist acknowledges these statements but does not consistently amplify or deepen them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing resistance and mostly accepts the client’s limits. However, sustain talk is redirected toward practical coping rather than resolved through reflective or ambivalence-based responses.
