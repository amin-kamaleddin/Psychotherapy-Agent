# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used respectful, nonjudgmental statements that normalized Marco’s difficulty. However, acceptance was not consistently deep, especially when Marco expressed feeling overwhelmed and self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was one strong early reflection, but most responses were pragmatic rather than emotionally responsive. The psychiatrist did not consistently reflect Marco’s stress, worry, fatigue, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on evening snacking and home food access, which was relevant. However, the psychiatrist introduced many specific tactics rapidly, making the direction feel more behavioral-management than MI-guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marco was allowed to choose among options, but the session was largely driven by the psychiatrist’s plans and scripts. Marco was not consistently treated as an equal partner in creating the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were some autonomy-supportive questions, but the psychiatrist frequently used directive language and detailed prescriptions. This likely undermined Marco’s ownership, especially as he expressed overwhelm.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made limited effort to elicit Marco’s internal motivations, values, or confidence. Most of the session relied on externally supplied strategies rather than helping Marco generate his own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some collaborative language, but the overall stance was quite counselor-driven. Marco’s contributions mostly narrowed the psychiatrist’s options rather than shaping the session independently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist produced small pieces of practical change talk by asking what felt doable. However, he did not consistently draw out Marco’s deeper reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not reinforce sustain talk and offered some normalizing statements, but responses often added more advice or planning. He did not consistently use MI-consistent reflections or reframing to resolve ambivalence.
