# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly seeks permission. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic, with fewer affirmations of the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and track the client’s main concerns. They often remain surface-level and miss deeper emotional meanings such as guilt, frustration, or disappointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and moves toward a concrete, manageable plan. Some time spent on logistics dilutes the momentum slightly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client helps shape the plan through choices such as day, stairs, gum, and no scale. Collaboration is solid but would be stronger if more ideas were client-generated rather than therapist-offered.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice, avoids pressure, and respects the client’s pace and decision to avoid the scale. Autonomy support is strong, though some suggestions remain therapist-initiated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness and confidence scaling usefully evoke some change talk. The therapist still introduces many strategies before fully developing the client’s own reasons, values, and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses permission-seeking and invites the client to choose priorities and actions. The stance is collaborative, though still somewhat solution-focused rather than fully co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness and confidence questions produce useful client statements about trying change. The therapist does not consistently amplify or deepen these statements into stronger change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing or invalidating it. There is less consistent use of double-sided reflections or strategic redirection toward change.
