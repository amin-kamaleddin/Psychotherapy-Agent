# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment throughout, validating Margaret's autonomy and self-concept. However, the warmth remains somewhat professional and structured rather than deeply personal, lacking specific affirmations of Margaret as a person beyond the behavior being discussed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking, precisely mirroring Margaret's points about social significance, ease, and the emotional weight of the fall. What prevents a 5 is that reflections tend to stay at the content level rather than consistently accessing deeper emotional undercurrents such as loneliness, identity threat, or grief of lost independence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a clear focus on alcohol reduction with a logical arc from opening through exploring patterns to the fall as a discrepancy point. What prevents a 5 is the lack of a concrete commitment step or specific target at the end, leaving the session somewhat open-ended rather than strategically directed toward a defined next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong collaboration through inclusive language, following Margaret's lead on topics, and inviting co-construction of meaning. However, the structure remains somewhat interviewer-led in a one-directional ask-respond-reflect pattern rather than truly negotiating direction or offering the psychiatrist's perspective as one data point among many.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Margaret's autonomy, never pressuring her to quit or adopt a specific plan, and explicitly affirms that her own agency matters. What prevents a 5 is that autonomy support could be more explicit in moments where it is threatened, such as when her son's concern is raised, and one question about 'one small thing' feels mildly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully draws out Margaret's own motivations using her experiences (the fall, the quiet house, the retirement shift) rather than imposing external reasons for change. The key gap is missing the opportunity to deeply evoke confidence and ability when Margaret says she could cut back, without developing it through scaling or exploring what gives her that sense of being able to.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance with inclusive language and follows Margaret's lead on topics, avoiding lecturing or expert positioning. However, occasional leading questions subtly direct the agenda rather than fully yielding to Margaret's direction, and there is less explicit acknowledgment that Margaret is the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk from Margaret but does not actively amplify, reflect back, or build on these statements when they emerge. The evocation is present but underdeveloped — the psychiatrist creates space for change talk but fails to cultivate it through strategic reinforcement, scaling questions, or exploring confidence and ability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk skillfully by validating Margaret's comparisons while gently exploring what's underneath, using sophisticated double-sided awareness without being confrontational. What prevents a 5 is that after Margaret's 'plenty of people have a glass with dinner' comment, a more deliberate pivot to the fall or pattern change could have been made.
