# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a nonjudgmental, reassuring stance and validated Elena's concerns without dismissing them. Some affirmations were mildly evaluative, and frequent shifts into informational mode kept this from being maximally person-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Elena's ambivalence, fear, and protective motives. Empathy could be deeper by exploring the emotional impact of her fear and guilt before moving into factual information.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on vaccine hesitancy, fear of reactions, and preparing for a pediatrician visit. The counselor consistently guided the conversation toward a clear, actionable next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Elena's input and offered choices about format, content, and next steps. Collaboration was strong, though the counselor remained the primary source of information and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena's choice and pace were repeatedly respected, with no pressure to agree to vaccination immediately. Autonomy support would be stronger if the counselor more explicitly positioned Elena as the expert on her values and decision-making process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some effective open questions early in the session to elicit concerns and preferences. However, the conversation often shifted into educational mode, limiting opportunities to draw out and expand Elena's own motivation and change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrated a collaborative stance by offering options and inviting Elena to shape the plan. The relationship remained somewhat expert-driven due to the substantial amount of information and structured guidance provided.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena produced meaningful change talk, especially toward the end of the session. The psychiatrist did not consistently use targeted MI questions or reflections to actively amplify and develop that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor responded to sustain talk with acceptance rather than argument, which was appropriate. However, the main strategy was providing factual risk comparisons instead of using more sophisticated MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or values-based exploration.
