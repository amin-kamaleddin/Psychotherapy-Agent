# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and reassuring, with supportive statements that validate Oliver’s feelings. However, the acceptance can feel formulaic and sometimes assumes emotions such as relief without fully exploring Oliver’s deeper ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is moderate recognition of Oliver’s anxiety, guilt, and hesitation, but empathy often remains surface-level. The psychiatrist quickly shifts into advice and scripting rather than deeply reflecting emotional complexity or using Oliver’s humor meaningfully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior of asserting a boundary and setting a move-out date. The main limitation is that the focus is task-rigid and advice-driven rather than guided through MI processes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates some client input, such as wording changes and timing preferences, but the interaction is largely counselor-led. The psychiatrist supplies much of the structure, scripts, and strategy rather than building primarily from Oliver’s own ideas and values.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through some choices, such as message options and timing, but frequent directives undermine client choice. Instructions like deciding a fixed date, limiting apologies, using a timer, and repeating a line make the psychiatrist the primary decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** This is the weakest area, with the psychiatrist providing reasons, scripts, and rules rather than eliciting Oliver’s own motivations, values, and confidence. There is little use of open questions, scaling, exceptions, or expansion of the client’s spontaneous change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates some collaborative moments by asking for input and offering options, but the overall stance remains expert-driven. Oliver’s choices influence details, yet the psychiatrist remains the main problem-solver and script provider.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Oliver offers several spontaneous change statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or develop them. The focus on closed choices and directives limits the strengthening of Oliver’s own motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Oliver’s guilt, fear, and concerns without arguing or dismissing them. However, responses often become practical problem-solving rather than using MI-consistent strategies like double-sided reflections to deepen ambivalence resolution.
