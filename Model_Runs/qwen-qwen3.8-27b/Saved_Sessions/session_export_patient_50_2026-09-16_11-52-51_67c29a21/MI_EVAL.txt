# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized Hana's anxiety and avoidance. Acceptance was strong, though somewhat procedural and task-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflections and noticed somatic cues such as Hana's shoulders dropping. Empathy was generally good but often stayed at the level of summary rather than exploring deeper fear, self-judgment, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed strongly focused on the target behavior of preparing a small, manageable exam-study step. There was minimal drift, and the conversation consistently moved toward a concrete, testable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana's ideas shaped the plan, including the small study step, text accountability, sticky note, and silent phrase. Collaboration was strong, though the psychiatrist remained the primary architect of many options and structures.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offered choices and asked which option felt most doable, supporting Hana's sense of control. Autonomy support was good but was sometimes limited by very specific directives and checklists.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked Hana's own motivations by asking what mattered, rating confidence, and exploring what would help change. Evocation was frequent, but later problem-solving and behavioral planning reduced the depth of client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and incorporated Hana's preferences, creating a cooperative tone. However, the interaction often became expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying detailed plans and instructions rather than fully positioning Hana as the expert on her own change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked and reinforced Hana's change talk, including her confidence increasing from 4 to 8 and recognizing that small steps matter. There were missed opportunities to further amplify change talk, such as exploring past successes or what higher confidence would feel like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with Hana's concerns and used normalizing language about the pressure voice and freezing. However, sustain talk was not consistently softened through complex reflections or by turning doubts into exploration, often relying instead on additional behavioral safeguards.
