# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating tone throughout, using statements such as “I’m here to listen, not judge.” However, some opening inferences and occasional leading framing prevented a consistently deeply affirming 5-level response.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used clear, accurate reflections that captured Maya’s ambivalence about drinking’s benefits and costs. Empathy would strengthen further by naming deeper emotions such as embarrassment, fear of being awkward, and concern about appearing like a “buzzkill.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on alcohol use and its effects on school functioning and future plans. The counselor moved logically from ambivalence to a concrete, behaviorally specific plan for not drinking on Thursdays.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor co-created the plan with Maya and invited her input on supports, timing, drinks, and follow-up preferences. It fell just short of a 5 because the counselor occasionally introduced structured options and phrasing before Maya fully generated her own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supported choice by asking permission, allowing “if anything,” and respecting Maya’s preference not to rehearse the refusal line. More explicit emphasis on Maya’s full ownership and freedom to modify or discard the plan would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicited Maya’s own reasons, confidence, and actionable ideas through readiness, scaling, and small-step questions. Evocation would be stronger by relying more on Maya’s own language and follow-up questions that build her self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treated Maya as the expert on her life and invited her perspective at multiple points in planning. The interaction remained counselor-structured at times, which limited a fully client-led 5-level partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicited meaningful change talk about reducing next-day fog, trying not to drink on Thursdays, and increasing confidence. More strategic reflections and follow-ups after Maya’s change statements would help her expand her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing with Maya’s sustain talk and acknowledged drinking’s benefits as a trade-off. However, the response often shifted to planning rather than using more skillful roll-with or double-sided reflections to gently soften her resistance and social concerns.
