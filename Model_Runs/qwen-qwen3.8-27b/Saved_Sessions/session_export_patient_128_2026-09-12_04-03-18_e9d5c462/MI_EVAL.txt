# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses warm, nonjudgmental statements and avoids shaming despite the conduct violation. Acceptance is strong, though it could deepen with more individualized affirmation of the client's ambivalence and effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's conflict between wanting a normal college experience and fearing consequences. Empathy remains mostly cognitive and misses deeper emotions such as fear, shame, and frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, probation, and practical risk reduction. Direction could be more MI-consistent by consistently linking the plan to the client's values and reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and the client chooses some strategies, showing a collaborative tone. However, the psychiatrist supplies most of the options before the client generates his own ideas, which limits true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and respects the client's hesitation to practice aloud. Autonomy support is inconsistent because the plan still feels framed by the psychiatrist rather than fully owned by the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicit some change talk about education, avoiding suspension, and possible strategies. Evocation is limited by early movement into practical advice before the client's own motivations are fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission to brainstorm and invites the client's preferences, supporting a partner-like stance. The interaction often shifts to an expert-driven format where the client selects from a list of clinician-generated options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses clear values and some willingness to try eating first and a brief refusal line. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or strategically deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's sustain talk about wanting to drink and not feel like a killjoy is largely met with practical tips for drinking while staying included. The session misses key opportunities to reflect ambivalence or explore the costs of maintaining current drinking.
