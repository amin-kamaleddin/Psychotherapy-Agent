# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepts David's resistance without arguing. Acceptance is strong but not a 5 because the warmth is somewhat clinical and occasionally includes mild normative framing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses good reflective listening, capturing both practical and emotional aspects of David's ambivalence. Empathy is limited by an initial misreading of David's need to "come down" and some repetitive phrasing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking, NRT, and the after-work driveway moment, with a clear progression toward a small trial. Direction is appropriate but could be sharper by reducing repetition and consolidating next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as a problem-solving partner and incorporates David's practical ideas into the plan. Collaboration is strong but not a 5 because the clinician still sets much of the structure and repeatedly steers toward the patch and doorframe pause.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a strength, with repeated acknowledgment of David's right to choose and avoidance of pressuring language. It falls short of a 5 because the patch and health-oriented framing still anchor the conversation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicits David's own practical change language rather than imposing reasons for change. Evocation is strong but could go deeper into values, confidence, and stronger personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats David as the expert on his own preferences and uses open, invitational questions to shape the plan. Partnership is strong but not fully co-created because the clinician still controls much of the structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes client-driven change talk, including manageable next steps and signs of progress. It is not a 5 because the change talk is not systematically developed or consolidated using DARN-C categories.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and respects David's ambivalence. However, the session does not actively soften sustain talk through double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic questions.
