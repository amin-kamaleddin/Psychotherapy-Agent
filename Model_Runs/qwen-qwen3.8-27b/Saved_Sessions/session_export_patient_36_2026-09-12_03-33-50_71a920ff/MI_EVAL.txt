# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and a nonjudgmental stance, directly honoring the client’s request for authenticity. They normalize the client’s plan and respond respectfully even when she labels herself weak.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and capture both behavior and felt meaning, especially around self-respect and keeping promises. However, the counselor sometimes moves into planning without fully sitting with deeper emotions such as shame, disappointment, or internal conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a strong collaborative process, inviting the client to set the agenda, define feedback style, and choose next steps. The counselor occasionally shifts into a more instructive role by offering scripts and tips, which slightly reduces the purely client-led nature of the interaction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the client directing much of the session through her choices about the boundary, script, alarm, and role-play. Brief instructive moments with alternative scripts and delivery tips slightly reduce full co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports choice and avoids controlling language, allowing the client to select her plan and script. Some counselor-generated suggestions, such as alarms and delivery tips, are helpful but slightly directive rather than fully client-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently draws out the client’s values, motivations, and confidence using open questions and scaling. Evocation is occasionally interrupted by direct advising, such as offering alternative scripts and specific tips.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that expertise resides in the client and invites her to define the agenda and interaction style. The counselor occasionally takes on a more expert or coaching role, slightly limiting the degree of pure power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively evokes client-generated change talk and confidence, especially through scaling and implementation questions. The score is limited by occasional shifts into providing concrete scripts and tactical options rather than continuing evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is minimal, but the counselor handles reasons for maintaining the status quo without reinforcing them and redirects toward the client’s boundary. There are few opportunities for more advanced double-sided reflection or pivoting sustain talk.
