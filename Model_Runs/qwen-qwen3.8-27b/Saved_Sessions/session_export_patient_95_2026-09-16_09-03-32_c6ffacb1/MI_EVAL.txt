# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally communicates respect, validation, and concern, without mocking or arguing with Maria’s statements. Acceptance is slightly weakened by repeated, intense safety questioning that can feel evaluative rather than fully accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflective listening and accurately captures Maria’s exhaustion, emptiness, and need to be understood. Empathic depth is somewhat interrupted by repeated safety check-ins rather than consistently deepening emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on suicidal ideation and safety rather than wandering, but the direction becomes repetitive. It could better guide the conversation toward a narrow, collaborative change target such as staying safe for the next hour.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and asks for Maria’s preferences, showing some collaboration. However, the clinician often drives the agenda with repeated safety questions despite Maria’s request to be heard.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are choices and affirmations that steps occur only if Maria wants them, supporting some autonomy. Repeated offers of safety interventions after she declines can feel pressuring, and stronger language about her decision-making power would be helpful.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not consistently elicit Maria’s own values, motivations, or reasons for change. Questions are mostly safety-focused, missing opportunities to explore what keeps her here or what relief would mean to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language, such as following Maria’s lead and offering choices. The repeated safety questioning and statements about needing to keep her safe make the interaction feel more like clinician-led crisis management.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maria offers some change talk, such as her mother being the reason she has not acted, but the psychiatrist mostly reflects rather than actively evokes it. There is limited exploration of desire, ability, need, or commitment related to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce statements like “nothing works” or “suicide is the only way,” and avoids arguing. However, the session does not consistently soften or redirect sustain talk, and the focus on means keeps suicide central in the conversation.
