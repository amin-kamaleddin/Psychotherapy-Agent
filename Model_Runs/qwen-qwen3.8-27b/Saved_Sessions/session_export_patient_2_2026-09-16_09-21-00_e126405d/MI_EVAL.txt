# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Aisha's time constraints and need for a quick checkup. Acceptance is clear but could be warmer by more explicitly affirming her worth, ambivalence, and identity separate from smoking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture practical strain and some ambivalence, such as running on empty and wanting a quick step. Empathy could deepen by exploring underlying worries, guilt, fear, or the emotional push-pull between wanting care and fearing being dismissed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays relevant to breathlessness, cough, and evening smoking, but repeatedly shifts into medical testing and planning. Direction would improve by consistently anchoring the conversation to a single change goal and using summaries to return to that focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Aisha to choose among options and builds on her willingness to cut back. Collaboration is limited by an expert-led medical framework, where the clinician offers plans and options rather than more fully co-constructing meaning with Aisha.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Aisha's choice, avoids coercion, and supports self-direction by normalizing slips and asking what feels easiest. Autonomy support could strengthen if Aisha had more ownership over the specific plan and the testing process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and asks useful questions about readiness, confidence, and preferred small steps. More evocation would involve drawing out Aisha's values, past strengths, ability, and personal reasons for reducing smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha is allowed to shape several decisions, including telehealth, reduction, and use of a phone alarm. Partnership is reduced by the psychiatrist's structured medical questioning, test recommendations, and concrete plan, which can feel less collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha expresses meaningful change talk, such as wanting to stay healthy for her son and trying to reduce evening cigarettes. The psychiatrist could better develop this by reflecting, amplifying, and asking follow-up questions that expand her own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Aisha's sustain talk and reflects some ambivalence about quitting. Softening could improve by using more double-sided reflections and validating concerns before emphasizing medical evaluation or redirecting.
