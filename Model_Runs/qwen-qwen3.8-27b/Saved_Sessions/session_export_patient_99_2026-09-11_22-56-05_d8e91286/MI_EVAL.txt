

# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
- [x] **Score (1–5):** 4/5

**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming; uses permission. Warmth is somewhat formulaic and medically framed, with limited deeper positive regard.

---

### Empathy
- [x] **Score (1–5):** 3/5

**Comments:** Provides basic reflections and summaries, but does not deeply track the client’s emotional experience, peer pressure, fear of exclusion, or social meaning of drinking.

---

### Direction
- [x] **Score (1–5):** 4/5

**Comments:** Discussion generally remains focused on alcohol use in relation to the ankle injury, missed work, and safety. Opening is broad, but the target behavior is maintained more consistently than not.

---

### Collaboration
- [x] **Score (1–5):** 3/5

**Comments:** Partial collaboration is evident in co-creating a simple text and plan, but the counselor supplies most of the content, structure, and scripts.

---

### Autonomy Support
- [x] **Score (1–5):** 3/5

**Comments:** Asks permission and offers choices, supporting autonomy. However, directive scripts and repeated reminders make the interaction more prescriptive than autonomous.

---

### Evocation
- [ ] **Score (1–5):** 2/5

**Comments:** Provides reasons for change early and uses checklist-like questions. The session ends with reminders rather than eliciting deeper internal motivation from the client.

---

### Partnership
- [x] **Score (1–5):** 3/5

**Comments:** Asks about preferences and incorporates some client input, such as using the ankle explanation. However, the session becomes expert-driven with advice, scripts, and repeated reminders.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
- [ ] **Score (1–5):** 2/5

**Comments:** Client offers some change talk, but the counselor mainly affirms rather than evokes or expands it. Little use of importance, desire, ability, need, or commitment language; no scaling or values-based evocation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
- [ ] **Score (1–5):** 2/5

**Comments:** Sustain talk is clearly present, but the counselor mostly bypasses it with brief normalizing and practical reminders. No meaningful reframing, double-sided reflection, or exploration of ambivalence.
