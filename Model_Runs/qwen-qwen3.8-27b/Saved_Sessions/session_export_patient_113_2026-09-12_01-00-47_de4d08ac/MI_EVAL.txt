# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance by reducing shame, normalizing difficulty, and avoiding blame. Acceptance was strong but somewhat procedural, with fewer moments of deeply warm affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were clear and accurate reflections of frustration, overwhelm, and the sense of judgment, showing meaningful perspective-taking. Empathy was adequate but shifted toward task planning too quickly and remained more content-focused than feeling-focused.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently elicited small behavioral intentions and captured some change talk, such as agreeing to try the one-minute step. These statements were not sufficiently amplified, scaled, or connected to Rowan’s own values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language, offered choices, kept the session short, and incorporated Rowan’s preferred word “annoying” into the plan. Collaboration became less balanced near the end when the psychiatrist repeatedly directed and recapped the same plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported autonomy by offering options, respecting boundaries, avoiding coercion, and removing pressure around gratitude or performance. Repeated reminders of the plan created a mild impression of supervision, slightly limiting full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempted to evoke Rowan’s experience and intention by asking about the “watching” feeling and whether he would try the step today. The evocation remained superficial and did not deeply explore his own values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist fostered a collaborative stance by inviting Rowan to choose between options and respecting his limits. The partnership became more clinician-led after Rowan chose the experiment, especially through repeated scripted instructions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rowan produced clear change talk, including agreeing to try the one-minute task and saying he would try not to make it a fight. The psychiatrist acknowledged these statements but did not consistently deepen them with affirmations, scaling, or exploration of confidence and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively avoided arguing with or moralizing Rowan’s resistance, instead validating his annoyance and offering a low-effort alternative. The score was not higher because fewer active softening strategies, such as double-sided reflections or reframing, were used.
