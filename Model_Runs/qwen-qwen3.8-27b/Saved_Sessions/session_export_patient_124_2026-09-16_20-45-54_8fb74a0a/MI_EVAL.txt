# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a nonjudgmental, validating stance and normalized Sofia’s referral and coping use of marijuana. Warmth was strong but remained somewhat professional and task-focused rather than fully embracing broader identity or emotional vulnerability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and captured key ambivalence around stress relief, cost, worry, and social pressure. Empathy could deepen further by more fully exploring underlying shame, fear of not belonging, family expectations, or identity-level values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior: reducing or managing marijuana use in high-risk situations such as stress, weekends, and social settings. The conversation progressed clearly from general stress coping to specific, behavior-focused options and a small test plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia’s ideas shaped the conversation, and the psychiatrist repeatedly invited her perspective on coping, weekends, and support planning. Collaboration could be stronger with more explicit shared-decision language and less therapist-directed question sequence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided direct advice, pressure, and pathologizing while keeping Sofia in the driver’s seat with open, reflective questions. Autonomy support would be more complete with clearer explicit language such as “This is your choice” or “Whatever you decide, I’ll support that.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked Sofia’s own ideas for coping, delaying use, avoiding risky situations, and using a text-based support plan. Evocation was practical and problem-solving-oriented but did not deeply evoke discrepancy through values, identity, or longer-term goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned Sofia as the expert in her own life and followed her ideas about waiting, weekends, and support plans. Partnership was respectful but could include more explicit agenda negotiation, shared decision-making language, and power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia generated several change-oriented ideas, such as waiting, testing on weekdays, leaving early, and texting someone if overwhelmed. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify, consolidate, or reflect this change talk, and used limited DARN-C, confidence, or importance-scaled language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well without arguing, and the psychiatrist respected the function of marijuana while reducing defensiveness. Softening could be stronger by more actively connecting sustain talk to values, self-image, and desired ways of feeling around others.
