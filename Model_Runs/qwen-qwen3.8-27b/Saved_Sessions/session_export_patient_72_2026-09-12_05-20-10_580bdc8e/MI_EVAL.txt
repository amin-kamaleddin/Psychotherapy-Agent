# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone while validating Leila’s goals and concerns. The main limitation was that warmth sometimes felt formulaic and did not always explicitly affirm deeper body-image or vulnerability-related feelings.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflective listening for Leila’s practical and emotional concerns, including pressure from gym sales and the need for flexible plans. Empathy could have been stronger by reflecting deeper feelings such as frustration, insecurity, anxiety, or relief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on restarting a flexible exercise plan and consistently moved toward concrete, behavior-specific steps. Each segment, including role-play, was directly connected to the target goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked as a true partner, inviting Leila’s input and adapting to her choices throughout the session. Leila’s preferences clearly shaped the plan, from role-play to email follow-up and reminders.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently checked in with Leila and respected her boundaries around commitment and pressure. Some sections remained mildly directive because of the amount of scripts and step-by-step planning offered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew out initial motivation and confidence through scaling and open questions, but then shifted more toward practical problem-solving. Deeper evocation of Leila’s values, past successes, and self-efficacy was missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally positioned Leila as the expert on her own life, with multiple invitations to choose her next steps. The reliance on counselor-provided scripts and structure added a slight expert-driven tone that limited a perfect rating.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Leila produced strong change talk, and the psychiatrist supported it with some scaling and affirmation. More follow-up questions could have further amplified her own reasons, desires, abilities, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively acknowledged Leila’s concerns about rigidity, rain, and sales pressure without arguing or dismissing them. More advanced strategies, such as double-sided reflections or turning sustain talk into autonomy-based values statements, could have strengthened this further.
