# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently validates Jason's perspective without judgment and supports trust by honoring confidentiality. Acceptance could be strengthened with more explicit statements of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Jason's ambivalence, need for fairness, and concern about loss of control. Deeper emotional themes, such as shame or fear of peer judgment, could be reflected more directly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, party risks, and conduct concerns. Discussion moves clearly from general concerns to a concrete, client-generated safety plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor works as an equal partner, building the plan from Jason's own ideas and preferences. The interaction is highly collaborative rather than directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Jason is repeatedly positioned as the owner of his choices and plan. The counselor avoids pressure or punishment framing while supporting realistic self-selected steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor draws out Jason's own reasons for change, such as fairness, staying in control, and remembering his night. Open questions and reflections effectively strengthen client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently treats Jason as the expert on his life, friends, and limits. The plan emerges from Jason's suggestions rather than being imposed by the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully elicits and amplifies Jason's change talk, including specific limits and exit strategies. Jason's confidence in taking a doable step increases throughout the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument or moralizing, especially when Jason resists being labeled or monitored. The counselor could more explicitly acknowledge the pull of peer judgment before redirecting to change.
