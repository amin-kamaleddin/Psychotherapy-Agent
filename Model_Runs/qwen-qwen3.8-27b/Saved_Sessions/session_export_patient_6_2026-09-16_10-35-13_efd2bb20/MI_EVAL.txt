# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids disapproval of Alexei’s experimentation. Warmth is generally supportive but somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and supportive, capturing both the practical concern and the social difficulty of refusing. However, the reflections stay somewhat surface-level and do not fully explore underlying feelings such as shame, fear, or peer pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on alcohol refusal, safety, and soccer goals, moving logically from concern to practical strategies. It becomes somewhat procedural and could more consistently tie the plan back to Alexei’s broader values and readiness for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates the refusal plan and invites Alexei to choose lines and rehearsal scenarios. However, the psychiatrist supplies much of the content, so the interaction is collaborative but not fully client-generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Permission is sought, options are offered, and Alexei’s preference for low-drama exits is respected. The directive scripts and preloaded plans slightly reduce the sense that the final plan is wholly client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions early on and affirmations of Alexei’s soccer-related values. Overall, the session is more advice- and skill-based than focused on drawing out Alexei’s internal motivation and deeper meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites input, and Alexei actively chooses the practice excuse, line, and exit plan. The many preloaded options make the interaction feel somewhat expert-driven rather than fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist links Alexei’s behavior to his goals and affirms emerging change talk. He does not consistently expand, amplify, or deepen Alexei’s own motivations before moving into skill-building.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through normalization, validation, and low-conflict alternatives rather than argument. It could be strengthened with more MI-specific skills such as double-sided reflections or pivots that transform sustain talk into change talk.
