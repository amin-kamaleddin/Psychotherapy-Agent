# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance. There is no blame, shaming, or pressure around Amina's phone use, ambivalence, or self-described nosiness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers strong reflections of Amina's ambivalence, social fear, and struggle to stay in the loop. However, some reflections remain more cognitive/behavioral than fully feeling-level, especially around loneliness, emptiness, and self-critical worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior: morning/bed phone use and its effects on sleep and schoolwork. The counselor purposefully narrows the conversation toward concrete, actionable change steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the counselor working as an equal partner rather than directing the session. The counselor builds on Amina's own ideas, such as the coffee ritual, tag-based checking, face-down phone, and the word 'meh.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Amina's choice and ownership by using conditional, question-based language. Suggestions are offered as options rather than directives, and there is no pressure to commit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor mostly elicits motivation and solutions from Amina rather than providing reasons for change. The score is lower than 5 because a few prompts are suggestive, such as naming a physical habit or a self-talk word, which could lead rather than evoke.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor clearly treats Amina as the expert on her own life, friendships, and phone use. Language repeatedly returns authority and expertise to the patient rather than positioning the counselor as the authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses open questions to evoke several change statements, such as trying a tiny rule, making bad coffee, checking tags, and putting the phone face-down. However, the change talk is not always systematically reinforced, summarized, or amplified to strengthen momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accepts and reflects sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it, which prevents a confrontational response. The session would benefit from more double-sided reflections that balance sustain talk with discrepancy and gently pivot toward change.
