# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, warm, and non-judgmental tone throughout, with specific validation of Ingrid's time constraints and frustration. Warmth occasionally feels slightly scripted, and acknowledgment of the emotional weight of her decision-making could be deeper.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening that names both emotions and experiences (e.g., 'I hear the frustration in that so?'). Reflections occasionally lean toward summary rather than exploring the underlying emotional depth, particularly when Ingrid expresses mild irritation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains general focus on the medication question but allows slight drift, including a misaligned suggestion to redirect to the pharmacist that frustrated the client. Repeated attempts to extend the conversation at the closing represent a loss of direction despite Ingrid's clear signals to leave.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Ingrid's input, asks clarifying questions rather than assuming, and adjusts approach based on her feedback (e.g., the 'what-if vs. current medication' question). Collaboration is somewhat constrained by the session's informational nature and the initial misstep of suggesting the pharmacist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirms Ingrid's self-direction and never pressures her to make commitments beyond what she offers. Repeated attempts to extend the conversation at the end could be seen as slightly undermining her autonomy to end on her terms, though the boundary is ultimately respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evocation is present but primarily reactive—responding to what Ingrid offers rather than proactively drawing out deeper motivation. No exploration of what 'checking first' means to her, what values drive her caution, or what would make her feel fully confident in her decisions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with inclusive language and positions Ingrid as the expert on her own situation. The apology and shared accountability after her pushback demonstrate genuine power-sharing, though the session's informational format limits co-creative opportunities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively identifies and amplifies change talk ('shows real care for your own health,' 'that's a smart approach,' 'that's a really solid plan'). A perfect score would require more active elicitation of deeper change talk—exploring what 'checking first' means or connecting it to broader values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles Ingrid's time constraint well by acknowledging it without fighting against it, and pivots directly when she pushes back without becoming defensive or lecturing. No reinforcement of sustain talk occurs; the psychiatrist never validates skipping the check or using time as a reason not to be informed.
