# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some validation and nonjudgment, but the advice-giving stance can feel directive and managing. Acceptance is present but inconsistent, especially after the client says she feels managed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are reasonable reflections of ambivalence and conflict, particularly around stress relief and protecting her son. The reflections could be deeper and less quickly followed by practical advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking and its impact on her son without major drift. However, the direction becomes heavily logistical rather than consistently exploring motivation or change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers options, and adjusts suggestions based on the client’s feedback. Still, the interaction often feels more counselor-led than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive language is used, such as offering no-pressure options and avoiding direct commands. However, repeated prompts to select a step may undermine her sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Early questions draw out some of the client’s concerns, but the counselor quickly shifts to advice and practical options. There is limited effort to elicit the client’s own reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some responsiveness by adapting to the client’s objections and inviting choices. Nevertheless, the overall stance remains fairly directive, contributing to the client feeling managed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers several statements of change talk, but the psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or develops them. The response tends to move toward harm-reduction advice rather than strengthening the client’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client’s sustain talk. However, there is limited strategic redirection of statements like “I can’t just turn it off” toward change or discrepancy.
