# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms Erik’s experience and choice. It falls short of a 5 because the stance shifts quickly into problem-solving and lacks deeper, individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Erik’s ambivalence, frustration, and concerns about embarrassment and failure. The empathy is strong but not consistently deep because reflections are often followed by advice or multiple questions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use and narrows the conversation into a concrete, stepwise plan. It is strong but incomplete because the transcript lacks a final summary or closing consolidation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik’s ideas meaningfully shape the plan, showing real collaborative elements. However, the psychiatrist frequently supplies options and practical tips before fully inviting Erik to generate solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects Erik’s choice and avoids pressuring commitment. The frequent provision of practical and medical advice slightly reduces the emphasis on Erik’s full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and the confidence ruler effectively elicit Erik’s own change talk and plan. The evocation is effective but not consistently pure because it is mixed with advice, options, and tips.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Erik’s input and allows his choices to shape the direction. This is limited by a repeated expert-advisor role, especially when multiple options and practical strategies are introduced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively strengthens Erik’s desire, ability, and commitment through affirmations and focused questions. It could be stronger by more explicitly reflecting and amplifying Erik’s values and ability talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Erik’s sustain talk and redirects it toward manageable change. It is not a 5 because double-sided reflections and deeper ambivalence work are not consistently used.
