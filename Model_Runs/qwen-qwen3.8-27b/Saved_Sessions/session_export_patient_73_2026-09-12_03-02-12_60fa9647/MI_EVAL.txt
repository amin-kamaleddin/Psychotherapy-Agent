# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes struggle while reframing relapse as information. There are only occasional mild evaluative labels that limit fully individualized acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures the client’s tension between belonging and change and validates emotional difficulty. Some reflections remain surface-level, with missed opportunities to explore shame, identity, and fear of judgment more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on tobacco use and narrows well to a specific trigger and coping experiment. It could benefit from stronger strategic summarization to keep the direction tightly anchored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is clear joint problem-solving, with options considered, client-selected strategies, and brief rehearsal. The psychiatrist supplies much of the content and framing, so the collaboration is not fully balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects choice, avoids pressure, and honors client preferences such as avoiding medication and a backup plan. More explicit language about client ownership of decisions would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-driven ideas and small steps the client is willing to try. Evocation is limited in depth and frequency, with a shift toward advice and skill-building rather than the client’s own motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, asks preferences, and co-creates a small plan in a collaborative rather than directive way. It falls short of a perfect score because the counselor provides much of the ideas and content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is elicited and affirmed, such as willingness to try gum and a 10-minute delay. The session leans more toward problem-solving and psychoeducation than systematically amplifying the client’s reasons, values, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by not arguing with reasons for continuing smoking and reframing failure non-punitively. The final sustain talk remains somewhat unresolved, with less consistent strategic redirection toward change.
