# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Milan without shaming. The acceptance is slightly weakened by frequent advice-giving, which can make the session feel more corrective than fully accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate content reflections about Milan’s drinking and desire not to be judged. Empathy is limited because it often remains factual and misses the frustration, defensiveness, and need for autonomy underneath Milan’s statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with a clear focus on alcohol use but quickly broadens into multiple topics, including harm-reduction tips, scripts, and school stress. There is limited strategic movement toward Milan’s own goals, values, or a coherent change-related focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and offers options, creating some appearance of joint decision-making. However, the interaction remains expert-driven because Milan’s repeated preferences to avoid tips, scripts, and planning are not used to reshape the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently affirms Milan’s right to choose and avoids pushing abstinence or a specific plan. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated offers of structured options that can subtly shift the session toward the counselor’s agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some initial open-ended questions, but the psychiatrist does not follow up by evoking Milan’s motivations, values, or concerns. The conversation repeatedly shifts into advice, which limits the emergence of client-driven change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some gestures toward partnership by offering choices and asking for Milan’s focus. The session remains largely expert-driven, with repeated provision of scripts, harm-reduction tips, and plans despite Milan’s resistance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is very little strategic effort to draw out Milan’s reasons, desires, abilities, or need for change. The psychiatrist largely offers advice instead of using evocative questions to expand change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Milan’s sustain talk and does not challenge statements such as “I’m not an alcoholic.” However, the response is mostly acknowledgement rather than effective softening, reframing, or gentle pivoting to explore ambivalence.
