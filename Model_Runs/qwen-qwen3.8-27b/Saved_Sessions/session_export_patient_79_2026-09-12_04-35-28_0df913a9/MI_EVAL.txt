# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids labeling or arguing with Luis’s resistance. Acceptance is strong but occasionally remains subtly clinical rather than fully affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is generally accurate and captures Luis’s frustration, overload, and irritation with being lectured. Empathy could be stronger by more deeply reflecting underlying fear, shame, or concerns about being judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management and moves toward a concrete, manageable action step. Direction could be improved by more consistently linking the chosen behavior to Luis’s personally meaningful outcomes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Luis is invited to choose among options and his preferences shape the final plan. Collaboration is effective but remains somewhat interviewer-led because the psychiatrist often provides structured choices before Luis generates his own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes choice, honors pacing, and reduces all-or-nothing thinking around missed doses. Autonomy support is strong but slightly weakened by the repeated offering of specific solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions invite Luis’s input, but the psychiatrist often shifts to concrete solutions before fully exploring his motivations, values, or confidence. Evocation is limited by a more logistical than motivational approach.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and follows Luis’s lead in choosing a small step. Partnership could be stronger by more consistently treating Luis as the expert and inviting client-generated ideas before offering suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Luis offers several change-oriented commitments, such as using the zip-top bag when needed. The psychiatrist could more skillfully amplify, deepen, and reinforce this change talk by exploring its meaning, importance, and value.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without direct confrontation, which helps reduce resistance. The psychiatrist could use more double-sided reflections and strategic redirection to move the conversation toward change.
