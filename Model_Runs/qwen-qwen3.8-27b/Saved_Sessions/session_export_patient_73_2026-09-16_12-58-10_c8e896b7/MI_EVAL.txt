# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized Linda’s ambivalence. Affirmations reduced shame, though warmth was occasionally formulaic and did not reach fully unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong, with some complex reflections of ambivalence and practical concerns. Deeper emotions such as fear, loneliness, identity, and repeated failure were not fully explored, and the final emotional statement was not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on tobacco use, especially the morning cigarette and small experiments. Direction was clear but occasionally felt more like guided planning than fully client-identified direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and honored Linda’s preferences, creating a collaborative tone. The plan was still somewhat structured by the psychiatrist, so collaboration was strong but not fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently supported choice and avoided coercion. However, repeatedly introducing plan elements after Linda said she was not ready may have created mild pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some of Linda’s own thinking through questions about past attempts and what would help. Evocation was limited by a reliance on suggestions and insufficient exploration of deeper values, hopes, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda’s preferences clearly shaped the session, and questions supported shared decision-making. Early introduction of several concrete options made the interaction somewhat expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evoked some change talk and affirmed small steps. There were missed opportunities to deepen change talk with stronger MI questions or to fully amplify Linda’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepted Linda’s sustain talk without arguing and reduced defensiveness. The work was more de-escalation than strategic softening, with limited use of double-sided reflections to pivot toward change.
