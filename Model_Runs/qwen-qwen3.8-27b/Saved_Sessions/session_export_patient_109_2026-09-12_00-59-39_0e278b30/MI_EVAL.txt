# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing tone. Acceptance is generally strong but feels somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes adequate reflections of Rafael’s concerns and feelings. Empathy is inconsistent because it often shifts quickly into advice rather than deeply exploring ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on returning to the gym and making manageable first steps. Direction is strong, though it occasionally moves too early into detailed behavioral planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rafael contributes to the plan, and the psychiatrist responds to his preferences. Collaboration is present but uneven because the psychiatrist remains the primary source of strategies and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, respects Rafael’s limits, and reduces guilt around missed days. Autonomy support is helpful but does not consistently emphasize Rafael as the expert on his own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some initial open questions, but the psychiatrist moves quickly into providing advice and plans. There is limited effort to evoke Rafael’s own reasons, values, confidence, and motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adapts the plan based on Rafael’s preferences. Partnership is evident, but the interaction is more therapist-led than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rafael expresses some change talk, and the psychiatrist acknowledges it in general terms. The psychiatrist does not consistently explore, amplify, or expand Rafael’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes concerns and reduces shame around failure and pressure. Some sustain talk is effectively softened, but the psychiatrist also spends substantial time responding directly to reasons for not changing.
