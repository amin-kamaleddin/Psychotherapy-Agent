# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout the session. The psychiatrist never labels, judges, or expresses disapproval, fully honoring the client's dignity and choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates skilled complex reflections that capture subtext and hidden feelings beneath the client's deflection. However, empathy doesn't consistently reach the deeper emotional layers (e.g., fear of losing independence, frustration of being told you need medication when you feel fine).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a general focus on medication adherence but the session lacks clear strategic momentum. The conversation feels circular—drifting between exploration and validation without building toward a concrete focus or action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance in language and intent, consistently framing the conversation as a joint exploration. However, the collaboration remains somewhat superficial, with questions that are generic rather than deeply building on the client's specific contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally strong and consistent throughout. The psychiatrist never uses persuasive, coercive, or directive language, repeatedly reinforcing the client's right to self-direction with explicit power-sharing statements.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses appropriate open questions consistently but lacks the strategic depth needed to elicit motivation from a precontemplative client. Missing more powerful techniques such as values exploration, scaling questions, or future-focused questions to create personal relevance for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicit power-sharing language ('You're in the driver's seat') and a genuine non-directive stance. However, the session structure remains largely psychiatrist-led in practice, without explicit co-creation of an agenda or shared decision-making about direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rare and weak efforts to cultivate change talk; no use of scaling questions, values exploration, or strategic elicitation of desire, ability, need, or values. Questions are not calibrated to evoke change talk, leaving it minimal and underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client's sustain talk. However, the psychiatrist does not actively soften or redirect it—no double-sided reflections or strategic pivoting—and some responses (e.g., 'You're right that they're just pills') inadvertently reinforce the minimization.
