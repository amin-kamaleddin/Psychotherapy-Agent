# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and accepts ambivalence without arguing. It falls slightly short of a 5 because warmth feels strategic and there is limited explicit affirmation of the person separate from the behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s practical and social ambivalence about connection and healing. Empathy could be deeper by naming underlying feelings such as fear of rejection, FOMO, or identity concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on alcohol use, ankle healing, functioning, and social routines. The progression from values to problem-solving to a low-risk test plan is logical and coherent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on the client’s own suggestions and frames the work as co-created. It falls just short of a 5 because the therapist still leads the structure and occasionally introduces suggestive options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s choice is emphasized through questions and by returning ownership of the plan. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the client’s right not to change and checking comfort with the direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively elicit the client’s own motivations, tradeoffs, and self-evaluations. Evocation could be stronger by drawing out broader values, identity, long-term goals, and confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that the client is the expert on his own life and social context. Partnership could be stronger with more explicit power-sharing language and agenda invitation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits desire, reasons, and workability through client-generated planning. It falls short of a 5 because confidence, commitment, and identity-based change talk are not systematically deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without being reinforced, often using double-sided reflections. Softening could be stronger by reframing status-quo concerns toward autonomy, values, or a workable alternative.
