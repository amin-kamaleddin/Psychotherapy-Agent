# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and reassuring about missed or incomplete tracking. The tone is slightly procedural and could be more warmly personalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are adequate but stay mostly surface-level and task-focused. The counselor misses deeper feelings about avoidance, anxiety, shame, and the emotional weight of seeing drinking more clearly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is well organized and stays focused on drinking and a concrete tracking plan. Direction is practical and clear, though it emphasizes task completion more than strategic motivation-building.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed, with the patient choosing the cue, format, and follow-up. The first half is somewhat expert-driven, presenting a fairly complete plan before fully opening space for input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through permission, choices, and respectful acceptance of the patient's limits. Some early prescriptive details slightly weaken the autonomy message.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor begins with useful open-ended inquiry but quickly shifts into advising and information-giving. There are missed opportunities to draw out the patient's deeper values, motivations, and change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor positions the patient as the decision-maker and follows the patient's lead on plan details. The opening is somewhat prescriptive, though partnership becomes stronger as the session continues.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is acknowledged and invited, but it is not consistently reinforced, amplified, or deepened. The counselor moves too quickly from motivation to practical planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without arguing, dismissing, or pressuring the patient. The response could be strengthened by more active softening, such as exploring the function of the last drink while still honoring the patient's choice.
