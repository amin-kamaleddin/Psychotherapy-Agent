# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, validating the client's courage and reframing without dismissal. Slightly formulaic in places and lacks an explicit statement of unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate, nuanced reflections that capture both content and emotion. A few moments where the reflection could have stayed longer with unnamed feelings before moving to interpretation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus but is somewhat diffuse, moving through multiple topics without consolidation. No summary or narrowing to a single target behavior or concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative stance that builds on the client's own language and ideas without imposing an expert framework. Remains in the facilitative range rather than fully co-creative in meaning-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent and strong autonomy support with no advice-giving, explicit permission to decline, and no agenda imposition. Could be elevated by more actively affirming the client's ownership of decisions in moments of self-doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of open-ended questions and value-based reflections to draw out the client's own motivations and change talk. Falls short by not reinforcing or amplifying the change talk that emerges, and by not using readiness or confidence rulers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames the session as collaborative and regularly defers to the client's expertise in her own life. Could be stronger by more explicitly communicating power-sharing and confirming the client is comfortable with the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicits meaningful change talk (wanting to be present, not running on a half-tank, the chef game image) but does not consistently reinforce or amplify it to deepen the motivation. Misses opportunities to reflect back and consolidate the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoids challenging sustain talk directly but does not actively work to soften or redirect it. Misses the opportunity for double-sided reflections (e.g., acknowledging the wine's function while also exploring the cost) or exploring what's worth preserving.
