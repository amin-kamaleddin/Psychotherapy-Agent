# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgmental respect with affirming phrases throughout, accepting the small step rather than pushing a larger goal. Slightly formulaic and occasionally administrative, lacking deeper affirmation of Dante's value independent of the exercise plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening with accurate reflections on hesitation, tiredness, and concern about making too big a deal. Stays largely cognitive and practical; could have explored deeper emotional meaning such as grief over lost routine or fear of failing again.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior with no topic drift, progressively narrowing from broad goal to a specific Tuesday-after-baths plan with fallback and cue. Clear change-oriented direction while remaining client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options rather than prescribing, and Dante actively selects and modifies plan elements like involving his wife and the five-minute fallback. Not fully equal because the psychiatrist supplies much of the structure, strategies, and sequence rather than deriving them more from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong repeated invitations for Dante's choice with explicit autonomy statements like 'you absolutely have that choice' and reinforcement of his own 'Then I can stop.' Avoids coercion, frames the plan as an optional experiment, and gives clear permission to stop.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions elicit goals and increase confidence from 4 to 7, but the early question about past restarts is not followed up. Moves quickly into practical problem-solving before fully developing Dante's internal motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites Dante's input and his contributions shape the plan, but the psychiatrist frequently assumes the expert role by supplying strategies and structuring the plan. Needs more explicit client-as-expert positioning and permission-seeking before offering information.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits desire and need for change through questions about benefits, small steps, and confidence, with Dante producing several change-talk statements. Good but not fully amplified; could have reflected back and deepened his own statements or asked what a higher confidence score would mean.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** One of the strongest parts: Dante's tiredness, couch pull, fade concern, and reluctance to make a big deal are all normalized without argument. Consistently reframes toward a doable, nonthreatening step with explicit permission to stop, maintaining a clear pivot toward change.
