# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, using affirming language such as “that sounds workable.” The stance is professional but somewhat task-oriented, with limited deeper validation of Andrei’s perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathy is limited; the psychiatrist offers practical understanding and one normalizing statement but does not strongly reflect Andrei’s ambivalence or uncertainty. There is little reflection of his minimizing style or inner experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear practical focus on blood pressure monitoring, logging, walking, and follow-up. Direction is somewhat weakened by drift into alarms, message drafting, and limited exploration of alcohol as a key target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying much of the plan and content. Andrei is invited to adjust minor details but is not truly engaged in co-creating goals or priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some respect for choices, such as asking about preferred options and accepting his preference not to use a breathing script. However, the overall style remains directive, with detailed instructions that may reduce ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist briefly evokes Andrei’s reasons with an initial open question, but mostly shifts to information-gathering and advice-giving. There is little sustained exploration of his values, confidence, or motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist takes a knowledge-expert role and sets most of the agenda. Andrei’s expertise about his own preferences, schedule, and concerns is not deeply integrated into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is a modest opening invitation for Andrei to identify what he wants to change or understand, plus mild reinforcement. The psychiatrist does not consistently elicit, amplify, or build on change talk through reflections or targeted questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Andrei’s minimizing or resistant statements and sometimes offers lower-burden alternatives. However, he does not consistently use MI skills such as reflections or gentle redirection to soften sustain talk and pivot toward change.
