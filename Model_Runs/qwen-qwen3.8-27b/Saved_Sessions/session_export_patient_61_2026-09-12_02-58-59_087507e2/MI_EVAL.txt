# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance, including respecting the client's choice to pause. Acceptance is strong but not perfect because some language feels scripted, repetitive, and procedural.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's burden, time pressure, and desire to return to normal. Empathy is generally good but remains more cognitive than deep, without fully exploring underlying fear, loss of control, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on diabetes self-management and repeatedly narrows toward a small, manageable step. Direction is clear but lacks strategic momentum, such as a brief MI-consistent summary to consolidate themes and keep a door open.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers options, and uses inclusive language to support equal-partner engagement. Collaboration is strong but not fully realized because the clinician continues proposing micro-interventions while the client minimally engages.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with consistent statements such as following the client's lead, no pressure, and respecting the client's choice to end. The psychiatrist does not coerce, persuade, or impose tasks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions about what feels hardest, what a small step might be, and what normal means. However, evocation is superficial and misses key opportunities to explore the client's own reasons, values, and concern about his numbers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates a partnership stance and avoids a didactic or authoritarian approach. Partnership is strong but partly verbal and scripted, with repeated formulations that may feel less co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes reasonable evocative attempts, and the client offers limited change talk about his numbers and willingness to call the doctor. The main limitation is that this emerging change talk is not amplified, reflected back, or explored in a way that builds motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing with or correcting the client's sustain talk and validates the desire to pause. However, the responses often accept the status quo rather than strategically using double-sided reflections to highlight ambivalence and concern.
