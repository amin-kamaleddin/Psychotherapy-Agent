# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Kenji’s ambivalence and discomfort with logging. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural, with limited explicit warmth or affirmation of Kenji’s courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Empathy is demonstrated through accurate summaries and reflections of Kenji’s perspective, including the tension between quiet, fog, and feeling judged by the log. Empathy remains mainly content-level and does not deeply explore underlying emotions or meanings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation remains focused on alcohol use and related awareness, with little topic drift. The plan becomes somewhat narrow and logistical, with less explicit connection to the broader goal of reducing or reevaluating drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is highly collaborative, with options offered and Kenji’s choices clearly shaping the final plan. Minor limitation: the psychiatrist still drives much of the structure and sequencing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, with repeated emphasis on Kenji’s freedom to choose, no pressure to change, and a forgiving, low-effort plan. The psychiatrist consistently frames the plan as Kenji’s own and respects boundaries around monitoring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, client-centered questions to evoke Kenji’s own motivations, concerns, and confidence rather than providing advice. Deeper evocation of values, identity, or future-oriented reasons for change would strengthen this criterion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative, non-expert posture and invites Kenji to make key decisions about the plan. Partnership is strong but not fully explicit in positioning Kenji as the expert in his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes change talk using trade-off questions and confidence/importance rulers, leading to Kenji articulating intention and a concrete plan. Some opportunities to amplify or consolidate change talk were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through normalization, avoiding argument, and reframing the log as a small, private, forgiving experiment. The response could more strategically integrate ambivalence by exploring both sides of Kenji’s concerns.
