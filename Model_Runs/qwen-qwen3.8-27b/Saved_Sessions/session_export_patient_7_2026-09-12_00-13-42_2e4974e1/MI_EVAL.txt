# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates respect, nonjudgment, and acceptance while honoring the client’s minimal disclosure. However, repeatedly offering options after the client asks to be listened to slightly undermines a fully accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers appropriate reflections about the loudness and exhaustion in the client’s life. The empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully name deeper feelings of being unheard, controlled, or feared to be pushed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays generally focused on immediate safety and the client’s presenting experience. Direction becomes less smooth when the psychiatrist repeatedly returns to structured menus of options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and adapts when the client chooses to vent. The repeated presentation of multiple options, however, can make the interaction feel more structured than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports autonomy by affirming the client’s control, privacy, and right to share only what she chooses. This could be improved by stepping back more fully rather than repeatedly offering new choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites some initial client input, especially early in the session. Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist does not consistently draw out the client’s own meanings, motivations, or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are clear attempts at partnership through offering choices and respecting the client’s stated preference. The repeated clinician-led menus keep the session from feeling fully client-led and equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client shows small signs of willingness, such as choosing to vent and staying present. The psychiatrist misses several opportunities to reflect and strengthen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly avoids argument and respects the client’s boundaries. Softening sustain talk is moderate because key statements about being pushed are not met with a deeper rolling-with-resistance response.
