# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance throughout. The warmth was somewhat efficient and could have more explicitly normalized the emotional conflict around kindness, guilt, and boundary-setting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist reflected key tensions and ambivalence but often stayed at the practical level. There were missed opportunities to explore deeper identity conflict, guilt, fear, and concern about losing closeness before moving into scripts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the target behavior and deadline. Direction became somewhat over-focused on tactical wording and script delivery rather than internal motivation and values-based commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative role-play and real-time coaching effectively. Collaboration was limited at times by the therapist frequently acting as an expert supplying scripts and options before the client generated his own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist offered choices and honored the client’s preferences. Autonomy support was reduced by frequent directive language, multiple supplied scripts, and a plan that placed the therapist in a more controlling coaching role.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some evocative questions and the role-play elicited useful self-evaluation. Evocation was uneven because the therapist often supplied reasons, scripts, and strategies instead of drawing out the client’s own motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input, incorporated the client’s choices, and collaborated on the role-play. The partnership was not fully MI-ideal because the therapist often took a problem-solving expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered meaningful change talk and the therapist affirmed some of it. The therapist did not consistently evoke, amplify, or expand the client’s own desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist responded respectfully to the client’s guilt and fear of being the villain. Softening was limited by frequent redirection into scripts and tactics, with less use of double-sided reflections or value-based reframing.
