# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Malik’s frustration, unfairness, and sense of violation. Warmth is somewhat formulaic, and occasional blunt safety statements or endorsement of the client’s minimization slightly reduce the empathy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Malik’s anger, feeling invaded, and feeling infantilized, but the reflections are sometimes repetitive and surface-level. Deeper themes such as trust, autonomy, and the conflict between independence and his mother’s concern are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session does not maintain a clear focus on cannabis use or motivation for change. It drifts into venting, safety checking, psychoeducation, and parent-conflict problem-solving without a consistent strategic line toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely collaborative, and the psychiatrist respects Malik’s preferences and rejections. However, the repeated use of therapist-generated menu-style options can make the session feel less client-led than fully co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids lecturing, pressure, and coercion, and consistently invites Malik to choose among options. Some repeated safety conclusions and suggested interventions feel slightly directive and surveillance-like, slightly weakening the autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open questions and checks problems or positives, but quickly shifts toward offering options, scripts, harm-reduction tips, and problem-solving. There is limited effort to elicit Malik’s own values, goals, discrepancies, or internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-expert-driven stance and respects the client’s choices. However, the session does not fully position Malik as the expert on his own life, values, and relationships, and often relies on clinician-generated interventions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Malik offers some change-relevant statements about autonomy, trust, and self-direction, but the psychiatrist does not effectively reflect, amplify, or build on them. The session lacks deliberate evocation of importance, confidence, values, or future-oriented change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Malik’s minimizing and peer-normalizing statements, but does not consistently use double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancy. As a result, the session does not effectively reduce the dominance of sustain talk around cannabis use.
