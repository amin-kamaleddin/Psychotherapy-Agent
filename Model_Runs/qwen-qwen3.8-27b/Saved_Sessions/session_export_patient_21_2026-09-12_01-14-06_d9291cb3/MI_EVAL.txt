# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful and nonjudgmental tone, using low-pressure language such as “no judgment if you don’t reply.” However, the acceptance felt somewhat task-focused rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathy was mostly factual and focused on plan logistics rather than the client’s emotional experience. A key missed opportunity occurred when the client’s fear of being seen as “lazy” was not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on increasing physical activity and produced a clear, concrete behavioral plan. Direction was strong, though the approach was more directive than exploratory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some collaboration in finalizing tracker details, timing, and the definition of “off” days. However, the psychiatrist remained the primary architect of the plan, limiting true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and reduced pressure by normalizing missed days. Nevertheless, the overall framework was clinician-directed, and the client had to negotiate boundaries around check-ins.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions were largely logistical rather than evocative, with little effort to draw out the client’s values, motivations, or confidence. The client’s motivation was mostly pre-existing and not strategically expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopted an expert-driven, consultative style, especially when presenting practical options. The client had to actively pull the conversation toward workability and partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was limited active elicitation, reflection, or amplification of the client’s change talk. When the client expressed interest in her morning routine, the psychiatrist shifted to logistical planning rather than exploring its meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk and ambivalence about check-ins were largely bypassed with practical adjustments rather than explored. Double-sided reflections or strategic redirection were absent.
