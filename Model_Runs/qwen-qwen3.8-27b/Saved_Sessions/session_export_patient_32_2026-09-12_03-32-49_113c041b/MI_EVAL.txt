# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Derrick’s concerns without shame. Acceptance was strong but somewhat procedural, with limited deeper affirmation of Derrick’s worth, strengths, or emotional difficulty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor provided accurate, mostly practical reflections of Derrick’s concerns about his license, job, and legal consequences. Empathy remained largely cognitive, with less exploration of underlying emotions such as fear, shame, ambivalence, or pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused and practical, mainly around the DUI, license, work, and legal next steps. Direction would be stronger if the counselor more consistently connected the legal urgency to changes in drinking behavior, safer choices, and relapse prevention.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated well by offering options, asking Derrick to choose, and using his preferences to shape the plan. The collaboration was slightly limited by the counselor providing much of the practical content and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supported Derrick’s autonomy by asking permission, offering choices, and respecting his desire to keep matters low-key. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly emphasizing that final decisions remain Derrick’s and by eliciting his own next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used appropriate evocative techniques, such as open questions and confidence scaling. Evocation was mainly practical and did not deeply explore Derrick’s values, fears, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a collaborative, nonauthoritarian stance by inviting Derrick’s input and allowing his preferences to guide the session. Partnership would be stronger with less counselor-led structure and more elicitation of Derrick’s own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk, including Derrick’s willingness to follow through, keep his job, and start a log. The counselor could have expanded further on Derrick’s values, strengths, and what would increase his confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoided arguing with Derrick’s minimize language and resistance to the interlock or employer disclosure. However, the session did not use strong MI strategies, such as double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance, to soften sustain talk.
