# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was polite, nonjudgmental, and respectful, but acceptance remained clinically neutral. There was limited explicit affirmation of Matteo’s honesty, effort, or recovery-related values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic responding was limited to brief acknowledgments rather than MI-style reflections. The psychiatrist missed key opportunities to explore Matteo’s ambivalence about drinking, social connection, and ankle healing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifted substantially toward ankle rehabilitation, pain management, and logistics. The target behavior of alcohol use was addressed only briefly and was not maintained as the central MI focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made some collaborative invitations and incorporated Matteo’s preferences, but most of the structure and advice came from the clinician. A stronger MI approach would have co-created the plan more explicitly with Matteo.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used several autonomy-supportive phrases and avoided overt coercion, but advice remained fairly prescriptive. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit affirmation that Matteo owns the decision about drinking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The conversation was largely didactic and medical, with limited evocation of Matteo’s own motivations, values, or change reasons. Client statements that could become change talk were not reflected, expanded, or deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although the psychiatrist asked some preference-based questions, the session was largely expert-driven. Matteo’s expertise about his own priorities, social life, and preferences was not fully centered in the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were few attempts to evoke or strengthen change talk related to safer drinking. When Matteo offered statements such as “I’ll keep it low key,” the psychiatrist did not reflect, amplify, or build on them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing or directly challenging Matteo’s sustain talk about drinking being normal or harmless. However, responses such as “That sounds reasonable” risked normalizing the status quo without resolving ambivalence.
