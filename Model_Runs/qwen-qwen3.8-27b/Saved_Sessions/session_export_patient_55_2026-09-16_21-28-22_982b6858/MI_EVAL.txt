# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating the client’s need for a clear, brief process. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and does not fully convey unconditional positive regard beyond the task.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s stated preference for a straightforward, non-grilling format. The empathy remains mostly cognitive and does not explore underlying feelings such as anxiety, embarrassment, or stress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays broadly on alcohol and the screening, but quickly becomes closure-oriented. It does not progressively clarify the target behavior or connect drinking to the client’s goals and roles.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is repeatedly invited to choose the pace, format, and documentation. Collaboration is evident but remains procedural rather than extending to the substantive alcohol-risk issue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client’s desire to avoid being grilled and does not pressure her to stay or elaborate. Autonomy support is strong but focuses mainly on session structure rather than future alcohol-related decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some initial questions draw out the client’s own frame and preferences. There is no consistent effort to evoke values, concerns, confidence, or reasons for change related to alcohol use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is clearly positioned as an expert on her own experience and shapes the session’s structure and closure. Partnership is strong but narrow, focusing more on format than on exploring the screening concern.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some client-centered questions but does not actively develop change talk. Opportunities to connect alcohol use to music, IB, or future goals are not used to evoke motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Sustain talk is largely accepted and reinforced by agreeing that use is low/occasional and no action is needed. The psychiatrist does not gently explore ambivalence or use double-sided responses to soften the client’s sustain position.
