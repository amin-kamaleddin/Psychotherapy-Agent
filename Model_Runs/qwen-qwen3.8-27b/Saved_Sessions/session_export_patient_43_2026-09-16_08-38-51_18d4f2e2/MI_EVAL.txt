# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids pressuring Eric to quit. Acceptance is slightly limited by formulaic responses that lack deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses reflective listening and accurately captures Eric’s ambivalence and emotional burden. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level because stronger underlying feelings are not deeply explored or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a clear focus on smoking and health, but quickly drifts toward conflict management and boundary-setting. Direction is present but does not consistently guide the session toward smoking-related change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, including invitations for Eric’s input and use of his own language. However, the psychiatrist generates most of the solutions, making the process more counselor-led than fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a strength, with repeated offers of choice and respectful acceptance of Eric’s rejections. The frequent focus on suggesting scripts and plans may subtly pressure Eric to adopt a behavior change strategy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some evocative attempts by asking about readiness and what smoking provides. Evocation is inconsistent because the session shifts to problem-solving instead of drawing out Eric’s own motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and builds on Eric’s language, but often assumes a problem-solving role. A stronger partnership would invite Eric to generate more of his own ideas and affirm his expertise over his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only limited efforts to evoke desire, need, ability, or reasons for change. Small openings related to the cough, willingness to listen, and desire to reduce conflict are not developed into meaningful change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Eric’s sustain talk but rarely redirects it toward possible change. Statements that reflect Eric’s current position can inadvertently reinforce the status quo instead of softly challenging it.
