# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Julian's feelings and motivations without judgment or minimization, respecting his framing of the situation. The acceptance is strong but occasionally borders on over-elaboration, giving it a slightly constructed quality rather than feeling fully natural.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is exceptionally nuanced and accurate, capturing both stated meaning and emotional subtext in real time. Complex reflections such as naming Julian's passive voice and the social weight of calling the case worker demonstrate high-level empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, logical progression from understanding the situation to identifying a concrete first step without wandering off-topic. However, the psychiatrist misses the opportunity to close with a commitment question that would solidify the plan into a specific, time-bound action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively co-constructs the planning process with Julian, using open questions to build on his own criteria and ideas. Collaboration could be strengthened by more explicit checks on whether Julian wants to continue in this direction or would prefer to focus elsewhere.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently credits Julian for his own thinking, never imposes direction, and refuses to inflate his agency beyond what he has claimed. Language throughout uses 'what would work for you' rather than 'you should,' leaving all decisions firmly in Julian's domain.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits change talk and planning through open questions rather than providing advice or information. Evocation could go deeper by reaching into values-based motivation rather than relying primarily on the avoidance-based 'I don't want to be in more trouble.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Julian as the expert on his own situation and delegates planning decisions to him throughout. The partnership is slightly weakened by moments where the psychiatrist's skillfully constructed reflections create a subtle dynamic of dominance over the conversational architecture.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively amplifies Julian's change talk by naming and giving weight to his tentative plans and decisions. The evocation could be stronger by using strategic questions to deepen confidence talk and elicit additional reasons for change beyond avoiding trouble.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates Julian's resistance and discomfort without arguing against or reinforcing it, consistently redirecting toward action. A 5 would require more explicit double-sided reflections to fully resolve the ambivalence in real time.
