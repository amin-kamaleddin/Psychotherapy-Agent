# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone is maintained throughout. Acceptance could become stronger with more individualized validation of Liora’s worth and confusion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the ambivalence, relief, and exhaustion around self-harm. Empathy could deepen by exploring underlying emotions such as shame, fear, or hopelessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on self-harm and distress management. It lacks clear momentum toward a consolidated, actionable next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and the client’s language are used effectively. The process still feels more therapist-guided than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Noncontrolling language and small-step framing reduce pressure. Clearer permission to remain stuck and explicit ownership language would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites Liora to describe possible relief, quiet, and small tests. Internal motivations, values, and confidence are not fully drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses collaborative questions and follows the client’s language. There is limited explicit power-sharing, agenda negotiation, or client-led direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some meaningful change talk is elicited, such as not wanting to keep cutting. Change talk remains brief and is not consistently expanded through evocative follow-ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing and acknowledges why cutting feels necessary. Repeated focus on why alternatives fail keeps sustain talk centered rather than gently pivoting toward change.
