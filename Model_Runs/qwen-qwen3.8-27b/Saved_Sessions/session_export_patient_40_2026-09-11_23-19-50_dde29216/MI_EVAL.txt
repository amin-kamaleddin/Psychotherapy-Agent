# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows general respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while validating Elias’s fear, shame, and effort. The score is limited because repeated crisis framing and safety language can overshadow affirmation of his recovery progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are genuine, accurate reflections of Elias’s pain and the meaning of his children’s drawings. However, the core ambivalence about whether he stopped by choice or under pressure is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on relapse, safety, and maintaining recovery momentum through practical next steps. The focus is slightly diluted by repeated crisis scripting and emphasis on the word “suicidal.”

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well on the sticky-note plan by offering options and incorporating Elias’s preferences for wording, format, and privacy. Collaboration is less strong on the broader agenda, which remains largely clinician-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias is offered choices and his refusals are accepted, especially around the sticky-note plan and messaging. Autonomy support is weakened by directive safety steps and recommendations that are not always framed as Elias’s choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few useful questions that begin to elicit Elias’s own reasons for change, such as what stopped him. The session then moves into prescriptive safety planning without further evoking his values, hopes, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows Elias to shape the final safety note. However, much of the session is clinician-led, and Elias is not consistently positioned as the expert on his own recovery.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias offers meaningful change talk, and the psychiatrist affirms some of it. The psychiatrist does not consistently ask questions that would deepen, amplify, or consolidate that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or punish Elias for his ambivalence or sustain talk. He could more effectively use double-sided reflections to explore uncertainty and connect it to change.
