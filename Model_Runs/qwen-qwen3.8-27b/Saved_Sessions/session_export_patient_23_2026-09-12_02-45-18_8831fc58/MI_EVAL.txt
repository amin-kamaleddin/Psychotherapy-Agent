# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone and respects Claudia's self-selected goal. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and does not consistently deepen around her fear of failure or feelings of judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate but often remain surface-level. Key emotional statements, such as fear of giving in or feeling monitored, are acknowledged only briefly before moving to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing evening alcohol use and moves logically toward a concrete plan. It becomes somewhat logistics-heavy and could more consistently connect back to Claudia's values and concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative planning, especially around Claudia's preferences for a simple, low-burden approach. However, the psychiatrist frequently supplies expert-generated options rather than consistently inviting Claudia to generate her own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Claudia's right to choose is clearly respected, and she is able to reject some suggestions without pressure. The psychiatrist still steers toward specific strategies and does not consistently reaffirm her full freedom to proceed differently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and action-focused prompts help Claudia articulate motivation and increase her confidence. Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist often shifts to advice before fully exploring Claudia's internal reasons, values, and resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is not authoritarian and Claudia's input shapes the final plan. Nevertheless, the psychiatrist often assumes the role of option-generator, which makes the partnership less balanced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk through scaling, action questions, and reinforcement of Claudia's chosen steps. The process could be deeper by exploring her personal values, confidence, and meaningful reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally handled without confrontation, and Claudia's resistance is accepted in a nonjudgmental way. Some sustain talk, such as concern about a friend judging her, is bypassed rather than gently reframed or linked back to change.
