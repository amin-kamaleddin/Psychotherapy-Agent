# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes the patient’s questions. The tone is warm but somewhat formulaic and does not deeply acknowledge the patient’s frustration with poor communication.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate acknowledgments of the patient’s relief and confidence. However, reflections remain light and miss deeper confusion, hesitation, and irritation about not being told what the medication was for.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited and mostly indirect effort to cultivate change talk. The psychiatrist reinforces the patient’s plan but does not strongly elicit or expand the patient’s own motivation for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and co-constructs parts of the plan, such as medication identification and follow-up questions. The interaction remains largely clinician-led, with the psychiatrist controlling the flow of information.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options, accepting the patient’s refusal to draft a script, and avoiding pressure. The interaction is still somewhat directive due to repeated practical reminders and information-giving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relies heavily on providing medication information rather than evoking the patient’s own reasons, values, or confidence. The patient’s increased confidence appears driven mainly by information rather than by MI-based elicitation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are partnership behaviors, such as asking for input and summarizing the plan positively. However, the patient mostly responds to clinician-led questions rather than shaping the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers some change-oriented statements, such as planning to switch to orange juice and start the medication. The psychiatrist reinforces this briefly but does not deeply develop or elaborate the patient’s own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates the patient’s caution and avoids reinforcing hesitation to start the medication. The handling of sustain talk remains neutral rather than using reflections or redirection to soften ambivalence.
