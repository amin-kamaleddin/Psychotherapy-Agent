# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated unconditional positive regard without judgment or moralizing. Acceptance was strong even when discussing self-harm and its function.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and nuanced, accurately capturing both the emotional experience and the function of self-harm. The psychiatrist demonstrated strong perspective-taking, especially when exploring the meaning behind highly charged language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remained strategically focused on reducing reliance on self-harm as a coping strategy. The psychiatrist consistently returned to specific, concrete moments of distress rather than allowing the conversation to drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative language was used throughout, and the session felt like a joint exploration rather than directive advice. However, the interaction remained therapist-led, and the client did not fully co-create the agenda or priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided pressure, advice, or directives while respecting the client’s limits. A stronger score would require more explicit affirmation of the client’s freedom to choose, including the choice not to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, evocative questions were used well to invite the client’s own perspective and possible ways forward. The main limitation was that evocation did not consistently develop or amplify change talk, confidence, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveyed that the client is the expert on her own experience and invited her perspective on priorities. However, the psychiatrist still controlled the session structure and pacing, limiting a fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some attempts to evoke change talk, but it was not systematically developed, amplified, or summarized. The session focused more on the problem and barriers than on strengthening the client’s own reasons, ability, or desire for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk well by avoiding argument and validating the client’s perspective without reinforcing it. A stronger score would require more consistent use of double-sided reflections to hold both the function of self-harm and emerging motivation for change.
