# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, with several supportive statements. However, acceptance feels more task-oriented than deeply warm, with limited affirmation of Mei's strengths, values, and insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is an adequate effort to understand Mei's practical and emotional concerns. However, reflections are often brief and quickly followed by advice, missing deeper exploration of her fear, self-doubt, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing alcohol use and improving contraceptive reliability. The main weakness is a shift toward practical risk management rather than consistently guiding the session toward Mei's readiness, values, and confidence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative questions and inclusive language. However, the session is largely driven by the psychiatrist's proposals rather than truly joint exploration or co-created ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Mei's choices in several places. However, the overall approach remains directive, with repeated detailed plans and scripts that can reduce Mei's sense of ownership and self-efficacy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some open and evocative questions are used, especially early on. However, the predominant mode is providing advice and practical strategies rather than eliciting Mei's own motivations, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and uses some collaborative language. However, the stance is mostly expert-driven, with the counselor functioning as the primary problem-solver rather than an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mei expresses some change talk, such as wanting to limit drinks and using the pill app. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently evoke, reinforce, or expand her own language about change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some sustain talk is normalized, such as doubt about following through. However, instead of using MI-congruent responses, the psychiatrist often adds more options and contingency plans, which may increase Mei's sense of overwhelm.
