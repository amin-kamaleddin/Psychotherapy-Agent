# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, avoiding labels and directly supporting the client’s concerns. Acceptance is somewhat task-focused and could be warmer or more personalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician shows reasonable perspective-taking and reflects some practical concerns, but empathy remains surface-level. There is limited exploration of shame, fear, regret, or lost travel dreams.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on gambling, debt-related spending, and practical change steps. The direction occasionally prioritizes action planning over connecting back to broader personal goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and accepts the client’s preferred wording, but the session is often counselor-driven. Frequent scripts, options, and checklists reduce space for the client to generate her own plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers reversible options and accepts the client’s choices, which supports autonomy. However, prescriptive checklists and scripts can make the process feel more directive than client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial questions are appropriate, but the session quickly shifts into advice and practical planning. There is limited use of MI-consistent questions, reflections, or values exploration to draw out intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and accepts her preferred text, showing some collaboration. Overall, the clinician maintains a fairly active directing role rather than consistently treating the client as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces some meaningful change talk, such as not wanting to be stuck or broke at 30. The psychiatrist validates these statements but does not consistently amplify or deepen them into a stronger motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk and offers low-pressure, reversible strategies. More skilled MI responses, such as double-sided reflections, could better hold and resolve ambivalence.
