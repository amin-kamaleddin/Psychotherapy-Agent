# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and did not criticize Thomas for smoking or alcohol use. Acceptance was strong but somewhat generic, with repeated crisis-line language that felt slightly formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective comments were present but largely surface-level, such as acknowledging that the friend's collapse sounded unsettling. The psychiatrist did not deeply explore Thomas's underlying concern, ambivalence, or emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session had some initial direction tied to referral concerns and a brief alcohol-use check. However, focus became inconsistent and ended without collaboratively narrowing a priority or establishing a clear next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a collaborative style by offering choices and avoiding an authoritarian stance. Collaboration was functional but not fully developed because the session ended before co-creating a concrete plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently supported by offering options and respecting Thomas's decision to leave. It could have been stronger by more explicitly affirming Thomas's ownership of decisions and capacity to choose in line with his goals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked an initial open question but did not follow through with deeper evocative questions about values, worries, or motivations. Thomas's own reasons for change were not meaningfully accessed or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally invited Thomas to direct the conversation and respected his choice to end the session. Partnership did not fully develop into deeper collaboration where Thomas's values and priorities shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were only weak attempts to evoke change talk, and the counselor did not build on Thomas's statements about quitting smoking or being a good dad. The session produced very little change-relevant motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Thomas expressed strong sustain talk, and the psychiatrist avoided arguing or reinforcing it. However, there was little strategic redirection, discrepancy work, or double-sided reflection to soften his minimization.
