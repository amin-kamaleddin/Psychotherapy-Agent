# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Amina’s fear, exhaustion, and caring concerns. However, acceptance is often formulaic and does not consistently deepen into individualized affirmation of her strengths or moral integrity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic responses acknowledge emotions but remain shallow and generic. The psychiatrist misses key nuances, such as Amina’s fear of appearing controlling, inauthentic, or ineffective when managing conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the presenting issue of co-parenting conflict and child safety. However, the direction becomes overly task-focused, shifting quickly to scripts and practical plans rather than clarifying client-defined goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely counselor-led, with the psychiatrist offering ready-made texts, options, and plans. Amina’s ideas and concerns do not meaningfully co-construct the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although the psychiatrist uses choice language, the overall stance remains directive. Autonomy is undermined by repeated offers of scripts and plans despite Amina’s expressed reluctance to take on that role.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with appropriate open questions but does not follow up to elicit Amina’s values, motivations, or confidence for change. Client-generated reasons for change are rarely drawn out or explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently adopts an expert, problem-solving role rather than positioning Amina as the expert on her family. Amina is invited to choose among pre-made options, but her own ideas and priorities are not meaningfully integrated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amina offers several valuable change talk statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on them. The conversation quickly shifts to practical scripts rather than strengthening her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or dismiss Amina’s concerns, and occasionally redirects toward child safety. However, skillful MI strategies such as double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, and reframing are limited.
