# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids shaming for nonadherence. Warmth is strong but somewhat formulaic, with limited deeper individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good reflective listening and captures several of Samir’s perspectives, including logistics and ambivalence. Some emotional layers under his humor are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on medication adherence, refills, reminders, and side effects. The session could better guide toward values, discrepancy, and clearer next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the session as joint work and incorporates Samir’s ideas about logistics and side effects. The approach can feel slightly expert-led due to structured questions and offered options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids pressure and consistently invites Samir’s input about what would be doable for him. Could more explicitly reinforce that he is ultimately in charge of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks open questions but sometimes supplies solution options, which can limit Samir’s own change language. Needs more elicitation of his reasons, values, and confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance and frequently asks for Samir’s perspective. Preconstructed options occasionally reduce the degree to which Samir leads the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflects some change statements but does not consistently amplify them. The session focuses more on logistics than on evoking and developing Samir’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Samir’s sustain talk is prominent, but the psychiatrist does not effectively shift focus away from it. More double-sided reflections and gentle exploration of values would help.
