# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated warm, nonjudgmental respect and unconditional positive regard. Shameful disclosures were normalized without correction, criticism, or moral labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both content and underlying emotion, including exhaustion, panic, fear, and ambivalence. The psychiatrist deepened understanding rather than simply paraphrasing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on sustainable food, exercise, and scale-related concerns without drifting. The conversation moved purposefully from ambivalence toward a realistic next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated the session as joint problem-solving, inviting the client to co-create the process. The client’s ideas were reflected, built upon, and made central to the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice was strongly emphasized through open questions and freedom to define what would work. The psychiatrist avoided controlling advice and reduced shame around eating concerns.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited the client’s own motivations, concerns, and possible next steps. Evocation could be strengthened by more deliberate scaling questions and follow-ups that amplify change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client’s contributions substantially shaped the direction of the conversation. The psychiatrist used collaborative language and avoided an expert-driven stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions successfully helped the client articulate goals, values, and sustainable actions. Change talk could be deepened with more targeted follow-ups on confidence, benefits, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validated ambivalence about weight and the scale without arguing or dismissing. More deliberate double-sided reflections could further reduce the scale’s dominance as proof.
