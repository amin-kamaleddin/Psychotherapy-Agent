# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician shows respect, avoids judgment, and affirms the patient’s perspective, though the warmth remains somewhat procedural. More explicit normalization of defensiveness would strengthen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers accurate reflections of ambivalence and the patient’s need for certainty, but misses deeper emotional validation. Greater reflection of the patient’s concern about being unfairly blamed would improve empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused and moves logically toward a concrete one-week test with clear tracking. The direction is more diagnostic than explicitly change-focused, so linking the test to broader reduction goals would strengthen it.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient’s ideas actively shape the plan, especially tracking and success criteria, showing genuine collaboration. The clinician still leads much of the structure, so stronger patient-driven agenda setting would be ideal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently uses choice-based language, avoids pressure, and affirms the patient’s ownership of decisions. This is a strong demonstration of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions effectively draw out the patient’s practical reasoning and personal standards. Evocation would be stronger if it more consistently targeted intrinsic motivation, values, and confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician shares power well and acknowledges the patient’s expertise about his own routine. A stronger sense of equal co-creation of the agenda would elevate this criterion.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits some action-oriented change talk around a short-term test, but change talk remains limited. Scaling questions and deeper exploration of reasons, values, and confidence would improve evocation of change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician handles sustain talk respectfully without arguing or correcting the patient. More active softening using double-sided reflections would help shift the conversation gently toward change.
