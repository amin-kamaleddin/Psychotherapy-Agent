# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalized Naomi’s concerns and physical reactions. Acceptance was strong but somewhat formulaic, with limited explicit affirmation of her worth beyond the anxiety.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Naomi’s immediate physical distress and experience of feeling cut off from the moment. However, empathy remained mostly surface-level and missed deeper themes of shame, fear of incompetence, and self-concept.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior and moved logically from triggers to desired change to a concrete coping plan. The direction became increasingly narrow and technique-focused, limiting broader exploration of motivation and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Naomi’s preferences influenced the final plan, and the psychiatrist used collaborative language while offering choices. The interaction remained largely clinician-led, with the agenda and options primarily generated by the psychiatrist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked permission, offered options, and avoided coercive language, supporting Naomi’s sense of choice. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming that Naomi is in charge of whether and how to use any strategy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early open questions helped Naomi articulate what she wanted to change and what matters about her work. Evocation was present but not consistently developed, as the session quickly shifted into practical advice and scripts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Naomi to choose among options and respected her preferences in shaping the plan. The session was collaborative but still expert-driven, with Naomi’s role mainly selecting from clinician-generated ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Naomi offered meaningful change talk about wanting to feel present and doable confidence during shoots. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or deepen this change talk before moving into practical strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged doubt and uncertainty without confrontation, helping maintain rapport. Sustain talk was often addressed with additional practical solutions rather than MI-consistent reflection, reframing, or redirection toward Naomi’s own motivation.
