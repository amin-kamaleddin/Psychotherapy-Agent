# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance, normalizing the social role of smoking while validating health concerns. Slightly more individualized warmth and affirmative positive regard would strengthen acceptance further.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is generally accurate and helpful, capturing the ambivalence between social belonging and health. Some reflections remain somewhat summary-like and could be deeper in accessing fear, identity, and emotional undercurrents.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation and the specific barrier of maintaining social connection. Questions progressively narrow toward a concrete, actionable first step without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as a genuine partner, building on the client’s ideas rather than imposing a plan. The final plan is clearly co-created through reflection, organization, and refinement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through repeated invitations for the client’s choice, control, and preferred pace. The psychiatrist respects limits and avoids pressure for a firm quit date or overcommitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently evokes motivation from within the client rather than providing external reasons for change. The client generates most of the change-related content, including values, concerns, and a specific plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the client as the expert on her own life and change process, inviting her perspective and judgment throughout. The client’s contributions clearly shape the session’s direction and content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open, evocative questions and affirming summaries consistently elicit strong client-generated change talk, including behavioral, relational, and identity-based reasons for change. The psychiatrist skillfully builds on this talk without adding their own agenda.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about social rejection, awkwardness, and belonging is acknowledged respectfully and redirected toward health-compatible alternatives. More deliberate double-sided reflections or pivot language could strengthen the softening of resistance.
