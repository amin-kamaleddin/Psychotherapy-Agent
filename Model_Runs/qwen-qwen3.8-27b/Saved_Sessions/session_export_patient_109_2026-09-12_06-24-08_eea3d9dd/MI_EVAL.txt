# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, with frequent genuine affirmations and normalization. The tone is consistent, though at times somewhat formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and capture Rafael’s meaning and ambivalence well. Emotional undercurrents such as shame, guilt, and anxiety are present but not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the exercise goal and progresses logically toward a concrete, actionable plan. The pacing is strong but can feel rapid and slightly interrogation-like.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed, with Rafael selecting the activity, cue, accountability, reminder, and backup. Collaboration is effective but somewhat constrained by the psychiatrist’s structured menus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rafael’s freedom to choose is regularly affirmed, and the psychiatrist avoids pressure while respecting his boundaries. Autonomy support is strong but not consistently explicit during moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out some motivation from Rafael, particularly around mental clarity and practical ease. The approach is more checklist-like than exploratory, with limited depth into Rafael’s values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and lets Rafael shape the plan. Partnership is real but not fully egalitarian due to the repeated use of multiple-choice options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk and affirms Rafael’s chosen actions. However, the evocation is repetitive and stays at a surface, action-focused level rather than deeply exploring values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and normalized without argument, which is appropriate. However, it is not actively softened, redirected, or used to build change momentum.
