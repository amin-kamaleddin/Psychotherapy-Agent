# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed respect, warmth, and nonjudgmental acceptance. Validation was strong even when the client expressed self-criticism or guilt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client’s values, tiredness, and desire for authentic presence. However, some reflections summarized plans or behavior more than they deepened the underlying emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the client’s goal of aligning behavior with family happiness. The therapist effectively moved from broad values to a specific, manageable action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist and client worked together to define the value, clarify the plan, and choose realistic supports. Collaboration was strong, though occasional specific suggestions made the process slightly therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently honored the client’s choice and avoided pressure to commit. The client was allowed to reject scripts or calendars and decide on her own preferred approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions helped the client generate her own reasons, values, and plan details. Evocation was strong, though some suggested interventions may have limited fully client-generated language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist generally treated the client as the expert and invited her preferences throughout the session. A few advisor-like suggestions, such as a scripted sentence, slightly reduced the collaborative balance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist elicited meaningful change talk through questions about confidence, authenticity, and next steps. The session would be stronger with more deliberate amplification of the client’s own reasons and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist responded to resistance without arguing, normalizing concerns about tiredness, guilt, and authenticity. More consistent use of complex or double-sided reflections could further soften sustain talk.
