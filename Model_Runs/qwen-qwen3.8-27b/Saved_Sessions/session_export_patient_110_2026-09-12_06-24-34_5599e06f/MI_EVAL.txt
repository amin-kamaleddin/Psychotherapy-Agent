# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates the client's concerns. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and less deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflects the client's perspective and practical concerns accurately. Less effective at exploring the deeper emotional meaning of fears or values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on the cholesterol medication plan and practical steps. Could more consistently tie actions back to the client's retirement and hiking goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-creates a useful plan with client input and involvement of his wife. Still somewhat expert-driven in the medical framework and final draft.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers options and avoids pressure to choose a specific path. Could more explicitly reinforce that the client is in control of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks some evocative questions about motivation and barriers. Much of the session shifts to medical information and logistics, leaving motivation underdeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance and invites client participation. Becomes more directive when providing medical details and planning structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites the client to express reasons for change, and the client offers meaningful change talk. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue with or reinforce sustain talk. Misses opportunities to use double-sided reflections or other MI strategies to gently redirect the client's ambivalence.
