# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, normalizes ambivalence and possible lapse, and affirms Hana realistically. Some affirmations focus on the plan rather than unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture key content and barriers, such as muscle memory and fitting in. They remain mostly surface-level and do not fully reflect emotional fear of failure or belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the after-work cigarette and narrows to a specific 10-minute delay experiment. Direction is strong but more plan-oriented than values-driven change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana contributes practical ideas and the clinician adapts to them, such as water and lip balm. The clinician still supplies most strategies and structures the problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invites choice and accepts Hana’s uncertainty without coercion. Autonomy is limited by offering a predefined menu of options and confidence targets.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and a confidence ruler draw out some of Hana’s internal reasons for change. The clinician often moves too quickly to advice instead of deepening Hana’s own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana is invited to choose options and plan details, showing some shared decision-making. The session remains largely clinician-led rather than fully equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana produces some change talk about wanting smoking to stop taking over and being in control. The clinician affirms it but does not consistently expand it into stronger desire or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician accepts ambivalence and avoids arguing or judging Hana’s sustain talk. However, they mostly move to problem-solving rather than using double-sided reflections to redirect it toward change.
