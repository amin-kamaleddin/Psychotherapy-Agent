# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but the acceptance feels more procedural than warm. The continued focus on compliance, labeling, and treatment can feel controlling when the client worries about being seen as broken.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some basic perspective-taking about the license, job, and court consequences, but the responses remain factual and logistical. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect Derrick’s shame, ambivalence, and discomfort with being labeled an addict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well organized around drinking, driving, license loss, work, and legal next steps. The focus is clear, though it is mainly externally focused on compliance rather than Derrick’s internal values and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates on practical logistics such as format, scheduling, and local options. However, he remains the primary problem-solver and plan-generator, so the partnership is only partially balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and asks for permission, which supports practical autonomy. Directive compliance language such as strict rules and compliance letters can still undermine Derrick’s sense of making his own choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few evocative questions are used early, but the session mostly shifts into information, recommendations, and practical planning. The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out Derrick’s own values, fears, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative questions, but the psychiatrist positions himself as the expert and directs the main plan. Derrick is treated more as a recipient of recommendations than as the expert in his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Derrick produces several instances of change talk, but the psychiatrist rarely amplifies or expands them into stronger motivation. Responses often move to logistical planning rather than building on Derrick’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing directly with Derrick’s resistance to groups, rehab, or the interlock, but often responds with practical alternatives or problem-solving. The sustain talk is not effectively softened through deeper ambivalence reflection.
