# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment by validating Hana's fatigue, panic, and effort. Acceptance is slightly limited because some affirmations focus on progress and performance rather than unconditional regard independent of her anxiety.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening and names Hana's inner voice, panic, guilt, and conflict between kindness and self-deception. Empathy is not a 5 because reflections sometimes move quickly toward problem-solving rather than deeply exploring shame, fear, and past identity resources.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on reducing exam avoidance through one small, concrete study step. The psychiatrist repeatedly anchors the conversation to a specific tonight action without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Hana's input and allows her choices to shape the final plan, including duration, phone strategy, post-it language, and success rule. Collaboration is slightly limited because the clinician still supplies many concrete options before fully inviting client-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Hana's ownership through choices, optional language, and a plan shaped around her schedule and preferences. Autonomy support is not a 5 because the initial plan is somewhat prescriptive and several solutions are clinician-generated rather than client-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits Hana's own confidence ratings, success rules, and preferred language such as 'Did a little' and 'One key point:' . Evocation is limited because it focuses mainly on immediate confidence and behavior rather than deeper values, reasons for change, or past mastery.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a collaborative stance by asking Hana what feels doable and allowing her preferences to modify the plan. Partnership could be stronger if Hana were invited to generate more options before the clinician offered a menu.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits meaningful change talk around confidence, commitment, and specific behavioral steps. The focus is narrower than a 5 because it does not fully evoke broader desire, values, ability, or past success to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes Hana's barriers and reframes avoidance without arguing or reinforcing resistance. Sustain talk is not fully held in ambivalence, as some concerns are quickly converted into practical planning.
