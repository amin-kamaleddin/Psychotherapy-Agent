# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the patient’s honesty and identity. However, over-validating the patient’s minimization of risk slightly reduces the clarity of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate reflections and demonstrates a good understanding of the patient’s perspective. It would be stronger by exploring the deeper emotions behind the need to be seen as normal and not labeled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on alcohol use at social gatherings and related next-day effects. It could be stronger by moving toward a specific, collaboratively chosen next step or personal boundary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session feels cooperative, with the patient invited to define terms, thresholds, and what would be fair or useful. The agenda remains therapist-led, limiting the degree of true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the patient’s choice, self-knowledge, and right to decide without pressure. It becomes stronger when the therapist more explicitly affirms full ownership of any future change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions that place the source of change with the patient. The evocation is more surface-level and does not deeply draw out values, desire, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the patient as the expert on his own experience and avoids taking an authoritative stance. The session would benefit from more explicit shared decision-making about the agenda and focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers several change-relevant statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or build on them. As a result, change talk remains limited and underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and often acknowledges the patient’s perspective without confrontation. However, the therapist tends to follow minimization rather than skillfully pivoting it toward change-relevant thoughts.
