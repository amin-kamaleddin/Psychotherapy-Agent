# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, supportive stance with statements such as “I’m here to listen without judgment” and “That sounds really exhausting.” Acceptance could be stronger by adding more explicit affirmation of Daniela’s competence and effort in balancing work, school, and parenting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The reflections are accurate but remain mostly factual and cognitive rather than emotionally nuanced. There are missed opportunities to explore deeper feelings such as frustration, fear, embarrassment, or worry about owing her neighbor.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reliable childcare and preventing missed class, repeatedly guiding Daniela toward practical next steps. Direction is strong, though it could be more strategically focused by reducing multiple alternatives and building toward a single client-owned action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by drafting messages and inviting Daniela to adjust tone, length, and wording. A fully collaborative stance would include more pausing, fewer rapid alternatives, and more frequent invitations for Daniela to set the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniela is repeatedly invited to choose her preferences, pace, and wording, and the psychiatrist avoids pressuring immediate decisions. Autonomy support could be further strengthened by more explicitly reinforcing that all decisions are entirely up to Daniela.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions about goals and next steps, but the session is more problem-solving and script-based than MI-focused. The psychiatrist could better elicit Daniela’s values, confidence, and internal motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a clear collaborative stance by asking Daniela’s preferences and allowing her to shape the plan. It could reach a higher level by more explicitly acknowledging Daniela’s expertise in her own life and reducing the counselor’s role as solution-provider.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela produces useful change talk about wanting reliable childcare and starting with Head Start, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen it. More strategic reflection and follow-up questions could better strengthen her own change motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela’s concerns about money, trust, owing her neighbor, and waitlists are acknowledged, but the counselor often moves quickly back to practical options. More MI-specific strategies such as double-sided reflections or gentle reframing would better soften ambivalence.
