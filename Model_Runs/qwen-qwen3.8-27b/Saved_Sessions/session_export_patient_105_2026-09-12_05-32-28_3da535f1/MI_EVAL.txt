# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently expresses respect and nonjudgment through validating statements and strength-affirming language. The acceptance is warm and supportive, though it occasionally feels formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects Naomi’s physical anxiety, cognitive burden, and ambivalence. The main limitation is that some reflections remain surface-level and do not fully explore deeper emotions such as shame or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on Naomi’s social anxiety and narrows toward a manageable target behavior. The direction could be strengthened by periodically linking back to motivation and change readiness rather than overfocusing on strategy selection.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor collaborates well by co-negotiating options and adjusting suggestions based on Naomi’s feedback. A limitation is that most ideas are counselor-generated rather than elicited from Naomi’s own experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports autonomy by offering choices, avoiding pressure, and respecting Naomi’s concerns. The autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming her freedom to choose nothing and reinforcing her ownership of the decision-making process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are used early to clarify goals and past experiences. The session shifts too quickly into practical options, limiting deeper exploration of Naomi’s reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a collaborative stance by acknowledging constraints and modifying suggestions responsively. The partnership could be improved by positioning Naomi more explicitly as the expert on her own experience and inviting her to generate options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some change talk by asking about small improvements and affirming Naomi’s desire for change. However, there is limited effort to amplify, deepen, or expand on that change talk before moving to practical solutions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively avoids argumentation and normalizes Naomi’s concerns and self-critical language. The sustain talk could be softened further by using more double-sided reflections that gently redirect attention toward change.
