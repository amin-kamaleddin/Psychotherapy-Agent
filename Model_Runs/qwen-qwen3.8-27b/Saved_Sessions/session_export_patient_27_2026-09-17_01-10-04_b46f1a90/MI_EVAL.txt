# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance by affirming Daniela’s strengths, normalizing her mixed feelings, and validating her fear without moralizing. The stance was solid but remained somewhat generalized, without fully explicit unconditional positive regard or deeper individualized validation of diabetes-related stigma.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected Daniela’s ambivalence, core fear, and hope for acceptance, showing strong perspective-taking throughout the conversation. Some reflections were slightly inferential or cognitive rather than deeply emotional, and the stigma-related pain could have been explored more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the target issue of disclosing diabetes to peers while preserving Daniela’s social identity. The direction was logical and client-relevant, though it could have been tightened into a more specific micro-goal to increase strategic momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and treated Daniela’s perspectives as central to the session, allowing her ideas to shape the direction of the work. The collaboration was effective, but it could have been more explicitly shared by inviting Daniela to help decide the session structure or priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided pressure and advice-giving, repeatedly emphasizing Daniela’s choice, pace, and right to step back. Autonomy support was strong, though it could have been made more explicit at key decision points by clearly reaffirming that Daniela had full control over disclosure, timing, and testing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist primarily evoked Daniela’s own values, ideas, and possible steps rather than persuading or recommending, allowing motivation to emerge from within. Evocation was effective but could have been deeper by more systematically drawing out confidence, reasons for change, or the meaning of being “just Daniela.”

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveyed a partnership stance by inviting joint problem-solving, pacing the conversation with Daniela, and acknowledging her expertise about her friends and boundaries. The stance was strong, though it could have been more fully power-sharing by explicitly inviting Daniela to choose the session focus or next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively created space for Daniela to articulate her own change talk, including ideas about one friend, small disclosure, and signals that the friendship would remain casual. The change talk could have been more strategically amplified using techniques such as DARN, confidence scaling, or stronger summary reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled Daniela’s sustain talk respectfully by normalizing her concerns and using double-sided reflections rather than arguing or dismissing the status quo. Softening was effective, but the pivots back toward change could have been more explicit and consistent, especially when sustain talk remained prominent near the end.
