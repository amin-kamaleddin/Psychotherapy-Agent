# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong non-judgmental opening sets a respectful tone, but four consecutive identical responses in the middle fail to validate or acknowledge Milan's specific pain. The single good complex reflection near the end is isolated and arrives after a significant dead zone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections bookend the session, showing the psychiatrist can access genuine understanding. However, four consecutive generic responses during Milan's most emotionally charged statements represent a complete breakdown in empathic tracking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with a clear target but collapses into a repetitive loop that fails to progress toward any clarifying goal. A good directional question about Milan's threshold for concern arrives only at the very end, too late to build momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good collaborative framing in the opening and a check-in question at the end, but four identical responses in the middle show the psychiatrist is running a script rather than building on Milan's specific contributions in real time.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects Milan's right to define his own experience, avoids lecturing or imposing moral judgments, and invites him to set his own standards for concern. The repetitive prompting subtly shifts the power dynamic but does not significantly undermine this otherwise strong performance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some evocative moves including the threshold question and final reflection, but misses opportunities to explore Milan's values, faith, and identity as sources of change motivation. The generic prompts in the middle are not evocation—they do not draw out any specific motivation or personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The non-directive opening and acknowledgment of Milan's preference for a collaborative approach are strong, but four identical responses suggest the psychiatrist is not genuinely engaged with Milan's specific words. A true partner would track, build on, and respond to the client's actual contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** One solid question about Milan's own threshold for concern elicits a form of change talk, but the psychiatrist does not reflect, amplify, or follow up on it. There is minimal and isolated evocation of change language without any strategic reinforcement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids reinforcing Milan's minimize and makes a good strategic probe at the end, but does not use double-sided reflections or actively help Milan see the tension between his stated position and the concern that brought him here.
