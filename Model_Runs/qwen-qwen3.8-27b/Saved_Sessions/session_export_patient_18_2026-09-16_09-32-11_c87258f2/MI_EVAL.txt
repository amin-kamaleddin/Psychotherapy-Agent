# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, and respectful tone while normalizing Anya’s discomfort and low-risk status. However, the acceptance feels somewhat scripted and could be more individualized to her specific identity, family context, and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses adequate content reflections and accurately summarizes Anya’s concern about keeping alcohol use from becoming problematic. However, empathy often remains surface-level and misses deeper exploration of her underlying emotions, values, and fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on refusal skills and planning for a specific social event. While the practical direction is strong, the motivational direction toward Anya’s values, confidence, and reasons for change is less developed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Anya’s input and allows her to select and practice a refusal line. However, the interaction is often counselor-led, with the therapist providing menus of scripts and routines rather than fully building on Anya’s own ideas and strengths.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers choices and avoids coercion, but the advice-heavy and prescriptive nature of the session limits Anya’s sense of self-determination. More client-generated planning questions would strengthen autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor starts with an open question but quickly shifts into providing scripts, routines, and practical advice. There is limited evocation of Anya’s own motivations, values, confidence, or self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites Anya’s preferences and supports some client choice during role-play. However, the counselor frequently takes an expert-driven coaching role by supplying much of the content and structure for the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anya expresses some desire and intention to practice refusal skills and reduce future worry. The counselor acknowledges this change talk but does not consistently deepen or amplify it using MI-specific elicitation questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoids arguing with Anya’s sustain talk about not currently having a problem. However, the response is more neutral than strategic and misses opportunities for double-sided reflections or building on her value-based statements.
