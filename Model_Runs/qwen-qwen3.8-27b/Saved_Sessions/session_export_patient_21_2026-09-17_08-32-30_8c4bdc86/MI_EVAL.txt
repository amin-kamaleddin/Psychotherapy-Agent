# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone with clear validation and normalization of the patient’s ambivalence. Some affirmations were slightly leading or overly positive, which could have benefited from a more grounded, non-reinforcing presence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and effectively captured cognitive ambivalence, especially around consistency and the “not enough” voice. Emotional depth was limited, as underlying feelings such as frustration, self-criticism, or fear were not explored more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on physical activity and moved clearly from ambivalence to a concrete, actionable plan. Pacing, sequencing, and focus were excellent throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was clearly co-constructed from the patient’s own ideas, with the psychiatrist serving as a facilitator rather than an expert. The patient’s lived experience was consistently positioned as the primary source of guidance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly and consistently supported, with no pressure, obligation language, or attempts to elevate the patient’s chosen level of effort. The patient’s right to choose the scope and meaning of her activity was strongly honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently used open, evocative questions and allowed the patient to generate her own motivations for change. Slightly more restraint in interpretive or value-laden comments would strengthen this criterion further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative, non-expert stance by inviting the patient’s perspective and working with her ideas. This could be strengthened by more explicit acknowledgment of the patient as the expert on her own body and life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively elicited meaningful change talk, including desire, reasons, and commitment language. The change talk could have been strengthened with more deliberate reflection and amplification of the patient’s own language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well through double-sided reflections and gentle reframing, especially around the “five minutes might as well be none” thinking. A few responses were brief or neutral and missed opportunities to more proactively resolve ambivalence.
