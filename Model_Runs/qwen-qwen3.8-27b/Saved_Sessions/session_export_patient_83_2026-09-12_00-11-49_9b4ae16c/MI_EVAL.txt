# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed basic respect and nonjudgment through statements like “totally understandable to worry.” However, the tone remained more clinical and task-focused than warmly affirming, with limited individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged the patient’s confusion and frustration with appropriate empathic statements. Still, much of the response shifted to factual education and safety advice rather than continued emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stayed related to the target behavior of deciding whether to start a statin. However, the focus became scattered with repeated warnings, lists, and instructions, which diluted the MI focus on ambivalence and readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some collaboration, especially when the psychiatrist honored the patient’s preference to write her own list. Nevertheless, the interaction remained largely counselor-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying much of the content and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported the patient’s right to wait for clear information before starting medication. This was a clear strength, though repeated directive medical advice and safety instructions slightly reduced the autonomy-based tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provided many clinician-generated reasons for change rather than consistently eliciting the patient’s own values and motivations. There were few open, MI-style questions designed to evoke the patient’s personal reasons, concerns, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction often felt educator-to-student rather than a true partnership. The psychiatrist did not consistently position the patient as the expert in her own life or guide the conversation through her insights.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient produced some change talk independently, such as planning to call the prescriber and set a reminder. The psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand that change talk with evocative MI responses.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient’s sustain talk was acknowledged but not skillfully redirected toward change. The psychiatrist mostly responded with information and practical guidance rather than reflective or double-sided responses that could resolve ambivalence.
