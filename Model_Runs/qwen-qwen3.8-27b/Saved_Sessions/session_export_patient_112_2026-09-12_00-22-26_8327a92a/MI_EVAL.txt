# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and uses normalizing, validating statements. However, acceptance is somewhat generic and occasionally shifts quickly to problem-solving, reducing warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects key concerns such as weight gain, cost, social pressure, and loss of ritual. Empathy is strong but does not always fully capture Rosa's identity-based ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on smoking cessation, cravings, and relevant concerns such as weight, cost, and social pressure. The direction is somewhat task-oriented and strategy-driven rather than moving purposefully toward motivation and change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates a collaborative atmosphere by offering choices and asking Rosa to select strategies. Collaboration is limited because the options are largely pre-selected by the psychiatrist rather than co-created from Rosa's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms Rosa's choice and avoids pressuring her to quit. Autonomy could be stronger by more explicitly emphasizing Rosa's control over whether, when, and how she makes changes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session includes some open questions, but it relies more heavily on advice, psychoeducation, and behavioral suggestions. The psychiatrist does not consistently draw out Rosa's own motivations, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Rosa to choose among options and uses collaborative language. However, the psychiatrist frequently assumes the expert role by generating options and steering the session toward a plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa offers several change statements, such as trying the toothpick, walking, and listening to a song. The psychiatrist responds with more options rather than consistently reflecting, amplifying, or deepening the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa's reasons for continuing smoking are acknowledged without argument or dismissal. The psychiatrist often responds with strategies rather than using double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk and move toward change.
