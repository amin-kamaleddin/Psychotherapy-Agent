# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, using supportive language such as 'It's brave you reached out' and 'I'll stay with you.' Acceptance could go deeper when Amira expressed strong shame, regret, or fear of sounding pathetic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and attuned to Amira's loneliness, exhaustion, doubt, and feeling stuck. The core hopeless belief that 'no one wants to help me' was not fully explored or reflected at a deeper level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on immediate distress, safety, and small coping steps without wandering off-topic. However, the larger goal of reducing drug use was not consistently made explicit or connected to the chosen actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated by staying with Amira, offering options, co-creating a text, and inviting her preferences. Collaboration remained somewhat therapist-led because the plan depended on prepared scripts, numbers, and clinical structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently supported Amira's choice, pace, and right to stop, while avoiding pressure or coercion. The highly specific scripts, numbers, and countdown became somewhat directive and limited a fuller expression of Amira's authority.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used some open-ended questions to draw out willingness and small motivations. Evocation was limited in depth because the session relied more on external coping options than on eliciting Amira's values, hopes, competence, or broader reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and offered choices, but remained the main agenda-setter and resource-providing expert. The stance would be stronger with more explicit acknowledgment of Amira as the expert on her own life and options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk around small immediate actions, such as trying breathing and sending a text. These were reinforced, but there was limited development of deeper change talk about reducing drug use, confidence, values, or hope.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled without arguing or direct contradiction, and doubt was reflected and reframed as understandable. The psychiatrist did not consistently redirect or strategically soften Amira's core narrative of helplessness and being unwanted.
