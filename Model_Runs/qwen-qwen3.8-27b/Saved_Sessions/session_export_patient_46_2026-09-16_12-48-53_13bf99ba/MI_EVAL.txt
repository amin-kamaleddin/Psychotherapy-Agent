# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but the tone can feel somewhat clinical and problem-focused for a 16-year-old. There is not enough unconditional warmth or deeper acknowledgment of Ethan’s discomfort and social pressures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate paraphrases and basic perspective-taking, especially around the social nature of drinking and the negative effects of vomiting. However, reflections often remain factual and do not fully validate the emotional or social meaning behind Ethan’s ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing risky drinking, especially the specific Friday plan, and moves clearly toward confidence ratings and practical steps. The main limitation is occasional drift into problem-solving and safety planning rather than deeper motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ethan contributes meaningful choices, such as the nonalcoholic beer, Friday timing, and backup plan. However, the psychiatrist supplies many solutions, scripts, and structured steps, which makes the interaction less equal than a truly collaborative process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledges Ethan’s choice by offering options and asking what feels doable, which helps preserve some sense of autonomy. However, frequent direct advice, suggested comebacks, and concrete instructions reduce the sense that Ethan is fully driving the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk through importance, confidence, and doability questions, and Ethan identifies useful reasons for change. Still, motivation is not consistently drawn out from Ethan’s own values, self-image, or future goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a partnership element because Ethan helps shape the plan, including the fake drink, Friday, and “I’m good” response. However, the counselor’s directive role with multiple strategies and scripts makes the session feel more counselor-led than client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits useful change talk, such as being tired of throwing up and wanting to avoid trouble. However, the psychiatrist often moves into offering options and suggestions rather than deepening Ethan’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged without being reinforced, such as concerns about ruining the vibe or being pressured by friends. The counselor could more consistently use reflective pivots to connect that social pressure to Ethan’s own values or goals for staying in control.
