# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental while addressing safety. The main gap is that warmth is more practical than deeply personal and does not explicitly neutralize Elias’ shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately tracks ambivalence, relapse fear, and desire for normalcy. It remains more cognitive and plan-oriented than emotionally deep, missing themes such as shame, burden, and grief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on recovery, urge management, and safety without meaningful drift. The limitation is that it narrows to micro-behavioral planning without consistently connecting it back to broader values and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is framed as joint exploration, and Elias’ ideas actively shape the coping plan. Collaboration is slightly limited because the psychiatrist often supplies structure and scripts before inviting more co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice, avoids coercive language, and accepts the client’s decision to change routes. However, the prescriptive, solution-focused approach can shift responsibility toward the clinician rather than leaving it with Elias.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions draw out urges, confidence, and past coping, but they focus mostly on behavioral details. Deeper evocation of values, identity, and intrinsic motivation is inconsistent, especially when Elias is stuck or self-critical.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Elias as the expert on his experience and lets his concerns shape the session. The main limitation is a therapist-led tendency to present ready-made options and scripts instead of fully sharing power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms meaningful change talk about sobriety, walks, and not being caught off guard. The main weakness is that the session often moves too quickly into planning instead of amplifying and expanding the client’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing and helps reframe all-or-nothing language such as cheating and running away. Important sustain talk, such as missing the pills or fearing deletion of the text, is not fully explored or reflected with double-sided MI responses.
