# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental stance with repeated affirmations. It could be stronger by more explicitly normalizing fear, shame, and aging-related limitations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflective listening to capture the client’s ambivalence and physical/emotional barriers. More nuanced reflection of possible shame, loss of independence, or fear of appearing frail would strengthen the response.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of increasing movement and moves from ambivalence to a concrete plan. The focus occasionally narrows to safety logistics rather than linking the plan to the client’s broader values and health goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by inviting the client to choose options and shape the plan. The use of multiple clinician-generated menus and checklists can create a mild expert-led dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is repeatedly given choice, and the psychiatrist respects the client’s pace and decisions. Some safety directives are more instructive than autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and readiness questions effectively elicit practical ideas for change. However, much of the content is prompted by the psychiatrist rather than deeply evoked from the client’s own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist establishes a non-expert stance and the client clearly shapes the session’s plan. More explicit power-sharing language could reduce the structured, clinician-led elements.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through doable-step and confidence-scaling questions. The session would benefit from more questions and reflections that deepen the client’s own reasons, values, and hope for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates the client’s sustain talk without arguing or dismissing it. More skilled double-sided reflections or pivots could help the client see how safety concerns and change goals can coexist.
