# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Malik’s wish to be respected. Acceptance is solid but not maximal because warmth remains somewhat clinical and does not consistently affirm Malik’s worth beyond his behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections to capture Malik’s perspective, such as being brought in by his mother’s worry and feeling caught between friends and home stress. The score is not a 5 because reflections often repeat the same themes and do not consistently explore deeper ambivalence, fear, shame, or guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a clear referral focus on substance use, but the discussion broadens toward general home stress and peer dynamics. The target behavior—cannabis use—does not remain a consistent anchor, and the session does not move clearly toward a specific change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist follows Malik’s lead and allows his concerns about peace, respect, and home stress to shape the conversation. Collaboration is strong but not maximal because the psychiatrist still guides the session through questions and reflections without explicit power sharing or co-created agenda setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by inviting Malik to define his goals, emphasizing that he is the expert, and avoiding coercive advice about use. The score is not a 5 because some questions could subtly place responsibility on Malik, and autonomy around the target behavior itself is not explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some client language about peace, navigation, and the difference between avoiding and choosing. Evocation is limited because it is not consistently directed toward Malik’s own reasons, desire, ability, or confidence to change cannabis use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative, non-expert stance and avoids telling Malik what to do. Partnership is good but not maximal because the interaction remains somewhat therapist-led, with less explicit agenda setting or shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited and indirect efforts to evoke change talk about cannabis use, and Malik’s few change-related statements are not amplified or developed. The psychiatrist does not consistently use DARN-C questions or build Malik’s own reasons, desire, or commitment to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Malik’s sustain talk but does not strategically soften it by exploring its costs or tensions with his values. There is limited use of discrepancy or double-sided reflections to shift the conversation toward change.
