# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses respectful, validating language and avoids judgment or shaming. Acceptance is sometimes framed practically rather than deeply emotionally present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s frustration, fear, and feeling dismissed by her GP. However, reflections often stay at the surface before moving quickly into action planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains focused on preparing the client to request tests and a gastroenterology referral. The direction is narrowly medical rather than motivational, steering toward diagnosis confirmation more than functional change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates on a short script and respects the client’s preference for brevity. The interaction shifts toward an expert-driven medical strategy, limiting a truly equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, respects the client’s choice to omit stress from the written script, and avoids coercion. The extensive expert content and scripting may subtly reduce the client’s sense of authorship.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides many steps and explanations rather than drawing out the client’s own motivations. The client’s pain, fatigue, and fear are not developed into self-generated change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist initially invites the client’s lead and offers choices, including script format and practice. The session becomes uneven when the psychiatrist assumes most of the agenda and expert authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change-relevant statements about fatigue and wanting to stop lying in bed, but the psychiatrist rarely amplifies or develops them. The session lacks evocation of values, hope, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client’s sustained focus on cancer and distrust of the GP is met with validation but reinforced through an extensive test plan. Little use is made of double-sided reflection or gentle redirection toward ambivalence and function.
