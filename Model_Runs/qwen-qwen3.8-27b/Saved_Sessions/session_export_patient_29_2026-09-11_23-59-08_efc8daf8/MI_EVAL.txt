# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, supportive, and nonjudgmental, with affirming statements such as “You’re not alone” and “You’re not weak.” Acceptance is not consistently emotionally attuned or individualized, as the session often shifts quickly into advice-giving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects some of David’s concerns accurately, but the empathy often stays at the content level rather than the emotional level. Important feelings such as fear of looking desperate, stigma, shame, and loss of control are not consistently reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on smoking cessation and directly related barriers such as cravings, stress, BP safety, and relapse. The focus could be stronger if the psychiatrist more consistently linked practical decisions back to David’s values and change momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites some input and tailors suggestions to David’s practical and social concerns, but the interaction often feels more clinician-led than truly collaborative. David contributes to some details, but the psychiatrist rarely positions David as an equal partner in planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options and referring back to David’s PCP, but does not consistently emphasize David’s ownership or right to choose. Some statements are directive, such as recommending NRT and providing a quick plan, without clearly framing them as suggestions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently provides information, options, and strategies rather than drawing out David’s own motivations, values, and reasons for change. A key opportunity to evoke David’s self-statement or coping language near the end is not fully used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, but the psychiatrist often positions himself as the information provider and plan-maker. The partnership could be stronger if David’s expertise about his own life, priorities, and choices were more explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not consistently evoke or amplify David’s change talk, such as his desire to quit, feel competent, or stay in control. Questions are often logistical rather than exploring motivation, values, confidence, or identity-based reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively softens some sustain talk around relapse by normalizing a lapse and reducing shame. However, many of David’s concerns are met with direct advice or problem-solving rather than reflective, double-sided responses that hold ambivalence.
