# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, reframing exhaustion as strength and never judging or minimizing the client's experience. Language is consistently warm, individualized, and communicates genuine respect for Nadine as a person beyond her role as a parent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate, nuanced empathic reflections that go well beyond surface-level paraphrasing, capturing specific emotional qualities the client hadn't explicitly articulated. Each reflection builds on the last with increasing depth and specificity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, purposeful trajectory moving logically from emotional validation through information to actionable planning with good momentum. The session ends without a clear next step or collaborative summary, leaving the client still in the 'it feels like a lot' space.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Model-level collaboration, consistently framing the work as joint and using the client's own language and metaphors back to her. Even the information-giving moment is prefaced with permission-seeking and framed as a tool for the client's own use.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames all decisions as the client's, offering options without pressuring toward either and never telling Nadine what she should do. The final question about partner conversation explicitly respects that Nadine may not be ready without pushing for a timeline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful evocation — every insight, realization, and plan emerges from Nadine's own mouth through strategic questions and reflections. The psychiatrist never provides a single reason for change or solution, consistently inviting the client to discover her own motivations and agency.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions herself as a partner rather than authority, establishing a collaborative contract from the opening and returning problem-solving to the client throughout. Never lectures or takes over, and consistently checks in with the client's perspective before moving forward.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively cultivates change talk through strategic open questions, guiding the client from despair to planning with partner. Could have gone further in explicitly naming the client's agency or evoking more specific commitment language toward a first action.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with remarkable skill, acknowledging and normalizing the client's concerns without either dismissing them or reinforcing the resistance. The response to the mother comparison is a perfect example of validating the feeling while gently separating it from its implications.
