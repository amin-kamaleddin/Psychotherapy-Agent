# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with normalizing language and clear affirmations. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic and could be more individually tailored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections that capture Sofia’s ambivalence, protective motivation, and fear of social judgment. Empathy could deepen further by exploring shame, self-judgment, and guilt more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a clear target behavior: avoiding alcohol in social settings during pregnancy. The direction could periodically link social problem-solving back to core values and broader change in drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative, non-authoritative tone and invites Sofia’s ideas and expertise. Collaboration could be strengthened with more explicit agenda-setting and shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure, advice-giving, and directives, supporting Sofia’s ownership of her choices. Autonomy support could be stronger by explicitly reinforcing that the pace, focus, and decisions are hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated ideas, such as “Passing tonight” and staying connected. Evocation remains somewhat surface-level and could more deeply explore values, identity, and confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Sofia as the expert on her own life and relationships while inviting her perspective. Partnership could include more explicit power-sharing and co-constructed session goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change-oriented language, but does not consistently amplify, reflect, or consolidate the strongest change talk. More strategic work on importance, confidence, values, and commitment language would strengthen this criterion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and gently redirects at times, but does not consistently soften or pivot it toward change. Using double-sided reflections to balance sustain talk against core values would improve this area.
