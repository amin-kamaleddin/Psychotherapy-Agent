# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Ryan’s competing concerns. It is slightly formulaic and could include more individualized affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly reflects ambivalence, loneliness, and fear of missing out. Reflections become repetitive and could explore deeper emotions such as shame or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on alcohol use, safety, school performance, and the conduct incident. It does not fully consolidate a specific behavior target or commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and builds on Ryan’s ideas throughout. The session remains somewhat counselor-guided rather than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids directives and emphasizes Ryan’s ownership and choice. Could more explicitly affirm his freedom to choose not to change at this time.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some self-generated ideas and values but focuses more on problem-solving than deeper motivation. It could better draw out stronger change talk and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Ryan’s input and positions him as the expert on his own life. The structured question sequence gently steers the session, limiting full client leadership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ryan offers several ideas and reasons for change, but the counselor does not consistently deepen or amplify them. Tools like importance or confidence rulers are missing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ryan’s reasons to continue drinking are acknowledged without argument. The counselor could use more double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancy to soften sustain talk.
