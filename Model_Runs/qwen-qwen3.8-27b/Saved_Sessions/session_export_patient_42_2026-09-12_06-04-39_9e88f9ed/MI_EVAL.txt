# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming of Elise’s ambivalence and social concerns. Acceptance was strong but occasionally procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made reasonable effort to understand Elise’s perspective, especially around belonging and avoiding drama. Empathy was often factual and problem-solving oriented rather than emotionally deep.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on smoking cessation in a specific social situation and helped Elise create a concrete plan. Direction was sometimes achieved through advice and planning rather than MI-style exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated well, particularly in co-creating a low-pressure follow-up plan with Elise’s choices. The collaboration was sometimes weakened by early, expert-led suggestions and advice-giving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported Elise’s autonomy by inviting her input, offering options, and allowing a low-pressure check-in plan. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming her ownership of the decision, outcome, and any setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk, but much of the session shifted into advice and practical planning. There were missed opportunities to deepen Elise’s own motivation and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and invited Elise’s input, but often assumed an expert role by providing lists and behavioral tips. The session was not consistently client-led or fully equal in power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise expressed desire, intention, and some commitment, and the psychiatrist affirmed her chosen step. Change talk was present but not consistently expanded, amplified, or explored in depth.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged sustain talk without arguing, but often moved quickly into practical solutions. Stronger MI-consistent reflections would be needed to soften ambivalence and explore the pull between social belonging and change.
