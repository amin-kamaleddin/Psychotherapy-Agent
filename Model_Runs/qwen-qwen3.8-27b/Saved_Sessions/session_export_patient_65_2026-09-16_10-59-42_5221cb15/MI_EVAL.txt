# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental stance while normalizing Julian’s concerns about logistics, distance, and court consequences. Acceptance is solid but sometimes feels more like approval of compliance behavior than deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening, especially around Julian’s logistical frustration and uncertainty. However, empathy remains mostly factual and misses opportunities to reflect the fear, shame, or anxiety behind statements such as not wanting to return in front of the judge.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on completing court-mandated community service by the deadline, with clear attention to hours, sites, documentation, and follow-up communication. The direction is clear but mostly procedural and logistical rather than motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering options, inviting Julian’s input, and adapting to his choices rather than imposing a plan. Collaboration could be stronger by co-creating solutions rather than presenting many ready-made scripts and structures.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Julian’s autonomy is strongly supported through choice-based questions, respect for his privacy, and acceptance of his decisions to type information himself and wait before follow-up. A stronger focus on his own competence and internal decision-making would further enhance autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits logistical information and preferences but does not effectively draw out Julian’s internal motivation, values, or confidence for completing the service. This is the weakest area, as the session largely stays within external compliance rather than evoking change from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist fosters a collaborative stance by repeatedly asking Julian what would help most and allowing his preferences to guide the session. Partnership is strong, though the psychiatrist still leads the agenda with structured options and prepared next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Julian expresses several statements of intention and action, such as sending the message, waiting, and using the follow-up if needed. The psychiatrist acknowledges these but does not consistently expand, deepen, or reflect them to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with Julian’s avoidance of extra effort and responds with normalization and lower-effort alternatives. However, there is limited use of more advanced MI-consistent strategies, such as highlighting the connection between his avoidance and his goal of avoiding court.
