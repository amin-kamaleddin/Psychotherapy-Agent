# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Mateo’s perspective without judgment, including his guardedness and distrust. A perfect score is withheld because some affirmations can feel therapist-driven rather than purely client-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and perspective-taking, accurately capturing Mateo’s ambivalence and underlying frustrations. Some reflections are interpretive or add meaning not explicitly stated by the client.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on Mateo’s re-entry goals and risk-related behaviors, but often drifts into broader exploratory territory. More consistent strategic guidance is needed to tie exploration back to reducing reoffending risk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the session as a joint effort, inviting Mateo’s input and co-constructing ideas for support and boundaries. The psychiatrist still does much of the summarizing and suggesting, which can overshadow the client’s own direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Mateo’s autonomy by emphasizing his right to make his own choices and avoiding coercive language. Some phrasing feels slightly leading by framing choices through the lens of family or “the right” choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions to draw out Mateo’s perspective, but sometimes provides reasons for change or interprets feelings before the client fully expresses them. More space is needed for Mateo to articulate his own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by inviting input and framing the session as a joint exploration. Occasional expert-like evaluations or “correct” perspectives can slightly diminish the sense of equality.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist occasionally evokes change talk by highlighting Mateo’s goals and potential for action, leading to some change talk. The psychiatrist sometimes inserts their own values rather than consistently allowing Mateo to articulate his own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively acknowledges and softens sustain talk without arguing, validating the difficulty of the “old crew” and redirecting toward alternatives. More double-sided reflections weighing pros and cons would strengthen this area.
