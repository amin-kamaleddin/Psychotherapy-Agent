### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, with frequent affirmations and normalization (e.g., 'It's not stupid at all,' 'I believe you'). Slightly structured rather than spontaneously flowing, preventing a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that captures both content and emotional meaning, including complex dual-meaning reflections. Could have gone deeper on moments of acute pain, particularly around shame and the 'I never really had that' statement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and logical progression from general goals to concrete action steps, maintaining momentum toward change without letting the session drift. Could more explicitly connect each step back to the overarching recidivism reduction goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue with consistent use of 'we' and 'together' language, building on Mateo's contributions to shape the next question. The clinical arc follows a somewhat predictable structure that limits the sense of organic co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary throughout — the psychiatrist consistently invites Mateo's own goals, choices, and analysis without ever imposing directives or prescriptions. Even reframes are offered as observations to explore, not prescriptions to follow.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out Mateo's internal motivations and creates discrepancy between his values and current patterns without providing reasons for change. Could go deeper in eliciting confidence/ability statements and exploring the emotional weight of his vision.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames the work as collaborative with exploratory, non-directive questions that position Mateo as the expert in his own life. Some transitions feel slightly more structured than truly co-created, preventing a perfect score.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicits and skillfully amplifies change talk, including evocative questions that draw out Mateo's own reasons and signals. Could have gone further in explicitly eliciting confidence and ability statements and commitment language.
