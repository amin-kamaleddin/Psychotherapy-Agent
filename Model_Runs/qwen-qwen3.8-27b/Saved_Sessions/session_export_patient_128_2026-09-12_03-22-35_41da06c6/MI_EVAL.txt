# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates warmth, normalization, and nonjudgment while respecting the client’s preference not to cut down. It is not a 5 because the acceptance is sometimes procedural and blends into quick problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s ambivalence around social drinking, the police incident, and the desire to avoid being a buzzkill. Deeper attention to emotional undercurrents such as fear, shame, or loss of freedom would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a reasonable focus on preventing a repeat police incident through safer planning. However, the target becomes fuzzy as attention shifts among drinking, driving, logistics, and app setup without a clearly collaborative goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input, follows his choices, and uses scaling to explore confidence. A 5 would require more emphasis on eliciting the client’s own ideas before offering a large menu of external options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly supports autonomy by stating that the client is in charge, respecting his choices, and avoiding pressure to cut down. Frequent offers of practical options can subtly steer decisions, limiting full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence scaling elicit some motivation, but the session is often driven by the psychiatrist offering solutions. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen the client’s values, importance, or commitment to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is invited to direct the session and choose among options, such as keys or ride credit. However, the psychiatrist frequently shifts into an expert problem-solving role by supplying multiple practical strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk about his degree and avoiding further police trouble, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand it. The frequent focus on practical menus reduces space for the client’s own change language to develop.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s preference not to cut down and his concern about being a buzzkill without arguing. The response is more neutral acceptance than active softening through double-sided reflections linked to values.
