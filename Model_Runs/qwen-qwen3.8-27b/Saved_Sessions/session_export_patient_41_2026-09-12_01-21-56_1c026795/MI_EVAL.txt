# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses normalizing, nonjudgmental language such as “slipping up doesn’t mean failure,” but acceptance remains more task-focused than deeply affirming of the client’s effort and self-worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor reflects frustration, doubt, and worry about being a burden, but reflections are often brief and quickly followed by directives, limiting deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on avoidance and follow-through, moving consistently toward a concrete, measurable plan with a specific check-in time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client contributes the task and backup preference, but the counselor supplies much of the plan, reducing the sense of working as equal partners.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor offers some choices, but frequent specific instructions make the plan feel assigned rather than client-directed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some plan details are client-generated, but the counselor does not deeply elicit the client’s internal motivation, values, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, but the counselor frequently defines the task, timing, steps, and follow-up, making the interaction more expert-driven than partnership-based.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers commitment language, but the counselor rarely reflects or amplifies her own reasons for change, confidence, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoidance and failure concerns are met without challenge and softened with normalization, but there is limited use of double-sided reflections or pivots into change talk.
