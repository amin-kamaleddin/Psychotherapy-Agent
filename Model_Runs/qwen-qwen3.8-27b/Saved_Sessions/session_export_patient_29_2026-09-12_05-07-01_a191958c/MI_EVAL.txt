# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance throughout the interaction. The warmth occasionally feels formulaic and tied to the patient’s cooperation or plan completion rather than being fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is adequate and captures the practical content of the patient’s experience. The reflections are mostly simple and factual, with limited emotional nuance around stress, embarrassment, fear of failure, or anxiety about side effects.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on the target behavior of smoking cessation. The psychiatrist guides the conversation effectively from initial concerns to a concrete, workable plan for Monday.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is responsiveness to patient preferences, such as choosing a lozenge over gum and adjusting the patch plan if dreams occur. However, the interaction remains more clinician-led than collaborative, with the psychiatrist supplying most of the information and final plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids overt pressure, which supports patient freedom at several points. The plan can become prescriptive in dosing and implementation, reducing the emphasis on the patient’s ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening question effectively invites the patient’s own goals and concerns. After that, the session shifts primarily into education and problem-solving rather than continuing to elicit the patient’s internal motivations and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers options, and adapts the plan to the patient’s concerns. However, the session is still largely expert-led, with limited explicit acknowledgment that the patient is the expert in his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions and affirmations that support change-oriented language. The session largely moves toward medication information and a practical plan, with missed opportunities to deepen desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient concerns are acknowledged and normalized rather than challenged. The psychiatrist often responds by adding information or refining the plan instead of using MI-consistent strategies to turn barriers back toward change.
