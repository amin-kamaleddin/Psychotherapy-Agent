# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses warm, nonjudgmental language and affirms progress without criticizing ongoing smoking or drinking. This stance is respectful but somewhat formulaic and not fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Key practical concerns are reflected accurately, such as nighttime flares, sleep, work breathing, and dust exposure. Deeper feelings like frustration, fear, financial stress, or guilt are missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on asthma management and moves toward concrete actions like checking inhaler technique. However, it drifts toward practical problem-solving and away from broader target behaviors such as smoking and drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and offers choices, supporting a collaborative stance. However, the interaction becomes therapist-driven, with the counselor supplying much of the content and direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly asks permission, offers choices, and avoids pressure to quit smoking or drinking. Frequent advice and prewritten messages can slightly reduce the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are used, such as asking what the client wants to see change and what small step he is willing to try. The session relies more on advice and education than drawing out deeper motivations, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's input and offers choices, which supports partnership. However, the session becomes quite therapist-driven with practical options, instructions, and message drafts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and asks about a small doable step. However, there is limited effort to consistently reflect, amplify, or build on the client's change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with sustain talk and accepts practical constraints. However, softening techniques such as double-sided reflections are not used consistently, and some comments may reduce urgency for change.
