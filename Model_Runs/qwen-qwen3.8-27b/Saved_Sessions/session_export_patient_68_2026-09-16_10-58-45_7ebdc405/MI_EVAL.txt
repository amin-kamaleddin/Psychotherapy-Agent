# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and validating stance, but acceptance remains fairly professional and functional. There is limited deeper acknowledgment of Kevin's embarrassment, shame, and social identity concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is present but generally brief and quickly used as a transition to problem-solving. Important emotional statements, such as not wanting to be 'that guy at twenty-nine,' are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on safer drinking and concrete behavioral change. The structure is logical but somewhat formulaic, following a repetitive cycle of options and confidence scaling.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for input and creates a collaborative frame, but most of the content is expert-driven. Kevin contributes ideas, yet the plan is largely shaped by the psychiatrist's suggested options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kevin's choices are respected and his 'not perfect, just safer' framing is affirmed. However, the repeated menus of options and tactical planning can feel subtly pressuring and may reduce his sense of autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited, with confidence scaling becoming the main technique. The psychiatrist provides many strategies and phrasings rather than eliciting Kevin's own deeper motivations and ideas for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language and asks Kevin's opinion, supporting a collaborative frame. In practice, the interaction is asymmetric, with the psychiatrist generating most of the plan and Kevin largely selecting from it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited through confidence scaling and commitment questions, but it is often reactive to the psychiatrist's suggestions. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or build on Kevin's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Kevin expresses clear sustain talk about fun, autonomy, and fear of feeling forced, but it is only briefly acknowledged. There is little use of double-sided reflection or strategic work to resolve his ambivalence.
