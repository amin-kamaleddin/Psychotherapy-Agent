# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates respect, nonjudgment, and warmth by normalizing Maria's concerns and avoiding blame or argument. This falls slightly short of a 5 because the repeated safety warnings later become more procedural than emotionally attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Maria's content but remains largely surface-level, often using formulaic phrases like “I hear you.” Deeper emotional themes such as frustration, fear of control, and defensiveness are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays on asthma management but shifts quickly into education and safety planning without a clear MI-focused agenda. There is limited movement toward a specific, client-centered change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist starts collaboratively by offering choices and asking for permission, but the session becomes more provider-led when Maria resists. Repeating the same safety plan after Maria asks to move on weakens the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through statements like “you’re the expert,” “I respect that choice,” and acceptance of Maria's decision not to change today. The repeated safety reminders and follow-up offers introduce a subtle provider agenda despite the respectful tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few open questions are asked early, but the psychiatrist does not follow up by drawing out Maria's values, motivations, or concerns. The session moves too quickly into facts and options, limiting evocation of change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with a collaborative stance and offers choices, but becomes increasingly directive after Maria pushes back. Maria's preference for brevity and autonomy is acknowledged in words, but she does not fully shape the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are few opportunities to strengthen Maria's implicit change talk, such as keeping her rescue inhaler available or agreeing to check the email. The psychiatrist does not consistently use questions or reflections to deepen desire, ability, need, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maria offers strong sustain talk, but the psychiatrist does not use double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, or reframing to soften it. The conversation remains focused on safety and symptom monitoring rather than gently expanding Maria's perspective on change.
