# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and validation without reacting defensively to resistance. The stance is warm and supportive, though some statements begin to sound formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Marco’s routine, frustration with repeated advice, effort, and practical limits. However, the clinician does not fully explore the deeper resignation, fear, or identity tied to his illness and 40 years of smoking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking urges and moves toward a concrete low-effort behavioral experiment. It is less than perfect because the clinician occasionally drifts into broader advice and integrates Marco’s dental priority late.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is co-constructed, with Marco choosing walking or pacing and setting his own conditions. However, repeated lists of clinician-supplied options can make the session feel somewhat expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician strongly emphasizes Marco’s choice, pace, and ownership throughout the session. The plan is framed as an optional experiment, and the clinician clearly respects Marco’s right to continue smoking if it does not work.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses appropriate evocative questions about smoking’s role, preferences, and past attempts. However, the session moves too quickly to offering options before fully drawing out deeper personal motivations and discrepancies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently invites Marco’s input, follows his lead, and allows his preferences to shape the conversation. It falls short of a 5 because the clinician still supplies several ready-made options rather than more fully eliciting Marco’s own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits some change talk, including willingness to try walking or pacing. However, the clinician does not consistently amplify, strengthen, or summarize Marco’s change statements, and does not use scaling or importance/confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with Marco’s sustain talk and does not reinforce resistance. However, the softening is not consistently strategic, with limited use of double-sided reflections or nuanced reframing of failure and resistance.
