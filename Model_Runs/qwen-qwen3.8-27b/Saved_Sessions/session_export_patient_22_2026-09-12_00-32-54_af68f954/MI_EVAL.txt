# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect and warmth, but the tone remains more clinical and task-oriented than deeply affirming. There is limited consistent affirmation of the patient's worth, courage, or struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but brief, missing deeper emotional pain, shame, and social anxiety. The patient's ambivalence is acknowledged but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use and moves logically toward a concrete plan. It is somewhat task-focused, prioritizing problem-solving over evocative exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient is offered choices, but the psychiatrist generates most of the content and solutions. The collaboration is more select-from-options than true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist honors the patient's reduction goal and asks for her preferences. However, the prescriptive scripts and strategies limit the patient's own agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions mostly gather information and select preferences rather than elicit the patient's values, motivations, or change talk. The session bypasses drawing out the patient's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist involves the patient in some decisions but remains the primary source of ideas and solutions. The patient's role is more reactive than co-creative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers clear change talk, but the psychiatrist does not reflect, amplify, or deepen it. There is limited elicitation of desire, ability, need, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is accepted without confrontation, and the patient's compromise is honored. However, ambivalence is not actively explored or softened through deeper reflection.
