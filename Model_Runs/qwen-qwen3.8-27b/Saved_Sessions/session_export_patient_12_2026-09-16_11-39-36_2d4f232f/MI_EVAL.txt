### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Amira’s effort, such as moving the bag. The support is strong but can feel somewhat repetitive or procedural.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Amira’s fear, helplessness, and ambivalence. Deeper exploration of shame, regret, or isolation could strengthen the empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on immediate safety and a small next step. It could benefit from connecting the crisis response to a longer-term change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works within Amira’s choices and offers collaborative options. However, the session can still feel somewhat therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is strong use of invitations and choice-based language, with limited pressure on Amira. The psychiatrist could further emphasize Amira’s ownership of her decisions and outcomes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions invite Amira to identify desired change and internal resources. The focus shifts quickly to safety planning before deeper motivation is explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amira is invited to make choices and shape parts of the session. More explicit acknowledgment of her as the expert on her own life would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk around not using and staying safe. There is less emphasis on values, confidence, reasons, or a broader future direction.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amira’s expressions of hopelessness and futility are validated rather than challenged. The psychiatrist could more effectively turn these statements toward agency, hope, or internal motivation.
