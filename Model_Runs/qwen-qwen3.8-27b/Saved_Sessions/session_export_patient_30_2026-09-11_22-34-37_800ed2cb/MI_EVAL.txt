# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats David’s concerns with respect and without judgment. However, the acceptance is somewhat formulaic and is often followed by medical information, which limits its impact.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects David’s fear of muscle weakness and his feeling of being fine. However, the empathy remains mostly surface-level and does not fully explore deeper ambivalence or emotional complexity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on cholesterol management and medication adherence. However, the direction becomes more problem-solving-oriented and does not consistently use MI-guided techniques such as agenda-setting or focusing on discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration, especially in drafting and revising the note to David’s doctor. However, the psychiatrist often drives the conversation with medical explanations, leaving David relatively passive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offers choices and accepts David’s refusals without pressure. Autonomy is well supported, with only minor directive suggestions toward scheduling or communication.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks helpful opening questions that elicit David’s main concerns. However, the session shifts toward information-giving and planning, leaving David’s own motivations and values underexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates some partnership by offering options and adjusting the note to David’s preferences. However, the strong expert role and frequent medical explanations reduce the sense of equal collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk, including David’s willingness to try lifestyle changes and contact his doctor. However, the change talk is not deeply developed through evocative questions about desire, ability, reasons, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or direct confrontation with David’s resistance. However, the session remains heavily focused on reasons to avoid medication, which does not effectively soften sustain talk or redirect language toward change.
