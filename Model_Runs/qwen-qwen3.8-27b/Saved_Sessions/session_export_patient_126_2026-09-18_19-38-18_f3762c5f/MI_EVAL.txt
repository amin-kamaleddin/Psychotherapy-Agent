# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Thomas's perspective without judgment and maintains a warm, non-pushy stance despite repeated minimization. However, reflections feel somewhat formulaic and lack explicit normalization of his ambivalence, preventing a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections accurately capture Thomas's emotional stance, such as the reflection on frustration and tuning out. However, some reflections lean toward interpretation rather than pure reflection, and the psychiatrist could have gone deeper into the emotional pain of feeling criticized or the fear of vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session follows a logical progression (smoking → alcohol → daily routine) with questions that build strategically on each other. However, the psychiatrist could have more clearly articulated the target behavior early on and periodically checked in on what Thomas wants to focus on, especially after drifting into complaints about his wife.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the conversation as a joint exploration using inclusive, non-hierarchical language and actively seeks Thomas's input. However, the collaboration is somewhat structured by a predetermined sequence rather than being fully shaped by Thomas's spontaneous responses, and the 'pulse check' metaphor introduces the psychiatrist's framing rather than eliciting Thomas's own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Thomas's autonomy, never imposing advice or pressuring him to change, and frames choices as his own. This could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming his capacity to make decisions in moments of resistance and by less frequently introducing the psychiatrist's own concepts (e.g., 'pulse check').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some effort to evoke Thomas's own motivations through open questions, but the evocation is inconsistent and sometimes gives way to the psychiatrist's own framing. The session produces limited change talk, and there is no clear strategy for deepening evocation when Thomas gives minimal responses or for using techniques like scaling questions or exploring future selves.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance with inclusive language and avoids an expert-driven approach. However, the questions feel somewhat procedural and guided, and the session follows a predetermined path that may not fully reflect Thomas's priorities, subtly undermining the sense of equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes attempts to elicit change talk and Thomas does produce some change-relevant language (e.g., being there for his kid). However, the psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce or amplify this change talk when it appears, missing opportunities to deepen it through strategic reflections or future-focused questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Thomas's sustain talk without judgment but does not actively use MI-specific techniques like double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance to redirect it toward change. Sustain talk is largely accepted at face value and the conversation moves to the next question rather than being strategically tilted toward exploration.
