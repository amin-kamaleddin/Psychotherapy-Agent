# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming of Camila's goals and concerns. Acceptance is clear but often brief and formulaic, without deeper person-centered affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects some of Camila's experience, especially social pressure and worry about caving. Empathy is adequate but inconsistent and does not fully attend to her stronger emotional disclosures near the end.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing weekday drinking and managing social drinking with friends. Direction is strong, though it leans toward practical tactics rather than balancing focus with deeper evocation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Camila's preferences and tailors suggestions, especially around Mike and natural-sounding lines. Collaboration is partial because the psychiatrist supplies most solutions rather than co-constructing the plan from Camila's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally supports choice by asking permission before offering strategies and asking which options feel doable. Support is strong but not full because frequent scripts and tactics can still feel somewhat prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few open questions, but the session quickly shifts to scripts, limits, and practical tactics. Evocation of Camila's own values, motivations, and confidence is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some partnership by inviting input and asking what feels doable. However, the interaction becomes increasingly expert-driven, with the counselor supplying much of the content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Camila generates some change talk independently, but the psychiatrist rarely captures, amplifies, or expands it. The session does not consistently ask MI-consistent questions to draw out her reasons, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges barriers and avoids arguing with or judging Camila's reasons for continuing to drink. However, it does not consistently use double-sided reflections or redirect ambivalence toward change.
