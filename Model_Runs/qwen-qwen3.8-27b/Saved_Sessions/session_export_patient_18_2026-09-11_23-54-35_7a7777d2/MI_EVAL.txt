# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental, using affirmations like “you’re not an idiot” and “no pressure” to validate the client’s concerns. Some clinical/risk-management language remains slightly distancing from a warmer personal affirmation of the client’s identity and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects the client’s view that tracking can feel stigmatizing and tied to social fun. However, it underexplores emotional themes like independence, control, family annoyance, and not wanting to become a formal problem.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, moving from screening interpretation to a concrete, low-effort plan. It would be stronger if it targeted one client-identified concern more strategically, such as a signal that weekend drinking is becoming a problem.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes preferences, especially accepting water swaps and the shirt-change idea, so the final plan is partly co-created. However, the counselor still leads much of the content-setting with thresholds, risks, and multiple options rather than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offers choices, avoids demanding follow-ups, and explicitly says the client does not have to stop if not causing harm. Slight limitation remains because the multiple suggestions can still feel directive without stronger affirmations that the client owns the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor uses some open invitations, but mostly explains risks and offers strategies rather than drawing out the client’s own values, confidence, or motivations. Missed opportunities include asking what the client wants to protect about weekends, what “a thing” means, or how they want to feel when out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks what the client would like first and incorporates selected options, supporting some shared decision-making. It does not fully position the client as the expert on their own social context, limits, and workable plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers small change statements such as trying water swaps and the shirt trick, but the counselor does not consistently deepen these into stronger change talk. More questions about confidence, desired outcomes, and what matters to the client would better build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor responds to sustain talk like “I’m not an idiot” and “I don’t want to stop at three” with validation and low-effort alternatives rather than confrontation. It would be stronger if these objections were more consistently reframed into the client’s own reasons for a small change.
