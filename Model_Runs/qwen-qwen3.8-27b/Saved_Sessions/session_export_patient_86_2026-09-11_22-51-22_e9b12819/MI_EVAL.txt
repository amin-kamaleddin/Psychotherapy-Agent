# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids pressure, moralizing, or direct confrontation. Warmth is functional rather than deeply affirming, limiting the score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician makes some effort to understand the client's perspective, but reflections stay mostly content-level. Emotional and meaning-level material, such as fatigue and the identity of the cigarette as a work-break ritual, is not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays connected to smoking, but the focus is inconsistent. The middle portion drifts through multiple interventions before settling on a small, practical cue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks for input and builds on the client's contributions, especially the hot drink, stretch, and client-generated cue. However, much of the session is driven by the clinician's menu of options, making the collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician regularly supports choice and avoids coercion, using language such as no pressure and do what fits. The final plan is client-owned, though repeated offers of options can feel mildly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician provides information and options rather than eliciting the client's own motivations and values. There is minimal sustained effort to draw out change talk or deepen the client's own reasons for trying something small.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician shows some partnership by incorporating the client's own phrase and tailoring the plan to his reality. However, the session becomes more expert-driven when the clinician presents a numbered list of options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only limited efforts to elicit the client's own change talk. The clinician does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen the client's emerging reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing and uses softening language, but does not consistently pivot sustain talk into change talk. The session remains focused on the clinician's options rather than exploring the pull toward change.
