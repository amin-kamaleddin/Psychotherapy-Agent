# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, never labels or dismisses Mai's feelings. Key moments include affirming her right to information and validating her experience as a 'really lonely place to be.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate, interpretive empathy that goes beyond surface-level reflection. Deeply captures emotional cores such as 'you wanted to matter to the person making decisions about your body.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a generally clear focus on the target behavior and progresses logically toward a concrete plan. However, the middle section wanders into broader emotional territory (childhood patterns) without always explicitly reconnecting to medication adherence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames the conversation as a joint exploration between equals, using inclusive, non-directive language. The session is genuinely co-constructed: Mai brings the problem, identifies the barrier, and generates the solution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never prescribes or dictates action; instead invites Mai to generate her own plan. Mai produces the concrete steps herself (writing down the question, timing it with her routine), and the closing 'That feels very you' reinforces ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is elicited from within Mai rather than provided externally. The psychiatrist asks open questions that draw out Mai's own values and reasons for change ('It's mine. I'm doing something for me').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Mai as the expert on her own life, explicitly ceding authority ('I may not be the person who can answer that with confidence'). Acts as a guide who draws out Mai's insights rather than an authority who provides them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes multiple instances of change language and amplifies them with affirmations. Could have been more systematic in exploring confidence or eliciting deeper commitment language (e.g., a readiness ruler).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles barriers and fears with remarkable skill—validating the 'sigh on the phone' image and the worry about being annoying without arguing or dismissing. Effectively transforms barriers into problem-solving opportunities by asking what would make it feel less like a big deal.
