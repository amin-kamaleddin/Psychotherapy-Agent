# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently validates Nadine’s exhaustion, worry, and caregiving concerns with a nonjudgmental tone. A slightly lower score reflects moments where reassurance may preempt further exploration of her guilt or fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses accurate reflections of Nadine’s fatigue, fear, and need for predictability. Deeper emotional themes such as shame or fear of being a failing parent could have been explored further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on infant sleep/waking patterns and moves toward a specific, manageable action step. The initial range of options and final response alternatives slightly dilute a single collaborative focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nadine actively shapes the plan, choosing the sleep focus and identifying cues and an off-ramp. The counselor still maintains a noticeable expert role and provides normative guidance that slightly limits full co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strongly supports Nadine’s choice, ownership, and right to stop the step if the baby is distressed. The off-ramp and emphasis on her judgment reduce pressure and support self-directed decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor draws out Nadine’s own motivations, preferences, and confidence in reading her baby’s cues. Some reassurance and information are offered before fully eliciting more client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor positions Nadine as the expert on her baby and invites her to define safety cues and acceptable limits. The flow remains strong, though the labeled alternatives in the final message could feel less naturally collaborative if spoken aloud.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nadine expresses desire, ability, and willingness to try a small, concrete step such as trusting one feed. The counselor could more systematically amplify and deepen this change talk once it appears.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled gently by making concerns acceptable and providing a clear off-ramp rather than arguing against them. Reframing her worry as evidence of care and attunement could further soften resistance and strengthen confidence.
