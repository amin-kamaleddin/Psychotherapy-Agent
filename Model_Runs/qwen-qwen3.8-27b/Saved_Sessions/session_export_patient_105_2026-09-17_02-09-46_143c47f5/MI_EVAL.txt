# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, patience, and nonjudgment while normalizing Naomi’s anxiety and validating her observations. It could be strengthened by more explicitly affirming her worth beyond performance or coping success.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Naomi’s mixed experience, including caring about doing well while feeling physically alarmed. Deeper empathy could include more explicit acknowledgment of the fear, shame, and exposure of being watched.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on Naomi’s social anxiety during shoots and on reducing in-the-moment self-monitoring, freezing, and shutdown. Some multi-part questions divide attention, so briefer or choice-focused questions could improve clarity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor works with Naomi rather than directing her, using language that suggests joint exploration. The focus could be stronger if the target behavior were more explicitly co-constructed with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a major strength, with repeated invitations for Naomi to choose, explore, and decide without pressure or direct advice. The counselor emphasizes freedom to try a small step rather than requiring commitment or mastery.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Naomi’s own motivations, values, and possible anchors, such as the ISO dial and holding the strap. It could be strengthened by more deliberate scaling, commitment language, or deeper exploration of photography identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction treats Naomi as the expert on her own experience and builds on her generated ideas. Partnership could be further supported by more explicit acknowledgment of her expertise and a less question-driven flow.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions help Naomi express clear change talk, such as wanting to shoot rather than monitor herself. The counselor could amplify, summarize, and scale this change talk to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Naomi’s doubts and past attempts are validated without challenge or argument, and the counselor redirects toward manageable action. Some double-sided reflections could better hold her ambivalence explicitly.
