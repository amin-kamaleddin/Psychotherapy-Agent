# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated the client's frustration and need for pain relief. Slightly undermined acceptance by repeating scripted dental instructions after the client said he had already heard them.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Understood the immediate practical concern of dental pain and the need to be examined. Did not consistently reflect the deeper relational themes of feeling lectured, exhausted, or infantilized.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on urgent dental pain and access to dental care, but drifted away from the stated MI target of smoking cessation. The session remained mostly practical rather than oriented toward exploring the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Included a few collaborative questions, such as what the client would prefer, but largely controlled the conversation with pre-made plans and instructions. The client's own plan and preference to be left to manage were not fully honored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respected the client's right to avoid lectures and acknowledged his choice to leave. Autonomy support was weakened by repeated directives, specific dosing, and repeated scripts after the client expressed not wanting hand-holding.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Began with an open evocative question and asked a few preference-based questions. However, there was little evocation of the client's own motivations, values, or confidence related to smoking cessation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was largely expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying scripts, dosing, and a detailed plan. The client's expertise and ownership were not sufficiently integrated into a collaborative problem-solving process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There was virtually no deliberate effort to elicit change talk related to smoking cessation. The session did not use questions, reflections, or scaling to explore any motivation for reconsidering smoking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with the client's resistance, which is a positive. However, the therapist did not use MI-consistent strategies, such as double-sided reflections, to acknowledge and soften the client's sustain talk.
