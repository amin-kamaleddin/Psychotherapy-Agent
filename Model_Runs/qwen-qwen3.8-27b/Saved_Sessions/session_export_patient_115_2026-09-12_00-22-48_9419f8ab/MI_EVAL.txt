# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental, with supportive statements about the client's progress and help-seeking. However, warmth is fairly formulaic, and key emotional distress is only briefly acknowledged rather than consistently validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor offers some reflections of feeling but mostly responds with medical questions and practical instructions. Important emotional and ambivalent themes are missed, so empathy is present but limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management, inhaler/spacer use, safety, and concrete next steps. It is directionally strong, though the approach is more task-oriented than collaboratively motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's preferences shape practical outputs such as the one-page checklist and provider note. However, much of the session is provider-led, with the counselor delivering information and a preformed plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids forcing immediate smoking cessation and offers options for next steps. Still, the interaction is quite directive, with many instructions rather than strong reinforcement of the client's own choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor asks some open questions about what the client wants to focus on and what might help. There is limited use of deeper MI evocation, such as exploring values, confidence, past successes, or personal meaning in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites the client to choose a focus, first step, and format, showing some partnership. The interaction remains largely expert-driven, with the counselor positioned as the knowledgeable provider.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client produces useful change talk about reducing smoking, trying a spacer, and getting through shifts. The counselor does not consistently evoke, affirm, or amplify this language to strengthen the client's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with or shaming the client's resistance, and accepts harm reduction as progress. However, sustain talk is often acknowledged or bypassed rather than strategically redirected with MI-consistent skills.
