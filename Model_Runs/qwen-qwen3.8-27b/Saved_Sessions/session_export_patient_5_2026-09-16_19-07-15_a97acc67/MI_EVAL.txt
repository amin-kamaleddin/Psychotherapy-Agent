# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, warmth, and respect while validating the patient’s caution as protective. Avoids judgment, minimization, or corrective stance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses frequent and often accurate reflections to capture the patient’s meaning, goals, and safety concerns. However, the reflections remain somewhat cognitive and do not fully address the emotional fear, anxiety, or sleep disruption underlying her worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a coherent focus on citalopram, its purpose, and the safety of switching from phenelzine. Progresses from general clarification to specific timing, but lacks a clear closing summary or final next-step consolidation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites the patient to co-construct the conversation and repeatedly asks for her perspective, allowing her concerns to guide the session. A few clinician-supplied question stems are mildly leading, but collaboration is otherwise strong and client-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supports patient ownership by asking her to identify priorities, concerns, and doable steps rather than directing her. Would benefit from more explicit affirmation of her right to choose when and how to proceed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out the patient’s own reasons for safety, readiness, and willingness to try the medication. Does not fully deepen motivation using broader MI techniques such as importance/confidence scaling, values, or future-oriented outcomes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Takes a collaborative, non-expert stance by inviting the patient to choose what to explore first and using her own language. Occasionally introduces structured suggestions that are helpful but slightly clinician-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits strong patient change language about safety, side-effect preparation, and willingness to try the medication if timing is clear. Change talk is relevant but not fully developed across multiple functions such as importance, confidence, values, or future motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reframes the patient’s sustain talk as protective caution rather than resistance, while avoiding argument or excessive reassurance. The session remains heavily focused on the safety barrier, with relatively limited active pivoting to broader motivation for change.
