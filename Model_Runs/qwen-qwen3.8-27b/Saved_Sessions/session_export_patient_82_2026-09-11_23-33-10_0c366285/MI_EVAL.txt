# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, normalizing ambivalence and validating Lukas’s mixed feelings. Acceptance is strong but not perfect because the language sometimes feels formulaic, explanatory, or interpretive rather than fully warm and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately captures Lukas’s ambivalence, identity conflict, and the emotional meaning of the drinking. Empathy is limited by reflections that often remain cognitive and do not fully attend to frustration, such as feeling pathologized or pushed toward a fix.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a relevant focus on the alcohol experience and its effects on school and practice, but gradually drifts into broader psychodynamic and self-esteem themes. Direction is weakened because no clear alcohol-related target behavior or decision is collaboratively established.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and uses questions rather than lecturing, creating some collaborative interaction. However, frequent multiple-choice prompts and interpretive suggestions make the conversation more counselor-guided than mutually co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through offers of choice and non-persuasive language, but the psychiatrist often introduces strategies or coping options before Lukas feels ready. It would be stronger with more explicit statements that Lukas is in charge and that no decision is required today.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions to explore Lukas’s experience, motives, and internal voice. Evocation is not consistently effective for MI because it does not systematically elicit or develop Lukas’s own change talk about alcohol, values, goals, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts partnership by asking what Lukas wants from the session and offering options. Partnership is inconsistent because the clinician often places themselves in an expert-interpreting role and asks multiple questions at once, prompting pushback on pacing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist notices some client change talk related to protecting school, athletics, and future goals. However, the clinician does not consistently reflect, expand, or amplify that change talk using standard MI elicitation strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk without judgment and sometimes reflects it neutrally. The score is limited because the clinician does not actively use double-sided reflections or strategic follow-ups to soften sustain talk while highlighting reasons for change.
