# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but safety-focused directives can feel controlling and reduce unconditional positive regard. Acceptance feels conditional on safety compliance rather than consistently warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are helpful reflections of exhaustion and distress, but the counselor often remains surface-level and repetitive. Deeper emotional pain, ambivalence, and frustration are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on self-harm risk, safety, and alternative coping strategies. The main limitation is that the direction is more crisis-managed than MI-facilitated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and uses some collaborative language, but the client does not substantially shape the session. The counselor remains the primary architect of the safety plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Choice is sometimes acknowledged, but frequent directive and commitment-focused language reduces perceived autonomy. The client is often guided through prescribed safety steps rather than supported as the owner of her decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few evocative opening questions, but the counselor does not consistently draw out the client's own values, motivations, or strengths. The session moves too quickly into procedural safety planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites some client input, especially in choosing coping steps and safety contacts. However, the partnership is uneven because the counselor sets the agenda and provides most of the structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor makes some initial invitations for change talk but does not actively build on the client's statements. Important change talk is missed when not reflected, amplified, or explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoids arguing with sustain talk and occasionally redirects toward safer coping. However, there is limited strategic use of double-sided reflections or deeper ambivalence work.
