# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used respectful, normalizing, and nonjudgmental language that reduced defensiveness and validated Erik's concerns about medication. The warmth was practical, but lacked deeper affirmation of broader self-worth for a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected Erik's frustration, fear of lifelong pills, and desire to avoid sounding argumentative with his doctor. Reflections were somewhat logistical and did not fully explore deeper emotions such as loss of control or identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on medication avoidance, diet, alcohol use, monitoring, and concrete next steps. There was clear momentum toward a practical plan, though the session became quite task-focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Erik's preferences and helped co-construct a one-page log, script choices, and reminder plan. Collaboration was solid, but the psychiatrist still introduced much of the structure and content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly offered choices, avoided coercive language, and respected Erik's boundary around reminders. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit affirmation that Erik is the decision-maker about medication and priorities.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some self-generated reasons for change, such as avoiding pills and keeping the plan simple. However, deeper exploration of Erik's values, fears, and intrinsic motivations was limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and allowed Erik to choose doable steps and a natural communication script. The interaction remained somewhat expert-led because specific behavioral targets and a log structure were introduced by the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik articulated concrete change intentions, and the psychiatrist framed them positively. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or deepen change talk through scaling questions, values exploration, or stronger statements of need, ability, or desire.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged Erik's reasons for maintaining the status quo without arguing or reinforcing resistance. It would improve with stronger double-sided reflections that pivot sustain talk back toward change talk.
