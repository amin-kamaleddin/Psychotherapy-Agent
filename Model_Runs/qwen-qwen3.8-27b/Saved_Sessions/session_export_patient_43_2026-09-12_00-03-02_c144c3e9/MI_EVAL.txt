# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a nonjudgmental and validating tone while avoiding blame or shaming. Some responses were generic, and the move toward practical problem-solving slightly reduced the depth of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured work stress, physical discomfort, and pressure from others, but stayed mostly factual. Deeper emotional themes, such as being judged, controlled, or conflicted about identity, were not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session began with smoking but quickly shifted to managing marital conflict and reducing his wife's pressure. Focus on smoking reduction and its meaning was not consistently maintained.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked for Eric's preferences and adjusted after some rejections, showing partial collaboration. However, much of the content, including scripts and plans, was counselor-generated rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Eric's choices and lack of readiness were respected, and the psychiatrist avoided direct persuasion. The frequent use of specific scripts and step-by-step plans made the approach more directive than strongly autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some evocative questions were asked early, but they were not developed further. The psychiatrist often offered external solutions instead of drawing out Eric's own reasons, values, and internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction included some choice-offering, but it quickly became expert-driven. Eric was not clearly positioned as the primary source of his own ideas, solutions, and direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Eric offered some change-relevant statements, such as willingness to consider cutting down if pressure stopped. The psychiatrist rarely reflected, amplified, or built on this change talk before moving into advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not directly argue with Eric's denial but did little to reframe or expand his ambivalence. Some normalization of smoking as helpful may reinforce the status quo rather than soften sustain talk.
