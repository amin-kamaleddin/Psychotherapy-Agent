# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates respect, validation, and nonjudgment toward the client’s goals and ongoing alcohol use. Statements affirm the client as a person rather than focusing only on the problem.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate, nuanced, and attuned to the client’s underlying values, relief, and desire to avoid shame. The counselor tracks meaning well without imposing interpretations.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains largely focused on reducing drinking and managing aggression, moving from strengths to concrete coping steps. Focus occasionally drifts toward abstract language about choice, agency, and pause.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor builds on the client’s own examples and language, such as the shed, tea, kettle, and partner’s role. The session is driven by the client’s ideas rather than a prescribed plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly invites the client’s perspective and emphasizes personal choice, ownership, and self-designed steps. There is no pressure to abstain or follow a fixed recovery program.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor elicits the client’s own motivations, values, and solutions through open-ended questions. Motivation emerges from the client rather than from counselor-provided advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor follows the client’s examples and treats the client’s experience as central to the session. Some therapist-generated conceptual labels subtly shift interpretation away from the client’s own meaning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is frequently elicited and strengthened through questions that lead to concrete, client-generated strategies. The counselor reinforces progress by naming the client’s own strengths and shifts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about continued drinking is acknowledged without judgment and reframed as manageable progress rather than failure. The response could be stronger with more explicit double-sided reflections or deeper exploration of the status quo.
