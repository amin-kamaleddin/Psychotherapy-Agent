# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warm, unconditional positive regard throughout. Never judges or labels, even when Marek is defensive or minimizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional reflective listening that captures deeper needs beneath surface behavior (e.g., the quiet, the not having to analyze). Reflections deepen progressively and accurately represent Marek's perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on alcohol use with logical progression from defensiveness to a concrete plan. The middle section could have been slightly tighter, and a more explicit summary of progress toward a target behavior before closing would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely equal power dynamic throughout; the session is a joint exploration, not an expert consultation. The final plan (3–4 beers) emerges entirely from Marek's own thinking.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never pressures or implies obligation; consistently frames any potential change as the client's own choice. Marek's cautious commitment is met with acceptance rather than pushing for more.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn out from within Marek through strategic open questions and reflections, with no didactic teaching or external reasons for change. Marek's change talk emerges organically from the process.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise consistently located within the client ('You know your own drinking better than anyone here'). The psychiatrist acts as a facilitator, never assuming authority or providing unsolicited advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits significant change talk through well-crafted questions. Could be more deliberate in amplifying and reflecting back Marek's strongest change talk moments (e.g., 'I'm not as set in my ways as I thought') to build momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated and agreed with ('You're right that nobody told you to quit, and it's not meth') rather than countered. Gently redirects focus from the behavior to the underlying need without confrontation.
