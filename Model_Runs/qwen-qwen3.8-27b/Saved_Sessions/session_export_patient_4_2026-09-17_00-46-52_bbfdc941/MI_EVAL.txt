# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, validation, and nonjudgment. Warmth remains professional rather than deeply personal, and unconditional worth/safety is not explicitly and repeatedly affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate, nuanced, and therapeutically useful. The psychiatrist captures Aizada’s underlying fear, ambivalence, and desire for control, such as naming “the dark” as central distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has clear momentum from values to a concrete walk plan, timing, one-week marker, and morning strategy. Direction could be tightened by explicitly defining a single target behavior instead of investing heavily in convincing Dr. Brown.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as a collaborative partner and builds on Aizada’s own ideas. The mutual, co-constructive framing could be made more explicit in language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice and ownership are regularly supported without directives. Autonomy support could be stronger by explicitly reaffirming Aizada’s right to choose any path, including not doing the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently evokes Aizada’s own goals, strategies, and values rather than supplying reasons for change. Evocation could be maximized by more fully developing confidence, importance, or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aizada is treated as the expert on her experience, and her ideas substantially shape the session. Partnership could become stronger by explicitly naming her authority and the shared decision-making process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections frequently evoke desire, ability, need, and small steps. Change talk could be maximized by explicitly labeling or systematically expanding DARN-CAT themes and exploring confidence more deeply.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with biomedical concerns and uses some nonargumentative, double-sided reflection. However, the session remains heavily focused on cancer fear, tests, and convincing Dr. Brown, and the final sustain talk is left unresolved.
