# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses warm, nonjudgmental, affirming language and avoids shaming the client’s weight or scale concerns. Acceptance is strong, though it could be more explicitly unconditional when the client admits she may slip or be imperfect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s ambivalence, fear of the scale, and desire to avoid restrictive dieting. Deeper empathy could be strengthened by exploring the emotional meaning of the number and the client’s isolation around asking for support.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays highly focused on a sustainable, non-diet approach to exercise, eating, and scale anxiety. The work progresses clearly from identifying goals to selecting an experiment, planning the weigh-in, and scheduling follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client actively contributes to the plan, including choosing a strength-focused experiment, limiting weigh-ins, and creating her own cue. Collaboration could become more MI by eliciting the client’s own ideas before presenting clinician-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, avoids pressure, and supports the client in making key decisions about the plan. Autonomy support could be stronger by inviting the client to generate her own options before introducing detailed recommendations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some reflective listening and preference questions, but does not consistently draw out the client’s deeper values, confidence, or self-efficacy. More evocative questions and scaling would help generate stronger client-driven change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and adjusts the plan based on her preferences. Partnership is strong, though it could be more client-led when detailed content is introduced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change statements, and the psychiatrist affirms some of them. The psychiatrist could more systematically evoke, amplify, and build on the client’s reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s weight-related goals and “need to diet” voice without arguing or dismissing them. Softening could be improved by using more double-sided reflections and directly working with the ambivalence around the scale.
