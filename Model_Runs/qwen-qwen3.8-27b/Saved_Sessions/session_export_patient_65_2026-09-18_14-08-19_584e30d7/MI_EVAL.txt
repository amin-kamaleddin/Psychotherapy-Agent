# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgmental stance with explicit validation of the client's frustration and confusion. However, the acceptance relies heavily on formulaic phrases like 'That makes sense' and lacks explicit affirmation of the client's worth independent of compliance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking and attention to nuance in the client's language. Reflections stay at a surface level with missed opportunities to explore deeper emotional themes like disempowerment or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior throughout the entire session with a clear, purposeful progression from rapport to plan to confidence. No topic drift or aimlessness; pacing is well-calibrated for a guarded, minimally engaged client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective collaborative framing that builds on the client's contributions rather than overriding them. The psychiatrist retains more of the conversational architecture with a structured question sequence rather than letting the client's leads determine direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout; the client generates his own plan, solutions, and final decision entirely on his own. No directive language, pressure, or implicit control at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Arguably the strongest element of the session; the entire plan emerges from the client's own mouth through strategic questioning. The psychiatrist serves as a mirror and questioner rather than a provider of answers, which is textbook MI evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-directive stance with open-ended questions and inclusive language. The session follows a somewhat predetermined path (logistics to plan to confidence) rather than genuinely co-creating direction with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through open questions and highlighting the client's own statements of commitment and activation. The change talk is primarily practical and logistical rather than values-based, limiting the depth of intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively handles resistance without arguing, using a sophisticated double-sided approach that lets the client self-correct. Could have used more explicit double-sided reflections to further honor both sides of the ambivalence.
