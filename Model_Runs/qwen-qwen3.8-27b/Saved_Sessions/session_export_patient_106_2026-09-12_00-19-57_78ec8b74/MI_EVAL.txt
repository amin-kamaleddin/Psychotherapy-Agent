# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, validating ambivalence and normalizing slips without judgment or moralizing. A slight limitation is that warmth can feel formulaic and continued suggestions after declines may reduce the sense of full acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is adequate reflective listening, but emotional cues such as feeling expected to “be okay” and walking a thin line are not deeply reflected. Empathy is inconsistent and often shifts too quickly toward problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking around the son, with clear momentum toward a concrete behavior-specific plan. Focus occasionally narrows to logistics rather than clarifying broader change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration improves when the patient contributes ideas like radio, coffee ritual, and reminder phrases, but much of the session is driven by the psychiatrist’s structured options. More patient-generated planning would strengthen equal collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames choices as optional, avoids pressure to quit, and invites the patient to select doable steps. Some directionality appears in repeated recommendations, timers, and reoffering declined options like nicotine gum.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some patient-generated strategies are evoked, including talk radio, coffee ritual, and self-reminder phrases. The approach remains more suggestive than evocative, with frequent practitioner-supplied content.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening is collaborative, but the psychiatrist frequently leads with multiple structured options and a quick plan, making the interaction feel more advisor-led. Continuing suggestions after repeated declines weakens partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient produces meaningful change talk, especially around protecting her son. The psychiatrist supports this but does not consistently deepen or amplify it with evocative questions or DARN-CRAF strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, but responses often move back into harm-reduction planning rather than strategically softening it. More double-sided reflections and values-based pivots would be more effective.
