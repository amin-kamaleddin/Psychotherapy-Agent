# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates respect and nonjudgment while affirming the client’s effort and progress. Affirmations are often behavior-focused and could be more individualized to the client’s broader struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shows good reflective listening to ambivalence, practical concerns, and emerging motivation. Could more deeply explore emotional themes such as shame, stigma, or the meaning of her daughter’s disapproval.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and consistent focus on smoking cessation, especially reducing morning smoking. Efficiently guides the session from ambivalence to a concrete, manageable action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructs a tailored plan using the client’s own ideas and invites her to tweak it. Could allow the client to generate more options before the counselor offers multiple strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites choice, respects preferences, and avoids imposing a plan. Still offers many counselor-generated options, which can subtly shift the interaction toward advice-giving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses questions and scaling to elicit some change talk, confidence, and plan components. Mixes evocation with problem-solving and offers tactics before fully drawing out the client’s values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and adapts to the client’s choices. Frequently preloads strategies, making the interaction collaborative but still somewhat expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits meaningful change talk around action, ability, and confidence. Could more strategically amplify deeper motivations, identity, and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responds to barriers without arguing and supports self-compassion after slips. Could more consistently use double-sided reflections and pivot obstacles into change talk.
