# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while validating the client’s conflict. Acceptance was strong, though a few mildly evaluative comments kept it just short of unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used accurate complex reflections and showed clear attunement to the client’s feelings and ambivalence. Empathy was effective but could be deeper in reflecting shame, fear, and identity-level struggle.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remained tightly focused on the target behavior of assertively setting a housing boundary with the flatmate. Each reflection and question consistently moved the conversation toward a clear boundary statement and timeline.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked with the client to construct the boundary language and timing rather than prescribing it. Collaboration was strong, though some suggestive prompts and evaluative comments slightly shifted the interaction toward counselor influence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly invited the client to choose wording that felt honest and true, supporting his sense of ownership. Autonomy support was effective, but could have more explicitly reinforced the client’s right to choose otherwise and own the consequences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew out the client’s own reasons, boundary language, and motivation to act. Evocation was strong, though occasionally the counselor supplied wording or structure instead of waiting longer for the client to generate it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invited the client to shape the conversation and did not dominate the session with an expert stance. Partnership was solid, but it could be strengthened by using more explicit equal-partner language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor successfully elicited concrete change talk, including a specific boundary statement and timeline. Change talk was strong, but could have been deepened by exploring confidence, ability, past successes, or personal importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled the client’s fear and avoidance without arguing or reinforcing them, using balanced reflections. Softening sustain talk was effective, though it could be more strategically pivoted toward values, independence, or readiness to act.
