# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental, respectful tone with no pressure despite the client’s smoking history and medical concerns. Warmth is somewhat procedural and could deepen further into individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid complex reflections capture ambivalence, routine, identity, and health-history concerns. Empathy could go deeper into fear, shame, fatigue, and the meaning of smoking as a “friend.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is organized and follows the client’s shift to dental concerns with clear summaries and transitions. A stronger score would require more consistent strategic direction toward a change-related smoking thread while still honoring autonomy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative language is used and client preferences shape the dental plan and focus of the session. The interaction remains somewhat counselor-led and does not fully demonstrate equal co-creation or shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongest domain, consistently respecting choice, avoiding pressure, and honoring the client’s decision to pause smoking discussion. The client is clearly positioned as the final decision-maker about priorities and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits preferences and concerns rather than prescribing or lecturing. Evocation is less strong for deeper motivation, values, reasons for change, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is treated as the expert on his life and routine, with invitations to define priorities and leave topics aside. More explicit co-creation or power-sharing language would strengthen full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are attempts to evoke change-related thinking and affirm emerging openness, such as skipping the dinner cigarette. The work would benefit from more systematic development of DARN-CAT, confidence/importance questions, and amplification of change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and uses balanced, softening reflections about smoking being woven into daily life. Responses are more neutral than strategically softening, with less use of decisional balance, paradoxical reflection, or redirection of sustain talk.
