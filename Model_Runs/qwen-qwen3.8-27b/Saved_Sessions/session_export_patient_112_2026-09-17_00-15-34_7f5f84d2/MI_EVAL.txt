# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard, respects Rosa’s smoker identity, and affirms her strengths without judgment or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects ambivalence and emotions, though some therapist-generated metaphors go beyond the client’s exact language and deeper identity/loss themes could be explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation and narrows the conversation to concrete situations, but the link to a cessation decision or readiness is sometimes implicit and the session drifts into identity/coping.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses shared language and builds on Rosa’s ideas, though frequent therapist interpretations, reframings, and suggested options limit fully equal collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Takes a noncontrolling stance, respects Rosa’s choice, and avoids pressure, but could more explicitly reinforce client ownership and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes client insight about coping and the “quiet,” but is less focused on eliciting change talk, confidence, or commitment to smoking cessation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats Rosa as the expert on her own life and invites her to generate options, but frequently supplied suggestions can subtly steer the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits values, desired benefits, and small willingness, but uses limited DARN-C strategies and does not strongly build Rosa’s own change talk or self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflects and normalizes barriers without arguing, but spends substantial time on the feared costs of quitting and does not consistently pivot toward change talk.
