# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, nonjudgment, and unconditional positive regard, even when the client admits to drinking four to six glasses of orange juice and gaining weight. Normalizing setbacks and affirming effort without moralizing demonstrates strong acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflective listening to capture the client's frustration, ambivalence, and practical concerns. However, the deeper emotional core—such as self-criticism, disappointment, and feeling out of control—is not fully explored, preventing a top score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on the target behaviors of weight loss, diet modification, and reducing orange juice consumption. The psychiatrist steadily guides the conversation toward concrete, change-oriented steps with little topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner, structuring the interaction around the client's preferences, ideas, and chosen tools. The client's contributions substantially shape the session's direction and plan, reflecting a strongly collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the client's perception of choice and avoids controlling language. The plan is presented as a tool the client can use and own, rather than as an external demand.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out some motivation, confidence, and planning through open questions, but the session frequently shifts into structured problem-solving and therapist-generated planning. More values-based exploration and amplification of the client's own change talk would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and avoids assuming an expert role, allowing the client to shape the focus and options. However, occasional shifts into agenda-setting or planner roles, such as drafting a written plan, subtly reduce fully collaborative partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits confidence and planning language but often moves quickly into problem-solving rather than amplifying or expanding the client's own change talk. Important statements about feeling tired, proud, and ready are acknowledged but not deeply developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk effectively without arguing, moralizing, or reinforcing the client's preference for orange juice. Reflecting ambivalence and framing slips as habits rather than failures reduces defensiveness, though the sustain talk is not consistently pivoted into change talk.
