# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard throughout the session. Even when Eric is defensive or minimizing, responses remain validating rather than corrective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate and consistent reflective listening, capturing both the content and emotional subtext of Eric's statements. Key reflections like identifying Eric as 'the steady one' show deep attunement to his identity and values.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent focus on smoking with clear progressive movement from context-setting through exploring consequences, function, and alternatives. Pacing is appropriate for a pre-contemplation client while maintaining momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Eric's ideas substantially shape the session's direction, with the psychiatrist following his lead into topics like stairs, wife's stress, and the meaning of breaks. The session feels like a genuine conversation between equals rather than a structured intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Eric's ownership and right to choose, never imposing advice, timelines, or prescriptive solutions. Eric's ambivalence is met with validation and continued exploration on his own terms throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within Eric through strategic questions that connect to his own values—his body, his marriage, his need for autonomy. Change talk emerges organically rather than being prompted by external consequences or information.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positions Eric as the authority on his own experience with questions like 'What's your own read on it?' and 'what would it have to be?' The power dynamic is equal throughout, with the psychiatrist functioning as a curious guide rather than an authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits change talk through strategic questions tied to Eric's values and relationships, producing organic statements about wanting to be less tense and not being the reason his wife is stressed. Could push slightly further in developing and strengthening these emerging change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by validating Eric's reasons for smoking while holding the change-relevant perspective simultaneously. The sustain talk around routine and stress relief could have been explored more deeply to help Eric see these functions are separable from smoking.
