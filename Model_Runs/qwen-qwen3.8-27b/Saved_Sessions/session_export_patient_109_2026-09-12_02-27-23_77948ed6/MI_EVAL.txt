# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes the patient’s struggle. Some acceptance feels formulaic, and repeated suggestions slightly reduce its depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflective listening to the patient’s stuckness and need to keep things low-pressure. The reflections could have gone deeper into underlying shame or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on returning to the gym and a small, actionable step. Occasional emphasis on planning details moves the conversation away from exploring ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and respects the patient’s final choice to try the timer in the car. However, most ideas and structures are introduced by the psychiatrist rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirms the patient’s right to choose, but repeated suggestions after hesitation can feel pressuring. This mixed approach limits autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited effort to elicit the patient’s own motivations for change. The psychiatrist often offers suggestions instead of exploring Rafael’s internal reasons for trying.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for preferences and works toward a low-pressure plan. The repeated introduction of multiple options makes the collaboration feel uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some small-step questions but does not strongly use DARN-CAT categories to draw out change talk. Commitments such as “maybe I’ll let you know if I get out” are not sufficiently amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without being reinforced, such as worries about effort and forgetting. The psychiatrist does not consistently redirect this ambivalence into change talk.
