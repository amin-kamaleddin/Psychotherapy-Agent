# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally respects Erik and normalizes his struggle, but warmth remains more practical and problem-solving than deeply affirming. More consistent unconditional positive regard is needed, especially during shame or self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate content reflections and acknowledges realistic barriers, but often stays factual and task-focused. Deeper emotional and perspective-taking reflections would better capture Erik’s ambivalence, pride, shame, and frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on concrete weight-related lifestyle changes and narrows well into a specific, testable plan. Some drift occurs in repeated plan summaries and generic safety reminders, but overall behavioral direction is clear.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik’s preferences shape the final plan, showing partial collaboration, but the psychiatrist supplies most of the content and solutions. A more equal partnership would involve Erik generating and shaping more of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are autonomy-supportive moves such as letting Erik choose the tracker, lift, and whether to use a script. However, directive language and prescriptive tips reduce the emphasis on Erik’s ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly offers strategies and plans rather than eliciting Erik’s own reasons, values, or confidence for change. More evoking questions and reflections would help strengthen Erik’s intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often adopts an expert, instructive stance, providing quick strategies and controlling much of the agenda. More explicit co-creation and treating Erik as the expert on his own life would improve partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Erik offers useful change-related statements, but the psychiatrist rarely amplifies or builds on them. More deliberate reflection, expansion, and evocation of Erik’s own reasons and commitment would strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing sustain talk and often redirects toward smaller, manageable steps. However, responses sometimes jump into problem-solving or prescribed reframes instead of using MI-consistent reflections to soften ambivalence.
