# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Richard’s hesitancy without pressure. Acceptance was strong but remained somewhat structured rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed strong reflective listening and accurately captured Richard’s practical and emotional concerns. Some reflections stayed at a practical level and did not fully explore deeper emotional undercurrents.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior of trying cholesterol medication and communicating a practical plan to the doctor. Pacing and questions consistently moved Richard toward a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborated well by inviting Richard’s perspective and building the plan from his own ideas. The process was still somewhat guided by the clinician’s structure and therapeutic agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported Richard’s ownership, choice, and language in developing the plan. There was no pressure to agree, and Richard clearly felt in control of the decision and wording.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively drew out Richard’s own motivations and practical criteria for trying treatment. Evocation could be deeper with more values-based, confidence, or commitment exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative, non-expert stance and treated Richard as the authority on his own choices. The session was strong but still followed a fairly clear therapeutic direction rather than being fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, evocative questions helped Richard articulate his own reasons and plan for trying medication. Change talk was frequent but could be developed further through scaling, values, and deeper commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled respectfully without argument or dismissal, and was integrated into a workable plan. The psychiatrist could use more explicit double-sided reflections to actively transform sustain talk into change talk.
