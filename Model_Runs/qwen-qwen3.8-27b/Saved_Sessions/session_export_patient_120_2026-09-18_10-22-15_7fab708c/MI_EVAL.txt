# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and warmth with genuine, contextually specific affirmations. Occasionally veers into interpretive territory, edging toward making meaning for the client rather than purely reflecting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening with accurate, nuanced reflections that capture core ambivalence and identity threats. Reflections occasionally add interpretation beyond what the client explicitly stated, inserting clinician voice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear progressive focus, narrowing from broad ambivalence to a concrete behavioral target (Saturday nights) without being pushy. Could benefit from brief summaries to consolidate themes before moving to the next question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session is clearly shaped by the client's contributions, with the psychiatrist following leads skillfully using fellow-explorer language. Could be strengthened by more explicit co-construction of the session agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support throughout; consistently reinforces the client's ownership and choice without ever imposing advice or a 'you do have choices' narrative. The client naturally identifies her own options without being corrected or directed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out the client's own motivations and reasons for change using classic evocation techniques. Missed the client's 'no choice' framing as an opportunity to evoke autonomy language, and could have more explicitly amplified the change talk produced.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with inclusive, non-directive language and frequent deference to the client's expertise. Occasionally disrupted by interpretive additions that subtly shift the power dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits desire, ability, and commitment talk through open, forward-looking questions. Could deepen the work by more consistently amplifying and summarizing the change talk as it emerges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk respectfully by validating concerns without reinforcing them, using reframing to soften the weight of resistance. One notable moment where the psychiatrist appears to take over the thinking process rather than staying curious.
