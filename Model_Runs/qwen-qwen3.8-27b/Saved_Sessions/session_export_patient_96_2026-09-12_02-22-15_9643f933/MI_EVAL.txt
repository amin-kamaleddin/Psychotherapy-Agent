# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a nonjudgmental, respectful tone and explicitly validated the client’s choice and perspective. Acceptance was solid but somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provided accurate and thoughtful reflections of the client’s frustration and intent. Empathy remained surface-level and did not explore deeper meaning or ambivalence around self-expression and control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did not maintain a clear strategic focus on the referral concern of oral health and the lip piercing. The session drifted toward managing annoyance and ending, without guiding readiness for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited client input and adapted to her preference to move on, giving her choices about session direction. Collaboration was responsive but not fully framed as an equal, co-created partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supported choice, self-determination, and nonjudgment, offering options rather than directives. Strongly respected the client’s right to decide how to handle the situation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked open questions to draw out the client’s perspective and preferred way forward. Evocation was more about session management than eliciting internal motivation, values, or ambivalence about change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited input and avoided imposing a fixed agenda, supporting a collaborative posture. Partnership was accommodative but did not strongly position the client as the expert in her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made only a limited initial effort to identify a change focus and did not use MI strategies to evoke reasons, desire, need, ability, or confidence. The session remained focused on the client’s current stance without generating change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Acknowledged and did not argue with the client’s sustain talk, but did not effectively soften or redirect it. Sustain talk remained dominant due to lack of balanced reflections or gentle pivots toward change.
