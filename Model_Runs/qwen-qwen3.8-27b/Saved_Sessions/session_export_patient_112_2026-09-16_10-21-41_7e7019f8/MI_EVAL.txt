# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming of Rosa’s identity, fears, and low-commitment plan. The main limitation is that the acceptance can feel formulaic and does not fully address her expressed pressure at the end.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate content reflections about cost, friends, withdrawal, and losing part of herself. However, the empathy is not consistently deep, especially for shame, judgment, “begging,” and the final overwhelm.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on smoking cessation and related barriers. However, it becomes scattered across patches, gum, bupropion, insurance, scripts, and social strategies rather than consolidating around Rosa’s chosen next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Rosa’s input and follows some of her preferences, such as asking the nurse and avoiding a formal quitline call. However, the interaction remains somewhat expert-driven because the psychiatrist supplies many options and scripts before fully following Rosa’s stated limit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses autonomy-supportive language such as choices, low commitment, and no promise needed. However, autonomy is undermined by adding extra scripts and backup questions after Rosa says she can only manage one question.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with good evocative questions about what matters to Rosa and what changes she hopes for. Later, the conversation shifts toward information and problem-solving rather than consistently building Rosa’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and asks Rosa to choose among options. However, the psychiatrist still functions as the information expert, introducing treatment options and scripts in a way that limits a true equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa expresses some change talk, especially around the nagging health concern and asking about cost. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, expand, or amplify this change talk to strengthen Rosa’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Rosa’s reasons for staying the same, but often remains focused on barriers and logistics. Strong sustain talk, especially the fear of freezing or “being done,” is not effectively reframed or redirected toward change.
