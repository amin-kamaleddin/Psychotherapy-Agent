# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and support while validating Mai’s need for clarity. Acceptance could become stronger with more individualized affirmation of her frustration about receiving a new medication without a clear explanation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates accurate reflective listening and tracks both cognitive and practical concerns. The main limitation is that some reflections remain focused on the plan rather than fully exploring deeper emotions such as frustration, anxiety, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear focus on the target behavior and guides the conversation progressively from understanding purpose to timing, note details, and a concrete next step. The session stays focused while moving toward actionable adherence support.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor uses collaborative language and invites Mai to co-create the plan, allowing her ideas to shape the questions, note placement, and next step. The interaction consistently reflects a sense of teamwork and shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports Mai’s autonomy by avoiding pressure, asking permission, and framing the plan as something she is in charge of. The counselor also appropriately acknowledges limits of medical advice while helping Mai clarify her own next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor effectively evokes motivation from Mai by helping her identify prevention and self-care as personally meaningful reasons for taking the medication. Mai’s own change talk and readiness are elicited and strengthened throughout the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strongly conveys that Mai is the expert on her own life and preferences, repeatedly inviting her to lead the conversation. Mai’s contributions substantially shape the session’s direction, content, and outcome.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a standout strength, with the counselor repeatedly eliciting Mai’s own desire, need, ability, and commitment language. The counselor amplifies change talk by reflecting and strengthening Mai’s statements without inserting his own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor handles Mai’s ambivalence well using double-sided reflections and redirects sustain talk into constructive next steps. The response to her statement that she might stop taking the medication could be deepened by exploring underlying fears, values, or ambivalence more explicitly.
