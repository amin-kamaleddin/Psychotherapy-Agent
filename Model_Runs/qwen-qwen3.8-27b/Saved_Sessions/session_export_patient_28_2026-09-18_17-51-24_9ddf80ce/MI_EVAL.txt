# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating—normalizes the referral, protects Dante's small step from self-dismissal, and affirms his realistic framing. Acceptance is strong but occasionally feels strategically deployed rather than flowing naturally throughout every exchange, and broader unconditional regard beyond the exercise context is not explicitly offered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate, well-timed, and progressively deeper, including strong complex dual reflections that capture both sides of Dante's ambivalence. Could go slightly deeper into the emotional undertones of moments like Dante's mild shame ('that sounds stupid') and frustration ('I keep going in circles'), occasionally prioritizing cognitive reframing over emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains excellent focus on the target behavior with a clear arc from exploration to a concrete, client-generated plan, never wandering into unrelated territory. Could have included a brief explicit agenda-setting moment early in the session to give Dante a clearer sense of direction from the start.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary collaboration throughout—the session is structured as a genuine joint exploration, the solution (shoes by the door) is entirely client-generated, and Dante's ideas substantially shape the direction and content at every stage. The psychiatrist consistently uses 'we' language and positions himself as a thinking partner rather than an expert.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently removes pressure, affirms client ownership ('it's yours,' 'I trust you to keep it honest'), and never prescribes or pushes commitment. Could be strengthened by explicitly affirming Dante's right to not follow through, which would further separate his worth from his behavior and reduce the fear of failure underlying his ambivalence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes motivation from within Dante through open questions that allow his own values, ideas, and solutions to emerge without external imposition. Primarily operates at the practical/logistical level; a 5 would include deeper evocation of identity, values, and confidence language (e.g., 'What would getting back to that feel like for you as a dad?').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance by asking rather than telling, inviting Dante to define the content, and affirming his authority over his own plan. Occasionally positions himself as the one doing the heavy lifting in reflection and reframing, creating an implicit hierarchy where the counselor becomes the 'meaning-maker' rather than fully ceding interpretive space to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently notices, names, and amplifies Dante's change language without over-interpreting, successfully protecting and building on his own statements of desire and readiness. Could push slightly further on eliciting confidence/ability language (e.g., 'How confident are you the shoes will be by the door?') and a brief readiness check at the close.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk effectively by validating concerns without arguing, actively protecting Dante from self-dismissal, and holding doubt and relief simultaneously. Could be more sophisticated with increased use of double-sided reflections, and one framing ('the question is narrower than that') slightly minimizes rather than fully honors the client's stated barrier.
