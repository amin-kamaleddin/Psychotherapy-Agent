# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client's concerns. Warmth is present but often concise, with limited explicit affirmation around fatigue and body-confidence worries.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses several accurate reflections and shows content-level understanding. Deeper emotional meaning, such as fear of failure and feeling weak, is not fully explored or reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on increasing physical activity and functional strength. There is little topic drift, and the conversation quickly moves toward a concrete, doable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist involves the client in choosing the plan, timing, fallbacks, and check-in format. However, a fairly detailed exercise menu is introduced early, limiting full client-led collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice and ownership are strongly emphasized throughout the session. The psychiatrist avoids pressure and repeatedly reinforces that the client can choose and adjust the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated motivation through scaling, confidence questions, and goal clarification. Evocation remains more planning-oriented and misses opportunities to deepen values, past successes, or personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and uses the client's preferences to shape the plan. Some expert-driven elements remain, especially in presenting structured options early.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates opportunities for change talk and the client expresses meaningful reasons for change. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, reflect back, or strategically deepen that change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not dismiss barriers and uses MI-consistent strategies such as a minimum dose and if-then fallbacks. The responses often remain problem-solving rather than actively softening sustain talk or reframing ambivalence.
