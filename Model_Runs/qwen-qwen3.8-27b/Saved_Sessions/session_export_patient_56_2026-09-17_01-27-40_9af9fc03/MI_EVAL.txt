# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed unconditional positive regard and validated the client’s values and insights without judgment. There were no moments of criticism, labeling, or coercion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate and nuanced reflective listening that captured both content and emotional meaning. The client felt genuinely understood, particularly around the value of family happiness and physical sensations of calm.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a clear goal: aligning daily behavior with the client’s value of family happiness. The therapist guided the conversation from values to a concrete, achievable plan without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was a genuine collaboration, with the therapist following the client’s lead and building on the client’s own ideas. The client’s suggestions, such as the shoe pause and breath, shaped the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supported the client’s sense of choice and self-direction. Language such as “what do you think would help” kept the client in charge of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist helped the client generate their own motivations, reasons, and solutions rather than giving advice. The final plan was strongly rooted in the client’s own values and self-knowledge.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positioned the client as the expert on their own life, values, and family. The stance was warm, non-authoritarian, and consistently respectful of the client’s perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist effectively elicited change talk by asking open questions that helped the client articulate motivations and a personal plan. Slight room for improvement was to more explicitly amplify the client’s strongest change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist handled minor expressions of doubt and self-doubt well by reframing toward agency, intention, and small achievable steps. A score of 5 could be reached by using more explicit double-sided reflections to fully normalize the client’s concerns.
