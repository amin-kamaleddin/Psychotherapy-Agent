# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming or arguing with the client. It reaches 4 rather than 5 because affirmations of the client's effort, self-awareness, or values are somewhat limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's ambivalence between social belonging and safety concerns. They are slightly under a 5 because they do not consistently deepen the emotional meaning beneath the stated conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on risky party drinking and its academic, legal, and safety consequences. The counselor also maintains good momentum by moving from broad ambivalence to concrete, client-generated first steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor builds on the client's own ideas rather than imposing a predetermined plan. Collaboration is strong but could be more explicit through shared agenda-setting and co-created summaries.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently invites the client's own preferences and avoids pressuring him to stop drinking. It falls just short of 5 because the client's freedom to choose and ownership of outcomes are not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently draws out the client's own motivations without lecturing about alcohol risks. It could be stronger by more deeply eliciting values, confidence, and ability language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positions the client as the expert on his own life and choices. It is not a 5 because the partnership is mostly behavioral rather than explicitly articulated or agenda-setting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicits change talk by asking the client to generate small, doable plans. It is not a 5 because the counselor does not consistently amplify, affirm, or summarize the client's change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with or dismissing the client's sustain talk, which is appropriate. However, the counselor does not consistently redirect sustain talk toward discrepancy or more actively soften it using double-sided reflections.
