# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, nonjudgmental, and repeatedly normalizes the client’s fatigue and caution. Acceptance is strong but can feel procedural and less individualized beyond the action plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects some important feelings and meanings, especially around the value of company and music. However, several emotionally significant statements about self-doubt, fear of failure, and burdening the clinician are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well focused on a concrete, realistic step toward increasing activity. The main limitation is that attention narrows toward logistics and repeated plan details rather than broader health values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and adapts when the client rejects more structured supports. Collaboration is strong, though the repeated detailed recaps make the interaction feel somewhat clinician-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes choice, low pressure, and the client’s right to stop or modify the step. Autonomy support is strong, but the prescriptive plan details slightly limit full self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generates useful plan elements, such as choosing the music group and keeping the plan simple. However, much of the behavioral content is supplied by the psychiatrist rather than evoked from the client’s own values and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, respects preferences, and allows the client’s choices to shape the session. Partnership is strong, though the psychiatrist still leads fairly heavily with detailed instructions and summaries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses several useful change-oriented statements, including agreeing to try the music group and keep expectations realistic. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk in terms of values, self-efficacy, or health importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about tiredness, bodily limits, and worry is acknowledged without argument. The response is generally kind, but it could be more strategic in resolving ambivalence and strengthening motivation for change.
