# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, using validating phrases such as “That makes sense” and “Totally fair.” However, acceptance is often brief and quickly shifts into problem-solving before fully sitting with ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s perspective that spending is tied to social connection and a sense of control. Yet the empathy remains somewhat surface-level, especially around embarrassment, belonging, and fear of being seen as “weird.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on practical spending issues such as rent and nights out. However, the focus is not consistently linked to the broader target behavior, including drug use, values, and deeper ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering options and ultimately respecting the client’s choice of a simple reminder. Still, the counselor frequently drives the conversation with concrete plans before fully building from the client’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported at points, especially when affirming that the client does not have to hand over control. However, repeated introduction of multiple behavior-control strategies can still feel prescriptive and somewhat controlling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some effective attempts to elicit the client’s own motivations, such as exploring feelings before and after spending. Overall, though, the psychiatrist provides more suggestions than sustained evocation of the client’s internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a partially collaborative stance by asking for the client’s preference and adjusting after pushback. The interaction can still feel expert-driven when structured options are introduced before the client’s own solutions are fully explored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk and affirms the client’s goals around balance and rent. However, the counselor does not consistently amplify, deepen, or summarize the client’s own motivations in a way that strengthens them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with the client’s sustain talk and sometimes reframes it in a validating way. A stronger use of double-sided reflections and strategic redirections would further soften the sustain talk while keeping change open.
