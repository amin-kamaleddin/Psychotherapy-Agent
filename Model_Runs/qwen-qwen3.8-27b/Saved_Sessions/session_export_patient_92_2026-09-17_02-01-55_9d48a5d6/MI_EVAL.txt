# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently avoids judgment and respects Marek’s identity, preferences, and boundaries. Affirmations are supportive but somewhat generic rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurately tracks Marek’s perspective, including his concern about labels and value of weekend connection. Emotional depth is limited in exploring fear, loss, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, screening meaning, weekend drinking, and Monday functioning. Direction is clear but leans toward assessment and monitoring rather than a concrete change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acts as an equal partner and lets Marek’s preferences shape the session. The monitoring plan is genuinely co-created rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Marek’s freedom to choose is consistently protected without pressure or persuasion. The client is repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker about his own drinking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections are used, but they remain focused on facts and monitoring rather than intrinsic motivation. More evocation of values, confidence, or personal reasons for change would strengthen the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marek’s input and co-creates the monitoring plan with him. Partnership is strong but not complete because the psychiatrist still sets much of the structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session creates some space for change-related language, but Marek’s change talk remains limited. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen statements about importance, values, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without arguing or direct reinforcement, and the psychiatrist often redirects to self-checking. However, the work remains more neutral monitoring than active softening toward change.
