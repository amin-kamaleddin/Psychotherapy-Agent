# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally nonjudgmental and normalizes Rosa's struggle. However, acceptance is sometimes generic and becomes mildly corrective or prescriptive rather than fully accepting her experience as it is.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are basic attempts to understand Rosa's ambivalence, but the reflections remain superficial. Deeper identity, social, and fear-based concerns are not fully mirrored or explored before the therapist shifts toward reframing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation and gradually narrows toward a concrete, manageable target behavior. Some drift into multiple practical topics dilutes the MI-style direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The therapist asks for preferences and incorporates some client choices, but most of the plan is therapist-generated. Rosa's ideas are accommodated within the therapist's framework rather than shaping the session as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are meaningful autonomy-supportive moves, such as not requiring commitment and respecting a smaller goal. However, repeated suggestions for scripts, checklists, and interpretive framing can pressure Rosa to accept the therapist's meaning-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The therapist asks some initial open questions but quickly shifts to advice, education, and problem-solving. Rosa's own reasons, values, and confidence for change are not consistently elicited or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some collaborative language but often assumes the expert role by supplying many options and conceptual statements. The interaction feels more like a structured plan than a partnership guided by Rosa's own perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to elicit change talk are limited and often steer Rosa toward therapist-generated options. Brief affirmations of her statements do not deepen or expand her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The therapist does not reinforce smoking, but often responds to sustain talk with facts and practical tips instead of softening it. This risks debate and does not consistently help Rosa move from ambivalence toward change.
