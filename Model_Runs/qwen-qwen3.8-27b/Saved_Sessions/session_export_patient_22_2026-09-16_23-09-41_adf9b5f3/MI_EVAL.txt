# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful, and warm stance while validating Camila’s concerns without imposing labels or pressure. The therapist avoids moralizing and keeps the space safe even when the client expresses fear or skepticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates strong reflective listening and accurately captures Camila’s ambivalence and social stress. The empathy could be deeper by more explicitly reflecting underlying fear, shame, or anxiety about not drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session moves logically from general concern to weekday drinking and then to a concrete behavioral experiment. The target behavior could be made clearer and more measurable by explicitly negotiating the focus and what success would look like this week.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as an equal partner, using Camila’s own ideas to shape the session and co-constructing a concrete plan. The final intention is clearly client-driven, demonstrating strong collaborative ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently signals that Camila is in charge of her choices, framing suggestions as options rather than directives. Ambivalence is respected, and the client is invited to decide at her own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist frequently helps Camila identify her own reasons, values, and possible next steps rather than supplying reasons for change. Some therapist statements are mildly interpretive or suggestive, which slightly reduces the openness of evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Camila as the expert on her own life and change process. The interaction reflects shared agenda-setting, collaborative language, and respect for the client’s self-determination.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist effectively evokes client-generated change talk about clarity, self-identity, and willingness to try a small experiment. The session could be strengthened by more consistently amplifying the strongest change talk and connecting it to confidence or next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and respectfully acknowledges Camila’s fear of social exclusion. The therapist could more consistently redirect sustain talk toward values, costs, or self-efficacy to deepen the change balance.
