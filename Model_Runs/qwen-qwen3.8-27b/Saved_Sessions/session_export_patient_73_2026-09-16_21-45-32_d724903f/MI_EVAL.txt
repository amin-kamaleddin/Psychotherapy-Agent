# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Linda’s agency and effort. Acceptance could be strengthened by more explicitly normalizing past failures and expressing unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Empathy is strong, especially in reflecting ambivalence around hope, familiar social meaning, support, and fear of defeat. It could go deeper into shame, identity loss, grief, and the emotional weight of repeated quit attempts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on tobacco cessation and moves toward a manageable first-hour craving plan. Direction would improve by consolidating the conversation into a more concrete, observable next step or commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor collaboratively builds on Linda’s own ideas, such as waiting an hour and using a private five-minute call. Collaboration could be more co-created by sharing pacing, agenda, and question structure more explicitly with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is central and highly effective, with consistent emphasis on choice, self-direction, and Linda remaining in charge. The counselor avoids pressure or control while framing support as optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes Linda’s own reasons, values, and problem-solving ideas rather than supplying reasons for change. Evocation could be stronger by more directly developing confidence, ability, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a non-expert, collaborative stance and lets Linda’s ideas shape the plan. Partnership could be strengthened with more explicit power sharing and less counselor-led, multi-part questioning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda offers useful change talk, such as “keep the company but change the habit” and “I’m scared of my lungs.” The counselor could more consistently reflect, amplify, and deepen these statements into stronger motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with sustain talk and reframes support as a private tool that preserves autonomy. It could soften sustain talk further by exploring valued relationships and how they might be preserved in a smoke-free ritual.
