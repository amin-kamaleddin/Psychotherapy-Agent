# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while using normalizing language. However, acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and could be more individualized in affirming Nadine’s identity and competence as a new parent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening to identify exhaustion, fear, and self-doubt. The main limitation is that reflections are often repetitive and miss deeper emotional complexity around guilt, comparisons, and fear of harming the baby.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation generally stays focused on practical safety and the immediate goal of helping Nadine rest tonight. A stronger score would require tighter MI direction by more explicitly linking her values to a specific change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, but the counselor supplies most of the practical options and problem-solving content. Greater collaboration would involve more joint planning and actively drawing out Nadine’s own ideas and past strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently asks for Nadine’s preferences and avoids coercion or pressure. Autonomy support is limited somewhat by the counselor-generated advice and scripts, which reduce the emphasis on Nadine as the ultimate decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is weak because the counselor quickly shifts to psychoeducation and practical solutions rather than eliciting Nadine’s internal motivations, values, or confidence. The client’s own change language and self-efficacy are not strongly drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are partnership moments, such as inviting Nadine to choose among options and speak in her own words. However, the interaction remains largely expert-driven, which creates an uneven power dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is only weakly cultivated through decision-making prompts, and there is little explicit exploration of desire, ability, need, or commitment. The counselor’s frequent external suggestions limit Nadine’s opportunity to express her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts Nadine’s fatigue and fear rather than arguing against them. However, there is limited use of MI-consistent techniques such as double-sided reflections or reframing to help her resolve ambivalence.
