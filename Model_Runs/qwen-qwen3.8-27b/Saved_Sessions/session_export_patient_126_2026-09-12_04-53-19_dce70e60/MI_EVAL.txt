# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids shaming or lecturing. Acceptance is solid, though it remains somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate but mostly paraphrase the client’s stated position. Deeper empathy would include more complex reflections of frustration, defensiveness, pride, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The conversation stays on target by focusing on smoking history and alcohol use. However, direction is more toward clarification and safety advice than toward a clear, change-focused agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, checks in before continuing, and respects the client’s preference for brevity. Collaboration is present but more procedural than fully co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes the client’s choice and respects the boundary against a lecture. Autonomy support is strong, though it could more actively affirm the client’s capacity to decide what is best for his health.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are appropriate attempts to elicit the client’s perspective on his drinking and responsibilities. The main limitation is that these responses are not developed into stronger change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and avoids a purely expert-driven approach. The session remains more clinician-managed than a fully shared, client-led exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only weak efforts to elicit desire, ability, reasons, or need for change. Statements reflecting responsibility and control are not reflected, amplified, or built upon.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce the client’s minimization, but also does not effectively soften it. More double-sided reflections would help create space for concern without confrontation.
