# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist is respectful and nonjudgmental, avoiding shaming or pressure around smoking. However, the warmth is somewhat procedural, and approving language can slightly emphasize compliance over unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist accurately reflects Marco’s perspective, including the role of routine, low priority, and stress triggers. The empathy is strong cognitively but does not fully capture the deeper emotional texture of his experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a concrete, manageable smoking-reduction experiment. Some procedural detail about gum use can make the conversation feel more practical than motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-created, with Marco choosing the gum, target cigarette, timing, cues, and follow-up. The therapist still provides a lot of practical direction, which slightly limits full client-led collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist strongly emphasizes Marco’s choice, avoids pressure, and respects his boundaries and limits. Statements such as “a slip is just information, not failure” support ownership very well.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist uses some evocative questions, including an importance scale and doable-step questions. However, Marco’s own reasons, values, and confidence are not deeply explored or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist invites input, asks permission, and lets Marco’s choices shape the plan. The amount of practical information can make the interaction feel more like brief advice than a fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist elicits some change talk around a one-week gum experiment. These statements are not consistently reflected, expanded, or developed into stronger motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing with Marco’s sustain talk and keeps the tone low-pressure. However, there is less skillful use of reflections or pivot strategies to turn sustain talk into change talk.
