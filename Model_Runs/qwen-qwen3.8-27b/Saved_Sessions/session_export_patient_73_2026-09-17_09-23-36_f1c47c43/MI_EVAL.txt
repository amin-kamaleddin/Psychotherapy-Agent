# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently shows warm, respectful, nonjudgmental regard for the client's values, ambivalence, and quit attempts. A minor growth area is more explicitly validating the emotional pain of repeated failed attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's health worries, social connection, fear of standing out, and ambivalence. The counselor could slightly deepen exploration of loneliness or rejection-related feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of tobacco use within the client's social routine. It effectively moves from broad ambivalence to a concrete, manageable next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor co-creates the plan with the client rather than directing advice, using the client's ideas about her friend group and social context. The final plan is client-generated and realistic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids pressure, directives, and persuasion while respecting the client's six-month timeline and preference for low-commitment change. More explicit language affirming the client's right to choose would strengthen this area.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor uses strong evocative questions to elicit the client's own values, plans, coping strategies, and motivation. A slight growth area is drawing out more explicit confidence or identity-level change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor clearly positions the client as the expert on her own life and social world. Expertise and problem-solving are shared, with the client's perspective shaping the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicits and affirms several change statements, such as protecting her lungs and feeling less weak. It could be strengthened by more systematically developing desire, ability, need, confidence, and identity-level change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor handles sustain talk without arguing or pushing against the client's social concerns. More explicit double-sided reflections could help resolve ambivalence while preserving the client's experience.
