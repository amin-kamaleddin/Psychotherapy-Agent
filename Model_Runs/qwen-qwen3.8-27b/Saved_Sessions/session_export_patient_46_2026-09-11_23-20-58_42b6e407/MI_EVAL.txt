# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance while normalizing Ethan’s low readiness. The warmth is generally strong but somewhat formulaic, with limited stronger affirmation of Ethan’s strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is adequate surface-level reflective listening and validation of Ethan’s perspective. However, deeper emotional and social themes such as belonging, identity, embarrassment, and autonomy are not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, consequences, and practical harm-reduction without significant drift. The direction is behaviorally clear but less oriented toward moving Ethan from ambivalence to readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, offers choices, and adjusts the plan when Ethan resists certain suggestions. The session remains somewhat counselor-driven, with Ethan mostly responding to the psychiatrist’s structure rather than co-creating it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly emphasizes choice, lack of pressure, and Ethan’s right to decide, including respecting refusals of strictness and a written template. The substantial advice-giving slightly reduces the sense of fully client-owned planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions about readiness, preferences, and ease of trying a change. These attempts remain brief and are often followed by direct advice rather than deeper exploration of Ethan’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows partial collaboration by offering options and backing off when Ethan declines certain suggestions. The interaction remains uneven because the psychiatrist frequently drives the content with detailed steps, scripts, and a structured plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few readiness and harm-reduction questions but does not consistently amplify Ethan’s own change language. Ethan’s statements about hating hangovers and wanting to feel better are acknowledged but not skillfully developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue against Ethan’s sustain talk and appropriately respects his autonomy. However, there is limited use of reflective or double-sided strategies to gently challenge the status quo or explore discrepancy.
