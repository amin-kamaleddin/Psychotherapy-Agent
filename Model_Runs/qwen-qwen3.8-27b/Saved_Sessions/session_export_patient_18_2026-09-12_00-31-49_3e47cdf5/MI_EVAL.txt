# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and nonjudgmental tone, but the session feels more administrative than warmly affirming. There is basic validation, but limited individualized positive regard or deep acceptance of the client’s experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges frustration and worry with brief reflections, but does not consistently explore deeper feelings such as embarrassment, fear of being labeled, or the meaning of being singled out.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear focus on resolving the screening flag and practical next steps, but it is more assessment-driven than MI-focused. The alcohol-related target behavior is only briefly addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is asked for input and chooses some options, but the counselor frequently leads with explanations, scripts, and reassurance. The session is partially collaborative rather than fully co-created with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers choices, asks permission, and avoids pressuring the client to role-play or send another email. This is a clear strength, though the client’s ownership could be emphasized even more explicitly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session relies heavily on assessment, psychoeducation, and problem-solving rather than drawing out the client’s own motivations, values, and concerns. There is limited use of open MI questions or reflections to evoke change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers multiple options and sometimes waits for the client to choose, showing some partnership. However, the counselor remains more expert-driven, positioning themselves as the problem-solver rather than a collaborator.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses some change talk, such as wanting to understand the flag and put the issue behind her, but the psychiatrist does not consistently notice, reflect, or amplify it. Evocation of the client’s own reasons for change is rare.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s reluctance to make a big deal of it as reasonable, but does not actively soften it with MI techniques. Sustain talk is more reassured or redirected than explored with double-sided reflections or curiosity.
