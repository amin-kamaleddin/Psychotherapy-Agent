# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Milan’s perspective without moralizing or arguing. Warmth remains somewhat procedural and does not fully affirm Milan as a person beyond the drinking issue.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist reflects the social and identity pressures behind Milan’s defensiveness using his own language. Empathy is strong but stays at a surface level and misses deeper emotions such as shame, exclusion, or faith/community conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on alcohol use at parties and social gatherings. Direction is present but lacks stronger momentum toward a concrete target, summary, or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist works from Milan’s language and asks him to identify what would fit, creating a reasonably collaborative tone. The interaction remains somewhat therapist-led, with less explicit co-creation of the agenda or plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist consistently emphasizes Milan’s choice, control, and right to decide what feels authentic. Some questions subtly steer toward not drinking or gaining more control, which slightly limits full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist asks questions about values, future, and authenticity to elicit Milan’s own reasons for change. Evocation remains superficial and does not fully expand Milan’s change talk around his specific values or strengths.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist positions Milan as the decision-maker and uses his perspective to guide the conversation. Partnership is not fully explicit, as the therapist still drives the flow without clearly co-creating direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist attempts to evoke change talk through questions about choice, authenticity, and control. Milan’s change talk remains minimal, and the therapist does not consistently develop it using DARN-C strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist acknowledges Milan’s sustain talk without arguing or minimizing his perspective. Some sustain talk is accepted without being strategically softened to increase ambivalence toward the status quo.
