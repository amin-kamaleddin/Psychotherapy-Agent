# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids pressuring the parent about vaccination. Positive regard could be warmer by more explicitly affirming her values as a parent and her effort to protect her child.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses clear, accurate reflections of the parent’s concerns about realistic information and reassurance for her son. The empathy could deepen by reflecting more of the emotional weight, such as fear, guilt, or anxiety about online information.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the specific concern about the varicella/chickenpox vaccine for her 3-year-old. It moves logically from clarifying concerns to risk information and practical preparation for the pediatrician visit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and adapts to the parent’s preference for a plain summary and concise questions. Collaboration could be stronger by more visibly co-creating the plan rather than supplying much of the content and language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering options and respecting the parent’s wish to hear information directly from the pediatrician. The approach becomes somewhat prescriptive when suggesting exact scripts and follow-up lines.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some practical engagement and a sense of feeling less stuck. Evocation could be stronger by drawing out the parent’s own values, priorities, confidence, and reasons related to the vaccination decision.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The parent’s input influences the session, but the psychiatrist remains the primary source of information, structure, and language. A stronger partnership would more explicitly treat the parent as the expert on her child and family values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The parent offers useful change talk, such as wanting to hear the doctor directly and feeling less stuck. The psychiatrist could better reflect, amplify, and build on this language to strengthen motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue or reinforce the parent’s hesitation, and acknowledges her ambivalence. The response to sustain talk is more didactic than evocative and could use more double-sided reflections and value-based exploration.
