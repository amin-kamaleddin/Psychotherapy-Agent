# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes imperfection without criticism. This reflects strong respect, acceptance, and unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong reflective listening and captures both practical barriers and emotional meaning. Some reflections remain more content-focused than emotionally deep, especially early in the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the target behavior and moves coherently from values to a concrete, manageable plan. Each question advances the conversation toward realistic implementation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input and builds the plan from the client’s own ideas rather than prescribing solutions. More explicit co-creation and shared agenda-setting would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes choice, flexibility, and the absence of pressure. The routine is framed as something the client owns, not a rule, test, or duty.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully draws out the client’s own values, standards, and motivation for change. Some questions are slightly leading, and more open questions could deepen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that the client is the expert on her own life and follows client-generated ideas. The collaboration is strong, but the psychiatrist still provides the main structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes the client’s desire, reasons, and plans for change. The session could go further by developing confidence language, identity, and importance more explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the client’s concerns, but gently redirects them toward flexibility and self-compassion. Potential failure is reframed as a practice with a built-in exit rather than a test.
