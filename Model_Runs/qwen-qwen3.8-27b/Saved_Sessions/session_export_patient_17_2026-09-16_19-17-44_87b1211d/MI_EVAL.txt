# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently used warm, nonjudgmental language that affirmed Anisa’s effort and ambivalence. Acceptance could be deeper around her shame, “bad mother” feelings, and fear of family judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured her practical struggles, health worries, and conflicting goals. Empathy was strong but could reflect more of the underlying fear of failure and emotional pain of being judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stayed tightly focused on smoking cessation and concrete, actionable steps. Direction was excellent in moving toward a workable plan without drifting to unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Anisa’s input, built on her ideas, and offered options rather than directives. Collaboration could be strengthened with more explicit co-creation and shared decision-making language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Anisa was consistently positioned as the expert in her own life and the final decision-maker. The psychiatrist avoided pressure, imposed plans, or insistence on a specific strategy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited Anisa’s own values, goals, and barriers related to quitting. Evocation could be enhanced by more fully reflecting back her change talk and deepening its meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction conveyed a respectful, collaborative stance and treated Anisa’s experience as central. Partnership could be further strengthened with more explicit power sharing and agenda-setting invitations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently invited and reinforced Anisa’s reasons for change, especially around health and protecting her daughter. Change talk could be more explicitly amplified and connected to identity and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well through reflective, non-confrontational responses that preserved momentum. Softening could be stronger by more consistently reframing doubt and fear of failure into experimentation.
