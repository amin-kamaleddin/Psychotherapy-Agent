# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates warmth and non-judgmental acceptance, explicitly granting Sofia permission to be authentic and correcting after being pushed back on. However, acceptance remains somewhat one-directional and validating rather than deeply relational, without fully normalizing Sofia's defensive responses as understandable survival mechanisms.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is the strongest dimension, with highly accurate reflections that capture emotional subtext beyond content, such as reframing school avoidance as a survival response and naming the futility Sofia feels. The psychiatrist never misattunes or flattens Sofia's experience, consistently reaching a 5-level of attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on peer conflicts and school avoidance but lacks a clear progressive arc or agenda-setting toward a specific change target. The conversation ends in the same diffuse 'I don't know what I'm supposed to do' space it began in, without narrowing focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative tone through inclusive language and by positioning Sofia as the expert on her own experience, without lecturing or imposing frameworks. However, collaboration is primarily in stance rather than structure, as the psychiatrist still directs the flow and topic selection without visible co-creation of session direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently removes pressure and judgment, never using coercive language and validating Sofia's lack of a plan without pressuring her to have one. Autonomy support could be elevated to a 5 by more explicitly affirming Sofia's capacity for self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One well-timed evocative question about Maya draws out genuinely important change talk about Sofia's values, but it is underexploited with no follow-up to deepen the thread. The session lacks scaling questions, values-based exploration, or strategic use of discrepancies that would draw out deeper motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inviting, non-expert language and meets Sofia where she is rather than pushing an agenda. However, the session is somewhat therapist-led in pacing and topic selection, and could be strengthened by explicitly checking in with Sofia on direction and focus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia offers several instances of change talk, including coping strategies, expressed hope for reconnection, and values-based statements about relationships, all of which go largely unexploited. The session is predominantly a validation/empathy session rather than a change-evoking one, with no use of scaling, strategic amplification, or exploration of confidence in specific strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist correctly avoids arguing against or dismissing Sofia's sustain talk, but also does not actively soften it through double-sided reflections or strategic pivots. Sustain talk is met with neutral acceptance that is adequate but not skilled, missing opportunities to use statements like 'it's a loop' as a bridge toward change.
