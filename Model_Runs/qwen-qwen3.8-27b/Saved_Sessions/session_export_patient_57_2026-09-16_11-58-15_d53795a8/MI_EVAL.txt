# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, non-judgmental tone with basic validating language. However, repeated “quick reminders” about legal and team consequences gave a mildly lecture-like impression.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some accurate reflections of Jamal’s tension between fitting in and protecting his goals. Deeper emotions such as shame, fear of exclusion, and being misunderstood were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target drinking issue and practical next steps. The counselor may have leaned too heavily into prescriptive planning, reducing client-led momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited choices and incorporated some of Jamal’s ideas, such as the Red Bull and “I’m full.” However, the counselor frequently supplied options and scripts, making the interaction less collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive language was used, such as asking which option felt doable. Frequent consequence-focused reminders and suggested plans made the counselor sound more directive than autonomy-centered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked questions intended to elicit Jamal’s own motivations and solutions. However, the counselor often answered by supplying reasons for change and specific steps, limiting deeper evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor began collaboratively but quickly shifted to a counselor-led style with repeated suggestions. Jamal’s expertise about the locker-room social rules was not fully ceded.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal expressed some change talk about keeping his spot, avoiding trouble, and limiting drinks. The counselor did not consistently amplify or build on Jamal’s own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal expressed strong sustain talk about peers, “knowing his limits,” and taking one shot to stop pressure. The counselor sometimes appeared to normalize risky reasoning and relied more on consequence-based persuasion than reflective softening.
