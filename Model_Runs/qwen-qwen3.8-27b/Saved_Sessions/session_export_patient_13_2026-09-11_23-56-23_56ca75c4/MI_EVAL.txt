# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, with supportive statements such as being present and listening. However, repeated directives and medical urgency reduce the fully accepting tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic responses are present but brief and often immediately paired with medical advice or action planning. Deeper reflective empathy is limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on symptom evaluation, safety, and next steps. Direction is strong but becomes somewhat directive in moments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration is present but uneven, with the psychiatrist often offering to draft lists, messages, and scripts. More patient-generated planning would strengthen this criterion.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through options and invitations, such as taking the test first and deciding later about messaging a clinician. More consistent emphasis on the patient's right to choose would improve autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist relies heavily on direct medical guidance and structured problem-solving. More patient-focused questions and reflections would better evoke the patient's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaboration and shared decision-making, including pacing and offering alternatives. The approach still feels more provider-led than truly collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked, especially when the patient agrees to take the test and try a grounding breath. The psychiatrist could more consistently invite the patient's own values, reasons, and strengths.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not directly reinforced, but repeated encouragement to test and contact a clinician may inadvertently validate resistance. More MI-consistent reflection and support for ambivalence would help soften sustain talk.
