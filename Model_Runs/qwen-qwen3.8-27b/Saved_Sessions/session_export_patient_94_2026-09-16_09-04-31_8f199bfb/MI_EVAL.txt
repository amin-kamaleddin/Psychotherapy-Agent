# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Maria’s time pressure and choices. Acceptance could be stronger by more deeply validating the emotional and bodily meaning of her steroid concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures surface-level practical concerns such as busyness and worry about long-term effects. It does not consistently address the deeper meaning of her fear of bodily change, control, or medical distrust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management and the practical step of carrying the rescue inhaler. Pacing is slightly weakened by repeated summaries, logistics, and redundant safety details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts to Maria’s time constraints and allows her preferences to shape the plan. Collaboration could be stronger by more explicitly framing the decision as shared problem-solving around her values and concerns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Maria’s choice, avoids pressuring her about daily medication or follow-up, and asks permission before next steps. Repeated offers of a trial, text, or check-in could still feel like gentle pressure in a time-pressed context.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions elicit a modest, doable behavioral intention. The session quickly shifts to medical information and safety guidance, limiting deeper exploration of Maria’s motivations, values, and ambivalence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, asks permission, and supports Maria’s decision-making authority. The interaction is collaborative but still somewhat led by medical information and safety parameters rather than fully co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reinforces a small, realistic change step by keeping the rescue inhaler in Maria’s purse. Change talk is present but narrow, with limited exploration of broader values, hopes, or readiness for better asthma control.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and accepts Maria’s refusal of daily medication. Handling of sustain talk would be stronger with more nuanced MI strategies, such as double-sided reflection or shared problem-solving around her safety concerns.
