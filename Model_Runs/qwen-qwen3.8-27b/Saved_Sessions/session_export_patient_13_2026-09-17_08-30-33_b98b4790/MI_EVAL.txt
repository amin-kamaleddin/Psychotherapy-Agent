# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing fear, avoidance, and body-related concern. Acceptance could deepen with more explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong perspective-taking and accurate reflections of practical and emotional concerns. Empathy could deepen further by explicitly reflecting fear, pain, or guilt behind the hesitation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear and logical focus on the target behavior of taking a next step toward medical evaluation. The session consistently returns to the concrete barrier and next step without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses collaborative language and invites the patient to help shape the plan and next step. More open-ended prompts with fewer clinician-provided options would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly leaves decisions and choices with the patient without pressure or imposed plans. Autonomy support strongly reinforces patient choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicits patient-generated strategies and confidence in her coping plan. Evocation could be stronger by drawing out deeper personal values and motivations for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treats the patient as the expert and invites her to contribute meaningfully to the session. Partnership could improve by reducing multiple-choice prompts and allowing more open co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some ability and confidence talk is elicited and affirmed, but change talk is not fully developed or amplified. The counselor could use scaling questions and summary to strengthen reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing and acknowledges the appeal of avoidance, but leaves sustain talk prominent without a strong pivot. A double-sided reflection redirecting toward change or confidence would be stronger.
