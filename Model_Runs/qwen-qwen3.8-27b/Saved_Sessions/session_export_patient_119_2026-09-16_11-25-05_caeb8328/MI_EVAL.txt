# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes Selene’s practical burden without criticizing missed doses. It could be stronger by explicitly validating ambivalence, guilt, and anxiety around the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately tracks Selene’s content, especially around busyness and needing a low-effort routine. Empathy remains more cognitive than emotionally deep, particularly around the meaning of staying healthy and defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on medication adherence and builds a concrete, practical plan. Direction is strong because the conversation consistently moves toward a specific, achievable change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Selene actively contributes ideas that shape the plan, including the coffee-cup cue, Sunday prep, 3-minute window, if-then rule, and calendar backup. Collaboration is somewhat limited by the counselor’s repetitive structured prompts and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, respects Selene’s preference to keep the plan solo, and avoids pressuring success or involving her husband. Repeated confidence-scaling prompts risk creating subtle pressure to increase her confidence score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session evokes practical client-generated strategies and problem-solving, but not deeper values, fears, or personal meaning. Evocation would be stronger if it drew out what health, consistency, or anxiety mean to Selene.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist invites Selene’s input and lets her choices meaningfully guide the plan. A fully expert level would require more explicit acknowledgment of Selene as the expert on her own life and more shared power over the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session produces some change talk, such as wanting to stay healthy, trying a plan, and not letting it slide. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen these statements into stronger intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Selene’s minimization, which helps reduce defensiveness. However, sustain talk is not actively softened or redirected using double-sided reflections or strategic MI techniques.
