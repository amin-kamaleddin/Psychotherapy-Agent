# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows a respectful, nonjudgmental, and strength-based stance by affirming Tariq's compliance without criticizing his frustration. Acceptance is solid but remains somewhat task-focused rather than deeply warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses accurate reflections of Tariq's practical concerns and urgency, but does not consistently attend to the deeper fear, loneliness, and anxiety he expresses. Empathy is adequate but often becomes generic or shifts quickly into problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on managing frustration around the phone suspension and maintaining a clean record before release. The direction is practical and appropriate, though it does not consistently move toward deeper motivational work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist presents options and incorporates Tariq's preferences, such as revising the language and deciding whether to go with him. Collaboration is strong, but could be more explicitly co-created by exploring Tariq's own ideas and values.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offers choices, respects Tariq's pushback, and frames actions as requests rather than demands. Autonomy support is strong, though the psychiatrist still organizes much of the plan and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some of Tariq's motivations around family connection, release, and doing the right thing. Evocation is present but remains superficial, with limited follow-up questions to deepen his values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Tariq to choose how to proceed and adapts when he rejects options, showing a collaborative stance. Partnership is strong but more task-oriented than fully MI-like, and at times led by the clinician's problem-solving agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tariq offers useful change talk, such as wanting to do the right thing and get out clean, and the clinician helps translate that into concrete action. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively avoids argument and redirects blame toward a manageable, rule-consistent request. Softening sustain talk is handled well, though it could be stronger with more explicit acknowledgment of ambivalence and double-sided reflections.
