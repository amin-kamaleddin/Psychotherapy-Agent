# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates consistent respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard without labeling the client by criminal history or probation status. Could be slightly more explicit in affirming the client’s worth during moments of self-criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate, meaning-focused reflections of the client’s ambivalence and concerns about fairness, work, and being judged. Could deepen further by naming underlying emotions such as shame, fear, grief, or anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on the target behavior and steadily narrows the conversation toward a concrete, workable first step. Each exchange remains oriented toward a practical, client-owned afternoon work plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engages as an equal partner rather than an authority directing the client toward a predetermined plan. The client’s proposed structure—an afternoon, one person, clear task, hard stop at four—becomes the central material of the intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports the client’s right to choose, set limits, and withdraw if the arrangement does not feel fair. Language consistently protects against coercion, probation pressure, and the message that the client needs to be “fixed.”

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits the client’s own thinking and practical commitments through questions about support, fairness, and boundary-setting. Could more directly explore deeper values, identity, and confidence in handling the first step.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the client as the expert on his own experience and repeatedly invites him to define what would feel fair, safe, and doable. The client’s contributions clearly shape the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses evocative questions and reinforcement to invite client-owned planning and boundary-setting. Could strengthen the criterion by more explicitly amplifying the client’s values, hopes, and confidence in change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats the client’s concerns as legitimate and reframes them to reduce defensiveness without reinforcing the status quo. The visible transcript contains limited explicit client sustain talk, so the skill is strong but not fully demonstrated.
