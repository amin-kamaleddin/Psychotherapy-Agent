# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Elias’s concerns without blaming him for relapse. Warmth is somewhat task-oriented and occasionally appraises recovery performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections on ambivalence, fear, and fear of performing, but often moves quickly to problem-solving. Deeper shame and burden concerns are underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on sobriety, reducing pill use, and preparing for a specific high-risk moment. Focus can become narrow and repetitive around the same plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias contributes key decisions, but the psychiatrist often provides predefined options and scripts. This makes the interaction feel partly therapist-led rather than client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Elias’s choices and avoids direct pressure, while honoring low-risk steps. Frequent suggestions of scripts and step-by-step plans can subtly direct the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are useful questions about what matters and what feels doable, but the psychiatrist often supplies rationale and plan content. More probing of Elias’s own reasons, ability, and confidence would strengthen evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The plan is shaped by Elias’s preferences, but the psychiatrist frequently assumes a directive, problem-solving role. The client is not consistently positioned as the expert on his own recovery.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-relevant language is evoked, but powerful client statements are not consistently reflected or amplified. The psychiatrist sometimes provides rationale instead of expanding Elias’s own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with doubts but often shifts to planning rather than exploring ambivalence. Important sustain talk, especially fear of burdening his brother, remains unresolved.
