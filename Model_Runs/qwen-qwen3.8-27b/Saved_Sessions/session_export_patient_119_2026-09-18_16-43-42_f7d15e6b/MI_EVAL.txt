# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and specific in validation throughout the session. Notably accepts the client's critique of the 'no judgment' framing without defensiveness, instead honoring her correction in real time.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, layered reflections that capture both cognitive and emotional dimensions, including subtle undercurrents like self-doubt and self-apologizing. Each reflection builds on the previous one and tracks the client's real-time shifts in perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear logical progression from rapport through ambivalence to identifying the systemic barrier. The session ends in a powerful insight but could have gently circled back to a concrete next step or commitment to add forward momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is genuinely shaped by the client's responses in unpredictable ways, with the psychiatrist building on what Selene offers rather than driving a predetermined agenda. The cup-on-the-counter solution and the final 'no quiet moment' insight both emerge entirely from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every intervention is a question or reflection that invites the client to generate her own answers; the psychiatrist never prescribes, recommends, or directs. Autonomy support is woven into every single turn of the conversation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every piece of change talk in the session is elicited through questions, never provided by the psychiatrist. The client's own words carry the motivation, which is textbook evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently defers expertise to the client, explicitly credits insights to Selene, and maintains a genuinely equal power dynamic. The psychiatrist functions as a guide, not a director, throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently elicits and reinforces desire, ability, and commitment language with strong affirmations. Could have more explicitly drawn out confidence ratings or asked the client to summarize her own reasons for change in her own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk effectively through double-sided reflections and normalization without arguing or amplifying. One moment (the 'problem is just me' statement) could have more explicitly reflected the embedded sustain talk before pivoting to the reframe.
