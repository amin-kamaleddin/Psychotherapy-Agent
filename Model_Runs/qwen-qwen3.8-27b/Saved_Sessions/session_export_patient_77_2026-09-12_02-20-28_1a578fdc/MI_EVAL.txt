# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while avoiding shame around weight, eating, or family dynamics. Acceptance is strong but slightly formulaic in places, such as “brave to bring it up,” without fully normalizing the plateau.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of exhaustion, ambivalence, and fear of being judged. The main limitation is that some underlying shame, fear of dismissal, and emotional meaning beneath the client’s words are not reflected deeply enough.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the presenting concerns of weight-loss frustration, self-pressure, plateau, and lack of support. Direction is generally strong, but the session lacks a clearer consolidating summary at the close.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices, co-constructs the plan, and treats the client as a partner in decision-making. Collaboration is strong but remains somewhat therapist-managed through structured questions, scripts, and a provided tracking plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering options, honoring the client’s pace, and explicitly avoiding pressure around food details. The limitation is that the therapist still provides a fairly structured plan and scripts, which can feel subtly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks helpful open questions and elicits some client-owned goals, but often interrupts evocation by supplying solutions, plans, and scripts. Deeper evocation would more fully explore values, identity, and reasons for change before moving into problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by inviting the client to choose focus areas, activities, timing, and communication approaches. Partnership is strong, though the therapist still retains a fair amount of agenda and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some client change talk but does not consistently amplify, deepen, or summarize it. The session moves toward planning before fully exploring the client’s own reasons, values, and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and gently reframes sustain talk around rest, guilt, and self-criticism without arguing against the client. However, worries about the scale and family pushback remain only partially explored, limiting full softening of sustain talk.
