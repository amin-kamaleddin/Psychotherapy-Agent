# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating, even when asking direct safety questions. The tone avoids shame, criticism, or minimization while framing care in a protective way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses strong reflections that capture ambivalence, exhaustion, and fear. Some reflections become repetitive around similar themes and could go deeper into meaningful details such as her mother, 'quiet,' or self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on suicidal ideation, immediate safety, and getting through the next hour. The direction is appropriate but repetitive, and could broaden more toward longer-term change, values, or coping.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids an authoritarian stance and meaningfully incorporates the patient’s preference for a text check-in. It is strong collaboration, though the repeated safety agenda keeps it somewhat clinician-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician avoids pressuring the patient to promise safety or take on burdensome responsibilities. Choices are offered in a low-pressure way, reinforcing the patient’s ownership and limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits client-generated change talk, including the patient’s willingness to accept a text check-in and not having a specific plan right now. Evocation could be stronger around deeper values, hope, and long-term reasons to live.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient is treated as the expert on her own experience, and the clinician avoids an expert-driven 'fix it' approach. The high-acuity focus on immediate safety somewhat limits the degree of shared power and equal agenda-setting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician actively invites the patient to articulate reasons for staying, small sources of safety, and possible next steps. It does not consistently expand that emerging change talk into deeper values, hope, or self-compassion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects sustain talk without arguing and gently reframes it in a more adaptive light. The sustain talk remains prominent, so redirection could be more strategic and consistently tied to broader change or hope.
