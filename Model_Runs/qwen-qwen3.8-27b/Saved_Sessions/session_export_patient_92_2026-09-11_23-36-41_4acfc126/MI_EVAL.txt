# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirming phrases like "nothing 'wrong' with you" and "it's totally okay if you don't want to be 'fixed.'" Warmth is somewhat limited and lacks explicit affirmation of the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers brief reflections ("that makes sense," "i hear you") but remains superficial, missing opportunities to deeply reflect the client's identity, pride, and ambivalence around drinking. A stronger double-sided reflection of his conflicting feelings would have been more effective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stays focused on alcohol use, related health risks, blood pressure, and driving concerns throughout the session. Occasionally becomes overly informational with long risk lists, but overall direction is clear and relevant.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Later interaction shows meaningful collaboration as the psychiatrist works with the client's selected low-effort options. However, much of the session is one-directional psychoeducation with definitions, risk lists, and official limits rather than truly co-constructed planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices, respects client rejections (counting, caps, nonalcoholic beer), and allows him to set his own smaller plan. The prescriptive driving-safety statement slightly diminishes the otherwise strong client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some open and preference-checking questions but relies heavily on telling rather than eliciting the client's own motivations. Would benefit from deeper value-based questions to help the client articulate personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites the client's agenda and offers multiple options, supporting a collaborative stance. Frequently shifts into expert-driven mode with definitions, risk lists, and guideline menus, making the partnership uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses some willingness (blood pressure monitoring, water/food), but the psychiatrist does not ask importance/confidence questions or strategically deepen the change talk. Reflections affirm lightly without amplifying the client's own reasons for adjustment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges some sustain talk ("you don't have to quit to be cautious") but often responds with additional risk information rather than softening or redirecting. Frequent re-listing of risks after client pushback keeps the conversation risk-centered without strategic reframing.
