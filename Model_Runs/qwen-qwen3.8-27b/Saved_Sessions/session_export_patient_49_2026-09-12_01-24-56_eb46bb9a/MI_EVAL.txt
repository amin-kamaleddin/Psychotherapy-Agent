# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids shaming, but the warmth is somewhat formulaic and shifts into didactic instruction. More individualized affirmation of Hana as a person beyond her smoking behavior would strengthen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes an adequate effort to understand Hana’s smoking contexts, readiness, and triggers, but reflections remain largely factual rather than emotionally attuned. A key miss is responding to Hana’s self-consciousness about smoking during a dental check-up with dental information.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains well focused on the target behavior of smoking cessation, especially the concrete goal of skipping the lunch cigarette. The psychiatrist moves logically from context to readiness to experiment to follow-up, which is a clear strength.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana contributes preferences and the plan is partly co-created, but the psychiatrist supplies many options, reminders, and next steps. More open-ended co-planning would make the interaction feel more like a partnership of equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices, accepting Hana’s readiness level, emphasizing low pressure, and framing slips as learning. However, repeated prescriptive reminders introduce a directive edge that could be softened.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps Hana identify a self-selected experiment and a personally meaningful measure, such as taste. However, much of the session relies on counselor-generated suggestions rather than drawing motivation from Hana’s values, hopes, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Hana’s input and allows her choices to shape parts of the plan, which supports collaboration. Frequent advisory and expert framing, however, limits the sense that expertise resides primarily with Hana.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicits some change talk through the concrete experiment and a personal win, such as noticing better taste. It rarely explores deeper reasons for change, values, or confidence statements, which would strengthen this area.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Hana’s sustain talk and normalizes ambivalence with phrases like “slips are learning, not failure.” More consistent use of double-sided reflections would help strategically redirect ambivalence toward change.
