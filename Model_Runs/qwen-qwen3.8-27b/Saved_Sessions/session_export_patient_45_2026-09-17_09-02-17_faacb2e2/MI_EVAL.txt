# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Erik’s concerns throughout. Acceptance could be stronger if it more explicitly acknowledged Erik’s defensiveness and sense of unfairness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is generally strong, accurately capturing Erik’s ambivalence and practical conflicts. Some reflections add interpretation and do not fully explore deeper fear or the emotional meaning of giving up beer.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays largely focused on relevant targets such as diet, alcohol timing, and a small one-week test. Focus is somewhat diluted by the recurring medication question and the session does not end with a clearly consolidated action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses joint language and allows Erik’s ideas to shape the conversation. Collaboration could be more explicit in shared agenda-setting and consistent power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is consistent and strong, with choices framed as Erik’s own and no pressure or guilt used. The psychiatrist respects Erik’s boundary that waiting for beer is acceptable while giving it up is not.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out Erik’s own reasons for change rather than supplying medical arguments. Evocation could be deeper by connecting the plan to Erik’s values, identity, or long-term goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Erik as the expert on his own life and avoids a lecturing stance. Partnership is present but not fully explicit in negotiating the agenda or emphasizing that Erik holds the primary wisdom about change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik offers useful change talk, such as waiting for beer after eating and trying the plan for a week. The psychiatrist could do more to amplify, reflect back, consolidate, or scale this emerging change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk and reflects Erik’s medication concern rather than dismissing it. More consistent use of double-sided reflections or strategic reframing would better soften this theme.
