# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Derrick's ambivalence and practical concerns. Acceptance remains functional rather than deeply warm, with performance-focused affirmations and limited explicit normalization of shame or social pain.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Derrick's conflict, including license/job worries, social easing, and fear of blacking out. Empathy is useful but practical and surface-level, without deeper exploration of shame, fear, or relationship pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on avoiding driving after drinking and reducing the risk of another DUI. Each question and reflection moves strategically toward a concrete, behavior-specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by offering options and allowing Derrick's choices to shape the plan. Collaboration is strong but somewhat consultant-like because structured solution menus are frequently provided.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice-focused language avoids directives and respects Derrick's preference for a low-drama approach. Autonomy support would be stronger with explicit affirmation of Derrick's right to choose and less emphasis on external legal/job pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently evokes Derrick's own reasons, solutions, and confidence through open questions and scaling. Evocation is practical rather than deeply motivational, not fully connecting Derrick's values, identity, or past successes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Derrick's preferences genuinely shape the session, showing a strong collaborative stance. Partnership is not maximal because the psychiatrist still provides many structured suggestions before eliciting more of Derrick's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions elicit frequent, concrete change talk about rideshare, cash, written reminders, and a short refusal line. Change talk is skillfully reinforced but remains focused on practical planning rather than deeper commitment or identity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Derrick's denial and externally imposed framing, redirecting the conversation toward practical problem-solving. Softening would be stronger with consistent double-sided reflections that validate sustain talk while gently pivoting toward change.
