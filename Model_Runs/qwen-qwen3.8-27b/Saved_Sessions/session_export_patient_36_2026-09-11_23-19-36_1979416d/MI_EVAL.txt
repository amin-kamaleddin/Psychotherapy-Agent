# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a polite, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone throughout. However, repeated insistence on the script near the end felt slightly rigid and evaluative, reducing the depth of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective statements were present but often formulaic and surface-level. The psychiatrist missed clear cues that the client was ready to end the session, focusing more on task completion than the client’s emotional state.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stayed focused on the target behavior of managing automatic “yes” responses. Direction was clear, though the guidance was more directive than collaborative.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapted to the client’s preference for simplicity, but still controlled pacing and provided a specific script rather than co-creating the plan. A more collaborative approach would have drawn on the client’s own choices for next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session began with a highly directive list and continued with repeated instructions despite the client indicating readiness to proceed. This undermined the client’s ownership and autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provided specific strategies rather than eliciting the client’s own ideas, values, or past successes. There were missed opportunities to ask questions that would draw out the client’s internal resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some collaboration when the psychiatrist simplified the approach, but the overall dynamic remained therapist-led. The client’s expertise in her own values and preferences was not fully leveraged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered strong change talk about feeling solid and ready to try the strategy, but the psychiatrist rarely deepened it with questions. There were missed opportunities to expand and strengthen the client’s motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist initially handled the client’s resistance to a lengthy plan by pivoting to a simpler approach. However, continued repetition of the script later in the session ignored the client’s preference for brevity and readiness to move on.
