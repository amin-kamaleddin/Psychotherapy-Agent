# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and does not become irritated by Amina's minimal responses. The positive regard is strong but somewhat procedural, and could more explicitly affirm Amina's worth or capacity beyond her current shutdown.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Amina's emotional burden with appropriate statements such as "That's a lot to hold" and "survival mode… can be exhausting." However, the reflections are somewhat general and do not consistently track the subtle shifts from defensiveness to irritation to resignation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays connected to the presenting concern of tension and conflict at home, but it does not maintain a clear, specific target behavior or steady progression toward change. A narrower, more contained focus would likely have strengthened the sense of direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves in a collaborative, non-authoritative way and responds well to Amina's stated preferences, such as quiet and sitting. The interaction is responsive, but it is still largely shaped by the psychiatrist's sequence of questions rather than fully co-constructed by the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly affirms Amina's freedom to choose and respects her decision to remain quiet. A slightly more restrained follow-up after repeated minimal responses would further strengthen the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts evocation through open, low-pressure questions, but these remain superficial and do not successfully draw out meaningful change talk. Shorter, more value- or safety-linked questions might have been more effective for a defensive, minimally communicative client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and recognizes Amina as the expert in her own life, using language such as "I want to follow your lead." However, the session is still largely structured by the psychiatrist's questions and reflections rather than being strongly redirected by the client's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks questions that could invite agency and choice, but the session does not produce meaningful change talk. The trainee could have used shorter, lower-pressure evocation questions and affirmatively reflected any slight openings to build motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging Amina's statements, avoiding argument, and offering an easy way out. The session does not, however, demonstrate repeated active redirection away from sustain talk toward change.
