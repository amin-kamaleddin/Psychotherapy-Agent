# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful, calm, and nonjudgmental, with multiple validating statements that supported the client. Acceptance was slightly formulaic and could be warmer or more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed consistent reflective listening and emotional attunement to shame, helplessness, and isolation. Deeper or more nuanced responses, especially to the final vulnerability, would improve empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on distress and a small coping step, but the target behavior of reducing drug use was not explicitly clarified or maintained. More purposeful connection to the client’s goal would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally collaborated by using joint language, inviting input, and allowing the client’s preference to guide the next step. However, the structured menu of options remained fairly therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was strong and consistent, with frequent invitations, permission-seeking, and nonpressuring options. The client maintained control over pace and content, including when inaction was acceptable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invited the client’s perspective but did not consistently elicit deeper values, hopes, confidence, or reasons for change. Evocative questions could better draw out the client’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance by asking for input and offering choices rather than lecturing. The session was supportive but still somewhat structured and paced by the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was evoked and appropriately affirmed, especially the client’s small step of completing the two-minute task. The clinician could have more consistently deepened the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally avoided, but the psychiatrist did not consistently soften or redirect it toward agency. More double-sided reflections balancing stuckness with the client’s small action would help.
