# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly validated Isabella’s choices and autonomy. The warmth was sometimes procedural, missing deeper affirmation of her identity, culture, and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used accurate reflections of Isabella’s perspective, ambivalence, and frustration. Empathy remained mostly cognitive and could have explored deeper emotions such as fear, guilt, and concern about being a good parent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol use during pregnancy and moved toward a concrete, relevant action step. Pacing could be improved with a clearer MI-style agenda and more deliberate movement from ambivalence to change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was generally collaborative, with options, invitations, and adaptation to Isabella’s practical needs. The psychiatrist still supplied much of the content and wording, making the interaction somewhat advisor-like.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was the strongest skill, with clear emphasis on Isabella’s right to choose and no pressure to commit to abstinence. The clinician respected her decision to pause drinking and supported it as her own step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial open questions helped Isabella express concerns and values, but the session relied too heavily on information and practical scripts. More evocation would have drawn out her own reasons and confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Isabella’s input influenced the session, and the psychiatrist offered choices and invited her preferences. The role as information provider and script generator limited the expression of Isabella as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was acknowledged but not consistently developed or connected to Isabella’s values and confidence. More value-based questions could have amplified her motivation to pause drinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without judgment and occasionally redirected toward practical steps. The responses did not consistently use reflections or value-based pivots to move sustain talk toward change.
