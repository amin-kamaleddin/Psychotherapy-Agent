# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remained respectful, nonjudgmental, and accepting, especially when the client was dismissive. The tone was supportive but somewhat clinical and formulaic rather than deeply warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client's perspective and emotional stance. The empathy was solid but stayed surface-level and did not fully explore underlying stress or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior and its relevance to the client's priorities. The psychiatrist guided the conversation logically toward a concrete action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited the client's input and framed the work as collaborative. However, the interaction was more guided selection from pre-formulated options than true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and choices supported the client's decision-making. The approach was still directive, limiting opportunities for the client to generate her own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relied heavily on providing information and advice rather than eliciting the client's intrinsic motivations. There was limited exploration of the client's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and offered options, maintaining a collaborative surface. The session was nevertheless psychiatrist-driven and structured around a predefined agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited, particularly around protecting school and band performance. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or deepen that change talk to strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without argumentation, which was appropriate. The psychiatrist rarely used advanced techniques to resolve ambivalence, leaving sustain talk largely unresolved.
