# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains nonjudgmental and validating, avoiding labels such as noncompliance or resistance. Acceptance is strong but somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are reasonable reflections of Kieran's frustration and perspective-taking, but the empathy stays surface-level. The clinician does not fully track or deepen the emotional meaning beneath his disengagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the practical compliance issue with mandated check-ins. Direction is appropriate but narrow and logistical rather than connected to broader values or engagement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative language and choices are offered, but the interaction is largely therapist-led. Kieran's own ideas do not substantially shape the session beyond selecting a prewritten option.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options, respecting Kieran's pace, and not forcing immediate elaboration or action. Autonomy support is strong, though ownership could be made more explicit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks some evocative questions about what Kieran wants and what small step might help. Evocation remains superficial as the session quickly shifts to problem-solving and a concrete text message.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and uses collaborative wording, but the power balance is uneven. Kieran's expertise and preferences are not deeply engaged, and the clinician structures much of the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kieran produces limited change talk, and the clinician reinforces the chosen practical step. Change talk is not consistently evoked or developed through deeper questions about motivation or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids challenging or debating Kieran's sustain talk and maintains a nonjudgmental stance. However, ambivalence is not strategically softened or redirected using double-sided reflections or client-based change talk.
