# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Maria’s pain without moralizing. Acceptance remains slightly safety-task oriented and does not fully validate Maria’s final request not to perform recovery.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture Maria’s ambivalence, exhaustion, and small willingness to stay. Some reflections are repetitive and miss deeper exploration of her fear that quiet may turn back into screaming and her desire not to analyze.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on immediate risk and moves from one minute to breathing, the next hour, and making it to this evening. The psychiatrist does not fully consolidate this emerging client-led direction into a coherent plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses “we” language and offers joint, small options. Collaboration is limited by structured safety-focused questions and does not fully honor Maria’s final preference for silence and non-performance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Maria’s choice, ownership, and self-determination without pressure. Safety steps are framed as invitations, and Maria is positioned as the one deciding what is possible.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions invite Maria to generate her own responses and reflect her internal reasons for staying. Evocation remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore her values, confidence, or strongest change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative, non-prescriptive stance and offers options instead of directing behavior. Partnership is not fully reciprocal because the session remains structured around safety tasks and does not follow Maria’s final lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits and reflects change talk such as staying, not packing up, and trying to sit with the pain. The strongest change talk is not consistently amplified or expanded into deeper motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with “nothing works” and uses double-sided language to validate both hope and weariness. Sustain talk is not fully transformed into a bridge toward change, especially regarding Maria’s fear that there is not enough time.
