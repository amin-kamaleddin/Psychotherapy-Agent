# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates exceptional unconditional positive regard throughout, consistently validating Elena's ambivalence and resistance without judgment. Notable moments include reframing her hesitation as 'protective wisdom' and explicitly telling her she 'doesn't need to apologize.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go well beyond paraphrasing to capture the emotional core of Elena's experience, such as the dissociation between relief and fear and the autonomy threat she perceives. The psychiatrist accurately reflects ambivalence without prematurely resolving it and picks up on subtle emotional shifts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on the target behavior with a logical progression from validating emotional impact to identifying information as the next step. A brief summary connecting explored themes and identifying a concrete next step would strengthen the sense of progress and closure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session establishes a genuinely collaborative dynamic from the start with 'we' language and consistent invitations for Elena's input. Her contributions directly shape the session direction, such as shifting from decision-making to information-gathering based on her feedback.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is woven naturally throughout with explicit affirmations like 'you know your own pace' and 'no selling, no agenda.' There is no moment where the psychiatrist implies Elena should decide now or that her hesitation is a problem to fix.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits change talk through strategic questions that draw motivation from within Elena rather than providing external reasons. However, moments like 'I know I need to do something' could have been explored more deeply to connect to underlying values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Elena as the expert on her own experience, from the opening question to affirming that decision authority rests entirely with her. The session feels genuinely collaborative with her contributions substantially shaping the conversation direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questions like 'what would make it feel ready to you?' and amplifying Elena's own change-oriented thinking effectively cultivate meaningful change talk. The evocation could be deeper by more actively exploring her values (e.g., what not wanting to think about it daily reveals about her priorities).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk skillfully without arguing or dismissing, using double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence. Some moments could have been more strategically reframed to gently pivot toward change, such as distinguishing between not being ready to decide and being ready to want a lasting decision.
