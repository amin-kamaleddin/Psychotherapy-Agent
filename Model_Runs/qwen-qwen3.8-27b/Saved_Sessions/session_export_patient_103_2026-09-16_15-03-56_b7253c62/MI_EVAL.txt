# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and often validates Nabil’s anger and experience. However, he sometimes over-identifies with Nabil’s externalizing/system-blaming position, which can reduce the balance of unconditional acceptance and agency.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows strong reflective listening and accurately captures Nabil’s pull between maintaining composure and wanting his time to be respected. Some emotionally important themes, such as shame, fear, and humiliation, are not fully explored, and a few reflections feel slightly leading or repetitive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays broadly relevant to Nabil’s anger, delays, and concerns about his time. However, the focus remains diffuse and does not settle on a specific, actionable behavior or target moment to address.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses questions, reflections, and Nabil’s own language, but the interaction often feels more therapist-guided than genuinely co-led. The client’s frustration about being asked to solve a puzzle is not fully addressed through more explicit agenda negotiation or recalibration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directives and scripts while emphasizing that Nabil knows his own schedule and standards best. Autonomy support is inconsistent because some responses reinforce external responsibility and do not clearly affirm Nabil’s freedom to choose whether or how to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions and reflections to draw out Nabil’s own concerns and goals rather than imposing advice. The evocation is limited in depth, with fewer opportunities to strengthen values, confidence, importance, or readiness for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Nabil’s perspective and allows his input to influence the session’s direction. However, the psychiatrist continues to steer with a series of reflective and strategic questions despite Nabil’s expressed frustration, making the partnership uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk, such as Nabil’s desire not to be “the angry guy” and to have a fair, clean exit. These statements are not consistently deepened, amplified, or connected to values and self-image through evocative follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and occasionally redirects toward fairness, values, and what would need to be true. However, much of the session remains centered on system failure and external responsibility, with limited skillful softening of the client’s stuck position.
