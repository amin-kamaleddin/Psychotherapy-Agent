# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone with strong anti-judgment statements and unconditional support throughout. Slightly held back from a 5 by moments where reflections read as polished or subtly leading rather than spontaneously genuine.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional and consistently layered empathic responses that capture emotional texture beyond content, including tracking real-time emotional escalation and catching the client's self-correction. A standout strength of the session with consistently 5-level empathic work.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on behavioral targets while skillfully following the client's lead into deeper material, with logical progression from barriers to insight to action plan. Could have been more explicitly anchored to the specific behavioral targets, as the emotional exploration takes significant time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Model collaborative practice with inclusive, exploratory language and a genuinely equal power dynamic. The action plan is entirely client-generated, and the client's own closing statement confirms the collaborative quality of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support with no pressure, persuasion, or directives—every suggestion is framed as an invitation. Explicitly removes judgment and outcome pressure from the check-in, and the client's closing confirms this approach landed effectively.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never provides the content of the client's insights; instead creates conditions and asks questions that allow the client to arrive at them himself. Masterful use of evocative questions that produced authentic, self-generated change talk and concrete action steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative and non-authoritarian, positioning the client as the expert on his own experience. Held back from a 5 by moments where the psychiatrist's reflections arrive at the insight slightly ahead of the client, subtly shifting the power dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully and consistently evokes authentic, self-generated change talk through well-timed open questions and reflective statements. The change talk that emerges is clearly the client's own language, not suggested by the psychiatrist.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by reflecting and redirecting rather than arguing, without dismissing or over-validating. Could have been more active in strategically pivoting away from sustain talk in one or two key moments.
