# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally nonjudgmental stance with validating language, though it sometimes feels procedural and mildly corrective. More individualized affirmation of the client's strengths and values would improve this area.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Provides accurate reflective listening that helps the patient feel heard, but lacks depth. The reflections stay at the surface and do not fully explore deeper emotional undercurrents such as defensiveness or his identity as a worker.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear and appropriate focus on the target behavior and related health risks without irrelevant drift. However, it lacks strong strategic momentum or progressive direction toward a specific change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some partial collaboration through offered choices and invitations for preference, but the interaction is uneven. The overall structure mostly remains a clinician-led clinical information exchange rather than truly following the client's agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly emphasizes the client's freedom to choose and avoids coercive or directive language. However, the continuous offering of provider-generated options keeps the client somewhat in a recipient role rather than fully owning the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a few questions that could have drawn out motivation but does not follow up to expand on them. Instead, the psychiatrist frequently shifts into providing medical risk information and practical suggestions rather than eliciting the client's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is treated as a participant by being asked what he wants to focus on and which options he prefers. However, the psychiatrist still frequently supplies medical risk information and concrete options, keeping the interaction somewhat expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are only weak and sparse efforts to cultivate change talk without the use of rulers or values exploration. The psychiatrist fails to reflect, amplify, or explore a small piece of future-oriented change talk offered by the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing with the client's reasons for maintaining the routine and acknowledges the sustain talk respectfully. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently use double-sided reflections or effectively pivot the sustain talk toward resolving ambivalence.
