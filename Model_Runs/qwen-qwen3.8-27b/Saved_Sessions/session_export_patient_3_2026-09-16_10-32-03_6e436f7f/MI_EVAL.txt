# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with warm, affirming language throughout (e.g., "real courage," "real self-compassion"). Never judges the client's body, past inactivity, or fear of looking "gross."

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate and helpful reflections of emotion and relational meaning, but largely summarizing/affirming rather than deeply exploratory. Could have reflected the body-image pain more explicitly or explored what "gross" means to her.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear, steady progression from motivation to a fully concrete action plan (day, time, script, backup, follow-up). No drift into unrelated topics while remaining responsive to her stated barriers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Highly collaborative in practice—she selects timing, texts, and rules that shape every step. The counselor nonetheless supplies multiple polished scripts and contingency texts, making it more counselor-guided than fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers options and asks for her preference ("Which feels more comfortable?"); affirms flexibility ("10 minutes absolutely counts") without pressuring her toward a larger commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong early open questions ("What matters most…?", "What would move your confidence from a 6 to a 7?") elicit her own plan and motivation. Evocation shifts to counselor-provided scripts and logistical questions later, with less deep internal/values exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** She generates virtually all plan elements and the counselor builds on her contributions, asking before offering options. Lacks an explicit "you are the expert on your life" framing or agenda negotiation, and the counselor still shapes the overall structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent scaling and scaling-boost questions elicit strong change talk ("Not waiting to feel motivated," "tired of being stuck") and confidence rises from 6→8.5. Could have evoked deeper "why now" or values-based reasons rather than staying logistical.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All sustain talk (tiredness, "out of shape," fear of partner rejection, all-or-nothing) is met with softening reframes and multiple backup options. Never argues against or dwells on the barriers; keeps focus on possible movement.
