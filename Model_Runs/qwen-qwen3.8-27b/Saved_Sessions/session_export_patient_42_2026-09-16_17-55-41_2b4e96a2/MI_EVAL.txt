# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remains respectful and nonjudgmental, affirming the client’s ambivalence without correction. It falls short of a 5 because the warmth is more process-focused than explicitly unconditional toward the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and capture the social, health, and fear-of-exposure dimensions of the client’s experience. The main limitation is that deeper emotional meaning, such as shame or identity concerns, is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on reducing smoking in social situations while protecting health. The conversation moves logically from ambivalence toward a concrete, low-pressure plan and early coping steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the client’s ideas clearly shaping the plan. The psychiatrist co-constructs next steps rather than directing or imposing an agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice and self-direction without pressure, emphasizing the client’s own judgment. It does not reach a 5 because the conversation still leans consistently toward non-smoking strategies rather than fully opening the right to make any choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client’s own reasons, plans, and coping ideas rather than giving advice. The main gap is that deeper values, identity, and future-oriented motivation are not consistently amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and a collaborative stance, positioning the client as the expert on what will work. It falls short of a 5 because the partnership is not explicitly framed through shared agenda-setting or shared expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to generate change-oriented ideas and reinforces useful planning and ability statements. It is not a 5 because change talk is not systematically deepened with stronger strategic amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or minimization and is gently redirected toward change. It falls short of a 5 because the sustain talk remains central and is not always transformed through more nuanced double-sided reflections.
