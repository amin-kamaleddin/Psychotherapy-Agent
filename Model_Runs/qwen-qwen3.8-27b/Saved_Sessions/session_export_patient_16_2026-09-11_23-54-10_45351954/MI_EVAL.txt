# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and supports the client’s choices. Acceptance is strong but becomes slightly formulaic and is undercut by frequent practical advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist offers accurate, pragmatic reflections of the client’s concerns about basketball, school, and social use. Deeper emotional and identity-related ambivalence is not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior and concrete goals around pre-game use. The therapist effectively keeps the discussion anchored to a specific, modifiable behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist invites the client to choose among options and builds the plan around her preferences. However, the therapist’s frequent scripts and suggestions make the interaction less fully collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist repeatedly asks for the client’s choice, respects boundaries, and avoids overt pressure. Autonomy support is somewhat weakened by the amount of advice and structured planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses clear internal motivation related to basketball performance, but the therapist does not consistently draw out or expand this motivation. There is limited use of values, importance, confidence, or future-focused evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist includes the client’s input and lets her choices shape the plan. The session remains unevenly directive because the therapist frequently positions themselves as the expert provider of tools and steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers useful change talk about wanting to play well and stay clear-headed for games. The therapist does not consistently evocate, amplify, or deepen this motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing with or reinforcing the client’s minimizing statements and resistance to being labeled. The response stays practical but does not consistently use MI-consistent techniques to pivot sustain talk toward change.
