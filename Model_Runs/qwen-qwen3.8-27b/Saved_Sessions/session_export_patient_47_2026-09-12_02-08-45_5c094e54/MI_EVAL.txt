# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and explicitly reduces stigma around labeling. Acceptance is slightly limited by a structured tone and fewer individualized affirmations of Evelyn's effort and strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Evelyn's ambivalence, the value of her evening wine, and her skepticism about having a problem. Empathy is not perfect because deeper emotional meanings, such as identity, shame, or concern about being seen as an alcoholic, are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear overall direction focused on alcohol use, moving from screening concerns to drinking patterns, risk, and a possible plan. The focus is sometimes diluted by broader information on screening, medication, and GP disclosure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is a collaborative tone, with frequent "we" language and invitations for Evelyn to choose options. The psychiatrist still carries much of the structure and content, and could invite more client-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects Evelyn's goal to manage rather than quit and repeatedly offers choices. Autonomy support is slightly reduced by some directive recommendations, such as considering GP disclosure and providing risk information.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions effectively elicit Evelyn's own perspective, change talk, and confidence in trying a plan. Evocation is less complete because much of the change rationale is externally provided through risk information rather than drawn from Evelyn's own values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a generally collaborative stance and invites Evelyn's input in shaping the conversation and plan. The session is still somewhat structured, with clinician-selected options and defined next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully elicits and supports Evelyn's own change talk, including measuring pours and limiting heavier nights. More targeted DARC questions could help deepen and amplify this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or moralizing and keeps the conversation open when Evelyn minimizes her drinking. Advanced MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections, are used less consistently, leaving some sustain talk active.
