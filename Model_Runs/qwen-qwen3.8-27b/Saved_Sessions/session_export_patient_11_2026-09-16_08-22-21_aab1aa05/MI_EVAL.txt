# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental tone while normalizing the client’s cautiousness. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and performance-focused rather than fully unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate content reflections but often stays at a surface level. Deeper emotions such as fear of failure, shame, fatigue, or body-image concerns are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a concrete, relevant target behavior: a small walking routine. Direction is behavioral rather than consistently motivational, with limited follow-through on values and readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices, adapts to the client’s preferences, and co-creates a simple plan. Collaboration is strong but not fully balanced because the psychiatrist remains the main source of options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client’s pace and low-stakes choices while avoiding pressure or coercion. Autonomy support could be stronger by more explicitly affirming the client’s freedom to choose not to walk.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open questions about doable movement and confidence, but does not consistently evoke intrinsic motivation. There is limited exploration of values, importance, or follow-up on the client’s own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a collaborative stance by inviting input, honoring refusals, and co-creating the plan. Partnership is strong but still clinician-led in generating options and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces some change talk, and the psychiatrist asks relevant questions about what feels doable. Change talk is present but not consistently deepened through importance, confidence, ability, or values-based questioning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with barrier talk and sometimes validates the logic of keeping the plan small. The response often shifts to problem-solving rather than using MI-specific strategies such as double-sided reflections.
