# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, normalizes Marcus’s concerns, and avoids shaming or argument. Minor pushiness in repeatedly offering extra planning steps limits the score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There are effective reflections of ambivalence and practical concerns, such as wanting to fit in while staying safe. The empathy remains more cognitive than emotional and misses deeper peer-pressure and autonomy themes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol-related risk and safe return from parties without drifting. The focus is somewhat narrow, emphasizing logistics more than broader motivation to change drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan builds on Marcus’s ideas, such as using his cousin, a ride app, and bringing a charger. The psychiatrist still supplies many specific options, so collaboration is strong but not fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently emphasizes choice, asks permission, and respects Marcus’s decisions. Repeated offers to set up texts, payments, or backups can feel subtly pressuring after he declines.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions invite Marcus’s input, but much of the session relies on the psychiatrist supplying reasons and options. More evocative questions about values, importance, and confidence would strengthen client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus contributes ideas, but the psychiatrist frequently sets the agenda and provides structured solutions. The interaction feels more counselor-directed than fully shared leadership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus expresses some change talk about practical safety steps like texting a cousin and using a ride app. The change talk is limited and does not deeply explore desire, importance, need, or ability to reduce alcohol use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids confrontation and reframes safety planning as low-effort and reasonable when Marcus minimizes risk. The sustain talk is respected, but it is not consistently transformed into ambivalence or change talk using more advanced MI techniques.
