# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating without stigmatizing the client. Acceptance could be strengthened with more explicit, individualized unconditional positive regard independent of behavior or future choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing mixed feelings, dignity, pride, fear, and ambivalence. Some reflections feel slightly leading or thematic and could explore deeper shame, anger, and fear more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains consistently focused on reducing recidivism and building a pro-social life. Pacing is purposeful, focused, and change-oriented while moving toward concrete next steps without pushing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently follows the client's lead and incorporates his language and preferences into the plan. Some interpretive summaries shape the conversation more than pure collaboration, slightly limiting the sense that the client is fully directing the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice, control, and non-commitment are clearly supported, especially by framing the call as only asking questions. More explicit statements that the decision is entirely the client's, including the freedom not to move forward, would strengthen this area.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively elicit the client's own motivations, goals, and realistic next steps. Some responses label or interpret the client's experience rather than continuing to draw out his own values, confidence, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The stance is collaborative and often invites the client's perspective rather than directing him. Occasional interpretive framing, such as naming the client's chosen path, slightly reduces the impression of fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits and reinforces meaningful change talk about calling Dave, showing up, and proving he can do this. More systematic evocation of confidence, need, values, and commitment language would move this to a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without arguing, reinforcing, or amplifying the client's fear of being seen as a liability. More double-sided reflections around the old crew and ambivalence would further soften sustain talk.
