# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental tone while normalizing Rafael's ambivalence and avoiding shame. It is not a 5 because some acceptance is subtly tied to Rafael's chosen actions or effort rather than fully unconditional regard for him.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Rafael's mixed feelings, fear of failure, and self-criticism. The main limitation is that reflections often move the session forward rather than sustaining deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of returning to the gym and building a realistic activity pattern. Each exchange supports the goal and moves Rafael toward a concrete next step without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by asking Rafael's preferences and co-creating a realistic plan with his input. It is not a 5 because the therapist frequently supplies preselected options, scripts, and behavioral experiments, which makes the process partly therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally supports Rafael's autonomy by inviting choices and respecting his limits. Some advisory language and structured suggestions slightly reduce the emphasis on his full freedom to generate his own plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative MI questions about change, importance, and strengths. However, the approach quickly shifts toward problem-solving and structured planning, so Rafael's own motivations are not consistently drawn out or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative, non-expert stance by asking for Rafael's preferences and allowing his choices to shape the plan. It is not a 5 because the therapist still leads with many structured options, which limits full client-directed power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through the importance ruler, strengths questions, and success-focused prompts. Change talk is present but not consistently amplified, reflected back, or deepened to strengthen Rafael's motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Rafael's sustain talk skillfully by normalizing concerns and avoiding argument or pressure. The limitation is that responses often steer the conversation toward task completion and small experiments rather than fully transforming the ambivalence itself.
