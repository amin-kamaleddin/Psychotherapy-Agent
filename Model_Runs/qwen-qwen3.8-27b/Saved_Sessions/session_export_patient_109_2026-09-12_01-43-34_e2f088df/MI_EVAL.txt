# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, reducing shame around hesitation. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and does not deeply personalize broader stressors or client worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is adequate reflective listening for the client's stated difficulty. However, the emotional texture of ambivalence, fatigue, self-criticism, and fear of failure is not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on the target behavior of returning to exercise. The conversation moves steadily toward a concrete, actionable small step with little drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates a small plan and adapts to the client's boundaries, especially around the clothes-on step. However, the psychiatrist still leads the structure through repeated recaps, reminders, and success criteria.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options, accepts the client's refusal to add another step, and supports choice in a non-coercive way. Frequent reminders and directives, however, somewhat reduce emphasis on client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is the weakest area, with the session quickly shifting to suggestions, options, and external strategies. There is minimal exploration of the client's values, desired outcomes, importance, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks doability questions and allows the client to shape the plan, showing some power sharing. The collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist remains the main agenda-setter and troubleshooting frame provider.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist occasionally affirms change-oriented language and the client produces meaningful change talk. The counselor could do more to consistently evoke, expand, and amplify the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with barriers and includes mild nonjudgmental reframes. Sustain talk is acknowledged, but it is not consistently softened using complex reflections or ambivalence-focused MI strategies.
