# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, avoiding labels or direct challenge. Acceptance is somewhat generic and could more specifically affirm Daniel’s values and strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture patterns and ambivalence, but remain more cognitive than emotional. The psychiatrist does not consistently explore feelings such as frustration, identity concern, or irritation at being flagged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use, training, and a concrete tracking plan. Direction is strong, though moving quickly into practical advice can slightly dilute motivation building.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniel’s preferences shape the plan, including tracking, timing, and check-in method. Collaboration is present but not fully equal because the psychiatrist supplies much of the structure, education, and tools.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices, avoids pressure, and supports a low-burden approach. It could be stronger by more explicitly affirming that all decisions remain Daniel’s.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some practical preference questions but introduces external reasons for change before drawing out Daniel’s own beliefs. Evocation is limited in consistently eliciting internal motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is a reasonable collaborative stance and Daniel’s choices influence the session. However, the psychiatrist still functions as a strong content provider rather than fully positioning Daniel as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel produces some change-relevant language, and the psychiatrist affirms it. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to use scaling, importance, or confidence questions to deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with minimization, which keeps the conversation safe. It does not consistently reframe or redirect sustain talk toward discrepancy and exploration.
