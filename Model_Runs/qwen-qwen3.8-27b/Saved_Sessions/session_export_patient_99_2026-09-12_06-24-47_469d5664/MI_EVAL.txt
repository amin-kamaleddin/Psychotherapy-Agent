# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental, and respectful stance toward Matteo. Acceptance is slightly reduced when the clinician occasionally moves ahead of the client’s stated confidence or plan details.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects key social and practical concerns accurately but remains mostly surface-level. Deeper ambivalence and the personal meaning of Wednesday nights are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on drinking and its impact on sleep, work, school, and ankle recovery. It becomes somewhat agenda-driven when the plan and follow-up are moved through quickly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and helps Matteo build a concrete plan from his own choices. A high number of clinician-generated options makes the interaction feel somewhat guided rather than fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Matteo’s autonomy is generally respected through permissive language, choices, and acceptance of his limits. The score is lowered because the psychiatrist sometimes inserts details before Matteo has fully made them his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ruler questions and open questions elicit some of Matteo’s own reasons for change. The session shifts too quickly into information and practical options, limiting deeper exploration of his motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Matteo contributes to the plan and is invited to choose options, but the psychiatrist often summarizes or adds steps on his behalf. This reduces the sense of true power-sharing and client-led co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked around protecting his ankle, work, and schedule. The psychiatrist could have more consistently reflected, amplified, and built on Matteo’s own statements about change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Matteo or challenging his minimization of the problem. However, sustain talk and ambivalence are not skillfully explored or reframed to support movement toward change.
