# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and affirming, avoiding judgment about partial drinking reduction. Acceptance is supportive but sometimes feels formulaic and achievement-focused rather than deeply validating the client's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track the client's confidence, realism, and planning concerns. Empathy is consistent but could go deeper into the fear, vulnerability, and burden-related emotions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing alcohol use and managing anger/impulsivity. It effectively moves from broad concerns to a concrete if-then plan and backup strategy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed, with the client choosing the shoes cue, Sunday habit, text option, and backup. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist sometimes offers solutions before fully waiting for client-generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids direct persuasion, supporting the client's decision-making. Autonomy could be strengthened by more explicitly reinforcing the client's freedom to choose or not choose each step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and planning questions successfully elicit useful change talk and self-generated strategies. Deeper evocation of personal values, reasons for change, and discrepancy is limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and lets the client's choices shape the session. Partnership is effective, but frequent provision of concrete options makes it feel slightly clinician-guided.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence scaling and affirmations elicit helpful ability- and planning-based change talk. More focus on values, needs, and reasons for change would make change talk richer.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without arguing or shaming, and doubts are reframed as realism. The response is competent but could use more advanced techniques such as double-sided reflections.
