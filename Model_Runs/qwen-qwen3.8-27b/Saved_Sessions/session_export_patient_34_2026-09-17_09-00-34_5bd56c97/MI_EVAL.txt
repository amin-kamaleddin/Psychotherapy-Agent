# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows respect, nonjudgmental curiosity, and validation of Dylan’s social concerns and fear of stigma. Acceptance is strong but falls short of a 5 because some reflections subtly steer toward responsibility without fully emphasizing unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track Dylan’s concerns about reputation, fairness, shame, and belonging, with some nuanced empathy. It is not a 5 because the clinician often summarizes concerns rather than consistently exploring deeper identity, shame, and fear-of-rejection themes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a coherent focus on the incident, fairness, and responsibility, but the target behavior of underage drinking is diluted into paperwork and administrative consequences. It would benefit from more consistent linking of Dylan’s values to future behavior and risk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician invites Dylan to define fairness, maturity, and what would make a warning acceptable, treating his perspective as central. Collaboration is limited by the institutional agenda and insufficient explicit negotiation of roles, confidentiality limits, and process boundaries.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Dylan’s agency through open questions and avoids overt pressure, advice, or coercion. Autonomy support is strong but not perfect because some questions imply a warning is the default next step rather than fully affirming Dylan’s freedom to choose differently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some of Dylan’s values around fairness, respect, social belonging, and reputation, and elicits limited change talk. It is not stronger because evocation is not consistently developed into deeper intrinsic motivation for changing alcohol-related behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative stance and positions Dylan as the expert on what would feel safe and fair. Partnership is constrained by the underlying conduct-office agenda, with less explicit shared decision-making about the purpose, limits, and priorities of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Dylan offers some change talk, such as acknowledging he was present and recognizing he “should’ve maybe stayed home,” but the clinician does not consistently deepen, amplify, or expand it. More follow-up on values, future choices, and confidence in different behavior would strengthen this score.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Dylan and reflects the concerns behind his sustain talk rather than reinforcing minimization. However, the session remains heavily anchored in consequence management and does not consistently redirect his sustain talk toward discrepancy, values, or future behavior change.
