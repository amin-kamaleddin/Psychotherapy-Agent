# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:**  Respectful and nonjudgmental overall, but warmth felt somewhat formulaic and tied to progress; quickly shifted into advice-giving, making the stance feel more instructional than fully accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:**  Offered brief, interpretive validations of guilt and fear but remained mostly surface-level; missed opportunities for deeper complex reflections on her underlying concerns about identity and relationship.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  Clear and consistent focus on the specific target behavior (parental boundary/Saturday protection) across all topics; slightly weakened by becoming quite task-focused on scripts and logging rather than periodically reconnecting to her stated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:**  Invited input and allowed Elena to choose and modify scripts, but still initiated and controlled much of the content by supplying multiple scripts, templates, and step-by-step plans, making it feel more like guided coaching than an equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:**  Regularly offered choices and options with no pressure or coercion, and validated lower-pressure alternatives; somewhat undermined by the volume of direct instruction and pre-written scripts, plus the missed opportunity to affirm her autonomy in answering the phone at the end.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:**  Only one broadly evocative question early on before rapidly moving into structured plans and scripts; limited further exploration of Elena's internal reasons, confidence, values, or desired outcomes for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:**  Overall expert-driven stance; frequently supplied complete scripts, templates, and directives rather than inviting Elena to generate her own words and explore what already works for her, positioning the counselor as problem-solver rather than partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:**  Elena did produce some change-relevant language and the counselor occasionally affirmed it, but did not consistently elicit, amplify, or deepen those reflections; few questions probing her values, confidence level, or personal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:**  Appropriately normalized her fears and redirected without arguing or shaming, but spent considerable time on obstacles and responded primarily with behavioral solutions rather than MI-style pivots or double-sided reflections that might have helped her move beyond the problem into her own reasons for change.
