# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly affirms Leila’s expertise and autonomy. Slightly reduced because some affirmations are therapist-generated evaluations rather than purely unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures Leila’s practical concerns, transition difficulties, and emotional meaning of the walk. Could be deeper by attending more explicitly to underlying fears around gym pressure, wasted time, and body confidence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior: starting with a sustainable post-work walk and exploring a low-pressure gym trial. The session stays actionable and does not drift away from the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Leila’s ideas strongly shape the plan, including the walk, river route, coffee reward, and flexible gym conditions. Slightly limited by a more therapist-led question sequence and occasional interpretive affirmations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Leila’s choice, avoids pressure, and keeps the gym as a “maybe.” No advice-giving, persuasion, or guilt-inducing language is present.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Leila’s own motivations, reasons for change, and preferred conditions through open, evocative questions. Leila generates most of the plan and change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently positions Leila as the expert on her own life and change process. Slightly reduced because the clinician still leads much of the direction with consecutive questions and evaluative comments.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks useful questions that draw out Leila’s confidence, ability, and reasons for making the walk and gym trial doable. Could be stronger by amplifying her exact change language and explicitly eliciting confidence or ability ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles barriers respectfully without arguing or dismissing, and often reframes them to preserve autonomy. Could be stronger by more consistently pivoting sustain talk into explicit change talk, especially around the gym uncertainty.
