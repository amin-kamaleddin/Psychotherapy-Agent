# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental about smoking and the client's frustration with medical advice. Affirming Lucía's effort, courage, or worth more explicitly could strengthen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence, pressure, and frustration with repeated advice. Some reflections are polished or interpretive, so deeper feelings and underlying concerns could be explored more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on smoking and moves toward a concrete, small experiment: skipping the morning cigarette. Connecting the experiment more explicitly to health goals, values, or long-term cessation would strengthen direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids an expert stance and allows Lucía's preferences to shape the plan. Reducing therapist-generated options and more explicitly negotiating the agenda would make collaboration feel more co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength is consistent emphasis on choice, ownership, and self-direction, such as acting on her own terms and keeping it between them. Suggestions remain noncoercive and support the client's sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are attempts to elicit Lucía's own motivations, but some questions are leading or offer limited choices. More open evocative questions could better draw out her values, beliefs, and internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Lucía's perspective and collaborates on a low-pressure experiment. The session remains somewhat counselor-guided through repeated question structures, which can subtly steer rather than fully co-create the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is present, especially around trying to skip the morning cigarette and observing whether it works. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify, deepen, and reinforce this change talk to build motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally rolled with without argument, and the psychiatrist keeps the tone low-pressure and nonjudgmental. Occasional over-accommodation, such as fully accepting no follow-up, may limit opportunities to gently explore accountability or support.
