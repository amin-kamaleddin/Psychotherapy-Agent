# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the patient's willingness to try. Warmth is supportive but somewhat procedural, with less explicit affirmation of the patient beyond the alcohol concern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures ambivalence around drinking, the fall, and the son's worry. Deeper emotional undercurrents, such as shame, fear, or independence, are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on reducing alcohol use. Each topic is tied back to the target goal, moving clearly from concern to plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by inviting patient input and building on her proposed ideas. The session remains somewhat clinician-guided because the counselor still provides multiple structured options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is consistently supported, and the patient is encouraged to select a manageable experiment rather than quit entirely. Explicit affirmation of her right to choose or change her mind without judgment is limited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor helps the patient articulate internal reasons for change, including clarity, safety, and reducing her son's worry. Deeper values-based evocation, such as independence and staying sharp, could be stronger.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient is positioned as the expert on her own life, and her preferences shape the plan. The agenda and structure are still fairly counselor-led, which slightly limits a fully partnered stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, rulers, and planning prompts successfully elicit clear change talk and increase confidence. The change talk remains somewhat practical and could be deepened through values and identity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoids arguing with sustain talk and acknowledges reasons for drinking. Important identity-based and habit-based sustain talk is not consistently reflected, reframed, or softened.
