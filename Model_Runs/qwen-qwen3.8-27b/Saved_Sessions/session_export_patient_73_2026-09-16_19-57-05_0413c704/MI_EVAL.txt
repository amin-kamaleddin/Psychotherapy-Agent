# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard while validating Linda’s ambivalence and fear of being seen as broken. The warmth remains somewhat procedural rather than deeply affirmative, keeping this from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate, complex reflections that capture Linda’s simultaneous attachment to smoking and concern about health and money. Deeper shame and repeated frustration with past failures are not fully processed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on tobacco cessation. It moves steadily from ambivalence to a concrete, well-guided action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interview is strongly collaborative, with Linda’s ideas directly shaping the session and plan. The psychiatrist follows her lead and co-constructs the approach rather than prescribing one.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by asking what Linda wants, what feels safe, and what to avoid, while avoiding control or pressure. Reinforcement of her ownership of both the decision and process is not as explicit as a top score would require.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits motivation and planning from Linda rather than providing reasons to quit. It could be stronger by evoking more confidence, self-efficacy, and value-based reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Linda as the expert on what feels possible and invites her to design the step. The power-sharing is clear but not consistently emphasized enough to warrant a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly evokes change-oriented language, and Linda produces concrete change statements such as delaying smoking after coffee and trying a Tuesday check-in. It could be strengthened by more explicitly eliciting confidence, values, and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged rather than challenged, and Linda’s reasons for maintaining smoking are incorporated into a smaller compatible plan. The conversation still spends considerable time on reasons for staying the course before fully pivoting to action.
