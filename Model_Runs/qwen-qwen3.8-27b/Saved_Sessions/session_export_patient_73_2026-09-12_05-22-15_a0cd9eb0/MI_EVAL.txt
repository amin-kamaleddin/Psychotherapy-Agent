# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes ambivalence and past relapse. Warmth is somewhat formulaic, with limited individualized affirmation of Linda’s effort and worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture fear, frustration, and ambivalence about smoking, health, and social connection. Empathy could be deeper by exploring financial stress, medical fear, and repeated failed attempts before moving to practical advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on tobacco cessation and a specific high-risk situation, moving toward a concrete plan. Direction is strong but narrows quickly to behavior management and scripts rather than sustaining MI-style exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is collaborative, with Linda choosing the drink, refusal phrase, and reminder wording. Collaboration could be stronger by allowing Linda to generate more ideas before the psychiatrist offers options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses noncoercive language and respects Linda’s uncertainty and willingness to try. Frequent specific suggestions can subtly shift the interaction toward advice-giving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out some of Linda’s motivations, barriers, and preferences. Evocation is limited by frequent advice-giving and clinician-generated scripts before fully exploring Linda’s own reasons and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda is invited to choose options and contributes to the plan. The psychiatrist often positions themselves as the source of solutions, which limits the sense that Linda is the expert on her own life and motivation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some meaningful change talk is elicited, such as willingness to try a drink, waiting ten minutes, and using a reminder. The psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or deepen this change talk before moving into practical planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is accepted without argument, avoiding a confrontational dynamic. The skill could be stronger by using more double-sided reflections or MI-style reframing to hold both sides and gently tilt toward change.
