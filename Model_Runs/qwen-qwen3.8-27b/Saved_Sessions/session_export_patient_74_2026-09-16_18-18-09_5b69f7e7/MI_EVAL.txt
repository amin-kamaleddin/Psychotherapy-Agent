# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance normalizes Linda's ambivalence and difficulty. Warmth remains somewhat structured and does not fully affirm her worth apart from the smoking issue.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the tension between guilt, relief, and the meaning of smoking. It falls short of fully deepening the emotional impact of her fear of being a bad mother.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays focused on smoking, guilt, daughter concerns, and evening routine without major drift. Direction remains more exploratory than forward-moving toward readiness or a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist invites Linda to set the agenda and builds on her perspective in a non-authoritative way. Collaboration is evident but somewhat implied rather than explicit through shared language or co-created planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports Linda's freedom of choice and avoids pressure to quit. Possible changes are framed as her choice rather than punishment, obligation, or external control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions invite Linda to speak from her own experience rather than providing reasons for change. Evocation remains somewhat superficial and does not systematically draw out intrinsic motivation for changing smoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance and avoids positioning himself as the expert. Falls short of 5 because explicit power sharing, mutual expertise language, and co-created next steps are limited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions allow Linda to generate change-adjacent language, such as stepping outside and valuing her break. Use of affirmations, amplification, and explicit elicitation of desire, ability, reasons, or need for change is limited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges the value and function of smoking without arguing or dismissing it. The session spends much time elaborating smoking's role, which can inadvertently reinforce reasons to continue rather than gently shift focus.
