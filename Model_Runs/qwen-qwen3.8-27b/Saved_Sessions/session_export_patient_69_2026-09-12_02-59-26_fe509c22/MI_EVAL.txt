# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent respect and validation without judgment. The acceptance feels somewhat procedural and the quick ending limits deeper regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist tracks the patient’s surface frustration with accurate reflections. Emotional depth is limited, missing deeper feelings such as resentment or powerlessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the check-in behavior and ends with a clear plan. The direction is practical but remains surface-level rather than strategic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient is invited to choose options and helps shape the final plan. The psychiatrist still supplies most of the structure and direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects the patient’s choices and avoids pressure. Autonomy is supported, though options are largely counselor-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are used to elicit the patient’s preferences. The evocation stays superficial and does not build deeper motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session starts collaboratively but quickly becomes expert-driven. The psychiatrist provides solutions and structure rather than working as a true partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few moments of change talk, but the psychiatrist does not follow up effectively. The focus shifts to practical scripting, limiting intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not confront the patient’s sustain talk, but the session reinforces minimal engagement. There is no effective reframing or redirection.
