# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates respect, warmth, and non-judgment throughout, validating Jamal's perspective multiple times without moralizing. Slightly formulaic in structure, with occasional subtle positioning of the athletic director as an authority figure rather than fully normalizing the referral context.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate complex reflections capturing dual motivations and the gap between in-the-moment and reflective states. Some reflections lean toward interpretation rather than pure reflection, and there is limited deeper exploration of the shame and anxiety underlying the peer pressure experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, coherent focus on the target behavior with logical progression from exploring the situation to examining resistance to the conversation itself. Could benefit from more strategic momentum toward a concrete MI outcome such as a small commitment or next step by the end of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative behaviors through open-ended questioning and framing the conversation as joint exploration rather than top-down intervention. Collaboration is primarily achieved through questioning rather than explicit co-construction of a shared plan or goal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Near-textbook demonstration of autonomy support, consistently centering Jamal's own values, never pressuring or telling him what to do, and validating his choices as his own. The response to his statement about not being confrontational perfectly supports autonomy by affirming that as his choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes change talk and self-motivation from within Jamal through well-designed questions that draw out his own insights. Could be more consistent and strategically targeted, with opportunities to use scaling questions or more directly evoke his own reasons for openness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance and frequently positions Jamal as the expert on his own experience, avoiding assumptions of authority. The dynamic remains somewhat one-directional with the psychiatrist still largely driving the agenda and structure of the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits several instances of change talk, including key insights about not wanting to be someone he is not and the gap between in-the-moment and reflective states. Lacks systematic elicitation of DARN-CAT language, particularly around confidence, ability, or specific next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handles sustain talk by reflecting it as values, reframing rather than arguing, and never amplifying resistance. Could more actively pivot away from sustain talk toward change language in moments like when Jamal questions the point of the conversation.
