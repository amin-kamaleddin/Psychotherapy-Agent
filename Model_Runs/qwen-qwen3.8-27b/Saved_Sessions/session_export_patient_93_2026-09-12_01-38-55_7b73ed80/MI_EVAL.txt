# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician is respectful, nonjudgmental, and generally supportive while avoiding blame or shaming. Acceptance is somewhat procedural because the session quickly moves into planning rather than sustaining deeper warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections show reasonable understanding of Margaret's routine, retirement, and the son's worry. However, they often stay at the content level and miss deeper feelings of being controlled, losing autonomy, or valuing the ritual of wine.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a clear practical direction toward alcohol-related risk reduction and a specific plan. Focus becomes somewhat diffuse by expanding to reassurance of the son, fall prevention, medication review, and conflict management.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret influences practical decisions such as the pub-free day, check-in time, and communication style. Collaboration is uneven because the clinician frequently supplies structured scripts, checklists, and advice rather than fully co-creating the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offers options and uses fair-compromise language, but the prescriptive plan and detailed scripts reduce autonomy. Stronger support would emphasize Margaret's choice, explore her own reasons, and directly address her concern about feeling monitored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is limited after an initial open question and brief options. The clinician mostly supplies reasons, scripts, and a plan rather than eliciting Margaret's own values, independence, confidence, or deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks questions and incorporates Margaret's preferences into logistics, showing some partnership. However, the interaction remains largely expert-driven through scripted advice and structured steps rather than a fully collaborative MI partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Margaret offers some change-relevant language, but the clinician rarely develops her own reasons, ability, need, or desire for change. The session relies more on supplying a plan than evoking and strengthening client-initiated change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Margaret's sustain talk is frequent, but the clinician does not consistently reframe or redirect it toward discrepancy or change. Risk education and the unaddressed final doubt about one glass leave important sustain talk insufficiently softened.
