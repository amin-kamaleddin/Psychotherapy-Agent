# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is largely respectful and nonjudgmental but occasionally sounds mildly corrective. Stronger acceptance would reflect the client's self-criticism without adding the therapist's judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening strongly captures the client's desire for steady energy and fear of all-or-nothing cycles. Some interpretive comments could have been checked with the client to keep them more collaborative.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on increasing sustainable physical activity. It moves logically from values to barriers and then to a concrete Monday-morning plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed around the client's own ideas, such as parking farther, mailbox walks, sticky notes, and a 'Reset' cue. The interaction feels like a true partnership rather than expert instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directive advice and pressures, keeping decision-making with the client. Questions repeatedly invite the client to define what would feel respectful, doable, and meaningful.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is evoked from the client's own values, such as accountability without guilt and being less cranky with his kids. The psychiatrist avoids persuading with health facts and instead helps the client articulate his own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is treated as the expert on his own life, with the psychiatrist following the client's lead. Statements like 'you're the one best positioned to answer it' reinforce a strong alliance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently evokes desire, ability, and need for change. Questions about small first steps and what would count as 'enough' helped the client move from skepticism to a specific plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Past frustration and the 'grind' voice are normalized effectively. However, statements like 'it's not doing nothing' risk sounding like a subtle correction rather than a reflection of ambivalence.
