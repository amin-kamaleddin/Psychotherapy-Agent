# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, validating, and consistently nonjudgmental, with strong respect for the client’s constraints and choices. The repeated “It makes sense/I hear” phrasing becomes somewhat formulaic and slightly reduces spontaneous individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture practical and emotional content, including the need for realistic, low-burden action. The counselor could deepen exploration of the emotional weight behind the client’s yo-yo dieting history and frustration with repeated cycles.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear and consistent focus on the target behavior, with a logical progression from broad activity ideas to a specific, actionable walking plan. There is no topic drift and strong momentum toward a concrete commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is strongly collaborative, with the plan built from the client’s own ideas, cues, and problem-solving. The collaborative frame could be made more explicit through language that names the partnership and invites joint evaluation of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, high-quality autonomy support is evident, with no pressure, advice-giving, or prescriptive language. The plan is framed throughout as the client’s own choice, reinforcing ownership and agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocative questions draw out client-generated reasons, practical strategies, and success measures. The counselor could more explicitly explore deeper values and motivational meaning to strengthen emotional grounding.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a collaborative stance and follows the client’s lead without imposing solutions. The partnership could be more explicit by directly naming the client’s expertise and the shared decision-making process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicits progressive change talk and concrete commitments through well-designed questions. Amplification of change talk could be stronger, and scaling questions could further strengthen commitment and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk and barriers are generally acknowledged and redirected toward problem-solving without dwelling on limitations. The counselor could more actively externalize the problem and reduce the sense of personal struggle or self-sabotage.
