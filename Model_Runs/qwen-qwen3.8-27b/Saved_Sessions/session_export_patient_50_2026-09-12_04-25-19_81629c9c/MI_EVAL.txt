# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and consistently validated Hana's anxiety and autonomy. The warmth was occasionally formulaic and progress-focused, preventing a higher score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There were several accurate complex reflections of Hana's somatic and emotional experience, including her ambivalence and fatigue. Deeper reflections of underlying shame, fear of inadequacy, or meaning of failure would have strengthened this.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remained focused on the target behavior and consistently narrowed the task to a concrete, achievable step. At times it became overly procedural with multiple techniques, which slightly diluted the motivational direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited choices and allowed Hana to select the final micro-step, but frequently generated most of the content and structure. More explicit co-construction and use of Hana's own expertise would have improved collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices, respected Hana's decision to try alone, and removed performance pressure. Repeated offers of additional strategies after Hana preferred simplicity slightly weakened the consistency of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective evocation occurred through open questions, scales, and attention to Hana's change talk. The counselor often shifted into advice and coping suggestions rather than consistently deepening Hana's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative invitations and respect for Hana's privacy, but the session remained largely therapist-led and agenda-driven. More explicit acknowledgment of Hana as the expert and greater power sharing would be needed for a higher score.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited and acknowledged some meaningful change talk related to relief, hope, and action. Further strategic reflection and amplification of Hana's values and confidence language were needed for stronger development.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately normalized fear and reframed possible failure as progress. A stronger MI strategy would have more actively pivoted the sustain talk toward Hana's change-oriented values and goals.
