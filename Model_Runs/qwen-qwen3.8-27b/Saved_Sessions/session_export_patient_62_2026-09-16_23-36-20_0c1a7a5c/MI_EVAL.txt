# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental tone throughout the session. Acceptance was strong, though it sometimes remained slightly clinical rather than fully individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered accurate reflections of the client's ambivalence, exhaustion, and self-critical experience. Empathy was solid, but at times shifted quickly toward problem-solving before fully sitting with the emotional pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed connected to the client's core concerns about work stress, financial strain, and feeling stuck. Direction was appropriate, though the target behavior could have been more clearly narrowed or operationalized.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used inclusive language and invited the client's perspective in a generally collaborative way. The interaction was collaborative, but the therapist still guided the conversation more than fully following the client's lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided coercive language and respected the client's freedom, including reducing pressure to fix everything at once. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly reinforcing the client's right not to act.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used open and evocative questions to draw out the client's values, meanings, and concerns. Evocation was effective, but it did not consistently lead to stronger change talk, agency, or confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveyed a collaborative stance and recognized the client as the expert on their own experience. Partnership was strong, though the therapist occasionally steered the agenda and introduced their own frames.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist noticed and responded to some client change talk, such as wanting to feel less stuck. However, change talk was not consistently amplified, summarized, or deepened into stronger motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validated concerns and gently refrained from arguing with the client's sustain talk. The session remained heavily embedded in helplessness and failure language, with limited systematic redirection toward change or agency.
