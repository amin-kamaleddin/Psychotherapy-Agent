# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor was respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing Mai’s uncertainty and concerns. However, acceptance became formulaic and shifted quickly into instruction rather than deeper validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic responses were limited and mostly generic, focusing more on medication facts than Mai’s perspective. The counselor did not adequately reflect ambivalence, frustration, or the emotional impact of starting a new medication.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target issue of understanding and starting the new medication. However, the direction was more didactic and information-driven than MI-focused, with less clarification of Mai’s own priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were some collaborative offers, but the interaction was largely expert-driven rather than co-created. Mai’s role was mostly responding and confirming the counselor’s information and plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor occasionally acknowledged choice, but overall the stance was directive with many specific instructions. Mai’s ownership of the decision was not consistently reinforced in a clear MI manner.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The counselor rarely elicited motivation from Mai and relied heavily on providing reasons for change. Mai’s increased confidence appeared to come from information rather than from being guided to express her own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor did not consistently convey that Mai was the expert on her own choices and priorities. Much of the session was directive, with the counselor supplying answers, scripts, and recommendations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mai offered spontaneous change talk, but the counselor did not actively elicit, reflect, or amplify it. There were few questions that would have helped Mai articulate her own reasons, confidence, or desire to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor did not argue with Mai’s sustain talk, but did not skillfully soften or redirect it. Responses were mostly informational rather than using MI-consistent reflections or pivots toward change.
