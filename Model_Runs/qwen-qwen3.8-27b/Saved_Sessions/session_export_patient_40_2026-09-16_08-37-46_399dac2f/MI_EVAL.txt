# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment while validating Elias’s ambivalence and difficulty. Acceptance could be stronger by more explicitly affirming his worth apart from any lapse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections that capture Elias’s shame, pride, and ambivalence about stopping. Greater emotional depth would come from reflecting the pain behind statements such as “I don’t want to be the guy ruining that.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the relapse, triggers, supports, safety, and practical relapse-prevention steps. Direction would improve by more consistently connecting practical tasks back to Elias’s values and reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-created with Elias contributing the sister, walking routine, timing, and preference for shorter texts. Collaboration would be stronger if the psychiatrist more consistently invited Elias to generate the solutions before offering options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly checks what feels doable, allows Elias to set limits, and avoids pressuring him to act beyond his current capacity. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit reinforcement of his ownership and freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins by drawing out Elias’s internal reasons, strengths, and ambivalence. Evocation would improve by more fully amplifying and expanding his change talk rather than moving quickly into options and scripts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias contributes meaningfully, but the psychiatrist sometimes shifts into an expert-driven mode by offering menus of options and drafting texts. A stronger partnership stance would more consistently draw on Elias’s own wisdom before providing suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias expresses useful change talk about his daughter, walks, and desire to avoid relapse. The psychiatrist could better cultivate this by asking more importance/confidence questions and more explicitly reinforcing his reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist responds to sustain talk with normalization and nonjudgment rather than arguing against it. Softening would be stronger if more double-sided reflections were used to acknowledge ambivalence and then pivot toward change.
