# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys clear respect, nonjudgment, and unconditional positive regard, especially in validating Maria's pain and rejecting self-blame. It is not a 5 because the early future/solution-focused framing and the final multi-response message slightly weaken the warmth and steadiness of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist offers increasingly accurate reflections of Maria's exhaustion, trappedness, and ambivalence, including strong use of her own metaphors. It falls short of a 5 because the opening is more conceptual than emotionally immediate and some shame/burden feelings are not reflected with deeper precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a generally coherent arc from exploring distress to immediate safety, including an appropriate 24-hour risk assessment. Direction is limited by the early drift toward future-oriented “small steps” and a somewhat scattered sequence of questions that reduces focus on a single clear target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist generally works with Maria's stated need for presence rather than imposing solutions, and co-constructs a low-pressure next step. Collaboration is weakened by the initial premature agenda, the menu-like offering of options, and the final multiple-response format that reduces cohesion.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist strongly supports choice by avoiding persuasion, honoring Maria's request for presence, and emphasizing that she does not have to fix anything. It is not a 5 because the opening “small step” and direct safety question could more explicitly frame Maria's freedom and lack of obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist uses open-ended questions to evoke Maria's experience of the darkness, safety, and tolerable presence. Evocation is not yet strong enough because the therapist does not consistently draw out values, ability, hope, or reasons for staying alive.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonauthoritarian, collaborative stance and adapts to Maria's redefinition of help as presence rather than fixing. It is not a 5 because the opening sets a direction before fully negotiating the agenda and does not explicitly position Maria as the expert on her own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions about safety and tolerable support, and Maria does produce meaningful statements about not wanting to hurt the people she loves. The therapist does not consistently catch, reflect, amplify, or expand these change statements into stronger motivation for staying.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist avoids arguing with or escalating Maria's hopelessness and acknowledges statements like “nothing works” without confrontation. However, the session lacks consistent double-sided reflections that validate sustain talk while gently introducing the change or hope side.
