# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout; the psychiatrist normalizes Farid's experience, validates his feelings without condescension, and gently reframes his negative self-labels without reinforcing them.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sophisticated complex reflections accurately capture both cognitive and emotional dimensions (e.g., the 'cycle' reflection, the 'not enough vs. not at all' double-bind). Empathic understanding is precise and tracks Farid's emotional undercurrent without over-interpretation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on the target behavior with logical momentum building from identifying barriers to exploring the smallest step and true motivation. However, the session ends at an open point without a brief collaborative summary or forward-looking question to consolidate gains.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative dialogue where Farid's contributions substantially shape the session's direction. Inclusive language and observations offered as invitations ('I'm curious,' 'Does that land?') maintain a shared exploration rather than an expert-led interview.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Farid consistently positioned as the decision-maker and expert on his own change. The psychiatrist never imposes a plan or tells Farid what to do; all suggestions are framed as options and questions invite his own determination.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No external reasons for change are provided; every intervention is designed to draw out Farid's own motivations. The result is authentic change talk emerging entirely from within the client (e.g., 'stepping out of my head for ten minutes,' 'stop keeping score').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise and wisdom consistently located within Farid; open questions direct him toward his own answers and observations are framed as invitations rather than directives. The closing affirmation of Farid's own insight ('That's a really honest observation') exemplifies the collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and amplified progressively, from initial vague interest to specific personal motivations (fresh air, quiet, getting out of his head). The session could have gone further in explicitly developing all four DARN categories, particularly eliciting confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never argued against or dismissed; instead, the psychiatrist uses separation of competing voices, reframing ('excuse' vs. 'starting point'), and validation to help Farid see his own patterns. This is exemplary handling that maintains Farid's autonomy over his own language.
