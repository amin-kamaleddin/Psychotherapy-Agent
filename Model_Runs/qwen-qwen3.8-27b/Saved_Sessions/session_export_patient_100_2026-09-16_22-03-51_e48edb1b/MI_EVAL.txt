# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the client's concerns without shaming. A slightly formulaic style prevents this from being a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's perspective around clarity, labeling, and workload pressure. They could go deeper into the emotional weight of the client's fear and stress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session quickly shifts away from the alcohol-related referral toward grades and scheduling. The counselor does not strategically guide the discussion back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently uses a collaborative stance and frames the conversation as joint exploration. The client's preferences clearly shape the direction without being overridden.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor supports the client's agency and avoids pressure or directive advice. It is slightly weaker because the client's right to choose around alcohol use is not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections help the client generate ideas about schedule and academics. However, evocation is not strongly linked to the client's motivation to address alcohol use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites the client's input and follows their lead in a generally collaborative way. The client's role as the expert on their own life and goals could be emphasized more clearly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some change talk, but it is mainly about grades and schedule management. These statements are not directly connected to motivation for reducing alcohol-related risk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids reinforcing the client's sustain talk, such as minimizing alcohol use or resisting the referral. However, the discussion is not effectively redirected back to the alcohol-related concern.
