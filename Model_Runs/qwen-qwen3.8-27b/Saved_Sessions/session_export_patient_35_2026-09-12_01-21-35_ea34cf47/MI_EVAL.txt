# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, consistently normalizing the client’s stress and mothering concerns. Some affirmations feel slightly formulaic or evaluative rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses appropriate reflections such as acknowledging stress and conflict, but often stops at surface-level empathy. Missed opportunities to explore deeper identity, guilt, and exhaustion limit the empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking, the child’s ear infections, stress, and practical next steps. The direction is steady but more behaviorally driven than consistently MI-oriented toward ambivalence and intrinsic motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and co-constructs parts of the plan, such as the jacket step, phrase, and reset choice. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist still sets much of the agenda and supplies the main options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client’s refusal to promise quitting and emphasizes her ability to act on her own time. Some directive suggestions and prescriptive language prevent a fully optimal autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with an evocative invitation but quickly shifts to advice, options, and task selection. The client’s existing motivation around protecting her son is not consistently elicited or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites preferences and the client helps shape the final plan, but the session remains fairly clinician-directed. The client is often asked to choose from provider-generated options rather than being positioned as the primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers change-related statements about quitting and protecting her son, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or develop them. Questions remain mostly practical rather than evocative of values, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing or moralizing and reduces defensiveness by accepting the client’s readiness limits. However, sustain talk is not consistently softened with double-sided reflections or gentle pivots toward change.
