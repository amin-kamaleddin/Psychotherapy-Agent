# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard without judgment, framing protective behaviors as understandable responses (e.g., 'You're not broken for protecting yourself that way'). Warmth feels genuine and emotionally attuned rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate, nuanced reflections that move beyond surface content into emotional and relational dimensions (e.g., 'safe and stuck can feel like the same thing'). Showcases self-awareness by acknowledging a repetition and re-attuning to what was actually being communicated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session circles through helplessness, past attempts, the grey, and exhaustion without progressive deepening or a collaboratively identified focus. Lacks summarization of themes or gentle pivots toward a concrete next step or change-oriented question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses inclusive language ('we're in together,' 'walk alongside you') and shows accountability by apologizing for repeating a question. However, the overall structure and topic selection remain largely counselor-driven rather than shaped by the client's own direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook-perfect autonomy support — never pressures, advises, or declares the client's capacity for change. Consistently frames change as the client's own process, using invitations like 'What do you make of that?' rather than assertions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully uses open questions and reflective invitations to let the client generate her own insights without imposing reasons for change. Could be more systematic in drawing out DARN-CAT categories, particularly ability and confidence statements (e.g., exploring what the two-week abstinence revealed about her capacity).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames the interaction as equal and collaborative, explicitly inviting the client to set the agenda and share the pace. Slightly passive in co-creating structure — when the client responds 'I don't know' to the opening question, the psychiatrist moves forward without further negotiating a shared direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully weaves change talk into broader understanding and uses the client's own behavior (showing up) as gentle evidence of capacity. Does not fully develop or sustain the thread when the client dismisses it ('I don't think that means anything'), missing an opportunity to honor both the dismissal and the underlying evidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without argument or minimization, using double-sided reflections and the client's own behavior as subtle counterpoints. Could be more strategic by consistently pairing acknowledged helplessness with a gentle forward-looking question about what a small step might look like.
