# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, with skillful reframing of negative self-perceptions into positive language (e.g., 'That's not doing it wrong. That's you paying attention'). The psychiatrist never labels, shames, or disapproves, maintaining a warm and genuinely respectful tone even when Luis is sarcastic or defensive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy with precise reflections that capture underlying emotional logic (e.g., 'Your brain learned early that hoping is dangerous'). The psychiatrist tracks real-time shifts in emotion with remarkable nuance, building each reflection on the last with increasing depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and logical progression from goal-setting through barrier exploration to a concrete plan, maintaining focus on the target behavior throughout. The final segment could have transitioned more explicitly toward a specific action plan or follow-up step before ending.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine two-way exchange where Luis's own language and ideas consistently shape the session's direction. The goal is co-constructed rather than imposed, with the psychiatrist building on the client's metaphors and framing at every stage.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently explicit autonomy support with no pressure, advice, or implicit expectations (e.g., 'You set the terms,' 'When you're ready — not before'). The client's freedom to choose is central to every interaction, and even questions are framed as invitations rather than demands.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is elicited from within Luis rather than provided externally, with questions designed to draw out his own values and goals (e.g., 'What would you want to feel?'). The psychiatrist never introduces external reasons for change, keeping the evocation skillful, progressive, and deeply personalized.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit positioning of Luis as the expert on his own body with a clearly equal power dynamic. The psychiatrist follows Luis's lead and asks questions that build on his own language rather than taking over or lecturing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies change talk (e.g., 'You used it for two weeks. That's not nothing'), with substantial client-generated motivation throughout. Could have pushed further toward explicit readiness or confidence statements (e.g., scaling questions or 'what would it look like if you tried it?').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and validated without being reinforced, with skillful reframing of doubt as protection rather than resistance. Could have more explicitly explored the exception (the two weeks of steroid use) as direct evidence against the 'nothing ever works' narrative.
