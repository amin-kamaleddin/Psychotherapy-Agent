# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and safety-focused, but the stance is often procedural rather than deeply warm or emotionally attuned. Affirmations such as “you’re doing something brave” feel slightly premature given Liora’s ongoing distress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections capture some of Liora’s physical pain and ambivalence, but they remain largely surface-level. There is limited exploration of the underlying confusion, shame, exhaustion, or helplessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target concern: self-harm, safety, and managing the tightness before cutting. The main limitation is that the direction is more acute safety planning than broader change motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frames the work as collaborative and responds to Liora’s choices, such as quiet check-in, 5 minutes, and fist squeezing. However, the overall agenda and many strategies remain largely clinician-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly asks permission and offers choices, respecting Liora’s preferences throughout. A higher score would require stronger explicit reinforcement that Liora has ownership over the entire process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some MI-style questions, but the psychiatrist often provides strategies instead of eliciting Liora’s own coping ideas, values, and motivations. Stronger evocation would draw out what Liora already knows has helped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some partnership language and responds to Liora’s input, but the session remains uneven in its power sharing. Liora is not consistently positioned as the expert on her own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk appears, such as being tired of cutting and willing to try holding ice, but the psychiatrist does not consistently develop or amplify it. Affirmations are often generic rather than tied to Liora’s specific statements or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges that cutting feels helpful without arguing, but often responds by listing many alternatives. This can feel solution-focused rather than using reflections to soften ambivalence.
