# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and validating, respecting the client’s framing and autonomy. Less emotionally warm or explicit about shame and identity, keeping it strong but not fully attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures social conflict and practical concerns. Does not consistently explore deeper shame, fear, or identity-level emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Stays tightly focused on drinking safety, getting home, and peer pressure. Progressively narrows to concrete behavioral targets without drifting or forcing abstinence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Shares language and co-constructs practical formulations with the client. Remains somewhat therapist-guided in sequencing, with less explicit joint agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms freedom to choose, avoids pressure or lectures, and supports client-generated small steps. Does not require commitment or impose a larger treatment plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits the client’s own behavioral strategies and problem-solving insights. Does not fully evoke deeper motivation, values, self-image, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is treated as the expert, and his choices shape session direction and content. Strongly conveys that the work is his process rather than imposed by the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforces client-generated plans such as cab money, skipping the first shot, and using short answers. Does not broadly expand into values, confidence, or identity-based change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and helps reframe ambivalence, such as “car insurance for the night.” Less skillful at holding both sides of larger sustain talk, especially not wanting to be “the responsible one” and not being able to ask friends for cover.
