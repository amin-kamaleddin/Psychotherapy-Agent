# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and repeatedly validates the client’s boundaries without shaming or lecturing. Acceptance is solid but remains more procedural than deeply warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client’s surface-level statements and practical frustration. Empathy is limited in acknowledging the emotional weight, fear, and fatigue behind the client’s “bigger worries.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks a clear change-focused agenda and drifts into dental logistics and practical advice. There is no strong return to a specific target behavior or collaboratively identified change step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adapts well to the client’s preferences for brevity, autonomy, and a simple written note. However, several moments feel expert-driven, especially when offering multiple dental and habit-change options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s choices are consistently respected, including his refusal to quit or adopt new habits. Minor prescriptive moments slightly limit the otherwise strong autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses an initial open question and importance scale but does not develop the client’s own motivations further. Clear change-related cues such as wanting to look good and preferring simplicity are not explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction is generally collaborative and responsive to the client’s stated preferences. However, clinician-generated menus and repeated options can feel more like management than shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is little active effort to build or follow up on client change talk. Statements such as “I like looking good” are not used as entry points for exploring motivation or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue or pressure the client to quit, which is appropriate. However, sustain talk is not effectively softened or redirected toward possible change.
