# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful and nonjudgmental stance while validating Linda’s ambivalence and fear. Acceptance is strong, though it could be warmer with more explicit affirmation of her effort and insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate complex reflections to capture Linda’s competing concerns about asthma control and medication burden. Deeper exploration of underlying fears, such as losing control or being wrong, would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior and moves from adherence difficulty to a concrete one-week inhaler trial. There is no topic drift, and the progression is clinically relevant.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed around Linda’s own routine, concerns, and door/leash idea. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist still structures much of the session and offers options rather than fully leaving direction to Linda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Linda’s autonomy using non-coercive language, choices, and a self-defined trial. Linda is explicitly allowed to keep her long-term safety worry open while still taking a small step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits Linda’s own ideas, preferences, and potential solution. Evocation would be stronger with deeper exploration of her values, confidence, and personal meaning around asthma control.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Linda’s perspective and builds the plan around her own idea, showing a collaborative stance. The session would benefit from more explicit agenda negotiation and clearer power-sharing to reach the highest level.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some willingness, such as trying the door idea for one week, but does not sufficiently deepen or amplify Linda’s change talk. More follow-up questions about confidence, meaning, and possibility would strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Linda’s safety, dependence, and side-effect concerns without arguing or dismissing them. Softening would improve if the psychiatrist more actively helped Linda see how a small trial can coexist with unresolved long-term worries.
