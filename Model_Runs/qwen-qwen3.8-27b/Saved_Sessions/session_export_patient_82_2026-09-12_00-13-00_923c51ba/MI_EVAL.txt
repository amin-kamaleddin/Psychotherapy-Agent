# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows respect, nonjudgment, and support through affirming statements. Some rapid shifts into problem-solving slightly reduce the depth of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are appropriate reflections, but empathy often remains surface-level. The final emotional question was not addressed, weakening overall empathic quality.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on alcohol use, peer pressure, and related concerns. The direction is more behavioral problem-solving than MI-focused motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and builds on the patient's ideas, such as suggesting pizza. However, the counselor remains the primary source of plans and scripts, limiting true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges choices and offers options. Autonomy support is inconsistent because scripts and plans continue after the patient says they feel forced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides advice and scripts rather than eliciting the patient's own motivations, values, and confidence. Evocative MI questions are limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative questions, but the session becomes expert-driven. The psychiatrist positions as a problem-solver rather than an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient expresses important change talk, and the psychiatrist affirms some of it. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or expand the patient's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with sustain talk and validates concerns. The sustain talk is not actively reframed toward the patient's values or change agenda.
