# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance while avoiding moralizing or pressure for abstinence. Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic, often affirming practical choices rather than explicitly valuing the person unconditionally.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately summarize Adrian’s views and concerns but remain mostly content-focused rather than emotionally nuanced. The psychiatrist misses deeper emotions such as shame, guardedness, exhaustion, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on methamphetamine use, the recent infection, and harm-reduction next steps. Direction is mostly logistical and problem-solving-focused rather than strategically MI-based, with limited use of summaries to move through ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adrian is invited to choose options and participates in creating a concrete plan. However, the interaction remains largely clinician-led, with more didactic advice and provider-driven problem-solving than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses permission-seeking, optional language, and respects Adrian’s rejection of naloxone and preference for minimal changes. Autonomy support is strong, though repeated offers to do tasks and imply a preferred course of action could slightly shift the client into a dependent role.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with open-ended questions and asks about barriers and follow-through, eliciting some client-driven concerns. Evocation is limited because much of the session is didactic and does not deeply explore values, confidence, readiness, or stronger personal motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Adrian’s input and asks him to choose manageable options. The session is still largely driven by the psychiatrist’s agenda and expertise, making the collaboration uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited, especially around testing for contamination and avoiding use if strips cannot be obtained. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen, amplify, or strategically expand this talk using rulers, values, or DARN questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or confronting Adrian’s minimization, which helps maintain a nonadversarial stance. However, sustain talk is not actively softened with double-sided reflections or strategically redirected toward change.
