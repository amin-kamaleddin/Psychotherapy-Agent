# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard without judgment. It explicitly protects the client's values while validating the need for self-care.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The response accurately reflects the client's internal conflict, exhaustion, and fear that asking for help signals failure. The empathy is nuanced, validating, and precise.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on staying aligned with family values while reducing personal depletion. It moves from abstract commitment to a concrete, manageable question about what 'enough' looks like.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as an equal partner and invites co-construction of solutions. The client's values and experience remain central to shaping next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The response strongly supports the client's pace and avoids pressure, persuasion, or directive advice. Any change is framed as the client's own choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The questions effectively elicit the client's own motivations, values, and possible options. It is slightly less than optimal because it introduces a mild framing before the client has fully generated the language of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist adopts a collaborative, non-hierarchical stance and treats the client as the expert. The tone is respectful and genuinely partnership-oriented.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The response elicits values-based reasons for change and invites the client to consider small options. It could be stronger by more explicitly amplifying and deepening the client's existing change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The response handles sustain talk skillfully by reducing shame and moving toward a smaller, realistic target. It could be improved by more explicitly reframing the sustain talk as a protective value rather than a block to change.
