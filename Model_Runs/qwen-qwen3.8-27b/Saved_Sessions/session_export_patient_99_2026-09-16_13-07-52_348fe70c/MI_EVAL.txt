# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows consistent respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while accepting Matteo’s low concern and desire to keep the social vibe. Acceptance is solid but somewhat task-focused rather than deeply affirming of Matteo’s values or strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening to understand Matteo’s social motivations, peer teasing, and discomfort with being seen as a buzzkill. Empathy is clinically useful but remains somewhat surface-level, with less exploration of deeper ambivalence or emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on Matteo’s alcohol use and its impact on work performance and social situations. It moves smoothly from assessment to concerns, barriers, and a concrete, targeted plan for specific nights and shifts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates a low-effort plan around Matteo’s preferences, adjusting strategies based on his feedback. The interaction reflects a collaborative partnership rather than clinician-driven instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Matteo’s choice, asks permission before offering options, and respects his limits around strict counting or involving others. The plan is framed as Matteo’s own decision and self-directed action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some useful change talk, especially around Thursday fogginess and willingness to try a small change. However, the session moves quickly into options and scripts, with less deep evocation of Matteo’s values, future goals, or internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintains a collaborative stance by asking for Matteo’s input and following his lead on preferences and boundaries. However, he still provides many options and scripts before fully exploring Matteo’s own ideas, limiting a higher partnership score.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out some change talk related to work performance and the plan’s feasibility. He does not consistently amplify Matteo’s own reasons for change or transform practical statements into stronger value-based motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Matteo’s sustain talk well by acknowledging the social normality and low priority of drinking without arguing or pressuring abstinence. It would be stronger with more double-sided reflections that turn sustain talk into productive ambivalence.
