# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment, validating the client's perspective even when she pushes back defensively. Falls just short of a 5 because affirmations occasionally feel clinically precise rather than deeply personal, and could include more explicit normalization of her struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional empathic understanding with complex reflections that capture the dual nature of the client's ambivalence (e.g., the push-pull of earned rest vs. guilt). Reflections are accurate, emotionally nuanced, and progressively deepen understanding throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with a logical progression from exploration to planning without being rigid. Could be elevated with more explicit summary transitions at key points to maintain momentum rather than relying on implicit shifts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative framing with the plan clearly co-created and the client driving the content. Could be elevated with more explicit agenda negotiation at the start and more visible moments of deferring to the client's expertise over the clinician's.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout—never prescribes, never pushes beyond the client's willingness, and consistently frames choices as hers to make. Explicitly affirms her ownership of the plan and removes pressure by lowering expectations ('just the one that feels possible').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation—every insight the client articulates is drawn out through strategic questions and reflections rather than provided externally. The client discovers her own motivations, values, and solutions (e.g., the shower insight, the 'better mom' framing) entirely through the psychiatrist's evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative, non-hierarchical stance and consistently invites the client's perspective. Could be elevated with more explicit power-sharing language and more visible co-construction, as the psychiatrist occasionally takes a slightly leading role with suggested questions and metaphors.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through strategic questions that allow the client to articulate concrete benefits and generate her own plan. Could be elevated by more explicitly drawing out ability talk and confidence talk (e.g., referencing her prior success with smoking cessation).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by validating the client's perspective before gently redirecting, without arguing or dismissing. Could be strengthened with more explicit double-sided reflections that honor both sides of the ambivalence while naturally tilting toward change.
