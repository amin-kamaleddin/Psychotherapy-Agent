# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes imperfect progress by framing slips as information rather than failure. However, the warmth is somewhat procedural and does not fully acknowledge the client’s emotional ambivalence or fear of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes effort to understand the client’s perspective but mostly offers content-level summaries and approvals. Important emotional material, including surprise, fear of failure, and the role of wine in mood regulation, is underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing alcohol use and moves steadily toward a concrete, behaviorally specific plan. There is little topic drift, and the clinician effectively guides the conversation toward a measurable change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s preferences materially shape the plan, especially in choosing nights, reminder methods, and reporting format. However, the early phase is somewhat clinician-driven, with a list of options presented before the client fully explores her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy by offering choices, avoiding pressure, and framing slips as non-failing information. The plan is still quite directive and prescriptive, which slightly reduces the client’s sense of leading the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with an open-ended question and later invites choices, but the session relies heavily on clinician-provided advice rather than evoking the client’s own motivations and values. There are missed opportunities to explore confidence, meaning, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to choose parts of the plan and adapts well to her preferences. The main limitation is a somewhat expert-driven structure, especially in the early recommendation of multiple options before eliciting the client’s own perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers some spontaneous change talk and the psychiatrist reinforces it with supportive affirmations. However, the clinician does not systematically cultivate change talk through deeper exploration of desire, ability, need, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by accepting it without arguing and redirecting toward a doable, personalized reduction goal. The main limitation is that more active MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections or strategic pivots, are not fully used.
