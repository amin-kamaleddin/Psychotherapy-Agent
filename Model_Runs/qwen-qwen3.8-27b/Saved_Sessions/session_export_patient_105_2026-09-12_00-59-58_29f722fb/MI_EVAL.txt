# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect and normalizes Naomi's difficulties, such as acknowledging how hard the situation is. However, validation remains somewhat formulaic and is interrupted by a clinical, directive assessment tone, limiting deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate reflections of Naomi's experience, but they are often factual or brief rather than emotionally deep. Missed opportunities include exploring the shame behind feeling like she is faking it, the fear in being too scared to practice, and her closing self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a general focus on social anxiety and eventually narrows to sleep. However, the middle portion becomes diluted by a broad list of techniques, making the direction feel scattered until the focus tightens.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Naomi is asked to choose from pre-determined options, but the psychiatrist largely remains in an expert, instructive role. The plan is not co-constructed, and Naomi's perspective does not substantially shape the session structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers several choices and respects Naomi's selections, which supports a sense of control. Autonomy support is limited, though, because the options are clinician-generated and there is no explicit affirmation of Naomi's ownership or freedom to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The approach is primarily information-providing rather than evocative, with little exploration of Naomi's values, motivations, or own ideas. Change talk is present but not actively elicited, reflected, or deepened through MI-style questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction does not strongly position Naomi as the expert on her own life or change process. The psychiatrist drives the session through assessment, techniques, and a structured plan rather than collaborating as an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Naomi offers some change talk, but the psychiatrist rarely reflects, amplifies, or explores it. There is minimal elicitation of desire, ability, or values-based motivation, and readiness statements are not meaningfully expanded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges and normalizes some of Naomi's sustain talk, but does not use MI-specific strategies such as double-sided reflections or exploratory reframing. Sustain talk is briefly accepted and then redirected through additional information or alternatives.
