# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental validation of Sofia’s choices and self-awareness is clear. It falls short of a 5 because deeper emotional validation of self-critical or failure-related identity is limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence and the push–pull experience. Empathy remains more cognitive than emotionally deep around guilt, self-criticism, and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior and moves coherently from vague inactivity to a concrete plan. There is no meaningful topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The intervention is co-created from Sofia’s own ideas, such as shoes, jazz, brief standing, and in-the-moment recognition. The psychiatrist functions as a partner rather than an expert prescriber.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sofia’s choices are consistently framed as invitations and owned by her. Avoiding punitive tracking and honoring “permission, not a rule” strongly supports autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions elicit Sofia’s own goals, barriers, preferences, and measures of success. The clinician helps Sofia discover meaning rather than supplying reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sofia’s judgments and preferences shape the direction and content of the session. The psychiatrist avoids prescribing and consistently treats Sofia as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia’s change talk is effectively evoked and affirmed throughout. The clinician could more strategically amplify the strongest change talk with values, scaling, or commitment consolidation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers are reframed in protective, non-punitive ways rather than reinforced. More consistent use of double-sided reflections or values-based redirection would strengthen this criterion.
