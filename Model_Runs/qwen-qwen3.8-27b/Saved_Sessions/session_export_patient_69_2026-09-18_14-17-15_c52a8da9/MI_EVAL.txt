# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently demonstrates respect and a nonjudgmental stance, validating Kieran's frustration without labeling or being preachy. The warmth occasionally veers toward being slightly performative or formulaic in its affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that captures Kieran's perspective and subtle nuances, but includes one notable over-interpretation ('theoretical' as fear of wanting) that Kieran directly corrected. Empathy is generally good but not consistently precise.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session lacks clear direction toward a specific target behavior or measurable change, circling around themes without progressing toward a concrete goal. The abrupt ending with Kieran asking if he's 'good to leave' suggests the psychiatrist may not have had a clear session arc.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a collaborative stance by inviting Kieran's input, treating him as an equal partner, and accepting correction without defensiveness. Could be deeper if the psychiatrist more explicitly co-created the session agenda or ceded more control of direction to Kieran.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports autonomy with explicit statements like 'You don't have to perform here' and 'I'm not going to push you to find one today.' Could be stronger by more explicitly affirming Kieran's right to NOT want to change rather than gently inviting exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes Kieran's own motivations through questions like 'what's the thing that, if you chose it, would feel like it was actually yours?' without imposing reasons for change. Could be more consistent, as there are moments where the psychiatrist is more reflective than evocative and misses opportunities to draw out additional change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance using language like 'I'm still curious' and 'I'm here for that too,' and is willing to be corrected without clinging to authority. Partnership could be stronger with more explicit acknowledgment of Kieran's expertise in his own life and more frequent ceding of conversation direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evokes change talk (wanting to make own choices, wanting to not be unemployed for his own reason) and reflects it back, but doesn't consistently amplify or build on it further. Notably misses the opportunity to deeply explore Kieran's statement about wanting to not be unemployed 'for a reason that's actually mine.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles Kieran's dismissive responses and sustain talk with validation rather than pushback or argument, acknowledging the reality of the mandate without being defensive. Could have more skillfully used a double-sided reflection when Kieran said 'what's the point of pretending I want it to be different' to open space for change.
