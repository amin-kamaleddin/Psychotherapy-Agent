# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows basic respect and avoids direct judgment, but warmth is often formulaic and problem-focused. Statements such as 'You're doing the sensible thing' can feel subtly evaluative rather than affirming Dylan as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are present but tend to be shallow, repetitive, and procedural. Deeper concerns about shame, a record, student identity, and feeling unfairly treated are not accurately explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the conduct incident rather than drifting to unrelated topics. However, the focus is more procedural and advocacy-based than directed toward a clear MI change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supplies much of the structure, scripts, and advice, leaving limited room for Dylan to shape the agenda as an equal partner. The interaction feels more like advocacy preparation than mutual problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some invitations to choose and to clarify documents before signing, which supports autonomy around the process. However, prescriptive guidance and implied expectations can undermine Dylan’s ownership of his own values and choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial open questions are followed by heavy advice-giving, so Dylan’s own reasons, values, and motivations are not consistently drawn out. The session does not meaningfully develop client-identified motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently acts as an expert advisor, providing plans, statements, and scripts rather than partnering with Dylan on his goals. Dylan often reacts to suggestions rather than directing the session with equal input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is not systematically invited or expanded, especially around alcohol use, responsibility, values, and future goals. Dylan’s admissions are not used as a meaningful entry point for exploring change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dylan’s minimization and 'not my fault' statements are not directly challenged but also not effectively redirected toward risks, costs, or personal responsibility. Much of the session reinforces defensiveness by helping prepare a low-responsibility statement.
