# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and accepts Marisol's choices without shaming her. Acceptance is somewhat task-oriented rather than deeply warm or individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects key themes about autonomy, appearance, and not wanting a lecture. Empathy remains surface-level and does not fully explore the emotional meaning of being judged or controlled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session keeps reasonable focus on the dental referral issue without major drift. Direction becomes practical and avoidance-based rather than strategically guiding toward oral health engagement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marisol's preferences clearly shape the session and the work is largely collaborative. The psychiatrist occasionally adopts an advising role by supplying scripts, clinical criteria, and a final plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects Marisol's right to choose and avoids pressure to remove the piercing or accept risk information. Some directive problem-solving prevents a fully autonomy-centered score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist usefully elicits initial client-driven material about values, boundaries, and the desired clinical answer. Evocation remains superficial and does not develop motivation for change around oral health.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marisol to direct the session and offers options that reflect a non-expert stance. Partnership is somewhat limited when the psychiatrist provides scripted responses and a final plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is very little change talk and few questions designed to elicit reasons, importance, need, or ability for change. The session misses opportunities to evoke Marisol's own language about protecting oral health.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marisol's strong sustain talk remains dominant and is largely accommodated rather than gently redirected. The checklist response may reinforce the belief that nothing needs attention.
