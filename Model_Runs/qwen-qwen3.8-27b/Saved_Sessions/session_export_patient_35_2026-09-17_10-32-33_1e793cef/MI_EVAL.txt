# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally shows respectful, nonjudgmental regard and affirms Elena's efforts and values. Some responses are supportive but surface-level, with limited explicit repair when she feels blamed or unheard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflections capture her time pressure, unfairness, stress, and ambivalence about being seen as a bad mother. Some responses remain generic or slightly corrective, missing deeper shame, fear, or blame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays loosely related to smoking reduction, stress coping, and protecting her son's health. It frequently drifts without clearly anchoring to a specific, actionable target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language is present, such as asking for her ideas and what would make a choice feel fair. The collaboration weakens when her request for a direct medical answer is not engaged and no plan is co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly supports choice, ownership, and realistic self-direction. Autonomy support could be stronger by explicitly naming her right to choose and by providing accurate, noncoercive information.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several questions aim to draw out her own ideas, motivations, and possible solutions. The evocation is often interrupted or not deeply followed up, so client language and reasons for change are underdeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some equal stance, including acknowledgment that Elena knows her day and stress better than he does. Partnership is undermined when her direct question is not answered and the agenda feels more clinician-controlled.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts to evoke change through small-step and quick-alternative questions. Genuine change talk is limited and not consistently caught, amplified, or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is often reflected accurately rather than reinforced. The psychiatrist could more consistently use double-sided reflections, reframing, or gentle pivots to weaken the weight of her barriers.
