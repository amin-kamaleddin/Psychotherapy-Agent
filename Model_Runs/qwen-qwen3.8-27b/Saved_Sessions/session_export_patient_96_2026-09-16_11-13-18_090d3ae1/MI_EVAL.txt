# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist remained respectful, nonjudgmental, and explicitly honored Marisol’s autonomy. Affirmations such as “reasonable” and “valid” were warm but somewhat procedural.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening was adequate but mostly summarized Marisol’s stated position rather than deeper meaning. Missed opportunities to reflect the identity, independence, and fear of judgment underlying her decision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the lip piercing, dental health, and communication with the dentist. The direction was practical and on-topic, though less focused on deeper MI exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Marisol’s input and used her language, especially the final titanium question. However, the psychiatrist remained the primary source of information and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marisol’s choice was consistently respected, and she felt the psychiatrist was not pushing an agenda. Autonomy support was strong, though continued offers of scripts and advice kept it from being fully client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were some good initial questions, but the session shifted quickly into information and scripts. Limited effort was made to elicit Marisol’s own values, motivations, or reasons for protecting her dental health.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked for preferences and incorporated Marisol’s wording. The interaction became somewhat expert-driven, with repeated offers of checklists and scripts despite Marisol saying she did not need them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marisol offered change talk about keeping her teeth healthy and asking for dental checks. The psychiatrist created space for this but did not consistently amplify or deepen her own motivational language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marisol’s sustain talk was strong, and the psychiatrist supported her without arguing. The responses often reinforced the status quo rather than strategically exploring long-term dental concerns or balancing autonomy with the referral issue.
