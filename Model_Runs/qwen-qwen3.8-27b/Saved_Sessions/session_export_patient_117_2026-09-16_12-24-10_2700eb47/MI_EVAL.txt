# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepted Samuel's pace, including his choice not to leave the pack at home. Growth would come from affirming Samuel as a person and acknowledging his emotional difficulty more explicitly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflected Samuel's triggers, confidence level, and readiness, showing good content-level understanding. However, the empathy remained more cognitive than emotional, with missed opportunities to reflect fear of failure, ambivalence, and social pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior of reducing lunch-time smoking. It moved clearly from journaling review to triggers, confidence, a specific plan, and a concrete commitment to try it at 12:00.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist worked collaboratively by allowing Samuel to choose the 10-minute delay, staying inside, using water, and telling his wife simply. The psychiatrist still carried some of the planning burden by offering structured options and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Samuel's choices were consistently honored, including keeping the pack and avoiding elaborate refusal scripts. Some of the language was slightly directive, such as providing a specific journal template and suggested phrasing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evoked Samuel's own motivation through open questions, confidence scales, and planning prompts. The score is limited by times when the psychiatrist offered solutions or scripts before Samuel had fully generated them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Samuel to identify triggers, set goals, and select coping strategies, making the work feel shared. It would be stronger if more of the plan components were generated by Samuel rather than presented by the psychiatrist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist encouraged concrete client-generated change talk, such as using the timer, staying inside, and telling his wife. It would be stronger with more follow-up questions that amplify Samuel's own reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk well by accepting that Samuel was not ready to leave the pack and normalizing possible slips. Active softening could be improved with double-sided reflections that connect concerns to Samuel's existing strengths.
