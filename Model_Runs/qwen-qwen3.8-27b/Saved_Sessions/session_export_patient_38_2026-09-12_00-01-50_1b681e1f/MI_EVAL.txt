# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a nonjudgmental, validating, and respectful tone throughout the session. Some responses felt formulaic and over‑validated avoidance, preventing a higher score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were generally accurate but focused mostly on surface feelings such as fear, relief, and concern. The psychiatrist did not consistently capture the deeper ambivalence between wanting to act and wanting to avoid.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed clinically relevant and focused on contraception and safety. However, it lacked clear, purposeful momentum toward a shared, client‑owned next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used optional language and invited input, supporting some collaboration. The interaction often became clinician‑driven by repeatedly offering lists and options beyond the client’s stated pace.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supported the client’s freedom to choose and avoided direct pressure or persuasion. The main limitation was that autonomy support sometimes became overly permissive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were only a few early evocative questions, after which the session shifted mainly to information‑giving. The client’s internal motivation, values, and confidence were not consistently drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made several collaborative invitations and avoided an authoritarian stance. The agenda, however, was still largely controlled by the clinician through repeated structured options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Client change talk appeared but was rarely amplified or developed by the psychiatrist. Missed opportunities included reflecting on the scare, safety, and future confidence in choosing a method.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoided arguing against the client’s sustain talk but did not actively soften it. Statements often reinforced the status quo rather than gently opening space for change.
