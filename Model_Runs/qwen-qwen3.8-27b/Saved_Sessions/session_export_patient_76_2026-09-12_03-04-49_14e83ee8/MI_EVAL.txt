# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming, especially around sensitive self-harm content. Acceptance is somewhat procedural at times due to repeated safety checks and structured guidance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the client’s ambivalence and physical-emotional distress accurately. Empathy is strong but often remains at the surface of pain, noise, and tightness rather than deeper meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on safety, urges, and immediate coping. Direction is somewhat limited by a strong emphasis on crisis management rather than longer-term change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites input, but most of the structure and tools are therapist-led. The client remains relatively passive and does not fully co-create the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently asks permission, offers choices, and affirms the client’s agency. Support is still somewhat limited by directive lists, specific instructions, and safety-checking structure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes appropriate evocative attempts with open questions and reflections. However, the session often moves quickly into advice, techniques, and safety planning before the client’s own motivations are explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes the client and validates their judgment, but the interaction is not fully equal. The therapist continues to lead much of the session through structured coping and safety content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, deepen, or connect it to values and confidence. The session shifts quickly into coping options instead of exploring the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing the client’s sustain talk and validates its function. However, there is limited use of double-sided reflections or strategic reframing to help the client hold ambivalence.
