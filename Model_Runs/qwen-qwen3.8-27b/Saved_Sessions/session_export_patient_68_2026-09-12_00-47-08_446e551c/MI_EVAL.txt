# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Kevin’s concerns without shaming him for his injury or drinking. However, the acceptance feels somewhat procedural and moves quickly into planning before ambivalence is fully resolved.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses adequate reflections of Kevin’s surface frustrations and concerns, such as the ankle injury and worry about being singled out. The empathy remains fairly light and misses deeper themes such as embarrassment, social belonging, and identity-related ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on Kevin’s alcohol use and its connection to recovery, with a concrete plan for Thursday. However, the direction becomes somewhat overstructured and task-focused rather than consistently guided by Kevin’s values and ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some collaborative interaction, but the overall stance is more expert-driven than equal partnership. The psychiatrist frequently offers rapid options, scripts, and coping strategies, which makes Kevin sound reactive and defensive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and acknowledges Kevin’s preference, which supports autonomy to some degree. However, the continued push toward scripts, coping strategies, and action steps undermines Kevin’s sense of ownership and freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with an evocative open question but then relies more on information, risk statements, and practical options. There is limited effort to draw out Kevin’s own motivations, values, importance of change, or confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Kevin’s input and builds on some of his own ideas, showing partial partnership. However, the session remains largely clinician-led in structure, problem-solving, and solution generation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Kevin offers some change talk and agrees to a small goal, but the psychiatrist does not consistently cultivate or amplify this language. There is little use of importance rulers, confidence rulers, or exploration of Kevin’s reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Kevin’s sustain talk and acknowledges some of the difficulty around social pressure and boredom. However, the responses are not particularly MI-skillful and do not effectively turn sustain talk into a basis for change.
