# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, and supportive tone throughout. Minor limitations include some formulaic warmth and repeated recaps that slightly undercut responsiveness to the client's time boundary.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows adequate understanding of the client's need for a direct answer and practical efficiency. However, empathy remains mostly task-focused and does not deeply reflect or validate the emotional meaning of the client's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the core medication-safety question and narrows appropriately when the client sets a boundary. Direction is strong but becomes slightly overextended near the end with repeated recaps and additional offers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client's input and collaborates well on the GP call script. Overall, the session remains more clinician-led than fully collaborative, especially when repeatedly providing information and summaries.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, choice-supporting language and avoids coercion. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated directives, recaps, and continued offers after the client repeatedly signals she needs to leave.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides useful medical information but does not effectively elicit the client's own motivations, values, or reasons for change. Client-generated change talk is acknowledged more than developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are clear moments of partnership, especially when the client proposes the GP script and the psychiatrist refines it. Power-sharing is uneven overall, as the psychiatrist remains the primary source of information and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits and acknowledges several statements of intent, such as contacting the GP and using the script. These are not consistently amplified or developed using MI strategies such as exploring confidence, ability, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or amplify the client's time-boundary statements. However, there is limited use of MI-based reflections or reframes to soften sustain talk and reconnect it to the client's goals.
