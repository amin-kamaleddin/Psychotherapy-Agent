# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates the client’s ambivalence and concerns. Acceptance could be stronger with more frequent affirmations of the client’s worth independent of her choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate, nuanced empathy by reflecting both the content and emotional meaning of the client’s experience. The reflections track shifting concerns without reducing the client’s experience to a single theme.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on reducing alcohol consumption and improving contraceptive reliability. The conversation steadily narrows from general ambivalence to a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates rather than directs, using the client’s ideas to build a workable plan. The final strategy is co-created with the client rather than imposed by the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids direct advice and invites the client to identify what would feel doable and meaningful. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit statements affirming the client’s freedom to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions to draw out the client’s own motivations and values. The evocation could be deepened by exploring values, identity, and confidence more fully before moving into problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the client as the expert on her own life and social world. The session is driven by the client’s input rather than a clinician-led agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes and reinforces the client’s change talk, especially around protecting her future and staying present. It would improve with more systematic elicitation of confidence, commitment, ability, and need.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk without argument, advice, or invalidation, and reflects the client’s barriers with care. The softening could be more strategic by using more double-sided reflections that end on the change side.
