# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, warm unconditional positive regard throughout, even at Malik's most dismissive moments. No labeling, disapproval, or conditional acceptance at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly nuanced reflections that capture both content and emotional meaning, including metaphors appropriate to an adolescent. Skillfully tracks the subtle shift from defensiveness to vulnerability without pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on target behavior with a logical progressive arc from rapport to emerging contemplation. Could benefit from a brief collaborative summary at the end to tie the session together and clarify next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue format where the session is shaped by Malik's contributions rather than a predetermined agenda. Could be more transparent in explicitly negotiating priorities or inviting Malik to set the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit and consistent support for Malik's autonomy, with no implication that there is a 'right' answer or that he should stop. Skillfully validates his sense of being stuck without challenging or pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No external reasons, facts, or advice offered at any point; all movement toward contemplation comes from Malik's own words. The final change talk is entirely his own conclusion, not something the clinician led him to.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Malik as the expert on his own life and cedes direction to the client. The power dynamic is genuinely equal, with the psychiatrist functioning as a curious partner rather than an authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful strategic questions that successfully evoke change talk from a resistant adolescent. Could be stronger by more explicitly amplifying and reflecting back change talk statements when they emerge (e.g., the final 'I guess that's something').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects without confrontation, validating before pivoting. Could be more strategic with double-sided reflections or pointed discrepancy questions on peer normalization ('Everyone around me does it').
