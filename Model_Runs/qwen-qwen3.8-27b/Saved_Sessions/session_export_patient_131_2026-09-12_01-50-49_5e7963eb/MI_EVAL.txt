# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, using supportive statements such as "I hear you" and "That sounds really painful." However, the tone becomes more task-focused and controlling when emphasizing rules, scripts, and written trials, with limited affirmation of Wiremu's identity, values, and whānau connection.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses adequate reflections and captures some ambivalence, such as the pull between stability and freedom. However, the session often moves quickly to practical solutions before fully exploring deeper feelings such as shame, fear of being judged, and love for his children.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The conversation remains broadly relevant to alcohol use, violence, and family accountability, but the focus shifts across withdrawal screening, housing, scripts, safety planning, and messaging. The psychiatrist does not consistently help Wiremu narrow the work into a single prioritized target behavior and concrete change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative moments, particularly when building the text message around Wiremu's own words. However, much of the session is driven by the psychiatrist's lists, scripts, and proposed solutions rather than a shared, client-led agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids overt pressure, but repeatedly introduces scripts, rules, trials, and behavioral plans that can feel controlling. Autonomy is partially supported, though it is undermined until the psychiatrist later aligns more closely with Wiremu's preference for trust and a conversation with his mum.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some interest in Wiremu's motivations by opening with broad questions and acknowledging his efforts toward change. However, the style is largely directive, and Wiremu's own reasons, values, and confidence for change are not consistently drawn out or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for Wiremu's preferences and adapts when housing and his mum become central concerns. Nevertheless, the psychiatrist frequently remains in an expert or managing role, and the session does not consistently feel client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist occasionally affirms progress, such as "making strong moves" and "taking real steps," but rarely uses evocative questions to elicit desire, ability, reasons, or need for change. Much of the session is advice-giving and problem-solving, which limits the development of Wiremu's own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk related to freedom, space, rules, and fear of being policed, but does not consistently soften or pivot it toward change. Language around written trials, family rules, and consequences can reinforce concerns about control, with limited use of double-sided reflections.
