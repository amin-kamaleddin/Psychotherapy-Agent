# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Linda’s ambivalence without correcting her smoking. Acceptance is strong but could be warmer and more affirming of her broader worth and identity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Linda’s mixed feelings about her smoke, quiet time, and concerns about her children. Deeper empathy would more consistently address the emotional loss, fear, and identity involved in changing her routine.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session moves clearly from a broad family concern to a concrete, observable target: not smoking in front of the children and trying a five-minute porch break. Direction could be stronger by more consistently linking the plan back to Linda’s stated value of being a role model.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with Linda’s ideas shaping the session from the children’s presence to the porch plan. The psychiatrist consistently works with the client rather than giving expert advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports Linda’s choice and avoids pressure, using phrases such as “You don’t have to be all-or-nothing.” Linda is clearly supported as the decision-maker throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-generated ideas, especially the porch plan and definition of a small win. More evocation of Linda’s values, confidence, reasons, and future vision would strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda leads much of the content, and her ideas substantially guide the session. The psychiatrist could strengthen partnership by more explicitly negotiating agenda and sharing control over the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generates some change talk, including the porch idea and a possible five-minute win. More direct and consistent elicitation of desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment would strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by reflecting both Linda’s enjoyment of smoking and her concerns about her children without arguing. Stronger softening would involve more frequent pivoting toward the costs of maintaining the status quo.
