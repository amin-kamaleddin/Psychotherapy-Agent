# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout the session. They normalized Ethan's concerns and accepted his partial willingness to change without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist accurately reflected Ethan's ambivalence and social concerns. However, the empathy could be deepened by explicitly validating the emotional weight of his social anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist kept a clear, logical focus on the target behavior of alcohol use. The conversation moved steadily toward a concrete, client-generated plan without unnecessary drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist worked as an equal partner, using Ethan's language and ideas to shape the session. The final plan was jointly constructed rather than prescribed by the therapist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist consistently honored Ethan's choice and avoided coercive or directive language. Change was framed as a low-pressure experiment under the client's control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist effectively drew out Ethan's own motivations and problem-solving ideas. Deeper evocative tools, such as rulers or values exploration, could further strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist clearly positioned Ethan as the expert on his own life and experience. They avoided imposing an agenda and instead followed the client's lead throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist elicited strong, client-generated change talk and practical alternatives. The change talk could be further deepened with additional MI-evocative follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist effectively normalized and softened Ethan's sustain talk without argumentation. More strategic double-sided reflections could help pivot sustain talk into change talk more consistently.
