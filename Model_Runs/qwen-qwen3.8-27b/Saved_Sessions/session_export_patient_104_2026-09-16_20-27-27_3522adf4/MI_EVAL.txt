# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates nonjudgmental support, normalizes exhaustion, and validates Nadine’s attentive monitoring of her baby. It falls short of a 5 because the warmth remains somewhat task-focused and does not fully affirm her worth as a parent beyond performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures both her cognitive worry and emotional strain. The reflections could go deeper by more fully acknowledging feelings of being behind other parents, fear of hurting the baby, and cumulative new-parent exhaustion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on a specific concern—normal infant sleep/feeding and reducing worry—and moves toward a simpler check. Pacing becomes slightly circular near the end, and there is limited explicit movement toward a concrete next step or broader plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses Nadine’s answers to shape the next step and invites joint problem-solving. It is somewhat clinician-structured, with the psychiatrist providing more options and framing rather than the client fully co-creating the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking for preferences, offering options, and framing permission to let some nights be imperfect. It could be stronger by more explicitly emphasizing that decisions and actions belong to Nadine and by consolidating her internal trust.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits client-generated language such as “latch, a quick listen, then rest” and “good enough.” It could be stronger by more deeply evoking values, identity, confidence, or internal motivation rather than relying mainly on reassurance and information.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and allows Nadine’s preferences to guide the session. It falls short of a 5 because the conversation is still structured with multiple-choice questions and clinician-led reframes rather than more overt power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session elicits meaningful change talk, including “permission to let it be imperfect” and “good enough is actually okay.” It could be stronger by amplifying, summarizing, or deepening this change talk with confidence, importance, commitment, or values-based language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Nadine’s worry and softens sustain talk through normalization and reframing. It could be improved by using more skillful MI moves such as double-sided reflections or turning fear itself into motivation for change.
