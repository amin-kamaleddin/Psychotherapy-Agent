# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Lukas’s discomfort and ambivalence. Language is generally supportive, though occasionally slightly interpretive or subtly judgmental, such as referring to the client as “defending the flow.”

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate, perspective-taking reflections and captures much of Lukas’s central tension between identity, social ease, and practical costs. However, some reflections stay closer to cognitive/behavioral content and do not fully deepen into the emotional weight, exhaustion, fear, or shame behind Lukas’s statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear focus on methamphetamine use and its practical consequences, moving toward a concrete, actionable plan around protecting rent. The main limitation is that the focus becomes somewhat narrow on money management without consistently tying that step back to broader reduction in use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally behaves as a collaborator, using Lukas’s language and building on his ideas rather than imposing an external agenda. However, occasional rapid consecutive questions and limited explicit checks on the client’s preferred direction make the collaboration feel slightly therapist-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a major strength, with consistent reinforcement of Lukas’s freedom, ownership, and right to choose. The psychiatrist repeatedly invites him to define the plan in a way that feels authentic, self-directed, and not like punishment or compromise.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Lukas’s own values, motivations, and change talk rather than giving advice or arguing for change. The evocation could go deeper by exploring identity, values, confidence, and self-efficacy more explicitly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites Lukas to define the focus, creating a largely shared sense of the session. Some limits remain, especially when multiple questions are asked in succession or a structured multiple-choice question subtly narrows the client’s agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses skillful questions and affirmations to elicit meaningful change talk, such as protecting rent, staying in on Thursday, and framing the plan as a tactical move for auditions. To reach a higher level, the therapist could more systematically deepen confidence, ability, and past success language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by avoiding confrontation and using double-sided language that preserves identity and autonomy. The main limitation is that deeper sustain talk around belonging, identity, and fear of losing place in the scene is not fully explored or redirected toward change.
