# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful, and accepting stance toward a resistant adolescent. However, the acceptance becomes somewhat formulaic and repetitive rather than fully individualized and warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides generally accurate reflections of Amina's frustration, annoyance, and sense of being forced to attend. The empathy remains mostly surface-level and does not fully capture her ambivalence or more complex emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with relevant focus on the presenting concern, but drifts toward logistics, safety, and ending the interaction. There is limited sustained progression toward a concrete change-related goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses options, preferences, and a signal agreement to collaborate with Amina. However, the session remains largely therapist-structured rather than fully co-created with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly affirms Amina's right to choose, avoids coercion, and respects her refusal throughout. Autonomy support is explicit and consistently maintained, especially through preference-based language and controlled options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open-ended questions and invites Amina to identify what is difficult, helpful, or changeable. The evocation remains superficial and does not effectively draw out deeper values, hopes, or ambivalence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys that Amina is in charge and repeatedly offers choices, which supports a partnership stance. The collaboration is solid but not deep enough to fully center Amina as an equal co-creator of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few early change-oriented questions, but the psychiatrist largely stops trying to evoke change language after resistance. The session does not use strategic reflections, scaling, values, or future-oriented questions to generate client change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Amina's sustain talk and maintains a respectful, non-confrontational stance. However, the softening is mostly acceptance rather than active use of double-sided reflections or ambivalence-based reframing.
