# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgmental positive regard and normalization of hesitation. The psychiatrist affirms self-knowledge and coping without pressure or blame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking on ambivalence. Could go deeper into underlying emotions such as exhaustion, fear of failure, or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior of small community engagement. Each interaction refines a concrete, low-demand action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is collaborative in practice because the client's ideas shape the work. Equal-partner language and explicit joint planning could be stronger.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforces choice, control, and the absence of obligation throughout. The client defines limits, exit cues, and what counts as a win.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits the client's own reasons, safety needs, and criteria for success. Motivation is drawn from the client rather than imposed externally.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the expert in her own life and experience. More explicit agenda negotiation would strengthen power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions effectively generate client language for action and are appropriately affirmed. Deeper exploration of importance, confidence, and values could be added.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is normalized and reframed without challenge or debate. The response could more actively pivot toward the client's competing desire for connection.
