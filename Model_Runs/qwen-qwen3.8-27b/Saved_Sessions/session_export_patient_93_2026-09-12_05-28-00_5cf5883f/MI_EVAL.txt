# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Margaret’s ambivalence without pushing abstinence. It falls short of 5 because some moments, such as her feeling old after the fall or fearing being stared at, are not fully emotionally affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are strong reflections of ambivalence and accurate summaries, especially later in the session. However, empathy is inconsistent because several important feelings are not deeply reflected before moving to advice and planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on reducing alcohol use and stays on track from ambivalence to a concrete Tuesday plan. Topic drift is minimal, and the progression is logical and well structured.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a collaborative style by inviting input, co-constructing a plan, and incorporating Margaret’s own ideas. It does not reach 5 because the therapist often leads with multiple preformed options rather than fully client-generated solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions are framed as Margaret’s choice, and the psychiatrist respects her preference to use her own wording. Some of the suggested scripts and monitoring plans can feel subtly directive for a client sensitive to being controlled.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and prompts help Margaret articulate confidence and a small step. However, evocation is limited because the session shifts quickly into problem-solving without deeper exploration of values, independence, or discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks permission, offers choices, and shares control in building the plan. Partnership is reduced by frequent provision of structured options and scripts, making the session more expert-driven than truly collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret produces clear change talk, including desire and ability to try Tuesday, and the psychiatrist affirms her progress. The change talk remains surface-level because deeper reasons, values, or identity are not further developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Margaret’s desire to keep drinking and frames goals as “being a bit careful” rather than abstinence. However, there are few double-sided reflections, and some practical suggestions subtly reinforce a controlling or punitive framing.
