# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard is consistently present without judgment or dismissiveness. The psychiatrist validates her symptoms, fear, and frustration while maintaining acceptance of her perspective.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and captures both medical and relational concerns. It could be deeper by exploring stronger underlying emotions such as fear, grief, or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on practical next steps and the client’s request for proof. However, the MI target behavior and change goal are not clearly established or consistently developed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is co-constructed using the client’s ideas, scan, diary, referral, and wording. Collaboration is strong, though the psychiatrist still introduces and structures parts of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is invited to choose and shape preferences without direct pressure or argument. Autonomy support is good but could be strengthened by more explicitly reaffirming client control over next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflective expansion help the client generate her own message. Evocation is strong but remains focused mainly on the medical request rather than broader motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A collaborative, non-expert stance is generally conveyed through inclusive language and invitations for input. Partnership is good but slightly weakened by introducing a specific plan early.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client produces useful change-related language about getting her life back and stopping fear. The psychiatrist reflects some of this but does not systematically amplify or deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client’s need for proof, tests, and distrust. Sustain talk remains dominant because the clinician does not strategically pivot or use double-sided reflections.
