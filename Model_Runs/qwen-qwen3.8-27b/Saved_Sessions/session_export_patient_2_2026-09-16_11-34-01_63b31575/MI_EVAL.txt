# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and validates Aisha’s busy responsibilities, but does not consistently affirm her right to set boundaries or maintain current behavior. Some praise sounds evaluative, which slightly weakens unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Aisha’s practical burdens and motivation reasonably well, but the reflections remain mostly content-level. Deeper emotional experiences such as exhaustion, ambivalence, and the meaning of the evening cigarette are not fully captured.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on reducing evening smoking and a measurable goal of cutting from five or six to about three. The psychiatrist avoids unnecessary topic drift, though the session becomes heavily action-planning oriented.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha contributes to the final plan by choosing hiding the pack, using a lozenge, and keeping the reminder simple. However, the psychiatrist introduces most strategies and scripts before fully eliciting Aisha’s own ideas, making the collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for Aisha’s preferences and respects her boundary about alcohol, supporting autonomy to some extent. Repeated strategy suggestions and multiple “ready to try this week?” questions can feel directive rather than fully autonomy-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and helps Aisha raise her confidence from 5 to 6. However, evocation is limited because the session quickly shifts into practical advice and planning rather than deeply drawing out Aisha’s motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Aisha’s input and allows her to select components of the plan. Still, the rapid introduction of expert strategies makes the interaction feel more counselor-driven than a true co-exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha expresses willingness to reduce evening smoking and identifies meaningful reasons tied to health and her son. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk before moving into practical problem-solving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directly challenging Aisha’s minimization and does not push the alcohol topic. There is, however, limited use of double-sided reflections or strategic redirection to soften sustain talk such as the evening cigarette being “my only break.”
