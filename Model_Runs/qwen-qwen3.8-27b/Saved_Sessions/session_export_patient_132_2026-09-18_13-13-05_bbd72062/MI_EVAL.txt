# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, never labeling, shaming, or minimizing. The psychiatrist holds both the client's strengths and self-doubt simultaneously without collapsing into either, and the warmth evolves naturally with the conversation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate and layered reflections that capture both content and emotional meaning, progressively building depth. The psychiatrist effectively uses the client's own metaphors ('character reference,' 'damage control mode') back to her, demonstrating genuine attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear logical progression from identifying the cycle to building an alternative framework for 'doing okay' without the scale. The target behavior is somewhat implicitly held in the middle-to-late portion and could benefit from occasional explicit naming to reinforce forward momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely equal power dynamic with the client's contributions substantially shaping the session direction. The psychiatrist adapts in real-time to what Yasmin offers (her metaphors, distinctions, and framings) rather than following a predetermined agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the client's ownership and right to choose, never using prescriptive language like 'you should' or 'you need to.' Autonomy support is woven throughout rather than stated once, and the psychiatrist stays with the client's fear rather than reassuring in a way that takes ownership away.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** All change talk comes from the client, elicited through well-paced open questions and strategic reflections. Could go one layer deeper in evoking confidence and self-efficacy language (e.g., 'What's something you've already done that shows you're capable?').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise and wisdom are consistently placed within the client through language like 'I'm wondering with you' and 'what would it look like to you?' The psychiatrist explicitly rejects the expert-authority role ('I'm not going to tell you it's fluffy') and follows the client's lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently evokes and amplifies change talk with well-timed open questions and reflections. Could more explicitly label change talk types (desire, ability, need, commitment) and evoke additional confidence/ability language to help the client recognize her own progress.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with care by validating without amplifying, never arguing against or minimizing the client's expressed needs. The final sustain talk instance (damage control mode: cutting meals, extra workouts) is left unaddressed as the session ends, representing a missed opportunity for softening.
