# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, using normalizing language and affirmations. Acceptance is generally strong but could include more unconditional positive regard for Lukas as a whole person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence and emotional conflict, such as wanting to belong while protecting the future. The empathy could be deeper and more sustained when Lukas expresses fear of being alone or boring.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on early alcohol use, peer pressure, and identity, with a clear arc toward a practical plan. A minor repeated prompt does not undermine the coherent, change-oriented direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses reflections, summaries, and open questions to build the plan from Lukas’s ideas rather than prescribing solutions. Explicit shared ownership language, such as “we” or “let’s figure this out together,” is limited but the stance is still collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lukas generates the plan and the counselor avoids pressure, advice-giving, or imposed abstinence. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit reminders that Lukas is free to choose and owns the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor evokes Lukas’s own values, goals, and solutions instead of giving external reasons to change. Deeper evocation of confidence, commitment, and the importance of change would move this toward expert level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Lukas as the expert, using client-centered questions that allow him to shape priorities and the final plan. This strong power-sharing supports a top score.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently elicits meaningful change talk, including desire, reasons, ability, and plan language. It could further develop confidence and commitment through more explicit evocation of future self and self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument or dismissal and is redirected toward belonging without drinking. More sophisticated moves, such as double-sided reflections, could more actively soften the peer-pressure tension.
