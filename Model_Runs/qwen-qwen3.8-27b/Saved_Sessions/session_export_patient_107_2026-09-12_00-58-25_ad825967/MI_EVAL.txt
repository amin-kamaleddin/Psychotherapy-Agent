# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally nonjudgmental and accepts Nathaniel's preference for tracking, using supportive phrases such as 'that sounds reasonable.' However, the acceptance feels more task-oriented and compliance-focused than genuinely warm, validating, or affirming of his ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mainly reflects factual or behavioral content rather than Nathaniel's underlying feelings, values, or ambivalence. Responses such as 'that sounds reasonable' miss the emotional meaning and identity value attached to coffee.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on coffee use but often drifts into practical monitoring, safety, and checklist instructions. There is limited strategic progression toward exploring Nathaniel's motivation, values, or readiness for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates some of Nathaniel's preferences, such as using a simpler jitter descriptor and tracking independently. However, the psychiatrist remains the main driver of the structure, templates, and monitoring variables.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some respect for Nathaniel's choice, including options to track or reduce and a reminder-free plan. Nonetheless, the rapid offering of multiple behavioral recommendations and checklist additions can reduce the sense of client-led autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes limited effort to draw out Nathaniel's own reasons, values, and motivation for change. The conversation quickly shifts to advice, monitoring templates, and external guidelines rather than evoking intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although the psychiatrist invites some input, the interaction is more expert-driven than partnership-based. Nathaniel is not consistently positioned as the primary expert on his own routine, values, and capacity for change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nathaniel mentions some change-relevant concerns, such as jitters and blood pressure, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen this language. The session lacks follow-up questions or importance/confidence probes that would strengthen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not directly reinforce sustain talk, but responses rarely shift the conversation away from it in an MI-consistent way. Nathaniel's statements about protecting his routine are accepted neutrally rather than explored through double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancy work.
