# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, with repeated validation of Liyana’s experience. It falls short of a 5 because the acceptance feels somewhat formulaic and does not fully affirm her worth independent of results or performance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are generally accurate and capture fatigue, isolation, and fear of failure. They become somewhat repetitive and miss opportunities to deepen the emotional core around body shame, feeling invisible, and self-worth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the presenting concern and moves toward a concrete, time-limited check-in. The focus is somewhat narrow, as emotional discouragement and lack of support receive less sustained attention than exercise planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language and the therapist adjusts the plan to Liyana’s constraints. However, most of the structure and solution is therapist-generated rather than truly co-created with Liyana.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by inviting Liyana to choose doable options and by allowing her to skip the scale. This is undercut by the highly prescriptive exercise plan, which can feel directive for a client afraid of doing it wrong.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with appropriate open questions and elicits some of Liyana’s own experience. After that, the therapist shifts toward advice and planning, with insufficient exploration of Liyana’s internal motivations, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and follows Liyana’s lead in several moments. However, the session becomes expert-led problem-solving rather than a fully shared exploration in which Liyana’s ideas and preferences shape the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Liyana offers some change talk, such as willingness to try a one-week trial and track energy and clothes fit. The therapist does not consistently deepen or amplify her own reasons, values, or strengths.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged respectfully rather than challenged, which is appropriate. However, the fear of wasting time, failing, or being tricked remains central, and the therapist does not consistently use double-sided reflections to pivot toward change.
