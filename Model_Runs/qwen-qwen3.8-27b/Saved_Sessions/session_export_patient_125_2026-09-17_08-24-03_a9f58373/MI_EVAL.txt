# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows respect, affirmations, and validation of Tariq's efforts and feelings. Some compliance-oriented framing introduces slight pressure, preventing a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture Tariq's frustration, sense of unfairness, and fear of being misunderstood. Some reflections remain somewhat formulaic and do not fully explore deeper emotions such as shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the current issue of release, fairness, and family connection, but the direction can feel circular. It would benefit from more collaboratively narrowing the focus toward a specific coping or behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Tariq's input, but repeated structured options can feel more like problem-solving than genuine co-creation. Tariq's resistance suggests the agenda was not always fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options and questions show respect for Tariq's perspective, but explicit affirmation of his right to choose and freedom from pressure is limited. The repeated focus on protecting release may create a subtle expectation of a preferred path.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some of Tariq's own goals, values, and strengths, such as wanting to keep his head down and be ready for release. The evocation is not fully developed, especially around deeper motivations, confidence, and personal values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are clear attempts at partnership through questions and shared decision-making, but the psychiatrist still steers the conversation with recurring options. More fluid following of Tariq's stated preferences would strengthen the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Tariq to identify what would help and affirms some change-related language. However, the session does not consistently deepen Tariq's own statements of desire, ability, reasons, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not endorsed, but the psychiatrist does not actively soften it or help Tariq see its function. The conversation often remains centered on the officer and fairness rather than redirecting toward Tariq's agency and capacity to stay on track.
