# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, though the warmth becomes somewhat formulaic. A top score would require more personalized acceptance of the fear behind the ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence and social fear, but they remain somewhat surface-level. Stronger empathy would include deeper, layered reflections on loneliness, shame, and fear of being left out.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on concrete social-smoking steps and maintains momentum with confidence scaling. It could end more strongly by consolidating motivation and closing with a client-owned commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client actively revises the text and chooses responses, creating genuine collaboration. The interaction is stronger if more of the plan begins with client-generated ideas rather than counselor-supplied options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice-focused questions and low-pressure framing respect the client’s autonomy. However, the repeated provision of scripts and plans subtly limits her opportunity to generate her own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some prompts draw out health-related motivation and self-efficacy, but the session relies more on problem-solving than evocation. More time would be needed to explore the client’s own values, identity, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elise clearly shapes the session’s plan, indicating a collaborative stance. However, the psychiatrist frequently supplies scripts and options, reducing the client’s role as the primary source of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence scaling and future-focused questions elicit some client change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or connect it to values. The client’s emerging motivation remains underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the social value of smoking without arguing against it, but does not fully redirect sustain talk into change-oriented language. An unresolved fear about being teased is left unreframed.
