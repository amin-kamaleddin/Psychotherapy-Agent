# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and repeatedly affirmed the client’s autonomy. However, acceptance was limited by occasional alignment with the client’s dismissal of the referral and by offering prescriptive scripts rather than remaining purely exploratory.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed some surface-level validation and perspective-taking regarding the dentist interaction. However, the empathy was shallow and missed deeper themes such as ambivalence, identity, fear of judgment, and the emotional significance of the referral.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session began with the referral concern but quickly narrowed to a boundary issue and then drifted into casual conversation. There was no sustained focus on the target behavior or re-anchoring to the original purpose of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and offered options, supporting some collaboration. However, the interaction became uneven because the therapist frequently supplied scripts, suggestions, and a closing safety framework, leaving the client’s ideas less central to the process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently acknowledged the client’s right to choose and avoided pressuring her to change. Autonomy support was somewhat limited by the therapist’s provision of specific scripts and suggestions that could subtly steer the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial open questions did not lead to eliciting the client’s own values, goals, ambivalence, or reasons for change. The psychiatrist instead provided answers, scripts, and suggestions rather than drawing out internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some collaborative moves, such as asking what the client wanted from the session and offering options. However, the therapist’s frequent provision of solutions and scripts made the interaction more counselor-directed than truly collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made little to no effort to evoke change talk or explore the client’s values, oral health goals, or possible reasons for reconsidering the piercing. The client remained in pre-contemplation without any change talk being elicited, reflected, or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided direct arguing, which is appropriate, but did not effectively soften or redirect the client’s sustain talk. Repeating the “my choice” script reinforced the status quo framing and limited exploration of ambivalence.
