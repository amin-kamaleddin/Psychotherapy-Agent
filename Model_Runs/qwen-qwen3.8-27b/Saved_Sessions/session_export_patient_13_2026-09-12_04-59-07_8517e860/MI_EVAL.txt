# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses nonjudgmental, validating language and avoids minimizing the patient's concerns. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic, with repeated phrases that limit the depth of genuine positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the patient's fear, overwhelm, and ambivalence. However, empathy is often followed quickly by problem-solving, making emotional attunement feel more transactional than deeply connected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, logical focus on helping the patient identify a concrete next step for seeking care. The conversation progresses steadily from exploration to a practical, time-specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and invites input, but the patient's role is often limited to selecting from pre-made solutions. True collaboration would involve more co-creation and greater use of the patient's own ideas and context.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the patient's choice by offering multiple options and avoiding direct pressure to act. Autonomy support is somewhat reduced by the prescriptive nature of the suggested scripts and specific timing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions early in the session successfully elicit the patient's concerns and motivations. Later, the psychiatrist shifts toward practical problem-solving, with fewer opportunities to draw out deeper internal change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates some partnership by asking for preferences and offering choices. However, the psychiatrist does much of the cognitive work, creating an imbalanced dynamic rather than a fully collaborative exchange.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient expresses some change talk, including identifying a phone call as doable and agreeing to a reminder. The psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or reinforce this change talk to strengthen the patient's sense of agency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient's sustain talk is acknowledged, but not actively worked through with MI-specific strategies. The psychiatrist accepts statements like not wanting to make a fuss at face value rather than exploring the other side of the ambivalence.
