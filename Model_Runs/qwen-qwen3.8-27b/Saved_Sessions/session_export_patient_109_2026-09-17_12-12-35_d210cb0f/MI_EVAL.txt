**Comments:** Clear logical progression from exploring barriers to identifying cognitive patterns to practical solution-finding, with each question building on the last. Lacks explicit summarization or clearer articulation of next steps that would push this to a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as a joint exploration rather than an assessment, with Rafael's contributions (his Tuesday, the guilt pattern, the office gym) driving the direction of the conversation. The psychiatrist never interrupts, redirects, or imposes a clinical framework.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supports Rafael's autonomy, from the opening 'I'm not here to tell you what to do' to framing all suggestions as exploratory rather than prescriptive. The psychiatrist never imposes a plan and consistently positions choices as Rafael's own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is elicited from within Rafael rather than provided externally — he generates his own solutions, identifies his own patterns, and articulates his own motivations throughout. The psychiatrist never provides external reasons for change, making this a hallmark example of skilled evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Rafael as the expert on his own life, using genuinely curious questions and explicitly attributing insights to him (e.g., 'you made it yourself'). The session is shaped by Rafael's contributions rather than the psychiatrist's agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively cultivates change talk through strategic questioning that leads Rafael to generate his own solutions, and explicitly highlights the shift in his language. Could go further in eliciting commitment language, confidence ratings, or specific action planning to reach a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged, validated, and then gently redirected without arguing or dismissing — for example, separating 'it might not work' from 'I might not follow through' as two distinct concerns. The psychiatrist handles barriers like the office gym feeling 'too small' with the same pattern of validation and reframing.
