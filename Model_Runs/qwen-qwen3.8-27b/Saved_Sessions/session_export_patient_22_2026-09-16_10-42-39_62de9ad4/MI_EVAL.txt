# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Camila’s willingness to make changes. However, acceptance is somewhat task-focused and does not deeply affirm her emotional struggle or ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately summarizes the impact of drinking. Deeper emotional undercurrents such as shame, social anxiety, and fear of rejection are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains strongly focused on reducing weekday alcohol use and building a concrete harm-reduction plan. The psychiatrist consistently helps make the goal specific, measurable, and actionable.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session reflects strong co-construction of a practical plan, with Camila selecting days, reminders, scripts, and tactics. The collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist brings much of the expertise and solutions early.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking what feels doable and respecting her decision not to quit completely. However, the rapid delivery of many suggestions can subtly shift the process toward following the clinician’s plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks some open questions and attempts to explore motivation, but evocation is relatively superficial. There are missed opportunities to draw out Camila’s values, intrinsic motivations, and confidence in change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Camila to choose and allows her to shape the plan, showing genuine power-sharing. The limitation is that the clinician provides a large menu of tactics before fully exploring Camila’s own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some attempts to elicit change talk by asking what she would like to change and what success might feel like. However, the session quickly moves into planning without consistently amplifying her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not challenged or reinforced, which is appropriate. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently explore ambivalence or gently turn her resistant thoughts toward change.
