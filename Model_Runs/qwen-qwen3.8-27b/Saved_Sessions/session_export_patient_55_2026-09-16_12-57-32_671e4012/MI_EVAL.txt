# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and validates the client's reluctance without shaming or arguing. The warmth is somewhat procedural and could more deeply affirm the client's identity, strengths, and emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects the client's content, sense of responsibility, and desire to avoid making a big fuss. The empathy is somewhat surface-level and misses deeper emotional themes such as independence, fear, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session begins with a clear focus on alcohol risk and safety, but the focus becomes inconsistent after the client resists planning. The conversation drifts into academic and music advice without returning to the core alcohol-related ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses collaborative language and offers smaller options, but the content remains largely counselor-driven through predefined plans. The client's own ideas and preferences do not fully shape the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor strongly supports autonomy by asking permission, offering a 'do nothing' option, and ultimately respecting the client's choice. The repeated offering of alternative plans after clear resistance may create subtle pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits some change-relevant statements about limits, responsibility, and goals, but does not consistently develop them into stronger change talk. The session shifts too quickly into planning and advice rather than building on the client's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and avoids a fully authoritarian stance, but often presents pre-made options and plans. A stronger partnership would have allowed the client's priorities to shape the conversation more fully.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes some change talk around responsibility and goals, but does not consistently amplify or expand it. Missed opportunities to explore the client's values and confidence limit the impact of the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoids directly challenging sustain talk, but repeatedly offers smaller versions of the same safety plan, which can reinforce the client's defensive stance. More effective use of double-sided reflections would help soften and redirect sustain talk.
