# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates nonjudgmental respect and treats Rosa’s ambivalence as understandable. Acceptance is strong but occasionally formulaic, limiting it to a 4.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections to capture Rosa’s conflict between health concerns and identity. Empathy is solid but sometimes stays conceptual rather than deeply exploring emotional pain and financial stress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session keeps a clear focus on smoking cessation and gradually narrows toward a small, concrete behavior. The main limitation is that some steps are not explicitly tied back to reducing smoking or a clear next action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open questions, builds on Rosa’s suggestions, and treats her as the expert on her own life. It falls short of a 5 because the psychiatrist still structures the session rather than fully co-constructing the next direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Rosa’s choice, ownership, and self-directed experimentation without coercive language. It would benefit from more consistently affirming her right to choose both quitting and not quitting, especially around setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws out Rosa’s own motivations, values, and ideas rather than giving advice. Evocation is effective but sometimes repetitive and does not fully develop change talk into stronger statements of desire, ability, need, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids an expert-driven stance and invites Rosa’s perspective throughout the session. Partnership is strong, though the psychiatrist could more explicitly negotiate agenda and share power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits Rosa’s own change language, such as identifying the “other me” and choosing to ignore the urge. These statements are not strongly amplified, consolidated, or used to build confidence, preventing a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the reality of weight, hunger, mood, and cost concerns. The session remains centered on sustain talk without consistently reframing these concerns or moving toward workable alternatives that resolve ambivalence.
