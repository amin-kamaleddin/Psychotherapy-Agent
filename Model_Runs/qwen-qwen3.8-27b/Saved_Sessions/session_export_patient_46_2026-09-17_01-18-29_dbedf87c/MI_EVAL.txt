# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance while acknowledging Ethan's ambivalence. Acceptance could be stronger with more individualized affirmation and less generic phrasing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections generally captured Ethan's tension between fitting in and avoiding negative consequences. Empathy was uneven, especially when responding to emotionally rich disclosures with a generic prompt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol use, its consequences, and practical alternatives. Direction could improve by more consistently guiding the conversation toward a specific change goal or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used Ethan's own examples and invited his perspective, creating a collaborative tone. Collaboration could be stronger through more explicit agenda-sharing and less provider-led steering.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions and noncoercive language supported Ethan's role as the decision-maker. Autonomy support would be stronger with clearer statements about his freedom to choose and more balanced exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used open-ended questions to help Ethan generate alternatives and reasons for change. Evocation was sometimes shallow, especially when key change talk was met with a generic response.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally followed Ethan's lead and incorporated his ideas into the conversation. Partnership could improve with more explicit negotiation of the session agenda and shared control.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ethan expressed several change-relevant ideas, and the psychiatrist explored alternatives with him. The psychiatrist did not consistently amplify, reflect back, or deepen the change talk to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without direct challenge, and some reflections held both sides of the ambivalence. The psychiatrist could more strategically redirect sustain talk toward change using stronger double-sided reflections.
