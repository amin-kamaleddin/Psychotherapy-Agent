# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and clearly accepts Isabella’s choices. Some statements remain procedural or mildly normative, preventing a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist gives accurate reflections of Isabella’s ambivalence, frustration, and need for nonjudgmental support. The reflections could be deeper, especially around family meaning, parenting anxiety, and feeling scolded.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays largely focused on alcohol use and moves toward a practical next step. Momentum is occasionally diluted by repeated options and broader themes of judgment and normalcy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works collaboratively by asking preferences and co-creating a half-page summary with partner scripts. At times, the amount of information and scripted language makes the interaction feel more clinician-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a clear strength, with consistent language such as “your call,” “I’ll respect your choices,” and no pressure to stop drinking. The partner script is offered as a way to reduce conflict, not as a directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks several useful evocative questions, but the session relies more on information-giving and problem-solving. Deeper value-based, future-oriented, or confidence-based questions are needed to develop Isabella’s motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Isabella is generally treated as the decision-maker, and her preferences clearly shape the session. The psychiatrist could strengthen partnership by more explicitly positioning Isabella as the expert on her own life and reducing clinician-led option menus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Isabella produces meaningful change talk, such as wanting a healthy baby and deciding together with her partner. The psychiatrist could do more to amplify, reflect, and build on this change talk with value-based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing Isabella’s sustain talk and supports her autonomy. However, it is not consistently softened with double-sided reflections or linked to her core values.
