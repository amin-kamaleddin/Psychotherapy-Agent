# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes the patient's struggle and avoids judgment about smoking, drinking, or adherence. This conveys unconditional positive regard and reduces the patient's fear of being labeled.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the patient's ambivalence, practical constraints, and concern about feeling overwhelmed or blamed. The patient's emotional meaning is clearly heard and understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Despite a broad referral, the session stays focused on nighttime asthma triggers and a simple monitoring plan. Each question moves the conversation toward a concrete, manageable next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works with the patient as an equal partner rather than prescribing a plan. The final plan is built from the patient's life, ideas, and practical constraints.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasizes that the patient is in charge of his choices. The plan is framed as a voluntary, doable experiment rather than an obligation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully elicits the patient's own goals, reasons, and practical solutions. The patient generates most of the change plan, including the fridge note and the keys-and-wash-up cue.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the patient as the expert on his own life and preferences. The session is shaped by the patient's input rather than expert-driven advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and strengthening reflections to draw out the patient's own motivation and ideas. The patient's change talk is supported and expanded rather than replaced by advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist rolls with the patient's sustain talk by affirming that he is not ready for a full quit. Sustain talk is reframed non-punitively into a small, feasible experiment, reducing defensiveness.
