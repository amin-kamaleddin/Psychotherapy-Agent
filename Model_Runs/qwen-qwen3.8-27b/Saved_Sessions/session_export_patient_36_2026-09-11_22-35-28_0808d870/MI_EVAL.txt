# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with supportive language throughout. It falls short of a 5 because some warmth feels formulaic and the deeper conflict is not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes a reasonable effort to understand Elena’s perspective, but reflections remain largely surface-level. Empathic depth is limited because the clinician often shifts too quickly to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on a small, realistic self-care practice at the end of workdays. It is slightly weakened by a repetitive close and added implementation tips.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist incorporates Elena’s input, but the interaction remains more advisor-client than fully collaborative. The clinician provides multiple options and tips, which keeps the process somewhat clinician-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena is invited to choose, set her own reminder phrase, define what counts as a win, and keep the plan flexible. Some directive language, such as “commit” and “non-negotiable,” introduces mild advisory pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some evocative questions to draw out motivation, such as which priority matters most and what would count as a win. However, evocation is often shallow and quickly replaced by clinician-generated recommendations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally positions Elena as the expert on her own life and uses her ideas to shape the plan. It is not a 5 because the clinician still acts as an active strategist rather than a purely facilitative partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena expresses meaningful change talk about desire, ability, and commitment. The psychiatrist reinforces some of it but does not consistently elicit or deepen change talk using MI-consistent methods.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by lowering expectations, reframing attempts as data, and reducing all-or-nothing thinking. The main limitation is that the clinician does not consistently connect barriers to Elena’s deeper values.
