# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but the acceptance feels procedural rather than deeply relational. The positive regard is somewhat generic, with fewer moments of individualized warmth for David’s specific struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some effort to understand David’s perspective, but empathic responses often remain surface-level. The psychiatrist normalizes emotions more than accurately reflecting feelings such as embarrassment, shame, or anticipated failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on smoking cessation, NRT options, safety, and the restaurant trigger. The direction is strong, though it is more instructional and plan-focused than MI-consistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative gestures, but the interaction is often clinician-led. The psychiatrist frequently supplies full plans and scripts rather than consistently co-creating solutions with David.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some options and invites input, but the session also includes many direct instructions. Autonomy is supported in parts, though not consistently reinforced with strong ownership language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are mostly factual, safety-related, or logistical, with limited effort to evoke David’s deeper motivations, values, or self-efficacy. The psychiatrist often provides reasons and strategies rather than drawing out David’s own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely assumes the expert role, providing detailed information, plans, and scripts. The interaction feels more like a provider-led consultation than a true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David offers several clear examples of change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently build on or amplify this language. There are few questions or reflections that deepen David’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce David’s concerns and acknowledges them respectfully. However, the response is mostly problem-solving rather than using MI-consistent reflections or reframing to soften sustain talk.
