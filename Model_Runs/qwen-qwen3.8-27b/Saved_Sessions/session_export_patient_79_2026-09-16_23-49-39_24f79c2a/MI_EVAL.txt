# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Luis’s experience without labeling him as resistant or noncompliant. Positive regard was strong, though not always explicitly affirming his worth independent of his asthma behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong and accurately captured practical frustration, shame, and fear of being judged. Empathy could be deeper by exploring underlying emotions such as anger, fear of losing control, or the meaning of being seen as failing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on asthma management and gradually narrowed toward practical issues such as reliability, refills, and nonjudgmental use. Direction could be stronger by collaboratively selecting one or two concrete goals and ending with a clear next step or follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Luis as the expert on his own body and routine, and the session was not one-way advice. Collaboration could be more fully equal by allowing Luis to set the agenda and generate options with less therapist framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported Luis’s choice, avoided pressure, and respected his boundary about not being judged. Autonomy support was strong but occasionally included subtle steering toward an expected conclusion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions frequently drew out Luis’s own ideas, concerns, and practical solutions without lecturing. Evocation was skillful but could be stronger by using scaling, exceptions, or future-oriented questions to deepen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently conveyed that Luis was the expert on his own life and let his statements shape the session. Partnership was strong, though some parts of the agenda and conclusions remained therapist-framed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk, including using the blue inhaler when tight and wanting a longer prescription at home. However, the change talk was not consistently amplified, reflected, or developed into deeper reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided arguing with Luis’s resistance and acknowledged his preference for simplicity. Sustain talk was often softened, though it was not consistently redirected into stronger change talk.
