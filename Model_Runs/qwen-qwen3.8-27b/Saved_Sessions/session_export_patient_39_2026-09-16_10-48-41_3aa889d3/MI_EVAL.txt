# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and honored Elias’s need for independence. It was slightly less fully accepting because the session moved quickly into agenda-setting and practical options.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections captured Elias’s ambivalence about independence, dependence, and fear of being a burden. Some reflections stayed more factual and did not deeply explore his shame, identity concerns, or emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on a clear behavioral target: medication adherence through a concrete Sunday plan. It was slightly less fully directional because the broader physical activity goal from the referral was largely dropped.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was largely co-created, incorporating Elias’s language, preferences, and boundaries. The psychiatrist still carried much of the structure by providing options, scripts, and problem-solving steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elias was repeatedly invited to choose, and his preferences shaped the final plan. It fell short of a 5 because the structured options and if-then plan remained somewhat directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked about values, barriers, confidence, and a small success, which evoked some client language. However, the session relied more on practical problem-solving than on deeply exploring Elias’s intrinsic motivation and identity-level meanings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a collaborative stance and let Elias’s preferences guide the plan. It was not a 5 because the therapist still led the session by offering structured menus, scripts, and follow-up logistics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias expressed desire, commitment, and intention, and the psychiatrist affirmed these statements. Change talk was not consistently developed or amplified through deeper questions or reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalized Elias’s concerns about hovering, new routines, and alarms without arguing or reinforcing resistance. It was slightly less advanced because sustain talk was not consistently transformed into change talk through double-sided reflections.
