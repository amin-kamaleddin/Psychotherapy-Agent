# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with clear affirmations of dignity and choice. It was not a 5 because the language was sometimes formulaic and could be warmer and more personally resonant.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used good reflective listening to capture ambivalence, history, and boundary-setting. It fell short of a 5 because deeper emotional layers such as resentment, identity, autonomy, and vulnerability were not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed generally focused on smoking, heart health, and the client's relationship. It was not fully strategic because it remained at a meta-level and did not narrow toward a concrete change focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input and framed the conversation as joint, but the collaboration was uneven. Repeated multiple-choice options and asking the client to define the medical danger made the partnership feel less balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This was a major strength, with consistent emphasis on the client's choice, no pressure, and no imposed quit plan. The psychiatrist repeatedly supported Marco's control over the conversation and decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were limited attempts to draw out Marco's own motivations, confidence, or reasons for change. The session increasingly centered on the clinician providing facts rather than eliciting the client's internal change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often treated Marco as the expert on his own life and respected his right to decide. It was uneven because the clinician still framed options and at times asked the client to define danger, which weakened full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk appeared, especially around partner time and feeling less stuck, but it was not systematically developed. The psychiatrist did not use scaling, commitment language, or deeper amplification of the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with sustain talk and acknowledged the client's resistance without confrontation. However, there was not enough use of double-sided reflections or gentle pivots to help the conversation move toward change.
