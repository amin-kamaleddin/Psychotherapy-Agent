# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine warmth and non-judgment throughout, with validation of conflicting thoughts and normalizing Marcus's defensiveness. Occasionally feels slightly performative or patronizing for a teenager, preventing a full 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with nuanced observations, particularly capturing the contradiction of calling the friend's dad an idiot while acknowledging his anger. Could have explored the fear of social exclusion more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with logical progression from rapport to identifying the specific barrier. Session trails off without a clear collaborative next step or summary to add momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames conversation as joint exploration and builds on Marcus's contributions. Slightly top-down in places where questions lead toward predetermined conclusions rather than genuinely co-constructing direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength—explicitly respects Marcus's right to set his own boundaries, never lectures or directs, and consistently positions him as the agent of change. Woven into nearly every intervention.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently draws out Marcus's own reasons and motivations without external input, including asking him to define his own line. Lacks strategic depth in amplifying change talk that emerges, such as the soda experience.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains collaborative stance with clear power-sharing language like 'you know these parties better than I do.' Some questions feel slightly leading, subtly steering rather than genuinely opening.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits change talk through strategic questions and reflections, and Marcus produces meaningful change language. Does not explicitly reinforce or amplify this change talk with summaries or deeper reflections to build on it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk reasonably by reflecting and not directly challenging, but does not consistently use double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to reduce its grip. Peer pressure sustain talk is acknowledged but not actively reframed.
