# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful and validating, using phrases such as “That makes sense” and “Those worries are reasonable.” However, acceptance was undercut by corrective medical statements that can sound disapproving of the client’s current choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some warm validations and simple reflections, but empathy often stayed at the surface level and focused on the topic rather than the client’s deeper fear and ambivalence. More complex reflections of her emotional experience and values would strengthen this criterion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on asthma control, inhaler use, and a next step with the prescriber. Direction was strong, though the session sometimes became didactic and did not fully close the client’s unresolved autonomy concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were occasional collaborative moments, such as offering a choice between a script and reminder options, but the psychiatrist frequently supplied expert-driven recommendations and plans. The client’s ideas and concerns did not consistently shape the next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged the client’s right to choose and offered a low-pressure trial, but also gave many directives and practical recommendations. Autonomy support would be stronger if options were more explicitly framed as invitations and the client’s final “can I stop without guilt?” question was directly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist began with a helpful open question, but much of the session relied on providing medical explanations rather than drawing out the client’s own motivations, values, or confidence. More open-ended questions and ruler scales would improve evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session included some collaborative language, but the overall stance was expert-led, with the psychiatrist supplying most of the information and plan. A stronger partnership would involve more questions about the client’s own view of what is getting in the way and what would feel manageable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offered some change talk, but the psychiatrist did not consistently highlight, expand, or build on it. More importance, confidence, ability, and values questions would help develop the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expressed clear sustain talk about avoiding daily medication and keeping using the rescue inhaler. The psychiatrist sometimes challenged this with counterarguments rather than using double-sided reflections or exploring the meaning behind the sustain talk.
