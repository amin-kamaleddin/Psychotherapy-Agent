# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects and validates the client without judgment or pressure. It falls short of 5 because warmth remains somewhat professional and lacks more explicit affirmation of the client’s worth independent of her choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both the emotional and cognitive dimensions of the client’s ambivalence. Deeper exploration of underlying fear, shame, or values would strengthen the empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on contraception after the scare and moves toward a concrete, low-pressure next step. It could be stronger by more explicitly connecting that next step to the broader change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client actively shapes the session, including deciding on a short list, one provider question, and a brief reminder. More explicit co-created language, such as “let’s explore this together,” would make the collaboration even clearer.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client’s right to choose and repeatedly reinforces that no decision is required today. This consistently fosters self-direction and avoids pressure or advice-giving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively draw out the client’s own motivations, values, and preferences rather than providing external reasons for change. More skillful amplification and consolidation of the client’s change talk would elevate this criterion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the authority on her experience and repeatedly invites her input. The session remains somewhat counselor-guided, with limited explicit negotiation of agenda or shared power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits and reinforces change-relevant language, such as wanting “less guessing” and feeling steadier. A 5 would require more deliberate development and amplification of the client’s reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is reframed as protective and pace-setting rather than met with argument or pressure. More consistent use of double-sided reflections and pivots would more actively transform sustain talk into change language.
