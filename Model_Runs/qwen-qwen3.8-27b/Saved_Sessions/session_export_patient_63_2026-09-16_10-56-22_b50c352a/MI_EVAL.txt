# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Jordan's effort, honesty, and insight without judgment. This communicates warmth and unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects Jordan's fear, social pressure, and feeling stuck, though responses often quickly pivot to practical options. Overall, the reflections show genuine perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behavior of stopping shoplifting and managing urges in stores. It builds toward a specific, concrete plan rather than drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers options and repeatedly asks which strategy feels most doable, inviting Jordan's preferences into planning. Some sections are solution-focused, but the exchange remains collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Jordan is consistently given choice, including the right not to rehearse aloud or carry his phone prominently. The counselor reinforces that Jordan owns the decision and can select a believable plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions, what-worked prompts, and importance/confidence rulers elicit Jordan's own motivations and reasons for change. The client's change talk and confidence level are drawn out rather than imposed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions himself as an ally and asks for Jordan's input on strategies, treating him as the expert on his life. The tone avoids lecturing while still offering useful suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor highlights Jordan's goal, honesty, and insight, and asks for confidence ratings that reinforce his reasons for stopping. This helps expand client-generated motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Concerns about losing the group, looking weak, and social pressure are acknowledged without challenge. The counselor then redirects toward low-confrontation exit options rather than arguing with the client.
