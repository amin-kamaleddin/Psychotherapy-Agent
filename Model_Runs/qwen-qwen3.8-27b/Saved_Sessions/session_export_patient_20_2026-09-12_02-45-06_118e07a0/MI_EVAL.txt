# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized the client's ambivalence and concerns. Affirmations were supportive but occasionally generalized, and unconditional positive regard did not fully extend to the client's defensiveness or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was generally good, accurately capturing the client's ambivalence, practical concerns, and desire to avoid feeling scolded. The reflections remained somewhat cognitive and task-focused, missing opportunities to explore deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on after-work alcohol use and its effects. The conversation stayed anchored to the referral reason and relevant behavior without meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited client input, responded flexibly, and co-constructed the plan around the client's stated preferences. Collaboration was strong, though the counselor sometimes offered structured options rather than allowing the client to fully lead the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed the client's freedom to choose and respected pushback on reminders and hiding the beer. Repeated focus on concrete steps and planning created mild directionality, slightly limiting open-ended autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicited the client's own motivations through questions about desired outcomes, confidence, and what would help. Evocation was frequent but somewhat superficial and behavioral, with less exploration of deeper values or identity-based motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveyed that the client was the expert on his own life and needs, and the client's preferences shaped the conversation. The partnership was strong but became somewhat counselor-led when multiple structured options and planning steps were introduced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited change talk through questions about desired differences, small steps, and confidence, and the client offered several change-related statements. The counselor sometimes moved quickly into planning rather than fully expanding the client's own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without arguing or condemning, and some change strategies were framed as preserving ritual rather than punishment. However, the counselor repeatedly introduced concrete change options, and some sustain talk was not sufficiently softened or pivoted toward change.
