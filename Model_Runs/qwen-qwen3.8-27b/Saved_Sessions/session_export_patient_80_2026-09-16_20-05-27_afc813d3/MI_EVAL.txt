# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates consistent respect, warmth, and nonjudgmental acceptance throughout the session. They affirm the client's efforts and strengths without moralizing partial progress or pressuring him to be perfect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate, specific, and meaningful reflective listening. They capture both the practical details and emotional significance of the client's statements, including the meaning of safety, control, and dignity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and purposeful focus on reducing alcohol use and managing anger. Each question progresses logically toward concrete, workable steps without drifting into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with the client actively shaping the plan through his own ideas and cues. The partnership could be slightly stronger by making the client's expert role and shared decision-making more explicit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports the client's choice, allowing a flexible and realistic plan rather than imposing rigid expectations. Autonomy support could be improved by more explicitly affirming the client's right to make his own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits the client's own motivations, cues, and solutions rather than providing advice. It could be stronger by inviting deeper exploration of values, identity, and longer-term meaning behind the desired changes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by inviting the client's perspective and allowing his ideas to guide the session. The partnership could be made more explicit by directly positioning the client as the expert in his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open, evocative questions that successfully draw out the client's own language for change. The skill could be enhanced by more explicitly amplifying and summarizing the client's change talk after it occurs.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk well by acknowledging the client's realistic concerns without judgment or argument. It could be improved by more actively redirecting sustain talk toward deeper change motivation or values.
