# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, nonjudgment, and support while affirming Derrick’s engagement and plan. However, acceptance remains somewhat surface-level by not fully normalizing his emotional ambivalence, shame, or the pain of balancing legal consequences with social belonging.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong, accurate reflections of Derrick’s stress, legal pressure, and ambivalence about belonging versus protecting his license. The score is limited because the empathy is more cognitive and situational than emotionally deep, with less exploration of shame, fear, loneliness, or relationship loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well focused on preventing another DUI, protecting the license, and building a practical safety plan. The main limitation is a procedural emphasis on scripts, cash placement, and exit tactics without consistently connecting the plan to Derrick’s broader values or life direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist creates a collaborative interaction and allows Derrick’s preferences to clearly shape the session. However, moments of offering multiple scripts and support options make the interaction slightly more therapist-led than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames options as optional and respects Derrick’s decisions to keep the plan simple. The score is not higher because repeated prompts to add supports or boost confidence can feel subtly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Derrick’s own reasons, priorities, and practical strategies, leading to a client-generated plan. The skill is not maximally strong because the psychiatrist sometimes supplies scripts and supports and does not fully evoke deeper values, identity, or past sobriety success.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance by inviting Derrick’s input and following his lead rather than imposing an intervention. The partnership is not consistently at the highest level because the psychiatrist occasionally offers specific scripts, behaviors, and contingency plans before fully eliciting Derrick’s own approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses relevant questions and affirmations to elicit Derrick’s own reasons, intentions, and practical change talk. The skill is limited by occasional shifts toward counselor-generated options and less exploration of deeper change motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accepts Derrick’s social concerns without arguing or escalating resistance, helping keep the conversation moving toward a manageable plan. The score is limited because the softening is mostly implicit, with fewer explicit double-sided reflections that acknowledge both the appeal of his friends and the value of change.
