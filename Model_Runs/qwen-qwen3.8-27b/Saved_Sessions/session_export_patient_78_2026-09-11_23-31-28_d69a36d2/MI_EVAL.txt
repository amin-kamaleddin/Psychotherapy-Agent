# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is generally nonjudgmental and respectful, but occasionally uses directive or mildly disapproving phrasing. Acceptance is not consistently warm or unconditional, especially when the client disengages.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor shows moderate empathy by acknowledging frustration, stress, and exhaustion. Reflections remain surface-level and do not fully capture the client’s ambivalence, fear of control, or emotional conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session frequently drifts between smoking, work stress, irritability, and parenting. There is no consistent strategic focus on the target behavior of smoking cessation or a clear change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites client input and offers choices, but often proposes multiple specific solutions and scripts. This limits true co-creation and makes the interaction feel less like an equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor frequently acknowledges the client’s choice and avoids direct pressure. However, repeated suggestions of specific techniques and experiments can reduce the client’s sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are used, but the session largely relies on counselor suggestions rather than drawing out the client’s own motivations. The client’s key change talk about smoking possibly being the problem is not explored further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor attempts a collaborative stance by offering preferences and choices. The interaction often feels uneven, with the counselor acting more like an advice-giver than a true partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are occasional attempts to elicit change talk, such as asking about likes/dislikes of smoking and past attempts to cut back. The client’s change-related statements are not consistently reinforced or developed into stronger motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with resistance and does not reinforce negative self-statements. However, sustain talk is often accepted and moved past rather than strategically reframed toward change values or goals.
