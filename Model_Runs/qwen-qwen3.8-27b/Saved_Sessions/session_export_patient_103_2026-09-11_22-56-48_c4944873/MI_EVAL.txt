# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Psychiatrist used a respectful, validating, and nonjudgmental tone throughout. Acceptance remained somewhat task-oriented and did not fully affirm Nabil's worth or deeper self-criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected surface-level frustration, anger, and practical stressors accurately. Deeper feelings such as shame, fear, and feeling disrespected were not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session focused on the immediate appointment delay and practical scripts. It lacked a clearly collaboratively defined MI direction or target behavior for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was genuine task-level collaboration in creating messages and choosing scripts. However, the psychiatrist often acted as the expert problem-solver rather than an equal collaborator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported Nabil's choices and right to leave without pressure or persuasion. Autonomy support was strong but mainly situational rather than tied to broader change ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial open-ended questions were appropriate, but the psychiatrist quickly shifted to providing solutions. There was limited effort to elicit Nabil's own motivations, values, or change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and invited input, showing some shared decision-making. Partnership remained uneven because the therapist took a leading expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk was rarely elicited or developed. The session relied more on practical scripts than drawing out desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue, but sustain talk about leaving and refusing follow-up was not effectively softened. Responses reinforced immediate exit rather than exploring ambivalence or future engagement.
