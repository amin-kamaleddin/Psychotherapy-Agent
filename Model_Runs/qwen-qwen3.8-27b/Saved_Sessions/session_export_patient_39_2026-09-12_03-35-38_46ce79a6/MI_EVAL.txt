# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and acknowledges Elias's difficulty without blame. The warmth is somewhat formulaic and could more deeply validate his fear, discouragement, or loss related to reduced mobility.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and acknowledge the meaningful but frustrating nature of the experience, yet often remain surface-level. There is less emotional depth in exploring fear, frustration, or loss before moving to planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on increasing physical activity and moves toward a concrete, manageable goal of standing for 20 seconds. It could better connect this activity goal to Elias's broader values and the medical purpose of the referral.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Elias to shape the plan through choices such as standing, timing, and safety limits. The interaction remains somewhat clinician-guided because the psychiatrist still provides structured options and a defined experiment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elias's autonomy is consistently supported through invitations, respect for boundaries, and acceptance of his choice to keep the experiment private. The plan is still fairly specific and includes repeated safety instructions, which slightly limits full client-led direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks useful evocative questions and helps Elias identify a personal definition of success. However, the session shifts quickly into practical planning and does not deeply explore his values, motivations, or internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates a collaborative stance by inviting Elias's participation and following his lead in many decisions. The session is not fully client-led because the psychiatrist still frames the work with structured options and a specific experiment.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk and asks questions that invite Elias's own reasons for trying. Change talk is not consistently reinforced or expanded, as the conversation often moves quickly into concrete planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without arguing or reinforcing it, which is important. However, there is limited use of strategic MI techniques such as double-sided reflections or reframing to fully soften Elias's expressed doubt.
