# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with phrases like “I hear you” and “I respect your plan.” However, the tone occasionally becomes mildly evaluative or directive and feels more like risk management than unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects Tyler’s feelings about his mother, his new peer group, and the role of using. The reflections could go deeper into underlying ambivalence, frustration, fear of conflict, and autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on substance use, peer context, parental conflict, and immediate safety. The direction is clinically relevant but repeatedly shifts toward harm-reduction advice rather than Tyler’s own change direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language such as “we” and invites Tyler to choose what feels doable. The psychiatrist still supplies much of the structure and practical safety content, making the collaboration slightly less equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices, asks permission, and respects Tyler’s decisions when he declines options. The repeated emphasis on safety recommendations slightly weakens the message that change is entirely up to Tyler.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks appropriate evocative questions about values, using, and what would feel doable. The session often moves from these questions into clinician-generated safety tips rather than fully developing Tyler’s own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates a generally collaborative stance by asking permission and inviting Tyler’s input. Partnership is somewhat uneven because the psychiatrist frequently introduces safety options and repeatedly summarizes the agreed steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited attempts to elicit change talk, such as asking what would feel doable. Tyler’s own change talk is acknowledged but not strongly reinforced, amplified, or explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with Tyler or reinforcing his sustain talk. Resistance is often accepted and then followed by safety advice rather than being explored with double-sided reflections or gentle evocative questions.
