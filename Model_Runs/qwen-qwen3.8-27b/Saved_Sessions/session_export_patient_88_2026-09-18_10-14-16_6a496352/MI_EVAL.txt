# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and warm, validating Marcus's frustration and pushback without shaming or labeling. Occasionally feels slightly scripted, and does not go far enough to explicitly affirm Marcus's worth as a person beyond the behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with nuanced reflections that capture ambivalence well, such as the 'pulling in opposite directions' reflection. Some reflections could go deeper emotionally rather than functioning as accurate summaries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with a logical progression from resistance to the mother-son dynamic to future-oriented ambivalence. Could benefit from more explicit collaborative agenda-setting or strategic summarizing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance throughout, consistently framing the conversation as joint exploration and adjusting approach based on Marcus's feedback. The collaborative process could be made more visible through explicit co-creation of the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids directives, advice-giving, and guilt while affirming Marcus's choice and driver's seat. Could more explicitly affirm ownership of both the choice to change and the choice not to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriate evocative questions that successfully elicit change talk such as 'it could get bigger.' Tends to move on to new questions rather than staying with and strategically deepening the change talk Marcus produces.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides within Marcus and invites his perspective rather than providing the psychiatrist's own. Could more explicitly acknowledge Marcus's expertise in his own life and social world.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus produces genuine change talk in response to evocative questions, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce, amplify, or strategically build on it. The 'it could get bigger' thread is a missed opportunity for deeper follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Manages sustain talk adequately with some double-sided reflections and reframing without arguing. Does not consistently use advanced techniques like rolled-in reflections or sit with resistance as a therapeutic opportunity.
