# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and nonjudgmental regard with warm, accepting language throughout. The acceptance is solid but occasionally feels formulaic and could be deeper in its individualization to Liora's specific emotional states.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong empathic reflections including powerful image extensions and a well-constructed double-sided reflection. Misses opportunities to fully sit in Liora's confusion and to more explicitly reflect the profound loneliness of 'just me and it.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Stays focused on the presenting concern but lacks strategic momentum toward a clear change goal. The shift to the relational dimension is not bridged back to the primary target of reducing self-harm, and no concrete next step is collaboratively identified.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative framing with language like 'figure it out alongside you' and non-prescriptive stance. However, collaboration is somewhat one-sided in practice — the psychiatrist does the bulk of guiding while Liora remains largely passive and responsive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respects Liora's pace, choices, and privacy without imposing advice or pressure. Could be strengthened by more actively affirming Liora's ownership and agency in moments where she expresses helplessness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some evocative moves, particularly the well-constructed question about what she'd most not want to lose if something changed. However, evocation is infrequent and not sustained, with missed opportunities to explore values, goals, or what a life without the compressed feeling might look like.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clearly collaborative, non-expert stance throughout with appropriate language and tone. Could be stronger by explicitly acknowledging Liora's expertise in her own experience and asking what she wants to focus on in the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked and present in Liora's own words (confusion, dissatisfaction, 'coming back to the same place'), but the psychiatrist does not effectively reflect it back as change talk or amplify it. The single evocative question is not followed up with further development.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk reasonably with some acknowledgment of function and one strong double-sided reflection. However, when Liora reiterates the reliability of self-harm, the psychiatrist validates but does not follow up with a question or reflection that gently pivots toward costs or alternatives.
