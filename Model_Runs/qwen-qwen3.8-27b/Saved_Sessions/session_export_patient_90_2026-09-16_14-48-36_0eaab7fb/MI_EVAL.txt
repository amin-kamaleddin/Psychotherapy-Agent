# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validated Marcus’s experience and avoided shame about asthma, finances, or the dog. Warmth was solid but somewhat efficient, without fully normalizing the emotional weight of chronic stress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Marcus’s burden, fear of nighttime wheezing, and ambivalence about the dog. Some disclosures of exhaustion, loneliness, and financial stress were acknowledged but quickly moved into problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on improving nighttime asthma management and efficiently narrowed broad stressors into a concrete, actionable puffer step. Progression from overwhelm to a specific plan was clear and strategic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus’s preferences shaped the intervention, including having his wife point out the puffer angle and using a TV-off cue. The counselor remained the primary guide, with limited explicit joint language such as “let’s figure this out together.”

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly invited Marcus to choose and respected his boundaries around no new gear, keeping the dog, and avoiding hovering. The final plan was framed as Marcus’s own action rather than a prescription.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor drew out Marcus’s own motivations for steady sleep, simple technique, and keeping his routine. Deeper values, readiness scaling, or amplification of stronger change talk could have been explored further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned Marcus as the expert on his own life and let his answers direct the session. The resulting plan emerged from Marcus’s own priorities rather than counselor-imposed goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions prompted Marcus to articulate concrete change talk, such as tilting the puffer before bed and aiming for one good breath. The counselor amplified some change talk but did not systematically explore broader DARN-CAT motivation or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about the dog and limited energy was handled respectfully by working within Marcus’s boundaries rather than arguing against them. Financial and appointment-related sustain talk was acknowledged but could have been reframed more actively.
