# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalized the client’s ambivalence and possible slip. However, acceptance was somewhat formulaic and did not fully affirm the client’s underlying pressure or struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provided adequate reflections of the client’s practical concerns about breathing, counting, and effort. However, the reflections remained mostly cognitive and did not consistently capture deeper emotional ambivalence or pressure from others.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed generally focused on after-work drinking and urge management without major wandering. However, it moved quickly into advice, coping options, and behavioral planning rather than MI-focused exploration of change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited the client to choose among options and adapted the plan to the client’s preference for simplicity. However, the therapist supplied most of the content and framed the session more as an expert than as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasized choice, no pressure, and no judgment if the client drank. However, the volume of behavioral suggestions and recommendations still subtly steered the client toward a specific plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist began with an evocative opening question but then shifted to psychoeducation and practical planning. There was limited use of open questions, importance rulers, or confidence rulers to elicit the client’s own motivation and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist included collaborative language and allowed the client’s preferences to shape the final plan. However, the session remained largely therapist-led, with the psychiatrist directing the structure, content, and suggested experiments.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirmed some client action talk, such as trying water or taking a short walk. However, there was limited effort to deepen or expand the client’s own reasons, desires, abilities, or importance for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with the client’s sustain talk and allowed the client to keep beer as an option. However, responses often relied on education or practical redirection rather than MI-specific reflections that explore ambivalence or gently pivot sustain talk.
