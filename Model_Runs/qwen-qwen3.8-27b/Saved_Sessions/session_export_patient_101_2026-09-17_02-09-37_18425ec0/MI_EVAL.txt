# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated warmth and nonjudgmental respect without moralizing. Risky behaviors were acknowledged as real concerns rather than failures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both the behavioral and emotional core of Mei's ambivalence. The counselor went beyond paraphrasing to understand the fear of invisibility and the social function of drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stayed focused on drinking, social settings, and contraceptive safety. Some emotional themes drifted without being consistently re-anchored to concrete behavioral steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor created a joint exploration and built on Mei's ideas such as Sarah and the two-drink plan. The collaboration was still somewhat clinician-led, with less explicit negotiation of the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided pressure and respected Mei's uncertainty. Autonomy support could be strengthened by more explicitly and repeatedly affirming that all choices remain Mei's.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew out Mei's values, motivations, and possible solutions. There was less strategic development of confidence, importance, reasons, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Mei was consistently treated as the expert on her own life and motivations. The counselor asked guiding questions rather than directing or imposing a preferred agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited and affirmed change talk, including Mei's emerging plan and capabilities. The strongest change talk was not consistently deepened, amplified, or reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled without argument, and self-critical statements were reframed respectfully. Some responses remained neutral or ambivalence-centered rather than actively pivoting toward change.
