# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrates basic respect and avoids judgment, but makes a notable error comparing dental situation to heart surgery, which the patient had to correct. The correction was acknowledged appropriately, but the overall acceptance is occasionally interrupted by the clinician's own reframing agenda.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes reasonable efforts to reflect the patient's perspective early on, but becomes increasingly superficial and formulaic as the session progresses. The repeated use of 'that's not nothing' and tendency to reframe neutral statements into change-oriented language prevents deeper emotional connection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a general direction toward smoking and dental health but wanders between topics without a clear strategic arc. By the end, the patient has retreated back into sustain talk and the session stalls without resolution.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frames the session as collaborative and the patient does make some contributions that shape direction, but the psychiatrist still drives much of the agenda. The collaboration is uneven and does not reach the level where the patient's ideas substantially shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges the patient's autonomy multiple times but in a generic way that does not fully land with a resistant patient. The repeated reframing of neutral statements into change-oriented language subtly undermines the autonomy support by implying the patient should feel good about being in 'just listening' mode.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some evocative questions but they feel mechanical and somewhat leading toward a predetermined conclusion. The 'slope vs. cliff' framing is largely clinician-generated rather than patient-generated, weakening the evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts a collaborative stance with appropriate phrases but the partnership feels performative rather than genuine. The session remains quite clinician-driven in terms of where the conversation goes and what gets explored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does some work to identify and reflect back the patient's change talk, but the evocation is somewhat forced in places where the patient is simply setting boundaries. The change talk that emerges is mostly the patient's own rather than something the psychiatrist skillfully drew out.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A significant weakness in the session; when the patient expresses sustain talk, the psychiatrist does not effectively soften or redirect it. Responses tend to be neutral acknowledgments or move past the sustain talk without using double-sided reflections or strategic redirection.
