# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental, using validating phrases such as understandable worry. However, acceptance feels formulaic and somewhat reduced by a heavily informational delivery style.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects Alessia's fear, nervousness, and exhaustion, but the reflections are mostly simple and factual. Stronger, more emotionally complex reflections were missed during moments of vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on safely transitioning from phenelzine to citalopram and moves toward concrete next steps. The focus is strong, though the pacing is more mechanical than strategically MI-consistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some collaborative moments, such as offering to draft a portal message or role-play a call. However, the session is more prescriptive and information-driven than genuinely collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and avoids forcing a decision, supporting autonomy in part. Still, moments of direct instruction weaken the consistency of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks an opening question and offers next-step choices, but does not deeply draw out Alessia's own values, reasons, or resources. The approach tends to provide answers rather than elicit the patient's motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some shared decision-making is present, but the overall dynamic is informational rather than truly co-created. The psychiatrist does not consistently position Alessia as the expert in her own experience and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports some change talk through practical next steps and acknowledges the patient's relief. However, change talk is not consistently reinforced, amplified, or strategically developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges concerns about crashing, added burden, and ineffective treatment, but mainly responds with information and options. MI-consistent reflections or reframing to soften sustain talk are limited.
