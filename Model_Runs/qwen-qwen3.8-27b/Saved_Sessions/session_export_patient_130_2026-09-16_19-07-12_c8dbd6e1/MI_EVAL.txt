# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and clearly respects the client’s autonomy and expertise. It is strong but not perfect because the focus on the home conflict is slightly problem-framed and unconditional acceptance is not fully explicit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections to understand the client’s ambivalence, need for control, and relational stress. The empathy is solid but more cognitive/situational than deeply emotional.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays broadly connected to drug use and its evening relationship consequences, and moves toward a concrete behavior plan. However, it drifts toward communication and boundary-setting rather than consistently focusing on reducing drug use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works from the client’s perspective and builds a plan from Viktor’s own suggestions. It is collaborative but still somewhat therapist-led through structured questions and summaries.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure, affirms ownership, and supports self-directed choice. It falls short of a 5 because it subtly steers toward a plan without explicitly affirming the right to choose no change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicit the client’s values, motivations, and practical ideas for staying calm and present. The evocation is useful but not deeply centered on motivation or confidence for reducing drug use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Viktor as the expert and keeps the session largely client-led. Power sharing is strong, but the structured sequence and lack of explicit agenda-checking limit it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes concrete client-generated change talk about presence, tone, and boundary-setting. It is limited because the change talk focuses more on relationship behavior than on reducing drug use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and accepts sustain talk without confrontation. The response is mostly neutral rather than using active softening, double-sided reflections, or discrepancy development.
