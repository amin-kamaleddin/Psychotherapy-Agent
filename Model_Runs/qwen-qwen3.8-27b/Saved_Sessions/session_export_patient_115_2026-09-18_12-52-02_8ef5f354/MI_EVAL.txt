# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates consistent, genuine unconditional positive regard throughout the session, with warmth that deepens as Samir shares more vulnerably. Never judges, labels, or minimizes Samir's choices or ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally accurate and nuanced empathic understanding that goes beyond surface-level reflection to capture the existential meaning of behaviors (e.g., the 'five-minute wall'). Empathy is progressive, deepening from capturing dual frustrations to identifying the function of the behavior.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, logical progression from identifying the problem through insight to a small concrete step. Could have been more strategically focused in leveraging certain motivational moments (e.g., the 'what if it doesn't work' fear) before transitioning to the next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses collaborative framing ('if we were to design that together'), permission-seeking language, and follows Samir's lead throughout. The session is structured as a genuine dialogue where Samir's insights shape the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy with statements like 'I'm not going to hand you an answer' and 'that pause is yours.' Never pressures or implies Samir 'should' quit; allows him to arrive at his own small commitment organically.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within Samir using open questions, never providing external reasons (no statistics, warnings, or advice). All motivation is self-generated: his kids, safety concerns, and desire for the 'pause'.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Samir as the expert on his own life and consistently uses 'we' language and open questions rather than providing solutions. The power dynamic is genuinely equal, functioning as a guide rather than an authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully uses strategic open questions that naturally lead Samir to articulate his own motivations and solutions. The session culminates in spontaneous change talk and early commitment ('Maybe that's the part I can try') that was organically evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk by validating rather than challenging or pushing back (e.g., 'cut down feels like the limit right now'). Sustain talk is never reinforced or argued against, but consistently acknowledged and gently pivoted without creating adversarial dynamics.
