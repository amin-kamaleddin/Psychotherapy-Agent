# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance without shaming Anika for vaping. However, the acceptance is somewhat formulaic and does not deeply acknowledge her ambivalence, pressure, or emotional complexity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are mostly accurate at the content level and capture her focus on basketball and avoiding trouble. Deeper emotional understanding is limited, with missed opportunities to explore embarrassment, fear, conflict with friends, or ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on relevant targets: vaping, school, eligibility, and the basketball season. The main limitation is a somewhat narrow, compliance-oriented focus rather than broader values or long-term goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and allows Anika to choose components of the plan, creating a respectful and joint process. Collaboration could be strengthened by allowing more of the plan to emerge from her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by inviting input and respecting Anika’s preference for an informal plan. It occasionally becomes mildly directive when many specific options are offered before fully exploring her own ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is drawn out, especially around basketball and avoiding suspension. However, the psychiatrist relies heavily on suggested strategies and does not deeply evoke Anika’s own values, meanings, or intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with frequent invitations for Anika’s input and choices. It would benefit from stronger power-sharing by more clearly positioning Anika as the expert on her own life, values, and solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anika offers meaningful change talk, such as wanting to protect her basketball spot and using a refusal line. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk into stronger intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This is the main area for improvement, as Anika repeatedly minimizes risk and expresses sustain talk. The psychiatrist avoids confrontation but does not effectively reflect, soften, or redirect her ambivalence and minimization.
