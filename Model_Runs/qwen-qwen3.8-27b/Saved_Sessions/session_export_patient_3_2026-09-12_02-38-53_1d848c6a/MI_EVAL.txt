# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirming statements such as "that took courage" and "I admire that." The warmth is competent but somewhat formulaic, with less individualized validation of the client's body-image and self-worth struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately captures the client's frustration, fear of failure, and exhaustion. Reflections could go deeper into the emotional core, particularly the ambivalence and body-image pain, to reach a higher level of nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior of restarting exercise, moving from broad motivation to a concrete, manageable first step. The direction would be stronger with a clearer closing summary and a concise affirmation that fully affirms the client's agency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, with the psychiatrist offering options and incorporating the client's preferences into the final plan. However, the psychiatrist sometimes leads with structured suggestions before fully eliciting the client's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports autonomy well by using choice-based language and validating the client's decision to reduce the task. Autonomy could be further strengthened by returning ownership to the client, especially when she asks for reassurance that the plan is "enough."

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes useful client-driven planning language by asking what feels doable and what "getting moving" looks like. However, the evocation is more procedural than motivational, with missed opportunities to draw out deeper change talk related to values, confidence, and hope.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys partnership by inviting the client's input and avoiding an authoritarian stance. It would be a 5 with more explicit acknowledgment that the client is the expert on her own life and more space for the client to generate ideas before options are offered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers clear change talk, and the psychiatrist supports this by asking about first steps and doable options. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify the client's change talk, moving quickly into problem-solving before fully developing intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles sustain talk effectively by normalizing the worry and reframing tiny action as meaningful rather than arguing. It would be a 5 with more deliberate double-sided reflections and strategic pivots to turn sustain talk into change talk.
