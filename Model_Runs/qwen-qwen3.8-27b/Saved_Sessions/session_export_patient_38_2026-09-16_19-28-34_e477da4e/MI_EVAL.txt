# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, validating tone and avoids shaming the client for hesitation or inconsistent contraception. Acceptance feels professional and supportive, though slightly less individually warm than a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates accurate reflective listening and understands the client’s ambivalence and practical concerns. Some deeper emotional meaning around the pregnancy scare and mild repetition limit the score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on the target issue of making a safer contraception decision. Direction is appropriate but could be more strategically narrowed toward a specific behavior, such as scheduling follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is collaborative because the client’s priorities, questions, and next-step preferences shape the conversation. It could be stronger by making the follow-up plan more explicitly shared and concrete.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is consistently positioned as the decision-maker, with clear respect for her pace and right to pause. The clinician avoids pressure to choose a method, make an appointment, or commit immediately.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frequently evokes the client’s own motivations by asking what would help her feel in control, informed, and safe. Evocation could be deeper by more explicitly drawing out values, confidence, and importance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly conveys that the client is the expert on her own body, values, and readiness. The clinician shares power by avoiding medical decision-making and not imposing a preferred method.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively evoke the client’s motivations, safety needs, and protective intentions around contraception. The clinician affirms this language but could further deepen or amplify change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hesitation is handled well without arguing, correcting, or challenging the client. Some sustain talk remains validated rather than fully transformed through more strategic contrast or double-sided reflections.
