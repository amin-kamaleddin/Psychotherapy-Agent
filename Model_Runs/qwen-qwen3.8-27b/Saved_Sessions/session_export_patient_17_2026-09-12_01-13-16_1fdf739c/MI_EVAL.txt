# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses respectful, nonjudgmental language and normalizes struggle (e.g., “you’re not a failure,” “you’re doing your best”). The warmth is supportive but relatively brief, with quick shifts toward advice limiting deeper affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shows some accurate reflections of distress and withdrawal symptoms, but often moves quickly from emotion to problem-solving. Missed opportunities to deeply reflect shame, identity threat, and fear of failing her daughter.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation and an actionable next step. The focus is strong but somewhat prescriptive, with the therapist supplying much of the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites some input on preferences and co-selects materials like the checklist. However, the session is largely clinician-led, with the patient’s ideas not consistently shaping the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choices and respects some patient decisions, such as not telling her daughter yet. Heavy directive guidance and detailed planning partially limit the sense that the plan belongs primarily to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies mainly on education, options, and directive guidance rather than eliciting the patient’s own motivations. Strong change-related statements (e.g., wanting to be okay for her daughter) are not deeply explored or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist invites the patient to choose among options, supporting some collaborative participation. The session remains expert-driven and structured, reducing the sense of equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is present, including choosing the patch and wanting to be better for her daughter. The therapist affirms but does not consistently deepen, expand, or systematically cultivate this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The therapist redirects concerns toward practical options and avoids entrapment, but responses are often corrective or solution-focused. There is limited use of double-sided reflections to hold and soften ambivalence.
