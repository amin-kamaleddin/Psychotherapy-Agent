# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates a warm, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes Rosa's ambivalence without labeling or criticism. Acceptance is strong, though some reflective statements add subtle interpretation or positive evaluation rather than simply affirming her stated experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers good reflections of ambivalence, fear, and the search for balance. Empathy could be deeper by exploring the emotional weight of fear of failure, shame, or loss of identity behind Rosa's statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on smoking cessation and gradually narrows toward cravings, food, mood, and a practical coping window. Direction is helpful but somewhat clinician-driven rather than collaboratively negotiated with Rosa.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session uses joint exploration language, and Rosa's ideas meaningfully shape the discussion. Collaboration remains somewhat guided because the psychiatrist provides much of the structure and introduces specific examples and clinical ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and repeatedly reinforces that Rosa is the decision-maker. Autonomy support could be stronger by reducing questions that subtly introduce clinician preferences or assumptions about how cravings should work.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open-ended questions to draw out Rosa's thoughts, fears, and possible strategies. Evocation is solid but sometimes leads toward specific coping options or clinical assumptions rather than fully waiting for Rosa to generate her own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Rosa to define what would feel safe, believable, and doable. Partnership is not yet exemplary because the psychiatrist occasionally introduces options and framing that can feel leading.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change-related language around wanting to know if cravings fade, not facing it alone, and considering gum. Change talk is not consistently deepened through explicit Desire, Ability, Reasons, or Need questions, summarizing, or amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates Rosa's concerns about food, cravings, mood, and coworkers without reinforcing them. Softening could be stronger with more double-sided reflections that honor the sustain talk while gently linking it to change.
