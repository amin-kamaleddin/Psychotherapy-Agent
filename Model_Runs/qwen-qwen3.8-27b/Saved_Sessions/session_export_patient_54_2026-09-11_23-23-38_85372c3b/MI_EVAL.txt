# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating of Isabella’s autonomy and concern for her baby. However, repeated emphasis on medical facts and guidelines created a subtly corrective tone, preventing a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist made sincere reflections of Isabella’s conflict, annoyance, and frustration, but the empathy stayed surface-level. Deeper emotions and meanings were not consistently explored, and reflections were often followed by information or plans.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remained clearly focused on alcohol use during pregnancy, concern for the baby, and practical next steps. The main limitation was that direction was more informational and problem-solving than strategically focused on resolving ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered choices and incorporated Isabella’s garage plan and nonalcoholic drink idea. However, the therapist frequently supplied solutions, scripts, and strategies before Isabella had fully generated her own ideas, making collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly affirmed Isabella’s decision-making authority and respected her refusal of scripts and monitoring. The score was limited by repeated medical advice and abstinence-focused recommendations that could feel subtly pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This was the weakest area, with the session being primarily informative rather than eliciting Isabella’s own motivations. The psychiatrist did not consistently ask open-ended questions that explored values, importance, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked what Isabella wanted to focus on and acknowledged her role in decision-making. However, the therapist often assumed an expert role by providing facts, plans, and partner scripts, which made the partnership feel less balanced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged Isabella’s emerging plan and supported her confidence with affirmations. However, change talk was not consistently amplified or developed through deeper questions about values, benefits, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and generally respected Isabella’s autonomy. However, when she expressed sustain talk, the response often repeated facts and guidelines rather than normalizing ambivalence or gently pivoting toward her values.
