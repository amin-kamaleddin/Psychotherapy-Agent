# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician remains respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental throughout. Liora’s limits and self-harm-related ambivalence are normalized without shame or excessive optimism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures exhaustion, ambivalence, and the function/cost of self-harm. Some reflections are slightly interpretive and miss explicit emotional content such as feeling like a burden.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on safer coping and reducing reliance on self-harm. Direction could be sharper by connecting micro-strategies to a clearer safety or self-harm-reduction goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician frames work as shared and follows Liora’s emerging ideas. However, the clinician still drives much of the conversation, limiting Liora’s role in setting priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong and consistent, with little pressure or direct advice. Liora is repeatedly positioned as the expert on her own experience, choices, and limits.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses many client-centered questions to draw out Liora’s own ideas. Evocation could be stronger by lessening supplied options and more systematically eliciting change talk dimensions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician conveys that Liora knows her own experience and invites her perspective. Partnership could be stronger by more explicitly sharing agenda control and co-creating next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician evokes some client-generated change language around safer coping. Change talk remains tentative and is not consistently deepened across desire, ability, reasons, need, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument or direct persuasion, often reflecting both the function and cost of self-harm. The clinician could more consistently use double-sided reflections or pivots to convert doubt into change talk.
