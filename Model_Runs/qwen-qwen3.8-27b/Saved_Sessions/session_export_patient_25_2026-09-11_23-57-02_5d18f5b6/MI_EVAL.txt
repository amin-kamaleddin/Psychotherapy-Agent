# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, repeatedly validating the patient’s effort and confidence. Deeper validation of the patient’s mixed feelings and preference for simplicity would strengthen acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed some factual understanding but generally reflected only surface-level content. More exploration of the patient’s pride, autonomy, worry, and social context was needed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behaviors of smoking cessation and alcohol use at the barbecue. However, the direction was more plan-driven than guided by the patient’s own reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were some collaborative moments, especially in scheduling follow-up, but the overall stance was advisor-driven. The psychiatrist offered repeated recommendations rather than co-creating a simple plan with the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged choice and declined scripted language at times, supporting some autonomy. Autonomy support was weakened by repeatedly recommending specific actions after the patient expressed a low-effort approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked some opening questions but did not deeply explore the patient’s values, confidence, or motivations. The patient’s statements of readiness were not meaningfully expanded into change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked for preferences but quickly shifted to offering practical options and reminders. A stronger partnership would have followed the patient’s lead more consistently and resisted repeated advice-giving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offered clear change talk, but the psychiatrist did not consistently reflect, amplify, or deepen it. More evoking questions would have helped build the patient’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not argue with the patient’s statements and used some normalizing language to soften sustain talk. However, the clinician repeatedly re-offered plan elements without using MI-consistent pivots to resolve ambivalence.
