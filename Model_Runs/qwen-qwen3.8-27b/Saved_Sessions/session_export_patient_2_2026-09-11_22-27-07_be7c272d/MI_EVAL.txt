# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance while honoring the client’s alcohol boundary. Warmth is generally consistent but can feel scripted and repetitive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is reasonable perspective-taking about the client’s busy schedule and respect for her limits. Deeper emotional material, such as exhaustion, protective pressure, and self-critical thoughts, is not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on evening smoking reduction and practical behavior change. It could be stronger with a clearer agenda, readiness/importance rulers, and concrete follow-up planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates on a simple plan using the client’s own ideas, such as locking the lighter and using the spoon. Collaboration is somewhat uneven because the counselor also offers multiple tactics and repeatedly restates the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports choice by avoiding quit pressure and reinforcing client ownership. Repeated provision of specific routines can slightly limit the client’s sense of directing the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client motivation is elicited through questions about the function of smoking and doable tactics. Evocation is inconsistent, with the counselor often supplying reasons and solutions rather than drawing out values, confidence, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally invites client input and builds on her practical preferences. The early delivery of a tactic list and mini-routine can feel more expert-driven than collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits a concrete reduction goal, a son-centered reason, and client-generated cues. Change talk is acknowledged but not consistently deepened or amplified through more targeted questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and accepts the client’s pace toward reduction rather than abstinence. Sustain talk remains largely unexplored and is not actively softened with double-sided or ambivalence-focused reflections.
