# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, nonjudgment, and a supportive tone. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic and behavior-focused rather than fully unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some adequate reflections of Mei’s perspective, but empathy is often interrupted by problem-solving. Important emotional statements are acknowledged rather than explored deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on reducing alcohol use and improving birth control reliability. The goal progression is organized, though achieved through a prescriptive agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The conversation feels more like expert-driven advice than a collaborative partnership. The psychiatrist presents pre-made plans and options rather than co-creating solutions with Mei.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although choices are offered, they are mostly clinician-generated. Mei’s own ideas are not prioritized, which weakens her sense of autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes limited efforts to draw out Mei’s own motivations, values, and reasons for change. The session shifts too quickly to advice and planning rather than evoking client change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently takes on an expert role and provides structured plans and options. This limits the establishment of a true partnership with Mei.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mei offers some change talk, but it is rarely reflected, amplified, or deepened. The psychiatrist does not consistently explore what matters to her or strengthen her reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates Mei’s ambivalence and does not reinforce sustain talk. However, the responses rely on practical alternatives rather than MI-consistent techniques to explore or soften resistance.
