# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated nonjudgmental support, affirmed Daniel’s strengths, and avoided moralizing. The acceptance was strong but somewhat procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathic responding was limited and often replaced with factual assessment or advice. Key emotional material, such as the hangover affecting bedtime and fear of ruining progress, was not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stayed focused on weekend drinking and built a concrete plan. However, repeated procedural safety content and checklist restatement reduced the strategic focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited input, offered options, and tailored the plan to Daniel’s preferences. The collaboration was strong but sometimes felt more like the clinician providing a plan than co-creating it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supported choice by asking what Daniel wanted to focus on and allowing him to select a doable strategy. Autonomy support was strong but somewhat diluted by the volume of specific instructions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist did not consistently evoke Daniel’s intrinsic motivation, values, or confidence. Rich material about quitting smoking, being present for his son, and avoiding guilt was not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used collaborative language and allowed Daniel’s choices to shape the plan. However, the frequent expert-led advice and detailed instructions limited a fuller sense of partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk through open questions and plan-building. Daniel’s own motivation was present but not consistently reinforced, expanded, or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was mild and was not reinforced or challenged. The psychiatrist mostly accepted the concern and moved into planning rather than actively softening ambivalence with MI skills.
