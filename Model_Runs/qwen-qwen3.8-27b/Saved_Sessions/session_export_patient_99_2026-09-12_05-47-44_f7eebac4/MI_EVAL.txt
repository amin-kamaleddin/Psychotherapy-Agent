# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Matteo’s priorities. Acceptance could be deeper and more emotionally affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the tension between recovery and social life. More emotional depth would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on drinking, medication safety, and recovery goals. It could be more client-driven and evocative.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on Matteo’s idea to contact the prescriber/pharmacist. More co-created options from Matteo’s perspective would improve collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly affirms choice and avoids pressure. Stronger explicit autonomy statements and fewer prescriptive scripts would help.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions are used, but practical planning interrupts deeper exploration. More values- and goal-based questions are needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Matteo’s input influences the session, but the psychiatrist often assumes an expert role. More power sharing and client-generated options would be stronger.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk about protecting recovery and basketball. Deeper evocation of importance, confidence, and future goals is limited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not challenged and is often validated appropriately. The psychiatrist does not consistently reframe sustain talk into change talk.
