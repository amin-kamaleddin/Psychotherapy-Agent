# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with multiple affirmations and validations. Warmth is somewhat procedural, affirming effort rather than extending deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some validation and perspective-taking present, but reflective listening frequently skipped in favor of problem-solving. Missed key emotional moments including the bedtime self-criticism and worry about guilt and defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong momentum with a clear, specific thread from values through to a concrete, testable behavior change plan. No topic drift; every response advanced toward the target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine co-creation in several exchanges with inclusive, option-offering language. Tilts toward clinician-heavy because much of the framework and script content was supplied rather than evoked.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, explicit affirmation of choice and respect for the client's selections throughout. Some suggestions are fairly directive in content (timer, specific script, partner text), subtly nudging rather than leaving full design open.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Values elicitation and confidence ratings effectively surfaced and tracked internal motivation (7→8). Heavily scaffolded by clinician-provided scripts and definitions at times rather than fully eliciting the client's own language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client's contributions shaped the session and options were offered warmly. However, the psychiatrist assumed a heavy expert/structuring role, supplying most of the cognitive work (frameworks, scripts, plans), which limits the sense of client-internal wisdom.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence ratings and targeted questions elicited clear desire, commitment, and ability language from the client. Could have more explicitly amplified and summarized the deeper meaning of the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mild normalization offered without harmful reinforcement, but no active softening strategies used (double-sided reflections, pivots, reframing). Mostly worked around the sustain talk by adding more structure.
