# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with supportive phrases, but warmth is often generic. Some advice and risk framing can feel evaluative rather than fully accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers helpful summaries of Ethan’s perspective, such as wanting to fit in and disliking hangovers. However, reflections remain surface-level and quickly shift into advice rather than exploring deeper emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, safety, hangovers, and a concrete harm-reduction plan. The main limitation is that direction is somewhat prescriptive rather than fully client-generated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks for input and adjusts some plan details based on Ethan’s preferences. Nonetheless, the interaction remains largely counselor-led, with Ethan often reacting to an expert-provided menu of options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist includes choice language and avoids forcing compliance, but repeatedly offers detailed strategies and scripts that can feel directive. Ethan’s pushback suggests some suggestions may not fully support his autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor makes some initial open-ended questions but quickly moves into advice and problem-solving. There is limited effort to evoke Ethan’s own values, goals, confidence, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session shows some responsiveness because Ethan’s choices influence the final plan, especially avoiding a phone reminder and accepting a silent vibration. However, the counselor’s agenda and repeated suggestions make the relationship feel more advisory than equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ethan offers some change talk, but the counselor does not consistently amplify, summarize, or develop it into stronger motivation. Missing evocative techniques such as importance and confidence rulers limit development.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor does not directly reinforce sustain talk, but often responds with advice and risk information instead of MI-consistent redirection. Double-sided reflections and gentle exploration of discrepancies are needed.
