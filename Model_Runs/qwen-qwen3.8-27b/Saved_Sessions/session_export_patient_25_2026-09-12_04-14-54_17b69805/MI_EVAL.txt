# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect, nonjudgment, and positive regard while affirming Daniel’s smoking cessation. The tone is warm but somewhat formulaic, with less validation of the deeper shame or anxiety around alcohol use.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflective listening to capture Daniel’s social triggers, low-key preferences, and practical concerns. Empathy does not fully reach the emotional core of Daniel’s fear of being seen as unable to “handle two beers.”

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on alcohol use in social situations and the upcoming BBQ. The progression from identifying the concern to a concrete, low-pressure plan is coherent and well directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is largely co-constructed, with Daniel contributing important elements such as the tumbler, self-pouring, reminder, and exit strategy. However, the psychiatrist remains the primary generator of many practical options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Daniel’s autonomy by using option-based language and respecting his preference for a low-key approach. It could be strengthened by more explicit affirmation of Daniel’s freedom to adjust, pause, or abandon the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Daniel’s confidence, barriers, and own problem-solving ideas through scaling and open-ended questions. Evocation is practical but does not deeply access Daniel’s values or identity-based reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Daniel as the expert by asking his preferences and incorporating his choices into the plan. Still, the counselor-led menu of tactics limits fully shared power before Daniel generates more of his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully draws out change-relevant language through importance/confidence scaling, affirmations, and Daniel’s own plan-oriented statements. It could be stronger by more explicitly eliciting deeper DARN-C language, especially values and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes and works within Daniel’s preference not to “make it a thing” without challenging him. More advanced MI moves, such as double-sided reflections, would better resolve ambivalence, especially the final concern about tiredness and peer pressure.
