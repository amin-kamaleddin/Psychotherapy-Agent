# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, with genuine validation of Sofia's experience (e.g., 'That's not a flaw. That's just what it looks like when you're still in the middle of it.'). Occasionally editorializes slightly (e.g., 'I want to gently push back'), which introduces a subtle directive element that softens unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional empathic accuracy with progressively deeper reflections that name what Sofia couldn't articulate (e.g., 'emptiness shaped like a Friday night,' 'You're present but not in it'). The reflections track the emotional arc from surface ambivalence to identity fear with remarkable sophistication.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains coherent focus on alcohol use while skillfully following Sofia's lead into deeper identity and autonomy issues, with each exchange building logically. Could tighten by periodically tethering abstract insights (identity, selfhood) back to the concrete behavioral target of abstinence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clearly positions Sofia as an equal partner using exploratory language ('I'm curious,' 'What do you think?') and never dominates the conversation. Actively incorporates Sofia's metaphors and language back into the session, demonstrating her contributions genuinely drive the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** One of the strongest elements, with consistent ownership language ('What does that mean for YOU?' 'What would it look like if it was yours?') and zero directive pressure. The psychiatrist never makes decisions for Sofia, never lectures, and explicitly removes pressure ('You don't have to build the whole house tonight').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits Sofia's own values and motivations without providing external reasons for change, using evocative questions that draw out her identity and self-awareness. Could be strengthened by building toward concrete commitment language (e.g., confidence scaling, specific plans tied to the baby).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Sofia as the expert on her own life, with the session clearly shaped by her contributions and metaphors. Even the 'push back' moment is framed collaboratively as a suggestion rather than a correction, and no information is provided in a didactic manner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and amplifies change talk through reflective deepening ('That's the first brick,' 'You checked in. For real.') and evocative questions about what change would look like. Could more explicitly target DARN-C categories and build toward concrete commitment language rather than staying at the values/identity level.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating, not arguing, and using classic MI moves like 'What part of it do you think you'd miss the most?' to acknowledge real value in the status quo. The most potent sustain talk (identity fear: 'I don't know who I am if I'm not that') is left somewhat unresolved, as the session ends at that point.
