# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respected Marisol’s choice and avoided judgment or pressure. Acceptance was clear and supportive, though somewhat procedural rather than deeply warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used good reflections of Marisol’s values, ambivalence, and preference for respect. Empathy was accurate but did not consistently deepen the underlying emotional significance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The conversation stayed broadly relevant to the referral around oral health and the piercing. However, the target behavior was not clearly clarified or consistently directed toward a specific change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Marisol’s perspective and treated her as an expert on her own mouth and preferences. Collaboration was strong, but the agenda remained mostly therapist-guided rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Marisol’s autonomy was consistently honored and actively reinforced throughout the session. The psychiatrist avoided advisory or coercive language and supported client-led decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicited practical self-care ideas and a useful boundary statement for the dentist. Evocation was present but limited in depth, with little exploration of values, confidence, or deeper motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative stance and avoided an expert-driven approach. Partnership was solid but could have been strengthened by more explicit agenda negotiation and client direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session elicited some client statements supporting care while keeping the piercing. Change talk was present but not systematically amplified, expanded, or developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handled sustain talk well by avoiding argumentation and using double-sided reflections. It would have been stronger with more consistent work to deepen ambivalence.
