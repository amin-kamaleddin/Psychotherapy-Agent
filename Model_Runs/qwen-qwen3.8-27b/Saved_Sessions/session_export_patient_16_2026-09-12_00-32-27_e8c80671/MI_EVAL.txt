# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses supportive, nonjudgmental phrases but quickly shifts into advice and accountability language that can feel pressuring. Acceptance is present but inconsistent and not deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are brief empathic statements about frustration and wanting to chill. However, the response remains surface-level and misses the client’s deeper ambivalence around fun, basketball, and trust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on THC use and related school/basketball concerns. Direction is clear but more managerial and compliance-focused than motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client is offered choices about messaging and disclosure options. Still, the interaction is largely therapist-led, with the psychiatrist supplying the plan and drafting the language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are moments of offering options and revising the plan to the client’s preference. These are outweighed by repeated directives, prescribed scripts, and suggested disclosures.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are mostly factual or procedural rather than evocative. Strong opportunities to explore values, confidence, and reasons for change are not fully used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client is asked what she wants to talk about and which option feels better. Nevertheless, the psychiatrist controls the agenda and solution process, limiting true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers clear change talk about basketball performance and a one-month goal. The psychiatrist captures some of it in drafts but does not actively elicit or strengthen it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not endorse the no-harm framing, which is a positive. However, sustain talk is met with practical directives and scripts rather than rolling with resistance or double-sided reflections.
