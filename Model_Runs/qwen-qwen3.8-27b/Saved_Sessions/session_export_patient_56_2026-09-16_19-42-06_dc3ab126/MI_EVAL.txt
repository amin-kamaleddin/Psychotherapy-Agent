# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, warm, and affirming, treating the client's family-centered values as meaningful. Repeated tension/burden framing is not fully attuned to the client's stated clarity, limiting the rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are frequent but often inaccurate, with several misreadings of the client's perspective. The psychiatrist repeatedly imposes a tension, burden, and harder-day frame that the client explicitly rejects.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior of being present rather than managing or controlling. A minor repeated question slightly interrupts momentum, and a clearer maintenance summary would strengthen it.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on the client's ideas and uses collaborative language, making the conversation feel partnership-based. However, continued exploration of protective strategies does not fully accept the client's statement that she does not need a new plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids directives and emphasizes that the client knows her family and needs best. Repeated focus on protecting against harder days subtly frames her as vulnerable despite her expressed confidence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out the client's values, confidence, and own reasoning through open questions. The main weakness is that evocation is often framed around risk or harder days rather than the client's stable maintenance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions the client as the expert and invites her perspective rather than giving advice. The main limitation is that the overall direction is still set by a protective, risk-focused frame the client does not endorse.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful maintenance and confidence statements, such as the client feeling herself rather than just a manager. The risk-focused framing constrains the development of more fully self-generated change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is little authentic sustain talk, and the psychiatrist does not reinforce a harmful status quo. The criterion is limited because it keeps returning to an artificial tension/burden theme that the client rejects.
