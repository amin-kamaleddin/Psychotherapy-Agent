# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive tone without shaming Elena. Acceptance is strong, though occasionally formulaic and can slightly minimize the emotional weight of her fear and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides adequate reflective listening but often stays at a cognitive/content level. More emotional depth is needed around her fear, loneliness in the decision, partner dynamics, and first-time reproductive uncertainty.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays relevant to contraception after a pregnancy scare but feels scattered across many topics. A clearer MI direction toward ambivalence and her own goals would improve focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and invites input, but the interaction often becomes expert-driven with long method overviews and repeated options. More client-led agenda-setting would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently affirms Elena’s choice and pacing, which supports autonomy. However, repeated suggestions and next-step guidance can still feel like the psychiatrist is managing the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with helpful open questions but quickly shifts to information-giving and concrete planning. More scaling, values exploration, and elicitation of Elena’s own motivations are needed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and uses collaborative language, but Elena is not consistently positioned as the expert on her body and readiness. Her contributions could better shape the direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elena offers a meaningful change statement about using condoms consistently, but the psychiatrist does not strongly amplify or build on it. The session shifts to practical tips rather than strengthening her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elena’s sustain talk is common, and the psychiatrist avoids arguing, but does not effectively soften or explore it. Responses like “calm, sensible plan” risk reinforcing avoidance rather than resolving ambivalence.
