# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently nonjudgmental, respectful, and supportive stance throughout the session. It falls short of a 5 mainly because the affirmations can sound formulaic and do not fully normalize Diego’s ambivalence and human struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early in the session, the psychiatrist offers a strong complex reflection of Diego’s ambivalence and pain. Later, empathy becomes more practical and repetitive, missing deeper emotional undercurrents such as exhaustion, shame, isolation, and fear of awkwardness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is strongly and consistently focused on a clear target: harm reduction and safety planning. The conversation stays directed toward concrete, doable behaviors such as making Narcan accessible, texting the housemate, and keeping emergency resources nearby.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** There is clear collaboration in developing practical safety steps, including the text, lock-screen note, and alarm plan. However, the collaboration is somewhat counselor-led, with options and scripts often offered before fully inviting Diego to generate his own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Diego’s autonomy by offering choices, respecting his decision not to quit, and honoring his boundary around setting up SOS features. It could improve by more explicitly reinforcing Diego’s ownership and role as the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some client-driven motivation and concrete action around harm reduction. However, evocation is limited because the counselor frequently moves into practical advice rather than exploring Diego’s values, confidence, or deeper reasons for staying safe.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Diego’s input and lets his choices shape the safety plan. Partnership is sometimes weakened by the frequent provision of multiple options, scripts, and detailed recommendations that can make the session feel more counselor-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego generates meaningful change talk related to safety and harm reduction, and the psychiatrist supports these statements. It would be stronger if the counselor more consistently reflected, amplified, or explored Diego’s reasons, confidence, and future self connected to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk and keeps the session focused on harm reduction. However, there are few active MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections or gentle pivots, used to explore and soften ambivalence around continued use.
