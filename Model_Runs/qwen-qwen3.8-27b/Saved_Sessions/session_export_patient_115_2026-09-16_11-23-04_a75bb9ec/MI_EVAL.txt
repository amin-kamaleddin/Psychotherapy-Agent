# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, and avoids criticizing Samir’s continued smoking or drinking. Acceptance is strong but not fully unconditional, as warmth is often tied to Samir’s practicality and plan adherence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections of emotion and burden, such as fear of nighttime chest tightness and family responsibility. However, empathy often remains at the content or practical level rather than deeply exploring what smoking and alcohol mean to Samir.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on asthma-related behavior change and progresses logically from concerns to a concrete plan. Direction is somewhat narrowed by task-centered logistics and limited exploration of dust/environmental exposures.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is collaborative, and the final plan is largely shaped by Samir’s own priorities and ideas. Collaboration is not fully balanced because the psychiatrist frequently supplies structured options, backup plans, and scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Samir’s sense of choice and avoids overt pressure through autonomy-supportive language. Autonomy support is slightly reduced by frequent clinician-generated options, plans, and suggested scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks helpful questions about focus, readiness, confidence, barriers, and possible steps. Evocation is limited because the session often shifts to practical advice instead of drawing out Samir’s deeper values and intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Samir as the expert and frequently invites his input when shaping the plan. Partnership is strong but not fully client-led because the clinician often introduces structured options and backup strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Samir offers meaningful change talk, and the psychiatrist affirms progress and asks readiness/confidence questions. Cultivation is limited by the quick move into behavior planning and reliance on clinician-generated options rather than deeper motivational questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with Samir’s sustain talk and validates the logic behind it. Softening is not consistently skillful because he often moves quickly to alternatives instead of using double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence.
