# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses supportive, nonjudgmental affirmations and avoids shaming, which creates a generally respectful tone. However, the warmth feels somewhat procedural and does not fully acknowledge Adrian's shame, embarrassment, or social worries.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers several reflective statements that capture Adrian's goals and ambivalence. However, the reflections remain mostly content-based and miss deeper emotional material such as shame, fear of conflict, boredom, or peer belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing infection risk and other harms related to snorting. The psychiatrist effectively narrows the discussion from general use to a concrete harm-reduction plan, though it becomes somewhat repetitive at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Adrian's input and incorporates his preferences, such as visible spares and a phone reminder. However, the frequent counselor-generated options and scripts create a somewhat expert-driven rather than fully co-constructed feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently acknowledges Adrian's choices and respects his decisions, including setting the reminder himself. Autonomy support is slightly undermined by repeated prompts to rehearse lines and increase confidence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks evocative questions about goals, small steps, and confidence, leading to practical commitment language. However, the session does not deeply explore Adrian's values, internal motivations, or the broader meaning of staying healthy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites Adrian's preferences and concerns. However, the interaction often becomes more like selecting from the counselor's menu than a fully collaborative, client-centered process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful change talk around avoiding another infection, using his own supplies, and setting reminders. However, the psychiatrist often supplies options and scripts instead of allowing Adrian to generate more of his own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Adrian's sustain talk and appropriately shifts toward harm-reduction options. However, the session does not use stronger MI techniques, such as double-sided reflections or explicit ambivalence exploration, before moving into planning.
