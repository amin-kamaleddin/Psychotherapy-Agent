# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating, using affirmations and low-pressure language. Warmth is somewhat procedural and misses a mild client pushback, keeping it below 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reasonably reflects the client’s perspective, especially around drinking as relief and ambivalence. Empathy remains mostly cognitive and moves too quickly into advice before exploring emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus stays clearly on alcohol use, negative consequences, and a concrete first-drink experiment with follow-up monitoring. The session becomes overly procedural on beer logistics, which slightly narrows exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The tone is collaborative and the client can choose or reject some options, such as the non-alcoholic beer. However, the psychiatrist supplies most of the plan content, so collaboration is moderate rather than fully co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently asks permission, offers options, avoids pressure, and accepts “no promises” without coercion. The prescriptive plan is somewhat directive, preventing a perfect score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are limited early questions about drinking, consequences, and preferences, but little deep elicitation of values, meaning, or confidence. The session shifts too quickly from evocation to supplying practical solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some early partnership appears through asking permission and inviting choices, but the stance becomes expert-driven with a detailed intervention list. The client’s input affects details but does not substantially shape direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers useful change talk, such as wanting clearer mornings and being fair to himself, but it is not meaningfully amplified or developed. The psychiatrist does not use follow-up questions, scaling, or summarization to strengthen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and validates the client’s points, but does not effectively soften sustain talk. Minimal double-sided reflection leaves ambivalence unresolved and keeps the plan centered on making the current pattern more acceptable.
