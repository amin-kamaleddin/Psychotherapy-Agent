# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and affirming while normalizing the client’s conflict. It is below 5 because some responses add positive attribution or interpretation rather than pure acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects ambivalence, fear of judgment, shame, and the client’s identity conflict. It is below 5 because a few reflections move into interpretation or reframing rather than staying purely in the client’s frame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on drinking during pregnancy and moves purposefully from ambivalence to practical refusal, self-talk, and values. There is no meaningful drift from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is strongly collaborative, with the client’s ideas about lunch instead of happy hour, refusal language, shame, and values driving the work. The psychiatrist builds on the client’s emerging themes rather than directing an agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice through open questions and avoids direct pressure or advice. It is below 5 because explicit affirmation that the decision is entirely the client’s is not frequent enough, especially in moments of self-doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws out the client’s internal values and reasons for change, especially protecting the baby and acting from worthiness. It is below 5 because some interpretations are more counselor-generated than client-evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats the client as the expert and invites her judgment, keeping the client central. It is below 5 because occasional conceptual framing is more counselor-driven than purely partnership-oriented.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits value-based change talk about protecting the baby, self-respect, and showing up. It is below 5 because confidence, ability, and commitment are not consistently strengthened or consolidated into a stronger plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client’s sustain talk about missing out, friendship, and social identity without arguing against it. It is below 5 because the session remains centered in ambivalence rather than more explicitly de-emphasizing the pull of the old pattern.
