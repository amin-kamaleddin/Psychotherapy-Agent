# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance throughout. The score is not higher because the acceptance feels somewhat procedural and tied to the client's chosen behaviors rather than deeper, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are helpful reflections of ambivalence and some affect, but empathy often remains cognitive or behavioral. The psychiatrist misses opportunities to more fully capture the client's deeper social fear, awkwardness, and worry about judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior and narrows the work to a concrete, manageable cue. The direction is strong, though it becomes overly procedural and plan-focused at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and adapts when the client rejects suggestions. However, the psychiatrist remains the main source of ideas, and the client is often choosing from a pre-generated list rather than leading the exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses low-pressure language, frames the plan as an experiment, and respects the client's preference for a minimal step. Some repeated suggestions of specific commitments introduce mild pressure despite the overall autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation is the weakest area, with limited elicitation of the client's own values, hopes, confidence, or reasons for change. Much of the session is more directive than evocative, and the client's change talk is not fully explored or strengthened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites choices and avoids an authoritarian style, supporting partnership. However, the client is mostly selecting from the psychiatrist's options rather than driving the agenda or contributing her own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or explore it to build motivation. The focus shifts quickly toward behavioral options rather than strengthening the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with the client's reasons for staying home and acknowledges that staying home feels safe. However, the session does not consistently or skillfully soften sustain talk, often moving to action lists before fully exploring the ambivalence.
