

# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4/5
**Comments:** Generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive. The psychiatrist normalizes upset/anxiety, avoids blame or failure language, and supports ambivalence without judgment. Acceptance could be deepened further around shame, fear of becoming “like my dad,” and affirmation of worth beyond compliance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4/5
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening, captures feelings of out-of-control, uncertainty, and ambivalence, and responds sensitively to reproductive concerns. Empathy could be stronger by lingering longer on deeper emotional themes, such as shame, family-history fear, and social awkwardness, before moving into options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4/5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol-related risk, contraceptive reliability, and a concrete short-term plan. The session moves logically from concern to immediate risk to actionable steps. Direction could be sharper by more explicitly linking the immediate plan to the broader goal of reducing risky alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4/5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative problem-solving is evident, with options adjusted based on the patient’s preferences and the final plan reflecting her choices. Collaboration could be strengthened by more explicitly inviting the patient to co-design or organize the plan rather than presenting structured menus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 4/5
**Comments:** Supports patient choice and avoids pressure, including respecting her decision not to send the text or set the alarm during the session. Some phrasing remains directive; autonomy support would improve with clearer affirmations that the decision is entirely the patient’s and with more invitational language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 3/5
**Comments:** Uses some open-ended questions to elicit motivation and plan-related thinking, but the session frequently shifts into providing clinical information, options, and scripts. More evocation of the patient’s own values, confidence, hopes, and past strengths would strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 3/5
**Comments:** Some partnership is present through questions about focus and preference, and the patient’s choices shape the final plan. However, the psychiatrist often takes an expert-driven role with structured option menus. More explicit positioning of the patient as the expert on her own life would strengthen partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 3/5
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces some change-relevant language, including concerns about family history, health, and avoiding future lapses. The psychiatrist could more consistently amplify, reflect, and expand the patient’s own reasons for change, including importance and confidence questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** [x] 3/5
**Comments:** Responds to sustain talk with normalization, low-pressure language, and avoidance of argument or judgment. The psychiatrist could more actively use MI strategies such as double-sided reflections, reframing, or exploring the implications of the status quo relative to the patient’s values to pivot toward change.
