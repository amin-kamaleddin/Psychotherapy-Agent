# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is consistently respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, using affirmations that convey positive regard. Acceptance is occasionally generic or task-oriented, with less explicit validation of ambivalence and deeper emotions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates good reflective listening and accurately tracks Laura’s values, independence, and concerns. Empathy is mainly cognitive and plan-focused, missing some opportunities to reflect deeper emotions and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on developing a small, values-aligned morning routine. There is no significant topic drift, and the flow from values to concrete steps is efficient and well organized.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates well by inviting Laura to choose, personalize, and adapt the plan. The collaboration is strong, though the clinician occasionally introduces more options than a fully client-led process would require.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is a major strength, with repeated respect for Laura’s choice, ownership, and desire to try independently. The psychiatrist avoids pressure and consistently reinforces that Laura is in charge of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively evokes Laura’s own motivation, cues, confidence, and self-compassion language. However, some clinician-generated examples reduce the purity of evocation, as waiting longer for client-generated ideas could strengthen this area.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and frequently invites Laura’s input in shaping the session. The interaction is strong but somewhat clinician-guided due to the structured agenda and concrete options offered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits substantial change talk, including readiness, confidence, specific commitments, and self-compassion. There is less consistent amplification or expansion of change talk after it appears.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids reinforcing sustain talk and handles some concerns by normalizing and co-creating compassionate responses. However, there is limited active softening through double-sided reflections or explicit reframing of Laura’s worries.
