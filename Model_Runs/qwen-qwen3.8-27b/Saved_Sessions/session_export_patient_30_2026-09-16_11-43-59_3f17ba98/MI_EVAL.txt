# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates David’s concerns without labeling him. Acceptance is slightly limited by repeatedly offering scripts and reminders, which can feel mildly directive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides accurate reflections of David’s worries about muscle weakness, work, and family. Empathy is somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore the deeper ambivalence behind his resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on cholesterol medication and practical next steps. Direction is narrow, with limited movement toward broader MI goals such as values, confidence, and change planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and acknowledges David’s preference for brevity. Collaboration is uneven because the psychiatrist still drives the content by supplying questions, scripts, and safety advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David’s autonomy is frequently respected, especially his preference for simplicity and his refusal of notes or reminders. Support is reduced somewhat by the repeated offering of the same exact line and reminder options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with appropriate MI questions about thoughts, values, and readiness. Evocation is limited because the session quickly shifts to providing scripts, medical questions, and practical logistics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and invites David’s preferences. Partnership is partial because the session remains expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying much of the plan rather than helping David develop it independently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few early attempts to invite change talk, but the psychiatrist does not effectively follow up on David’s statements about being present for family. Change talk is not systematically evoked, expanded, or turned into commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges David’s side-effect concerns respectfully but does not effectively soften or redirect them. Sustain talk remains prominent because the plan repeatedly centers stopping the medication if symptoms occur.
