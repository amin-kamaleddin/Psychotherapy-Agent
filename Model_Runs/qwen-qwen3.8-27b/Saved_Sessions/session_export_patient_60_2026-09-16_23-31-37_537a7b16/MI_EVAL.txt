# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and conveyed positive regard through validating statements. Acceptance was strong but slightly formulaic, without fully affirming Jason's worth independent of the behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captured Jason's frustration, concern about consequences, and desire to be seen as more than one incident. Empathy could be deeper by exploring underlying fear, shame, anger, or identity threat.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the incident and Jason's concerns, but it centered more on procedural fairness than the target behavior. More MI direction would connect alcohol use, risk, values, and future choices.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated Jason as a partner by inviting him to define fairness and shaping the conversation around his priorities. Collaboration was present but could be strengthened with explicit joint problem-solving and a shared next step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided coercion, advice, or pressure and supported Jason's sense of control through open questions. Autonomy support could be stronger by explicitly affirming Jason's ownership of choices and capacity for change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some of Jason's motivations were elicited, especially around protecting his record and standing. Evocation was limited because motivation remained mostly external rather than connected to intrinsic values, identity, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a collaborative, non-expert stance and followed Jason's agenda through open questions. Partnership would be stronger with explicit agenda negotiation and clearer shared decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicited some change talk around accountability and preserving values, but did not consistently deepen DARN language. Missed opportunities included amplifying Jason's own statements about not liking out-of-control parties.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and gently redirected toward responsibility, but the session allowed considerable minimization and sustain talk. More effective softening would include double-sided reflections and discrepancy-focused redirection.
