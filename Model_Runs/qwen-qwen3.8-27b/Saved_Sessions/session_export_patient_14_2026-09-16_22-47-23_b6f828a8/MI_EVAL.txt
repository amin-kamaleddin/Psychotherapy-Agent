# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the client's burdens without shaming. Acceptance is strong but sometimes remains practical rather than consistently deepening unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track the client's need for efficiency, low mental load, and practicality. Empathy is generally strong but could explore underlying emotions such as frustration or fear more directly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on weight, blood pressure, and developing a realistic behavior change. Direction could be stronger by consolidating the emerging plan and preparing for the immediate next action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor builds on the client's language and allows his preferences to shape the plan. Collaboration is effective, though it could be more explicitly framed as equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids pressure and uses questions that emphasize the client's choice and ownership. Autonomy support is good but could include more explicit affirmation of the client's right to choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively draw out the client's own ideas, standards, and practical values. Evocation could deepen further by exploring past successes, confidence, and broader reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the expert on his own schedule and workflow. Partnership could strengthen through more explicit co-construction of the agenda and plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist helps the client generate useful change ideas such as water substitution and automated BP logging. Change talk could be strengthened by explicitly amplifying and reinforcing the client's own commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing but does not consistently use double-sided reflections to validate and soften sustain talk. More skillful reflection of ambivalence would help the client resolve doubts while moving toward change.
