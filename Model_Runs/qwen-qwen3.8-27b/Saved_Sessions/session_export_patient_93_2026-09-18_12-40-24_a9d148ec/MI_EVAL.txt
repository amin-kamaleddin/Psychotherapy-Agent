# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently validates Margaret's minimization and frustration without judgment, using warm and specific affirmations throughout. Notable example: 'You're miffed, and that's fair' and 'I'm not trying to make it dramatic' perfectly model unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate, nuanced, and deepen over the session, such as connecting 'the kind where you just drift' to the emotional weight of post-retirement loneliness. The complex reflection 'Both of those can be true at once' skillfully captures Margaret's ambivalence without taking sides.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session follows a clear logical arc from establishing the reason for the visit through exploring function to generating a concrete action plan. The psychiatrist maintains focus on the target behavior even when Margaret tries to minimize.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session follows Margaret's lead throughout, incorporating her themes (loneliness, the pub, gardening) into the exploration. The final action plan is entirely client-generated, with the psychiatrist's role limited to asking, reflecting, and affirming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every suggestion is framed as a voluntary option with explicit ownership language ('It's your call,' 'You're not committing to anything permanent'). Autonomy support is the default mode of interaction rather than a tactical intervention.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never provides information or advice, instead using open-ended and future-oriented questions to draw motivation entirely from Margaret. The result is genuine client-generated change talk that moves from minimal to specific and actionable.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Margaret as the expert, using invitational language and following her lead on content and direction. Margaret's contributions substantially shape the session, including the central thread and the action plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses future-oriented questions, amplification, and identity-linked affirmations to elicit Margaret's own change talk. Margaret moves from 'I don't see how a couple of glasses is a big deal' to a specific, self-generated action plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled through textbook double-sided reflections that validate the client's perspective while gently opening a door toward change. Each instance is acknowledged and then the conversation is redirected without the sustain talk ever dominating.
