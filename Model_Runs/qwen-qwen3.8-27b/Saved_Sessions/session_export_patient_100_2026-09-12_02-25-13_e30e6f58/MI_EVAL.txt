# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist opens with a warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming stance. It is slightly less than ideal because the warmth is brief and does not explicitly normalize the client's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks initial questions that invite the client's perspective. However, there is limited evidence of nuanced, accurate understanding of the client's specific situation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening is broad and does not clearly focus on the target behavior or referral concern. It lacks a gentle but specific connection to alcohol use and its impact on school functioning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses open questions and an autonomy-supportive stance that create space for client input. However, genuine two-way collaboration, such as co-creating priorities or a plan, is not strongly demonstrated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The statement "I'll listen and support whatever you decide" clearly supports client freedom of choice. It avoids pressure or directives, though it is based on a single statement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks a strong evocative question about what the client wants to change and why it matters. There is no further follow-up, reflection, or development of the client's own change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and supportive language position the client as the decision-maker. The counselor does not yet clearly demonstrate the client as the expert or co-creating the session agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist directly invites the client to identify a goal and its personal importance. The evocation is present but limited to one initial question without reinforcement or amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is no explicit sustain talk addressed, and the counselor does not argue or minimize concerns. The stance is neutral and non-harmful but lacks clear evidence of active softening or redirection.
