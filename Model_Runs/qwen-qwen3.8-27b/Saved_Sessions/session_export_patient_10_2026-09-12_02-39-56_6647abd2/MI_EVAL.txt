# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful stance and validates the client’s frustration. Acceptance is strong but remains brief and task-focused rather than deeply affirming the client’s broader identity and challenges.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician offers accurate reflections that capture both the emotional and practical dimensions of the client’s experience. Empathy is effective but sometimes surface-level, with missed opportunities to explore deeper values, fears, or identity-related concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on the target behavior of enabling Amina to attend job interviews despite childcare barriers. The conversation moves logically from understanding the barrier to practical options, script refinement, and rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and collaborates on choosing and refining the final wording. Collaboration is strong but somewhat uneven, as the clinician carries a large portion of the problem-solving burden.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently seeks permission, invites choice, and respects the client’s preferred wording and approach. The client is never pressured and remains clearly in control of the session and next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open questions that invite client input and self-generated decision-making. However, evocation is more practical than motivational and does not consistently draw out the client’s deeper reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician treats the client as an expert by inviting her input and allowing her choices to shape the session. Partnership is strong but could be further strengthened by inviting more client-generated ideas before offering practical suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses strong change talk, and the psychiatrist supports movement toward action by encouraging rehearsal. However, the clinician does not consistently amplify or deepen the client’s confidence, desires, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing the client’s barriers and gently redirects toward doable options. Sustain talk is softened effectively, but there is less consistent use of MI-style techniques to pivot barriers into change motivation.
