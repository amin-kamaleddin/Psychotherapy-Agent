# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment, validating the client and avoiding pressure. Acceptance is slightly procedural rather than deeply individualized, especially as the focus shifts toward planning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client’s ambivalence about coffee, jitteriness, sleep, and blood pressure. However, empathy becomes more surface-level as the conversation quickly moves into problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on excessive coffee consumption and its effects on sleep, productivity, and blood pressure. The conversation is well guided toward a concrete, behavior-specific experiment and follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client contributes meaningfully to the plan, including the tracking method, start time, and fallback strategy. Collaboration is somewhat uneven because the psychiatrist provides much of the structure and options early in the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly supports choice by asking the client to select options and confirm the plan. There are mild directive moments, such as suggesting reminders and specific decision rules, but the client remains the decision-maker.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relies more on practical options and planning than on drawing out the client’s own motivations, values, and confidence. The client’s change reasons are not deeply explored beyond the initial question.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client’s input and allows the client to shape key elements of the plan. However, the psychiatrist also supplies a substantial amount of structure, creating a less balanced collaborative dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some change-related statements, but the psychiatrist does not strongly elicit or expand them. Questions focus more on practical steps than on importance, confidence, values, or desired outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with the client and does not reinforce sustain talk, gently redirecting toward the experiment. However, the response is mostly neutral redirection rather than skilled softening or double-sided reflection.
