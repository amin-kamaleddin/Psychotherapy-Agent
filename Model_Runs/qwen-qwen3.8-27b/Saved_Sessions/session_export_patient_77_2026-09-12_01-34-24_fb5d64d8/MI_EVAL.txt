# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a nonjudgmental, validating tone and repeatedly reduces shame around the plateau and fear of failure. Acceptance is strong but more practical and reassuring than consistently affirming Liyana’s value independent of her body or productivity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects Liyana’s frustration, exhaustion, fear, and isolation. However, the reflections are often brief and quickly move into education or problem-solving without deeply exploring the emotional meaning behind her statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session generally stays focused on the weight-loss plateau, exercise, eating, and related emotional concerns, with a clear move toward a concrete next step. Direction is strong but occasionally shifts toward medical/hormonal explanations rather than staying tightly aligned with Liyana’s stated need for hope and support.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively adapts the plan to Liyana’s constraints, such as using yogurt or peanut butter and offering wall push-ups for privacy. Collaboration is strong in practice, though the clinician remains the main architect of the plan rather than fully co-creating it with Liyana.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently uses permission-based language and offers multiple low-pressure options, supporting Liyana’s sense of choice. Autonomy support is strong, but it is slightly limited by the introduction of a fairly structured plan and some prescriptive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows some interest in Liyana’s perspective, but the session relies heavily on psychoeducation and plan construction rather than eliciting her own motivations. There are few deep questions exploring Liyana’s values, hopes, confidence, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and adjusts the plan operationally, creating a partially collaborative stance. However, the session remains expert-driven, with the clinician providing most of the framework, rationale, and steps without fully placing Liyana as the primary source of wisdom or change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician elicits some action and commitment language, such as trying the yogurt, wall push-ups, and checking in later. However, change talk is not consistently deepened through questions about Liyana’s values, abilities, needs, or confidence in making change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist handles Liyana’s fears and reasons to maintain the status quo without arguing or dismissing them. Sustain talk is effectively softened by normalizing concern, reframing the experiment as low-pressure data collection, and introducing alternative measures of progress.
