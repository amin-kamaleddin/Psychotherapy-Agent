# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers some respectful and validating statements, but acceptance is inconsistent because it is quickly followed by directive medical instructions. The patient’s concern about being dismissed is not fully repaired with deeper unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are genuine moments of reflecting fear, frustration, and exhaustion, but the empathy remains somewhat surface-level. Deeper themes such as cancer fear, identity concerns, and existential worry are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the medical issue and does not drift into unrelated topics. However, it maintains a clear medical direction more than a consistent MI direction toward values, ambivalence, or personal change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative gestures, but the interaction remains mostly expert-driven rather than co-created. The patient’s concerns about not wanting to be difficult are not collaboratively worked through.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Choice is occasionally acknowledged, but the psychiatrist frequently provides scripts, tests, and firm next steps in a directive way. This can undermine the patient’s sense of ownership over her own process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited use of open questions, reflections, affirmations, or summaries to elicit the patient’s own motivations. Missed opportunities include her statements about self-strength, handling the truth, and identity tied to fighting.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often adopts a medical-advocacy stance rather than a true collaborative partnership. The power dynamic remains uneven because the plan is largely prescribed rather than shaped by the patient’s preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient offers several moments of change talk and self-efficacy, but the psychiatrist does not actively amplify or build on them. Responses tend to shift back to scripts and medical next steps instead of exploring her motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue directly, but the focus on urgent testing and escalation reinforces the patient’s fixed biomedical certainty. There is little use of double-sided reflection or softening to help her hold both possibilities.
