# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating tone throughout. Warmth was strong, though slightly task-focused rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate, complex, and emotionally attuned, capturing both hope and high-risk triggers. Minor tracking limitations and unexploited opportunities to deepen shame/fear processing prevented a perfect score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on reducing reoffending risk and moving toward a stable, prosocial life. Questions and reflections remained anchored to concrete next steps without irrelevant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used a collaborative stance and frequently invited the client’s input. However, structured menus made the interaction feel somewhat counselor-structured rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was consistently treated as the authority on his own life, goals, and readiness. Choices were presented without pressure, persuasion, or directive advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively drew out the client’s own values, capabilities, and reasons for change. The client generated meaningful change talk, self-efficacy, and action-oriented planning language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly conveyed that expertise about the client’s life and change resides within the client. Power was shared effectively through client-led questions and shared-process language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively elicited and strengthened the client’s own change language. Desire, reasons, ability, and planning statements were reflected, validated, and built upon skillfully.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without arguing and redirected toward agency and next steps. More advanced softening, such as double-sided or values-based reframing, was not consistently used.
