# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrates warm, genuine unconditional positive regard throughout, consistently affirming Daniel's worth and the legitimacy of his perspective. Praise is individualized and specific to Daniel's qualities (e.g., 'that's careful, that's consistent with who you are') rather than generic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate complex reflections capture not just content but the meaning and emotion beneath Daniel's words, such as 'it's less a choice and more of a default' and validating his epistemology ('your body's been your evidence'). The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates understanding of Daniel's internal frame of reference.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains crystal-clear focus on the target behavior (alcohol use in marathon training context) with a strategic, progressive arc from exploration to commitment. Each step builds on the previous one with clear momentum, and pacing is well-timed without rushing or drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames the interaction as a joint exploration between equals, using 'I'm curious' and collaborative questions to invite Daniel to co-construct solutions. The session ends with Daniel designing his own plan, reflecting a genuinely equal power dynamic throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every suggestion is framed as a question or invitation, never as 'you should' or 'I recommend.' The psychiatrist explicitly reinforces that Daniel is in control, the experiment is optional and low-stakes, and the decision is entirely his.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never provides factual information about alcohol's health effects or cites research; instead, Daniel is asked to examine his own data and draw his own conclusions. Motivation is consistently elicited from within Daniel through evocative questions like 'what would you need to see?'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Daniel as the expert on his own life and experience, never taking the expert role or offering medical information. The entire session is structured around Daniel's own reasoning, values, and data, with the psychiatrist serving purely as a curious, supportive guide.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful use of techniques like 'is there any part of you, even a small part, that's wondering...' and open-ended questions that naturally invite Daniel to articulate his own reasons for change. The psychiatrist skillfully draws out discrepancy between Daniel's values (being on top of his game) and his behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled with remarkable skill by fully validating Daniel's reasons for maintaining the status quo before gently redirecting. The psychiatrist never argues against the sustain talk; instead, it is validated so thoroughly that Daniel feels safe enough to consider the other side.
