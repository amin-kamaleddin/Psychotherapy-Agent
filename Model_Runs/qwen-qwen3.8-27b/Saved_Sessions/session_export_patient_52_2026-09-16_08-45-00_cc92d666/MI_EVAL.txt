# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, normalizes concerns, and affirms the client's efforts. The main limitation is that the affirmations feel somewhat formulaic and do not deeply validate the emotional pain of single parenting and fear of judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the client's context, ambivalence, and practical coping needs. Deeper emotional reflections about guilt, shame, and fear of being seen as a failing mother are underdeveloped.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing alcohol from two drinks to one. There is minimal drift, and the conversation consistently returns to the target behavior and concrete steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is largely co-constructed around the client's own choices. The interaction is somewhat leading, with many clinician-offered options before the client's ideas are fully explored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist respects the client's boundaries and frames the work as optional and client-directed. The high volume of practical suggestions creates some subtle pressure toward a plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions and concrete planning prompts effectively elicit client-generated reasons and steps. More evocation of values, identity, and past strengths would strengthen the client's self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and invites the client's input throughout. The number of specific suggestions makes the interaction feel somewhat expert-guided rather than fully equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively uses rulers and planning questions to elicit readiness, ability, and commitment. Deeper exploration of the client's values and meaning-based reasons for change would strengthen this skill.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and normalizes the client's reasons for drinking. However, responses often shift to practical problem-solving instead of using MI strategies to gently pivot sustain talk toward change.
