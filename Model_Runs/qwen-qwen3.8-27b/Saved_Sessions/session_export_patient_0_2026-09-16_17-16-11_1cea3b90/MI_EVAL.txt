# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warmth, respect, and nonjudgment, validating Adrian's discomfort and defensiveness. There is no moralizing, shaming, or coercive language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective statements accurately capture Adrian's ambivalence between weekend energy and health consequences. Empathy could be deeper by explicitly attending to underlying shame, fear of labeling, or automaticity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session quickly focuses on preventing nose/infection harm and remains anchored to the target behavior. Progress moves from reduction to a concrete, client-selected plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs the plan with Adrian rather than prescribing it, letting the client's preferences drive goals and steps. The final plan is clearly client-generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly frames Adrian as the decision-maker and avoids directive advice. Language supports choice, reduction, and self-determined goals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions successfully draw out Adrian's own reasons and practical solutions. Deeper values, identity, or confidence-focused evocation would strengthen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a non-expert, collaborative stance and asks Adrian to shape the conversation. More explicit acknowledgment of the client as the expert in his own life would improve this.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invites and reinforces client-generated change talk about reduction, the gym, and practical steps. Amplifying or values-based expansion of change talk is less consistent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Adrian's sustain talk and reframes it toward harm reduction and agency. More double-sided reflections and autonomy-focused reframing would make this stronger.
