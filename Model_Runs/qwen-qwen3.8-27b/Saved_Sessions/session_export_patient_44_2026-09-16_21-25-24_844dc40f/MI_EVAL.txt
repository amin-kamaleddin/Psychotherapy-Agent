# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows respect, warmth, and nonjudgment without labeling or blaming Erik. Validation is offered even when Erik expresses self-criticism or doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Erik’s ambivalence about the gym, comparison, and health goals. Deeper emotional reflections about fear of failure and self-criticism would strengthen the score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behaviors: exercise consistency, diet, and coping with discouragement. The conversation moves purposefully from ambivalence to a specific weekly plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as an equal partner, building the plan from Erik’s own ideas and responses. The final actions are co-created rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik is repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker, with strong emphasis on ownership. The score is limited by occasional mildly leading suggestions and less explicit affirmation of his right to choose not to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions help Erik articulate his own goals, values, evidence, and confidence. Motivation emerges from Erik’s own change talk rather than external advice or persuasion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly respects Erik as the expert in his own life. Erik’s contributions shape the direction, plan, and language of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively elicits and amplifies Erik’s desire, ability, need, and commitment language. Affirmations strengthen Erik’s sense of agency and competence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Concerns about failure and comparison are not argued with and are redirected toward Erik’s own standards. More double-sided reflections would better soften the pull of sustain talk.
