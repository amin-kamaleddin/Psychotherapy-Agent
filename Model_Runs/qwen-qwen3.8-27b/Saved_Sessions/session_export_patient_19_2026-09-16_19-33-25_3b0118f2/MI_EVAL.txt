# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepts Anya’s ambivalence without shame or argument. It is strong but somewhat formulaic, with room for more individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor reflects the main tensions in Anya’s story, especially between fun/relief and protecting her future. Emotional material such as embarrassment, fear of being “weird,” and possible loneliness is not deeply reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing gambling exposure and protecting financial stability. The conversation moves clearly from ambivalence to concrete, behavior-specific planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Anya’s ideas and builds a practical plan from her suggestions. However, the session remains noticeably therapist-led, with limited explicit agenda-setting or joint decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently frames choices as Anya’s own and avoids giving direct advice or pressure. It would be stronger with more explicit acknowledgment that the decision to change remains entirely hers despite external referral or workplace concerns.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicits useful change talk through open-ended questions and reflects Anya’s emerging plan. Deeper values, dreams, and identity-based motivation, such as the Egypt trip or self-respect, are not fully evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Anya as the expert on her life and uses her generated ideas to shape the session. It falls short of maximal partnership because the psychiatrist still controls the sequence and pacing fairly directly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Anya generates several concrete change statements, including cutting up cards, avoiding venues, limiting money, and trying a one-hour boundary. The cultivation is effective but remains mostly practical rather than deeply values-based.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges reasons to continue gambling without arguing and gently redirects toward safer alternatives. It is not maximal because sustain talk is not consistently linked to deeper values or defused with richer double-sided reflections.
