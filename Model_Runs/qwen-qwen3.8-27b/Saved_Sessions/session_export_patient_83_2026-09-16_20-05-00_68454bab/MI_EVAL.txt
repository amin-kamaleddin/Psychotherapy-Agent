# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Mai’s concerns about the new medication. Acceptance is slightly procedural and could show warmer, more individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician uses accurate reflections and a double-sided reflection that captures Mai’s ambivalence. Empathy could go deeper into her emotional frustration, distrust, and need to feel like a full partner in the decision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on understanding the medication and making it possible to fit into Mai’s routine. The flow moves purposefully from confusion to a concrete, self-generated action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mai’s ideas strongly shape the session, and the psychiatrist avoids imposing a prescriptive solution. Collaboration is slightly reduced by counselor-controlled sequencing and a couple of consecutive counselor openings.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Mai as the decision-maker and avoids pressure or direct advice. Her skepticism is treated as legitimate rather than resistance, supporting ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mainly elicits Mai’s own reasons, preferences, and solutions rather than lecturing. Evocation is somewhat narrow and could explore deeper values, motivations, and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and repeatedly treats Mai as the expert on her own life. Partnership is slightly weakened by two consecutive counselor turns and somewhat scripted question phrasing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mai generates useful change talk around a concrete plan, and the psychiatrist reinforces her emerging intentions. The change talk is mostly logistical and could be deepened by scaling or values-based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Mai’s hesitations without arguing, dismissing, or reinforcing them. The score is limited by less consistent use of double-sided reflections or strategic reframes to turn sustain talk into change talk.
