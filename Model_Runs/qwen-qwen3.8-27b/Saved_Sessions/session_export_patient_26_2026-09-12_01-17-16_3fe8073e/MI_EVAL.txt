# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and provides validating statements about the client’s burden. However, acceptance feels brief and service-oriented rather than deeply warm or fully affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects some surface-level emotions and asks open questions early in the session. However, the reflections do not fully track the client’s loneliness, shame, exhaustion, and emotional complexity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session remains connected to the client’s main concerns and moves toward concrete practical steps. However, the direction is more logistical problem-solving than MI-focused guidance toward change processes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites the client to edit drafts and choose between options, but the session is largely clinician-led. The counselor provides extensive lists and scripts rather than building the plan around the client’s own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and optional help, supporting the client’s sense of control in several moments. However, directive recommendations and problem-solving reduce emphasis on the client’s ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session begins with some open questions, but the psychiatrist mostly provides advice, options, and scripts. There are missed opportunities to draw out the client’s own motivations, values, and readiness for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some collaborative invitations, but the psychiatrist largely assumes the role of expert and problem-solver. The client is not consistently treated as the primary expert in her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers several change-oriented statements, but the psychiatrist does not consistently highlight, amplify, or explore them. The session moves toward practical suggestions before fully evoking the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue with or reinforce the client’s barriers. However, the responses are not strongly MI-consistent and do not skillfully soften ambivalence or pivot toward change.
