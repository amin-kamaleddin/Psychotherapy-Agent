# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with genuine affirmations throughout (e.g., 'That's a real strength,' 'You're not fragile'). However, acceptance remains somewhat surface-level, occasionally feeling performative without deeper acknowledgment of the cultural context of Jordan's position.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately tracks Jordan's cognitive framing and emotional state. Misses deeper emotional layers, particularly the autonomy threat from his mother's push and the pride embedded in his self-sufficiency narrative.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains focus on the target behavior but oscillates between exploring personal risk, the meaning of 'need,' and affirming self-sufficiency without a coherent strategic arc. The session starts and ends in the same place, lacking a purposeful pivot toward a different motivational domain.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally conversational rather than directive, with open-ended questions inviting Jordan's perspective. However, the session remains clearly counselor-led in structure, with Jordan giving minimal responses and never substantively shaping the direction of the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently non-coercive, never implies irresponsibility, and frames exploration around Jordan's own values. Could be stronger with explicit language affirming his right to decline or to take time deciding (e.g., 'Whatever you decide, it's your call').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The most significant weakness: all evocation targets personal risk and cost, the exact domain where Jordan has the strongest sustain talk. The family/grandparent dimension—identified as his key area of openness—is never accessed, and his one change talk statement ('I don't take chances') is never developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a collaborative stance with questions that explicitly center Jordan's authority ('not someone else's lens'). However, the dynamic is asymmetric in practice, with the psychiatrist doing most of the talking and framing rather than yielding direction or letting Jordan's input reshape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only minimal, indirect attempts to evoke change talk, all framed around personal cost rather than values or social responsibility. The single change talk statement Jordan produces is neither reflected back nor built upon, representing a major missed opportunity given his motivational profile.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoids arguing against sustain talk and reflects its underlying logic without judgment. However, no sophisticated softening techniques (double-sided reflections, gentle reframing) are used to broaden the frame beyond the 'I'm healthy, I don't need this' loop.
