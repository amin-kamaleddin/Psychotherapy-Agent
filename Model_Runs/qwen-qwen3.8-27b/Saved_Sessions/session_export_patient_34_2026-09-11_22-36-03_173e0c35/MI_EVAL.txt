# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids blaming Dylan for the incident. Acceptance is somewhat formulaic, with repeated stock reassurances that limit deeper individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate surface-level reflections of annoyance, frustration, and unfairness. However, the empathy remains one-dimensional and does not explore underlying ambivalence, fear, shame, or loss of control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifts into procedural problem-solving rather than maintaining a clear MI target behavior or motivational focus. The repeated offer of similar low-effort scripts makes the conversation feel circular rather than purposeful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is some collaborative language, but the interaction remains largely advisor-driven. Dylan’s preferences do not meaningfully shape the session, as the psychiatrist repeatedly returns to email, voicemail, and protective-script options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently offers choices and accepts Dylan’s decision to go to class without forcing action. Autonomy support is weakened by repeated suggestions of protective scripts and a directive caution that ignoring the issue may lead to escalation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows limited evocation by providing solutions instead of eliciting Dylan’s own motivations, values, or reasons for change. There is little exploration of what matters to him or how his desired approach aligns with his goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some partnership cues appear, but the psychiatrist often assumes the expert role by offering specific procedural steps and scripts. This uneven power dynamic prevents Dylan from being positioned as the expert on his own life and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is very little active cultivation of change talk, and the questions and options remain procedural rather than change-focused. Dylan’s admission that he knows what he did is not reflected, amplified, or expanded into motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session remains heavily focused on sustain talk, with Dylan minimizing responsibility and wanting to ignore the issue. The psychiatrist avoids arguing but does not effectively soften or redirect this minimization through double-sided reflections or discrepancy exploration.
