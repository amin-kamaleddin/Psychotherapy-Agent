# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive, and the patient explicitly feels accepted. Acceptance is limited because positive regard is often tied to the plan and progress rather than unconditional worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows perspective-taking, especially early on, and reflects confidence. Empathy remains procedural rather than deeply emotional, with limited exploration of frustration, embarrassment, or relapse-related feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is tightly focused on the target behavior and moves clearly from intention to a concrete action plan. Momentum is strong, with each turn tied to reducing orange juice consumption.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-creates the plan and adapts to Marco's preferences. Collaboration is occasionally less balanced because the psychiatrist frequently supplies practical options and coaching-style suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marco is invited to make choices and his boundaries are respected. Autonomy support is not consistently at the highest level because the psychiatrist still offers multiple prescriptive tips and sets a directive follow-up expectation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses scaling and open questions to elicit planning details. Deeper evocation of values, meaning, self-identity, and intrinsic motivation is not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are meaningful collaborative moments and Marco clearly influences the plan. However, the psychiatrist frequently shifts toward an expert advice-giver by supplying options and directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively encourages change talk about next steps, confidence, barriers, and follow-up. The evocation is strong but remains more action-focused than values-based or identity-based change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce sustain talk and responds nonjudgmentally to cravings and possible slips. The score is limited by a lack of more nuanced double-sided reflections or reframes of sustain talk.
