# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout; the psychiatrist never labels, disapproves, or minimizes Marco's struggles. Even when Marco expresses self-critical language, the response is gentle exploration without correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally accurate and nuanced reflective listening that evolves from surface-level summaries to deep functional understanding (e.g., the scorecard as a control mechanism). Reflections capture social dynamics, emotional mechanisms, and underlying functions with increasing sophistication.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear and logical progression from surface barriers to deep functional understanding of the self-critical pattern. Could be strengthened by more clearly connecting insights to potential next steps or anchoring progress with a brief summary before closing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Creates a genuinely collaborative interaction where two people think together rather than one interrogating the other. Builds on Marco's own language and metaphors, co-constructing understanding with equal power balance throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Marco's ownership and freedom to choose, never giving advice or implying a 'right' answer. When Marco asks 'do I just stop?' the psychiatrist refuses to fill in the answer, respecting his autonomy to figure it out.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within Marco through well-chosen questions rather than providing external reasons for change. Could be more strategically deployed to build momentum, such as amplifying the 'I've never let myself stop the self-talk' insight as a pivot point.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Marco as the expert on his own life, using phrases like 'what's your own sense' and 'what's your sense of how.' The final exchange—refusing to answer Marco's question and instead inviting further exploration—is the essence of true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Marco's own change language through strategic questions, particularly the 'what would you say to a friend' prompt. Could have more explicitly amplified and consolidated change talk moments with a brief summary highlighting the shift Marco made.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never argues against or dismisses Marco's concerns; instead validates the protective function of the scorecard while gently exploring its costs. Sustain talk is consistently treated as valid, understandable, and protective—never as an obstacle to be overcome through argument.
