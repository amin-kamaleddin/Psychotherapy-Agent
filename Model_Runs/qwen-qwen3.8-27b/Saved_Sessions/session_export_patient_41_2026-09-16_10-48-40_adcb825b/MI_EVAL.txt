# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, supportive stance with affirming language and no evident pressure. It is somewhat task-focused rather than consistently expressing deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections track Elira’s ambivalence, self-doubt, and anxiety effectively, capturing both cognitive and emotional experience. The main limitation is that empathy often summarizes symptoms rather than exploring deeper meanings such as shame or fear of incompetence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session has a clear, consistent focus on reducing avoidance and trying a small step after Tuesday practice. It is somewhat narrow and logistics-heavy, with less strategic movement toward values or the broader avoidance pattern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elira’s preferences shape parts of the plan, such as trying alone and choosing a physical anchor. However, many core strategies are introduced by the psychiatrist, making it more guided problem-solving than equal collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently offers choices and honors Elira’s desire to proceed alone and write her own phrase. The volume of practical suggestions is somewhat directive and may reduce the sense of a fully self-generated plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and prompts draw out useful client-generated ideas, including a 10-minute step, exit option, and self-talk. Evocation is limited because the psychiatrist often supplies concrete answers and options before allowing deeper client generation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids a fully authoritarian stance, but the session is heavily shaped by the counselor’s structured steps. This creates some shared decision-making while still feeling more like guided problem-solving than equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elira produces meaningful change talk, such as the 10-minute step feeling doable and the realization that not trying repeats avoidance. These statements are not consistently amplified, deepened, or consolidated before moving into planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument or invalidation, including embarrassment, wanting to do it alone, and fear of a bad first note. The repeated focus on barriers and anticipated failure limits consistent pivoting toward values, confidence, and change.
