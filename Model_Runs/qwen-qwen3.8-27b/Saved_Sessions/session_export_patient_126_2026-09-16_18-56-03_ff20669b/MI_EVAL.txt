# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys nonjudgmental, respectful support through validating statements such as "I hear you" and "that makes sense." Acceptance is strong but somewhat formulaic and could include more individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate two-sided reflections to capture the patient's ambivalence about tiredness, phone use, and wanting brief help. Reflections are often structurally similar and do not always explore deeper emotions behind the patient's dismissiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on energy/tiredness and follows the patient's preferred path. However, the original referral concerns of smoking and alcohol are largely dropped, causing the therapeutic target to drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats the patient as an active participant and avoids expert-driven advice or lecturing. The use of closed, multiple-choice questions sometimes limits fully open collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports choice with language such as "if any" and "if you're open to it," while avoiding pressure or ultimatums. Autonomy support could be stronger when the patient asks to wrap up or resists changing routine.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks open-ended questions that elicit patient-generated ideas about practical steps and evening routines. Evocation is uneven because menu-based choices interrupt spontaneous change talk and deeper motivations are not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites patient input and allows choices about bloods, sleep, and screen time to guide the session. Menu-style questions and repeated structuring can make the collaboration feel less fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some patient-generated ideas, such as blood tests, Do Not Disturb, and putting the phone face down. Change talk is present but not consistently affirmed, amplified, or developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and respectfully reflects the patient's minimization of alcohol, smoking, and routine change. Sustain talk remains prominent and is not consistently redirected toward exploring change.
