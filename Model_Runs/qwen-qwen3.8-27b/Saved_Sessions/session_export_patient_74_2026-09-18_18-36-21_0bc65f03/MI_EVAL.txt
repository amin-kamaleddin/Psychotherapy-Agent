# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrates unconditional positive regard with zero judgment or pressure, explicitly validating Linda's ambivalence and correcting herself when she over-interprets. Key moments include affirming 'nobody's asking that of you' and 'You don't have to solve a puzzle you haven't agreed to solve.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate dual-sided reflections that validate ambivalence without taking sides (e.g., 'Both can be true at the same time'). The psychiatrist consistently reflects at the emotional level, naming the underlying fear that being a mother means giving up a piece of herself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on smoking and the ambivalence surrounding it without wandering into unrelated topics. However, one evocative question briefly shifted toward problem-solving that Linda correctly identified as a 'puzzle' she didn't want to solve, and the session ended without a collaborative summary to consolidate the emerging perspective shift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Linda's responses genuinely shape the session's direction, and the psychiatrist is willing to be corrected by the client ('You're right. I did make it sound bigger than it is'). The conversation is consistently framed as a joint exploration rather than an expert delivering information.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a defining feature of the session, with explicit statements like 'That's your call, full stop' and 'This hour is yours.' The psychiatrist never gives advice, uses directive language, or frames any suggestion as a recommendation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocative questions help Linda access her own motivations and name underlying needs (e.g., 'What is that break actually giving you that you need?'). The score is limited by the absence of varied techniques like scaling questions and by the failure to explicitly amplify the change talk that emerged.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions herself as a curious partner rather than an expert, explicitly attributing insights to Linda ('That's your own insight, not something I gave you'). Her willingness to be corrected by the client is a powerful demonstration of genuine partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocative questions help Linda articulate a genuine perspective shift ('I think I'm going to think about it differently than I was this morning'). However, the psychiatrist does not explicitly reflect or amplify the change talk that emerges, nor does she use scaling or confidence questions to further develop it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never argues against, persuades, or challenges Linda's sustain talk, instead validating and removing pressure ('That's your call, full stop'). The score is slightly reduced because one evocative question could be perceived as gently pushing toward change despite Linda's stated lack of readiness.
