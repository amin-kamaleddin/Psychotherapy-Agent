# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepts the client's guardedness, uncertainty, and preference for a low-effort approach. Some acceptance remains task-oriented rather than deeply warm or validating of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers basic reflective understanding of the client's frustration and desire to keep things simple. Reflections stay practical and do not deeply explore underlying emotions, avoidance, or complex ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on completing court-ordered community service and obtaining the approved-site list. The psychiatrist consistently moves toward a clear, concrete next step without wandering off topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates by offering options, inviting the client's choice, and adapting to the client's preference for a shorter text. Collaboration is limited by the counselor's role as the main planner and script-writer.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options and affirming the client's decisions, such as choosing the shorter text. Autonomy support is slightly weakened by repeated directive instructions about sending, saving, and following up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is one useful open question at the start, but the session quickly becomes procedural. The psychiatrist does not consistently evoke the client's own motivations, values, or confidence for completing the service.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and respects the client's chosen option, but the interaction remains largely expert-driven and managerial. The client is not consistently positioned as the primary expert in the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some affirmations and highlights a clear next step, but does not strongly evoke intrinsic change language. Most of the discussion remains compliance- and logistics-focused rather than motivation-focused.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or escalating the client's resistance and accepts concerns about effort and follow-up. Sustain talk is not actively softened through deeper reflections or use of ambivalence to expand motivation.
