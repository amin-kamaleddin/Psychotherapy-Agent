# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses respectful, nonjudgmental, and autonomy-respecting language and closes warmly. Slightly evaluative comments, such as labeling the client’s behavior as a real step, keep this from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reflects the client’s practical concerns and work pressures. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore defensiveness, fear of being lectured, or deeper ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a coherent focus on monitoring gum and throat irritation. Direction is inconsistent with the target tobacco-use goal because the clinician does not return to reducing or stopping mini-cigar use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist co-constructs a practical, routine-based monitoring plan with shared language. Collaboration is stronger than typical advice, but the clinician still guides the meaning of the plan rather than fully letting Ahmed define it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Ahmed’s choice and avoids coercion, persuasion, or ultimatums. Occasional interpretive statements about the meaning of his monitoring slightly reduce full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions elicit some client-generated practical ideas for monitoring. Evocation is limited because it does not reach Ahmed’s own values, concerns, or motivations regarding tobacco use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats Ahmed as the expert and avoids lecturing or imposing a cessation plan. Partnership is reduced by occasional expert-driven interpretations of the client’s behavior.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client mostly offers sustain talk, and the psychiatrist does not effectively evoke change talk about reducing or stopping mini-cigars. This is a major gap for a tobacco-change goal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and sometimes reframes the client’s behavior positively. Softening is limited because the session remains anchored in the client’s status-quo plan to merely watch the irritation.
