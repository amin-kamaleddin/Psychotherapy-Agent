# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating. However, acceptance remains more procedural than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is an initial attempt to understand the patient’s feelings. The psychiatrist does not sufficiently reflect or explore frustration about the study workload.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on exam preparation and the target behavior. The psychiatrist consistently guides the conversation toward practical study planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drives the agenda and provides much of the cognitive work. The patient’s role becomes passive rather than collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial choices are offered, but the session becomes highly directive. Specific methods and time frames are prescribed despite patient reluctance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist gives advice and reasons for change rather than drawing out the patient’s own motivations. Intrinsic change talk is not effectively elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently assumes an expert role and dictates study strategies. This limits equal collaboration with the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to evoke change talk are weak and sparse. The psychiatrist moves quickly into advice rather than exploring the patient’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient’s preference for familiar, low-effort study methods is minimized rather than explored. The psychiatrist often responds by pushing a more directive plan.
