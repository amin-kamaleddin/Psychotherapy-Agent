# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses a nonjudgmental, normalizing tone and avoids blame. Acceptance is sometimes corrective and could more fully validate the client's fear before offering reassurance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some empathic statements, but the session shifts quickly toward factual problem-solving. The client's deeper emotions and vulnerability are often not reflected in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The discussion generally stays connected to the client's anxiety in work situations. The session becomes less focused due to many practical techniques and suggestions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client contributes some choices, but the psychiatrist largely provides menus of options and plans. The session feels more expert-led than truly collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices are often offered, and the client's preference for silent rehearsal is accepted. Autonomy support is weakened by directive suggestions and planning after the client expresses hesitation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not consistently evoke the client's own reasons, values, or confidence for change. External advice and skill-building largely replace evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist often takes an expert role by providing specific techniques and a plan. The client's own insights and preferences are not consistently positioned as the primary source of direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some willingness to try small strategies, but these are not consistently reflected or amplified. The psychiatrist tends to move toward practical solutions rather than building change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client expresses notable ambivalence and resistance, but responses mainly normalize the worry and add more options. There is limited use of MI-consistent reflections or redirects to soften sustain talk.
