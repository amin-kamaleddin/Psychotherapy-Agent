# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates warmth, respect, and nonjudgment while validating Oliver’s ambivalence. Occasional positive evaluations of the client’s wording make the acceptance slightly directive rather than fully accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects the central conflict, practical concerns, and emotional nuance accurately. Some repetition and a mechanical quality in parts of the session limit the depth of empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on the target behavior: assertively asking the flatmate to move out by a clear deadline while preserving kindness. Questions consistently guide Oliver toward planning and acting without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Oliver to contribute ideas and builds on his suggestions without dominating. More explicit co-creation and reduced interpretive labeling would make the collaboration fully fluid.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids commands and encourages Oliver to generate his own wording and pacing. Missing explicit ownership language, such as stating it is Oliver’s choice, prevents a perfect score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully draws out Oliver’s own reasons, values, and confidence. The evocation could be stronger by more explicitly exploring commitment, confidence, and future-oriented change language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treats Oliver as the expert and invites him to refine his own language. A structured agenda and repeated question slightly weaken the sense of a fully egalitarian exchange.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently elicits and reinforces Oliver’s change talk, strengthening his confidence and plan. More explicit work on confidence, commitment, and importance would deepen the change talk further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Oliver’s guilt and fear of being the bad guy without arguing or dismissing them. The reframing is strong, though a few phrases could subtly pressure the client if interpreted as labeling hesitation.
