# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect and nonjudgment with statements such as “not to judge” and “no lecture.” Acceptance is strong but can be deepened by affirming Milan’s worth independent of risk concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers accurate reflections of Milan’s stress relief, frustration, confidence, and desire to feel normal. Empathy remains somewhat surface-level and could explore deeper values, fear of being labeled, or underlying conflicts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on drinking, risk, social evenings, and follow-up with clear structure. The focus becomes somewhat repetitive and overly tied to behavioral safety options rather than Milan’s own emerging direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input, adapts to objections, and asks Milan to choose among options. Collaboration is limited because most plans are counselor-generated rather than fully Milan-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice with permission-seeking, offers options, and respects Milan’s decision to continue current behavior. Subtle pressure persists through repeated attempts to secure at least one safety behavior or check-in.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist begins with evocative questions about drinking, values, and future identity, but quickly shifts to practical strategies. Change talk is minimal and not consistently drawn out or amplified from Milan’s own language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative tone and repeatedly seeks Milan’s preferences. The relationship remains partially client-centered because the psychiatrist still drives much of the agenda and plan development.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some attempts to elicit Milan’s own reasons for change, but the session does not sustain or develop change talk effectively. The psychiatrist does not skillfully reinforce DARN-CRAFR language or follow up on Milan’s small openings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Milan’s sustain talk and acknowledges his perspective. However, the psychiatrist does not consistently soften sustain talk using double-sided reflections or by exploring its meaning in relation to Milan’s values.
