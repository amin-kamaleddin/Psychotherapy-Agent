# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used respectful, affirming language and validated the client's goals and choices. The tone was supportive but somewhat focused on the practical planning process rather than deeper unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected the client's logistical and emotional barriers accurately and clearly. Empathy did not fully explore the deeper ambivalence or emotional weight of her stress and parenting pressures.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on reducing evening smoking, without drifting to other topics. The psychiatrist respected the client's boundary about alcohol while maintaining a clear, logical focus on the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction was framed as collaborative, but the psychiatrist was the main source of strategies and action steps. The client largely selected from the counselor's options rather than co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used several autonomy-supportive questions and honored the client's preferences, such as skipping phone reminders. However, the repeated provision of specific solutions slightly reduced the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used scaling questions and some evocative language, but relied heavily on providing strategies rather than drawing out the client's own motivations and ideas. The action plan was largely counselor-driven rather than client-evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist initially asked the client what she wanted to focus on, but the session shifted toward a more expert-led coaching stance. The client had a more passive role in selecting options rather than guiding the direction of change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offered several meaningful reasons for change, especially around her son and avoiding illness. The psychiatrist acknowledged these points but did not consistently amplify or build on them throughout the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expressed clear sustain talk about smoking as stress relief, and the psychiatrist acknowledged this. However, the response moved quickly into practical problem-solving rather than using MI techniques to explore the ambivalence.
