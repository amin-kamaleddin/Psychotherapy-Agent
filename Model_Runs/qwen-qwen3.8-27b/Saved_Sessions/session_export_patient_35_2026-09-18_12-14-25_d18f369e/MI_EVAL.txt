# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates nonjudgmental acceptance, powerfully normalizing Elena's smoking as a human response to overwhelm rather than moral failure. The acceptance is deeply personalized to her specific situation as a stressed single parent, never formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Empathic understanding is accurate and nuanced, reflecting both behaviors and underlying values (e.g., 'you're clearly someone who cares deeply about protecting him'). The psychiatrist captures the emotional weight and meaning behind Elena's coping without over- or under-reading her.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with logical progression from acknowledgment through insight to a specific, actionable target (the morning cigarette). Every question and reflection ties back to the target behavior without drifting into broader life issues.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elena's ideas consistently shape the session direction, with the psychiatrist building on her spontaneous insights as the foundation for each next step. The power dynamic is genuinely equal, with concrete targets emerging from Elena's own words rather than being imposed externally.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never uses directive language ('you should,' 'you need to') and explicitly validates Elena's choice to reduce rather than quit. Language consistently emphasizes Elena's capacity for self-direction and right to choose her own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Evocation is layered and progressive, drawing out Elena's own motivations, values, and solutions at each stage without providing external reasons for change. The psychiatrist asks questions that help Elena generate her own confidence and direction rather than supplying answers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never assumes the expert role, consistently ceding the agenda to Elena and using inclusive language ('if we took... what would that look like for you?'). Solutions are explicitly attributed to Elena, reinforcing her as the agent of change rather than a passive recipient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and amplified through strategic questions that invite Elena to articulate her own reasons and confidence for change. Each piece of change talk is reflected back with reinforcing affirmations that strengthen and build on it progressively.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and validated before gentle redirection, but the double-sided reflection could be more explicit at a couple of points. The transcript ends before the psychiatrist can respond to Elena's final sustain talk, so full softening skill cannot be assessed.
