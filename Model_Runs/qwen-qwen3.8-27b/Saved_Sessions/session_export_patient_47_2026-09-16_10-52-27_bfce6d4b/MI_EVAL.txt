# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, validating, and generally nonjudgmental, while honoring the client's low-drama preference. The score is limited by a shift toward cautionary safety guidance near the end.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture ambivalence, values, and the tension between normalcy and safety. Deeper emotional vulnerabilities, such as feeling viewed by her son as “sick,” are not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on a concrete one-week goal for reducing weekday alcohol use. Rulers, barrier discussion, and follow-up planning keep momentum toward a specific behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client’s preferences clearly shape the plan, and the therapist uses reflective and question-based engagement. However, repeated suggestions, scripts, and safety directives make the partnership feel uneven at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist frequently emphasizes choice, independence, and the client’s own decision-making. Some directive offers—such as scripts and multiple safety checks—reduce the consistency of the autonomy-supporting stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some goals, confidence ratings, and barriers, but does not consistently deepen the client’s own motivations and values. The balance of evocative questioning is often interrupted by advice and planning suggestions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client’s choices largely drive the session, and the therapist affirms her self-direction and experience. The therapist still contributes a meaningful amount of direction through suggested messages, plans, and supports.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is useful elicitation of change talk, including specific goals, importance ratings, and confidence statements. The therapist often moves into suggestions or planning before fully amplifying the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without arguing or reinforcing resistance, which is a strength. The response lacks more skillful MI-specific strategies such as double-sided reflections, rolling with resistance, or strategic reframing.
