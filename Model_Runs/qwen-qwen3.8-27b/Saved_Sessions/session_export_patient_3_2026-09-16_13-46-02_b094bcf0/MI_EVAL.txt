# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys consistent, nonjudgmental acceptance and respects Aisha's experience without moralizing her weight, body, or past attempts. Statements about being accepted even when defensive or unsure strongly affirm her worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Aisha's underlying fear, shame, and need for safety rather than surface content only. The empathy remains precise and emotionally attuned, though the repeated use of "I hear..." is slightly formulaic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays relevant to movement and body-related distress, but it drifts from consolidating a specific exercise plan toward the therapeutic relationship and future check-in style. A stronger direction-focused response would summarize the tiny step and clarify timing, feasibility, or commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates with Aisha by inviting her to choose what would feel like care, what a small step could look like, and how the psychiatrist should respond in future pauses. The interaction is co-constructed rather than prescriptive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported by framing choices as Aisha's own and validating her need to avoid another rule or source of self-punishment. The psychiatrist avoids directives, pressure, or taking over decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes Aisha's own values, motivations, and preferred language, such as energy, capability, gentleness, and "progress isn't perfect." Evocation could be even stronger by further expanding or amplifying these change statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Aisha as the expert on her own life, inner critic, and needs. Questions about preferred support and realistic steps demonstrate a strong collaborative stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out meaningful change talk about energy, capability, and a gentler relationship with movement. However, it could be stronger by more deliberately summarizing, amplifying, or exploring Aisha's own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is limited, and the psychiatrist handles concerns skillfully by normalizing and reframing them as autonomy and self-care issues rather than resistance. The response consistently softens worry without reinforcing reasons against change.
