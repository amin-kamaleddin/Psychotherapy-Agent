# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is largely nonjudgmental and normalizes Marco's repeated dieting struggles, reducing shame. The warmth is supportive but somewhat generic and could more explicitly affirm his values and effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows surface understanding of Marco's fatigue, convenience needs, and family dynamics. Reflections are often brief and repetitive, and do not fully capture deeper fears about household tension or being seen as controlling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on the after-work snack trigger and narrows toward a concrete, manageable car-based plan. The focus is occasionally diluted by multiple practical strategies, but the target behavior stays consistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Marco's input and adapts when he rejects reminders, tracking, or extra snacks. However, the interaction remains counselor-driven, with the psychiatrist providing most of the structure and options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client choice is supported through options and acceptance of Marco's preferences. The psychiatrist still remains fairly directive and advisory, which can subtly reduce client self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits logistical information and workable options but does not deeply evoke Marco's own values, reasons, hopes, or confidence for change. Motivational content is not consistently amplified or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids a fully authoritarian stance by adjusting to Marco's concerns. However, the psychiatrist remains the primary problem-solver and plan generator, limiting full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some affirmations and simple choice questions, but the psychiatrist rarely elicits or deepens Marco's own reasons, desires, or confidence for change. Change talk appears but is not systematically cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Marco's doubts and normalizes his concerns about fatigue, family reaction, and convenience. However, responses often add more problem-solving rather than using reflections to redirect toward change.
