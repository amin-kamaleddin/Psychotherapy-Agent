# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is generally respectful, nonjudgmental, and normalizing, using statements that help counter Jordan's self-criticism. The warmth is sometimes slightly formulaic and moves quickly into practical problem-solving, limiting deeper acknowledgment of his distress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflects exhaustion, frustration, and the reasonableness of Jordan's feelings accurately. However, the reflections often stay surface-level and miss deeper shame, fear of being seen as unreliable, and loss of agency.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains clearly focused on the immediate work, transportation, and financial stressor driving Jordan's distress. The direction is practical and relevant, though it is more crisis/problem-solving oriented than explicitly connected to larger motivational goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses inclusive language, offers options, and asks for Jordan's preferences, creating some sense of teamwork. However, the interaction is still partly expert-driven because the psychiatrist frequently supplies structured scripts and step-by-step solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by offering options and asking which step feels doable. At the same time, directive prompts such as telling Jordan to send the text now reduce his sense of ownership and self-determination.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist mostly provides practical options, scripts, and coping steps rather than evoking Jordan's own motivations, values, or reasons for change. There is little exploration of what getting to work would mean to Jordan, what he hopes to gain, or what past strengths he could draw on.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and avoids a purely authoritarian stance, but the partnership is uneven because the psychiatrist frequently sets the agenda through structured options and practical instructions. A stronger partnership would follow Jordan's lead and co-create the next steps more consistently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jordan expresses change talk, such as wanting to get to work and not feel stuck, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reflect, amplify, or expand these statements. The psychiatrist also rarely asks questions that target desire, ability, reasons, need, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoids arguing with or reinforcing Jordan's pessimistic sustain talk and instead validates the difficulty in a nonblaming way. However, he does not consistently use MI-specific strategies such as double-sided reflections to explore ambivalence or redirect sustain talk toward change.
