# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, frequently affirming the client's strengths and normalizing his ambivalence. However, the warmth is somewhat structured and misses opportunities to explicitly express unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening and accurate summarization of the client's ambivalence. However, reflections remain mostly cognitive and behavioral, missing opportunities to deeply explore the emotional meaning behind his fears and identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear therapeutic focus on refusal skills and moved coherently from exploration to planning. However, the focus occasionally narrowed too much on procedural scripts rather than the client's underlying values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is evidence of collaboration as the client actively selected his final coping strategies. Nevertheless, the counselor generated much of the content and scripts, making the interaction resemble a consultation rather than an equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and respectfully accepted the client's boundaries regarding practice. However, explicit language reinforcing the client's ownership was lacking, and repeated suggestions subtly pressured his plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some effort to evoke motivation using open questions and confidence scaling. However, the session remained largely superficial and advice-based, missing opportunities to draw out deeper personal values and sense of possibility.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited the client's input and allowed him to shape the final plan. However, the presentation of multiple prescriptive scripts and tactics made the interaction feel somewhat expert-driven rather than a true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicited some change talk using confidence ratings and affirmations of the client's progress. However, frequent shifts into providing scripts and advice limited deeper exploration of the client's intrinsic reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged the client's social concerns but did not consistently soften or pivot his sustain talk about fitting in. Frequent provision of scripts kept the focus on social risk rather than resolving ambivalence with techniques like double-sided reflections.
