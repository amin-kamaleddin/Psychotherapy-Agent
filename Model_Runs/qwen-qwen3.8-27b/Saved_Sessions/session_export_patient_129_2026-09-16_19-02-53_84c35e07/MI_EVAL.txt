# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows warmth, respect, and nonjudgment despite Tyler's guardedness and resistance. He avoids moralizing, blame, or side-taking, strongly communicating unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** He offers accurate simple and complex reflections of Tyler's mixed feelings, frustration, and need for control and respect. Empathy is strong but remains somewhat repetitive and does not fully explore deeper emotions or substance-use-specific concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session frequently drifts toward parental conflict, privacy, and autonomy rather than maintaining a clear focus on substance use and related risk. It lacks a collaboratively clarified target behavior or specific guidance toward the clinical concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses nonauthoritarian, relationship-based language and invites Tyler's perspective, allowing the client to shape much of the conversation. However, the agenda and health framing are not fully negotiated or co-created with Tyler.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, with repeated emphasis on Tyler's right to choose, no pressure or persuasion, and respect for self-direction. This stance likely reduces defensiveness and centers Tyler's freedom to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits useful client language around control, trust, and responsibility but does not consistently develop it into change motivation. It remains mostly about autonomy and the therapeutic relationship rather than evoking reasons, ability, or confidence related to substance use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally treats Tyler as the expert on his own life and avoids expert-driven advice or directive solutions. Some of the clinician's health agenda still influences the session, and explicit co-creation of purpose is limited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few evocative moments, but the psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, expand, or strategically follow up on Tyler's change-relevant statements. It lacks scaling, discrepancy, or DARN/CARE-style questions to build internal motivation for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or reinforcing Tyler's sustain talk about not being an addict or making a huge deal of it. He sometimes redirects toward health, freedom, and consequences, but does not consistently pivot toward change or use double-sided reflections.
