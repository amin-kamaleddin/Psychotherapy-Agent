# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Hana’s ambivalence and choices. Acceptance is competent but somewhat procedural, with less explicit warmth or deeper affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several good reflections capture Hana’s ambivalence and social concerns, especially early in the session. Empathy is weakened by a repeated identical prompt and a missed opportunity to deepen reflection on her self-consciousness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking, progressing from health worry to specific triggers and practical low-key strategies. Direction is slightly interrupted by the repeated prompt and a lack of a clear closing summary or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist works as a collaborative partner, exploring and building on Hana’s ideas rather than imposing a plan. Collaboration is strong but less visible where the same prompt is repeated without fully tracking Hana’s latest contribution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana’s autonomy is respected, with no pressure to quit and repeated emphasis on keeping choices small, simple, and under her control. Autonomy support could be made more explicit, especially when she expresses doubt or a need to avoid pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes client-generated practical strategies such as a phone note, pause, mints, drink, and phone check. Evocation remains surface-level, with limited exploration of personal meaning, values, confidence, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Hana as the expert and uses open-ended, client-driven questions that allow her ideas to shape the conversation. The repeated identical question slightly reduces the impression of real-time partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana offers several change-related ideas and the psychiatrist reflects some of them supportively. Change talk is present but not consistently amplified, summarized, or developed into greater confidence or personal importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Hana’s sustain talk and acknowledges the social pull to smoke with friends. However, the session remains focused on social difficulty and could use more consistent double-sided reflections or gentle reframing.
