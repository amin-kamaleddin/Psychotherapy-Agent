# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional and explicit unconditional positive regard from the first statement throughout; never labels or expresses disapproval. The psychiatrist consistently validates Ryan's ambivalence and sustain talk with warmth and specificity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately capture both the cognitive dissonance and emotional complexity of Ryan's experience. Demonstrates deep attunement to internal experience beyond surface-level paraphrasing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and purposeful progression from incident through ambivalence to values and agency, avoiding tangents. Could benefit from a more explicit summary or consolidation toward the end of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong shared-inquiry language and incorporation of Ryan's own framing throughout. Could be deepened by more explicitly inviting Ryan to set the agenda or steer the conversation in real-time.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support—no direct advice, no pressure to commit, and every question invites Ryan's own perspective. Ryan's choices, timeline, and direction are consistently respected and reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out Ryan's own motivations through values exploration and past success rather than external reasons. Could push one level deeper by developing Ryan's self-efficacy narrative more fully (e.g., scaling questions).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance that consistently positions Ryan as the expert on his own life. The session is collaborative in tone but still has a somewhat guided quality where the psychiatrist leads the exploration rather than fully ceding direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questions effectively elicit authentic change talk, including recognition of past success and genuine concern about losing his spot. Change talk is present but remains somewhat tentative, with lingering 'I guess' qualifiers throughout.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently uses dual-focus reflections that honor sustain talk while creating space for change talk to emerge. Could be more strategic in moments—e.g., reflecting the hopelessness in 'I don't know how to do both' or gently challenging that assumption.
