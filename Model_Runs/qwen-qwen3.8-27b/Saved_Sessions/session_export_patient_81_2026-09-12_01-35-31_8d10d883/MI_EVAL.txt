# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental, and validating stance. However, the stance can feel somewhat transactional and problem-solving-oriented rather than deeply affirming the client’s agency.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are adequate surface-level reflections of the client’s discomfort and practical concerns. The responses miss deeper emotional meaning, such as shame, fear of losing belonging, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session has a coherent practical focus on money, work, and social belonging. However, the target issue of substance use is not consistently connected to the client’s broader goals or harm reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client influences parts of the session by choosing options and pausing the Friday plan. Still, the psychiatrist largely generates the plans, scripts, and behavioral strategies rather than co-creating them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and respects some of the client’s boundaries. Many instructions, however, remain directive and prescriptive, which can undermine the client’s sense of being in charge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited effort to elicit the client’s own motivation, values, or reasons for change. The questions are mainly logistical and lead toward prepackaged solutions rather than internal change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses options and asks for client input, but often assumes the expert or problem-solver role. The session is collaborative in form but directive in substance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offers some action-oriented statements, but they are largely prompted by the psychiatrist’s suggested solutions. There is limited use of evocative questions to strengthen the client’s own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client’s sustain talk is acknowledged but not therapeutically reflected or reframed. The psychiatrist moves quickly to practical alternatives instead of exploring the ambivalence around belonging, identity, and harm.
