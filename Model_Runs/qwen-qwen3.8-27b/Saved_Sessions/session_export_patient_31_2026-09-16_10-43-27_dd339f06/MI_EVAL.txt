# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates David’s ambivalence and limits. Acceptance could be stronger by explicitly naming the emotional burden behind his statements, not just the practical content.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor reflects David’s main concerns accurately but stays mostly content-focused. There is insufficient attention to the feeling-level meanings behind his resistance, stress, and worry about visibility.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains tightly focused on a low-pressure nicotine replacement trial without major topic drift. However, the direction is more procedural than motivational, with repeated recaps that can feel checklist-like.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David is invited to choose among options, and his preferences genuinely shape the plan. Collaboration is strong, though the counselor still supplies much of the structure and detailed instruction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist clearly supports David’s choice by emphasizing that the trial is optional, low-pressure, and under his control. A few early prescriptive moments, such as proposing a 2-week experiment, slightly reduce the consistency of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are some evocative questions early on, but the counselor does not follow up deeply enough to elicit David’s own motivations or reasons for change. The session shifts quickly into practical planning rather than drawing out internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor largely treats David as the expert on his own routine, preferences, and comfort level. Partnership could be stronger by allowing David to generate more of the plan rather than providing detailed step-by-step guidance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David offers some weak change talk, but the counselor does not consistently reflect, amplify, or consolidate it. More follow-up on his scaling responses and personal reasons for reducing would strengthen this criterion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with David’s resistance and accepts his “not quitting” frame. However, the sustain talk is not strategically reflected or pivoted toward exploring ambivalence or potential change.
