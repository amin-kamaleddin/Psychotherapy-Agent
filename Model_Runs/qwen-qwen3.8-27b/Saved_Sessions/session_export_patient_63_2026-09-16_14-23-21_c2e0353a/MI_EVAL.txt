# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes pressure without moralizing. Acceptance could be stronger with more explicit, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures ambivalence and the social pull behind the behavior. Empathy could go deeper into emotional meaning rather than repeating similar reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on shoplifting triggers and the in-store moment, with little topic drift. Direction could improve by consolidating a clear, mutually agreed behavioral target or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative language and incorporates the client's ideas into problem-solving. The session remains somewhat structured by the psychiatrist, so more client-led co-construction would be ideal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently avoids pressuring language and affirms the client's choice. Autonomy support could be made more explicit during moments of doubt or hesitation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client is invited to generate options and offers several ideas, but evocation remains surface-level. Deeper questions about values, meaning, and self-efficacy would strengthen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the client as the expert and follows the client's contributions. Partnership could be more fully client-led if the agenda and plan were more explicitly co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client expresses change talk, but the psychiatrist does not consistently reinforce or amplify it. More deliberate eliciting and expansion of change talk would improve this criterion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without judgment, but the session remains centered on reasons against change. More strategic reframing or double-sided reflections would better soften sustain talk.
