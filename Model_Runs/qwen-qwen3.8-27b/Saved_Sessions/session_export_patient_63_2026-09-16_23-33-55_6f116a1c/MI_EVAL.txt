# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance without moralizing about shoplifting, court involvement, or peer behavior. The main limitation is that the warmth remains somewhat clinical and includes fewer individualized affirmations of Jordan’s worth or effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses strong complex reflections that capture Jordan’s ambivalence between belonging and future consequences. The key weakness is that one response is too generic at a highly emotional moment, missing deeper fear, shame, or helplessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the specific split-second shoplifting trigger and peer context. It would improve with a more collaborative statement of the target behavior and a more gradual move from ambivalence to change talk and action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses “we” language and invites Jordan to co-create the conversation rather than acting as the authority. The collaboration is still largely shaped by the psychiatrist’s framework, pacing, and agenda rather than being fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports Jordan’s autonomy by asking for his own ideas and avoiding direct commands or pressure to choose a plan. Some questions subtly push toward having a strategy or “way out” before Jordan feels ready to formulate one.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks effective open questions that invite Jordan to generate his own thoughts and possible responses. The evocation is somewhat surface-level, with less consistent elicitation of deeper intrinsic motivation, confidence, values, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative stance and positions Jordan as the source of his own solutions. It is not a higher score because the psychiatrist still largely structures the topics, options, and direction of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some change talk around protecting the future and avoiding court, but does not consistently deepen or expand it. More use of scaling, discrepancy development, or values-based follow-up questions is needed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with or dismissing Jordan’s peer-pressure sustain talk and reflects the conflict between belonging and future consequences. The conversation remains too centered on social pressure, with few clear strategic pivots toward personal reasons for change.
