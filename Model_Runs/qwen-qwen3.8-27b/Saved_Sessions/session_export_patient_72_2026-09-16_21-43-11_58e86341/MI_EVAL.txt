# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance while affirming Leila’s plan and preferences. The language is supportive, though somewhat procedural and plan-focused rather than deeply warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Leila’s practical concerns about ease, pressure, and maintaining optionality. The empathy is strong but remains mostly cognitive and behavioral, with less exploration of deeper emotional states.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on making a short walking option doable and moving toward concrete steps. The direction is effective but narrow, with less consistent connection to Leila’s broader goals such as energy, mood, or self-confidence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Leila’s ideas shape the plan substantially, including the lunch walk, coworker, short loop, saved text, and sneaker cue. The counselor is collaborative but still guides the sequence through structured planning prompts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent, with repeated emphasis on choice, optionality, and absence of pressure. The plan is consistently framed as something Leila can initiate, modify, or abandon without guilt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes practical problem-solving ideas but does not consistently draw out deeper intrinsic motivation. Values, hopes, confidence, or identity-related reasons for change are underexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor invites Leila’s input and builds on her ideas, showing a strong collaborative stance. However, frequent menu-style suggestions make the process less fully client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Leila produces useful action and commitment statements through the counselor’s targeted questions. The counselor does not consistently amplify, summarize, or deepen this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Potential barriers such as pressure, low energy, and skipping are reframed in a low-pressure way. The sustain talk is limited, so the softening is more preventive than actively redirecting strong ambivalence.
