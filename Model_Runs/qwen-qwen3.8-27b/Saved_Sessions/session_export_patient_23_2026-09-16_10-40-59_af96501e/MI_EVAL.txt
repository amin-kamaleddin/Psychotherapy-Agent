# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and uses affirming language that normalizes ambivalence. Acceptance could be stronger with more explicit validation of the client’s emotional experience and the difficulty of changing a valued ritual.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses adequate reflective listening and captures important themes, but reflections often remain surface-level and repetitive. Deeper empathy would involve exploring the emotional undercurrents and nuanced tensions in the client’s experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on reducing alcohol use while preserving the client’s evening routine, moving clearly from assessment to practical planning. Direction could be strengthened by more strategic consolidation of the client’s values and personal reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites client input, adapts to preferences, and builds a plan that reflects the client’s own choices. Collaboration could be further enhanced by having the client generate more of the plan and reducing counselor-led structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly emphasizes choice, avoids pressure, and supports the client’s preferred limits and plan. Autonomy support could be stronger by eliciting client-generated strategies before offering specific options or structured templates.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits some change talk through confidence ratings and planning questions, but evocation is often mixed with frequent provision of practical options. The session would benefit from more open-ended questions that draw out deeper values, motivations, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a collaborative stance and allows the client to shape the direction and content of the session. Partnership is slightly limited by moments where the counselor takes a more expert-driven approach by offering multiple suggestions or a structured template.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits useful change talk, including intentions to cap drinks, pre-pour, and use alcohol-free alternatives. More consistent amplification and reflection of the client’s own motivations would help deepen and reinforce change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled reasonably by avoiding confrontation, respecting ambivalence, and preventing the plan from feeling punitive. The counselor could use more advanced techniques, such as double-sided reflections, to better hold and reframe the client’s conflicting motivations.
