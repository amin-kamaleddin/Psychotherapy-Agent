# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes the patient’s concerns without blame. The tone remains respectful and supportive, treating the struggle as understandable rather than a character flaw.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates highly accurate and nuanced reflective listening, capturing both surface behavior and deeper meaning. Reflections about belonging, routine, and protecting against false starts show strong perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear and consistent focus on smoking cessation, progressing from triggers to tools and a practical quit plan. There is no aimless drift, and each question remains tied to the central change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is strongly collaborative, with the psychiatrist acting as an equal partner rather than an authority. The patient’s ideas directly shape the plan, making this a joint exploration rather than a one-way interview.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently supports the patient’s freedom to choose without coercion or pressure. Language emphasizes the patient’s ownership of decisions and what feels realistic for him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently elicits the patient’s own motivations, preferences, and strategies, with key plan elements coming from the patient. However, evocation is sometimes slightly leading through specific options or tools.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly conveys that expertise and wisdom reside within the patient. The patient is repeatedly positioned as the expert on his own life, and the session direction is shaped by his ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor often encourages the patient to generate reasons, plans, and confidence in change. It could be strengthened by more deliberate amplification of ability, commitment, and values-based change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by avoiding argument and reframing worries as reasonable and safety-oriented. A higher score would require more consistent pivots toward change, values, or past successes.
