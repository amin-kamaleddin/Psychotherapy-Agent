# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and accepts Jordan’s ambivalence without pressure. Acceptance is solid but somewhat formulaic, with less opportunity for deeper emotional validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections that capture Jordan’s conflict between self-protection and concern for his grandparents. Empathy is generally good but often remains at the surface level rather than exploring deeper values or internal conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the target behavior, Jordan’s decision about receiving the flu vaccine. There are brief shifts toward logistics and information provision, but the overall direction remains MI-consistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates with Jordan by inviting his preferences and adjusting to his chosen focus. Collaboration is strong, but the psychiatrist occasionally introduces structured clinician-generated options rather than fully co-creating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan’s autonomy is strongly supported, with no pressure to commit and clear acceptance of his boundaries. Autonomy support is slightly reduced by advice-giving and drafting message options, which shift some focus toward external planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively uses scaling and open questions to elicit Jordan’s own reasons for change. Evocation is interrupted by information provision and problem-solving, limiting deeper exploration of values, fears, and identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys a collaborative, non-hierarchical stance by inviting Jordan to direct the session. Partnership is strong but could be improved by more explicitly framing Jordan as the expert on his own values and decision-making.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and scaling successfully evoke several instances of change talk from Jordan. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify, summarize, or deepen the change talk, and the session ends without a strong commitment summary.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not argue against Jordan’s reasons for waiting, but sustain talk remains a major focus throughout the session. The session could be stronger by using double-sided reflections or reframing to gently shift focus toward change.
