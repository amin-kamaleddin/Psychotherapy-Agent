# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates exceptional non-judgmental warmth throughout, validating painful beliefs without challenging or dismissing them. Normalizes client's experience and honors her subjective reality even when she expresses self-critical or dismissive statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and progressively deepen from cognitive to emotional to meaning-level understanding. Skillfully identifies the protective motivation behind behaviors and validates painful beliefs about her daughter without either agreeing or disagreeing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear session arc from resistance to articulation of personal reasons for change while following the client's lead. Could benefit from more explicit summarization of progress and tying the various themes together into a coherent change narrative.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is clearly a joint exploration rather than a clinical interview, with the session co-constructed from the client's contributions. Uses invitational language throughout and maintains an egalitarian power dynamic without performativeness.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly affirms autonomy ('that's your call, not mine') and completely honors the client's 'not ready' without challenging it. Never prescribes, minimizes choices, or frames smoking as something she 'should' stop.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All change motivations emerge from the client through skillful, progressively paced open questions. The final articulation of a concrete vision ('if I could handle the 2am without reaching for one') is a product of pure evocation without any direct persuasion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert on her own experience using open questions and invitations rather than directives. Explicitly shares power and never positions as the authority on smoking cessation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicits desire, need, and ability statements and skillfully reflects and amplifies them. Could be strengthened by more explicitly naming and reinforcing the change talk or incorporating a scaling question about confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk skillfully by validating past failures without arguing and explicitly respecting 'not ready.' Could be enhanced with more explicit double-sided reflections to help the client hold both truths simultaneously.
