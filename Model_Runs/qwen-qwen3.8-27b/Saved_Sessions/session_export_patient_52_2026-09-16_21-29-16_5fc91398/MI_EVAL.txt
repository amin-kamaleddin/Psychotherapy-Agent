# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained unconditional positive regard without judgment, labeling, or pushback. The apology and nondefensive responses helped create a safe, accepting space.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and nuanced, capturing both practical and emotional experience. The psychiatrist validated exhaustion, guilt, and the protective function of alcohol without being generic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on reducing alcohol to one drink while respecting the client’s prioritization of childcare. A brief summary explicitly connecting the childcare plan to the alcohol goal would have strengthened the through-line.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited the client’s input and followed her lead throughout. However, taking on the task of finding the respite program slightly shifted the dynamic toward a helper role rather than full co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforced the client’s right to choose her own pace and boundaries. Options were offered as noncoercive choices rather than directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions elicited the client’s preferences, criteria, and practical solutions. The evocation could have gone deeper by exploring values, identity, or future envisioning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client was positioned as the expert on her own life, and her boundaries were respected. The partnership could be strengthened with more explicit power-sharing language and joint planning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively built on the client’s one-drink idea and reinforced her commitment. There were missed opportunities to elicit deeper confidence- or values-based change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well through validation rather than argument or direct challenge. More active double-sided reflections could have helped pivot from minimization toward the change side.
