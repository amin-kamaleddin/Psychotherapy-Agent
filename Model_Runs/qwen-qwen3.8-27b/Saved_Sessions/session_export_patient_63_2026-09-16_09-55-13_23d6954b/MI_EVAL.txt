# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalizes Jordan’s ambivalence and concerns. However, the acceptance is somewhat task-oriented and is interrupted by frequent shifts toward practical advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Jordan’s surface conflict about peer pressure and wanting to stop. Deeper emotional layers, such as shame, fear of being seen as a coward, loyalty, and craving, are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session remains focused on shoplifting, peer pressure, refusal skills, and a concrete next step. The direction is clear but more behavioral and skill-based than focused on MI change processes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan is invited to choose lines, practice, and set a weekly goal, which supports collaboration. However, the psychiatrist dominates problem-solving by providing most of the scripts, tactics, and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and asks Jordan to make choices, supporting some autonomy. The session becomes somewhat prescriptive, and phrases like “commit to this week” may create mild pressure in an MI context.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A few evocative questions are asked early, such as reasons to change and readiness. Much of the session, however, relies on the psychiatrist providing solutions rather than eliciting Jordan’s own motivations, values, or change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial partnership through offering choices and inviting Jordan to pick lines and practice. The psychiatrist remains the primary source of ideas and directs the content with scripts and step-by-step plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jordan offers some change-related statements and affirms a willingness to try “Let’s bounce.” These moments are not consistently expanded through focused follow-up questions about desire, need, values, ability, or outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not reinforce sustain talk and validates concerns about peer pressure and making a scene. However, sustain talk is not skillfully softened through double-sided reflections, exploring costs, or reframing the tension.
