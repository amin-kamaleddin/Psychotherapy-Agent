# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment through validating statements. A few mildly directive or expectation-setting statements slightly reduce the acceptance stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interviewer offers several accurate reflections of frustration and exhaustion, but often remains at a surface level. Deeper emotions such as loneliness, shame, fear, and feeling unseen by her mother are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays fairly well focused on the target issue of weight loss efforts, exercise, eating, fatigue, and irregular periods. The interviewer maintains a clear direction by narrowing the conversation toward a small experiment and follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some collaborative moves, such as asking whether the client wants help building a plan and inviting input on how to report back. However, much of the structure, plan, and content is still supplied by the psychiatrist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interviewer supports choice by asking permission and inviting small steps, which is autonomy-friendly. This is partially undercut by prescriptive suggestions, exact amounts, scripts, timelines, and expectations for the next-day message.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist provides many reasons for change and medical rationales rather than eliciting the client's own motivations. Fewer questions are used to draw out the client's values, hopes, and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interviewer asks the client to participate in planning and checks her willingness to try small steps. Overall, the session remains more expert-led than equal, with the psychiatrist supplying much of the structure and content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client does express some change talk, but the interviewer does not consistently use eliciting questions or reflections to expand it. The psychiatrist often supplies reasons for change and a plan rather than helping the client articulate her own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interviewer avoids reinforcing the client's reasons against change and offers some normalizing or reassuring responses. However, the interviewer also directly counters sustain talk with factual advice, which can feel argumentative rather than reflective.
