# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses nonjudgmental, affirming language and validates Farid’s workload and fatigue. The stance is warm and respectful, though slightly generic at times.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures Farid’s ambivalence and value conflict. However, the empathy does not always fully explore the deeper emotional weight of shame, fear, or repeated self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on small, feasible movement and progressively narrows the conversation toward specific options. The main limitation is that the early focus is somewhat broad and the plan is not fully consolidated at the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist behaves as a partner, uses joint problem-solving language, and builds on Farid’s own ideas. However, the counselor still directs the focus toward a tiny step and a routine cue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is one of the strongest domains, with consistent support for Farid’s choice and ownership. The counselor avoids advice, persuasion, or pressure while still helping identify a small step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open, evocative questions effectively draw out Farid’s own motivations, obstacles, and ideas. The performance could be stronger by more systematically developing desire, ability, need, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Farid to lead and follows his emerging ideas, showing a collaborative stance. The counselor does not fully share power in every moment, as they still select the therapeutic frame and pace.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently creates opportunities for Farid to express change-oriented language and affirms his suggestions. The change talk could be deepened more strategically using scaling, importance questions, or confidence questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing and gently reframes self-blame, which helps reduce sustain talk. However, the session remains heavily focused on barriers, with fewer strategic pivots toward values, benefits, or change talk.
