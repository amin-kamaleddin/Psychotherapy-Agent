# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally conveys respect and nonjudgment, but the warmth can feel somewhat scripted and procedural. A brief legal correction slightly interrupts the relational flow, preventing a higher rating.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and helpful but remain largely factual and repetitive. Deeper emotional meaning—such as frustration, pressure, or fear of the system—is not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus, but it is mainly on administrative resolution rather than the target behavior. The psychiatrist does not consistently guide the conversation back toward change-relevant goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers options and follows the client’s preferences, showing a collaborative stance. However, the collaboration remains limited to logistics rather than co-creating a broader change plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms the client’s choice, permission, and control. There is no pressure to contact probation or accept extra requirements, making this a strong area.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions are asked, but the psychiatrist often shifts to providing information or options. The client’s own motivations and values are not deeply drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shares control and asks for input, but does not consistently position the client as the primary expert. The focus remains on practical tasks rather than the client’s own perspective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are attempts to elicit change-relevant language, but these are limited and not effectively built upon. The session shifts toward administrative completion before exploring the client’s reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with neutral or factual responses rather than skillful MI techniques. The psychiatrist does not effectively redirect or soften the client’s resistance to change.
