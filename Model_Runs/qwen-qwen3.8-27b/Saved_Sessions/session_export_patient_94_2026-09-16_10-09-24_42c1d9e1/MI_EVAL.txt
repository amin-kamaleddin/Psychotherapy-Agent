# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids labeling Maria as noncompliant. Acceptance is strong but occasionally feels generic, and the 'quick correction' can sound slightly authoritative rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Maria's concerns, practical constraints, and fear of breathlessness at work. There is room to go deeper by exploring the emotional meaning of that fear and her need for control.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma management, rescue inhaler use, safety, and a practical plan. It could be more strategic by consistently guiding toward change motivation rather than logistical details and documentation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist collaborates effectively on a concrete safety plan that fits Maria's preferences. However, the psychiatrist still takes the lead, so the collaboration is respectful but not fully fluid or equal-expert.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist strongly supports Maria's choice not to start a daily controller and protects her from being flagged as noncompliant. A few prescriptive suggestions slightly reduce the emphasis on her full ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some motivation by asking what matters most and inviting a small action. More questions and reflections were needed to fully draw out Maria's internal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and asks what feels doable, which supports a collaborative stance. The partnership becomes uneven when the psychiatrist shifts into a more expert, instructive role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maria expresses several change-relevant priorities, such as keeping her job and feeling safe at work. The psychiatrist does not consistently deepen or amplify this change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges Maria's resistance respectfully and redirects toward an achievable safety action. Direct medical correction and continued focus on the medication limit more skillful softening of sustain talk.
