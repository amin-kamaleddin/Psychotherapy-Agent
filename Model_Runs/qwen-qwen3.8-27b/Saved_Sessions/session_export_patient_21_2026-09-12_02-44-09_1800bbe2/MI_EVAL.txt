# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and gives the client permission to stop. It falls short of a 5 because affirmations are practical rather than deeply strength-based.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are adequate and validating, but mostly practical and surface-level. Deeper complex reflections of the client’s ambivalence and underlying feelings would strengthen empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on increasing physical activity in a manageable way. It moves effectively from ambivalence to a concrete, testable plan with clear follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client meaningfully contributes to the plan, including the cue, cutoff, signals, and reminder. The psychiatrist still drives much of the structure through menus and a detailed protocol.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is repeatedly supported to choose, set parameters, and retain control over the plan. The therapist’s structured suggestions still slightly narrow a fully client-generated approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses evocative questions to elicit plan elements and client input. Evocation is limited by frequent advice-giving and therapist-supplied strategies before exploring the client’s own reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses collaborative questions and allows the client to shape the plan. The session remains somewhat clinician-led because the therapist introduces many options and explanations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers useful change statements, such as trying the plan after coffee and choosing a cutoff. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or deepen the client’s own change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validates concerns without arguing, dismissing, or moralizing. Softening could be stronger by using more double-sided reflections and strategic pivots before moving into problem-solving.
