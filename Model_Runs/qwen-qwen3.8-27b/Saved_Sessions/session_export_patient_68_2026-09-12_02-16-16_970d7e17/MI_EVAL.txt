# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful, nonjudgmental, and avoids shaming Kevin for the injury or drinking. Acceptance is consistent, but the warmth feels somewhat formulaic and task-oriented rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers adequate reflections that capture some ambivalence and social context. However, the reflections remain largely content-level and miss deeper emotions such as shame, fear, and peer pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use, injury prevention, and harm reduction without wandering. The main limitation is a somewhat mechanical move toward behavior planning before fully exploring discrepancy or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kevin contributes to the plan, and his preferences shape some of the final steps. However, the psychiatrist often drives the process by presenting option lists and narrowing choices, which limits true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly invites choice, asks for permission, and avoids coercion. Autonomy support is weakened at times by the amount of specific advice given, especially when Kevin expresses discomfort about being managed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist evokes some confidence and practical steps through rulers and targeted questions. Evocation is limited by frequent advice-giving and insufficient exploration of Kevin’s own deeper reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial partnership, with Kevin invited to set the agenda and select options. The psychiatrist often shifts into an expert/advice-giving role, which reduces the sense that Kevin is the expert on his own life and change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kevin offers useful change talk around safer venues, smaller glasses, earlier leaving, and support strategies. The psychiatrist affirms this but does not consistently deepen, reinforce, or expand Kevin’s own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately acknowledges the positive functions of drinking and avoids arguing with Kevin’s sustain talk. Softening is not consistently strategic, with fewer double-sided reflections or skilled pivots that transform sustain talk into change talk.
