# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids blame. However, repeated safety concerns make the tone feel somewhat procedural and intrusive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses accurate reflections that capture the client's core concerns. The empathy remains somewhat surface-level and does not fully explore underlying frustration or loss of autonomy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on asthma self-management and inhaler access. The direction is clear but becomes narrow and repetitive around backup plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites input and allows the client to shape parts of the plan. Collaboration is limited by repeatedly presenting clinician-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms the client's freedom to choose and respect his decisions. Persistent requests for backup plans create subtle pressure that weakens autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client motivation is drawn out through open-ended questions. The psychiatrist often supplies solutions rather than allowing the client to generate them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist attempts partnership by asking permission and inviting input. The interaction remains uneven in power-sharing because the psychiatrist keeps introducing clinician-driven options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client articulates a concrete plan, but the psychiatrist does not consistently affirm or amplify the change talk. There are missed opportunities to build confidence and explore motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges the client's sustain talk without arguing. Repeated emphasis on safety and backup plans can reinforce resistance rather than resolve ambivalence.
