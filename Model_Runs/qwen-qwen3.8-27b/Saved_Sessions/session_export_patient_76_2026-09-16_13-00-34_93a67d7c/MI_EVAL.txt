# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates respect, warmth, and nonjudgment while normalizing the function of cutting. However, the warmth is somewhat procedural, and repeated safety checks can feel more risk-management than deeply accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows strong reflective listening and accurate perspective-taking around exhaustion, noise, and relief. The empathy is somewhat repetitive and misses deeper emotional themes such as shame, loneliness, or hopelessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on self-harm, urges, safety, and coping, without wandering into unrelated topics. However, the direction is circular and lacks strategic momentum or a clear shared next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is collaborative language, but the interaction remains more counselor-driven than equal-partner. The psychiatrist often offers options rather than deeply co-constructing interventions from Liora’s own perspective.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses permission-seeking, choice-focused language, and respects refusals without coercion. The main limitation is that repeated suggestions for coping steps can create a subtle sense of being steered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some open questions elicit Liora’s experience, but the session relies heavily on suggesting external strategies. There is limited evocation of her own ability, hope, values, or motivation for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist uses some partnership language and invites input, but the session remains largely therapist-led. Liora’s contributions shape parts of the content but do not substantially redirect the agenda or create a co-owned plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to cultivate change talk are weak, and the conversation remains dominated by sustain talk. Missed opportunities include evoking Liora’s emerging desire for less exhaustion and reflecting hope for a different outcome.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing but does not effectively soften or pivot away from sustain talk. The repeated focus on risk, access, and failed alternatives keeps the conversation anchored to the problem rather than shifting toward possible change.
