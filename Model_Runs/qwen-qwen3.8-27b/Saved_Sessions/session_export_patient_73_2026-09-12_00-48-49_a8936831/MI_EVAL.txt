# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental, and supportive stance with consistent validation and low-shame language. Acceptance was strong but somewhat formulaic and not fully individualized or explicitly unconditional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist reflected the client’s fear, worry, and ambivalence in a reasonable but surface-level way. Deeper understanding of the client’s identity, belonging, and shame related to smoking was limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on tobacco use and narrowed the target to a manageable behavioral experiment. Direction was clear and task-focused, though somewhat more plan-driven than motivation-driven.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered options and incorporated client preferences into the plan. However, the counselor remained the main source of strategies and problem-solving, limiting fuller collaborative balance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized low pressure, invited choice, and respected the client’s preferences and boundaries. Autonomy support was strong, though somewhat offset by frequent concrete directives and repeated reminders.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked mainly assessment- and plan-oriented questions rather than questions designed to draw out the client’s own reasons for change. The client’s internal motivation and values were not consistently explored or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited client input and allowed the client to shape parts of the plan. Partnership was present but partial, as the counselor frequently positioned himself as the primary guide and problem-solver.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client produced some spontaneous change talk, and the psychiatrist occasionally affirmed it. There was limited use of MI-style questions, reflection, or amplification to actively strengthen and expand change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing with sustain talk but did not consistently soften or redirect it toward change. Sustain talk was often met with practical coping strategies rather than exploration of discrepancy or alternative perspectives.
