# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoided moralizing, arguing, or shaming. Acceptance was consistent but sometimes procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client's ambivalence, pressure, and desire for control. Empathy could be stronger by exploring deeper emotional undercurrents and reducing repetitive reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session opened toward the smoking-cessation target but quickly shifted to dental logistics. It did not consistently connect the conversation back to the client's own reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited client input and built a practical plan around the client's stated preferences. The interaction remained somewhat therapist-managed despite the client's ideas shaping the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice was repeatedly and clearly affirmed, with strong respect for the client's need to avoid pressure and maintain control. This was the strongest aspect of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions tended to focus on logistics rather than drawing out the client's internal motivations, values, or reasons for change. There was limited follow-through when the client showed brief openness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist took a collaborative stance and respected client boundaries and preferences. The session remained fairly therapist-directed in pacing and structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There were brief attempts to elicit change talk, but the counselor did not strategically expand or amplify it. The conversation shifted to practical details rather than strengthening the client's own arguments for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoided arguing and acknowledged the client's resistance without amplifying it. However, sustain talk was not consistently used as an opportunity to gently redirect toward change.
