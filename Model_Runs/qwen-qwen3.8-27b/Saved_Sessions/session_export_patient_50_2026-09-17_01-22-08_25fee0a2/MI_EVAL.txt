# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warm, nonjudgmental, and individualized positive regard throughout. There is no labeling, disapproval, or conditional acceptance, even when Hana expresses intense self-doubt or avoidance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate, complex, and emotionally attuned, capturing ambivalence, core beliefs, and the weight of self-evaluation. The psychiatrist tracks the shift from practical barriers to deeper identity concerns with sustained sensitivity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a generally clear focus on the target behavior of a small study step, with a logical progression from action to barrier to belief. It could be tightened further by more explicitly linking deeper insights back to a concrete next step, especially near the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely collaborative, with Hana’s language, metaphors, and contributions shaping the session’s direction. The psychiatrist facilitates without lecturing, advising, or imposing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with nonpressuring, open questions and respect for Hana’s pace and decisions. It could be slightly strengthened by more explicit affirmations of her right to choose or not choose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully uses open, curious questions to draw out Hana’s own insights, motivations, and change language. Key insights such as the five-minute plan, permission to stop, and the 2am looping image are largely client-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Hana is consistently positioned as the expert on her own experience, and the psychiatrist avoids assumptions or interpretations imposed by the counselor. The session reflects a real partnership in which Hana’s responses guide the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is effectively evoked, including desire, ability, and future-oriented statements about stopping the avoidance cycle. The psychiatrist could more systematically amplify and build on these statements, especially by making them explicit reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled competently without arguing, minimizing, or reinforcing it, and several reflections reframe fear as self-awareness. However, the psychiatrist could pivot more actively toward change and could better process the final intense sustain statement before the session ends.
