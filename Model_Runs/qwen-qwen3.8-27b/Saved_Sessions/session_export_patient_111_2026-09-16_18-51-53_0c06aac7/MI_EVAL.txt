# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and avoids arguing, lecturing, or invalidating Richard. Acceptance is strong but could include warmer, more individualized validation to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects Richard’s resistance, role of his wife, and discomfort with being pressured. The reflections are helpful but remain somewhat surface-level and formulaic, missing deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session generally stays connected to smoking and health without drifting into unrelated topics. However, the focus repeatedly shifts toward ending the session rather than creating strategic momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invites Richard’s input and adapts to his preferences, including working with the blood-pressure note idea. Collaboration is present but remains fairly surface-level rather than fully co-constructive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through frequent emphasis on choice, no pressure, and respect for Richard’s decisions. The therapist could more consistently reinforce Richard’s agency within the change process itself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist makes some evocative attempts by asking what would make the conversation meaningful or less pressuring. However, there is limited exploration of Richard’s internal values, fears, hopes, or personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions Richard as the expert on his own life and repeatedly invites his perspective. The partnership is respectful but reactive, with less evidence of a truly shared agenda or co-created purpose.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are a few attempts to invite Richard to speak in his own terms, but change talk is not consistently elicited or strengthened. Important statements about not wanting to be the reason his wife worries are not fully developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledges sustain talk without escalating resistance or arguing against it. More advanced MI skill would include double-sided reflections or reframing to gently open space for change.
