# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated respect, nonjudgment, and validation of Hana’s fear, self-doubt, and ambivalence. It fell short of a 5 because the warmth remained somewhat clinical and did not fully affirm Hana as a whole person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening and perspective-taking were strong, especially when capturing Hana’s mixed feelings and somatic distress. Empathy was limited at times by moving too quickly from emotion to problem-solving and not fully exploring deeper shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on exam-related anxiety, avoidance, postponement, and preparation while maintaining logical momentum. It was slightly weakened by not narrowing the discussion into a clearer, concrete, client-led next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist invited Hana to contribute to problem-solving and adapted to her concerns about privacy and self-doubt. However, the psychiatrist still led much of the structure, so the process was not fully co-created by the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana was generally supported to choose her own path, with minimal pressure or direct persuasion. Autonomy support was limited because the psychiatrist did not more explicitly affirm Hana’s right to decide any option, including not trying or changing the exam plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist used many evocative questions to help Hana explore her fears, values, and possible responses. Evocation could be strengthened by more deeply eliciting Hana’s own motivation, confidence, and change-oriented goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana was repeatedly positioned as the expert on her own experience, and her perspective was frequently invited. The partnership was not fully at a 5 because the psychiatrist still maintained a fairly structured agenda and offered some leading conceptual framing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana expressed desire, reasons, and motivation for change, but the psychiatrist did not consistently amplify or deepen this language. The session could have better developed DARC components, especially ability, confidence, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided arguing with or dismissing Hana’s reasons for staying the same, and responded to fear with normalization and nonjudgment. The score was limited because strong sustain talk was not always strategically reframed toward change or action.
