# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently uses warm, validating, nonjudgmental language and normalizes the client's struggles. Acceptance is strong but occasionally evaluates past coping and lacks an explicit statement of unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects the client's ambivalence, exhaustion, and fear of turning self-care into pressure. Some reflections are repetitive or slightly interpretive, with room to explore emotional weight more directly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on aligning self-care with self-respect without topic drift. It moves clearly from values to a concrete, manageable evening check-in and sticky-note plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist builds on the client's own ideas, such as the tea anchor and sticky note, rather than imposing a plan. Collaboration is strong, though long therapist turns and framing make it slightly less fully shared.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasizes the client's choice and avoids pushing the plan. It could be stronger by more explicitly stating that the decision is entirely the client's and reinforcing ownership during moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions encourage the client to generate her own solutions and meanings. Evocation is effective but does not fully elicit deeper values, confidence, or explicit commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's perspective guides the session, and her ideas shape the final plan. Partnership is strong, though the therapist still provides substantial framing and summarization that feels somewhat directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits clear change talk through affirmations and questions about workable self-care steps. It is effective but does not systematically evoke all types of change talk, especially confidence and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Concerns about self-judgment, tiredness, and ignoring the sticky note are gently reframed rather than challenged. Softening is strong, though it could use more double-sided reflections to explicitly resolve accountability versus self-compassion.
