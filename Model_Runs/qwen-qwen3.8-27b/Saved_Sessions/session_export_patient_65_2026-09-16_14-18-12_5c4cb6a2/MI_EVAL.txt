# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance toward Julian's guardedness and legal situation. Acceptance is somewhat procedural and task-focused rather than deeply affirming Julian as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Julian's stuckness, worry, and self-protective motivation. A higher score would require deeper exploration of shame, fear, or the meaning of the court mandate.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the court-mandated community service and concrete next steps. Each interaction advances practical planning with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan and text are co-constructed using Julian's ideas and wording. The psychiatrist still frames many options and steers the conversation, so collaboration is strong but not fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supports choice by asking what would feel natural and safe without pressuring action. More explicit affirmation that the decision is Julian's would strengthen this criterion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits practical intentions, wording, and action steps from Julian. Evocation remains mostly logistical and does not consistently access deeper personal motivation or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Julian is invited to contribute throughout, and his input shapes the plan. The psychiatrist still guides the sequence fairly strongly, limiting full partnership in some moments.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session draws out useful action language, such as texting the case worker and confirming details. There are missed opportunities to deepen change talk using importance, confidence, or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with compliance-focused statements and acknowledges external motivation. Responses remain closely tied to avoiding trouble, with limited reframing toward intrinsic motivation.
