# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates the client's distress without blame. Acceptance is strong but sometimes feels procedural due to repeated safety checks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician shows adequate understanding of exhaustion and internal pressure. Reflections are helpful but often remain surface-level and miss deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on safety, urges, and harm reduction. The direction is clinically sound but somewhat repetitive and mechanical.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are collaborative gestures, but the psychiatrist often drives the agenda with lists and plans. The client's stated concerns are not fully integrated into a shared approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician uses choice language and allows the client to refuse options. Autonomy support is weakened by the high volume of advice and repeated plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some early questions evoke the client's experience, but the session is mostly advice-based. There is limited effort to draw out the client's own motivations or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction has some surface-level partnership, but the stance remains largely clinician-led. The client's expertise about what feels manageable is not consistently central.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There are small moments of client willingness, such as trying ice or a cold pack. The psychiatrist does not consistently amplify or strengthen the client's own change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoids arguing with sustain talk but often reinforces cutting as effective. There is not enough MI-consistent turning or softening to explore ambivalence and alternatives.
