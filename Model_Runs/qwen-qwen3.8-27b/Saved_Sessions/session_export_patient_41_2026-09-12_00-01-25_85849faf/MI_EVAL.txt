# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and normalizes the client's distress. However, the acceptance is somewhat task-focused rather than deeply relational.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides adequate but surface-level reflections of the client's feelings. Frequently shifts to behavioral instructions rather than exploring the deeper emotional weight of her shame and fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent, and tightly focused direction toward the target behavior of reducing avoidance and increasing follow-through. There is little irrelevant drift throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Incorporates some of the client's preferences into the final plan. However, the interaction remains largely clinician-led, with the psychiatrist supplying most of the specific behavioral options and contingency plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices and respects boundaries with low-pressure options. However, frequent moment-to-moment directive instructions slightly undermine client-led autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Begins with an open question but quickly transitions into directive problem-solving. Missed opportunities to deeply explore what follow-through would mean to the client or to draw out her internal confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites the client to make choices within the provided framework. However, retains a strong expert role by frequently supplying specific techniques without explicitly emphasizing the client's own wisdom as the source of direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicits some action-oriented behavioral commitments. However, lacks deeper exploration of the client's underlying desire, ability, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalizes potential slips and reduces shame by preventing total failure interpretations. Could have used more double-sided reflections to redirect focus from the barriers back to the client's reasons for change.
