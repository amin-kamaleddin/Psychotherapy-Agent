# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unconditional positive regard even at difficult moments, responding to the self-harm disclosure with 'there's no judgment here' and compassionately reframing the client's 'broken' self-narrative. Normalizing ambivalence from the first exchange set a nonjudgmental tone that held throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate complex reflections captured both content and underlying meaning, e.g., 'what cutting gives you isn't the pain itself — it's the quiet afterward.' This depth of listening was validated by the client's elaboration and deeper disclosures.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, purposeful focus on the client's coping and distress, with each question building coherently toward understanding her reliance on self-harm. Direction was exploratory rather than change-focused, with no concrete step or target behavior agreed upon.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A fully collaborative stance throughout: disclaiming expert status and letting the client's own contributions (roommate's dinner, childhood caretaking) shape the session's course. Followed up on her words rather than redirecting to a clinician's agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly and repeatedly supported the client's choice — 'you don't have to force hope right now' and offering options like talking or just listening. Offered options rather than directives and never imposed a plan, even implicitly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicited rather than dispensed motivation, with excellent evocative questioning about what calm might look like without the cost. Motivation was drawn out mostly as ambivalence and glimpses of hope rather than developed into explicit client-stated desire or commitment for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as the expert ('you're the expert on your own life') and offered choice at every turn. Her answers genuinely shaped the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited hope and change-adjacent language, such as 'maybe part of me thought, fine, one visit won't hurt,' and concrete positives about more bearable days. Fell short of a 5 because the client remained mostly in sustain talk and commitment or confidence language was never explicitly invited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well with double-sided reflections and never argued against cutting or reinforced the 'nothing works' narrative, instead pivoting to 'what was different about that day?'. A point deducted because sustain talk dominated the session and was not always actively redirected toward change.
