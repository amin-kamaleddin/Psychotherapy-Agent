# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental throughout, validating the client's frustration without criticism. Affirmations were somewhat sparse and formulaic ('Good choice', 'Nice, doable step'), keeping it below a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured the client's weariness and imposed motivation ('worn out and a bit pressured... A 3 makes sense'). Reflections were often brief and bundled with questions, leaving deeper emotional nuance (shame over quitting, fatigue with lectures) unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on medication adherence with no topic drift; the session moved logically from engagement to a concrete plan, slip-recovery rule, definition of success, and a specific follow-up with a clear recap.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed—the client chose options, timing, success criteria, and slip rules. Sometimes the counselor presented pre-packaged option lists in advisor-like bursts rather than fully eliciting ideas from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength: asked permission, framed all decisions as the client's, and accepted his limited one-week commitment and boundaries without any coercive or persuasive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions elicited the client's own solutions, key insights ('don't let one miss turn into zero'), and definitions of success. The client's underlying personal 'why' for managing diabetes was left relatively unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as the expert—asking permission, offering choices, and letting his contributions shape the plan. Options were sometimes stacked rapidly in one turn, tilting slightly toward a counselor-driven agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance and confidence rulers produced measurable movement (importance 3; confidence 5→6→7), elicited commitment language, specific planning, and the client's own closing endorsement that the session 'wasn't as bad as I figured it'd be'.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled without argument—'a second job' concerns were met with pivots toward low-effort options and resistance was accepted rather than challenged. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections, and some sustain talk (soda preference) was passed over rather than strategically softened.
