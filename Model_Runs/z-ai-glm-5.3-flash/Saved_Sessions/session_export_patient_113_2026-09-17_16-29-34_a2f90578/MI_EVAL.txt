# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

**Client:** Rowan
**Session Focus:** Charging/using health monitoring device

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance despite blunt resistance ("I don't know, and I still don't wanna charge it"). Validations like "That makes sense," "Valid," and "I respect that" communicate acceptance; however, warmth was somewhat formulaic, and individualized affirmations of the client's strengths were lacking. ☑ Criteria met

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on ("feels like one more thing on a crowded list"; "feels pointless when you feel fine"). Did not deepen exploration of the emotional core (e.g., fear behind "what if the numbers say nothing?"), and later reflections became perfunctory repetitions of logistics. ☑ Criteria met

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stayed firmly focused on the target behavior throughout, moving efficiently from exploration to scaling questions to a concrete plan (Mon 10pm, Thu 8am, alarm, phone charger). Excessive repetition of the plan summary at the end slightly undermined purposeful progression. ☑ Criteria met

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed work as a joint experiment ("we'll look at them together") and co-constructed the plan by asking the client to pick times and choose between options. Troubleshooting was delivered in a more expert-driven, one-directional manner, slightly tilting the power balance. ☑ Criteria met

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary. Honored client choice at every step: "If you're open," "no pressure," agreeing to no reminder texts, "I'll only look at the readings if you bring them up." Client's boundaries ("don't make a whole thing") were fully accommodated rather than overridden. ☑ Criteria met

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the client's own motivations via scaling questions (importance 3, confidence 5–6) and open questions ("What would need to change for it to matter more to you?"). Moved quickly to proposing solutions rather than developing "maybe it wouldn't kill me" ambivalence into stronger self-generated motivation. ☑ Criteria met

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client's contributions shaped the session: accepted "no reminders" request, offered choice between experiments, asked for input on times and modifications. Slipped into an advice-giving expert role with charger troubleshooting tips. ☑ Criteria met

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions effectively evoked change talk ("Maybe... maybe trying it a couple times wouldn't kill me," confidence 5–6, "I'll do the two times this week"). Missed opportunities to deepen these statements (e.g., why confidence is 5 rather than lower). ☑ Criteria met

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ("nothing's gonna change," "then it was a waste," "I'm done with it" ultimatum) was acknowledged neutrally rather than reframed with double-sided reflections or gently challenged. The "done if normal" ultimatum predicting discontinuation went unaddressed. ☑ Criteria met (partially)

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.0/5**

**Summary:** Solid MI session with a highly resistant, precontemplative client. Strengths: excellent autonomy support, clear direction with a client-chosen behavioral experiment, effective scaling questions, and accurate early reflections — Rowan moved from flat refusal to "I'll do the two times." Areas for improvement: (1) actively soften sustain talk, especially the discontinuation ultimatum; (2) deepen change talk before rushing to solutions; (3) reduce expert-mode advice-giving during troubleshooting; (4) avoid repetitive closing summaries; (5) add individualized affirmations of client strengths.
