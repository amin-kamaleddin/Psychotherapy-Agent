# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with affirmations that validated the client's style and choices. Repeatedly offering scripts after Marisol declined multiple times slightly undermined unconditional regard for her stated preference for independence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening captured the client's attachment to the piercing and her dismissal of the dentist's warnings. Reflections were sometimes brief and formulaic, and the deeper identity meaning ('I feel more like myself with it') went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on the oral health risks of the lip piercing throughout, never drifting off-topic. The session culminated in a specific, actionable plan covering metal, flat-back post, and stopping chewing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sought permission, offered option menus, and co-constructed a plan shaped largely by the client's own contributions. Repeated unsolicited script-offering temporarily shifted the dynamic toward coach-client and required the client to push back.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed everything as the client's choice and explicitly honored her decision to keep the piercing and use her own words. Repeated offers to write scripts and reminder texts came across as gently steering despite her stated preference.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from the client's own values, eliciting self-motivating statements like 'it's my teeth' and 'now I've got an actual plan.' Evocation relied partly on information-giving and option menus rather than deeper exploration of her reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine partnership moments included permission-seeking and honoring the client's own wording. However, pushing scripts, drafted messages, and reminder lists after at least three declines suggests the counselor sometimes assumed an expert role rather than trusting the client's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and capitalized on change talk, moving a pre-contemplative client toward self-generated plan language and commitment. Some change talk was counselor-suggested rather than fully client-generated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing, using acceptant reflections and pivoting to protective options, which softened resistance over the session. Redirection sometimes involved immediately listing options rather than strategic double-sided reflections to develop discrepancy.
