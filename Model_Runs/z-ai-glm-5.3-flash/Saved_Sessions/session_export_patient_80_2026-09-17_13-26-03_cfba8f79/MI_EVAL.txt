# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and slips framed as 'useful info, not failure.' However, warmth is somewhat formulaic and lacks individualized regard for Luka's deeper values, such as not becoming like his father.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is sparse; emotionally rich disclosures (the father comparison, 'It's not just about me, eh') are met with strategy lists rather than reflections. Feelings like evening restlessness and fear of repeating his father's pattern go unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on reducing alcohol use, anchored in a concrete 4-beer Saturday goal with a structured Monday follow-up plan. Deducted because the violence/aggression component and relational goals Luka flagged as most important receive limited sustained attention.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Real collaboration in planning—Luka co-constructed the goal, chose strategies, and set the check-in agenda. However, the counselor often responds to disclosures with directive tip lists and 'Quick recap' monologues resembling a coach delivering instructions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly offers choice, asks permission, and frames setbacks as information rather than failure ('Thanks for not lecturing me'). Deducted for advice blocks delivered without permission and handing Luka pre-made strategies rather than trusting him to generate his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions successfully elicited client-generated change talk, but evocation is not sustained. Deeply personal motivators—avoiding his father's path, his partner's reframe—are not drawn out or used to build discrepancy, with most of the session spent providing solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Luka's choices clearly shaped the session and the counselor incorporated them. Still, the counselor frequently slips into an expert/instructor role with rapid-fire advice lists, undercutting genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is elicited effectively (e.g., 'Maybe the weekend limit thing? That feels doable'), but is not deepened or reflected when it occurs. Luka's insight that 'fine and what I actually want might be two different things' receives no reflective response.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing and does not amplify sustain talk, but also does little to soften or redirect it. Statements like 'Pint with the mates — that's just part of who I am' get no double-sided reflection, just practical tips instead of helping Luka resolve ambivalence himself.
