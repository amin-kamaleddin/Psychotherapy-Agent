# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with individualized affirmations ('the fact that you keep showing up... says a lot about your determination'). The counselor never judges the missed interviews and reframes them as a need for backup, not personal failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening capturing both content and emotion, e.g., 'That feeling of being stuck between two things you care deeply about must be exhausting.' Reflections precisely tracked Amina's shift from 'frozen' panic to 'nervous with a plan.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on securing backup childcare to enable interview attendance, with each response advancing toward a concrete action step. Progressed logically from options to a first step (Saturday morning), setback planning, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Equal-partner dynamic throughout; Amina's own ideas (neighbor, barter, written script) formed the substance of the plan while the counselor facilitated rather than directed. The follow-up plan was explicitly co-constructed ('we brainstorm plan B together').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Amina was offered genuine choice ('which of those two feels most worth a closer look') and designed her own plan and backup plan. No advice or pressure was given; agency was consistently attributed to her ('what comes next from your side').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from Amina rather than supplied ('What would it mean to you, if Ms. Delia says yes?'), eliciting rich self-motivators like 'My girls are worth me being brave for ten minutes at a door.' The counselor named and returned her own wisdom ('saying things out loud made them real').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned Amina as the expert on her own life ('You know your life and options better than I ever could'). The neighbor idea, barter arrangement, and Saturday plan all came entirely from her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and amplified, notably the reflection on her confidence rating: 'Once, not if. That's a strong place to stand.' Open questions deepened commitment language, culminating in 'I'm showing up with good news.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about barriers and fear of rejection was validated but not reinforced, with effective reframing ('The fear makes sense... But it also opens the door'). Minor deduction because barrier content was occasionally acknowledged at moderate length before pivoting to action.
