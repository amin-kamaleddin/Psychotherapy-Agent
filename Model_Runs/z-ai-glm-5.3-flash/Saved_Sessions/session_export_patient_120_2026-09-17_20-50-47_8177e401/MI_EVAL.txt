# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, e.g., 'Missing feeling normal doesn't make you ungrateful or a bad mom. It makes you human.' Warmth is genuine and never judgmental, directly contrasting the patient's experience with her doctor.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and emotionally deep, capturing both content and underlying meaning (e.g., 'failing at both,' the secret weight she's carrying). Key insights like the fear of judgment are named with precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, purposeful focus on alcohol abstinence during pregnancy and the social dynamics sustaining use. Every exchange converges on concrete, self-chosen steps with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully co-constructed: every element of the plan (telling Jess, texting Maya, the lock screen) originated from Sofia, with the counselor facilitating rather than directing. The patient explicitly notes the non-lecturing, collaborative feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced ('I'm not going to rush you into announcing anything'), and autonomous motivation is highlighted ('it's because you need to, for your kid'). Sofia leaves with a self-chosen plan and explicitly states 'not because someone's making me.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is systematically drawn from the client — her analysis of the Maya night, her realization about secret-keeping, her coping strategies, and her 'proud' future self. The counselor reinforces rather than supplies motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is repeatedly located within Sofia ('You found your own path here; I didn't hand you that'). Her own contributions substantially shaped the session's direction and final plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and amplified: 'Maybe it's possible' is highlighted, her own words are reflected back ('That's not me talking; that's you'), and commitment language ('I can do that') is elicited and reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently reframed rather than amplified, with brief accepting acknowledgment before pivoting to change. Not a 5 because grief over her former identity receives relatively quick redirection, leaving slightly less room for full emotional processing.
