# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance and affirmed progress, but affirmations were brief and formulaic, lacking deeper validation of Dante's discouragement and repeated failed attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections showed strong reflective listening, but emotional content around discouragement and the all-or-nothing pattern was summarized functionally rather than explored emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on re-establishing physical activity, ending with a fully specified action plan (days, duration, backup plan, cues, reminders, and follow-up) with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A model of equal-partner collaboration: asked permission before advising, co-constructed the plan from Dante's preferences and wording, and incorporated his backup-plan idea and check-in request.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently sought permission and let Dante choose among options, but option menus were counselor-generated and there were fewer explicit statements emphasizing that the change process belongs entirely to him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation from within via open questions, incremental confidence scaling (5 to nearly 8), and a role-play that let Dante discover his own capability, with commitment language clearly originating from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Dante's expertise was central — his script tweak, walk choice, and accountability insight shaped the plan — but the counselor still drove much of the menu generation rather than consistently drawing solutions from Dante first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk: scaling questions moved confidence from 5 to 7.5–8, and the role-play elicited strong commitment and self-efficacy statements ('I'm doing this,' 'I'll actually stick to it').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and effectively reframed (e.g., the Wednesday backup addressing the all-or-nothing pattern), but it was mostly bypassed rather than strategically softened with double-sided reflections.
