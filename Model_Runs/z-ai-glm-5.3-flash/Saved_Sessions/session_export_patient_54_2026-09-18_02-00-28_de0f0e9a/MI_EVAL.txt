# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard with no judgment about drinking during pregnancy, even when the patient expressed skepticism. Genuine affirmations throughout, and the clinician welcomed her admitted defiance ('I came in here ready to argue') without defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that mirrors the patient's exact language ('pressing pause, not giving up,' 'wine for him, mocktail for me') and captures her ambivalence from the first utterance. Fell short of mastery because reflections stayed at the level of skilled summary/affirmation rather than exploring deeper emotions like fear for the baby or the loss of her stress-relief ritual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on alcohol use during pregnancy, progressing purposefully from values to a concrete, client-generated, time-bound plan. The session never wandered despite early defensiveness, and direction felt disciplined without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every turn asks rather than tells, and all plan elements originated from the patient. The session structure was fully co-constructed, with the clinician seeking her evaluation at each decision point.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary calibration to a client resistant to being told what to do — no advice given, ownership consistently reinforced ('your plan, your choice,' 'on your own terms'). Her shift from ready-to-argue to owning a plan demonstrates the approach worked.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Zero prescriptive content; the entire plan and motivation came from the patient's own reasons, ideas, and confidence statements. The clinician used her language as raw material for each subsequent evocative question.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** 'You're the expert on your life' was stated explicitly and enacted throughout, with the clinician deferring to her knowledge of her own life and Marco. The power-sharing successfully disarmed her defensive stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematic evocation using open questions anchored to her values, deepening commitment language from 'maybe' to 'I think I can actually do this' with a concrete plan. Change talk was then reflected and reinforced ('Listen to what you just did').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never debated or argued against sustain talk; instead pivoted toward change while honoring underlying values (e.g., 'holding onto your tradition while protecting your baby'). Sustain talk progressively diminished across the session, indicating effective handling.
