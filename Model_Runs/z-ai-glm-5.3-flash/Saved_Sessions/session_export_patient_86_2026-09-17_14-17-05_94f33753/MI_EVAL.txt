# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental, keeping a guarded, lecture-averse client engaged with explicit autonomy validation. Affirmations were somewhat generic rather than reflecting Marco's specific strengths and history.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate but the session quickly shifted to instruction blocks, leaving his identity attachment to smoking, fatalism, and tentative hope largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking with logical progression to a concrete negotiated step. Direction came via checklist-style prompting, and the plan was repeated verbatim four times without adding momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing in the final plan, which Marco substantially shaped himself. However, the dynamic was largely menu-picking from counselor-generated options rather than co-creation, with no elicitation of Marco's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong explicit autonomy support ('I can listen without pressuring,' no preaching) and acceptance of refusals and slips as resettable. Occasionally crowded the client with prescriptive instructions and nudging check-ins.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited real change talk ('maybe I could push it back,' 'maybe this is the thing that sticks'), but relied on offering solutions rather than drawing out Marco's reasons, values, and hopes. Key evocative opportunities were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco chose the option and start day, showing some genuine power-sharing. The counselor often slid into an expert posture with dense instruction lists, underusing Marco's expertise about his own habit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicited meaningful change talk via the readiness ruler and harm-reduction menu. The evocation was shallow—pivoting quickly to options, and final change talk was never reflected back or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk and redirected fatalistic statements. However, actively invited sustain talk without resolving ambivalence, using neutral restatements rather than double-sided reflections or strategic pivots.
