# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Polite and nonjudgmental, but acceptance falters when Amina discloses fear of consequences at home—no warmth or validation was offered. Also, agreeing to write that the client 'engaged' in exchange for a quick exit shows transactional compliance rather than genuine positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only minimal reflections ('being required to come can feel annoying,' 'I hear you'); the interaction is checklist-like. The pivotal disclosure about arguments at home and fear of her stepdad went unexplored and unacknowledged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No therapeutic focus was maintained; the referral concerns (household conflict, stepdad, safety) were never meaningfully addressed. The session devolved into drafting an administrative note to satisfy the caseworker.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction stayed at a surface service-delivery level; the client even noted 'You're just giving me what I want' with no reframe toward shared goals. There was no joint problem-solving about family conflict or what would be useful to her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frequent affirmations of choice ('on your terms,' 'say skip and I'll stop') are a strength, but autonomy support became problematic when the clinician agreed to omit safety-relevant disclosures without exploring implications, and gave no guidance on confidentiality limits despite a fearful minor.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation and meaning were never elicited—not a single open question about Amina's feelings, family, fears, or hopes. Even her own relational curiosity ('What's the catch here?') was not leveraged to draw out her perspective.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Offering menus of options is superficial choice-giving rather than genuine partnership. The clinician passively followed the client's transactional framing without exploring what Amina wanted or co-creating an agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No effort to evoke change talk; her goals, values, and hopes were never asked about. The client's own curiosity ('Aren't therapists supposed to dig into stuff?') was a missed opening.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician avoided arguing with sustain talk but made no effort to redirect minimization ('normal family stuff, everyone's house is like that') toward reflection or change-oriented exploration. Requests to omit disclosures also went unaddressed.
