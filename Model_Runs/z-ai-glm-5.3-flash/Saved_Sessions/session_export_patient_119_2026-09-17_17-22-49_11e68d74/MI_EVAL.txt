# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and non-judgmental throughout. Patient's explicit thanks for not being made to feel stupid confirms unconditional positive regard was conveyed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening that accurately captured ambivalence and routines. Reflections sometimes stayed at the summary level rather than exploring deeper emotions, e.g., the patient's fear of self-distrust was met with a plan-oriented response.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on medication adherence from opening to closing with no topic drift. Every exchange advanced logically toward a concrete plan and accountability check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Asked permission before advising, offered options rather than prescriptions, and co-constructed the final plan from the patient's own routines and preferences. Patient's statement that 'the plan is mine' confirms genuine collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered genuine choices, which the patient explicitly recognized. Occasionally drove with instructions (if-then framing) and a somewhat fixed check-in time, slightly constraining choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from the patient via rulers, strengths elicitation, and small-step questions. Evocation was strongest around logistics; deeper values and emotional motivations were less explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission, trusted the patient's expertise, and let her contributions substantially shape the plan. The counselor still supplied a fairly structured list of options, nudging rather than fully deferring to her problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used scaled questions, open invitations, and commitment language that generated rich patient change talk with confidence rising from 7 to 8. Change talk was not deeply deepened or reflected, e.g., exploring what 'staying healthy for my family' meant to her.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk and generally pivoted to values and solutions. Sometimes merely restated ambivalence without strategic double-sided reflections linking it to the patient's own change reasons.
