# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard; never judgmental despite Marco's smoking history after surgery and cancer. Marco explicitly confirmed it: 'That's the first time someone hasn't had a go at me.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections that repeatedly capture both sides of Marco's ambivalence ('tired of them having that hold / not ready to say goodbye'). Marco's elaborations confirm the reflections landed and helped him go deeper.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation throughout, with every exchange advancing toward change. Momentum was maintained MI-consistently via structuring questions rather than pushing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan—gradual cutting back, partner's support without nagging, the notebook—was entirely Marco's creation, elicited rather than prescribed. No trace of an expert-driven stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is the therapeutic engine: 'that choice is still yours,' 'you'd do it on your own terms,' with Marco's limits accepted without pressure. His framing ('My way. Nobody else's') shows autonomy was elicited, not imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within rather than argued for—no lecturing or scare tactics. The payoff is Marco's own words: 'Never once for me... that might be the first time it's ever actually been mine.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Marco was explicitly positioned as the expert ('You're the expert on your own life here'), and his answers genuinely shaped the session direction. His 'Nobody's ever asked me that before' validates the power-sharing as genuine.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Targeted questions ('What would need to be different?', 'What's one small step?') generated progressively stronger change talk, culminating in Marco's self-generated commitment to the notebook. Change talk evolved from precontemplation to preparatory language in one session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every sustain statement was met with reflections that validated then pivoted toward change—never argued with or challenged. Marco's language visibly softened from 'I can't see myself changing' to 'the notebook's happening.'
