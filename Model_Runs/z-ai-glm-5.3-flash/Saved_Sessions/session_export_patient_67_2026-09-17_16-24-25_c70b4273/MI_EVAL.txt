# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, non-judgmental, and accepting — even normalizing missed tracking nights and Kenji's heavier Saturday drinking without any conditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Kenji's ambivalence and drinking context, but stayed at content level; deeper emotions like loneliness and fear of failure were not explicitly reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol reduction throughout, weaving diversions back into the plan and progressing logically to a concrete, time-bound tracking commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were co-constructed with permission-seeking and incorporation of Kenji's ideas, but the counselor generated most concrete options (template, reminders, experiments) rather than inviting his strategies first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced choice, accepted Kenji's decision to just record rather than change behavior, and never pressured; Kenji explicitly noted the session felt 'less preachy than expected.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used the importance ruler and open questions to elicit Kenji's own reasons (money, fog, boredom), but evocation was somewhat formulaic and change statements were not consistently deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kenji's preferences shaped the plan and permission was explicitly requested, but the counselor often supplied ready-made options rather than eliciting his own ideas first, limiting full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled use of the importance ruler successfully elicited genuine change talk and a self-generated tracking commitment, but confidence/readiness was never assessed and emerging change talk was not deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally responded to sustain talk with neutral reflections and effective double-sided reflections, but at times mirrored sustain talk ('makes a lot of sense') without strategic redirection or discrepancy.
