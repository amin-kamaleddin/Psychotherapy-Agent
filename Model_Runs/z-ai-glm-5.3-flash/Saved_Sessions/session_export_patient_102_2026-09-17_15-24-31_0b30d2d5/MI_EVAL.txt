# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with consistent affirmations and respect even when Milan dismisses concerns. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and generic rather than tailored to Milan's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, generally accurate reflective listening that tracks Milan's minimization, his worry about parents finding out, and his 'just being smart' framing. Reflections tend to summarize content rather than explore deeper emotional meaning (e.g., anxiety about being caught, feelings about the youth minister's involvement).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and drinking-related safety with no topic drift, progressing to a concrete action plan. Direction narrows prematurely to safety logistics rather than revisiting the drinking behavior itself, which Milan explicitly set aside.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative tone with a co-constructed plan — Milan selected the rideshare option, rewrote the text to Dejan, and set his own pacing. The raw material (tip listicle, pre-drafted texts) often came from the counselor rather than Milan's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: explicit ownership statements ('Whatever you decide, it's your choice'; 'You're in charge'), offering options rather than directives, asking permission before advising, and accepting Milan's self-set limits on commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine attempts to draw out Milan's motivations (likes/worries questions, readiness ruler with follow-up), but change motivation never really emerges. The counselor shifted to supplying information and practical steps rather than persisting in eliciting Milan's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some real power-sharing — Milan's input shaped the plan, and the counselor offered choices ('Want one tweaked to sound more like you?'). Slides into expert mode with a five-item safety list and pre-drafted texts despite Milan's low readiness rating and explicit statement that he only wanted the ride plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocative questions and a readiness ruler elicited modest commitment-adjacent behavior (charger, addresses, texting Dejan). Emerging change talk was never deepened — no elaboration on why the safe-ride idea appealed, and small autonomy statements were not reflected in a way that grew them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing or moralizing and acknowledges sustain talk without reinforcing it. However, the counselor repeatedly restates Milan's minimization rather than using double-sided reflections or strategic pivots, resulting in neutral tolerance rather than active softening.
