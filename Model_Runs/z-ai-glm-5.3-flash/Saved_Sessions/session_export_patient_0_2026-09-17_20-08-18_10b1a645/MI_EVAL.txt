# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated warmth and unconditional positive regard, affirming Adrian's courage in disclosing his shame and secrecy without judgment. Accepted his defensiveness and minimizing without chiding.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, emotionally attuned reflections throughout, including a double-sided opening reflection capturing ambivalence and reflections touching the core of his shame. Mirrored his cautious change talk without exaggerating it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on meth use and built progressively toward a concrete action plan (one alternate weekend, texting sister). Deduction for leaving the reduction goal loosely framed as 'less, not quitting' without clarifying what success would look like over time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving with the client proposing all plan elements himself and the counselor asking for input at every juncture. The client explicitly noted the absence of lecturing made it easier to think.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed change as Adrian's choice, accepted 'not promising anything' without pushing, and framed setbacks as learning opportunities. The client credited the lack of pressure for his openness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Relied on evocative open questions that drew all reasons for change from the client himself—fear of hospitalization, the Sunday crash, family connection. Never supplied reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert ('You know your life and your people better than I do'), with every plan element originating from him. Used permission-style, curiosity-driven questions rather than directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified change talk, selectively reinforcing the client's own proposals and anchoring them to his intrinsic motivations. This produced concrete commitment language ('I'll text my sister tonight... that's the deal I'm making with myself').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively pivoted away from sustain talk using double-sided reflections rather than arguing, and never dwelt on it. Minor deduction for a few reflections briefly restating the client's minimizing language, though each was balanced against his concerns.
