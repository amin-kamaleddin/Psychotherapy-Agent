# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and respect with no judgment, explicitly validating the patient's anti-lecturing preference and affirming her perspective. Slips were framed as normal planning material rather than disapproved.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate perspective-taking, including capturing the emotional nuance that defining success on her own terms felt 'freeing.' Deducted for following reflections quickly with questions, limiting emotional space (e.g., the grandfather reference was not explored).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with progressive movement from values through barrier planning to concrete actions and relapse planning. No topic drift; all tangents tied back to the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine equal-partner problem-solving with an explicit 'Deal' contract and no prescribed plan elements. The counselor acted as a thinking partner, and the patient left with a self-authored plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were consistently placed with the patient, including support for her self-defined success criteria and her decision on what to do first after a slip. No advice was imposed at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All reasons for change emerged from the client—her grandfather's illness, wanting to stay active, boredom triggers, and Emi's accountability—rather than from the counselor. Open questions systematically elicited desire, ability, reasons, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was repeatedly located within the client, and every plan element originated from her, confirmed by her closing remark 'A small one, but mine.' Questions consistently invited her to generate solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation of desire, ability, and commitment talk through open questions about values and small steps, culminating in 'I think I actually want to try it.' Change talk was consistently reinforced and deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not amplified and was folded into affirmations of self-knowledge. Minor deduction for occasionally restating sustain talk in early reflections, though this was strategically justified as accurate empathy.
