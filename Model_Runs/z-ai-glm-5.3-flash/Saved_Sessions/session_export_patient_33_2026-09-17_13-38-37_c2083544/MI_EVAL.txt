# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental harm-reduction stance with no labeling or disapproval, and the client explicitly notes the absence of an expected lecture. However, affirmations are sparse and generic, with limited individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects concrete facts and provides responsive safety advice, but reflective listening is limited. Diego's emotionally significant disclosure about using heroin to escape painful thoughts goes unreflected, with most turns being informational or directive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on harm reduction and concrete safety steps, ending with a specific action plan and follow-up. The focus stays behavioral without connecting the plan to the underlying emotional function of Diego's use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative moments exist in the role-play and the co-created text-message script, but much of the session is counselor-led information delivery. Agenda-setting and problem-solving are not negotiated together; the counselor supplies answers rather than building solutions jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Suggestions are framed as options and Diego's choice not to pursue treatment is explicitly respected, with plans client-selected and self-paced. The dense, prescriptive safety checklists at times control the agenda rather than reinforcing client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor rarely draws out Diego's own motivations or meanings; insights come almost entirely from the client, and no open questions explore his hopes or reasons for change. Meaningful material like his fear, readiness, and self-generated metaphor receives logistics rather than evocative exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-style questions honor Diego's preferences, but expertise flows heavily from counselor to client with the counselor positioned as the safety expert. Diego's own knowledge is incorporated only minimally, making power-sharing lukewarm and erratic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego generates strong change talk spontaneously, but the counselor does little to deliberately evoke or deepen it, missing chances to explore what makes change feel doable. The counselor benefits from an unusually motivated client rather than actively cultivating change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does not reinforce sustain talk and appropriately accepts Diego's ambivalence without arguing. However, key sustain statements like 'heroin shuts it up better than anything' receive no acknowledgment, reframe, or double-sided reflection — ambivalence is sidestepped rather than worked with.
