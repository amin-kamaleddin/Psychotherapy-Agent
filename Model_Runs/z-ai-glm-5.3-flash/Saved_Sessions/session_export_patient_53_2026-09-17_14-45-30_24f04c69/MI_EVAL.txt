# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with validating statements ('That makes sense,' 'That's completely okay') and reframed avoidance gently. However, warmth felt somewhat formulaic and repetitive, lacking deeply individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening, e.g., capturing both Isabella's motivation and fear of letting people down. Reflections sometimes stayed surface-level, missing the emotional history hinted at by 'letting everyone down again.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on community engagement with zero topic drift, advancing steadily from barriers to a concrete, measurable micro-goal ('tonight at 8, just peek') with accountability built in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative — asked before suggesting, offered menus, and built the plan from the client's own ideas. Frequent pre-written wording and option menus tipped some exchanges toward guided/scaffolded rather than fully equal brainstorming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced client ownership with language like 'no commitment,' 'you can change your mind anytime,' and 'just peek, no pressure.' Accepted the client's low-stakes check-in preference and framed the plan as entirely hers to execute or cancel.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited the client's own solutions through open questions about barriers and doable steps, well-matched to her contemplation stage. Deeper personal motivations — why connection matters to her — remained unexplored, leaving the 'why' of change underdeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Isabella as decision-maker, and her contributions genuinely shaped the final plan. The counselor's drafting of texts and structured menus introduced a somewhat expert role that slightly limited full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully moved Isabella from 'I haven't done anything yet' to 'I could try that this week' via scaled options and deepened commitment by having her choose wording and timing. Lacked explicit evocation of her own reasons for change or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk but rarely redirected it — repeated doubts ('I'll probably just forget,' 'I might not do it') were acknowledged without strategic pivots. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and affirming 'that's more than I've done in a while' as evidence of movement.
