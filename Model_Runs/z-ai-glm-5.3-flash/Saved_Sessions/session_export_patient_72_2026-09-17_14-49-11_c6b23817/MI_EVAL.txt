# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with consistent affirmations of Leila's efforts and self-respect. However, praise is somewhat generic and repetitive ('Nice work'), feeling formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture content but stay at the informational level rather than engaging with Leila's deeper emotions about past setbacks and nervousness. Frequent multiple questions per turn reduce listening depth and create an interrogative quality.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary, consistent focus on re-establishing physical activity from the opening question onward, with every exchange advancing toward concrete action without any drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine back-and-forth with Leila shaping the plan (choosing the polite script, adding Sara, reframing failure as information), and a nice co-created role-play. Collaboration tilts counselor-led during the planning phase, where the psychiatrist proposed more solutions than a fully equal partnership would.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicit choices are offered and Leila's right to decline is affirmed, with the plan genuinely hers in substance. Frequent and sometimes unsolicited advice (podcast reward, drafted text, prescribed alarms) slightly undercuts the ownership message.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good scaling questions and elicited success measures, but the session relies heavily on the psychiatrist supplying strategies for a client who stated 'I know exactly what works for me.' Deeper exploration of why past attempts failed and what makes this time different was largely absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Leila's input is sought at several points, but the psychiatrist increasingly adopts the expert role by offering scripts and drafting her text to Sara. Power-sharing becomes uneven in the middle portion despite the client's demonstrated expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited through scaling and first-step questions, though this mostly reflects Leila's already-high baseline motivation. Missed opportunities to deepen commitment language, and the psychiatrist too often supplied plan content rather than drawing solutions from her.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk is handled adequately with prompt pivots to problem-solving and backup plans, and Leila's 'information, not failure' reframe was allowed to emerge naturally. Some loss due to the psychiatrist elaborating barriers at length, which amplified obstacle-focused discussion.
