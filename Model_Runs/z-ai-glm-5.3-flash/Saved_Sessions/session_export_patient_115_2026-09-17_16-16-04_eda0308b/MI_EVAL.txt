# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, warm, nonjudgmental stance, opening with validation and repeatedly affirming Samir's efforts while avoiding the lecture he expected about smoking. A 5 would require deeper individualized expressions of positive regard beyond affirming his practical efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening with double-sided reflections that accurately captured Samir's ambivalence (e.g., hope and worry about the filter). Reflections were often brief and quickly paired with planning questions, missing chances to explore deeper emotional material like the frightening nocturnal symptoms and family fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on asthma management and trigger reduction from the opening question through concrete action planning. Every turn advanced the target—choosing a step, specifying logistics, building backups, tracking, and follow-up—and the session never drifted off-target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was genuinely co-constructed, with menus of options, plans built around Samir's real-world resources (Karim, his wife, shift schedule), and practical artifacts (mechanic slip, tracking chart) developed at his request. Samir's remark that the session was 'more organised than I expected' underscored the shared, non-hierarchical process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced choice with preference questions and accepted Samir's declines on smoking and the mask without pressure. His closing 'I'm not promising anything — but I'm not saying no either' reflects his retained sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation largely from Samir through confidence rulers, preference questions, and letting him articulate his own rationale, including his spontaneous insight that cutting down isn't the same as stopping. Could have evoked more deeply by exploring why change matters to him personally—his family, his wife's scare—rather than moving quickly to logistics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Samir as the expert on his own life, with options drawn from his world and explicit questions like 'What feels better to you?' Samir's choices (filter, wife as tracker, timing of the smoking conversation) substantially shaped the session, reflecting full power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk through confidence rulers ('What would raise your confidence from a 6 to an 8?') and preference questions, eliciting commitment language ('The filter, that I'll commit to this week') and spontaneous insight about smoking. A 5 would require more consistent deepening of Samir's own reasons for change linked to his values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well without amplifying it, reflecting Samir's 'not ready yet' neutrally and offering no-pressure choices rather than arguing. Occasionally restated sustain themes (mask awkwardness) instead of actively pivoting toward change.
