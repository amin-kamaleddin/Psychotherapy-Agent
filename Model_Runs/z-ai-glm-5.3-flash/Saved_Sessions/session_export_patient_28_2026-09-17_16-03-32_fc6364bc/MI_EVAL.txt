# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Dante's fear of failure and normalizing small wins, but warmth is somewhat brisk and affirmations of his strengths are limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective summaries capture Dante's ambivalence and fears, but reflections often stay at the logistical level and miss deeper emotional content, e.g., his disclosure about why he quit last time.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains exceptionally clear focus on re-establishing exercise, with every exchange advancing toward a concrete plan (days, times, obstacles, buddy, tracker) and consistent summarizing recaps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided, with Dante's preferences shaping the plan, but the counselor often arrives with pre-packaged option lists rather than eliciting Dante's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language with permission-seeking, choice-offering, and 'no pressure' framing, though menus subtly steer toward pre-formulated options and pacing is occasionally over-structured.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from Dante through scaling questions and eliciting past successes, producing client-generated commitments, but deeper values and motivations are not explored and a key disclosure goes unexamined.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly asks permission, offers to tweak materials, and lets Dante's contributions shape the plan, but sometimes supplies the menu of options rather than eliciting his ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses importance/confidence scaling and invites Dante's own plan, eliciting clear commitment language, but change talk is not systematically deepened (e.g., no probing of why importance is 6 rather than higher).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids dwelling on barriers and skillfully reframes all-or-nothing thinking ('one walk is progress, not a failure'), though an early 'what's gotten in the way?' question briefly amplifies obstacle talk.
