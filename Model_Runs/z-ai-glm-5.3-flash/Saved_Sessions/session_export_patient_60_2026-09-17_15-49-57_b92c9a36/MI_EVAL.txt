# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates consistent respect and a nonjudgmental tone without labeling Jason or disapproving of his drinking. Affirmations are generic ('got it,' 'makes sense') and miss chances to affirm specific strengths like calling his roommate.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections are accurate but shallow, and reflections largely disappear as the session becomes checklist-style and transactional. Little exploration of the emotional meaning beneath Jason's minimization, such as his anxiety about probation or feeling singled out.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session is focused and organized but on the wrong target—entirely on conduct-meeting logistics rather than the alcohol use the referral calls for. The target behavior is never discussed as a clinical concern in its own right.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaboration exists—options are offered and Jason's edits incorporated—but the counselor supplies drafts, scripts, and model answers with little client input. Functions more like a coach or advisor than an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices and defers to Jason's preferences, including accommodating his wording edits without pressure or coercion. However, autonomy support centers on procedural choices and never affirms Jason's capacity to decide his own path regarding drinking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts are almost entirely didactic—providing sample statements, question lists, and model answers—treating insight as residing with the counselor. Jason's own values and motivations about drinking are essentially never evoked despite clear openings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partnership gestures appear early ('What feels most helpful to start with?'), but the counselor shifts into expert mode, unilaterally drafting the statement and scripting responses. Jason is not explicitly recognized as the expert on his own life or drinking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Makes almost no effort to evoke change talk, missing natural openings like 'I don't even remember much of it' to explore Jason's relationship with alcohol. No exploration of discrepancy or development of desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not amplified and is occasionally neutralized, but it is largely ignored rather than rolled with strategically. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections, e.g., after 'Everyone drinks before 21—it's not like I hurt anyone,' the counselor pivots straight to logistics.
