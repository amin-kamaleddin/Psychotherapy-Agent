# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, thanking Ethan for his honesty and normalizing his reluctance without labeling him. The rapid pivot to warning signs without deeper acknowledgment of Ethan's minimization slightly undercuts unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections early on, but the counselor frequently skips over emotional content to jump into assessment questions and advice lists. Feelings about peer belonging and fear of parental consequences are never explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session consistently focused on the target behavior of weekend alcohol use, never drifting off-topic. However, direction sometimes manifests as rushing to solutions before consolidating why change matters to Ethan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative elements like using Ethan's ideas in the plan, but the counselor repeatedly delivers structured advice lists, shifting toward expert-delivery. Ethan's reaction ('this is a lot of rules') suggests the plan felt more imposed than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks permission before advising, offers menus of options, and respects Ethan's pacing and choices, including deferring the mom conversation. Falls short due to some prescriptive framing and little explicit reinforcement of Ethan's ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation surfaced from Ethan despite, rather than because of, counselor technique. There are virtually no evocative questions, no confidence/importance scaling, and no exploration of the discrepancy between Ethan's drinking and his values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly checks in, asks permission, respects Ethan's choices, and lets his ideas shape the plan. Falls short of 5 because the plan content is largely counselor-generated rather than drawn out of the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ethan generated meaningful change talk ('I hate the mornings after'), but the counselor did not deliberately evoke or deepen it. Change talk emerged mostly from Ethan himself, with the counselor affirming but rarely amplifying it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and sometimes redirects it effectively. However, sustain talk about fun and peer belonging is often met with advice lists rather than strategic softening via double-sided reflections.
