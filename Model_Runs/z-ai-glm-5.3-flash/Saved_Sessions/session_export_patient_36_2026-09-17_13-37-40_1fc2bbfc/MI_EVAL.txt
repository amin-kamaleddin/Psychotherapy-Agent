# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental, supportive tone with affirmations that normalized Elena's experiences (e.g., her urge to jump in 'shows you care'). However, praise was somewhat formulaic ('nice work,' 'solid two-way move') and lacked deeper individualized positive regard for her values work.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered accurate reflections of content, but largely operated as a coach giving feedback rather than reflecting Elena's feelings and meanings. Her statements about wanting 'actual conversations, not just emojis' and awareness of almost hijacking the conversation were not deeply reflected emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained very clear focus on a concrete target (calling her sister), moving efficiently from values naming to alignment rating to a specific action plan, obstacle/if-then plan, role-play, and follow-up. Every exchange advanced this agenda without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction resembled coach-and-trainee rather than equal partners, with the counselor evaluating performance, assigning tasks, and supplying corrections. Although Elena shaped content actively, the counselor retained directional control and rarely negotiated the agenda or invited her ideas about methods.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Offered only a token choice before frequently prescribing exact script wording, precise timing (8:15), reminder setup, and 'quick refinements.' Elena's own phrasing and pacing were overridden in favor of the counselor's optimized version, with little emphasis on her owning the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Few open questions elicited Elena's own reasons, hopes, or confidence; most questions were procedural ('Ready to practice the next line?'). Her rich material about values and self-respect was left unexplored, reflecting an assumption that motivation must be installed via technique rather than drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Assumed substantial expert control by providing scripts ('say exactly: Hey—missed you'), prescriptive refinements, and coach-style performance evaluations. Rather than drawing out Elena's own ideas about what would work, the counselor repeatedly decided what she should practice and how.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena arrived with strong spontaneous change talk ('I feel great,' 'making sure I keep this momentum going'), but the counselor did not deliberately elicit or deepen it. Instead of exploring what makes the momentum matter to her, the counselor pivoted quickly to structuring a task.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena displayed almost no sustain talk, and the counselor never reinforced reasons against change, keeping the interaction oriented toward action and strengths. This score reflects an easy context rather than demonstrated skill, as there was little opportunity to soften sustain talk.
