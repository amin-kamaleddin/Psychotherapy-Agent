# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental tone with no labeling or disapproval, and affirmations like 'That's real progress.' Falls short of 5 because warmth is brief and the emotional weight of the new diagnosis isn't deeply normalized or validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, but many turns bundled multiple questions and advice, leaving little room for reflective listening. Deeper emotional content — fear of bad numbers, feeling of 'failing at' diabetes — received only surface acknowledgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained exceptionally clear focus on beginning blood sugar monitoring, culminating in a fully operationalized plan (Thursday 5:30 fingerstick, backup, kit placement, demo with wife). No topic drift occurred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor asked for input at every decision point and co-constructed logistics and the final plan. Some expert content (ranges, tips) was delivered as information dumps, and multi-question turns limited true two-way dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were consistently framed as Marcus's ('pick a backup,' 'okay to cover that?'), with no pressure or moralizing. Withheld from 5 because rapid-fire questioning constrained deliberation and some solutions were supplied before Marcus could generate his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective use of rulers and values questions elicited strong motivations ('I'd like to be around for a while'), but much of the session relied on counselor-provided checklists rather than drawing out Marcus's own reasons and strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus was positioned as decision-maker and his ideas genuinely shaped the plan (kit by coffee maker, testing on arrival, wife joining, feelings column on checklist). Some turns were prescriptive, offering tips before they were fully requested.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled use of confidence rulers tracking progress (5→6→7) and linking change to Marcus's values. Commitment statements ('I'm ready for this') were not consolidated with a summary of his own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced and was followed by affirmations, but hesitations were met mainly with task-focused responses rather than reflective softening. No double-sided reflections were used, and anxiety was addressed with technique rather than MI-style resolution.
