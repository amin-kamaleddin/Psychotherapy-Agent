# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, accepting Wiremu's self-description without labeling and respecting his remorse. Affirmations were somewhat repetitive and formulaic, lacking fresh, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Wiremu's core motivation (his kids) and named his ambivalence about moving in with family precisely. Some reflections stayed surface-level and missed deeper emotional nuance around shame and fear of rejection from Kirsty.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained exceptional focus on target behaviors (alcohol, violence, family reunification) with every exchange advancing concrete action. No topic drift occurred; each turn built progressively toward commitment, including the safety check about the kids.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was genuinely co-created: the counselor asked permission before drafting, incorporated Wiremu's specific edits, and rewrote the urge script in his own words. Decisions about the wait rule, removing alcohol, and calling mum all came from Wiremu with the counselor facilitating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with repeated framing of choices as Wiremu's ('You're in charge of what we focus on,' 'Want any tweaks?'). Options were frequently presented as pre-packaged menus rather than open-ended exploration, keeping this just below a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicited motivation from Wiremu rather than imposing it, drawing out his own reasons for change ('Think of the kids') and developing them into concrete tools. At times the counselor supplied solutions (letter template, coping plan items) before evoking Wiremu's own ideas first.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Wiremu as the expert, inviting his edits which he used substantively to shape the final letter and script. Some scaffolding was offered as pre-formed structures rather than co-constructed from scratch, and the counselor never explicitly named Wiremu's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked change talk with targeted questions that elicited strong commitment language ('It's about the kids, not my pride') and skillfully deepened commitment through the letter and urge script. Missed opportunities included explicitly exploring confidence/ability ratings or success imagery.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification—briefly validated then redirected toward change. The counselor effectively reframed 'feeling like a kid again' as 'trading independence for safety' and pivoted to a choice question that led Wiremu to his own safer conclusion.
