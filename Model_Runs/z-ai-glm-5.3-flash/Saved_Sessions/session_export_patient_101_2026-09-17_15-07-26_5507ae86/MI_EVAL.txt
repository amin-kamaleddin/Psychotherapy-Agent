# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Mei's fears without labeling or disapproval. Affirmations were somewhat generic and normalization of past slips was limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured Mei's triggers, successes, and fears (e.g., repeating family patterns, risky choices when drinking). Reflections stayed summary-level and cognitive rather than exploring deeper emotional content like shame or anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on the dual targets of alcohol reduction and contraceptive reliability, with every exchange tied to concrete next steps. The session progressed logically from importance to confidence to a specific, actionable plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked permission before drafting texts or role-playing, and Mei's own ideas substantially shaped the plan. However, frequent counselor-supplied bulleted option lists created a partially expert-driven dynamic rather than true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were consistently framed as Mei's ('Which of those would you add?', 'Want these tweaked?'), with no pressure or imposition, and the plan built on what already worked for her. Pre-packaged option menus subtly steered her and ownership of setbacks wasn't explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew motivation from within via scaling, asking what helped before, and eliciting Mei's own 'why' (the alcohol–contraception link). Some evocation opportunities were passed over in favor of supplying practical tips, and Mei's reasons were never explored in depth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asking for Mei's input showed respect for her expertise, and her ideas shaped the plan. Yet the middle-to-late session became option-list-driven, with the counselor supplying most strategies and shifting toward an expert prescriber stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of confidence scaling (4→7/8) and targeted questions generated concrete commitment language ('I'll text her tonight,' 'I'll stick to two') and Mei's own motivation statements. Change talk could have been deepened further rather than quickly shifting to planning options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mei expressed sustain talk ('weekends are hard,' 'I've said that before and broken it'), which the counselor did not reinforce but also did not actively soften. Responses moved straight to planning rather than using double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
