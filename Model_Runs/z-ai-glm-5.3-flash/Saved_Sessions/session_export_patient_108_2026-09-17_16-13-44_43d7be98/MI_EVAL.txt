# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirmations of Oliver's kindness and progress. Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, with little explicit validation of deeper emotional struggles like the abandonment theme or grief over reclaiming solitude.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opening complex reflection was accurate, but subsequent reflections stayed at content level rather than emotional depth. The most vulnerable disclosures (the nan/abandonment history, 'I needed permission') were never reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Laser-focused on the target behavior from first turn to last with no topic drift. Every exchange built momentum toward concrete action, from readiness assessment through scripting, rehearsal, and a locked-in plan (tomorrow at 6, three breaths).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session was genuinely joint work — checked in at every step, negotiated role-play conditions Oliver set, and followed his requests. However, the counselor dominated technical content, favoring counselor-drafted scripts over Oliver's own phrasing ('my job's done').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices, asked permission, and accepted Oliver's preferences without resistance, letting him retain ownership of the plan and timing. Fell short because substantial content (scripts, rebuttals, breathing technique) was supplied with little reinforcement of Oliver's own authority.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied on providing solutions rather than drawing motivation from within; beyond initial scaling questions, the counselor prescribed scripts and tips. Oliver's rich self-generated material ('my job is done,' 'I needed permission') went unexplored — he essentially evoked himself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered menu options and asked permission, supporting some power-sharing. But the counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role with drafted scripts and numbered advice lists rather than co-constructing strategies from Oliver's own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions effectively elicited confidence talk tracking 6→9, and role-play produced strong commitment language ('No. There. I said it.'). But change talk was captured, not cultivated — powerful statements like 'My job's done' received no reflection or evocative follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept focus on change without reinforcing sustain talk, briefly acknowledging fears ('sounding cold') before pivoting to action. Lacked strategic pivots using Oliver's own change language when guilt resurfaced, sometimes moving past ambivalence rather than resolving it.
