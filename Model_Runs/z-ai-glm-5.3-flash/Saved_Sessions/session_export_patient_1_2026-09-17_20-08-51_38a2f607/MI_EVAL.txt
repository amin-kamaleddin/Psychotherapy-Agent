# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard despite the patient's defensiveness, including affirmations of his practicality and strength. The patient's 'Thanks for not just lecturing me' confirms he experienced acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening throughout, including precise double-sided reflections. Every reflection landed — the patient repeatedly built on them rather than correcting them.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on tobacco use and dental findings while staying patient-centered, moving steadily from exam findings to a concrete plan with a timeline. Final exchanges consolidated the plan without derailing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine open questions and treating the patient's counter-explanations (dust, grinding) as legitimate leads. The patient explicitly experienced equal footing, contrasting it with prior lectures.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed the patient's ownership of the change process with no pressure or coercion. Repeatedly tied outcomes to the patient's own agency and noted he raised the parenting motivation himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation entirely from the patient, most powerfully through the fatherhood question that elicited his deepest values. Amplified self-generated motivation through to a concrete commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the patient as the expert ('You're the expert on your life'), and the plan was entirely patient-generated. Agenda built around the patient's own leads rather than imposed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk, including highlighting self-motivational statements and commitment language. The fatherhood question produced rich values-based change talk and a concrete commitment (Monday call, written questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk and pivoted toward change, though occasionally restated sustain talk himself (e.g., 'small potatoes'), slightly re-evoking the argument. Minor cost given the double-sided framing.
