# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validating Jonathan's overwhelm and honoring his self-defined success criteria. Warmth occasionally felt formulaic, and explicit affirmations of his strengths were limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate, capturing Jonathan's ambivalence, fatigue, and the 'one treat' meaning of soda. However, the counselor did not deepen exploration of the emotional weight beneath his dismissiveness, such as fear of failure or feeling controlled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus throughout — the session moved from vague overwhelm to a concrete, dated, observable experiment (half/half bottles, Monday start, fridge-first strategy, one-week follow-up). Every turn advanced the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor framed the work as joint and incorporated Jonathan's ideas (half/half mix, fridge rule), but generated most content himself, creating a menu-selection pattern rather than true co-creation. Asking for the client's own ideas before offering suggestions would improve collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was repeatedly emphasized ('Which feels more helpful right now?', accepting rejected suggestions), including the option to simply sit with feelings. Early rapid option-offering slightly pressured action when Jonathan said he wasn't looking for steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation remained largely compliance-driven; the counselor never elicited Jonathan's own reasons for managing his diabetes or what benefits mattered to him. The confidence ruler was a genuine evocative technique, but much of the drive came from the counselor's plan design.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Input was invited and some client contributions shaped the plan, but the counselor mostly supplied all options (drink swap, pre-mixing, fridge strategy, fallback) rather than drawing solutions from Jonathan, keeping the role prescriptive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked, moving Jonathan from pure sustain talk to commitment language ('I'll grab some bottles this weekend'), aided by a well-executed confidence ruler. Jonathan's own reasons for change were never explicitly elicited, leaving motivation partly compliance-based.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged and redirected without amplification, and a shame-free lapse plan prevented resistance. Some 'pointless' remarks were met with quick problem-solving rather than strategic reflection, leaving ambivalence unresolved at session end.
