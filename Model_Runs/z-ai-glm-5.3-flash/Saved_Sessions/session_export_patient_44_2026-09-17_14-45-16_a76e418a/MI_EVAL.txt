# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance with effective normalization of Erik's struggles (e.g., 'totally normal that candy wins') and affirmations of his progress. Affirmations occasionally felt formulaic and tied to outcomes rather than unconditional regard for the client regardless of progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of content and barriers were present ('Work and feeling out of place at the gym make sense as barriers'), but the counselor rarely reflected the emotional depth beneath Erik's words, such as his shame about self-comparison and self-critical patterns. Reflections tended to summarize facts and plans rather than feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on gym consistency and diet, progressively moving from barriers to concrete, actionable plans (6:15 alarm, 20-minute backup, drawer-clearing reminder, vending Plan B). Direction was achieved largely through rapid solution-firing, with little summarizing or checking whether the focus Erik most valued was being addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The tone was consistently that of two equals problem-solving together, with Erik's wife's involvement, reward ideas, and Plan B contingencies incorporated into the plan. The counselor occasionally took the lead in generating solutions via option menus rather than letting Erik's contributions fully shape the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently supported through questions like 'Which of those feels most doable?' and asking what kind of follow-up Erik wanted. However, prescriptive option lists slightly pressured toward counselor-generated solutions, with little explicit affirmation that setbacks and choices belong entirely to Erik.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence scaling and future-oriented questions effectively elicited client-owned motivation, evidenced by Erik's strong closing change talk ('I actually feel ready for this week. That's new.'). Much of the session centered on plan generation rather than evoking Erik's deeper reasons and values, leaving the emotional core unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positioned Erik as the expert, with nearly every plan element (gym bag, alarm, $5 reward, shirt reward) originating from him. Reliance on menus of pre-generated solutions reduced the degree to which Erik's own ideas generated the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence scaling and future-oriented questions elicited meaningful change talk from Erik, including spontaneous insights like 'that sundae would be two hours on the treadmill.' These moments received only brief acknowledgment rather than evocative follow-up, and opportunities to elaborate desire, ability, and reasons were underused.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not amplify sustain talk and occasionally reframed effectively ('Planning for snoozes and vending urges shows you know your patterns — that's a strength, not a failure'). Erik's 'why bother?' discouragement and track-record doubts received no direct double-sided reflection, so sustain talk was tolerated rather than strategically engaged.
