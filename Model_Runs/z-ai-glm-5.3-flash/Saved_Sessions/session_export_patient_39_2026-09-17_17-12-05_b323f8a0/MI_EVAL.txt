# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with warm affirmations ('Going once was a brave step') and validating non-completion as 'useful information, not failure.' Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and focused on the plan rather than Elias as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects core ambivalence and fear of failure. Reflections often repeat surface content verbatim rather than deepening emotional nuance around loneliness or shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on physical activity with a progressive, concrete plan (Tuesday, 10:00, one 60-second song, backup moves, follow-up). Plan restatements loop without new momentum, and the medical-adherence concern is never addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructs the plan, incorporating Elias's adjustments to timing, duration, and backup strategies. Counselor tends to present pre-packaged option menus rather than jointly generating ideas, giving a slightly didactic quality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces ownership via menu-based questions, explicit permission to stop, and framing lapses as information rather than failure. No pressure or persuasion; Elias independently strengthened his commitment and raised his own confidence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from Elias using open questions and a confidence ruler, eliciting meaningful values talk about connection. Evocation stays focused on concrete plan logistics and misses opportunities to deepen exploration of dignity, identity, and hope.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Elias as decision-maker, and his contributions genuinely shape the plan (chair-music option, 10:00 time, shoulder-roll backup). Counselor often supplies the option menus and framing rather than eliciting Elias's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes commitment via scaled questions, a confidence ruler, and success-oriented prompts, producing strong change talk ('Tuesday, ten o'clock. I'll keep my end of it.'). Evocation is plan-focused and structured rather than exploring deeper values and hope.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Briefly acknowledges sustain talk about fear of failure and pivots toward lower-stakes options without reinforcing hopelessness. Redirects are sometimes quick, jumping to solutions before fully honoring the ambivalence.
