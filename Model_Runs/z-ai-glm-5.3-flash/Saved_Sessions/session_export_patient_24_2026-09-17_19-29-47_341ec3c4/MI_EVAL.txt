# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with frequent affirmations and normalization of Daniel's skepticism. However, affirmations are somewhat generic and athlete-flavored, occasionally verging on flattery rather than grounded, individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including accurate complex reflections on the tension between drinking and running and the experiment reframe. Empathy is selective, though — the emotionally loaded wind-down ritual and 9pm loneliness worry received practical responses rather than deeper emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on reducing drinking with a coherent arc from discrepancy to commitment and concrete plan. Direction was somewhat indirect at times, relying on Daniel's spontaneous momentum rather than strategic steering or early summarization of the discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership: the clinician solicited Daniel's ideas first, co-constructed the experiment and check-in plan, and treated his training knowledge as authoritative. Daniel's own comment — 'this felt like my idea by the end' — confirms the mutual exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with conditional framing ('if you decided'), explicit choice about start date, and genuine options for the check-in. No advice, persuasion, or pressure anywhere; even the plan was framed as Daniel's own experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key motivator — the plateau, sunk-cost comparison, curiosity, 'I'd rather know' — came from Daniel himself and was named and amplified as self-generated. The session moved from contemplation to full commitment language and a client-authored plan, the hallmark of high-level evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed that expertise resides in the client, with the entire change plan originating from Daniel and ownership explicitly attributed back to him. Exemplary power-sharing confirmed by the client's own reflection.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout domain: open evocative questions elicited a cascade of change talk, moving Daniel from 'I could probably skip the beers' to 'I want to try it' and full commitment. Change talk was reinforced with values-tied affirmations and consolidated through a collaborative plan and check-in.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well — reflected neutrally, reframed as curiosity, and never argued with or amplified. Fell short of 5 due to minimal use of deliberate double-sided reflections and sidestepping the emotionally loaded wind-down sustain talk rather than addressing it head-on.
