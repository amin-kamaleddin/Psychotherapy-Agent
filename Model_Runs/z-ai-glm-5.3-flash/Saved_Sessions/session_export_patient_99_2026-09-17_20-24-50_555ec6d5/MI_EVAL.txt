# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains consistent, unconditional positive regard with zero judgment or moralizing, including when Matteo jokes about drinking and driving. Affirmations are specific, genuine, and individualized rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including a skilled double-sided reflection and high-level summaries of meaning. Deduction: Matteo's vulnerable first-ever admission that his fall was alcohol-related received no explicit empathic acknowledgment, a missed opportunity to deepen the bond.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, purposeful focus on alcohol use throughout, with a visible therapeutic arc from ambivalence exploration to concrete commitment. The clinician guides without dragging, and even the closing question keeps momentum toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is a genuine exchange between equals, with the plan co-constructed iteratively and framed as joint work. No expert-driven pronouncements or prescribed homework—the tone is two people thinking together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly and repeatedly reinforced ('you're the one steering'), with the client defining his own limits, reasons, and plan without pressure toward any particular target. Matteo's closing comment that it didn't feel like a lecture is direct evidence of perceived autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every motivation and solution came from Matteo himself—drawn out through strategic open questions rather than put in by the clinician. The session reached evocation at its deepest level, with Matteo voicing things he hadn't acknowledged to himself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats Matteo as the expert on his own life, and every substantive element of the plan originated from the client. The clinician's contributions are exclusively questions, reflections, and affirmations with no unsolicited advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's standout strength—systematic evocation via discrepancy questions, values linkage, past-success evocation, and concrete planning. Matteo's change talk evolved from minimization to commitment-level language within a single session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk and minimization are handled with neutral and double-sided reflections rather than argument, and cleverly converted into discrepancy. Deduction: the psychiatrist re-quoted Matteo's problem talk verbatim at one point, a risky amplification even though it happened to work.
