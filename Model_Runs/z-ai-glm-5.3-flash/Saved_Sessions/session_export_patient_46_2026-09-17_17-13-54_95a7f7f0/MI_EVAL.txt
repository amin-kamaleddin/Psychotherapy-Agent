# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with a defensive adolescent, affirming Ethan ('That makes sense,' 'That sounds solid') and never moralizing about underage drinking despite multiple opportunities. Warmth was present but somewhat formulaic, with few individualized affirmations of Ethan's own strengths (e.g., loyalty to Diego, self-awareness about his excuses).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and captured ambivalence well at a summary level, but were sparse and lacked turn-by-turn depth. Emotionally rich material (fear of being mocked as the 'designated babysitter,' 'the comments... that's what kills me,' parental phone confiscation) went largely unexplored, missing chances to reflect the social anxiety underlying his drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained crisp focus on the target behavior throughout, from assessment through rulers to a concrete, rehearsed weekend plan (two-drink limit, no shots, exit signal, sober ride, pre-drafted texts). Each turn advanced toward change, safety issues were addressed within focus, and the session ended with a follow-up plan anchored to Ethan's stated goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was genuinely two-sided: Ethan drafted casual texts, selected his own refusal lines, designed the Diego emoji signal, and edited during role-play rehearsal. Collaboration fell slightly short of full because the counselor frequently provided pre-built option menus (six refusal lines, safety tip lists) rather than co-creating each element from scratch.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly and repeatedly reinforced ('You're the one in charge of any decisions,' 'pick what feels normal,' 'Which plan do you want to lock in now'), with no pressure toward abstinence and Ethan setting his own moderate-reduction goal, ride plan, and wording. Ethan's growing ownership ('more of a plan than I usually have') reflects highly effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Ethan rather than lecturing — rulers elicited his own reasons ('tired of getting in trouble') and he generated his own solutions, with the final follow-up question letting him name the payoff himself. Deducted because evocation leaned on structured tools and suggestions rather than deepening spontaneously offered motivations such as his fear of peer ridicule.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The opening positioned the client as expert ('You're the one in charge of any decisions'), and power-sharing was genuine, with Ethan choosing strategies, lines, and ride plans that all originated from him. The counselor sometimes supplied menus of options rather than trusting Ethan to generate his own first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong structured evocation using importance and confidence rulers moved Ethan from ~4-5 importance/5-6 confidence to 7-8 confidence and produced desire ('tired of getting in trouble'), ability, and commitment language ('I'm saving these in my phone'). Deducted because some change talk was elicited via leading multiple-choice suggestions rather than open evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections kept balance, but the counselor twice directly asked 'What do you like about it?' deliberately amplifying sustain talk, and minimizations ('it's not a big deal') received little affirming double-sided pivoting. Ambivalence was honored but not strategically resolved.
