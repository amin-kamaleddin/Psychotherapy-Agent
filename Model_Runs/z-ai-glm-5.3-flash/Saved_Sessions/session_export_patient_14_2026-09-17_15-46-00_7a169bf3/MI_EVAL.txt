# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with an initially hostile client, validating his frustration and affirming his straight approach. Warmth was somewhat formulaic, with few individualized affirmations of the client's strengths beyond praising the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, accurately capturing the client's core concern that Thursday stress is the real test. Missed opportunities to deepen into the emotional meaning of health decline for a self-sufficient, competent man.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on dietary change throughout a potentially chaotic session, redirecting drift back to the actionable plan. Ended with a fully specified, behaviorally concrete commitment including items, if-then rule, prep timing, and a scheduled follow-up call.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructed the plan, using the client's expertise on organization, stress patterns, and scheduling preferences. Retained structural control (menu options, confidence scale) without negotiating the agenda at the outset, and some suggestions were counselor-initiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematically reinforced the client's ownership of every decision, offering choices rather than prescriptions and accepting rejections without pressure. The change plan was fully authored by the client, including honoring his request to focus on bad days rather than guilt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence scale and follow-up questions drew out honest self-assessment and commitment, but evocation remained superficial. Never explored the client's own reasons for change in depth—breathlessness and blood pressure were reflected briefly rather than developed through values or importance exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the client as the decision-maker, with his contributions visibly shaping the plan, including the follow-up focus on bad days. Structured menu options, while efficient, occasionally constrained space for his own ideas to emerge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk through the confidence question and commitment-focused questions, drawing out the client's own strategies and 'I'll give it an honest go.' Change talk was elicited through structured questions rather than deepened through reflective amplification of why getting hold of the weight matters.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided amplifying barriers, respecting sustain talk without reinforcing it—pivoting to alternatives when walking and breathing exercises were rejected. Redirections relied on offering options rather than reframing sustain talk itself, such as double-sided reflections linking 'no time' with his own motivation.
