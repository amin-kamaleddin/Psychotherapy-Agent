# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard: validated 'I'm not promising anything' as 'learned wisdom' and affirmed individualized concerns like losing 'the funny one.' Warm, non-judgmental throughout, even during humor-deflection.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Deep, accurate reflections such as 'the pills let you down first' prompted the client to say 'nobody's really put it like that before,' and later reflections were directly confirmed by the client ('You're getting it, I think'). Empathy evolved from reflective to emotionally attuned.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally clear focus moving from resistance to a concrete step (trying one adjusted pill) via strategic prioritizing questions. Minor deduction for early circling (football exchange) and occasional lengthy summaries that slowed momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — 'figure this out together, with you leading the way' — with no advice given until the client explicitly asked, and even then the counselor returned to the client's own words. The target and plan were co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy explicitly and repeatedly reinforced ('that's completely okay here,' 'at your pace,' honoring 'don't make a big deal of it'). The final plan was framed entirely as the client's terms, with no pressure or over-celebration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within: questions like 'what did feeling human actually look like?' elicited the client's own deepest motivations (evenings with nephews) and his own solution (one pill, trial run, his terms). His shift to 'I'd try it' was self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was consistently positioned as the expert ('you know things I don't') and chose the agenda ('the feeling human part'). His contributions substantially shaped the session's direction, and the final plan was entirely his construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked ('what would have to be different for this to feel like your call?') and developed from desire ('I want those evenings back') into commitment ('Trial run. My terms'). Consistently reflected and never steamrolled.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged, validated, and pivoted via double-sided reflections and reframes ('that's a bind, not stubbornness'). When the client softened ('maybe it's a little broken'), the counselor let the shift develop without pouncing.
