# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with affirmations like 'good resource' and 'nice work' and no criticism even when Kevin minimized his drinking. Acceptance remained somewhat formulaic and brief, lacking explicit normalization of ambivalence or unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections captured Kevin's perspective (e.g., juggling being 'the funny one' with avoiding fallout), but reflections were sparse relative to questions, with several stacked-question turns and minimal reflective listening. Emotional nuances like his embarrassment about the injury were never explicitly validated, keeping empathy at a surface level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, consistent focus on reducing risky drinking from start to finish, with every exchange advancing a coherent arc from importance/confidence assessment to a concrete plan for Friday. There was no topic drift, and each turn tied back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was largely co-constructed from Kevin's preferences (Dave, the funny text, the 'doctor's orders' line), and the psychiatrist asked permission before suggesting. However, the balance sometimes tipped toward the counselor generating solution menus, with Kevin mostly selecting rather than co-generating ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was regularly affirmed through permission-seeking and choice-based questions, and Kevin's 'no promises' framing was honored without pushing for commitment. Frequent multi-option lists and rapid-fire questions occasionally pressured quick choices, and Kevin's ownership was not explicitly affirmed at key moments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and prompts about past success drew out some of Kevin's own reasons and rising confidence (5 to 6.5), but much of the session's content came from the counselor's suggestions rather than the client. Deeper motives—drinking's role in his identity and the emotional weight of the injury—were never actively evoked or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kevin was consistently positioned as the decision-maker, with options offered as a menu and his choices directly shaping the entire plan. Suggestions occasionally arrived in dense packaged lists, tilting slightly toward advice-giving rather than fully drawing out the client's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was consistently elicited and visibly strengthened across the session, from 'maybe worth a shot' to 'six and a half now, maybe' and 'If it works, maybe I do it again,' supported by planning questions. However, several change statements were met with new questions rather than affirmation or deepening.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I still want to have fun,' 'Most nights nothing bad happens,' 'No promises') was never reinforced, which is a plus, but it was also largely skipped over rather than strategically softened. No double-sided reflections or reframing were used to roll with Kevin's ambivalence, and some sustain talk was bypassed in the move to planning.
