# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental opening with affirmations of Eric's strengths (past quits, family motivation). Affirmations are somewhat practical rather than deeply personal, and skepticism isn't explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that captures Eric's minimizing stance and frustration, including his skepticism. Reflections are sometimes cut short by stacked questions, limiting deeper exploration of emotionally significant material.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, consistent focus on smoking, progressing from rulers and triggers to a concrete delay plan, if-then statements, and a scheduled Wednesday 5 pm follow-up. Each exchange builds toward action without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuinely joint enterprise: the counselor checks before planning, lets Eric choose start day, strategies, and follow-up timing, and incorporates Eric's own solutions. Eric's ideas visibly shape the plan, which he acknowledges.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice offered throughout (options vs. pros/cons, picking strategies), with hesitations accepted without pressure and slips framed as learning moments. Falls short of a 5 due to limited explicit affirmation that the decision is entirely Eric's own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from Eric via rulers and open questions, yielding personal reasons (family, father's early death, windedness) and his own coping ideas—no lecturing. Questions are sometimes bundled rapid-fire, and deeper ambivalence could have been developed more fully.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently placed with Eric: he selects the delay strategy, generates his own slip recovery approach, and crafts his own if-then statements. Slight deduction for fairly dense prescriptive option menus rather than fully co-constructed elements.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful ruler questions ('what would move a 3 to a 5 or 7?') elicited family concerns, windedness, and his father's early death, leading to commitment-adjacent language ('I could try the delay thing this week'). Could have deepened exploration of his stated reasons before moving to planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without amplification and pivots toward change, with his quit history reframed as a strength. Some summaries restate sustain talk without a change-side counterbalance, slightly diluting the pivot.
