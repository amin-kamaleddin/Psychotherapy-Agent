# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently shows unconditional positive regard—refusing to argue about numbers, affirming Evelyn's pub life, and promising 'no lectures.' No labeling, disapproval, or condescension appears anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including the double-sided reflection and mirroring Evelyn's 'insurance' metaphor. Minor listening slips—saying 'two dry weeknights' and re-asking an already-answered question—prevent a perfect score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on alcohol reduction anchored to Evelyn's values, progressing to a concrete plan. The counselor drifted from the client's pace by proposing two dry nights when Evelyn said one.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative, with the final plan built entirely from Evelyn's own ideas. The 'two dry weeknights' suggestion and rejected mid-week check-in reveal occasional acceleration beyond the client's stated pace.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support—repeatedly affirming 'your plan, your terms' and accepting Evelyn's rejection of the mid-week call without protest. Evelyn's self-assertions confirm the counselor successfully created space for her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within—linking drinking to Evelyn's independence value elicited unprompted change talk about the fall. Her reasons (home, stairs, avoiding dependence) are all voiced by her, not the counselor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions Evelyn as the expert ('You know your life better than anyone'), and the entire plan consists of her own ideas. Genuine deferral to her judgment about the counselor's role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk by linking Evelyn's valued independence to the drinking, producing her own admissions and a client-generated plan. Rich commitment language ('I guess I can cut down some') emerges entirely in Evelyn's words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids debate and uses double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while amplifying change talk. Reflections occasionally repeat Evelyn's minimization ('not promising anything beyond that') rather than pivoting.
