# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing imperfect effort ('10-minute backups absolutely count; they're progress, not failure'). However, affirmations were somewhat generic rather than deeply personalized reflections of Aisha's character strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor reflected Aisha's emotional experience accurately, including the painful mirror feeling and her core value of feeling comfortable again. Reflections were occasionally brief and task-focused, missing deeper exploration of the emotional pain behind 'feeling gross'.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on initiating exercise, moving efficiently to a concrete, scheduled plan with backups, supports, and a follow-up check-in. Every turn advanced the goal, closing with actionable steps and accountability.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely collaborative, with the plan co-constructed from Aisha's own choices and even edited in her own voice. Decisions were consistently made with her, not for her, exemplifying equal-partner problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offered choices and permission-based questions rather than directives. Some urgency nudges ('send the text now') slightly tipped toward directing behavior, and explicit ownership statements could be stronger.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor drew motivation from within by asking what mattered to Aisha, what helped her before, and what would feel like a meaningful win. Evocation leaned practical/planning-oriented, with fewer questions exploring deeper values beneath her desire to exercise.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently treated Aisha as the expert, asking what cues and supports would work for her and incorporating her edits to the sister text. Some moments leaned toward the counselor generating the plan structure rather than Aisha's ideas shaping it from the start.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor actively evoked and reinforced change talk through the confidence ruler (7 to 8) and weaving Aisha's own words ('feel like me again') back into the plan. Limited use of deeper evocative questions about her 'why now' or values left room for amplifying commitment language further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor never amplified barriers or reasons against change, consistently redirecting Aisha's mentions of tiredness and past giving up toward solutions and self-efficacy. No reinforcement of sustain talk occurred anywhere in the session.
