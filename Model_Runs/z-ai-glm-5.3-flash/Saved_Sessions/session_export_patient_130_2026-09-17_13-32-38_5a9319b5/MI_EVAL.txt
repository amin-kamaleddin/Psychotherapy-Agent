# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with respectful acknowledgment of Viktor's honesty. However, warmth often felt formulaic and was followed by question batteries rather than genuine affirmations of his strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections captured his frustration, but most turns bypassed emotional content for checklists and advice. Vulnerable moments, like hating the cold silent Sunday mornings, were met with practical scripts rather than emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed generally relevant but drifted across four domains without consolidating focus on the target behavior. The counselor followed Viktor's redirection without gently steering back to developing discrepancy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration existed through offering choices and tailoring language to Viktor's words, but the rhythm was counselor-proposes/patient-accepts. Long prescriptive blocks dominated over genuine joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choice was offered ('which feels easiest to commit to?'), but advice was heavily prescriptive and delivered before client interest emerged. Repeated procedural safety checks and lack of explicit affirmation of Viktor's ownership of change weakened autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions were predominantly closed and information-gathering rather than evocative of Viktor's values or reasons for change. His genuine value statement about cold Sunday mornings surfaced spontaneously and was met with a practical script rather than exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Viktor framed the counselor as expert, and the counselor largely accepted that frame with expert-driven question batteries. Occasional collaborative moments and tailored language existed, but client contributions shaped the session only intermittently.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerged spontaneously from Viktor rather than through skillful evocation. Open questions designed to elicit motivation were absent, and his key self-motivational statements went unreflected or unamplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly validated and echoed sustain talk ('doesn't mean you've got a problem'), actively reinforcing minimization. The core discrepancy between cocaine use and Viktor's valued 'fewer fights' was never named or developed.
