# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, validating the patient's skepticism and honoring his desire to define what a worthwhile follow-up visit looks like. Acceptance was affirmed even when the patient pushed back or minimized concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong, capturing both the patient's ambivalence and his values (the quiet half hour, being a 'numbers guy'). Empathy was generally accurate, though a few reflections leaned more toward reframing than pure understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintained clear direction toward exploring health behavior change while following the patient's lead on priorities. The focus moved naturally from salt to drinking to relapse planning based on the patient's own cues.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently framed the plan as co-created, asking for the patient's input at every step rather than prescribing. The closing exchange explicitly established a mutual 'deal' with commitments from both sides.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's autonomy was actively protected: 'I'd rather help you shape it around what works for you than hand you a rule,' and credit for the plan was consistently returned to the patient. His ownership of the trial was reinforced at closure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was evoked through open questions that drew out the patient's own motivations, values, and solutions, such as the decompression ritual and the weekend/non-weekend distinction. The plan emerged entirely from the patient's own words.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist framed the relationship as a partnership throughout, including positioning the wife as a partner rather than an audience and agreeing to review numbers together. The 'fair warning taken' exchange reinforced egalitarian collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully cultivated and reflected ('maybe a couple fewer on weekdays wouldn't be the end of the world,' 'a weeknight trial I could actually do'). Strategic questions helped the patient articulate concrete, self-generated commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (father drank and lived to 80, 'the drinking, I honestly don't think it's a big deal') was met with acceptance and curiosity rather than confrontation. Doublesided reflections softened resistance and invited the patient to voice his own reasons for change.
