# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a neutral, nonjudgmental tone with occasional affirmations like 'That was a solid win,' but warmth is minimal and transactional. Guilt and ambivalence are treated as problems to solve rather than feelings to accept.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is nearly absent; emotionally rich disclosures about loneliness, guilt, and self-criticism are met with scripts and bullet-pointed advice rather than reflections. No complex reflections or validation of the difficulty of the role-play.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Crystal-clear focus on the target behavior of setting a firm move-out deadline throughout the session. Every exchange ties back to scripting, rehearsing, and planning concrete steps with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is predominantly expert-driven, resembling coaching with advice, scripts, and structured steps delivered before soliciting Oliver's ideas. His expertise about his friend and his own patterns is never explicitly drawn upon.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choice is offered (role-play or coach, which anchor line to use) and Oliver's preference for in-person talk steers the session. However, the overall stance is highly prescriptive, supplying exact dates, scripts, and consequences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Provides ready-made reasons and solutions rather than eliciting Oliver's own motivations. Virtually no open questions inviting him to articulate why change matters, and all substantive motivation comes from Oliver unprompted.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Assumes the expert role for most of the session, supplying scripts, dates, and step-by-step plans. Oliver's valuable contributions—his self-authored message and his insight about vague promises—are rarely reflected or built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Oliver spontaneously generates substantial change talk ('I have to be more assertive,' 'my job is done') but the psychiatrist does almost nothing to reinforce or deepen it. Repeated opportunities to strengthen his motivation language are missed in favor of drafting messages.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Never acknowledges Oliver's sustain talk about comfort in companionship, guilt, or fear of hurting his friend, rushing past ambivalence toward action plans. This avoids amplifying it but leaves it unaddressed beneath the surface.
