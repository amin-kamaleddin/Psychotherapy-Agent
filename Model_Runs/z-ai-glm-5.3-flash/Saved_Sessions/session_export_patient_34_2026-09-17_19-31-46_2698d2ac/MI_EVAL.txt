# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone even as Dylan deflected blame and minimized the referral, conveying genuine regard. Held at 4 because warmth felt somewhat strategic rather than deeply individualized, with little explicit personal affirmation beyond the client's usefulness in problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Complex reflective listening was a clear strength, accurately capturing both content and feeling (e.g., the 'punishment that doesn't fit' reflection). Reflections occasionally stayed at the level of reframing without exploring the emotional undercurrents beneath Dylan's defensiveness, which would be expected for a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed anchored to the referral and alcohol-related risk throughout, ending with a concrete behavioral plan. One detour mirroring Dylan's dismissiveness and some early circling around fairness kept this from a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly framed the work as joint and asked genuinely open questions, with the final plan reflecting equal partnership. Occasional slipping into echoing the client rather than co-constructing, especially during the sustain-talk lapse, kept this at 4.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Dylan's ownership ('You know your life and your priorities best'; 'That's your insight, not mine') with no coercive or controlling language, and the plan was explicitly the client's own. Meets the criterion for a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, well-timed evocation produced genuine self-persuasion ('That's kind of annoying to admit'; 'It's just smarter'). It sometimes served practical harm-reduction framing rather than exploring deeper values or discrepancy between Dylan's self-image and the incident.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent power-sharing, with expertise explicitly located in Dylan and client-generated insight genuinely affirmed. The session's practical plan came entirely from Dylan, meeting the criterion for a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The strongest technical area — the 'Jake' comparison question skillfully converted resistance into change talk and deepened it into concrete commitment language. A 5 would require more consistent reinforcement of every change-talk instance, some of which (e.g., 'maybe just a warning') went undeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Weakest area: the clinician voiced and amplified Dylan's sustain talk directly ('If it were up to me, we'd just close this whole file'), doubling down on the client's dismissiveness and briefly derailing the session. Recovery was strong, but the lapse caps this score.
