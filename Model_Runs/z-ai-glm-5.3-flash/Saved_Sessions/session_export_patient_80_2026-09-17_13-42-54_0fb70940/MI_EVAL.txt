# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance; normalized ambivalence and relapse, and accepted Luka's decision to cut down rather than quit. Kept from 5 by generic warmth, repeated safety scripts, and no affirmations of Luka's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections (calling could defuse things; asking for help is protective), but reflections were sparse given Luka's rich emotional disclosures about pride, shame, and fear. Often jumped to strategies rather than mirroring the emotional core.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on alcohol use and aggression, building steadily to a concrete plan with no drift. Momentum was somewhat checklist-driven, with repeated safety questions briefly diverting the thread.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered topic choice and role-play, but plans were mostly delivered as pre-packaged lists rather than co-developed. Luka's own ideas shaped the plan only modestly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent choice points, acceptance of reduced-drinking goal, and letting Luka end on his terms. Kept from 5 because options came from a counselor-set menu without emphasizing Luka's ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely drew out Luka's own motivations; his powerful change talk and self-insight went unreinforced. No evocative questions or importance/confidence exploration; the plan largely originated from the counselor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional invitations to choose, but the counselor largely operated from an expert position with scripts and strategy lists. Luka's input influenced the plan somewhat, but power-sharing was lukewarm.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged (via the importance question and role-play) but was acknowledged rather than deepened. Missed chances to reflect it back, explore confidence, or ask how the rehearsal felt.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about continued drinking was tolerated respectfully without conflict, but no double-sided reflections linked drinking to his goal of not scaring his partner, and the 'down the track' ambiguity went unexplored.
