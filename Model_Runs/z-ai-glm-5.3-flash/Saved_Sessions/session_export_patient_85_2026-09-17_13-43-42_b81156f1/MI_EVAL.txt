# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful and non-judgmental ('That honesty is really important — and not a failure'), but warmth feels formulaic, with repeated 'I'm proud of you' closings that seem scripted rather than genuine. Marco's emotional struggle is never deeply affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal — emotionally loaded statements ('that stings to say out loud,' 'walking out of here lighter than the scale says') go unreflected and are instead met with informational lists and plan recaps. No complex reflections are used.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing OJ intake and produces a concrete plan (Sunday start, grocery change, tally tracking, two-week check-in). It falls short of top marks because direction is imposed through agenda-driven lists, and Marco's cue about extending walks is ignored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco selected and adapted options (the morning-glass exception, fridge tally), but the counselor supplies most content and never invites his expertise. Repeated 'Quick recap' passages read as instruction rather than joint planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice menus ('Which of these feels doable this week?') offer some autonomy support, but frequent prescriptive advice and reminders take ownership of the plan. Marco's autonomy statements ('I'll ask my wife,' 'Sunday it is') are never explicitly affirmed as his choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplies motivation rather than eliciting it — Marco's strong confidence ('A 10') and past success are never explored or deepened. After the opening question, evocative questions and change talk development are largely absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menus and 'which feels doable' questions gesture toward shared decision-making, and Marco's ideas shaped the plan. However, the overall posture is expert-driven, with the counselor as troubleshooter and coach rather than equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Structured menus allowed Marco to generate his own plan, but his change talk was harvested opportunistically rather than skillfully cultivated — no affirmations targeting motivation or evocative questions like 'Why does this matter to you now?'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** When Marco said 'Boy, do I love that OJ,' the counselor responded reasonably and pivoted to options without amplifying sustain talk. This was neutral redirection, though — no double-sided reflections or exploration of the loss of giving up something he loves.
