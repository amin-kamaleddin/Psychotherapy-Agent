# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warmth, respect, and non-judgment, even when Jordan expresses helplessness. However, the check-in question risked feeling evaluative—Jordan explicitly said 'don't make it a big thing, I'll feel judged'—and setbacks could be normalized more explicitly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing Jordan's weariness, fear of rejection, and the couch trap insight. But the poignant admission 'Nobody really checks in on me... that's kinda sad to say out loud' received only brief acknowledgment rather than deeper attunement to his loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout: the session moved systematically from vague hopelessness to a fully specified behavior plan (10-minute walk, 5:50 reminder, playlist, if-then, check-in). Even when Jordan drifted into global hopelessness, the counselor gently returned to the concrete step without dismissing his concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Jordan supplying timing, music preferences, and the shoes-by-door idea. However, the counselor often pre-packaged solutions before exploring Jordan's own ideas, so Jordan shaped details while the counselor framed the plan's architecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan's choices consistently drove the plan and he was never pressured. Still, dense bundles of suggestions occasionally felt like scripting, and Jordan's self-generated contingency plan ('I don't want one bad day to be my excuse') missed an explicit affirmation of his ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence rulers effectively drew out Jordan's own motivations ('I'd like to not feel stuck anymore,' 'the couch hasn't fixed anything'), moving his confidence from 5 to 7. The counselor's suggestions sometimes crowded out deeper evocation, and the isolation disclosure wasn't explored for underlying values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan's expertise was treated as central, with his answers visibly shaping the plan. But the counselor sometimes supplied many concrete solutions rapidly (if-then scripts, routines, song titles), drifting toward an expert-advice stance rather than first inviting Jordan's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook skill: open questions, confidence scaling, and 'What would raise your confidence?' progressively strengthened Jordan's change talk from 'maybe it's worth a shot' to 'sitting on that couch hasn't fixed anything either.' Confidence rose from 5 to 7 through structured evocation rather than persuasion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally met with neutral acknowledgment followed by redirects, never argued with or reinforced. However, some hopelessness statements went unreframed, and no double-sided reflections explicitly paired sustain talk with change talk to pivot away from the helplessness narrative.
