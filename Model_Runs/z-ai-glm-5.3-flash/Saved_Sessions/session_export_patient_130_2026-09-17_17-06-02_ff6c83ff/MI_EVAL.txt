# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a neutral, respectful stance without judgment or confrontation. However, affirmations are rare ('Good move,' 'solid, practical step') and there is little explicit validation of Viktor's difficult position or his values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture surface content ('you feel pushed here by your wife... you see your job as under control'). But the counselor misses emotional layers—Viktor's care for his wife ('She's a good woman, I don't like the fighting either'), his fear that changing means admitting something's wrong, and his irritation at role-play all pass without meaning-level or feeling-level reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus on the agreed target—reducing fights without eliminating Viktor's social life—ending with a concrete behavioral plan and follow-up structure. However, the original substance use is never revisited after the opening, and rapid pivots to logistics foreclose deeper exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early collaboration is decent, with permission-seeking and open questions, and Viktor's own rewordings are sometimes incorporated. But the session degrades into the counselor proposing plans while Viktor accepts, amends, or rejects—Viktor is never invited to generate solutions himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Steps are framed as optional ('Would you consider...?', 'Want me to draft...?') with genuine choices offered. Yet the volume of unsolicited suggestions (scripts, alarms, reminders, tracking templates) creates pressure, and the counselor keeps offering after Viktor declines.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation comes largely from the counselor's suggestions rather than being drawn out of Viktor; only the confidence ruler and one open question are genuine evocative moves. Viktor's intrinsic motivations—caring for his wife, wanting a quieter house, pride in competence—surface spontaneously but are never actively evoked or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission is asked early and choices are offered, but the middle session shifts heavily toward the counselor generating all ideas—scripts, reminder tricks, tracking formats—even after Viktor pushes back ('I fix machines for a living'). Viktor occasionally shapes the session, but the counselor remains the 'ideas person.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited ('I could maybe be home more some nights,' 'I'll try it,' confidence rising from 7 to 8.5), and the confidence ruler is used well. But Viktor's most significant statement ('She's a good woman, I don't like the fighting either') is never reflected or amplified, and no questions explore why the changes matter to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with minimization and skillfully reframes the ETA as 'common courtesy, not treatment,' working within Viktor's framing. However, sustain talk ('nothing needed fixing,' 'she'll think that means I'm admitting something's wrong') is largely ignored rather than met with double-sided reflections that pair it with his own change talk.
