# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Marek's frustration. However, affirmations were sparse and warmth felt somewhat formulaic, with little explicit recognition of his strengths or honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were adequate, but responses became increasingly checklist-driven as the session progressed. Marek's disclosure of his father's liver failure—a significant vulnerable moment—was never reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept focus on alcohol use throughout and produced a concrete plan (7-day drink log, taxi commitment, Friday follow-up). Direction sometimes felt prescriptive rather than collaboratively guided, and deeper motivational threads weren't developed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (offering options, promising neutral review), but the plan felt largely counselor-designed in a transactional checklist manner. Marek's own ideas weren't actively invited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: accepted Marek's boundary ('not agreeing to drink less'), honored his 'no lectures' request, and offered choices without pressure. Occasionally drifted into unsolicited advice with pre-packaged tip lists.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied heavily on information delivery rather than drawing out Marek's own motivations. Never asked open questions about his reasons for change, and never explored the discrepancy between his self-image and his family history.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some effort to position Marek as the expert, but much of the session had a psychoeducational quality with counselor-prescribed plans. Marek's genuine expertise wasn't explicitly recognized or leveraged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited and reinforced (tracking commitment, taxi use, 'I'd rather know than not know'). Key opportunities to deepen motivation—especially around his father's liver failure and driving concerns—were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing, but led with risk information and prescriptive plans that sometimes triggered more defensiveness. No double-sided reflections or strategic reframing of sustain talk toward change.
