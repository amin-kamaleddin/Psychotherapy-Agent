# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive; validates Lucía's frustration, affirms her autonomy, and honors her preferences without any judgment or pressure. Occasional affirmations (e.g., 'I love that') feel slightly effusive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent reflective listening that captures both content and emotional nuance, including Lucía's shame and her insight about the quiet. Reflections occasionally summarize rather than deepen (e.g., the divorce-era history isn't explored).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus and progressive momentum, building from ambivalence exploration to a concrete experiment and follow-up plan. Minor circular moment late in the session with the hands question repeating already-answered material.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician asks rather than tells, and all plans (coffee-on-the-step experiment, notes-app tracking, check-in format) originate from Lucía's own material. Genuine power sharing with no expert content or advice introduced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength: every decision, plan, and success criterion is framed as Lucía's own ('on your terms,' 'completely yours'), with options offered rather than directives. Control over the process remains genuinely with the client throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client — no persuasion, advice, or health information is supplied. Confidence/ability talk is touched only lightly and could be elicited more systematically.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lucía is explicitly positioned as the expert ('what's your instinct?'), directly countering her history of dismissive medical encounters. Occasionally the clinician names her expertise for her rather than letting it stand more quietly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and deepened, progressing from 'I might test it' to a concrete plan for tomorrow morning. Held back slightly by a few reflections that circle back rather than push further (e.g., the hands question).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('they always say the same thing,' 'I'm not promising anything') is met with accurate empathic reflections and double-sided reflections amplifying willingness, never argument. De-emphasized consistently without dismissal.
