# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout ('I'm not the one who gets to label your life', 'That took honesty'), never judging even when the client deflects blame. Warm, non-judgmental stance is consistent even amid dismissiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including deep reflections on the blackout night ('Getting lucky with your safety isn't the same as being in control of it'). Captures surface content and deeper meaning around safety, control, and dignity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent focus on the target behavior despite the client's redirections toward the unfairness of conduct. Session progresses coherently from denial to ambivalence to concrete change strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner, thinking 'out loud' with the client and building on client-generated ideas. Slightly limited by doing much of the interpretive framing early on and lacking explicit agenda confirmation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly reinforces client ownership ('That's the first thing tonight that sounded like it came from you'), never pressures or imposes goals. Frames change as fully the client's choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key insight comes from the client—the embarrassing blackout, safety being luck, the desire for control. Strategic questions successfully deepen the client's own reasons for change, moving him from pre-contemplation to self-motivated action.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert, inviting his own solutions rather than prescribing. The client's contributions visibly shape the session direction—exemplary power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully notices and amplifies early change talk ('maybe I don't need to be the guy at every sketchy party'), producing progressively stronger client language and a concrete harm-reduction plan. Elicits authentic personal motivation beyond external pressure.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses effective double-sided reflections and avoids arguing, but sometimes validates sustain talk extensively before redirecting. Client retains substantial sustain talk, so pivots could be tightened.
