# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with frequent, genuine, individualized affirmations ('That's real self-care, Ingrid'). No judgment appears even when the patient is curt or rushed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including an excellent double-sided reflection balancing the appointment with her concerns. However, emotional reflections were occasionally shallow—the patient's stated alarm and relief were not briefly reflected before pivoting to tasks.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on safe medication management despite time pressure, moving efficiently to a concrete plan. The final exchanges drifted into repetitive goodbyes and affirmations rather than consolidating the plan concisely.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was largely co-constructed, with the patient's ideas (photos, list plus review) shaping the plan. Early on, the medication question was answered in expert fashion without first negotiating the agenda, though this was largely appropriate given her direct request.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support—choices repeatedly reinforced ('Your pace, your choices') and options offered rather than imposed. The plan was fully credited as the patient's own with no coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from the patient rather than lecturing, eliciting her own reasons and solutions. Evocation was stronger in the second half; the repetitive closing could have been used to evoke deeper commitment language (e.g., confidence about calling).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient was positioned as the expert and her contributions genuinely shaped the session. The stance leaned slightly informational early on with the pharmacological details, though this was appropriate in context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced the patient's change language, with the photo-of-bottles idea emerging entirely from her after a good evocative question. Several closing exchanges circled affirmations rather than deepening change talk further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about time pressure was never argued with; double-sided reflections legitimized the constraint while keeping focus on her intentions. Time constraints received considerable airtime across the closings without redirection.
