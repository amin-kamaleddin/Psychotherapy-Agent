# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard shown throughout, with zero judgment despite Erik's defensive opening. Consistent validation ('Both of those make sense to me') and respect for his dignity, even when he fears being policed by his wife.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate, concise, and reach emotional depth, including naming Erik's fear that his wife has been 'waiting for him to mess up.' This elicited significant emotional disclosure from the client.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus and progressively narrows toward a concrete, self-directed action plan. A 5 is withheld because the diet component Erik flagged as 'messier' is deferred without being flagged for future work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine exchange between equals—every plan element originates with Erik, and the clinician follows his lead even when he redirects the conversation. No trace of an expert-driven stance even when Erik explicitly requests one.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is reinforced relentlessly ('on your terms,' 'which one could you realistically try first?'), never giving direct advice despite Erik asking 'what should I do first?' This successfully shifts Erik from external attribution ('doc's orders') to internal ownership ('I'm doing it because I decided').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within—Erik's own reasons, solutions, and coping wisdom (e.g., 'one bad batch doesn't mean the whole line's scrap'). By the end, Erik articulates intrinsic ownership absent at intake.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Erik is repeatedly framed as the expert ('You're the expert on your own life'), and his contributions directly shaped the plan. His remark that nobody had asked his opinion before demonstrates the power-sharing genuinely shifted the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions evoke concrete change talk (weekend beers, swapping chips), which is then deepened with evocative slip-planning questions. The climax—Erik planning to tell his wife 'I need you beside me, not watching me'—is client-generated commitment language actively amplified by the clinician.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I feel fine,' 'not promising miracles') is absorbed with neutral double-sided reflections rather than argued against, and quickly pivoted to Erik's own reasons. A 5 is withheld because sustain talk is mostly absorbed rather than actively reframed; targeted affirmations against the 'sod it' prediction could have been deployed earlier.
