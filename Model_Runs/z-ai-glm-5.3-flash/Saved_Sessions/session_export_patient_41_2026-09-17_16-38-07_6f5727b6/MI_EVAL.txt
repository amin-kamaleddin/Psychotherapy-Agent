# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone with warm affirmations of Elira's effort and structure-building. Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, and there was limited explicit normalization of her fear of failure and recurring avoidance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening, accurately capturing the avoid-anxiety cycle and Elira's insight about handling the scariest part before becoming tired. Reflections occasionally trailed into question bundles, and the emotional weight of her critical voice and past discouragement went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, unwavering focus on the target behavior, with every exchange tightening the plan from general avoidance to a highly specific behavioral experiment (5-minute start, exact problem, 6:40 alarm, roommate text). The session progressed logically toward concrete action without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adjusted the plan when Elira proposed modifications, but the pattern was predominantly counselor-designed options with Elira choosing among them—a menu-driven rather than co-created approach. Multi-question bundles left little room for Elira's own ideas to emerge organically.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and incorporated Elira's preferences (5 minutes instead of 10, texting rather than talking to her roommate). At times the structured 'Plan' framing and repeated confidence-scaling questions felt slightly pressuring rather than purely choice-supportive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivation through repeated scaling questions and open prompts, drawing out Elira's own solutions and raising her confidence from 4–5 to 7 through her own articulation. Missed opportunities to evoke deeper values and personal reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for Elira's input, but the counselor largely generated the options and solutions while Elira mainly selected among them. Elira's own ideas (text-based roommate check-in, choosing the problem Wednesday) shaped the plan somewhat, but power-sharing was lukewarm.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully used scaling questions that moved Elira's confidence from 4–5 to 7, and she produced increasingly strong change talk ('I'll do that today'). Rehearsing the coping statement aloud was a strong evocative technique, though deeper commitment language and exploration of her 'why' were not elicited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to sustain talk with affirmations and reframes rather than amplifying doubt, acknowledging difficulty and quickly pivoting to problem-solving. Did not use double-sided reflections strategically to resolve ambivalence, and her distrust of herself was only lightly addressed.
