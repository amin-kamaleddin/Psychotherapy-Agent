# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, most notably immediately accepting Marcus's refusal regarding his dog without judgment. Affirms his values warmly and validates his experience of being judged elsewhere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are precise and emotionally attuned, capturing both the overwhelm of Marcus's situation and the emotional core of his motivation (his dog, his breathing). Careful tracking of emotional subtext across the whole session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on asthma management while honoring Marcus's agenda, moving coherently from overwhelm to a specific, committed plan. The opening is somewhat broad, converging on the target behavior slightly later than ideal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed from Marcus's own routines and knowledge, with the psychiatrist contributing only structure. Marcus's offer to hear 'what's next' reflects a balanced, future-oriented dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly hands over choice at every juncture with no persuasion or pressure, and affirmatively accepts Marcus's boundary of not adding anything else. Marcus confirms ownership: 'it did feel like my idea... I usually shut down when people push.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rather than supplying reasons for change, the psychiatrist draws out Marcus's own deeply personal motivations — breathing easier, keeping up with his aging dog. The resulting motivation is entirely client-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise within the client, and the plan originates entirely from Marcus's ideas. Marcus directly notes the difference from prior experiences of being lectured.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies Marcus's own change language, anchoring it to his values and reframing doubts into realistic motivation. Session ends with clear commitment language ('I'll commit to that much').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing barriers, closes the dog discussion respectfully, and reframes 'imperfect mornings' into change-oriented language. Scores 4 rather than 5 because softening relies on redirection rather than explicit double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
