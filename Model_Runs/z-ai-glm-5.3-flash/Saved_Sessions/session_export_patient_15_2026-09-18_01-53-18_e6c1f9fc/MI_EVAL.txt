# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirmed the patient's autonomy and expertise, never criticizing his minimization of risk. His choices—including keeping weekend drinking—were fully respected as his own decisions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently captured both sides of the patient's ambivalence (e.g., unwinding ritual vs. wanting to know the truth). The tone was warm and understanding throughout, including reflections of feeling stretched thin and the hope for closeness with his wife.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange moved purposefully toward the patient's own reasons and plan for change, from exploring ambivalence to designing a concrete self-experiment. The conversation maintained clear momentum without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly positioned the patient as the expert, inviting his ideas ('What do you think a realistic experiment might look like?'). All elements of the plan came from the patient himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient designed the experiment, chose the tracking method, and decided how to talk to his wife. The clinician explicitly avoided persuasion ('Rather than me telling you what the research says') and reinforced agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully elicited strong change talk, including 'I'd rather be the one in charge than the habit' and 'it's worth knowing for sure.' The patient articulated his own reasons, motivations, and anticipated obstacles.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue was genuinely two-way, with the patient volunteering problems, doubts, and solutions ('would cutting back even move the numbers?'). The clinician followed the patient's lead rather than directing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician reflected and deepened each instance of change talk, evoking desire, ability, commitment, and taking-steps language. The patient moved from tentative wondering to a firm, self-directed plan with implementation intentions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (father lived to eighty, 'it's just habit,' 'it won't budge') was neither argued against nor reinforced; it was acknowledged and reframed as part of the experiment. The clinician allowed doubt to coexist with commitment ('one evening doesn't ruin the whole experiment').
