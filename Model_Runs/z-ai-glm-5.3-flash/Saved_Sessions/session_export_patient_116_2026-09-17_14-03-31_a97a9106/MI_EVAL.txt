# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Samir's humor and his goals as reasonable. However, warmth feels somewhat formulaic and specific affirmations of his strengths appear only late in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections acknowledge the 'zombie' side effect and his sense that routines feel intrusive, but many turns skip reflection and jump to question batteries. Emotionally significant disclosures — especially his fear of being 'trapped' — go unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on medication adherence from the first exchange; every turn ties back to reasons for skipping, tolerable regimen options, prescriber communication, and a concrete plan. Even role-play serves the behavioral goal of preparing Samir for the doctor visit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative — pros/cons presented as choices, plans co-developed through role-play feedback, and Samir's preferences incorporated. Some turns are monologue-like, with bulleted option lists and a pre-drafted script slightly tipping toward the expert role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: consistent permission-seeking, honoring conditional willingness without pressure, and a plan emphasizing client control with explicit stop criteria and a bail-out option. The counselor reinforces Samir's ownership rather than prescribing a course.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Samir's own motivations — the 'zombie' experience, desire for autonomy, and self-generated commitment language like 'I could try that.' Relies somewhat on structured question lists rather than following spontaneous cues, and key motivational material (fear of being trapped) isn't explored further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Samir as the decision-maker, and the role-play transfers expertise to prepare him to advocate for himself. Some advice-heavy responses dominate, with Samir's contributions receiving only brief acknowledgment before moving on.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through structured questions and role-play, moving Samir from 'What would I even change?' to 'I could try that.' Missed opportunities to deepen key change statements, such as 'makes it sound like a decision, not laziness.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids dwelling on sustain talk, briefly acknowledging and reframing resistance before pivoting to what Samir wants different. The devil's advocate role-play was framed as skill practice, strategically useful rather than reinforcing resistance.
