# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with supportive responses to Linda's vulnerabilities and some affirmations ('three weeks is meaningful'). Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, with limited normalization of her repeated failed attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing her social identity around smoking, her discouragement, and fear of 'the forever part.' Reflections became less frequent later as the counselor pivoted to logistics, missing chances to reflect the emotional weight of her shame pattern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on tobacco cessation with every exchange advancing concrete action: the social cue, specific line, Tuesday target, Margaret's support, and slip plan. No topic drift, culminating in a confirmed Wednesday 10:00 am follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan was largely co-constructed from Linda's own choices, with regular check-ins ('Want to rehearse the exact words now?'). Short of a 5 because the counselor occasionally supplied content directly, like the five-step slip protocol and self-talk phrases.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Linda's ownership through open choice questions about focus, format, and confidence-boosters, with advice offered only as options. Linda's own comment — 'the first time I've left feeling like I might actually manage it' — validates the approach, contrasting it with past 'doctor lectures'.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and rehearsal drew out some strong client-generated insights ('not let one cigarette turn back into twenty years'), but evocation was inconsistent. The counselor frequently supplied solutions rather than asking what Linda thought would help in critical moments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda was repeatedly positioned as the expert, and her choices demonstrably shaped the session (social situations focus, 'cutting down' line, Tuesday target). Sometimes options were offered in a slightly prescriptive, rapid-fire manner rather than drawn from Linda herself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda generated meaningful change talk, and confidence rulers (used twice) shifted her self-efficacy from 4-5 up to 6-7. However, much change talk was client-initiated, and the counselor didn't deepen it when it emerged, moving quickly to logistics instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification, and 'cutting down' was reframed as a legitimate step. Little strategic work to resolve ambivalence, though — no double-sided reflections, and the counselor pivoted past her recurring concerns about the group and identity.
