# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with explicit choice language and no pushback when Marcus resists commitment. The opening 'brave step' misread the referral, and warmth remains somewhat brief and functional rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures Marcus's peer-oriented framing, defensiveness, and priority of avoiding parental consequences. Reflections sometimes stay surface-level and miss emotional undercurrents like fear of his dad's reaction and belonging anxiety when sober.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent focus on alcohol use at parties throughout, with every exchange tying back to drinking or risk reduction. Steadily moves from exploration to a concrete, behaviorally specific plan for a specific upcoming event with no meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative—strategies are chosen jointly, lines are co-tweaked to match Marcus's voice, and he shapes the agenda (choosing backup-plan over rehearsal). Falls short of 5 because the counselor sometimes presents pre-formed menus before evoking Marcus's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with explicit choice language, no coercion, and clear assurances of confidentiality ('I can't contact them'). Accepts Marcus's refusal to commit to quitting, resulting in strong client ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Marcus's own pros, cons, and motivations ('avoiding getting caught matters more than health stuff'), yielding commitment talk. Motivation remains anchored in risk management rather than deeper personal values, limiting depth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Marcus as the expert, inviting him to select strategies and tweak lines to fit his voice. The counselor still generates most option lists top-down rather than evoking ideas from Marcus first, so full power-sharing isn't achieved.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evokes commitment and planning language ('Saturday, at Dev's, I'll try it. Cup trick, pacing line, no pics'). Settles for harm reduction without exploring any deeper motivation toward longer-term change after Marcus resists quitting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argues with abundant sustain talk, instead reflecting it briefly and pivoting to workable change ('keep the social confidence but reduce those risks'). Sometimes restates sustain talk verbatim without a strategic double-sided reframe.
