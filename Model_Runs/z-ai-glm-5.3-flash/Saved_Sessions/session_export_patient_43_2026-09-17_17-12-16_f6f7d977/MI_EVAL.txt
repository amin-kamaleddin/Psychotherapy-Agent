# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, earning Eric's thanks for not lecturing. Affirmations were somewhat generic rather than personalized unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflective listening captured Eric's perspective, including his ambivalence. Reflections stayed at surface level, not exploring emotions beneath the minimization or smoking's role in identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on smoking with structured agenda, rulers, and a concrete one-week trial with follow-up. Occasional drift into suggestion lists slightly muddied focus and moved toward action prematurely.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission before offering help and offered choice menus that shaped the final plan. Sometimes moved to advice-giving too quickly, prompting Eric to push back against the premature planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with explicit statements of choice, menu options, and acceptance of Eric's rejections without pressure. Some packaged advice was offered before checking if Eric wanted that detail.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and questions about raising importance generated modest client reasons. Motivation remained anchored to external pressure ('keeping the peace'), and Eric's internal values were never deeply drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed expertise residing in Eric through option menus and permission-seeking, with Eric's choices shaping the plan. Could have more explicitly acknowledged Eric as the expert and elicited his ideas before supplying suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk via rulers and highlighting past success, yielding a small behavioral commitment. Rarely deepened change talk about Eric's own reasons, leaving motivation externally framed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally without endorsement or amplification. Limited strategic pivots; 'I feel fine' was accepted at face value rather than softened with double-sided reflections linking it to health findings.
