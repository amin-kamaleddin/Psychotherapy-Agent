# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveys warmth, respect, and non-judgment ('I'm here to listen without judgment,' 'it's a tweak, not failure'). However, affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic, and moments of client self-criticism are only briefly acknowledged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several accurate reflections capture her overwhelm and concern for Leo. However, emotional moments (fear of rejection — 'If she says no... I don't know what I'd do') are skipped in favor of moving to drafting and problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear, consistent focus on two interrelated targets (childcare stress and coping via smoking/drinking) with a progressive structure: identify problem → options → commitment → coping plan → rehearsal → follow-up. Each turn builds toward concrete action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuine co-creation — drafts are edited by the client ('whatever works for your schedule'), and the trial week and session cadence are co-developed. Language like 'we can brainstorm' and 'we can role-play' conveys equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong throughout: the counselor repeatedly offers menus of options with no pressure ('No pressure — just wanted to ask,' 'Which feels more doable?') and accepts Daniela's own follow-up schedule without modification.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within — scaling questions surfaced strong change talk and open questions elicited her own solutions (meal barter, Head Start, one-hour delay). It falls short of 5 because evocation relies heavily on the same scaling format, and moments of doubt are met with advice rather than exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions Daniela as expert with frequent choice questions, and her contributions substantially shape the session (text format, wording, Thursday timing, delay strategy). Score stops short of 5 because the counselor supplies most of the concrete content (scripts, checklists) rather than drawing more from her first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions effectively elicited strong commitment ('It's a 9, honestly') and the client generated her own solutions and ended with 'I can do this week.' Missed opportunities include the 'why a 9 and not a 10' follow-up and deepening her statement about growth from a month ago.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced and some gentle reframing is offered ('it's a tweak, not failure'). However, discouragement ('tea-and-walk stuff feels useless') and fear of rejection are met with problem-solving rather than validation, double-sided reflections, or strategic exploration of ambivalence.
