# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm opening and respectful affirmations were present, though some felt formulaic. Deeper emotional disclosures (loneliness, feeling like a ghost) went unvalidated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections showed empathy, but reflective listening disappeared during advice-giving and role-play. Key emotional disclosures about isolation, the breakup, and fear of being an outcast were never reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed anchored to DUI-related change targets with the readiness ruler and role-play tied to the concern. Slight drift occurred into role-play mechanics and repeated check-in questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan blended the patient's own ideas (cab money, peer distance, scripts) with counselor information in a co-constructed way. Dense advice lists were delivered counselor-style before fully exploring the patient's own knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Permission-asking and choice-offering were consistent, and the 90-day frame was positioned as reassessable. However, advice was sometimes offered densely, and explicit statements reinforcing the patient's ownership of change were absent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The importance ruler and role-play drew out strong commitment language, but evocation was superficial elsewhere. The counselor did not probe the loneliness behind the prior relapse or elicit confidence around reducing drinking itself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient's ideas and judgments shaped the plan, with questions like 'Which feels most useful?' conveying partnership. Large advice blocks were delivered somewhat expert-driven before exploring the patient's own knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The readiness ruler and role-play successfully elicited strong change talk and commitment language. Change talk was not consistently deepened or summarized, and confidence around not drinking (vs. not driving) was never explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ('bad luck,' 'just beer,' 'I don't have a problem') was repeatedly passed over without double-sided reflections or gentle pivots. The tension between minimization and the 2.8 BAC was never acknowledged.
