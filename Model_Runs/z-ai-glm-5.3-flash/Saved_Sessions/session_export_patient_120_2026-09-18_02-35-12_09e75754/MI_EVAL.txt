# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard and never judges Sofia's drinking; affirmations feel genuine and individualized. Some affirmations are tied to reinforcing change talk rather than expressed independent of her progress toward change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with several accurate complex reflections capturing loneliness, fear, guilt, and the past-guilt vs. present-choice distinction. Occasionally blends affirmation with reflection, flattening deeper emotional nuance—e.g., the fear about pre-pregnancy drinking at 3 a.m. is met with a practical plan rather than emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use during pregnancy and navigating the high-risk weekend, with no topic drift. Guides momentum progressively from exploration to a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely open questions and session direction visibly follows Sofia's answers, with all plan elements originating from her. The psychiatrist builds on rather than overrides her ideas, positioning alongside as a partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently choice-based language and self-generated plan elements, framed as 'just this weekend, on your own terms.' Could be stronger with more explicit acknowledgment that Sofia can change or abandon the plan without failing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys that motivation resides within Sofia, eliciting her own reasons and resources throughout. The pivot from 'I put hope in you' to 'that's hope coming out of you' perfectly embodies evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The entire plan—cousin, Saturday movies, ultrasound picture, voice note—is Sofia's creation, with the psychiatrist facilitating and amplifying. The clinician consistently positions expertise within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully and consistently evokes change talk, with Sofia's commitment language deepening across the session from 'I could probably handle that' to 'I didn't just lose partying—I get to be somebody's mom.' Strategic and highly effective evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk competently using double-sided reflections and reframing (e.g., loneliness as expected but not plan failure). Effective, though slightly more direct redirecting of the 'losing my social life' talk could strengthen this further.
