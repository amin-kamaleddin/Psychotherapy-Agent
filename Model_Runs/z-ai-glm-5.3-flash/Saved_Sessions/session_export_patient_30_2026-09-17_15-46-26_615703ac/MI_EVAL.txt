# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout and validated the patient's preferences, though explicit affirmations of the patient's strengths were limited. Warmth was functional rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected the patient's skepticism, side-effect fears, and work concerns. Reflections were accurate but somewhat list-like, with less exploration of the emotional meaning behind his guardedness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on cholesterol management and medication adherence throughout, moving consistently toward a concrete action plan despite patient deflections.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently invited input and co-constructed the plan and script using the patient's own words. However, sometimes offered ready-made lists rather than fully eliciting the patient's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed choice as the patient's, offering menus of options, accepting modifications to goals, and positioning medication as a backup. The patient clearly owned the final plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from the patient's plan and wife's support, moving him from surface compliance to genuine commitment. Missed opportunities to explore the patient's own reasons for change or use confidence/importance scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the patient as decision-maker and incorporated his adjustments into the plan. Slightly prescriptive question lists and SMART goals tipped toward expert-provision at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change language, leading to the patient's own commitments ('The walking starts tonight,' 'I'm willing to follow through'). Missed chance to amplify his optimism ('maybe this is more doable than I thought').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it, using double-sided reflections and pivoting to data. The buddy's side-effect story was not actively explored—only statistics were supplied.
