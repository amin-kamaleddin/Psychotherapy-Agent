# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and warm with affirmations like 'That's a strong, protective choice,' and no judgment of the relapse. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and missed chances to normalize Elias's shame or affirm his courage in saying 'I need help' to his brother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflection early on capturing Elias's core ambivalence between external and internal motivation. Later, the counselor shifted heavily to advice-giving and left shame, fear, and 'hoping's what got me the relapse' emotionally unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus on pill removal and a post-argument coping routine, culminating in a concrete timeline (text tonight, call tomorrow, pharmacy Friday). Repetition of the same options and failure to return to the deeper 'want vs. have to' ambivalence kept it from a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely equal interaction with menu-style options, and Elias shaped most of the plan (back-door routine, brother as support person, text wording). At times the counselor pre-empted co-creation by drafting scripts before Elias finished exploring, making exchanges feel like propose-and-approve.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent choice-framing ('pick or tweak,' 'Which of those feels doable?') and Elias retained control over key decisions. Some repeated nudges ('are you ready to send this tonight?') felt slightly pushing, with little explicit emphasis on his ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective early evocation drew out family-based motivation and strong client-generated commitment language. Motivation-generating was front-loaded; the second half supplied content (scripts, routines) rather than continuing to elicit Elias's own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing early (choice of coping routine, brother as support person), but the counselor increasingly took the expert role mid-session with verbatim scripts, prescribed routines, and repeated micro-directives that edged toward steering.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions successfully elicited strong commitment language ('I can do this tonight,' 'more of a want') and a confidence scaling question. The counselor often answered its own evocative questions by pivoting quickly to solutions, limiting depth of client-generated talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias's key sustain statement about stopping out of necessity versus desire was acknowledged once via a good double-sided reflection. The counselor never strategically returned to resolve this ambivalence, redirecting toward plans instead.
