# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, affirming the patient's honesty and validating wine as a small pleasure without labeling. Deducted for the repetitive safety screening (three times), which felt formulaic and slightly patronizing given the low-risk presentation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured the patient's perspective ('wine is a small pleasure... feels normal') before gently introducing discrepancy. Reflections were sometimes brief, and the counselor moved quickly to questions and advice without exploring the emotional meaning of the fall or her defensiveness in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on alcohol use throughout, with every exchange tied to quantifying intake, exploring the fall, standard drink education, and a concrete tracking plan. Session concluded with a defined follow-up to review the checklist.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the session as joint and reviewed the plan together, with the patient's choices shaping the plan (dry days, tea substitute, printable checklist). However, the plan was largely pre-constructed and delivered, with deeper negotiation of goal parameters absent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with 'it's completely up to you' framing, offering keep-the-same as a legitimate option, and respecting the patient's refusal to stop altogether — evidenced by her noting 'you didn't lecture me.' Minor deductions for prescriptive tips ('don't buy wine today') and directive, repetitive reminders.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk but relied heavily on providing information, menus, and reminders rather than drawing out and developing the patient's own reasons and values. Much of the motivational content came from psychoeducation rather than from within the client (e.g., 'my own head, and my hips' went unamplified).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed client expertise by asking which goal felt most doable and letting her choose dry days, substitutes, and tracking format. The pre-formatted goal menu with caps and targets decided in advance slightly constrained co-creation of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited change talk ('maybe cutting back a bit isn't crazy') through open questions about goals, the fall, and prior attempts. Missed opportunities to deepen these moments with elaboration questions, moving quickly to the plan once readiness appeared.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ('I still don't think I've got a serious problem') was met with neutral reflections and information rather than double-sided reflections or strategic reframing. Sustain talk was not actively softened or resolved — the patient left still stating she doesn't have a serious problem.
