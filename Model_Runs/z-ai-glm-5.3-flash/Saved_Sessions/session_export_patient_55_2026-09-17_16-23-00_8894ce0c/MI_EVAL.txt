# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was consistently polite, nonjudgmental, and thanked Isabelle for her openness, though warmth felt somewhat formulaic. Repeatedly pushing safety questions after Isabelle signaled she felt pushed suggests the counselor's agenda took precedence over genuine unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal and superficial, restating surface facts like 'balancing IB and band' without exploring meaning or feeling. Isabelle's expressed frustration at feeling accused and concern about her parents seeing her phone received only brief acknowledgment before pivoting back to options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear focus on alcohol use and risk throughout, which was a strength. However, the focus was on fact-gathering and screening rather than collaboratively exploring Isabelle's relationship with alcohol, and the session ended with logistics rather than a jointly defined target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was predominantly counselor-directed, with Isabelle answering questions and selecting from counselor-generated menus while the agenda and conclusions remained clinician-determined. The only collaborative element—scheduling around band—was logistical, and her pushback never meaningfully reshaped the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered choices (checklist format, check-in modality, appointment times) and obtained permission before sensitive questions. However, choices centered on logistical compliance rather than genuine behavioral autonomy, and persisting with risk questions after Isabelle asked to leave subtly pressured her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was assumed to reside in the counselor's information and safety checklist rather than elicited from Isabelle. Beyond one 'what would have to happen' question, her values and goals (band, grades, family) were never drawn upon, and her agreement to a check-in reflected compliance rather than internalized motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role for most of the session through structured screening, a pre-packaged safety list, and menu-driven logistics. Isabelle's expertise about her own life was acknowledged only minimally before returning to the checklist approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** One good question ('what would have to happen for you to see this as a problem?') elicited conditional change-relevant language about band and grades. However, the counselor did not follow up on or deepen this response, immediately pivoting instead to closed-ended safety screening questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Isabelle's strong, repeated sustain talk ('it's just social,' 'these feel like stuff you'd tell someone with a real problem') was mostly ignored or glossed over with brief 'I hear you' responses. No double-sided reflections or strategic use of her own values were employed to soften her resistance.
