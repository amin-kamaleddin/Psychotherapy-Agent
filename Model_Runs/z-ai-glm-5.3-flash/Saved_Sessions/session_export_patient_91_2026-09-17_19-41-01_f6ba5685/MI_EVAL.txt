# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, affirming uncertainty as 'a completely fair place to be' and normalizing fear of bad numbers. The session closed warmly, welcoming 'the good and the messy.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately captured ambivalence, e.g., 'Both of those can be true at once' validating fear and readiness together. Echoing the patient's own words and metaphors showed deep, precise understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on blood sugar monitoring and progressed steadily from overwhelm to a concrete weekly plan with a values-based reason and follow-up. Every exchange advanced the agenda without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully collaborative — 'let's think together' and co-constructed next steps throughout. The patient's contributions genuinely shaped the session, creating an equal power balance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was consistently reinforced: the patient picked 'one small piece,' was offered options, and defined his own pace and plan. There was no pressure or directive advice, with regular check-ins on how he felt about the direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the patient — his work analogy, his daughter as his 'why,' and his own coping statement were elicited rather than supplied. Marcus left having generated his own reasons, plan, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was clearly located in the patient, with Marcus's ideas (wife's involvement, breakfast ritual, morning-only checking) shaping the plan. He was invited to script his own words to his wife, reinforcing shared ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions ('What would a start look like this week?') elicited commitment language culminating in 'I could actually see myself doing this,' which was explicitly reflected back. Linking change to his daughter deepened motivation from within.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently reframed: fear of mistakes was normalized as 'part of learning the machinery,' and the workplace analogy bridged doubt toward confidence. The psychiatrist repeatedly pivoted from barriers to concrete, supportive next steps.
