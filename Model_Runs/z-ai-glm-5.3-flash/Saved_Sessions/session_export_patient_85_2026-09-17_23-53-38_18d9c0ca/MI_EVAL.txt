# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, reframing the regain as discovery ('it was you paying attention') with no judgment even when Marco admits six glasses of juice daily. Affirmations like 'That's wisdom from your own experience' and 'I believe you' feel genuine and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, complex reflections capture both feelings and ambivalence (e.g., 'like I never even tried,' and 'Missing the morning OJ is real, and it can coexist with being ready'). Marco consistently elaborates rather than corrects, a strong sign of empathic accuracy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior while progressing logically from setback → juice discovery → options → tapering plan → success definition → commitment. Each question advances the agenda without rushing, with consolidating summaries at key moments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Marco's ideas drive the session, and the counselor builds on rather than redirects his contributions. When he asks for help with the wife conversation, the counselor explores his own framing ('don't buy it, but don't nag me either') rather than supplying a script.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fosters choice throughout: offering options (fridge cleanout vs. tapering), asking what would feel most helpful from the counselor, and framing the plan as Marco's own. No advice-giving or pressure; all decisions originate with the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every substantive insight—the tapering strategy, weekly weigh-ins, the plan-adherence reframe, the opener for the wife conversation—comes from Marco via open evocative questions. The counselor never lectures, and Marco ends with an unprompted confidence rating of 10.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly attributes expertise to Marco ('you caught the juice yourself,' 'That reframe is yours') and repeatedly asks him to generate solutions. Even the closing question treats Marco's preferences as authoritative for next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions consistently pull for Marco's motivations and plans, eliciting rich commitment language ('I'm ready. This week — the tapering starts'). Change talk is deepened by connecting it to Marco's own prior successes, culminating in his spontaneous confidence rating of 10.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk masterfully—acknowledging 'I really love that OJ' without amplifying it and pivoting to change-oriented questions. The counselor normalizes the loss while framing it as compatible with readiness, and focus never lingers on reasons against change.
