# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, normalizing slips and affirming progress. Falls short of a 5 because warmth is brief and transactional, with little validation of emotional weight or partner pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate simple reflections are present, but emotional content (stress, fear of snapping, defensiveness) goes largely unexplored. The session stays at a practical, factual level with few feeling-level reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on daytime smoking reduction with NRT; every exchange builds concretely toward the agreed plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Joint planning and rehearsal exist, but power-sharing is uneven: long monologues, counselor-supplied scripts, and yes/no questions that David passively accepts. Feels more like an advice consultation with buy-in.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong explicit autonomy language ('You're in charge of the focus'), repeated choices, and respect for his non-negotiable nights. The advice-heavy middle tilts toward directive suggestion and less client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client motivation is elicited (confidence scaling, his own slip plan), but much plan content originates from the counselor. Deeper reasons, values, and the confidence rating go unexplored, making evocation superficial.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens with client-led agenda and choices, but the middle becomes expert-driven with rapid pharmacology lectures and counselor-scripted lines. Expertise is frequently located in the clinician rather than drawn from David.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked (commitment to tomorrow's plan, raised confidence), and a good scaling question is used. However, much change content is counselor-supplied and David's own reasons and tentative thoughts about quitting are not deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I like smoking', non-negotiable nights) is consistently met with acceptance and redirection rather than argument, and slip-normalization reframes failure as information. Reflections occasionally restate sustain talk without a double-sided pivot toward change.
