# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental statements ('no judgment if it doesn't go perfectly,' 'be kind to yourself') are present but formulaic. Her vulnerable worry 'Will you be annoyed?' goes unaddressed, missing a chance to affirm unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ('that dread makes sense') but responses quickly pivot to instructions. Rich emotional disclosures — fear of failure, shame, and 'I'm scared I'll let you down' — go unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on the target behavior throughout, with a logical progression from assessment to planning to follow-through. No topic drift; clear and consistent momentum toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client shapes some details (tea reward, 7:00 choice, keeping it small), but the overall pattern is expert-prescriber to implementer. Her final 'Does that sound dumb?' signals uncertainty about relational equality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional genuine choices are offered, but extracted promises ('Promise to tell me') and directives create pressure. Her fear of 'letting you down' suggests the balance tilted toward external accountability rather than her owning the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation comes from technique assignment rather than eliciting the client's own reasons for change. No importance/confidence scaling, and her nascent change talk ('even I can do one sentence') is met with logistics instead of deepening.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor dominates with prescriptive instructions and checklists; the framework and plan originate from the counselor. Client contributions serve only as confirmations, with minimal true power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Commitment is elicited through concrete planning ('Seven. Definitely seven') and success is reinforced by asking what felt helpful. However, change talk arises from task structure, not MI-consistent evocation of desire, ability, or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Repeated sustain talk ('I'll just skip it,' 'one good day and then nothing') is bypassed rather than acknowledged or reframed with double-sided reflections. Her final vulnerable fear of failure receives no MI-consistent response at all.
