# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, including unconditional regard when Marcus firmly refused to discuss the dog ('You get to decide the pace'). Score limited because warmth leans instrumental, with much of the session absorbed in logistics rather than deeper personal affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong early summary reflection on overwhelm, but empathy shallows in problem-solving mode. Emotionally loaded statements about the dog and shame in 'I've been doing this wrong for years' go unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, coherent focus on one manageable target—refilling the rescue inhaler—moving from exploration to a concrete, confidence-rated plan. Direction is narrow, with no acknowledgment of how the step connects to broader asthma goals or the deferred dog issue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely co-constructed—options offered, Marcus selects, scripts rehearsed together, details negotiated. Falls short because the counselor supplies most concrete solutions, functioning more as a helpful advisor than an equal partner drawing out Marcus's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The standout strength: genuine choice architecture, explicit pacing permission, and immediate honoring of the dog boundary without protest. Marcus's own words confirm his ownership ('this one — this one I'll do'), and the counselor accepts a self-calibrated commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation elicited via the urgency and confidence questions, but much verbal content is informational rather than elicitive. Missed chances to explore values, vision of well-managed life, meaning of being there for his wife, and his self-identified skipping pattern.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus is repeatedly positioned as decision-maker and his preferences genuinely shape the plan (inhaler refill, short script, morning visit, dog boundary). Stops short because much planning content is counselor-generated and his self-knowledge is met with logistics rather than exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moderate evocation—questions draw out fear of nighttime breathing, commitment ('this one I'll do'), and a concrete plan, with rehearsal strengthening commitment. However, change talk stays on a small logistical task and is never reflected back or amplified toward broader values and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with or amplifying sustain talk, and the dog refusal is respectfully sidestepped. But softening is passive—no double-sided reflections, strategic reframing, or linking barriers back to Marcus's stated motivations.
