# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist honors Malik's boundaries without argument or disapproval, even when he firmly refuses to promise change about cannabis. Warmth is never withdrawn, and there is no moralizing or labeling anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track both content and feeling, including double-sided reflections on ambivalence and the self-protective meaning of Malik's strategy. Depth is slightly limited — the deeper affect beneath the bravado (surveillance hurt, peer-pressure costs) is never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Despite a permissive stance, the psychiatrist keeps the conversation focused on reducing conflict at home and building specific behavioral steps, without ever pivoting to persuasion about cannabis.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is explicitly framed as joint problem-solving ('let's put our heads together'), with the psychiatrist following Malik's lead rather than imposing an agenda. Sessions goals are elicited from the patient at the outset.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Malik is consistently invited to generate his own plan ('what's one thing *you* could do?'), and the psychiatrist frames change as serving his goals, not his mother's. His self-directed plan is affirmed as 'you figuring out a move that works for *your* life.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Malik's own motivations and solutions are drawn out rather than supplied: he identifies the first step, anticipates his mother's reaction, and names his own coping strategy for avoiding blowups. The psychiatrist's questions invite his reasoning throughout.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Turn-taking is balanced, with questions and reflections flowing naturally from Malik's own words. The psychiatrist builds on his contributions rather than redirecting them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly every response skillfully elicits and amplifies change talk — Malik articulates his own desired outcome (trust, less drama), names concrete steps (texting, staying chill), and reasons that are self-motivated ('that's a win for me').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** When Malik minimizes the cannabis problem ('that's still not a big deal to me'), the psychiatrist neither argues nor confronts, simply reflecting and moving on. Sustain talk is met with acceptance, which allows Malik to volunteer change talk voluntarily.
