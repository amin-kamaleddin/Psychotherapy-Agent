# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with multiple affirmations of honesty and effort, and no judgment about whether the drinking qualifies as a 'problem.' Ambivalence is allowed to stand without disapproval or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including complex double-sided reflections capturing ambivalence and emotional nuance. Reflections even reached process level, e.g., naming 'I've done harder things' in the client's voice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on reducing weeknight drinking, with every counselor turn advancing toward motivation and concrete planning. It ended with a specific plan (wife tonight, gym Thursday) and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative and egalitarian — every plan element emerged from the client's answers to open questions rather than prescription. The client's comment ('You didn't lecture') independently confirms the stance landed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership were repeatedly reinforced ('What do you think might work for you?'), and client-set limits were accepted without pushing. The closing affirmation protected autonomy even against possible setback.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic evocative questions drew out values-based motivation ('Which one matters most to you?' eliciting the kids) and self-motivating statements. The client's arc from 'maybe' to commitment language reflects textbook evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located within the client, and the plan was entirely client-generated with the counselor organizing and reflecting it back. Insights and strengths were consistently attributed to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk, moving the client from 'maybe' to specific commitments (talk to wife tonight, gym Thursday). The counselor highlighted the client's own progression and 'doable' language without pushing for more.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (weeknight stress, friends who drink, 'not an alcoholic') was acknowledged without amplification and met with double-sided reflections redirecting to the client's own solutions. The counselor never argued, letting the client resolve ambivalence himself.
