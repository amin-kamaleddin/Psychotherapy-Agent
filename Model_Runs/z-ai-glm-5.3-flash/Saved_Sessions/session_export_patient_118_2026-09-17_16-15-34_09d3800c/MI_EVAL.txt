# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard despite Seamus's defensiveness, validating his wariness and honoring his 'no lectures' request. Never labeled or scolded, and even collaborated with the dental team to minimize lecturing, showing exceptional respect for the client's wishes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were generally accurate and complex, capturing his ambivalence about quitting and his distrustful experience with medications. Fell short of 5 because reflections occasionally bundled multiple points and the emotional depth of his shame ('I'm the one still puffing') was not explicitly explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking cessation while balancing the dental agenda, using structured probes (reasons for/against, importance and confidence rulers) that built progressive momentum toward a concrete plan. Prioritizing smoking over the client's stated dental focus early on risked disengagement with a resistant client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely between equals, with the plan co-constructed around Seamus's preferences and explicit checks like 'What sounds tolerable to you?' The counselor occasionally adopted a semi-directive planning role (offering tips, suggesting alarms) without first soliciting the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: 'I'll support whatever you choose,' no pills unless chosen, respecting his refusal of Sunday check-ins, and advocating for his preferences with the dental team. Change was consistently framed as being on Seamus's terms, matching his insistence on self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from within using open questions and rulers, with Seamus generating his own reasons and plan rather than receiving persuasion. Not fully a 5 because option menus and practical tips occasionally supplied content rather than purely evoking it from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated Seamus's contributions as decisive, incorporating his preference for gum/patch, his 'no pills' boundary, and his request to defer into the plan. At times offered option menus with slight prompting pressure, and his initial dental-focused agenda was gently redirected rather than fully honored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change language via the importance ruler, exploring what would move him from 4 to 5-6 and eliciting a concrete micro-step, yielding genuine commitment talk ('maybe I try that for a week,' 'start Monday,' confidence 6). Missed opportunities to deepen the commitment language (e.g., defining what a successful Monday would look like) kept it from 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, validating briefly then pivoting to what could be different ('What would a quit approach that doesn't wreck you actually look like?'). Sustain talk about past side effects was explored purposefully to inform planning, though a few moments lingered on past failures without active reframing.
