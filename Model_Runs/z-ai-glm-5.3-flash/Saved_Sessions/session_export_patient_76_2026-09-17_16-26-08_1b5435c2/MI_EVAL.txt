# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth throughout a difficult session, with affirmations and nonjudgmental attention to a withdrawn client. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and repeated safety checks occasionally interrupted reflective depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured ambivalence and validated the function of self-harm without endorsing it. Expressions of hopelessness, fear of losing the coping tool, and shame received only brief acknowledgment rather than sustained exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained tight focus on self-harm and safer coping from start to finish, advancing risk assessment, means restriction, coping strategies, social support, and crisis steps. Concrete action steps were established and a specific check-in scheduled (Wednesday 3pm).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was framed as joint work, with the client's input visibly shaping the plan and co-editing of text drafts. Question-heavy, checklist-style exchanges (three to six questions per turn) made the counselor the primary agenda-driver at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary reinforcement of client ownership: the client set her own compromise, key location, communication preferences, timing, and edited the Dana text. Her refusal to promise permanent cessation was accepted without pressure, with partial steps explicitly permitted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the client's own motivations and ideas (music, walks, Dana, ordering the box) and produced commitment language ('one week. I can try for one week'). Evocation remained focused on immediate safety logistics; deeper reasons and hopes for change went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly conveyed that expertise resided with the client, inviting her input and edits, which genuinely shaped the plan. The plan's structure was largely counselor-initiated with stacked questions rather than emerging primarily from the client's own agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out change talk progressing to commitment language ('I'll try the box this week,' 'I can order the box tonight'). Change talk was channeled heavily into safety-planning logistics rather than the client's broader reasons and hopes for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk, reflecting it neutrally and pivoting toward what is doable, such as affirming the realistic one-week goal. Repeated safety-plan questions mildly kept focus on the problem behavior, but sustain talk was handled without reinforcement.
