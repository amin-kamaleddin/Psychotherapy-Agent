# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with explicit reassurances like 'no judgment' even when Maya disclosed vomiting and a possible blackout. Falls short of 5 because affirmations were occasionally formulaic ('That sounds good') rather than personalized to Maya's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, but the counselor quickly shifted into information delivery and planning with fewer reflective statements. Emotional moments were under-explored — Maya's fear about the blackout, her parents finding out, and the social role of drinking received little reflective attention.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, unwavering focus on alcohol use and its school-linked risks, with every exchange tied back to the target behavior. Session ended with a specific, time-bound commitment (Friday two-minute check-in) without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone was collaborative and the plan was framed as tweakable, with Maya's practical constraints (no phone reminders, no notebook, two-minute check-ins) genuinely incorporated. Power balance tilted toward the counselor during middle information-delivery blocks that resembled expert psychoeducation more than shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Maya's choice, respecting her refusal to practice aloud and her 'not promising anything' stance without pressure. Stops short of 5 because the counselor presented pre-packaged menus (safety steps, thresholds) rather than inviting Maya to generate her own options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening question and importance ruler were genuine evocation attempts, but motivation increasingly resided in the counselor-supplied plan rather than in Maya herself. Little effort was made to evoke her feelings about the blackout or her own reasons for change, producing compliance ('low effort, so fine') over internalized motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently treated Maya as the decision-maker, letting her choose which signs fit, which steps felt doable, who to ask, and which phrasing sounded like her. Not a 5 because the check-in template, thresholds, and action menu were counselor-designed with Maya only choosing among options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened well with a change-talk question and importance ruler, but did not explore the discrepancy between Maya's future goals and drinking when she rated importance only a 3. Maya's own stirrings of change (fear about the blackout, volunteering Jess) were not reflected back or strengthened with targeted follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Never argued with or amplified Maya's sustain talk, meeting statements like 'I don't even know if what I do counts as a problem' with neutral acceptance. However, sustain talk was sidestepped rather than redirected — no double-sided reflections linked her ambivalence to her own concerns (blackout fear, late work, secrecy with her mom).
