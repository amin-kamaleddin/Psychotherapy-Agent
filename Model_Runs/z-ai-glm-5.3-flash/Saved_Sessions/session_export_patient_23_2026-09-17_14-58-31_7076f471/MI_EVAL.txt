# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, normalizing slips and affirming the patient's efforts throughout. Respect and unconditional positive regard were maintained even when the patient disclosed heavy drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured the patient's ambivalence and 'small and doable' framing well. Reflections leaned toward summaries rather than deeper emotional exploration (e.g., 'I don't like who I am the next morning').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, unwavering focus on alcohol reduction from opening question through concrete planning. Every exchange tied back to the target behavior, culminating in a specific actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan was largely co-constructed, with the patient generating most strategies herself. If-then plan and sister script were provided as finished drafts rather than fully co-authored, though she was invited to tweak them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced patient ownership with menu options and invitations to change or decline ('Want to change any part?'). Her choice to decline role-play and set her own two-drink goal was honored without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out the patient's own reasons, steps, and coping strategies via confidence rulers and open questions. Some evocation was cut short by the clinician quickly supplying concrete options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the patient as the expert, and her ideas directly shaped the session's products. Providing drafted plans and scripts shifted slightly toward an advising stance, albeit with her consent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Confidence rulers and open questions repeatedly drew out commitment language ('closer to a 7 now,' 'Monday it is'). The patient generated her own strategies, which the clinician affirmed to deepen her motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively redirected sustain talk toward attainable goals without reinforcing reasons to keep drinking. Could be strengthened with more double-sided reflections acknowledging the pull of drinking while linking to change.
