# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicates consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, welcoming slips without judgment ('the door's open here — mess included'). Affirmations are specific and individualized rather than generic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate and emotionally attuned, going beyond content to underlying meaning (e.g., the father/beach reflection). The patient repeatedly responds with deepened disclosure, confirming accuracy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on the home eating environment while guiding steadily toward change, with purposeful transitions and no aimless wandering. Even emotional moments are tied back to the change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is fully collaborative — the counselor asks rather than tells, builds on the patient's words, and the plan's content is entirely patient-generated. The power balance is genuinely equal, with the counselor following the patient's corrections ('Not this time. Small and real.').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice is imposed; every idea comes from the patient, and pacing is honored ('I want to slow down on that'). The patient's own insight ('that came from me') directly evidences effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is evoked rather than instilled, surfacing deeply personal reasons (his father, being present for his kids) through open questions. Confidence rising from six to eight demonstrates the effectiveness of this approach.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the patient ('You know her better than anyone'), and the entire plan originated from him. The counselor never gives advice and attributes ideas to the patient ('That came from you, not a diet plan').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions strategically elicit progressively stronger change talk, from 'maybe I could talk to her' to 'I actually want to try this. Really try.' The counselor highlights and elaborates change statements while confidence rises from six to eight.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently met with double-sided reflections pairing difficulties with the patient's own successes. Concerns are reframed structurally (changing the setup vs. relying on willpower), and slips are transformed into a compassionate relapse-prevention plan.
