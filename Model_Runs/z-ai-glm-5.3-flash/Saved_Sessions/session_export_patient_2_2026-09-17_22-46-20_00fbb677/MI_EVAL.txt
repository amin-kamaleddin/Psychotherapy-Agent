# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard is maintained throughout — Aisha's fear of being 'lectured' or 'broken' is directly countered ('Nothing about you is broken. You're steering this yourself'), and her boundary around alcohol is respected without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently land on the emotional and motivational core, e.g., reframing her confidence rating of 7 as 'reading yourself accurately, not doubting yourself,' and capturing her wish to keep drinking 'on the list, not tonight.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session keeps clear focus on reducing evening smoking while appropriately parking the alcohol topic, and every turn advances the change process toward a concrete written plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is composed in Aisha's own words, she chooses the follow-up focus, and every strategy originated from her; the counselor positions themselves as ally ('I'll be here when you're ready').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ownership is actively reinforced ('a quiet experiment you're running on your own terms'; 'this was always going to be your plan, not mine'), and the client's explicit boundary on drinking numbers is honored completely.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key motivations — protecting evenings with her son, the sister call, the confidence rating — come from the client, with questions that draw out her own reasons and values rather than any persuasion or advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within the client ('You know your rhythms better than anyone'), and her contributions shaped every element of the session plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and amplified throughout — from 'three instead of six' to 'I can do that even on my worst day' and 'I've written it on my phone already.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk like fear of quitting everything and weekend slipping is acknowledged in detail during planning; while handled constructively (reframed as 'real wisdom' and turned into planning opportunities), the counselor could use more double-sided reflections here.
