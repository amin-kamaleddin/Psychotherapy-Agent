# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicated consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, affirming Marcus's feelings and efforts without judgment. Affirmations were specific and individualized, such as reframing his anticipatory worry as 'planning, not panicking.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, capturing Marcus's ambivalence between meals feeling manageable and monitoring feeling high-stakes. The counselor tracked subtle shifts in his language and mirrored his own words ('information, not report card') back to him.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear, consistent focus on the target behaviors of blood sugar monitoring and meal adjustments without drift. The session progressed logically from choosing a starting point to a concrete plan and relapse preparation, culminating in 'I'm actually going to start tomorrow.'

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Information was offered only after Marcus requested it and framed as a 'starting point many people use' rather than expert instruction. The final plan was genuinely co-constructed, with the notebook system, morning timing, and involving his wife all originating from Marcus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforced Marcus's ownership, highlighting 'I want to, not just because the doctor said so' as his own motivation. Even when providing information, choice was immediately returned to Marcus, and all plans were self-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within rather than supplied, using strategic questions like 'what's your gut tell you the answer is?' and future-focused visualization. Marcus's change talk progressively strengthened from 'maybe once a day' to 'I want to... I think I've got this.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located within the client ('You know your routines and what's realistic better than anyone'). Every suggestion was returned to Marcus for his evaluation, and his contributions clearly shaped the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evoked and deepened change talk, eliciting commitment language ('I want to, not just because the doctor said so') and strong self-motivating statements through future visualization. Marcus's change talk steadily intensified across the session toward firm commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently pivoted away from without dismissal, such as reframing high numbers as 'information, not an emergency.' When Marcus anticipated wanting to quit during a rough patch, the counselor turned it into a self-efficacy building question rather than dwelling on barriers.
