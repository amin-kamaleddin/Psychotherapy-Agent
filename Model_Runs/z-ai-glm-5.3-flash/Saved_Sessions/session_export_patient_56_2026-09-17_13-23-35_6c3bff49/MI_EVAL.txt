# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, but affirmations are sparse and focus on task performance rather than the client's values or insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections, but emotional disclosures like fear of being judged and guilt over snapping were bypassed in favor of task-focused strategies.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on aligning behavior with family happiness and concrete follow-up planning, though direction was at times imposed rather than collaboratively set.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The map and scripts were built from the client's input, but the session often resembles consultation, with strategies and drafted text coming from the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices and accepts the client's modifications, though heavy pre-packaged advice slightly undermines ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dominant mode is advice-giving with few open questions to elicit the client's own reasons, resources, or confidence; the client's motivation was pre-existing rather than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative gestures, but the psychiatrist repeatedly assumes the expert role with numbered solutions rather than drawing ideas from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A scaling question successfully evoked commitment language, but the psychiatrist did not deepen or elaborate on the client's change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's hesitation about the boundary script was effectively sidestepped by supporting her self-generated solution, though the underlying fear was never acknowledged or reframed.
