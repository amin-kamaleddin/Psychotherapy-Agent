# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated warmth and unconditional positive regard, validating Richard's autonomy and courage even under provocation. He explicitly protected the client from premature commitment, saying 'You don't have to promise anyone anything today, including me.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were highly accurate and consistently captured both surface frustration and underlying concern, including the unspoken guilt about 'failing her again.' His empathy actively moved the session forward rather than merely mirroring content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on smoking and its relational consequences, progressing from defensiveness to a concrete plan for talking with his wife. Direction was slightly delayed in the first half, as the explicit directional focus emerged only after substantial alliance-building.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was fully co-constructed, with Richard's ideas driving the agenda and the psychiatrist's contributions offered as hypotheses for Richard to evaluate. Richard's request for help preparing the conversation exemplified the equal partnership established.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was repeatedly and explicitly reinforced ('You're the expert on your own life'), and Richard's emerging motivation was framed as entirely self-generated. No pressure toward any particular outcome was applied; cutting back remained Richard's own idea.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew out Richard's own reasons for change—love for his wife, fear of failing her, hidden guilt—through strategic open questions and selective reflection. The final plan was entirely Richard's motivation, elicited and developed rather than installed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positioned Richard as the expert, asking permission before offering support and checking reframes with 'What do you think?'. Richard's contributions demonstrably shaped the session's direction and outcome.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and reinforced, with selective reflection of movement ('that came from you, in your own words, on your own terms'). Richard progressed from refusing to change anything to voluntarily planning a conversation with his wife within one session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided amplifying sustain talk and never colluded with 'I've made it to 54, I'm fine,' validating 'it's just talk' as self-protection. A few sustain-talk moments (routine as 'his time,' fear of giving in) were passed over rather than explicitly softened with double-sided reflections.
