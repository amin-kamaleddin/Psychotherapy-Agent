# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance, acknowledging the mandated nature of the session and thanking Isabelle for her honesty. However, limits of confidentiality weren't named upfront, and the early rapport-building inadvertently deepened her suspicion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, complex reflective listening that accurately captured her commitments, minimization, and feelings of being trapped. Did not fully explore the emotional meaning of being flagged or her fear of judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kept a general focus on stress, coping, and alcohol, but the approach to the target behavior was extremely indirect — the alcohol topic was only named after the client raised it herself. Focus became clear only in the final exchanges.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed the session as a joint endeavor and co-constructed the agenda, following Isabelle's leads. Could have gone further in genuinely negotiating the agenda shift once the alcohol topic surfaced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding autonomy support — repeatedly affirmed Isabelle's ownership and expertise, honored her request to judge the screening data herself, and never pressured or lectured.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evoked genuine engagement by asking what would make the session useful, but motivation for change regarding alcohol was never elicited. Evocation focused on agenda-setting rather than the core target behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary power-sharing: explicitly positioned Isabelle as the expert, and her requests for directness and the screening data genuinely redirected the session. Influence over session direction was real, not token.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk was rarely evoked; a future-oriented question was met with strong sustain talk that wasn't followed up. The implicit positive ('I'm careful, mostly') was not deepened into desire, ability, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue with or reinforce sustain talk and used some double-sided reflections, but responses were acknowledged neutrally rather than strategically pivoted. Sustain talk was neither amplified nor softened with skill.
