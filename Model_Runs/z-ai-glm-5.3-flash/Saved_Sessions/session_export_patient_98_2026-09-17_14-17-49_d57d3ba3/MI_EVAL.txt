# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with validating statements like 'that sounds really hard' and normalized doubt. Affirmations were somewhat brief and generic rather than personalized to Mateo's fears about appearing weak.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made reflective efforts early but quickly shifted to problem-solving scripts when Mateo shared vulnerable content about hopelessness and shame. Deeper feelings like the fear of being seen as 'the old man complaining' were never explicitly reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on communicating pain to the rehab team and persisting in rehabilitation, culminating in a concrete, rehearsed plan. The direction narrowed early to one solution without exploring whether it matched Mateo's own preferences.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened with joint exploration framing and responded flexibly to Mateo's requests, but power tilted toward the expert with counselor-supplied templates and checklists. Mateo remained mostly in an approving/consumer role rather than co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed actions as optional, respected Mateo's initial refusal of role-play, and never used coercive language. Advice was frequently front-loaded before asking what Mateo already knew or wanted, subtly steering toward the counselor's plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions were largely supplied by the counselor rather than drawn from Mateo, with almost no open questions about his own reasons, values, or confidence. Genuine change talk like 'I didn't come this far to quit' emerged spontaneously but was not capitalized on.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some choice and asked willingness, but the interaction was heavily advice-driven with scripts, checklists, and templates. Mateo's expertise about his own body, routine, and relationship with Rosa was never solicited or treated as central.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Structured scripts unintentionally put commitment language in the counselor's mouth, which Mateo then repeated, though he did generate some genuine change talk. The counselor rarely elicited Mateo's own reasons for change or deepened change talk with follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not amplified or reinforced, and doubt was briefly reframed, but no double-sided reflections were used to hold ambivalence. The approach relied on providing solutions rather than actively softening and resolving Mateo's discouragement.
