# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard, with strong affirmations and normalization of anxiety. The client explicitly confirmed feeling accepted without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections captured both content and emotional meaning, including the precariousness of relying on her mom and her shift toward agency. The client repeatedly confirmed feeling understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on childcare instability and reducing smoking/drinking, linking every response to concrete action. The session ended with specific, time-bound commitments without feeling rushed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session unfolded as genuine dialogue between equals, with all plan elements originating from the client and 'we' framing reinforcing teamwork. The client experienced it as co-constructed rather than directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced the client's ownership through open questions and framing solutions as hers ('Those are your steps, in your hands'). No persuading or prescribing occurred, and the client expressed autonomous commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Core method was drawing out the client's own motivations, insights, and solutions rather than supplying advice. She articulated vivid personal motivation, demonstrating change was evoked from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the client as the expert—the neighbor barter idea, Sunday night plan, and Head Start idea all came from her, with the counselor reflecting and amplifying rather than supplying solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and deepened change talk through open questions and strategic highlighting ('That one was a choice'), leading to concrete plans and strong commitment language ('I'm doing it this week').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying barriers, validating fears briefly and redirecting to the client's own ideas. A few reflective moments lingered slightly on difficulty, though they were well-handled and brief.
