# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental ('I'm here to listen without judgment,' affirming the client's caution as 'calm and responsible'). Warmth is genuine but somewhat task-focused, with limited emotional normalization or validation of anxiety.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections like 'You sound relieved you checked and anxious about staying stable' are accurate but brief and formulaic. The client's fear of destabilizing, work pressure, and tendency to minimize symptoms are noted but never deeply explored or reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on safely managing the citalopram/phenelzine transition. Every turn advances concrete goals (safety facts, prescriber questions, email draft, symptom checklist) with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor co-constructs the question list incorporating the client's edits, drafts the email at her request, and invites her to shape next steps. The initial medication safety block remains more one-directional education than joint inquiry.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices ('How would you like to proceed?', 'Would you like a printable PDF?') and affirms the client's own decision to wait. The opening 'do NOT take them together' is directive—clinically necessary but limiting full ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits client expertise ('What has helped you stay steady before?') but motivation for the safety plan is largely driven by information provision. The client's own reasons, values, and confidence are not consistently evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly positions the client as the expert, and her contributions (work question, revised wording, checklist request) substantially shaped the session. Some exchanges, especially the initial didactic safety block, remain expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Commitment language emerges ('I'll call my prescriber's office this week'), but largely as a natural byproduct of the consultation rather than systematic evocation. No deliberate deepening of the client's goals, confidence, or readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor responds helpfully to the client's worry about the 14-day washout by redirecting to a taper and monitoring plan. This is effective problem-solving, but no deliberate MI strategies (e.g., double-sided reflection, exploring ambivalence) are used to soften sustain talk.
