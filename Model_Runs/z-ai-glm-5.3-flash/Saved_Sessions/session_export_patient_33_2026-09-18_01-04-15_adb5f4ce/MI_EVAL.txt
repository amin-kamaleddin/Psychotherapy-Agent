# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a warm, non-judgmental stance toward the patient's continued heroin use, explicitly honoring his choice not to quit while validating his overdose concerns. Both realities are held together without pressure toward abstinence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections consistently capture meaning and feeling beneath the patient's words, such as the worried part underneath the plan and his nervousness about his housemate's potential pushback. Reflections precede and follow disclosures, reinforcing change talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The gap between the patient disliking overdose risk and his current practices is surfaced through his own concerns and drives strong change talk. Deeper exploration of the underlying distress ('things I don't want to think about') is left untouched, though this largely reflects appropriate pacing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session opens by establishing the patient's own agenda and every step of the plan is co-constructed, including the consented role-play and next-session preferences. Power-sharing is explicit and sustained ('let's figure it out together').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Safer use is framed entirely as the patient's decision 'on his terms,' with no nudging toward abstinence despite the screening referral context. The psychiatrist affirms choices as the patient's own throughout, including his self-corrected closing statement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently draws out the patient's existing knowledge, priorities, and own wording — he generates his own rationale, his rehearsed response to pushback, and his refined ending. Safer-use information is offered as a menu and largely elicited rather than lectured.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuine expert-stands-beside stance pervades the session: the patient identifies the priority step, designs the plan details, and evaluates his own rehearsal performance. The clinician's role is facilitator, never director.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Commitment language is strong and sustained ('I'm ready to take these steps,' '10 out of 10'), culminating in a concrete plan with timing and a rehearsed response to anticipated pushback. The rehearsal itself is an excellent technique for consolidating commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and accepted ('not ready to stop, and that's your call') rather than confronted, which prevents defensiveness. The patient's ambivalence is held skillfully, though the deeper sources of sustain talk remain unexplored.
