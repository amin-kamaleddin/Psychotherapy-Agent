# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed respect and nonjudgment, normalizing Daniel's defensiveness and treating his skepticism as reasonable. Warmth remained somewhat procedural and agenda-driven rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Daniel's ambivalence and athlete identity throughout. Reflections sometimes stayed at content level, missing deeper emotional nuances like his discomfort at being flagged and identity threat.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on alcohol and training/recovery with no drift, culminating in a concrete plan (14 alcohol-free nights, tracking, day-7 check-in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Menu-based options and check-ins created genuine two-way decision-making, with the plan co-created. The menus, while collaborative in form, were somewhat clinician-controlled in content, with Daniel selecting among offered paths rather than initiating direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support through flexibility on plan length, slip-normalizing ('treat slips as data, not failure'), and accepting Daniel's adjustments. Occasional directive recommendations (e.g., 'I'd recommend 14 consecutive nights') preceded full negotiation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was drawn from Daniel (curiosity about performance data), but the psychiatrist relied heavily on psychoeducation and structured options rather than open questions into his own reasons and values. No importance/confidence rulers or exploration of what cutting back would mean for him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniel's expertise was repeatedly deferred to, and his contributions substantially shaped the plan (day-7 check-in, foam rolling, no reminders). Some expert drift surfaced in the didactic explanation of alcohol's effects delivered before Daniel shaped it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The experiment framing effectively evoked genuine commitment language ('I can do Sunday,' 'part of me wants to see those pace numbers improve'). Change talk emerged largely through offering pre-set options rather than open evocation like rulers or direct reason-eliciting questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly and then pivoted toward change via the experiment, avoiding arguments and reframing slips as data. Little use of double-sided reflections explicitly pairing sustain talk with change talk, and some sustain statements passed without strategic redirection.
