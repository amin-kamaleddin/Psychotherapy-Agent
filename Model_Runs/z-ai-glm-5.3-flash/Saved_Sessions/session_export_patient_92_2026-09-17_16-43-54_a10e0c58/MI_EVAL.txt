# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 ☑
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone maintained despite Marek's initial defensiveness. Statements like "That makes sense, and I hear why you'd question the big deal" validate without disapproval; no labels or judgments. Closing "I'm glad you're willing to try" is warm. Affirmations are somewhat sparse and formulaic — few explicit affirmations of Marek's strengths (self-awareness about sleep, honesty about confidence 5–5.5).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 ☑
**Comments:** Good reflective listening ("You sound skeptical — you see your drinking as social, limited to weekends...") accurately captures Marek's frame; "You value those nights and feel ambivalent..." acknowledges core ambivalence. Reflections sometimes serve as brief preambles to advice rather than deep emotional exploration — the emotional meaning of weekend drinking (unwinding, belonging, identity) is not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5 ☑
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use from opening to closing. Session progresses logically: clarify concerns → feedback → explore ambivalence → generate options → concrete plan (lower-ABV pilsner, dinner at 7, alternating water, cap, comeback line, Sunday success markers, follow-up). Each turn advances the target behavior without being pushy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 ☑
**Comments:** Session framed as joint work ("explore options together," "We can review..."); final plan co-constructed from Marek's own preferences (his beer, his gym line, paying separately). Power balance tips slightly toward counselor in the feedback/options phases, where information and strategy lists are delivered rapidly with limited back-and-forth.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 ☑
**Comments:** Choice repeatedly affirmed ("Would you be open to... or would you prefer...?"); Marek's "no promises" stance accepted without pressure. Sometimes presents lengthy counselor-generated strategy lists resembling advice-giving rather than choice exploration; ownership of the change is not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 ☑
**Comments:** Motivation effectively drawn from within: open questions about concerns, what the nights mean, confidence scaling, and client-defined success markers evoked genuine change talk ("I'll give it one weekend"). Evocation is intermittent — counselor frequently supplies reasons for change (health risks, sleep, gym recovery); missed chances to explore "maybe if I got sick" or build on the 5–5.5 rating with "why as high as a five?"

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 ☑
**Comments:** Marek positioned as the expert; his contributions (pilsner choice, "early session" line, pay-separately idea) genuinely shape the plan. Falls short of 5 because fairly dense pre-packaged option lists are offered rather than eliciting Marek's own ideas first ("What would cutting back even look like?" answered with a counselor-generated list).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 ☑
**Comments:** Successfully evoked change talk — Marek moved from "It's not a problem" to "Maybe there's a middle ground" and concrete commitments. Sunday-morning success question elicited client-generated criteria; confidence scaling well placed. Change talk not deepened when it appeared — "maybe if I got sick" was not explored, and the 5–5.5 confidence rating received no commitment-strengthening follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5 ☑
**Comments:** Does not argue with or reinforce sustain talk; uses neutral reflections ("I hear surprise..."). However, sustain talk is often left unaddressed rather than actively softened — "Maybe if I got sick..." and "Two or three beers feels a bit thin" receive no double-sided reflections or strategic pivots. Little use of double-sided reflection linking enjoyment of beer to sleep/gym effects — a missed opportunity to resolve ambivalence rather than coexist with it.
