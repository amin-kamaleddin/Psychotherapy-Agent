# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Isabelle's tiredness and thanking her for sharing without pressure or labeling. Warmth is present but somewhat generic, with few individualized affirmations of her strengths (e.g., saxophone, managing IB workload).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers accurate but surface-level, repetitive simple reflections. Misses the emotional depth of Isabelle's disclosure about not wanting to appear as if she is 'struggling,' with no complex reflections about identity, pressure, or pride in coping.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Establishes a clear focus and reaches a concrete behavioral commitment, which is a strength. However, the counselor steers entirely away from the presenting alcohol screening concern without ever touching it, making the direction only partially aligned with the referral reason.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans are co-constructed step by step, with Isabelle's ideas (after-dinner timing, backup cues) directly incorporated. Falls short because the counselor frames most options and structure, while Isabelle largely complies rather than genuinely negotiating direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength: the counselor repeatedly emphasizes 'you're in charge,' offers menus of choices, asks permission at every step, and returns the choice when challenged (e.g., tracking in her head). Isabelle freely selects, modifies, and shapes the plan throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation through the confidence ruler and barriers discussion, but never connects change to Isabelle's own values or reasons. Motivation remains focused on logistics rather than meaning — her concerns about not appearing to struggle and whether change is 'worth anything' go unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly hands expertise to the client and lets Isabelle's contributions shape the plan (Pomodoro choice, after-dinner cue, backup timing). Slightly limited by the counselor supplying most of the plan content (menu, tracking format, check-in structure).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evokes commitment language ('I can start tonight'), a confidence rating of 8/10, and concrete intentions. However, change talk is never deepened — the counselor doesn't explore how success would feel or why energy matters, keeping it tied to a counselor-selected micro-behavior rather than values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Isabelle voices considerable sustain talk ('my grades are good,' 'everyone in IB is tired,' 'too easy to be worth anything'), which is acknowledged but not actively softened. No double-sided reflections, reframes linking small steps to her pride in coping, or pivots amplifying reasons for change.
