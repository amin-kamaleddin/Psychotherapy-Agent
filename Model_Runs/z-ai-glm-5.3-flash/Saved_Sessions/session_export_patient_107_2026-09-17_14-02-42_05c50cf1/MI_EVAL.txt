# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone maintained throughout with warm statements like 'That sounds fair' and 'Nice choice.' However, affirmations felt brief and formulaic rather than reflecting genuine, individualized positive regard for Nathaniel's honesty and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was largely absent; the counselor skipped opportunities to reflect feelings and ambivalence (e.g., 'the coffee works... I don't want to fix what isn't broken') and instead jumped to instructions. Almost no simple or complex reflections of the client's perspective were offered.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed appropriately focused on coffee consumption, but narrowed prematurely to data collection without exploring values, motivations, or goals. The same logging reminders and safety warnings were repeated at least five times rather than advancing motivational movement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Offering a menu of options was a surface-level form of choice, but agenda-setting, framing, and plans were all counselor-driven. The interaction resembled a clinician delivering a protocol rather than two equal partners exploring the issue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine autonomy-affirming statements were present ('no pressure', 'not a commitment to change', 'Which sounds doable?'). However, the rapid expert presentation of options and repeated prescribing of the log plan tilted the interaction toward counselor-directed decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There was essentially no evocation of the client's own motivations; the counselor never asked what mattered to Nathaniel about his health or what he wanted to be different. His spontaneous change talk ('maybe I'd feel better with fewer jitters') went completely unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role with checklists, psychoeducation, and pre-packaged plans. Nathaniel's input shaped the choice of the log, but his ideas were incorporated lukewarmly and the session followed the counselor's agenda and templates.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nathaniel spontaneously offered change talk ('maybe the log wouldn't hurt', 'if the numbers come back ugly, I'll hear you out'), but the counselor never elicited, reflected, or deepened it. No open questions explored his reasons, confidence, or desired outcomes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Frequent sustain talk ('I love my coffee', 'people drink way more than me') was ignored rather than strategically softened. No double-sided reflections or reframing were used, and opportunities to pivot toward the client's own concerns (jitters, BP) were missed.
