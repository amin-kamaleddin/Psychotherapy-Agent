# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and respect for her cultural attachment to wine despite initial defensiveness and minimization. Explicitly affirmed her choices and stated 'I'll support whatever you decide' at the close.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening throughout, capturing her skepticism, cultural connection, and core ambivalence ('You want to keep the ritual and protect the baby'). This reflection directly elicited her vulnerable disclosure about wanting to be a good mom.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, unwavering focus on the target behavior with no topic drift. Every exchange tied back to drinking and guided progressively toward a specific, achievable change plan with follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative—asked permission before sharing medical information and co-constructed the plan around her preferences (brochure, phone follow-up, email delivery, 'a counselor I saw' wording). Her contributions substantially shaped the session, demonstrating true shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support well-matched to this autonomy-driven client—emphasized choice throughout ('you get to choose how'), offered options as a menu rather than directives, and mirrored her 'this is my choice' statement. Patient explicitly noted the effect: 'It's my call and I'm making it—not because anyone made me.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from within via open questions and value-congruent alternatives, letting Isabella articulate her own reasons and plan. Falls short of 5 because confidence and ability talk wasn't deliberately deepened (e.g., exploring what makes her confident she can do the full week).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered power sharing by asking permission before informing and letting her requests (brochure, brand names, script) shape the session. Slightly limited by proactively supplying detailed plans and scripts rather than leaving more room for her ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked strong change talk through open questions and value-congruent options ('I want to be a good mom,' 'Guess I'm actually doing this'). Not a 5 because commitment language wasn't deepened—after her week-long commitment, the counselor moved quickly to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided amplifying sustain talk—responded to minimization with brief factual information rather than debating, then pivoted to values and alternatives. Didn't use explicit double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence, and ritual framing was occasionally validated in ways that could subtly reinforce sustain elements.
