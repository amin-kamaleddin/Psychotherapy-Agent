# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with no judgment even when the patient minimizes his cholesterol concern. Frequent affirmations ('Nice concrete plan,' 'You've got this') reinforced the accepting tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening, including strong complex reflections of ambivalence and his core value of protecting an active retirement. Fell short of 5 because emotional nuances—fear of failing again and the weight of entrenched habits—were never explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on cholesterol management across the entire session with no topic drift. Every turn advanced the agenda logically toward a concrete plan and follow-up check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuine joint endeavor—the plan (6:30 walk, Friday smaller patty, mall backup) was co-constructed from Richard's own ideas. The collaborative stance took root in the patient, who said 'if something flopped, we tweak it.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed patient choice with no pressure or imposed advice. Not a 5 due to occasional expectant prompts ('Are you ready to start tonight?') and pre-supplied option menus that subtly steered rather than leaving choices fully open.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicited the patient's own motivations (beach mornings, trails, keeping up with his wife), which carried the session. Fell short of 5 because evocation was channeled more into logistical confidence-building than deeper exploration of meaning and emotion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed that expertise resides with the client, letting him choose realistic options and define his own success metrics—the patient explicitly noticed this ('You let me figure out my own reasons'). Not a 5 because the counselor at times supplied the menu of solutions rather than drawing them out first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, sustained evocation using importance/confidence rulers and refinement questions, with confidence rising from 5 to 8 and culminating in 'Let's do it.' Change talk was then reinforced through reflections and celebratory summaries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk, responding to minimization with values-based reflections rather than argument. However, obstacle-focused questions kept attention on barriers longer than necessary, and strategic double-sided reflections were underused.
