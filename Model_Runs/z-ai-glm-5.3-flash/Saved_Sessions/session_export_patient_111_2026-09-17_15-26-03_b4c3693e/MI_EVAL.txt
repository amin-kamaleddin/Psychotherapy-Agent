# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone even when the patient was defensive and dismissive, with affirmations conveying positive regard. However, warmth felt somewhat procedural and affirmations were brief and formulaic rather than deeply personalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered accurate complex reflections capturing the patient's ambivalence, such as being torn between not wanting to lose his wife and his attachment to smoking routines. Fell short of full marks by rarely exploring the deeper emotional layer—his father's death and the cough incident—moving quickly to option-giving instead.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on tobacco use throughout despite the patient's attempts to redirect toward his wife's nagging. The session progressed logically to a concrete, measurable goal: skip car cigarettes for three days with a delay strategy and a slip plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed decisions as shared by offering structured choices and building the plan from the patient's own ideas, which clearly shaped the final plan. The craving-script segment was delivered prescriptively rather than co-constructed, and the patient noted it 'sounds a bit goofy,' indicating that part wasn't fully negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength: repeatedly and explicitly reinforced the patient's control ('You're in charge of this hour') and offered menu options rather than prescriptions. Accepted the patient's refusal to promise more than three days without pressure, and even conditioned risk education on his consent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from the patient through open and scaling questions, eliciting client-generated reasoning and genuine change talk. Missed opportunities included not exploring what quitting might mean to him personally beyond appeasing his wife, and not following up on 'the worries part, maybe a little.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Stated 'You're in charge of this hour' and asked for preferences, but the patient had to push back multiple times before his priorities were incorporated. The counselor also shifted to a directive, advice-giving mode in the craving-script segment, temporarily assuming the expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited substantial change talk, including 'maybe I could cut out the ones in between,' a commitment to starting tomorrow, and importance/confidence ratings. Missed the chance to deepen the most powerful change talk—'I just don't want to lose her'—by pivoting quickly to menu options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided amplifying sustain talk, reflecting without reinforcing and never lecturing on risks even when invited. Occasionally restated sustain talk (e.g., 'smoking soothes you') without a double-sided reframe pivoting toward the patient's own change reasons.
