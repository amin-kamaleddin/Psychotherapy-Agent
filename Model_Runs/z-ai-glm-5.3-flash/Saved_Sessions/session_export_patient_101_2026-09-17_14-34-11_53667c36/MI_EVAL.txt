# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with normalizing statements ('slips are useful information, not failure,' 'you're handling a lot'). However, affirmations are somewhat generic and warmth is diluted by rapid-fire clinical instruction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections, but the session quickly shifts to information-delivery mode. Emotionally loaded material (fear of becoming her father, self-doubt, shame about peer pressure) is met with advice and checklists rather than deepened reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the linked targets of alcohol use and contraceptive reliability throughout the session. Slight deduction because the focus stays on safety logistics and action steps with little attention to the motivational core of confidence around drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative moments exist (adjusting two alcohol-free nights to one, drafting the roommate text), but the power balance tilts clinician-expert: the psychiatrist supplies nearly all strategies and Mei largely approves or edits them. No exploration of what Mei thinks would work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Steps framed as choices, plans normalized as modifiable ('one alcohol-free night is a good, realistic start'), and imperfection explicitly permitted. Deduction for frequent prescriptive instructions and options presented as menus to accept rather than decisions Mei generates.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies almost entirely on providing information and tools rather than drawing out motivation. Mei's rich intrinsic motivators ('I don't want to turn into my dad') go unexplored, with no scaling questions or questions about her own ideas for managing peer pressure and cravings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine but uneven: the counselor invites input and incorporates Mei's suggestions, but the session is dominated by clinician-generated plans and menus, with Mei reacting rather than co-creating. Her expertise on her own triggers and social context is never explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nearly all change talk is client-initiated and passively received, never elicited or amplified. No open questions about Mei's reasons, hopes, or vision of success; the counselor instead generates limits, scripts, and checklists.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Responds supportively to doubt by normalizing and scaling the plan down, and never reinforces sustain talk. But there's little strategic work: no double-sided reflections, no exploring the ambivalence, and Mei's fear of failure and peer pressure are bypassed with practical tips.
