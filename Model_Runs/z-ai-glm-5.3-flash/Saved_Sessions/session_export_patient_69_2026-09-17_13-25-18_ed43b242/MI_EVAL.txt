# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with unconditional positive regard for Kieran's apathy. However, warmth feels somewhat generic, and the counselor never directly affirms Kieran's strengths (e.g., his honesty with the job guy or decision to send the text).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening that accurately tracks Kieran's resignation and demoralization ('You're showing up but not being seen'). Reflections are sometimes brief and formulaic ('I hear you'), and deeper emotional exploration is touched rather than fully developed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session anchored to Kieran's frustration with the compliance loop and converted vague venting into a concrete, dated action. Repeated redundant safety re-checks sapped momentum, and the session tilted toward task completion over deeper motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely an equal exchange: counselor solicits preferences and co-constructs a practical text and reminder plan shaped by Kieran's choices. Would be stronger if Kieran had drafted the message in his own words or jointly brainstormed follow-up strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choice through option menus and explicit non-directive statements ('I won't try to fix it unless you ask'). Falls short of top marks because menus pre-define options rather than trusting Kieran to generate his own path, and autonomy support stays implicit rather than explicitly named.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions successfully elicited Kieran's story about the job guy and his desire for follow-through, but motivation was more often supplied by the counselor through scripts and menus than drawn out from Kieran. No evocative questions about his hopes, values, reasons, or confidence were asked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly solicited Kieran's input and preferences, and Kieran himself noted 'You're the first one who's asked what I want'. Expertise sometimes shifted toward counselor-drafted scripts and checklists rather than Kieran's own words and solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively moved Kieran from 'whatever, get it over with' to genuine first steps of change talk ('I could send that text', 'I'll do that. Small steps'). Reliance on menus and drafted scripts rather than open questions about Kieran's own reasons or confidence limits the depth of evoked commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kieran produced substantial sustain talk ('what's the point', 'probably just another thing that goes nowhere'), which the counselor neither amplified nor actively softened. Double-sided reflections, affirmations, or gentle pivots were mostly absent, though the shift to concrete action helped implicitly.
