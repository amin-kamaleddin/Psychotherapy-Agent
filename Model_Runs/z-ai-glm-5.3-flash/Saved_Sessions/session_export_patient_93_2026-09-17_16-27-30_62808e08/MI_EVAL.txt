# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental, and respectful throughout, with affirmation of her plan and acceptance of her preferences without pushback. Affirmations could be more individualized and emotionally attuned to deepen the score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures both sides of Margaret's ambivalence and her core values. Reflections stay mostly cognitive and miss opportunities to explore the emotional weight of the fall, fear of losing independence, and defensiveness about her son's nagging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol-related fall risk with no topic drift. The session progresses steadily from exploration to a concrete, behaviorally defined plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the session as joint exploration and actively solicits Margaret's input, which substantially shapes the final plan. The option menu and two-week structure were largely counselor-generated, limiting full co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated permission-seeking, offered choices, and no pushing when Margaret refused practice or quitting entirely. Margaret's closing remark confirms she experienced genuine choice and ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws motivation from Margaret's own values and words, yielding her reasons for change and commitment language. Could deepen by exploring her own hopes beyond her son's approval or eliciting confidence/importance scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently asks permission, invites Margaret's expertise, and incorporates her contributions immediately. The option menu and plan structure were more counselor-generated than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively links drinking to Margaret's stated values of independence and safety, eliciting clear change talk and commitment language ('I'll start Thursday,' 'one glass and a taxi'). The two-week plan was offered somewhat prescriptively rather than letting Margaret generate it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret's frequent minimization ('seven drinks a week wasn't much') was reflected neutrally but never strategically softened. Lacked double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk or reframes to actively manage it.
