# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with consistent reassurance of support regardless of Elena's choice. Affirmations were somewhat generic ('That's great to hear') rather than specific to her strengths like her proactive research.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were largely content-level summaries rather than deeper emotional reflections. Vulnerable moments — calling in a panic, feeling like a 'nagging mom,' expecting to say no again — were answered informationally rather than with empathic reflection of the underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior with a logical progression to a concrete action plan (schedule, handout, scripts, follow-up). Direction was often achieved through counselor-driven structure and information delivery rather than collaborative goal clarification, and some turns drifted into logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative gestures were present (permission-seeking, offering options, co-developing the handout and scripts), but large blocks of expert-delivered content were presented didactically. Power-sharing was inconsistent, with the counselor acting as information authority for much of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's strongest element — choice was explicitly reinforced at every step ('Whatever you choose, I'll support you'), with options offered throughout and no pressure even when Elena signaled readiness. Elena consistently retained ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was created primarily through education rather than drawn from the client; the pivotal shift followed counselor-supplied risk data. Few evocative questions were asked after the opening, and Elena's own values and reasons for change were never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena was consistently positioned as the decision-maker, and her requests genuinely shaped the session agenda. The counselor's extensive monologue-style information delivery kept Elena in a largely receiving role at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged ('I'm starting to think this is worth doing,' 'I think we can do this') but was not deliberately evoked or deepened. Missed opportunities included exploring what shifted Elena from 'ready to say no' and asking her to rate her confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged nonjudgmentally without argument or amplification. However, no double-sided reflections or strategic use of Elena's own framing were employed — ambivalence was resolved mainly through information provision rather than MI technique.
