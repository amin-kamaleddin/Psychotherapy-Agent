# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with explicit reassurance when Elena feared judgment; no labels even when disclosure of inconsistent protective behaviors occurred. However, affirmations were formulaic and lacked personalized recognition of Elena's specific strengths like her honesty and self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing Elena's relief and ambivalence about the pregnancy scare. Empathy thinned during planning, when emotional disclosures (fear of partner pushback, worry about 'chickening out') were met with practical scripts rather than deeper reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior of reducing pregnancy risk through contraception, with every turn connected to the goal and clear acceptance of Elena's boundaries without losing momentum. Session concluded with a specific, time-bound plan including ordering date, scheduled text, reminder, and follow-up appointment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Elena selecting the step, message tone, follow-up style, and editing scripts verbatim into the final plan. Frequent multi-option menus made interactions feel like guided menu selection rather than open brainstorming, with Elena's ideas often chosen from counselor-generated choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of Elena's ownership of decisions, honoring her 'nothing permanent today' request and accepting deferrals without any pressure. Repeated choice points and 'start small' framing reinforced that Elena controlled the pace throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective questions elicited Elena's own reasons and self-assessment, producing strong self-generated motivational statements including 'I don't want to sit in a bathroom waiting for a test ever again.' Motivation was sometimes channeled too quickly into logistics rather than amplified through exploration of her values around safety and self-advocacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena was consistently treated as the expert on her own life, with repeated fit-checking and genuine incorporation of her choices into the plan. The counselor sometimes front-loaded multiple suggestions in a single turn, tilting toward advice-giving rather than drawing ideas from Elena first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Spontaneous change talk was well-reflected and anchored to action, with readiness rulers and open questions producing commitments and culminating in Elena's 'I actually think I can.' Missed opportunities to deepen, such as exploring what succeeding would mean to Elena or elaborating on her confidence statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena's resistance was acknowledged respectfully with appropriate pivoting to smaller steps, but no double-sided reflections linked sustain talk to change motivation. Sustain talk about partner dynamics and minimization ('it's worked so far') was met with problem-solving rather than reflections developing discrepancy with 'that was terrifying.'
