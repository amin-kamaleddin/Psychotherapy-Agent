# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout with validation of fears and no labeling of her desire to lose weight. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and repetitive, keeping this from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent reflective listening with complex reflections that captured Yasmin's ambivalence well. Deeper emotional nuance, such as the shame of the repeated dieting cycle, was only briefly acknowledged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with each turn tying back to concrete change and a specific week plan. Questions progressively clarified goals and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were co-constructed step by step with the counselor inviting input before suggesting. Occasionally introduced pre-packaged solution lists rather than eliciting all ideas from the client first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced ownership by offering advice as options, celebrating her wording edits, and framing the work as her own experiment. She controls the change process throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out intrinsic motivations like feeling strong and mental quiet through open questions. Motivation was elicited more around action planning than the core weight-loss ambivalence, which was never fully explored from her perspective.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Yasmin as the expert, with her contributions substantially shaping the plan and her wording adopted. Some advice-giving (anti-scale strategy lists) was counselor-driven rather than fully elicited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective open questions and a well-used confidence scaling question produced strong change talk and commitment ('I'm in. Tuesday's my first go'). Change talk was sometimes rushed past into planning rather than deepened with reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally but not systematically redirected, with no double-sided reflections pairing it with change talk. The counselor leaned toward practical problem-solving rather than strategically reframing the 'what if I gain' fear.
