# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, affirming Naomi's resilience and normalizing her self-criticism. However, praise is somewhat generic and formulaic, lacking deeper individualized validation of the emotional toll of her years of avoidance and dread.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections appear early on, including validating her freeze response, but reflections are brief and quickly pivot to questions or advice. The psychiatrist does not deeply explore the emotional core of Naomi's experience—her shame, fear of being 'dumb,' or her surprise at feeling more okay—leaving empathy adequate but inconsistent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains excellent, consistent focus on managing anxiety symptoms during photography jobs, with every turn tied back to the event shoot. The session moves progressively toward a concrete, achievable plan with no drift, culminating in Naomi's realization that it's 'less of a giant scary blob and more of a... plan.'

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Joint practice, permission-seeking, and tailoring scripts to Naomi's voice show collaborative elements, but long counselor monologues with lists of suggestions shift power toward the expert. Naomi's ideas are incorporated as refinements to the counselor's proposals rather than generators of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist regularly offers meaningful choices and frames success on Naomi's terms ('I did 2 pauses' rather than 'feel perfect'), with Naomi freely shaping the plan. It falls slightly short of a 5 because advice is dense and prescriptive at times, with decision points quickly followed by more suggestions rather than pauses for her ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine evocative moves occur, including the importance rating, exploring what was different at a shoot where she felt in control, and drawing out her own compassion phrase. However, much of the session involves the counselor supplying strategies, and Naomi's most motivating moments go unelicited and unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and offering choices ('Which script feels more like you?') demonstrate genuine collaboration in spots, but the session becomes technique-driven with the counselor supplying most of the content. This positions the psychiatrist as the expert on solutions rather than drawing solutions from Naomi's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist successfully evokes change talk through the importance scale and asking about a shoot where Naomi felt in control. However, this change talk is rarely deepened with reflective reinforcement—her closing statement about dreading events is never reflected back, and commitment language is not explicitly elicited beyond agreeing to try two pauses.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist briefly acknowledges Naomi's doubts and responds with practical troubleshooting, which is helpful. However, softening relies on problem-solving and reassurance rather than double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to her hopeful statements, missing the opportunity to explore her 'maybe that's me making excuses' comment.
