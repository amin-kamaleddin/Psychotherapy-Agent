# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong acceptance throughout: normalizes ambivalence without judgment, affirms the patient's identity struggle ('the man sitting here today') and respects his wish to not make his partner a babysitter.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflective listening, including reflections of deeper emotion ('you loved him and feared becoming him, and you're carrying both at once'). The patient repeatedly feels understood and builds on reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear therapeutic direction toward relapse prevention and coping with stress without alcohol, pursued flexibly via open questions that the patient's own answers keep advancing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan ('tight jaw, walk, before the drink') is explicitly elicited from and credited to the patient ('a plan that came from you, not from me'); ideas are offered as questions for joint exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices and self-direction are emphasized throughout — 'in your own way', respecting that change is 'on him, mostly' — with no advice-giving or pressure; the clinician reinforces his self-generated strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently evoke the patient's own reasons, strengths, and solutions (walking, talking, early warning signs, what's at stake with his partner), drawing out commitment language naturally.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** True partnership tone: the clinician follows the patient's lead, frames themselves as curious rather than expert, and invites joint problem-solving including involving the partner as a teammate.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is rich and clinician-elicited: 'I'm trying to be different', 'I don't want to make that trade again', 'That's my way, I reckon' — all emerging from skillful evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (the 'old voice' about drinking, 'I should handle it myself') is met with non-confrontational double-sided reflections and reframed into manageable problem-solving rather than argued against.
