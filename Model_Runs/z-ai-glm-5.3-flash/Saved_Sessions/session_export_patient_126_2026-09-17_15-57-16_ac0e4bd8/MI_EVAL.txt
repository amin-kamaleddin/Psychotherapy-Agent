# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, affirmed autonomy, and addressed the confidentiality concern transparently. A 5 would require more individualized affirmations of the patient's strengths rather than generic 'good choice' statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered accurate reflections of content but rarely touched the emotional layer (mild defensiveness, distrust of health professionals, family responsibilities). Also repeated the doctor question after the patient had already answered, indicating imperfect listening tracking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior from early on, and every exchange advanced a concrete plan ending in a specific, measurable commitment. Some redundancy (repeated doctor question, re-offered reminder options) slightly diluted momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint and co-constructed the plan from the patient's own ideas, which genuinely shaped the session. Collaboration was somewhat procedural—supplying menus and rulers rather than open joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength: repeatedly and explicitly reinforced choice, offered options rather than prescriptions, accepted 'no promises,' and honored the patient's 'facts, not lecture' framing. No coercive or pressuring language appeared.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Operated from the premise that motivation resides in the client, using open evocative questions and rulers to elicit the patient's own reasons and conditions for change. Did not explore deeper values (fatherhood, prior success quitting smoking) that could connect change to core values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed that expertise resides with the client—menu-based choices, patient-selected tracking method, start date, and follow-up time. Falls short of 5 because the counselor still drove the structure (rulers inserted rapidly) rather than fully negotiating the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked early change talk via a hypothetical question, and readiness/confidence rulers drew out commitment language ('I could give it a go,' 'I'll set the alarm for Thursday'). Missed opportunities to deepen commitment language and reflect the change talk back to strengthen it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with minimization and pivoted to feasibility questions, but sometimes restated sustain talk extensively in reflections without pairing it with the change side (no double-sided reflections used). Re-questioning about the doctor's warnings invited more sustain talk.
