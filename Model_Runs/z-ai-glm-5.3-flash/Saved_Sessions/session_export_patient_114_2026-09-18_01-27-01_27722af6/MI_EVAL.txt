# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with no labeling or moralizing, validating Ryan's defensiveness and affirming his ideas. To reach 5, warmth would need deeper individualized affirmation of his worth and normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures both sides of Ryan's ambivalence, including the core conflict between self-care and belonging. Missed deeper emotional reflection on the shame/pride material, moving quickly to action-oriented questions instead.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, progressive focus on the drinking behavior, with each exchange building from understanding the incident to concrete commitment. Ends at a natural action-planning point with a self-generated next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine equal-partner exchange — every plan element originates with Ryan and the clinician builds on his answers co-creatively. No expert-driven directives at any point.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforces Ryan's ownership ('that's a smart move, and it's yours') with no advice imposed. Subtle steering in some questions and no explicit 'it's your call' statement kept it from a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully draws out Ryan's own values, problem recognition, strategies, and commitment rather than supplying reasons to change. Ryan's fully articulated plan demonstrates motivation genuinely evoked, not installed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Ryan as the expert, and the plan evolves entirely from his ideas. Agenda-setting is somewhat clinician-guided, with the clinician steering which strategies to develop.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategically sequenced open questions escalate Ryan's change talk from tentative ('something's gotta change') to concrete commitment ('I could literally text them this week'). Consistently reinforces agency by highlighting that Ryan figured things out himself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles substantial sustain talk well with double-sided reflections and effective pivots back to change-oriented questions. Occasionally briefly amplifies sustain talk (e.g., inviting discussion of peer pushback) before redirecting.
