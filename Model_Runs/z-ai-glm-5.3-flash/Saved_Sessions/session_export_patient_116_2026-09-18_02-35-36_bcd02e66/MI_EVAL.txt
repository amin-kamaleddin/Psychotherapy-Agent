# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental stance with strong validation of the client's disclosures and non-defensive handling of sharp pushback. Only slight limitation is repeated verbatim affirmations ('That's not stupid at all'), which reduce authenticity slightly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections that often capture deeper identity and emotional content, such as the client's fear of hope and grief over his father. Some reflections feel slightly interpretive, and the 'stuck between two dead men' moment deserved deeper emotional exploration before pivoting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on medication nonadherence throughout, with even the emotionally deep material used to illuminate the medication struggle. The session progresses logically from ambivalence toward conditional willingness to investigate.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided interaction—the client's contributions demonstrably shape the session, and tasks are framed as joint ('investigate what went wrong'). Only minor point is that the collaboration had not yet fully matured into a co-constructed plan by the transcript's end.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with explicit disclaimers of coercion ('I'm not here to argue you into taking them'), to which the client visibly responds ('you're the first one who didn't just lecture me'). A full 5 would require more overt client ownership of a chosen direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** All key motivational material comes from the client himself, drawn out through strategic questions like the five-year vision. Missing is evocation of confidence/ability talk, and the client's commitment language remains tentative.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions the client as the expert on his own life and frames contributions as curiosity rather than authority. Only limitation is that the counselor still steers the agenda fairly tightly, so client influence doesn't fully determine session structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked pivotal change talk, including the 'fun uncle' vision and 'maybe I don't want to end up like him either.' The counselor could have anchored this further with direct confidence or importance questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by validating the reasons behind resistance and pivoting toward change rather than arguing. Sustain talk occasionally received equal airtime to change talk without an immediate tilt.
