# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth with no judgment or moralizing despite the patient's defensiveness. Withheld from 5 because some affirmations feel slightly formulaic and normalization of typical teen experimentation could deepen unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening, including complex double-sided reflections capturing his sense of unfairness and social belonging. Emotional layers around the embarrassment of the couch incident could have been reflected more explicitly before pivoting to questions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use at parties without appearing controlling, with summaries keeping momentum. Slight drift early on and tolerance of repeated minimization rounds before steering back prevent a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative: agenda is co-shaped, check-in plan proposed as a question, and the patient's ideas drive interventions. Occasional decisive steering without checking in and little explicit negotiation of the session agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with zero prescriptive advice and the choice explicitly framed as the patient's ('you get to decide your pace'). The patient's increasingly strong ownership statements ('that's for me, not for you') directly evidence the clinician's success.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates on the premise that motivation lives within the patient, drawing out his own reputation concerns, discomfort with loss of control, and self-directed reasons. His closing 'It's my plan, my call' confirms the evocative success.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently locates expertise within the patient ('you know these parties better than I ever will'), and every plan element originates from Milan. Exemplary power sharing, with the closing summary explicitly crediting the patient's authorship.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematic, skillful evocation progresses from the first desire statement to progressively stronger commitment language through scaled, client-generated questions. Culminates in a fully client-owned plan—a textbook change talk cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk with neutral acceptance followed by redirection, never argument, and closing summaries pair acknowledged ambivalence with the emerging plan. Some sustain talk passes without strategic pivots, and opportunities to reframe minimization were not fully leveraged.
