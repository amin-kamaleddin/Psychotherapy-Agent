# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard despite sustained defensiveness and blame-externalizing. No judgment anywhere in the session; validation of frustration ("If I were in your seat, I'd probably feel the same way") and explicit removal of moral evaluation ("No judgment from me on how you and your friends do Saturday"). Affirmations are genuine and specific.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Reflective listening goes well beyond parroting — captures content and emotional undercurrent (e.g., reframing FOMO as "it's about belonging"). Complex reflections land accurately enough that the client keeps building on them, evidenced by escalating self-disclosure from "drop it" to "I hadn't really thought about it like that until right now."

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive arc: validating the unfairness frame → workable goal within it → identifying tipping point → extracting strengths → locating the decision moment → concrete plan with timeline and social support. Direction is firm but invisible; the client never feels steered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative framing ("help me think this through with you"). The psychiatrist explicitly positions himself as the junior expert on the client's life; plan, stake, ally, and script all originate from the client. Converts an adversarial conduct-referral dynamic into joint problem-solving, which the client acknowledges ("sitting here beat doing nothing").

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Explicit throughout: "totally your call," "that part's yours," "not to change your weekends." No advice given at any point — even the one-minute pause strategy is the client's own idea. Final summary reinforces ownership and gracefully accepts the face-saving disclaimer rather than claiming credit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn entirely from within the client: he names his own tipping point, his own discrepancy ("my friends aren't on probation — I am"), his own motivation, plan, and script. The client's closing "it's not like you changed me" is, from an MI standpoint, the ideal outcome — self-attribution of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Consistently positions the client as the expert ("you know your weekends better than I ever will"). Every plan element originates from the client; questions actively locate expertise in him. "Nobody hands that back" explicitly attributes ownership of the change — notable given a client who arrived externalizing all responsibility.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Strongest dimension. Client-expertise questions systematically evoke change talk, producing the client's own articulation of consequences, commitment-adjacent language ("I could actually do that"), a specific plan, timeline (tonight), and script. Final statement — "I just don't want to be back in this office" — is genuine self-generated motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled well without reinforcement; blame-externalizing met with validating reflections then pivoted toward agency. The "drinking's not a problem" framing is absorbed rather than challenged — appropriate for pre-contemplation. Only deduction: the fairness grievance is acknowledged but never explicitly neutralized through a double-sided reflection; the redirection is effective but implicit.

---
**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.9/5**

Near-model MI practice with a pre-contemplative, blame-externalizing client — one of the most difficult MI presentations. Recommended refinements: (1) make pivots away from sustain talk more explicit via double-sided reflections; (2) consider brief confidence/readiness ratings to consolidate commitment; (3) invite the client to state his plan aloud one final time to strengthen commitment language.
