# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, responding to minimization with respect for her choice rather than confrontation. The patient's closing remarks ('I came in thinking you'd lecture me') confirm she felt fully accepted and non-judged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately captured her ambivalence about drinking and the pub nights. However, the counselor missed chances to explicitly reflect the emotional weight of the fall and her irritation at her son's hovering.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol reduction with no topic drift, progressing logically from exploration to a specific, measurable plan. Every element—strategies, barriers, supports, follow-up—tied back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The final plan was assembled entirely from the patient's own selections and adaptations, with the counselor asking rather than telling. Her surprise that the session was 'more useful than I expected' reflects the palpable non-hierarchical stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported through permission-seeking, framing options as her choices, and accepting her decisions (e.g., declining the phone reminder and her son's check-ins) without pushback. Her plan and framing ('I'll blame you') confirmed full ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was effectively drawn from within via open questions, confidence rulers, and eliciting her own reasons and solutions. Evocation could be deeper—no importance ruler was used to develop her 'why,' and stacked multiple questions reduced space for her to elaborate.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly solicited her input and offered options as a menu, with her choices directly shaping the plan. Slightly short of a 5 because the option list was presented somewhat didactically before she had fully articulated her own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked through open questions and confidence rulers that moved the patient from 6–7 to 8, plus barrier planning that elicited practical commitments (water pacing, refusal line, start date). Her closing commitment to report honestly Monday reflects successfully cultivated change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing with sustain talk, instead using double-sided reflections to pivot toward change (e.g., 'keep the social time but reduce risk'). Some minimization ('maybe six on weekends') passed without a double-sided reflection that could have deepened ambivalence resolution.
