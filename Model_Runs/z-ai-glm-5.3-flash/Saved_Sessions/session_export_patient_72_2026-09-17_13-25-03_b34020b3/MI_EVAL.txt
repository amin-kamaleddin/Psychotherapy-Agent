# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive tone with no judgment or labeling. Warmth is somewhat formulaic, with little deep individualized affirmation beyond generic encouragement like 'you've got this.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers one decent reflective opening but quickly shifts to problem-solving. Meaningful emotional content (feeling stuck, past sales pressure, renewed hope) goes largely unreflected, with no complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains an unwavering focus on re-establishing exercise from the first exchange through scheduling, contingency plans, and next-session follow-up. The target behavior never drifts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration: the counselor invites input and Leila's choices shape the plan. However, the rhythm is therapist-offers-solution, client-agrees, with ideas rarely co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks Leila which activity to prioritize and what she can realistically commit to, offering options rather than commands. Rapid-fire advice on calendars, alarms, and scripts subtly narrows her choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Treats expertise as residing in techniques rather than in the client. Leila's own motivations (strength, energy, mood) are stated by her but never elicited or developed; motivation is assumed rather than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Assumes the expert role for most of the session, delivering prescriptive plans and ready-made questions and scripts. Though Leila's contributions are incorporated, the flow is largely clinician-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Leila volunteers abundant change talk ('I'm ready,' 10/10 confidence) but the counselor rarely evokes or deepens it. No reflections of her desire, ability, or commitment language, and no exploration of her high confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Largely ignores rather than softens sustain talk, responding to barriers (weather bail-outs, home distractions, sales pressure) with tactical workarounds instead of acknowledging ambivalence. No double-sided reflections used.
