# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor communicates consistent respect and non-judgment ('no lectures, no judgment') and affirms Isabelle's courage in sharing. Warmth occasionally feels formulaic, and affirmations of her worth independent of the screening context are limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures Isabelle's ambivalence and irritation with adults, including complex double-sided reflections. Early reflections of her defensiveness were lighter, and the emotional weight of her fear of losing the friendship was not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains focus well within the client-led conversation, progressing from agenda-setting to a specific plan. However, the actual referral concern—Isabelle's own alcohol-risk screening—was never addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is framed as joint work from the start, with every direction following the client's lead and the counselor positioning herself as curious rather than expert. The plan for talking with her friend was fully co-constructed from Isabelle's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is exemplary—'Your call entirely,' offering choice throughout, and apologizing when questions felt like pressure. Isabelle explicitly noted that being asked what she wants feels novel, evidencing genuine experienced choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently elicits motivation from within, generating client-produced words, plans, and self-insight. Motivation centers on the friend's drinking rather than Isabelle's own risk behavior, and her own confidence or importance for personal change was not explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly conveys that expertise resides in Isabelle and credits her insights as originating from her ('That came from you, not a checklist'). The client's contributions demonstrably shaped the entire session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation produced meaningful change language ('I don't want to just... watch,' 'it feels less impossible now') and concrete plans. Change talk centers on helping a friend rather than Isabelle's own behavior, and was not consolidated into commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through double-sided reflections rather than argument, and is redirected effectively. The counselor never addresses Isabelle's own normalizing statements ('that's just normal stuff at my age'), leaving that sustain talk untouched.
