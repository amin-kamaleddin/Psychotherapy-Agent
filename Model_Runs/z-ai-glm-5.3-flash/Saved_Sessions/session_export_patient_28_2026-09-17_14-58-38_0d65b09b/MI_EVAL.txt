# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental tone with no disapproval of the patient's inactivity. Affirmations were somewhat generic and repeated ('That's solid,' 'That's a clear, doable plan'), lacking deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including nuanced captures of the patient's ambivalence and discouragement about small efforts compared to past routines. The emotional weight of 'one miss and I'd quit' was not deeply explored, and questions were sometimes stacked.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Excellent, clearly directed focus on re-establishing exercise, progressing strategically from barriers to a small step, confidence rating, specific days, contingency planning, and role-play. Occasional bundling of multiple topics in a single turn slightly diluted the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed (his cue, his days, his backup), and role-playing the invite was a collaborative move. Some steering occurred through prescriptive menus rather than letting the patient fully generate elements himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently reinforced with choices offered throughout ('I'm here to support whatever you choose'), and the patient explicitly recognized it: 'Feels more like I actually decided something myself... That's new.' Textbook autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was effectively drawn from within via confidence rulers and eliciting the patient's own solutions, culminating in his self-generated insight that 'I made it too big.' Deeper values and the meaning of his past fitness identity were not explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient was consistently positioned as the expert ('What do you think got in the way?', 'Which feels most doable?'), with collaborative role-play of the wife invitation. Rapid-fire multiple questions occasionally limited the patient's role in shaping the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk using a small-step question, a confidence ruler, and specific day commitments; the patient's confidence rose from 4–5 to 7 with self-motivating statements. Missed opportunities to explicitly reinforce or deepen these statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient's dismissal of walks as 'pointless' was reframed as 'least like a compromise and most doable' rather than argued with. Sustain talk (tiredness, schedule, kids) was sometimes restated extensively without pivoting sharply toward change.
