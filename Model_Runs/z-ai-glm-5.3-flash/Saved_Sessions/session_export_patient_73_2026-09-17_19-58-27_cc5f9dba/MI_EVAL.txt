# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and nonjudgment throughout ('That fear sits right alongside... Both feelings are real'), with strong affirmations of Linda's honesty and courage. Full marks held back only because explicit unconditional positive regard is warm but occasionally interwoven with directional framing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly accurate, complex, dual-sided reflections capturing both fear for health and attachment to smoking identity ('Both feelings are real, and both make sense'). Empathy runs deep across the session, including reflecting self-doubt and weariness without judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on smoking as the target behavior while guiding progressively toward reasons, readiness, and concrete first steps. Each response advances the change agenda without ever feeling forced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda's own ideas (telling Sandra, the deadline, the money motivation) are consistently reflected back and built upon, making the plan genuinely hers. Slightly below top marks because the clinician sometimes frames next questions quite actively rather than jointly negotiating the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong ownership language ('a plan with your own hands', 'that'll be mine regardless') and no pressure or lecturing; Linda explicitly notes she isn't being lectured. Falls just short of 5 as there is little explicit statement that the choice and pace are entirely hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocative questions drew out deep intrinsic motivations (her mother's death, the eleven years, proving something to herself). A 5 would require more deliberate exploration of the client's own confidence and commitment rather than primarily reflecting what emerged.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda is positioned as the expert: her past attempts are framed as accumulated knowledge, and her plan is endorsed as self-generated. Room for 5 is limited by occasional expert framing such as 'it's exactly what I'm here for' steering toward specific actions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled open questions ('what might need to be different?', 'which step feels like the right place to start?') elicited rich change talk including commitments ('I could do that', 'I'll aim to see her this week'). Missing full marks because emerging change talk was sometimes answered with reflections rather than deepened through targeted evocation of commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (past failures, social smoking, self-doubt) is consistently acknowledged and honored within double-sided reflections without reinforcement, then gently reframed ('that's someone quietly preparing'). Reframing occasionally leads rather than letting Linda resolve ambivalence herself, keeping it from a 5.
