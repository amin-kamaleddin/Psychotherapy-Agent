# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard throughout, with no judgment of Adrian's use and explicit affirmations like 'It takes guts to say that out loud.' Even anticipated relapse was met with acceptance rather than disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that tracked both content and emotion, such as decoding 'not that serious, right?' as permission-seeking and mirroring the weight of secrecy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on meth use and related risks from the start, with each exchange building toward Adrian's self-identified goals and a concrete commitment window (Saturday).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operated between equals with zero unsolicited advice; every plan originated from Adrian, positioned as the problem-solver.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support ('it's yours, not anyone else's prescription') and patient acceptance of non-commitment without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn almost entirely from Adrian via strategic questions like the six-month vision, moving him from 'I've got it under control' to 'Saturday. Maybe.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in the client, and Adrian's contributions demonstrably shaped the session's focus, such as the realization about telling Dave.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation and reinforcement of Adrian's own change language ('you're the one who said it, not me'), culminating in his self-generated commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was neither argued with nor endorsed, instead met with double-sided reflections and respectful acknowledgment that kept defensiveness low.
