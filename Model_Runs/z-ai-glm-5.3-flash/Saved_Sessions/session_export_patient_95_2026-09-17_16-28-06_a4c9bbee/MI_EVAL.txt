# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance even when Maria expressed irritation, validating her feelings ('That's real, not pathetic') and accepting her limits without pushing. Falls short of 5 due to occasionally formulaic warmth and safety questioning delivered in an impersonal checklist style.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, accurately capturing Maria's bodily and mental experience ('underwater, chest-heavy, the mind repeating what's the point'). Some reflections were brief and the counselor moved to questions too quickly, missing chances to sit longer with her despair about good moments.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on suicidal ideation, means restriction, and building relief moments, with each segment ending in a concrete forward step. Direction weakened slightly during mid-session option menus that sometimes stalled momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were co-constructed from Maria's own words and her pushback shaped the plan (dropping cousin calls, choosing spontaneous texts). Early safety assessment was delivered as a checklist rather than negotiated, and option menus sometimes replaced genuine idea generation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choice ('which step, if any, feels doable') and accepted Maria's minimal commitments without pressure, framing experiments as 'tiny, reversible.' Rapid multi-part question clusters reduced her sense of open choice, and the grounding technique was suggested rather than elicited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out motivation from within, with 'what keeps you here?' eliciting her cousin and hope, and the puppy call exploration surfacing desire for 'relief without the permanent option.' Evocation was uneven, sometimes defaulting to offering options rather than continuing to draw out Maria's own reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maria's contributions consistently shaped the session, from her narrative choice to her own selection of the coffee experiment ('Which would feel more real and useful?'). Safety questioning was more clinician-driven, and a previously rejected option (scheduled cousin calls) was re-offered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced Maria's own change language, including 'part of me does want more of those hours... more relief without the permanent option,' then shaped it into a concrete commitment. Missed opportunities to deepen change talk with confidence/ability questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk and reframed hopelessness, but when Maria said 'convince me this is different,' the counselor offered a menu of experiments rather than a deeper reflection. Sustain talk like 'nothing's ever changes' was acknowledged but not consistently redirected toward her expressed hope.
