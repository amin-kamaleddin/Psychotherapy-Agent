# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance despite the client's dismissive opening and resistance, accommodating her time constraints and requests for plain, honest language. Warmth was somewhat utilitarian, missing opportunities to affirm her self-advocacy and research efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some accurate reflections and normalized her concerns, but often responded to emotional statements (e.g., fear of steroid effects) with clinical facts rather than reflecting underlying feelings. Her feeling of being lectured by her doctor was never deeply explored or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on asthma management and inhaler use throughout, tying every response to safety, control assessment, or a concrete plan. Direction was heavily information-focused rather than motivation-focused, but topic drift was essentially absent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered menu choices and personalized materials to her situation, but often assumed the expert role with dense clinical information. The session's direction was shaped by the counselor's products rather than negotiated with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected the client's autonomy ('my breathing, my choice') and consistently framed next steps as optional, accepting her refusal to rehearse without pressure. Avoided all coercive persuasion, though more explicit reinforcement of her ownership of the decision would strengthen this.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicited motivation from within the client, using almost no open questions about her values or reasons for managing her asthma. Change talk emerged as a side effect of information-giving rather than intentional evocation—the weakest area of the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and solicited her input, but frequently delivered expert content rather than drawing out her own ideas about managing her asthma. Her contributions shaped logistics but not the substance of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged ('that actually makes more sense,' 'I can probably do that') and was supported with concrete tools. However, it arose from information-giving rather than deliberate evocation, and the counselor rarely reflected or reinforced her change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Largely ignored the client's sustain talk ('I'm not promising anything changes,' 'my breathing, my choice') and pivoted to facts and materials. This avoided amplifying resistance but missed double-sided reflections, and her ambivalence remained unresolved at the close.
