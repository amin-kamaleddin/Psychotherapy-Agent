# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates consistent unconditional positive regard through affirmations like 'You know exactly what kind of support works for you' and 'I like that you set the terms yourself.' No judgment of Mateo's past, ambivalence, or self-criticism; even dismissive moments are met with normalization and reframing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate, capturing both surface content and underlying emotional tension (e.g., 'I hear two truths in what you're saying — those friendships are comfort and history, AND they come at a real cost'). Mateo repeatedly elaborates in response, confirming the reflections landed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (disengaging from high-risk peers and building prosocial connections) throughout the session. The conversation progresses logically from exploration to a concrete behavioral plan with a follow-up and contingency planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction is genuinely between equal partners — the final plan is co-constructed ('Deal — and I like that you set the terms yourself'), with Mateo's ideas directly shaping the session's direction. The counselor offers suggestions only as options and seeks input at every step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Mateo's ownership, offering choice in check-in formats and adapting immediately when he rejects texting as feeling 'like reporting to a parole officer.' Mateo defines his own accountability structure, which the counselor honors.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly every turn is an evocative question, and all key insights came from Mateo himself — the cellmate precedent, the accountability mechanism, the plan B, the ten-minute delay tactic. The counselor never lectures or supplies motivations, instead drawing out Mateo's own values and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Mateo as the expert ('You'd know better than anyone what that pull feels like'), and his contributions substantially shaped the session — he designed the plan and set the terms of accountability. The counselor adopts his framing rather than imposing an agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes desire, ability, and commitment language through targeted questions ('What might a sure thing that actually helps you look like?'). Strategic summaries name and amplify shifts ('That's a shift I want to name'), drawing out commitment ('one coffee, I can do that').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently pivots away from sustain talk without dismissing it, using double-sided reflections and redirecting toward solutions ('What do you think would help you push through that moment?'). Affirms Mateo's own problem-solving ('plan B') rather than dwelling on barriers.
