# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, even when Amina was dismissive or hostile. Her pushback ('don't ask a million questions') was reframed as strength rather than met defensively.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflections, including a pivotal double-sided reflection ('tired of acting like it doesn't bother you' vs. 'careful about trusting someone new') that unlocked her first real disclosure. Her feelings of being on trial and her protectiveness of her mother were accurately captured.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus stayed on the household conflict and engagement throughout, building progressively from refusal to disclosure and commitment to return. Score limited by prioritizing rapport over the presenting behavior—appropriate for a precontemplative adolescent, but no explicit agenda-setting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked from Amina's frame with questions rather than telling, and honored her rules for the interaction. Collaboration was largely implicit in restraint; fewer moments of explicit joint problem-solving or shared goal negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly and explicitly reinforced Amina's choice ('You don't have to explain anything,' 'You set the pace here') and adapted his behavior to her stated boundaries. Her acknowledgment that 'You didn't push' confirms this autonomy support was the engine of engagement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All forward movement was client-generated—her trust criteria, her 'it's kind of nice saying it out loud,' and her decision to return all came from her own words. The counselor never lectured or supplied reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned Amina as the expert ('You're the expert on what hasn't worked with adults before'), and her contributions directly shaped the session's direction. Her request for fewer questions and her criteria for trust structured how he handled her disclosure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked engagement and mini-change talk ('maybe if I didn't have to pretend everything's fine for once') from a highly resistant client. Change talk remained largely about the relationship rather than the presenting concern—appropriate but limiting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided reinforcing sustain talk, pivoting toward agency and reflecting positive shifts ('it felt kind of nice'). Some sustain talk like 'waste of time' was acknowledged more than strategically pivoted.
