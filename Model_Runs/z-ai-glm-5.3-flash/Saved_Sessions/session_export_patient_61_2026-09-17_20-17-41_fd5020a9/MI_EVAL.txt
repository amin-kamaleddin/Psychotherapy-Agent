# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, including non-defensive responses to pushback ('Man, you don't let up, do you?') and validation of hesitance ('that's okay'). No judgment about resistance, drinking, or eating habits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and deep, capturing ambivalence early and gently surfacing painful material about his father. Skilled reflections of 'keeping things normal' and the hesitance in 'I suppose' show strong understanding of both content and feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on diabetes self-management, progressing from engagement to values to a concrete behavior (workday lunches) to planning and follow-up. Each question stays tethered to the target behavior despite resistance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely two-sided, with Jonathan's ideas, constraints, and concerns shaping the agenda. His closing comment ('This was more... talking') confirms the non-lecturing, collaborative feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently reinforced ('that's yours to sort out,' 'you set the pace, you picked the step') with no pressure. The counselor explicitly validates boundaries like his Saturday beers and frames change as his own choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is drawn from the client—his work, fear of his father's fate, desire for normalcy, and his own food ideas and identified hurdles. Questions like 'what would make this feel worth your time?' consistently elicit internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is repeatedly located with the client ('You know your week and your weak spots better than anyone'), and Jonathan notes 'Nobody's really asked me that before.' Power-sharing visibly changed his engagement.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked ('What would make this feel worth your time?') and reinforced with reflections and affirmations, deepening into commitment language ('I'll give it a proper go this week'). Follow-up questions about first steps and food preferences further develop commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally honored then redirected toward the client's own reasons, but some reflections mirror resistance content fairly directly (e.g., repeating 'not peeling oranges at your desk required') without actively pivoting. Double-sided reflections could be used more explicitly to resolve ambivalence.
