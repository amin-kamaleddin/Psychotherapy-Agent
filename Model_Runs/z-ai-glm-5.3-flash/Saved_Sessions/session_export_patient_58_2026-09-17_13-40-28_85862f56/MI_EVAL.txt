# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with consistent validation and no labeling. Affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic, missing chances to affirm Jamal's self-directed insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is notably sparse; the counselor relies on rapid-fire questions and advice menus instead of paraphrasing. Jamal's ambivalence and frustration with nagging go largely unexplored or mirrored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on the target behavior (punctuality/morning routine) with each turn advancing toward a concrete plan. Focus is occasionally too narrow, never exploring deeper personal benefits beyond compliance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration emerges in the final half as Jamal co-constructs the plan and adds his own backup strategies. The opening half is heavily one-directional, with solution menus delivered before hearing enough from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear strength: repeated choice offerings, nonjudgmental framing of failure, and endorsement of Jamal's ownership request. The sheer volume of prescriptive advice slightly undercuts the message of client choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited only once directly ('what's one personal reason you'd want to get up on time?'); the rest of the session provides reasons and solutions rather than drawing them out. Deeper intrinsic motivation — Jamal's goals and what success means to him — remains untapped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal's input is invited and does shape the plan (clothes, toast, phone across the room). However, the interaction repeatedly reverts to an expert stance with prescribed solutions, pre-written scripts, and checklists, keeping the session structure counselor-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The question about personal reasons evoked meaningful change talk, and the reward question secured plan commitment. Key intrinsic-motivation statements ('I'd rather figure it out myself,' 'prove something... to me') received no reflective reinforcement or deepening.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not amplified and is briefly acknowledged ('being nagged is annoying'), which is appropriate. But when Jamal voices defiance about daily nagging, the counselor skips past it rather than using double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence — sustain talk is tolerated rather than softened.
