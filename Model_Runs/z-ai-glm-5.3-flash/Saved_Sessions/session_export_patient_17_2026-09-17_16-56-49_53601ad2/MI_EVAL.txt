# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with explicit validation of Anisa's dual pressures and normalization of her fear of failure. Praise was somewhat formulaic, lacking deeper unconditional regard for Anisa beyond her smoking behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections, including a strong identity-level reflection ('quitting feels tied up with who you've been for years'). Reflections stayed brief and content-focused, missing the emotional weight of her shame and discouragement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on smoking cessation from start to finish, progressively building a concrete plan tied to Anisa's specific triggers. The role-play and follow-up commitment were well-directed uses of session time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Joint framing, co-constructed script and relapse plan, and responsiveness to Anisa's edits and requests. Collaboration was not fully equal, as the counselor drove the agenda and Anisa largely selected from pre-built options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices, asked permission before advising, and reinforced ownership through nonjudgmental reframing of slips. Option menus were counselor-generated, subtly limiting Anisa's role as the expert on her own life.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The opening evocative question successfully drew out Anisa's core motivations and intrinsic hope. Evocation was front-loaded; afterward the counselor shifted to advice-giving rather than deliberately deepening change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission before suggestions, but the counselor supplied nearly all concrete solutions—scripts, plans, craving kits, weekend strategies. Anisa selected from a menu rather than generating her own ideas, and her expertise was never explicitly positioned.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocative opening elicited rich change talk ('I'm tired of feeling like this') and Anisa's own hopeful conclusion. The counselor missed opportunities to deepen or reflect change talk when it emerged, moving quickly to planning instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk, but the 'what pulled you back' question invited elaboration on reasons against change, and the counselor restated the failure narrative. The 'slips are data, not identity' reframe was effective, but the session dwelt on failure more than strategically redirecting toward change.
