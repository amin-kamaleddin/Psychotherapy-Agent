# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, normalizing fear and ambivalence ('that's the fear... trying to protect you'). No labeling or disapproval anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Deep, accurate reflections capturing both the emotional weight of avoidance and its protective function. Skillfully held nuance like 'Both can be true right now.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear arc from exploring the moment of avoidance to a concrete behavioral plan, relapse planning, and evidence collection. Each question built on the last with no drift toward unrelated content.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative—every plan element was co-developed through open questions, with no advice offered. The counselor functioned as a thinking partner throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No directives or pressure; the client set her own stakes, dose, and accountability structures. Counselor asked how she'd want to handle obstacles rather than prescribing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation came from the client—her insights about avoidance-as-protection, lowered stakes, and evidence collection. Counselor only reflected and questioned; confidence rose from 4-5 to 6/10.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly credited the client as the author of her own plan ('You came up with this whole plan yourself'). Counselor contributions were reflections and questions only.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk with questions like 'What's the smallest possible step?' Client articulated a concrete plan and commitment language ('I think I could try that') with confidence rising from 4-5 to 6/10.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently reframed without amplification—the 'nope' voice became a protective part rather than a saboteur. Pivoted toward the client's own solutions like two-minute fallbacks.
