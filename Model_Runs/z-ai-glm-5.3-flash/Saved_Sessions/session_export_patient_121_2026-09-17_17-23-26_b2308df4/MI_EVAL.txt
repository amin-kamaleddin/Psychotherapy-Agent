# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validating Sofia's frustration and honoring her requests (no practice, no check-ins, confidentiality). Acceptance sometimes reads formulaic ('smart,' 'makes total sense') and affirmations stay focused on the plan rather than her broader worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening capturing her exhaustion ('You've been dealing with this for years') and desire for control and privacy. Reflections occasionally stay surface-level, missing vulnerability cues ('I miss her, kind of. Whatever'), and the stacked opening questions reduced pacing attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on peer re-engagement from the opening question through concrete planning (texting the friend, teacher backup, coping plan), with no drift. Redundant near-identical restatements of the plan slightly bloat the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative — menus of options, three text drafts to choose or modify, and Sofia shaping every detail (wording, timing, location, follow-up terms). Power-sharing is evident throughout, with her contributions driving the final plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent emphasis on Sofia's control ('you're allowed to stop trying,' 'teacher only if you want'), accepting her refusals to rehearse or do check-ins. Offering pre-written texts slightly steers wording, though she freely rejected two of three.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation drawn largely from Sofia herself — she chose the friend text, identified music coping, and voiced her own reasons ('I just miss my friend'). Evocation remains somewhat shallow, with little probing of what change would mean to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively conveys that expertise resides with Sofia ('that's info you can use'), deferring to her judgment and letting the plan be entirely hers. Occasionally over-structures (three scripted texts, repeated plan restatements) rather than fully trusting her lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Modest change talk elicited through low-pressure questions ('Which feels most doable this week?'), yielding commitment ('tomorrow after school'). The counselor fails to deepen or reflect this change talk back, missing chances to amplify her hope about reconnecting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ('nothing changes,' 'one text doesn't erase years') is met with acceptance and reframing rather than reinforcement. However, double-sided reflections and direct engagement with her hopelessness are rarely used — redirection is neutral rather than strategic.
