# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, immediately validating Natalia's ambivalence ('both are valid') and reassuring her she's not a terrible person. However, the warmth is often formulaic and brief before pivoting to advice checklists.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections captured the function of smoking ('a reliable pocket of calm and control'), but empathy was shallow and quickly overtaken by advice-giving. Vulnerable disclosures like the father memory and fear of losing her release received little deep reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on smoking throughout, moving logically from exploration to a concrete, time-bound plan with a scheduled check-in. Weakness: direction defaulted to the clinician's pre-set agenda rather than following the emotionally significant direction the client raised (her father).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menus were offered and Natalia's input shaped the final plan, but most turns were long expert monologues with bulleted instructions. Her skepticism ('chipping at the edges') was not explored jointly; the counselor proceeded with the plan as designed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was repeatedly affirmed through explicit choices and acceptance of her limit ('I'm not ready to promise anything') without pressure. This was undercut somewhat by immediately supplying prescriptive advice after each choice and failing to affirm her own expressed initiative.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early open questions elicited rich self-exploration, and the readiness ruler showed evocative intent. But the counselor frequently answered questions with advice and missed prime opportunities to elaborate on the client's own change talk ('becoming him', 'the mom who never smelled like smoke').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered options and followed Natalia's preferences, but quickly reverted to an expert posture with prescriptive advice lists. When Natalia challenged the jacket advice as 'guilt management,' her critique was not engaged—only restated the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged (the Wednesday trial, 'wants to be the mom who never smelled like smoke') largely through offered choice and a concrete experiment. However, it was never deliberately deepened with open evocative questions, and the closing discrepancy statement went unamplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk dominated the session with little strategic reframing, and the counselor's framing 'You're right to protect that release' explicitly affirmed reasons to keep smoking. No double-sided reflections were used, and Natalia's final shift ('maybe there's a difference there') went unreflected.
