# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** ☑ Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout. Opening ("however this conversation goes is up to you") communicates unconditional acceptance; affirmations feel genuine. Slightly formulaic warmth at times ("I love that you're being practical") and limited explicit normalization of past avoidance prevent a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** ☑ Strong, accurate, complex reflective listening (e.g., "skipping was supposed to be your escape, but it backfired"). Emotional nuance underexplored—the hurt of being blamed since fifth grade and the loneliness beneath "it's whatever" could be reflected more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** ☑ Clear, consistent focus on reducing avoidance and re-engaging with school. Every exchange moves forward—values, hard/good moments, the library plan, contingency planning—culminating in a specific, actionable plan. Guides deliberately without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** ☑ Genuine equal-partner exchange ("let's figure it out together"). Ideas consistently originate from Sofia (library, headphones, moving tables); counselor adapts smoothly when Sofia pushes back ("I literally just said that"), demonstrating true power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** ☑ Explicit, repeated reinforcement of Sofia's ownership ("on your own terms, only if it's actually quiet"; "what would feel most helpful to figure out together"). Never pressures or prescribes; her "not promising anything" is fully accepted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** ☑ Masterful elicitation of motivation from within. Questions consistently draw out Sofia's own reasons and confidence; payoff evident in "maybe I could go tomorrow," "I can do simple," "even a bad day, I could try again." Counselor attributes motivation back to Sofia ("that was all you").

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** ☑ Sofia positioned as the expert ("You're the expert on what works for you"); her contributions visibly drive the session. Slightly below 5 due to occasionally leading follow-up questions (repeating variations of "What would it take for a day to feel less awful?" after client pushback).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** ☑ Excellent, consistent evocation via open questions and skillful sequencing. Sofia progresses from "it's whatever" to clear commitment and confidence language ("maybe I could go tomorrow," "I can do simple," "I think I could").

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** ☑ Mostly avoids dwelling on reasons against change; sustain talk handled via brief double-sided reflections with pivots. Occasional direct echoing of sustain talk ("I usually just quit stuff when it goes bad") keeps some negative focus alive, though used strategically.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.6/5**

*Strong MI session with a 15-year-old client in contemplation. Strengths: exceptional change talk cultivation, autonomy support, collaboration, direction, and evocation—client moves from defensive "it's whatever" to a self-generated concrete plan (period five library, headphones, non-reactive strategies) with emerging commitment language. Areas for improvement: deepen emotional empathy, avoid repeating questions that frustrate the client, and use more double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk.*
