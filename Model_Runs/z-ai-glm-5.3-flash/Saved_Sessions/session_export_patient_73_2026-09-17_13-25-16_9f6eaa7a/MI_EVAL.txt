# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and effective normalization of slips ('Slips are common and don't erase progress'). However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and often bundled with task instructions rather than offered as genuine warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were occasional accurate reflections ('That's a smart worry to name'), but reflections were sparse and shallow overall. Key emotional material — her fear from the lung specialist visit, fear of being the 'odd one,' and shame over repeated failures — went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear focus on smoking cessation throughout and converged on a concrete, achievable next step (a 3-day tracking trial with a check-in). Points were lost because direction was driven by preset content, pushing plan elements Linda twice declined or found excessive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did seek Linda's input and adjusted to her preferred small experiment after pushback, but the overall pattern was counselor-provides-plan, client-reacts. Linda's own ideas (e.g., her social strategies) were not explored before supplying solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were genuine autonomy-supportive moments, such as offering choices ('Which step feels most doable?') and accepting her rejection of a quit date without pressure. However, these were undercut by multiple directives, prescribed self-talk scripts, and pushing the three-day trial as a package before she freely chose it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Linda's motivation was largely already present; the counselor supplied reasons and tactics rather than drawing out her own. Her strongest change talk ('It's for me, for my health, and for the money too') was never explored or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role, offering pre-packaged option lists, prescribing the tracker format, and supplying coping scripts unprompted. Linda explicitly pushed back twice ('Whoa, that's a lot all at once'), indicating the counselor's pace outstripped collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda offered natural change talk, and the counselor's questions about past attempts and what felt doable helped elicit her three-day commitment. But the counselor never deepened this talk with evocative questions (e.g., confidence rulers) or reflected her change talk back to strengthen it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk — smoking as social identity, self-doubt ('I don't fully believe I can'), and the six-month deferral — was largely bypassed rather than strategically softened. The counselor jumped straight to a four-step plan after her ambivalence, with no double-sided reflections acknowledging both sides.
