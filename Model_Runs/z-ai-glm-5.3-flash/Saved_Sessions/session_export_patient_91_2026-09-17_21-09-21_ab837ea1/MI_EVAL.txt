# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, affirming Marcus without judgment (e.g., 'Anyone learning something new fumbles at first' and 'That takes some guts'). No labeling, disapproval, or conditional regard was present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were consistently accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both surface and deeper meaning (e.g., fear of feeling exposed, and the connection between his worry and his wife). The counselor tracked Marcus's emotional transformation from overwhelm to nervous resolve.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear, progressive focus on blood sugar monitoring throughout, with each exchange building deliberately toward a concrete plan (desk note, Monday call, one finger stick). Summaries consolidated momentum with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session functioned as a genuine partnership, with the counselor asking rather than telling and co-constructing the plan entirely from Marcus's own strategies. Marcus's reactions shaped the session's direction at every step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor protected Marcus's ownership and choice throughout, explicitly allowing ambivalence ('is it okay if it stays a maybe until it feels right?'). No direction, persuasion, or imposition occurred, and affirmations reinforced that the change belonged to Marcus.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was entirely client-sourced — the counselor elicited rather than supplied reasons for change, culminating in Marcus's self-generated motivation tied to his wife and his own insight ('doing the scary thing first'). No lecturing occurred.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly located expertise within the client ('That's your own wisdom, not mine'), and every plan element originated from Marcus's ideas. A textbook demonstration of the client as expert in their own change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evoked, highlighted, and amplified change talk (e.g., 'I can't just wait around' and 'more yes than maybe'). Marcus ended the session with strong, client-owned commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged briefly and gently pivoted without arguing — doubt was reframed as 'nervousness and curiosity at the same time,' and the gym relapse was treated as a learning opportunity rather than evidence of failure.
