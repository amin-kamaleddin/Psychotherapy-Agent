# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout — Mei explicitly notes not feeling judged. However, affirmations are somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized, and warmth occasionally feels formulaic amid rapid advice lists.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is notably sparse; Mei's meaningful material (family alcoholism, fear of not trusting herself, vulnerability about Sara) is rarely reflected back. Nearly every response pivots immediately to bulleted action plans with no complex reflections of feelings or meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on reducing Saturday-night drinking and improving contraceptive reliability, with every response tied to concrete action. The session progresses logically from concern to a testable plan with structured follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is genuine collaborative planning (co-drafted text, Mei's choices, follow-up scheduling), but the power balance tilts toward the counselor. Mei reacts to prescriptive lists rather than co-creating ideas from the start.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices rather than commands, and Mei clearly experiences ownership of the plan. Falls short because choice-based menus still position the counselor as the plan-generator rather than reinforcing Mei's own solution-building capacity.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dominant mode is providing reasons, strategies, and plans rather than drawing motivation from Mei, whose motivation is largely self-generated despite the counselor's style. The early advice dump and scarce evocative questions undercut this criterion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mixed: Mei is offered doable options and choices about the text draft and checklist, but the stance is strongly expert-driven. Mei's contributions shape the plan because she volunteers them despite the advice-heavy style, not because her expertise is systematically elicited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness and confidence questions successfully evoked Mei's importance rating and commitment language. However, key opportunities to deepen change talk are missed — e.g., her pride statement is never reinforced or explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Doubt about trusting herself is acknowledged neutrally without amplification, which is appropriate. But no double-sided reflections pair her doubts with her strengths, and the core self-trust issue is routed around with logistics rather than resolved.
