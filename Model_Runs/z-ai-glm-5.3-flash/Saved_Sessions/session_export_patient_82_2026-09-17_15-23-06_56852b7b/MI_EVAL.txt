# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor opened with a non-judgmental, autonomy-supportive stance and responded warmly to all disclosures. Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, lacking individualized recognition of Lukas's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections (e.g., of Lukas's ambivalence about drinking vs. friends and goals) demonstrated strong perspective-taking. Reflections sometimes merely restated client content, and deeper emotional themes (fear of not belonging, identity conflict) went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, consistent focus on avoiding/limiting drinking to protect athletic and academic goals. Every exchange built progressively toward actionable change, culminating in a concrete plan and commitment-oriented follow-up question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely a joint enterprise, with role-play and plans co-constructed from Lukas's own material (Marcus, his practice line). Minor deduction because the counselor sometimes offered pre-packaged solution lists rather than brainstorming together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently framed everything as Lukas's choice, offering options as menus and deferring to his judgment, including accepting his decision to skip further role-play. Lukas's growing ownership and self-generated plan demonstrate strong autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within the client through open questions, rulers, and prompting his own coping imagery. Lukas arrived ambivalent and left self-motivated with his own plan, with confidence rising from 4 to 7.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positioned Lukas as the expert ('Which one feels most doable?') and built directly on his contributions. Slight deduction for offering dense pre-packaged scripts before fully exploring Lukas's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evoked change talk through strategic open questions and importance/confidence rulers, and reinforced change-oriented statements. Lukas's confidence growth (4 to 6 to 7) and self-generated motivation reflect expert change talk cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (peer pressure, fear of being 'the boring guy') was met with neutral, non-reinforcing reflections that pivoted toward change. However, the counselor sometimes restated sustain talk without reframing it, missing opportunities for double-sided reflections.
