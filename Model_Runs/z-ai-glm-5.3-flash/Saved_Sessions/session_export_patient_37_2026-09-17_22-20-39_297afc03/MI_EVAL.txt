# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, affirming Elena's identity as a mother ('You're the expert on your daughter,' 'Being nervous doesn't make you dumb'). Never judges her hesitancy or her reliance on informal sources, and explicitly honors her request to flag anything that doesn't sit right.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, frequent, accurate reflections including an effective double-sided reflection capturing her ambivalence about necessity vs. doing right by her daughter. Reflections are sometimes formulaic (last statement plus affirmation) and could delve deeper into her fear of what she might find and her guilt about avoidance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains steady focus on the vaccination decision throughout, progressing logically from exploring ambivalence to negotiating how to review risk data. Every exchange moves toward the agreed goal without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed consistently as joint work ('let's look at those numbers together,' 'you asking, me explaining'), with an explicit negotiated agreement on real studies, full sources, and answered questions. Sequence and pace decisions are co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms Elena's ownership ('you're the one who gets to decide,' 'you can pause me anytime') with no persuasive pressure toward vaccination. Her explicit requests for control over pace and questioning are honored and celebrated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Elena's own motivations and decision criteria, eliciting strong client-generated language like 'I don't want to decide out of fear.' Evocation focuses mostly on process preferences rather than deeper personal values, such as what 'doing right by her' means to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions Elena as the expert and asks permission before sharing information, inviting her to shape the session's pace and content. Her contributions visibly redirect the session, such as choosing risks first and requesting plain language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully highlights and reinforces Elena's emerging change talk ('stop deciding out of fear,' 'I'm ready') and evokes commitment-oriented thinking about what confidence would require. Change talk could be developed further toward commitment language, and Elena's own reasons could be elicited more rather than the counselor providing risk information.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk effectively without amplifying or arguing against it—reflecting doubts neutrally in double-sided reflections and pivoting to values ('doing right by her'). Reframes avoidance as readiness, though the handling is somewhat formulaic.
