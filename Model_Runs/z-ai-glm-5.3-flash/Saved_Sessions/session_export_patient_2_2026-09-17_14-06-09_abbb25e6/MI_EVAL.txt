# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, even when the patient is defensive, and safety screening is delivered calmly. However, warmth feels somewhat formulaic, with repeated safety questions despite denials and few individualized affirmations of her strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several good reflective moments capture her need for evening downtime and her fear as a parent. But empathy is inconsistent — the counselor pivots quickly away from her deepest emotional disclosure (fear of leaving her son) and glosses over her boundary about alcohol.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintains good focus on evening smoking reduction, ties the plan to her stated motivator, and moves toward a concrete commitment. Some drift from repeated safety screening and alcohol digressions, plus offering too many options at once dilutes focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed — she contributed the drawer idea, timer wording, and reward. However, most turns begin with counselor-presented strategy lists, casting her in a receiver role rather than a true co-equal, and the alcohol agenda was never negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are regularly framed as the patient's ('Which of these feels okay to try tonight?'), she freely declines the gum, and slips are normalized without self-blame. Advice is nonetheless delivered in dense prescriptive lists before asking what she wants, and reintroducing alcohol tips disregards her stated choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient's strongest motivation emerged largely from her own initiative and was affirmed rather than elicited. The counselor rarely uses open questions, scaling, or exploration of what success would look like, often substituting counselor-provided rationale for client-generated change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-adjacent questions are used and the patient shapes the plan. The interaction is frequently advice-heavy and expert-driven, with bulleted prescriptive tips delivered before exploring her own ideas and little explicit power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is reinforced when it appears, with affirmations and plan-building that let her choose her own steps. But most change talk was client-initiated — the counselor rarely used open questions or scaling to evoke it, and affirmations are generic rather than deepening her own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly returns to alcohol advice after the patient explicitly declined to discuss it, reinforcing sustain talk and risking the alliance. Sustain talk is met with advice lists rather than double-sided reflections or gentle redirection toward her own values.
