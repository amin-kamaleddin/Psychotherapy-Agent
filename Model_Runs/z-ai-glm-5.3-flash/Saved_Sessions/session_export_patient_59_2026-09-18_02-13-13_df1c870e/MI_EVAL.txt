# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently respected the patient's perspective, never moralizing about the cannabis use, and openly acknowledged that attendance was mandated. The patient's autonomy and right to his own view were honored throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections repeatedly captured both the surface content (probation stress, finals, mom) and the underlying feelings (frustration at unfairness, desire for peace at home). The clinician accurately reflected ambivalence without judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully guided the conversation toward personally meaningful change goals (graduation, mom's trust, less court stress) without imposing an agenda. Each exchange moved the patient toward articulating his own reasons and plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently framed the process as joint ('let's figure it out together') and elicited the patient's ideas rather than offering expert solutions. Plans originated entirely from the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient was repeatedly affirmed as the expert on his own life ('you know your world better than I do'), and the clinician explicitly honored his limits ('one week, nothing more promised'). No pressure or persuasion to change more than he chose.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open-ended questions skillfully drew out the patient's own motivations — grades, his mom easing up, peace at home, relief about the court matter. The change talk was generated almost entirely by the patient himself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversational flow was balanced, with the clinician following the patient's lead and building on his statements. The tone was side-by-side rather than expert-patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently recognized, reflected, and deepened change talk (e.g., 'I'd probably take that trade,' wanting to return and report success). Strategic questions about the future and the patient's plan amplified commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'm not going to quit,' 'it's blown out of proportion') was met with accurate reflections and acceptance rather than argument, allowing it to dissipate naturally. The clinician never confronted or debated the patient's defenses of continued use.
