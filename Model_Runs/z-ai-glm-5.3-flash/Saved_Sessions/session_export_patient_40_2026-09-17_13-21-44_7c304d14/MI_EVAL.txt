# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, including an appropriate safety check, but warmth feels formulaic and no affirmations are offered despite vulnerable disclosures about shame, burden, and three months of sobriety.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal — emotionally rich statements about shame, burden, and ambivalence go unreflected as the counselor pivots to plans and instruction; no complex reflections or reflections of feeling appear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on relapse risk and recovery throughout, without drifting; the weakness is that focus is counselor-driven rather than jointly constructed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partially collaborative — inclusive framing and periodic choices, with Elias's ideas woven in — but the plan structure, scripts, and exercises are counselor-authored, with Elias mostly approving or adapting them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine choice-offering (values exploration, text options, exercise choice), but the style remains heavily prescriptive with counselor-designed plans and scripts, and Elias's ownership of his change is never explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is largely supplied via counselor-generated plans and tools rather than drawn from Elias; one values prompt elicited powerful material, but evocation was not sustained and no confidence exploration followed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role with numbered plans, scripts, and dictated behaviors; Elias's own wisdom and ideas are incorporated only briefly before the counselor returns to a pre-formatted agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elias generated rich spontaneous change talk ('I want to be the dad who shows up'), which the counselor almost never reflected, deepened, or consolidated; the one useful values question was quickly converted into prescriptive homework.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Core ambivalence about whether sobriety stems from desire or fear, plus doubts about long-term strength, are reassured away and bypassed rather than explored; no double-sided reflections or strategic use of change talk against sustain talk.
