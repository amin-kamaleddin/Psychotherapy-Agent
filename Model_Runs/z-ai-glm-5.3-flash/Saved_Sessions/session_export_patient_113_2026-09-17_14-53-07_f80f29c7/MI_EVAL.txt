# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, affirming Rowan's choices throughout. Affirmations of his own strengths and resourcefulness could have deepened positive regard further.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate simple reflections track his terse answers well, but reflections rarely capture the emotional layer beneath his resistance, such as irritation at being nagged. Missed opportunities to reflect feeling statements keep this adequate rather than strong.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior from open to close, with every turn moving purposefully toward a concrete plan, troubleshooting, and commitment. No drift despite the client's resistance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were built from Rowan's own suggestions, and he noted 'You didn't push.' Occasionally the psychiatrist structured choice menus rather than jointly generating options, and summaries mostly restated rather than invited correction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy strongly supported via 'I'll support whatever you decide,' options over directives, and a fallback keeping control with the client. Direct client evidence: Rowan explicitly said 'You didn't push.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from the client using rulers and future-oriented questions, eliciting his own reasons ('one less thing nagging me') and self-generated solutions. A 5 would involve deeper elaboration of his values and the emerging desire talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rowan positioned as the expert, and his own ideas became the actual plan. Some questions still funneled toward predetermined options rather than fully open co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance/confidence rulers and consequence questions drew commitment language ('Tonight. I'll actually do it') from a resistant client. Moving to a 5 would require deeper elaboration of change talk once elicited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged with reflective listening and not reinforced, but few double-sided reflections or strategic reframes were used. Sustain talk was often simply reflected and bypassed rather than pivoted toward his ambivalence.
