# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor showed consistent respect and nonjudgment, using affirmations ('that's a realistic plan,' 'love the plan') and normalizing relapse as 'data, not failure.' Warmth was occasionally diluted by repetitive instructions at the close.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured the client's frustration with lecturing and her ambivalence ('it works, so it's hard to ditch'), which she explicitly appreciated. Emotional depth was limited—her anger toward her daughter and fear of repeated failure were not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on smoking triggers, progressing logically from exploring function to a concrete behavioral experiment with follow-up. Minor drift came from offering multiple options and repeating the plan three times at the close.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was framed as joint troubleshooting, with Lucía substantially shaping the plan (choosing the trigger, the daughter line, the 'good mug'). The counselor still supplied most specific strategy options rather than brainstorming them together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was consistently strong: offering menus, accepting Lucía's limited commitment ('that's as far as I'll commit right now'—met with 'that sounds fair'), and explicitly removing pressure ('no shame if you smoke'). The conditional commitment was honored rather than pushed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew motivation from within, eliciting change talk like 'I had a reason, a really strong one' and a concrete commitment to the tea experiment. Evocation stayed somewhat surface-level—importance/confidence scaling and deeper exploration of her current 'strong reason' were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor positioned Lucía's expertise as central and offered choices at each step, with her own ideas shaping the plan. It fell short of top marks because the concrete alternatives (drink swaps, delay tactics) were counselor-supplied rather than elicited from Lucía first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was effectively evoked through exploring smoking's function, past quit success, and triggers, yielding commitments like 'maybe next time she sasses me, I try the tea on the porch.' Opportunities to deepen this with confidence ratings or exploring her 'strong reason' were not pursued.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement, and reframing outcomes as 'data, not failure' softened defenses. Harm-reduction framing replaced argument when Lucía predicted failure, though more explicit double-sided reflections could have been used.
