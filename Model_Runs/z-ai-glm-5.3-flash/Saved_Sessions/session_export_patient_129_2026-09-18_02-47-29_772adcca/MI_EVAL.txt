# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong affirmation of autonomy and personal choice throughout, with clear emphasis on the patient being the expert on his own life. Affirmation was authentic and specific, acknowledging his competencies (passing grades, self-awareness) without being excessive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong verbal empathy throughout, with consistent accurate reflections that validated feelings about his mother, his friendships, and his sense of unfair treatment. The clinician normalized his experiences without agreeing that substance use was harmless.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation was well-organized and moved purposefully from engagement toward exploring ambivalence, self-assessment of risk, and concrete potential steps, while remaining patient-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interactions were consistently collaborative, with the clinician positioning the patient as the expert and partnering in exploration rather than directing. Open-ended questions invited his perspective on goals, risks, and potential supports.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced personal agency and control, explicitly stating this is his call and timeline rather than the clinician's or mother's. No pressure, threats, or authoritarian directives were present.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently used open questions and reflective listening to draw out the patient's own motivations, values, and self-defined thresholds for concern rather than imposing the clinician's views. The patient articulated his own goals and risk markers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a side-by-side stance, explicitly deferring to the patient's expertise and timeline, even when the patient pushed back on the idea of slowing down now. Sought his input on what would help rather than offering unsolicited advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through forward-looking questions (e.g., imagining life at 18, what would signal a problem) and reflective prompts on his own stated concerns about not wanting to end up like 'that guy.' Patient generated reasons for change himself, including self-monitoring and enlisting Marcus's support.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** When the patient reasserted sustain talk (weekends are his time, mom winning), the clinician did not argue, quickly acknowledged the misstep, and reflected both sides (wanting freedom with friends AND keeping school on his terms), effectively rolling with resistance.
