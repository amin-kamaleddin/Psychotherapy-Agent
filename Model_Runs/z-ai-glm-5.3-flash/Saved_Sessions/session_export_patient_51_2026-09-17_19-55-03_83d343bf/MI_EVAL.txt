# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and individualized affirmations (e.g., 'Coming in today... shows that your health matters to you') with no judgment about her procrastination. Occasional clustering of affirmations slightly risks feeling formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections consistently land and prompt elaboration, including the deeper insight that her health feels 'optional.' Some reflections are affirmative restatements rather than deeper emotional exploration, e.g., the guilt around self-neglect is touched but not fully developed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains good focus on medication safety and follows the client's natural shift to self-care without aimless drift. Notable lapse: the counselor repeated the identical opening question after the session had concluded, which Ingrid pointed out ('We covered this, didn't we?'), disrupting momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely joint, with Ingrid's ideas (phone calendar, work-meeting framing, one-week experiment) substantially shaping its direction. The counselor offers information but consistently defers to her expertise about what works for her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support: choices are explicitly framed as hers ('Whichever one you'll actually trust, that's the one that counts'), and clinical decisions are left to her prescriber. No pressure or persuasion is evident.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from Ingrid rather than imposed; open questions elicit her own solutions and identity-level insight. She progresses from logistics to profound self-reflection ('I'm usually last on my own list') through the counselor's evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions Ingrid as the expert ('That's an important insight, and you got there yourself'), and her contributions demonstrably redirect the session. Power sharing is authentic and impactful.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and deepens change talk ('What feels different for you now, walking into that appointment with this in your pocket?'), eliciting strong commitment and activation language ('I'll set it now,' 'Okay, that's done'). Affirmations reinforce her agency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('everything else is never done,' 'I'm terrible at saying no') is met with reflections that pivot toward capability and choice rather than dwelling on barriers. Time constraints are reframed as manageable, with no reinforcement of reasons against change.
