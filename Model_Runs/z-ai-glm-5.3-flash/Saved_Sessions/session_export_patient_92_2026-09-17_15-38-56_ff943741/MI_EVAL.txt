# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental ('That flag isn't a diagnosis; it's a marker,' 'No judgment — just info') with affirmations of the client's practical first step. Falls short of 5 due to a somewhat clinical warmth, few personalized affirmations, and unvalidated humor/defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, frequent reflections that track ambivalence and the social identity tied to drinking. Reflections stay at content level, missing deeper emotional undercurrents like fear of illness, pride in self-sufficiency, and vulnerability in admitting occasional driving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on alcohol use and risk, successfully narrowing to a concrete target (driving after drinking) with a genuine commitment. Some dilution of momentum from scattering multiple options, and the closing summary/next steps are implicit rather than consolidated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Inclusive language and co-construction of the no-driving plan with barrier troubleshooting; power is shared at decision points. Risk-education segments are somewhat didactic, and the client's own solutions are not invited before offering professional options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: 'You're in charge of where we go,' genuine menu choices, no-pressure framing, and honoring refusals of experiments and BAC calculations without pushback. Affirms the client's self-selected step rather than prescribing broader change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocative questions elicit the client's own ambivalence and change talk ('I've got things to think about,' the driving commitment). At times supplies reasons for change before exploring the client's interpretations, and omits confidence/importance scaling to expand emerging change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions the client as decision-maker with menus and choice points ('Would you like me to share... or would you prefer...?'). Retains a data-gathering role without explicitly naming the client's expertise, and his contributions shape logistics more than overall session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evocative questions successfully elicit change talk, including the spontaneous commitment to never drive after drinking. Misses opportunities to deepen commitment language — moving to logistics rather than eliciting confidence/importance ratings or amplifying the change talk that emerged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk but doesn't strategically soften it; responses to 'I'm healthy,' 'my friends drink the same,' and 'it's not a drinking problem' rely on neutral informational reframes rather than double-sided reflections. Sustain talk occupies substantial session airtime without consistent redirection toward the client's own reasons for change.
