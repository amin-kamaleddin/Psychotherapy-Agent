# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with appropriate and non-punitive safety screening; the client explicitly affirmed not feeling judged. Warmth was somewhat mechanical and checklist-like, slightly reducing felt depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections ('that's really demoralizing') resonated, but reflections were sparse and quickly overtaken by assessment questions and advice. Missed opportunities to explore her loneliness, self-criticism, and fear of rest days more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on weight-loss behaviors and converged on a concrete, actionable plan with follow-up. Some direction was lost in dense multi-question information dumps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menu-based choices and plan adjustments (walks instead of full rest) showed collaborative elements, but the flow was largely expert-to-patient. Did not ask what Liyana herself thought might work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered options and let the client choose her own plan; framing Day 1 as 'information, not proof' supported self-directed learning. Some prescriptive moments (protein grams, prefilled log) reduced ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited task-level willingness ('I want to see what happens') but relied more on rationale-giving than drawing out deeper personal reasons for change. Motivation around self-esteem and feeling supported remained underdeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and invited input, which the client responded to positively, but the interaction was heavily expert-driven with rapid assessment questions and counselor-generated tools. Her own expertise was rarely drawn upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively scaffolded commitment through menu options and prefilling Day 1, producing clear commitment language ('I could try the breakfast thing for a week'). Lost credit for not deliberately deepening or affirming her change talk as her own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of rest days, skepticism about eating more) was acknowledged but mostly met with immediate psychoeducation and correction. Double-sided reflections and validation of her fears before pivoting were needed to resolve ambivalence collaboratively.
