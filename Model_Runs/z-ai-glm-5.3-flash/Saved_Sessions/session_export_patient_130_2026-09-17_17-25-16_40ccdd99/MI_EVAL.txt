# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, avoiding criticism of Viktor's drug use or externally motivated attendance. However, warmth felt somewhat formulaic and opportunities to explicitly affirm Viktor's honesty were missed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered accurate reflective summaries and validated barriers like the friends' teasing. Fell short by not exploring emotional nuance, e.g., responding to Viktor's admission that his wife calls him 'distant' with an options list rather than a deeper reflection on the marital disappointment underneath.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus on reducing conflict at home, with scaling questions and homework tied to this goal. However, when Viktor declined the drug-use topic, the counselor conceded immediately, letting the core target behavior drift out of focus without re-anchoring.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely two-way, with plans co-constructed and Viktor reshaping the counselor's proposals (e.g., changing 'home by 12' to 'home late'). Collaboration was slightly constrained by frequent reliance on pre-set three-option menus rather than open co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Viktor's ownership with no pressure or persuasion, even when he declined the drug discussion or refused rehearsal. Viktor explicitly framed the deal on his own terms and the counselor honored it, a strong indicator of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Viktor's own values (peace at home, keeping weekends) via scaling and open questions, with commitments self-generated. Evocation was at times mechanical — confidence was asked four times with little new information, and Viktor's ambivalence ('things aren't how they used to be') went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Viktor as the expert, and the plan was built almost entirely from his own ideas and wording. Partnership was occasionally diluted by option menus funneling toward the counselor's pre-selected solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully used scaling questions that reliably elicited change talk and secured concrete commitments (specific text, timing, comeback line, check-in). Change talk was never deepened or amplified — meaningful statements like 'things aren't how they used to be' were passed over.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately did not argue with sustain talk and reflected it neutrally, but allowed it to stand without strategic softening. Viktor's closing reframe that success would 'prove there's no real problem' was never challenged, and no double-sided reflections linked his use to the marital peace he values.
