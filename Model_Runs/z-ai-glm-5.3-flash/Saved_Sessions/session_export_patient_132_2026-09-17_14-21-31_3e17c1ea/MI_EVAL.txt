# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, including validating the patient's desire for weight loss. However, affirmations of her strengths are sparse and the warmth feels somewhat procedural.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only one brief reflection early in the session, then nearly all questions and instructions. Key emotional disclosures—her exhaustion, spiral fears, skepticism, and awkwardness with the phrase—went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed well-focused on sustainable, non-diet habits with concrete goals. Direction was clinician-driven, and the central ambivalence about weight loss was never directly engaged.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction followed an expert-prescribes, client-selects pattern with menus, scripts, and templates authored by the clinician. The patient's own ideas were not elicited or incorporated into plan design.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered and the patient set some parameters, but the framing was largely prescriptive. Her repeated permission-seeking ('is that pushing it too fast?') suggests accommodation rather than active ownership-fostering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient's motivations (gym enjoyment, feeling better, hope) surfaced despite the interviewer rather than through elicitation. Almost no open evocative questions, values exploration, or confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician assumed the expert role—designing plans, scripts, and trackers with minimal patient input. Her contributions did not substantively reshape the session, and her final permission-seeking underscored the power imbalance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient spontaneously offered meaningful change talk ('I want something more sustainable,' 'I'm willing to see how this goes'), but the clinician rarely deepened it. No open questions or strategic reflections were used to evoke or consolidate it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met neutrally without reinforcement, which avoided harm. But the ambivalence about weight loss was sidestepped rather than worked with—no double-sided reflections or exploration of what weight loss means to her.
