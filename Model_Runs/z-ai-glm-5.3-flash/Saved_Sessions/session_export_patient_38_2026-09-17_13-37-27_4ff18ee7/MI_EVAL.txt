# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating Elena's pace and normalizing her resistance without labeling or disapproval. However, affirmations are somewhat generic and miss chances to affirm her specific resourcefulness, such as researching emergency contraception on her own.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflects Elena's feelings accurately, including recognizing her defensiveness about medical pressure. Falls short of 5 because deeper emotional layers — the fear during the two-day wait, tension with her sister's expectations — are acknowledged but not explored with nuanced, elaborated reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The target behavior (contraception planning) is named early, but the psychiatrist follows Elena's avoidance into anxiety management that Elena repeatedly declines, with concrete progress emerging only near the end. Direction is shown in the small final plan, but the clinician never steers back to contraception earlier or consolidates themes into a values-linked summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeated choice points and a final plan that emerged from Elena's own suggestion support collaboration, but the counselor tends to deliver information monologues (EC details, harm-reduction lists) rather than inviting Elena's expertise first. Elena's pushback about not wanting a checklist someone else made signals the offerings outpaced her preferred collaborative pace.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The strongest element of the session: the psychiatrist consistently and explicitly reinforces Elena's ownership ('This is yours to control'), respects her boundaries, and immediately steps back when she insists on setting her own reminder. The plan that emerged was chosen by Elena herself and framed as hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena's motivation surfaced organically thanks to the non-pressuring stance, but the clinician relies on menu-style option questions rather than open evocative questions drawing out her own reasons and confidence. Her ambivalence is named and normalized but never strategically explored, making change talk evocation incidental rather than skillful.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asks Elena what she prefers, reflecting some power sharing, but much of the session consists of presenting unrequested menus of options and information lists. Elena twice had to push back ('I'm not helpless'), indicating the partnership was not fully equal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerged ('getting pregnant right now would wreck everything'), aided by low-pressure options she engaged with. However, the clinician does not systematically deepen it — no evocative follow-up to 'I don't want to go through that waiting again' and no summary consolidating her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Elena's sustain talk, instead offering autonomy-preserving reframes and small actionable steps that successfully shifted her toward commitment. Some sustain talk is acknowledged neutrally rather than strategically reflected, but Elena's own pivot toward change was allowed to lead.
