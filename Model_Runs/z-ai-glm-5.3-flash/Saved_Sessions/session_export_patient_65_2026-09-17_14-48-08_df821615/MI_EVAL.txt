# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with validating statements like 'That makes sense — you want to get it right but asking feels invasive.' Falls short of 5 due to limited explicit affirmation of Julian as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made accurate reflections and validated anxiety, but pivoted quickly to problem-solving. Missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions about being court-mandated, feeling watched, or fearing judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on completing community service hours with no topic drift. Session progressed logically through clarify, contact, schedule, and verification steps, each ending with a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered menu choices and asked permission, but the session frequently shifted into expert mode with pre-written scripts and an unsolicited checklist. Would be stronger if Julian's own ideas were elicited before offering solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice ('You're in charge here') and offered options for messaging and scheduling, with Julian's preferences shaping the plan. Occasionally provided unsolicited detailed advice, keeping it from a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied heavily on providing solutions rather than drawing motivation from Julian, with almost no open questions about his reasons or confidence. His moment of hope ('feels more doable') went unexplored and extrinsic motivation was never connected to personal values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and offered choices, supporting some power sharing. However, the session tipped into advice-giving expert mode, with drafted messages and a checklist largely provided on the counselor's initiative rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Julian offered spontaneous change talk that the counselor supported through practical tools, but change talk was never deliberately evoked or amplified. It emerged from the session's structure rather than skilled MI technique.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and gently redirected toward action, which was appropriate. However, there was little active softening — no double-sided reflections or reframing of ambivalence, and some sustain talk was simply passed over.
