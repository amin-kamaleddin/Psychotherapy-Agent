# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, honoring Ahmed's boundaries without protest and complying with the chart-labeling request without defensiveness. Warmth was present but somewhat formulaic, with little deep personal affirmation of Ahmed's work ethic, family role, or self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and frequently multi-element, capturing Ahmed's minimization, reward framing, and non-addicted self-perception. However, reflections remained largely content-level, with little exploration of the deeper emotional meaning of the ritual or the ambivalence beneath his sustain talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus on smoking-related oral health was maintained throughout, and the session moved coherently from exploration to a concrete behavioral plan with follow-up. Slightly short of 5 because Ahmed's stated willingness to 'cut back' was never revisited and no explicit check on longer-term goals occurred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered options rather than directives, asked which steps fit best, and Ahmed's truck-bottle routine shaped the reminder plan. However, the risk information and menu were delivered wholesale, and the plan's structure was proposed by the clinician rather than truly co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support: the psychiatrist repeatedly affirmed choice, immediately honored Ahmed's 'don't push it' boundary, and gave him full control over follow-up contact and chart documentation. Ahmed's own comment — 'You didn't lecture me the whole time' — provides direct evidence of perceived autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was treated as residing in clinician-delivered information and options; Ahmed's deflection of the strongest evocative question was not followed up, and his father's gum disease was acknowledged only once and never deepened. No scaling, importance/confidence exploration, or elaboration of the patient's own reasons was pursued.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were genuine power-sharing gestures (asking permission, inviting additions to the plan), but the session shifted toward psychoeducational advice-delivery with clinician-generated menus and monitoring. The clinician retained the expert/plan-owner role more than a true partnership would allow.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although good open evocations were used, the pivotal moment of Ahmed spontaneously committing to 'maybe the rinsing and water stuff' and mentioning his dad's gum disease was acknowledged but not deepened. No commitment language, confidence scaling, or elaboration of the patient's own reasons was pursued.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never argued with or confronted Ahmed's minimization, reflecting it accurately and neutralizing it. However, sustain talk was repeatedly restated in summaries without double-sided reflections pairing it with the patient's emerging concern about his father's gum disease.
