# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unconditional positive regard throughout — nonjudgmental opening, direct yet caring risk questions, and affirmations like 'That's a really brave step.' Respected Maria's request not to be fixed and stayed present without any judgment or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective statements accurately tracked Maria's exhaustion, skepticism, and ambivalence. Slightly limited by brief, formulaic reflections ('I hear you' repeated) and missed opportunities to explore deeper emotional meaning, such as the mother memory and underlying grief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on suicidal ideation and safety throughout, with systematic risk assessment, means restriction, coping planning, and follow-up arrangements. No topic drift despite client resistance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as joint, with Maria's own ideas (neighbor, record, sister) forming the core of the plan. Occasionally structured the plan unilaterally, and repeated checklist-style safety scripting read more like protocol delivery than co-created dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered genuine choices (options for means restriction, silence vs. music, text vs. call) and asked permission before direct questions. Some phrasing was slightly leading, and repeated safety-plan prompts could feel pressuring, though client refusals were respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Relied on Maria's spontaneous change talk (sister, mother's memory, 'part of me wants something different') without deepening it through open exploration of her reasons for living and hopes. Motivation elicited stayed focused on safety logistics rather than values and meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly deferred to Maria's expertise with open questions ('What would help most right now?') and offering choices; her contributions visibly shaped the session. Safety planning elements like means restriction and 988 were somewhat prescribed rather than collaboratively developed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully built on Maria's spontaneous change talk, translating it into concrete commitments (moving pills, calling Rosa, charging phone), ending with clear client change talk. Exploration of the 'part that wants something different' and her hopes beyond safety steps remained brief.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk ('nothing works') and consistently pivoted toward safety, connection, and Maria's own helpers while acknowledging her skepticism. Redirection was sometimes abrupt, shifting quickly to risk questions without first validating her hopelessness.
