# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, with no judgment even around continued use, framing the session with 'no judgment here' and affirming Diego's practical thinking. Even the stigmatized injection marks topic is handled with sensitivity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including a skillful double-sided reflection on the relief/worry trade-off and nuanced noticing of emotional content. Scored 4 because disclosures like the 'low-level dread' and dead acquaintances were acknowledged but not deeply explored before pivoting to planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear harm-reduction focus with coherent progression from risks to concrete steps and specific planning of the housemate conversation. Not a 5 because emotional exploration was kept deliberately brief in favor of action planning, and one counselor turn contained duplicated content suggesting a lapse in flow.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely co-constructed, with the plan shaped entirely by Diego's input on each decision point. Suggestions are offered only as questions, keeping power balanced throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces Diego's ownership of every decision and never pushes toward abstinence or a particular timeline. Diego's harm-reduction framing ('not about quitting yet, it's about not dying') is fully honored, and self-forgiveness is validated rather than corrected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly every turn contains open questions eliciting Diego's own reasoning and plans ('What made you land on those two things?', 'What would getting it done mean for you?'). All motivation and commitment language is self-generated and then reflected back and consolidated by the counselor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions Diego as the expert ('you're the expert on that relationship', 'You've basically laid out your own plan here'). Every action step originates from Diego, with the counselor limited to reflecting, affirming, and open questioning.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evokes change talk with questions like 'what would getting it done mean for you?', drawing out strong commitment language ('This matters too much to keep putting off'). The motivation is never supplied by the counselor — it is all drawn from Diego and consolidated through summaries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not dwelt upon; the pro-using statement ('it works') receives a balanced double-sided reflection followed by a pivot toward readiness. Not a 5 because redirection is not always active — the escape function is reflected sympathetically without an explicit pivot back to change.
