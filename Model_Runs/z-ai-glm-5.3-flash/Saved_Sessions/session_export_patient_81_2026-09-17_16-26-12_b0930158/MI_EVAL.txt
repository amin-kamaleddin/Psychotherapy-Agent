# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and non-judgmental, affirming that attending after friends' intervention 'takes courage.' Loses a point for somewhat formulaic warmth and brief affirmations lacking deeper normalization during vulnerable moments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good reflections (the pull between belonging and acting future), but empathy was inconsistent. Key vulnerable disclosures about defensiveness when judged and loss of social identity were not reflected, with quick pivots to offering strategies.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on meth use consequences, building progressively from importance ruler to concrete plans and rehearsal, tied to Lukas's values. Falls short of 5 because actual reduction in meth use was never explicitly addressed as a target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—client chose agendas, options, and plan details, and rehearsal was responsive to his requests. Power-sharing was uneven at times, e.g., rewriting his script rather than jointly editing it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed choice with permission-seeking and menus of options Lukas selected freely. Dense, prescriptive advice-giving (multi-item lists, script rewrites) subtly positioned the counselor as the source of solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used classic evocative techniques (importance ruler with follow-up), but evocation was superficial overall. Missed key moments like Lukas's self-efficacy statement ('not as hopeless as my flatmates think') and his insight about defensiveness; much expertise came from the counselor rather than the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Lukas as expert on his own life, honoring his choices about what to rehearse and which strategies felt doable. Sometimes slipped into expert role, providing full scripts and logistics without first inviting his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited genuine change talk via the importance ruler and direct questions, but failed to reinforce or deepen these moments. Lukas's self-affirming statement was met with psychoeducation rather than affirmation or further evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and used some reframing, but responses to strong sustain talk about his social life were neutral or quick pivots to solutions. No double-sided reflections or deliberate processing of ambivalence were evident.
