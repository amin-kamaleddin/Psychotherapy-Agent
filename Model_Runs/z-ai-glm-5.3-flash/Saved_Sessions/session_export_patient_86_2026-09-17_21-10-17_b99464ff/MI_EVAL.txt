# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, responding to overt resistance with affirmations ('You've earned the right to run your own life') rather than pushback. No judgment, labeling, or disapproval appeared at any point, including at the close.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured Marco's weariness with 'the talk' and his attachment to routines (coffee cigarette, after-dinner one). Empathy fell short of a 5 because deeper emotional layers—Marco's fatalism after surviving heart surgery and cancer while still smoking, and possible underlying shame—were reflected only at surface level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on smoking tied to the dental context and progressed purposefully toward a concrete consideration (gum, gradual reduction). Early exploration of resistance and the 'what would be worthwhile' discussion slightly delayed direct engagement, and a mid-session consolidating summary would have tightened direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operated between equal partners, with the counselor asking permission, co-constructing the agenda, and joining Marco's humor. Marco experienced this as novel and positive: 'nobody's really asked me that before.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: 'Your call, Marco. Always your call,' 'No pressure from me, ever,' with options offered neutrally and permission explicitly sought. This approach directly disarmed Marco's defensiveness and was the session's standout strength.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor drew out Marco's own motivations—money, his partner, his identity as a decision-maker, preserving rituals—rather than persuading. The arc from 'I can't see myself changing now' to 'I might surprise you' demonstrates masterful evocation of latent motivation in a precontemplative client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Marco as the expert on his own life, with information-giving given only in response to his explicit request. Marco directly confirmed the partnership landed: 'Thanks for treating me like a grown-up for once.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked ownership from the start ('What are your own thoughts on where you'd like to start?'), and the pivotal question 'What would make any conversation actually worth your time?' shifted Marco from resistant to engaged. Tentative change talk was consistently amplified, culminating in strong statements ('something I could actually manage,' 'I might surprise you') that were deliberately reinforced ('I'll hold onto those words').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally redirected sustain talk effectively, reframing past failures non-blamingly and acknowledging resistance briefly before pivoting. One notable slip occurred when the counselor echoed Marco's sustain talk ('If I wanted to quit, I'd have done it already'), risking reinforcement of reasons against change.
