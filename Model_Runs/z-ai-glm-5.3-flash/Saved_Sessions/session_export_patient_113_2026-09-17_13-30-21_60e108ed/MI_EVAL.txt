# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone even when Rowan was blunt and dismissive ('Thanks — I appreciate the honesty', 'Totally fair'). However, warmth felt somewhat formulaic, as repeated menu-style questions made regard seem procedural rather than genuinely individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered several accurate reflections, including the deeper autonomy theme ('having your week decided for you erodes control and respect'). Reflections sometimes stayed at surface level, with quick pivots to options rather than deepening emotional understanding, e.g., the shame in 'everyone calls me unreliable' went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Helped produce a concrete micro-plan (9 PM alarm, 10-minute plug-in, consistent spot), but the session then drifted fully into work venting without linking back to device adherence, despite Rowan himself making that connection. Direction was present but inconsistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided an authoritarian stance and offered choices, but Rowan was choosing between counselor-generated packages rather than co-creating solutions. His own ideas (e.g., chaotic shifts causing forgetting) were not integrated into a joint plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor's strongest domain — every intervention was framed as Rowan's choice, with rejections accepted without pushback ('Totally fair') and the plan framed as a 'tiny, non-binding experiment.' This consistently reinforced Rowan's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was primarily supplied through counselor proposals rather than drawn from within Rowan; there were few open questions about his own reasons or values. His most motivational statement ('if something bad did happen and I could've prevented it with ten minutes, that'd be dumb') emerged spontaneously and was left unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly handed decision-making to Rowan, whose contributions genuinely shaped the session (e.g., rejecting the manager message and choosing the alarm component). Falls short of 5 because the counselor supplied nearly all solution content rather than drawing on Rowan's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited ('maybe the alarm thing wouldn't be the worst', 'I'll do it tonight'), but largely through proposing concrete options rather than evoking Rowan's own motivations. A spontaneous reason for change ('prevent something bad') received no reflection or exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and occasionally normalized resistance, but did not use double-sided reflections or actively redirect sustain talk toward change. Rowan's ambivalence ('Nothing bad's happened yet, so why bother?') was acknowledged but met with new menus of options rather than strategic redirection.
