# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, honoring Jamal's confidentiality requests and accepting his honesty about continued use without judgment. Warmth is somewhat brief and task-focused, with limited deeper affirmations of Jamal as a person beyond his practical choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captures Jamal's perspective, including his view that the real problem is getting caught rather than the weed itself. Reflections occasionally remain at the summary level rather than exploring deeper emotions like his frustration with the system.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior from the opening question through concrete action planning (what, when, where, confidence) and follow-up logistics. Excellent goal-directed structure without being coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is largely co-constructed, with Jamal's choices genuinely shaping the plan and negotiated decisions about reporting and scheduling. Minor limitation: the counselor sometimes drives the pace with structured checklists and leading questions, making some exchanges feel formulaic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: explicit permission-seeking before advice, offering menus of options Jamal can accept or reject, and respecting his refusal of the exit script rehearsal. Jamal consistently controls the pace, wording, and content of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation present via the opening goals question and confidence scaling, but the counselor relies heavily on supplying options and rationales rather than drawing out Jamal's intrinsic reasons. Deeper exploration of personal values (school, job, sports) and their connection to use would be expected for a higher score.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership: the counselor defers to Jamal's expertise, asks permission before offering options, and lets his contributions shape the plan, timeline, and P.O. wording. Somewhat limited by prescriptive bullet-point menus that reduce space for Jamal's own idea generation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicits commitment and implementation language (specific plan, 8/10 confidence, realistic harm-reduction deal), but most motivation remains externally driven by P.O. compliance. The counselor does not deepen change talk toward Jamal's own values, and the 8/10 confidence rating is not followed up to consolidate commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rolls with sustain talk effectively, never arguing or confronting when Jamal says 'nobody even cares' or 'I'm not gonna quit altogether,' instead reframing honestly and pivoting to risk reduction. Missing double-sided reflections to gently develop discrepancy, and initial option menus were presented before Jamal showed readiness.
