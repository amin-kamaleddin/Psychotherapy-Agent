# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance with genuine positive regard and helpful reframing of the client's fear. Affirmations occasionally feel formulaic and repetitive ('that's real movement, and it came from you' used nearly verbatim multiple times).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate complex reflections that capture both content and meaning (e.g., relationships as anchor while work pulls energy away). Some emotional exploration opportunities were missed, such as probing what 'scary' or feeling worn out meant to her.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, values-anchored focus on protecting family time via work-life boundaries, with every question tied to this thread and no drift. The session progresses logically from exploring the gap to planning and confidence-building while remaining client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Counselor behaves as an equal partner, offering invitations rather than advice and letting the client's own contributions (dinner as starting point, Saturday drafting plan) shape the session direction. Client generated all ideas herself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently honors client choice and pace ('at your own pace') with no pressure or coercion. Could more explicitly affirm her freedom to decide against or delay change to eliminate any subtle forward pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation—motivation is drawn from within the client rather than supplied, eliciting vivid values-driven vision and confidence rooted in her own track record. All ideas (boundaries, manager conversation, Saturday drafting) came from the client and were acknowledged as such.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within the client ('you know your situation better than anyone'), and client contributions demonstrably shaped the session. Power-sharing is actively fostered throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength: skillful questions evoke desire, ability, and commitment talk, and each client statement of hope is reflected back and amplified. Culminates in strong commitment language ('I really think I'm ready to try').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal, and when fear of seeming uncommitted appeared, the counselor used a skillful double-sided reflection reframing it as evidence of caring. Did not amplify doubt, instead redirecting to realistic expectations of the manager.
