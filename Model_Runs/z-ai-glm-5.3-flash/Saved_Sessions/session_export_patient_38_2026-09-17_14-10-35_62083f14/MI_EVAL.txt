# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a consistently respectful, warm, nonjudgmental stance, validating Elena's feelings and hesitance without criticism or pressure. Warmth was somewhat formulaic at times (e.g., repeated grounding instructions), and a more individualized affirmation of Elena's courage would elevate the score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and validating, capturing Elena's relief, shock, and terror of the waiting period. However, the counselor relied on repeated stock phrases rather than deepening into nuanced emotions like fear of her mother's reaction or shame about forgetfulness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor kept clear focus on contraception decision-making after the pregnancy scare, respecting Elena's pace while gently keeping a pathway open and progressing to a concrete plan. Direction was slightly weakened by jumping ahead with offers rather than letting Elena consolidate her own next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offered menu-style choices and followed Elena's lead, with her ideas visibly shaping the session. The score was held back by drifting into a more expert-driven, unidirectional format during the options overview and question-list drafting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforced Elena's freedom to choose, offered choices rather than directives, and Elena explicitly appreciated being unpushed. Autonomy support could be strengthened by explicitly affirming Elena's ownership of her change process and connecting the plan to her own values.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena's motivations emerged largely spontaneously from her pregnancy scare rather than through deliberate evocative techniques. The counselor did not use importance/confidence rulers, elaboration of change talk, or readiness questions, instead relying on offering choices and providing information.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Preferences were requested and information was tailored to Elena's needs, but the session shifted noticeably into information-delivery mode during the options overview and question drafting. Elena's own expertise about her life was acknowledged implicitly but not actively leveraged to shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerged ('I never want to feel that kind of terrified again') but mostly spontaneously from Elena's fear rather than counselor evocation. Missed opportunities included targeted evocative questions and explicit exploration of importance and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor handled sustain talk reasonably well by not arguing or pressuring, and consistently validated Elena's right to wait. However, sustain talk was simply accepted and pivoted past, rather than addressed with double-sided reflections pairing hesitance with Elena's own change talk.
