# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard shown throughout—no judgment, labels, or moralizing about prenatal drinking; Sofia explicitly validates this ('Thanks for not just lecturing me'). Opening and closing affirmations reinforced a non-judgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening captured both sides of ambivalence and tracked emotional nuances (fear of 'becoming real,' the putting-off cycle, quiet-moment identity) with precision. The psychiatrist consistently named Sofia's self-discoveries as they emerged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior (abstinence during pregnancy) and its proximal steps, moving progressively from ambivalence exploration to a concrete behavioral commitment with a timeline. Every question advanced toward change without feeling pushed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session functioned between equal partners, with direction emerging from Sofia's answers rather than a preset agenda. The entire action plan was co-constructed from her own reflections about Priya, quiet moments, and her putting-off pattern.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice or directives given at any point; all ideas came from Sofia, and her pace and preferences (including not wanting to feel monitored) were anticipated and respected. Ownership was actively reinforced with questions like 'What works for you?'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within Sofia—never arguing for abstinence or listing risks. Her closing insight ('I already knew the answers. I just needed someone to make me look at them') is a hallmark of effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located within Sofia ('you're the one who knows these friends best'), and the plan was entirely her own. Her closing realization about having the answers herself confirms the power-sharing stance succeeded.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions ('What would it be like to have that conversation with Priya?') elicited escalating commitment language from desire to concrete plans ('Tonight I text Priya. Friday, dinner. Monday, I talk to you.'). Change talk was selectively reflected and amplified, e.g., sitting with 'Maybe I'm actually fixing it.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally met with effective reframes (fear as 'trying to talk you out of what you already know is possible') without dwelling on reasons against change. A few reflections restated fear/sustain talk ('When Friday comes and it feels hard') that could have been weighted more toward the change side.
