# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, supportive tone with affirmations of David's self-referral and validation of his worries. Praise was somewhat brief and formulaic, with little normalization of past failed quit attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflected some content accurately but reflections were shallow and infrequent, with the session quickly pivoting to checklists and information. Significant emotional material—chest tightness fears, the automatic pull to follow Tom outside—went largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation throughout, efficiently moving from assessment to medication selection, quit date, trigger management, and follow-up scheduling. Every exchange was steered back to the target behavior and a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructed the plan with David, negotiating quit date, titration concerns, text wording, and appointment logistics, with his input materially shaping the plan. Several phases were delivered as expert information-giving, and the opening turn contained five stacked questions, slightly unbalancing the exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered genuine choices about quit date, coping options, and scheduling, and asked permission before providing information. A 5 would require more consistent framing of change as fully David's ownership rather than occasional expert steering of options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial evocation was good (meaning of quitting, confidence scale) but the bulk of the session relied on the psychiatrist supplying reasons, strategies, and medical rationale. Missed opportunities to explore why previous attempts failed or what David thinks will make this time different.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David's contributions shaped practical decisions, but the psychiatrist largely operated as the expert with detailed protocols and minimal exploration of David's own ideas. When David asked for a recommendation, expertise was supplied rather than elicited from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opened with a strong evocative question and used a confidence ruler, eliciting David's own motivation and commitment ('More than I have before'). Did not deepen change talk when it emerged, and the session shifted to psychoeducation rather than continued evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged barriers without dwelling on them and redirected toward solutions. However, sustain talk was often answered with advice rather than strategic reframing—no double-sided reflections were used to resolve ambivalence.
