# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with warm normalization of setbacks at the close. However, affirmations of Jamal's strengths and ownership were sparse and generic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was nearly absent — no simple or complex reflections in the entire session. Emotionally meaningful statements (tuning out mom's nagging, 'getting out of bed's the hardest part,' his ambivalence) were skipped over in favor of logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear and consistent focus on punctual attendance and its drivers (wake time, alarms, bedtime, morning routine). Every turn advanced the behavioral goal with no topic drift, ending with a well-specified Day 1 plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative gestures and incorporation of Jamal's chosen times, but the plan structure was unilaterally proposed before agenda-setting, with the counselor prescribing and Jamal ratifying rather than true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices ('Which wake time feels doable?', options for scripts and apps), and Jamal selected his own wake/bedtimes. However, the overall stance remained directive with the counselor generating most solutions, and no explicit reinforcement of Jamal's control over the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no effort to draw out Jamal's own reasons, values, or confidence — no open evocative questions or confidence ratings. The approach was advice-driven, and his spontaneous intrinsic motivation ('For me,' 'gotta prove it first') went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role, supplying nearly all content — calculations, routines, scripts, and app names. Jamal's input was absorbed but did not meaningfully shape a largely predetermined plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal volunteered rich change talk ('For me,' 'I'm tired of detention,' 'I just gotta prove it first'), but none of it was reflected, amplified, or deepened. Each instance was met with a jump to tactical planning, missing clear gold-shoe opportunities.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not amplified or reinforced, which is appropriate. However, it was also not actively acknowledged, reframed, or strategically addressed — responses simply moved past it.
