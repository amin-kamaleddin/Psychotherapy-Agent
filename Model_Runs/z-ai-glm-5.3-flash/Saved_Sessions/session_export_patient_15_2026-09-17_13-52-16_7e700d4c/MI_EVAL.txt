# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental and polite throughout, but warmth was formulaic ('Thanks for being frank') with little individualized affirmation. Repetitive recapping irritated the client at the end ('Alright, alright, you've covered it all twice now').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections (family history), but emotional meaning was rarely reflected — e.g., drinking as his way to unwind, skepticism about alcohol's effect on BP, and humor about owing his wife an apology were glossed over with task instructions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on hypertension and alcohol with a concrete deliverable (7-day tracking sheet, one alcohol-free evening). Minor deductions for scattered rapid-fire option lists early on.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client was offered choices, but the plan, sheet, and swap ideas were pre-built by the counselor rather than co-created. Client contributions (sparkling waters, NA beer) were acknowledged but not integrated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed everything as optional and accepted refusals and 'no promises' without pushback. However, choice-offering sometimes felt like menu-presenting rather than reinforcing the client's ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied on closed questions, menus, and instruction-giving instead of evoking the client's own motivations. Values like his relationship with his wife were never explored as motivational resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Gestured toward client expertise with 'What feels most helpful?' and options, but content was delivered as an expert-driven package rather than built with the client's input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk ('maybe I'll reconsider,' 'one alcohol-free evening') emerged passively or from pre-packaged options, not skillful evocation. Tentative commitment language was never deepened or reflected back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk (years-long routine, family history attribution, skepticism) was bypassed with instructions rather than softened. No double-sided reflections or pivots to his prior success quitting smoking.
