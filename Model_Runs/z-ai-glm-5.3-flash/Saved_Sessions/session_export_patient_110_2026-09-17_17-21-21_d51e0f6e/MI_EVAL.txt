# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with repeated affirmations of Richard's past success and commitment. However, affirmations are somewhat generic and formulaic, with little normalization of his fear of lifelong medication.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured Richard's ambivalence and incorporated his values (hiking with Maria, retirement). Reflections stayed somewhat at summary level, never naming the deeper emotional core of his anxiety, and often blended into immediate questions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on cholesterol management from start to finish, with every exchange tied to the target behavior. The session converged on a concrete, time-bound plan with specific follow-up parameters and no meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely an interaction between equals—Richard shapes the walking plan, question selection, and check-in timing. The unsolicited 10-item question list briefly overwhelmed him and the plan structure was sometimes counselor-generated, reducing full power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices consistently framed as Richard's own, with menus of options and full accommodation of his preferences (no weekly check-ins, no note to Maria). His explicit appreciation for 'not being lectured' confirms the autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evocation was strong early, drawing out past success and core fears rather than lecturing, as Richard confirmed. In the latter half the session shifted toward information provision and logistics rather than continued elicitation of his motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Richard was consistently treated as the expert on his own life, with his preferences honored without pushback. The counselor occasionally slid into a service-providing expert role (detailed question list and script), slightly reducing power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open-ended questions effectively elicited strong change talk ('I'll take them,' 'Retiring healthy with her—that's the whole point'). Missed opportunities to deepen commitment language further (e.g., confidence rulers or explicit commitment questions) kept this from a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly and non-judgmentally, and side-effect worries were effectively converted into manageable doctor-visit questions. Slight risk of amplifying the worry through extensive focus on side effects, but Richard moved toward commitment by session end.
