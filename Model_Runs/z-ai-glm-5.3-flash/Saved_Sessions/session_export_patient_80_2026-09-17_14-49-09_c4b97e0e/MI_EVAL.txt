# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a consistently warm, nonjudgmental stance, validating Luka's partial progress without criticizing continued drinking. Relapse risk was framed as a normal challenge to plan for rather than a moral failing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured both sides of Luka's ambivalence early on, but reflections became sparser during planning. Emotional nuances like his fear of failure and violence-related work stress triggers could have been reflected more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use, progressing logically from ambivalence to triggers to concrete action steps. Every question advanced the agenda while remaining client-centered, with no drift off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session functioned as genuine collaboration, with every plan element co-constructed and Luka's own knowledge shaping the backup plans and risk strategies. The closing 'Deal?' exchange reflects true partnership framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforced Luka's ownership of decisions, never pressured abstinence, and offered advice as optional menus. Luka's own choices defined the entire plan, confirmed by his statement that it 'feels more doable than anything I've tried before.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers and option menus effectively elicited Luka's own motivations and solutions, including client-generated ideas like moving the beer. However, motivation was drawn out mainly through structured tools rather than open questions about deeper values, leaving his relationship goals and history of violent incidents underexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently located expertise within Luka, following his lead and letting his contributions shape the plan. At times the clinician supplied option menus before eliciting Luka's own ideas, slightly reducing the sense that his wisdom led the process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Importance/confidence rulers and open questions successfully evoked substantial change talk, including 'this feels more doable than anything I've tried before.' The clinician could have deepened and reflected change talk back more consistently after Luka offered it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification, and the double-sided reflection kept change salient while validating reluctance toward abstinence. Repeated 'what might get in the way' questions inadvertently invited sustain talk; reframing toward agency and past successes would have strengthened this.
