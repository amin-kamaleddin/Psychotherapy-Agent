# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental with affirmations ('That's a strong start,' 'Nice work') and validation of the client's self-doubt as understandable. No judgment, labeling, or disapproval at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured ambivalence and the core barrier ('sitting down is the real trap'). Reflections sometimes doubled as transitions to problem-solving, and emotional weight (discouragement, all-or-nothing history) was under-explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on increasing physical activity with zero topic drift from opening to close. Every exchange advanced the plan: nights, cue, reminder time, slip-plan, contingencies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine teamwork — the client supplied barriers, solutions, schedule, and contingencies while the counselor provided structure and wording support. The client's closing comment ('more doable than anything I've tried') reflects the collaborative success.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice offered throughout ('Which would you like me to finalize?'), and all client choices were honored without pressure. Choices were often structured within counselor-generated menus, and 'I'll expect your end-of-week check-in' was mildly directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions drew out client-owned reasons and solutions, with confidence rising from 4 to 5. Exploration stayed somewhat instrument-focused; the personal meaning and values behind exercising remained lightly explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client was positioned as the expert (his schedule insights and rain contingency shaped the plan). The counselor at times supplied ready-made options rather than letting the client generate ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Importance and confidence rulers effectively evoked commitment language ('I could give it a shot this week') and client-generated solutions. Some change talk was structurally prompted by pre-formed plan options rather than fully client-generated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I half expect to miss Tuesday') was redirected smoothly via concrete contingency planning rather than argument or reassurance. Doubts could have been more explicitly acknowledged as legitimate before pivoting to problem-solving.
