# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental despite Nabil's hostility, validating his anger without endorsing aggression. Affirmations remain brief and generic rather than deeply individualized, with limited normalization of his struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes reasonable reflections of Nabil's perspective but keeps them superficial and pivots quickly to advice. Misses deeper emotional layers such as job fear, implied shame, and his quiet pride after the role-play.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on managing anger around medical delays with no aimless drift. Direction is occasionally driven by the counselor's pre-set strategy menu rather than Nabil's evolving agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session is largely collaborative — Nabil's own words become scripts and the receptionist role-play is a well-executed joint activity. Collaboration is uneven at times, with counselor strategy lists presented before joint exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choice and respects Nabil's rejections and modifications without pressure. Could more explicitly reinforce his ownership of the change rather than framing success around counselor-provided tools.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation through the confidence scale and rehearsal, yielding genuine self-generated insight. Much content is counselor-supplied, with few open questions exploring Nabil's own values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and lets Nabil shape his own script, showing genuine power-sharing. The counselor frequently shifts into expert advice-giving mode, keeping partnership partial rather than full.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through role-play, rehearsal, and the confidence scale, producing commitment language ('I'll give it a go next appointment'). Could deepen by exploring why confidence was 6 and eliciting reasons for change beyond job consequences.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely avoids amplifying sustain talk, skillfully reframing rejected techniques and redirecting blame toward practical action. Occasionally validates external blame ('the system can be lousy'), briefly reinforcing sustain talk.
