# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with genuine affirmations and normalization of potential slips ('a kind fallback if things go sideways'). No judgment, labeling, or conditional regard anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and deep, capturing both content and emotional nuance (e.g., the social pull being stronger than craving). Complex reflections consistently add meaning, including the insightful final reflection on shifting from 'surviving' to 'enjoying.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Steady focus on alcohol use at social gatherings with every exchange advancing the target behavior. Direction is achieved through questions and reflections rather than pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuine partnership of equals — the follow-up plan is co-created and every strategy originated from the client. Explicit 'we'll figure it out together' framing extends to outcome interpretation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice is reinforced throughout ('whatever you drink or don't drink,' 'notes or no notes') with options explicitly presented. The closing reflections actively strengthen the client's sense of ownership, confirmed by his own autonomy language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited almost entirely from the client rather than supplied — no lecturing or advice-giving. Daniel spontaneously generates his 'why,' strategies, fallback, and optimism, all deliberately evoked and attributed to him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positions Daniel as the expert ('You're the expert on these situations,' 'That's your insight, not mine'). All strategies came from the client, and the intervention is built around his contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions (linking smoking success to party moments) directly produced strong change talk and commitment language. The counselor amplifies and attributes insights back to the client, yielding a concrete plan and follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is mostly met with curious exploration rather than argument, effectively pivoting toward change. Early exploration of high-risk moments briefly elaborates reasons for the status quo; sharper double-sided reflections could tighten this.
