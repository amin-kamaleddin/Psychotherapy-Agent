# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, with affirmations like 'that's not small at all.' Held back from a 5 by leaning on positive reframing rather than sitting more fully with Elias's grief and discouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening, including a powerful double-sided reflection that produced Elias's breakthrough ('That's it, exactly'). Occasionally moved to the next question before fully reflecting the emotional depth of his shame and grief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear trajectory from ambivalence to values clarification to a concrete, time-bound plan, with every question tied to the target behavior. Guided progressively while keeping Elias in the driver's seat.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A genuine two-way exchange with a jointly emergent agenda, as confirmed by Elias's own surprise at leaving with his own plan. Slightly leading, polished questions pre-shaped the emotional register of some answers.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly affirmed client control ('You get to set the pace') and offered real choice rather than directives. The plan was entirely Elias's, reflected in his closing 'Small, but it's mine.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn out rather than supplied, with values (light, belonging, his wife) emerging from Elias through open, meaning-centered questions. His own desire and commitment language visibly expanded across the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise repeatedly located in Elias ('You know your body and your daily life better than anyone'), with every plan element originating from the client. Elias's contributions demonstrably shaped the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook cultivation: evoked, reflected, and amplified change talk at every step, with Elias's language escalating from 'maybe that's something' to 'I'd better ask her tonight.' Closed with a projective question eliciting anticipatory success language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk skillfully via selective reflection and double-sided acknowledgment without reinforcing it. The core shame and loss talk ('this is all you've got now') was validated but bypassed rather than resolved toward change.
