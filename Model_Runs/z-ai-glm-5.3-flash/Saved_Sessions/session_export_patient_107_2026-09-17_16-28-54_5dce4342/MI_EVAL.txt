# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance ('no judgment if you slip') and validated the client's attachment to coffee. However, warmth was brief and formulaic, with few explicit affirmations of the client's strengths or honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections captured the ambivalence accurately, but responses frequently pivoted to questions or advice rather than deepening understanding. Defensive statements ('I'm not saying I have a problem') and the client's own motivational framing went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the afternoon coffee target and progressed steadily to a concrete, measurable plan with specific times, tracking, and a check-in. No topic drift; every exchange advanced the agreed experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructed the plan, with the client choosing the change, timing, and template additions that were incorporated immediately. The counselor often presented solutions first and asked for approval, rather than evoking the client's own problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered choice menus, asked permission, and explicitly honored the client's 'data, not a permanent change' framing without pressure or conditions. The client's closing comment—'feels like my idea instead of something I got talked into'—is direct evidence of strong autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within using rulers and open questions, eliciting confidence escalation (6 to 7) and spontaneous change talk. Evocation leaned mechanistic on scales and experiments, and deeper values (e.g., health/blood pressure concerns) were never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned the client as decision-maker ('What would an acceptable, manageable change look like for you?'), with strong client testimony of power-sharing. Supplied many ready-made suggestions (reminders, tips, template drafts) rather than consistently drawing them from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk via importance/confidence rulers and an experiment framing, drawing out meaningful client language ('a tweak is different from quitting,' 'maybe my sleep's worse than I think'). Focused more on strategies than on the client's deeper values and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk and skillfully reframed the data-gathering stance without confrontation. Did not use explicit double-sided reflections linking sustain and change talk, and logistics sometimes displaced ambivalence work.
