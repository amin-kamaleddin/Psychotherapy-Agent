# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, validating Irina's exhaustion and avoiding labels or lecturing. However, warmth became formulaic, with the safety script repeated mechanically despite the client's visible irritation ('You've told me four times now!').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing Irina's exhaustion and guilt ('You're juggling a lot...'). Reflections grew shallower later, replaced by advice lists, and the emotional weight of her guilt and fear of failure was never deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on alcohol use while flexibly incorporating the childcare stressor appropriately as a driver of drinking. The session progressed to a concrete, trackable plan, though closing summaries became repetitive and list-heavy rather than strategically consolidating.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was genuinely co-constructed, with the final plan coming almost entirely from Irina's own words and preferences, shaped through back-and-forth negotiation. Her closing comment — 'I didn't expect to actually agree to anything today' — reflects effective equal-partner work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were consistently framed as Irina's own, her limits were respected ('That's it for now, okay?'), and the plan was negotiated around her capacity. Some deductions for offering option menus prescriptively rather than inviting her to generate her own alternatives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective evocation occurred, but much of the session shifted to counselor-provided solution lists (swaps, urge tools, breathing techniques) rather than drawing motivation and solutions from the client. No confidence/importance exploration and little elaboration on her values beyond initial reflections.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed client expertise through permission-seeking, offering choice ('Which of these feels doable?'), and building the plan from her suggestions. Occasional slips into advice-list mode rather than consistently eliciting her own solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was effectively evoked and reinforced — Irina offered 'maybe I could try one drink instead of two' and connected reduced drinking to improved childcare. Missed opportunities to deepen commitment language, such as confidence rulers or exploring what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided argument when Irina minimized the problem, responding with curious exploration instead. However, some reflections restated sustain talk without double-sided framing, and there weren't always deliberate pivots back to change after her justifications.
