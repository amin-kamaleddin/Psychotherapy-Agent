# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations of Maria's reliability and honesty, and no criticism of her resistance to tasks. Positive regard was somewhat formulaic and limited beyond safety-focused praise.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good empathic reflections (loneliness, exit-door metaphor), but reflections were sparse and quickly followed by risk questions and checklists. Maria's explicit request for understanding over 'papers and numbers' went largely unmet.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on safety: risk assessment, means restriction, a 24-hour delay, grounding exercise, and concrete follow-through. Occasionally mechanical and narrowly task-driven, neglecting Maria's request for emotional focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maria's input shaped the session (deferring the pill task, breathing, music), and plans were co-created. However, collaboration often felt like offering menus of counselor-generated solutions rather than genuine joint problem-solving, with a transactional tone she pushed against.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Steps were consistently framed as choices, and Maria's refusal to do the box mid-call was respected. Options were sometimes pre-packaged menus rather than open-ended, and crisis-line directives felt somewhat pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied heavily on closed safety questions and option menus rather than open questions. Significant change talk (her mother, the album memory, keeping her word) surfaced spontaneously but was never explored or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and deferred to Maria's pacing, allowing her contributions to shape the session. The task-driven checklist stance Maria resisted, with little explicit acknowledgment of her as the expert on her own life, limited the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk was elicited and reinforced (24-hour delay, moving pills, keeping her word, the album memory). However, the counselor never deepened or expanded on these moments, leaving evocation shallow.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('Nothing works,' 'it's just more of the same') was not amplified, and the counselor acknowledged feelings before redirecting. Little strategic softening—no double-sided reflections or reframing to help resolve ambivalence.
