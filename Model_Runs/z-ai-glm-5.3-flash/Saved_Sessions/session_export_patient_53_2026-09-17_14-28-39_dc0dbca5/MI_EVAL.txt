# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental tone throughout, honoring the client's requests with no lecturing or disapproval. Falls short of 5 due to brief, formulaic affirmations with few individualized statements affirming Isabella's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were adequate, but most responses were dense with advice rather than reflective listening. Emotional disclosures like 'I've been telling myself it'd be awful for so long' were met with planning logistics instead of deepened reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Crystal-clear focus on community engagement via small outside steps with no topic drift, progressively refining the plan through to completion and a scheduled next step. A clear strength of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some equal-partner elements existed (negotiating check-in style, adjusting plans), but the counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role with multi-item advice menus. The client's best discoveries emerged despite, not because of, the counselor's elicitation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were consistently framed as optional and the client's preferences (no backup alarm, no lecturing) were honored without pressure. Slightly undercut by 'You've got this' cheerleading and failure to explicitly affirm Isabella as the author of her success.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor occasionally conveyed that motivation lies within the client, eliciting client-owned change talk and a self-generated insight. However, evocation was superficial, shifting to solutions and leaving key motivational material like 'I didn't hate it' unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partnership-style questions and genuine responsiveness to client preferences were present, but the counselor supplied most of the plan rather than eliciting Isabella's own ideas. Her expertise (e.g., the alarm-across-the-room strategy) went unexploited until she volunteered it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was successfully evoked through open questions and choice, yielding commitments and a completed walk. But pivotal moments like her insight about telling herself it'd be awful passed without deepening via confidence or importance questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally without reinforcement, but never strategically softened. No double-sided reflections were used, and the counselor's accommodation of 'don't make it a big deal' bypassed a chance to reframe her shame.
