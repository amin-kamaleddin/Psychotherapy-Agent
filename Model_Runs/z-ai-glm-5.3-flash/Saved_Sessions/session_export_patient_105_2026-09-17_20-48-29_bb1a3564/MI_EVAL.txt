# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance with genuine affirmations and normalized ambivalence, never pressuring or labeling the client's avoidance. Falls short of 5 only because warmth occasionally reads as formulaic rather than fully conveying unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and often captures meaning beyond content, including validation of the client's skepticism. However, some reflections shift to evaluative praise ('that's a genuinely useful insight') rather than pure understanding, and deeper emotional content like fear of freezing or shame about canceling goes under-explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on managing social anxiety around photography jobs and builds momentum toward a concrete behavioral commitment. It stops short of 5 due to occasional looping back to re-affirm progress and a slightly diffuse forward probe near the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely two-sided, with the plan co-constructed and the counselor inviting the client's input throughout. A few exchanges have a slightly Socratic feel where the counselor's questions shape the direction rather than the client's agenda fully steering.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership are consistently reinforced, with no persuasion, pressure, or prescribed advice anywhere in the session. The counselor repeatedly emphasizes that the motivation and ideas came from the client herself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully elicits motivation from within the client through open questions ('what would feel like the smallest, most manageable piece'), with every idea and commitment emerging from the client. The pivotal self-insight about trusting herself was developed into a full, client-authored change plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly locates expertise in the client ('You know your work and your patterns better than anyone') and credits her as the origin of every plan element. This is textbook power sharing where the client's contributions substantially shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is the session's greatest strength: the counselor strategically evokes change talk, reflects it back, and deepens it until the client articulates desire, commitment, and a concrete plan—all client-generated. The amplification of 'a better maybe than I had coming in here' exemplifies high-level change talk cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor never reinforces sustain talk, instead using double-sided reflections that hold hope and doubt side by side without amplifying doubt. The client's own resolution ('canceling stuff hasn't exactly made my life better either') suggests effective softening, though the counselor occasionally dwells briefly on the skeptical part before pivoting.
