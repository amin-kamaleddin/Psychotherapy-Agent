# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard; no judgment about the piercing. Affirmations of client's self-care ("you already show up to dental visits, which tells me looking after yourself matters to you"). Tone remained warm and respectful even when the client dismissed the dentist's concerns; no disapproval or labeling conveyed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening throughout (e.g., reflecting bias perception and basic self-care). Pivotal reflection—"the thing you like and the thing you'd want to protect are kind of in the same spot"—was meaning-making and resonated deeply ("Huh. I never really thought about it like that"). Reflections were consistently accurate, not repetitive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior from open to close. Each exchange built progressively: clarifying stance → defining client's own evidence criteria → identifying most concerning outcome (chipping) → generating her own mitigation (shorter stud) → concrete commitments. No topic drift; strong forward momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Interaction functioned between equal partners. No lecturing or prescribing; every plan element originated from the client ("that's my kind of answer"). Counselor invited input at every step and adapted to client preferences ("no big system needed" accepted without imposing structure); decisions were co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Ownership consistently reinforced; "on your evidence, your timeline" framing. Genuine choice in questions (piercer vs. dentist vs. checking the spot first). Plan repeatedly framed as "completely yours." Client's closing remark—"less preachy than I expected"—confirms strong autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Fundamentally evocative rather than persuasive; no advice given. Motivation drawn from client's own values (appearance, permanence of chipping, autonomy). "Where does that leave you?" elicited her self-generated solution (shorter stud). Risk criteria, confidence rating, and her own reasons ("to prove the dentist wrong") were systematically elicited and developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Expertise positioned within the client: "That's you taking charge in a way that works for you," "it's completely yours," "do you have your own way of keeping track?" Counselor explicitly accepted the client's framing ("proving them wrong if you can. That works for me"), showing her contributions genuinely shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation and deepening of change talk: "What would count as something actually shows up?" and "Which one would worry you most?" produced strong commitment language ("I'd take it seriously... even about the stud"), a client-generated solution, concrete steps, and high confidence. Confidence scaling question with follow-up ("what makes it that number and not lower?")—textbook technique.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4 / 5
**Comments:** Counselor avoided arguing against sustain talk and pivoted toward client-generated reasons for vigilance. Sustain talk reflected neutrally ("I hear that it's part of your look and you like it") then redirected. Minor area for growth: brief moments amplified sustain talk (e.g., "worth keeping an eye on, or not really?"), though this served strategic evocation and was effectively channeled toward change-oriented planning.

---
**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.9/5**

*Summary: Exceptionally strong MI session with a client in pre-contemplation. Flawless autonomy support and collaboration transformed a resistant client into one generating her own change plan; masterful evocation produced a client-generated harm-reduction solution (shorter stud) and concrete commitments. The pivotal reflection linking the stud's placement to the client's fear of chipping is a textbook sophisticated MI move. Confidence scaling and implementation planning (linking checks to brushing) demonstrate advanced skill. Minor improvement possible in avoiding brief amplification of sustain talk.*
