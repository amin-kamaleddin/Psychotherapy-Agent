# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates warmth and unconditional positive regard, normalizing ambivalence and welcoming the patient's grumbling. There is no judgment, labeling, or disapproval anywhere in the session, even when the patient admits avoidance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections land precisely and go beyond surface content to the emotional function of the behavior (e.g., avoidance as keeping worry at arm's length). The patient explicitly confirms the reflections, showing they deepened his insight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (charging and using the device), with every question and reflection tying back to it. The session progresses logically from exploring ambivalence to a concrete plan with a built-in two-week review.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is fully collaborative, with the plan co-constructed from the patient's own ideas (kettle, timing, check-ins). The patient explicitly notes it is 'an actual plan, not just me being told what to do,' and the 'Deal?' exchange demonstrates equal-partner interaction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces the patient's ownership and choice, offering genuine options and framing the plan as tentative and client-controlled ('if it's not working in a couple weeks, we adjust'). The patient's self-imposed limits are accepted without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key element of the plan originates from the patient: the timing idea, the visibility strategy from his past success, his self-identified warning sign ('the tomorrow-ing'), and his check-in preference. The counselor draws out self-motivators rather than supplying motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly and repeatedly positions the patient as the expert on his own life, and his contributions directly shape the session. The patient notices the difference: 'That's an actual plan, not just me being told what to do.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evokes change talk through open questions that elicit the patient's own solutions, then reinforces each instance. This leads to strong commitment language ('I'll actually do it. First time in a while I've said that and sort of meant it') and a concrete self-generated plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoids amplifying sustain talk, briefly acknowledging doubt then redirecting toward change. A few reflections (e.g., about lacking energy) gave airtime to the sustain side, though always within balanced double-sided reflections.
