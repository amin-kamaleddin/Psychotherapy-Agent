# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirmations and normalization, though praise is somewhat formulaic and often paired with advice-giving rather than individualized affirmation of Nadine's strengths as a mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections are accurate, but as the session progresses reflections become repetitive and are quickly followed by questions or advice, leaving the emotional weight of her exhaustion, loneliness, and partner resentment largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on infant sleep and parental exhaustion, moving efficiently from assessment to a concrete, dated, measurable plan, with only brief, clinically relevant drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated check-ins, negotiated follow-up, and honoring of Nadine's preferences demonstrate good collaboration, though the counselor's expertise dominates the content with few invitations for Nadine's own problem-solving ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong use of choice menus and permission-asking, with Nadine's decisions driving the plan, but advice is dense and prescriptive and the counselor rarely reinforces her ownership of outcomes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions come predominantly from the counselor rather than being drawn out from Nadine; her own ideas are not amplified, and core motivational content is counselor-supplied, the defining MI deficit here.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor solicits Nadine's input and negotiates plans, but the expert-driven structure—with counselor-supplied milestone lists, trackers, and scripts—limits genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice menus effectively elicited commitment language ('I want to try. I need this to work'), but pivotal moments of change talk were not deepened; the counselor pivoted to advice instead of exploring reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about exhaustion and lack of backup was acknowledged but not strategically softened; no double-sided reflections pairing doubts with strengths, though the counselor never amplified reasons against change.
