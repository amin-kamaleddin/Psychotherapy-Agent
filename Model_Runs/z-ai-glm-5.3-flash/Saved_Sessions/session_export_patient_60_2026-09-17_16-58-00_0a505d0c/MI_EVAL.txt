# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validates Jason's frustration and affirms his performance without judgment, but praise stays focused on the rehearsed script rather than his personal strengths. Missed opportunities to affirm his honesty about drinking and his self-awareness, especially important given his pre-contemplative stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections are accurate (feeling singled out, worrying about his record and housing), but reflections remain focused on logistics rather than underlying feelings. A significant empathic lapse occurred when Jason's disclosure about not remembering 'chunks of that night' went unacknowledged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintains clear focus and strong structure, moving steadily toward a concrete outcome (informal resolution, rehearsed statements, next steps). The only weakness is that the target drifted from exploring alcohol use toward hearing preparation, despite the disclosure of memory blackouts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early collaboration is genuine — agenda check, permission-seeking, and options framed around Jason's values. However, the session degrades into a coaching rehearsal where the clinician authors the content and Jason merely performs it, creating uneven power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options are framed as choices ('Which option sounds most doable?') and Jason's edit of the script is honored. However, heavy scaffolding — handing Jason scripted lines to recite — controls his narrative rather than eliciting it, and his ownership of the change process is never explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The best evocative question ('What would an acceptable outcome look like?') targets the conduct outcome rather than behavior change. Jason's motivations are never drawn out, and his own most change-relevant material (memory gaps) is raised by him and entirely passed over.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong start with invitations for Jason's perspective and permission-seeking before advising. But once options are laid out, the interaction becomes clinician-driven — Jason rehearses the clinician's words rather than generating his own, leaving relevant expertise with the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician never asks about Jason's own reasons to reconsider drinking, never explores the discrepancy between 'I don't have a problem' and his memory blackouts, and elicits no desire, ability, need, or commitment language regarding alcohol. Jason's spontaneous change talk ('I could be more careful about which parties I go to') goes unreinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jason's heavy sustain talk ('I don't have a problem,' 'this isn't my fault,' 'the course is probably a joke') is absorbed without reinforcement — a plus — but rarely pivoted back toward change. No double-sided reflections or development of discrepancy between Jason's values (record, housing, future) and his drinking behavior.
