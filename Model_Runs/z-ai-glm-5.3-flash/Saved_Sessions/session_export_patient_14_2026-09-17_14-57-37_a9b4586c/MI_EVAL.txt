# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating the client's frustration about the 45-minute wait without defensiveness and never showing disapproval of his dismissive attitude. Warmth was somewhat transactional and efficiency-oriented, fitting the client's style but lacking deeper individualized affirmation of him as a person beyond the tasks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening, accurately capturing the client's opening anger and his core dilemma of juggling time while wanting one practical change. Reflections tended to be brief summaries that quickly pivoted to structured options, leaving the emotional weight of his breathlessness and health fears underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior from the second turn onward, channeling the session efficiently through behavior selection, confidence building, backup planning, and concrete commitments without drift. The session progressed logically from engagement to action, even using the client's need to leave as a way to consolidate the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction functioned largely between equals, with the client's practical ideas (auto-order, glovebox system, calendar entry) genuinely shaping and driving the plan. The rapid-fire menu format created a somewhat transactional, counselor-structured rhythm, and detailed clinical guidance was delivered in list form rather than negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced client choice and ownership through opening focus choice, option menus rather than prescriptions, and contingency framing that positioned the counselor as a resource rather than a director. Joint decision-making was clear throughout, even when the client asked about future directions for treatment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within the client using the confidence ruler and 'what would raise you to an 8?' questions, leveraging his identity as a numbers man as intrinsic motivation. Missed opportunities included not exploring the deeper personal meaning of getting his weight under control and not eliciting his own ideas before presenting structured options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client's expertise as central, with his choices and systems genuinely driving the session. Occasionally took the expert role by supplying detailed clinical instructions without first asking what the client already knew, and the menus were counselor-generated rather than client-generated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and built on the client's change talk using evocative questions, the confidence ruler ('What might raise your confidence to an 8?'), and commitment-securing questions about timing. Deepening of desire/need talk about what getting the weight under control would mean for him was less developed, preventing a higher score.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided amplifying the client's substantial sustain talk about time pressure, reframing it as a design constraint and briefly acknowledging it without dwelling. The counselor did not argue when the client rejected walking; however, rapid practical problem-solving sometimes gave more airtime to solutions than to resolving ambivalence.
