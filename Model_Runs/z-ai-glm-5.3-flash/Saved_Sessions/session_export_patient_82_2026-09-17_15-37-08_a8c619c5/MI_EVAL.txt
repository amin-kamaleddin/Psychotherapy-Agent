# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates respect and nonjudgment throughout, validating Lukas's hesitancy and refusing to label or dispute his minimization. Missed opportunities for specific, individualized affirmations of his strengths (scholarship athlete, four years of responsibility) keep it from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and captures Lukas's core identity conflict between achievement and peer belonging. However, poignant emotional material (2am rumination, lingering regret about trying alcohol) was moved past rather than deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use from the opening question through to a concrete action plan. The arc from exploration to prioritization to planning to implementation is coherent and well-sequenced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functions largely between equals—Lukas's own ideas shaped the plan, and the counselor flexed when he pushed back on overplanning. Occasionally undermined by multi-question bundles that made dialogue feel checklist-like.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces ownership: never pressures toward abstinence, endorses harm-reduction, and lets Lukas select and adapt strategies. His closing statement ('more like I walked into it myself') is direct evidence that autonomy support succeeded.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out Lukas's own reasons for change and self-generated solutions rather than lecturing. Confidence rulers with follow-up questions are textbook evocation, producing measurable movement from 5 to 8.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing—the plan (script, soda strategy, Danny) came almost entirely from Lukas, with the counselor offering menus rather than prescriptions. Sometimes offers option menus too rapidly, crowding the client's generative space.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently evokes and deepens change talk through open questions and confidence rulers, with confidence rising from 5 to 8 across the session. Lukas produced commitment language, self-motivated reasons, and ownership statements that the counselor developed rather than bypassed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Does not argue with sustain talk, responding instead with accurate reflections and pivoting to Lukas's own workable solutions while accepting harm-reduction. Missed chances for strategic double-sided reflections linking sustain talk back to change.
