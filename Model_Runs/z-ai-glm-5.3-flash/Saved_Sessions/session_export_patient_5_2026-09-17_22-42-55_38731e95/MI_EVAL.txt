# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, reframing the client's caution as a value rather than a difficulty. No disapproval or labeling appeared, even when the client admitted an unhelpful instinct.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and frequent, mirroring the client's values and emotions. Reflections occasionally pivoted quickly to questions rather than deepening the emotional nuance, such as the frustration with the rushed appointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on medication education and safe citalopram initiation throughout, with each exchange progressing logically toward concrete steps. The closing summary confirmed no topic drift and strong momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully collaborative, with the psychiatrist soliciting input before offering information and the plan co-constructed from the client's routines. Knowledge flowed both ways, with the client's self-knowledge shaping the outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforced client ownership, and advice was offered as information while decisions remained with the client. No pressure appeared; the client's choices defined every element of the final plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solutions were drawn from within the client — her diary, pharmacist relationship, and morning routine became the plan's architecture. Motivation for adherence was largely assumed rather than explicitly evoked and developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positioned the client as the expert, and her contributions genuinely shaped the session. This was true power-sharing, with the client's expertise substantially driving the outcome.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively elicited the client's commitment and planning language, which she responded to strongly and the psychiatrist reinforced. Focus leaned heavily toward planning rather than sustained amplification of motivation and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The skipping-dose concern was gently reframed with a practical alternative, and nervousness was validated then redirected toward her strengths. Few opportunities arose to demonstrate strategic redirection, and one moment was simply answered rather than pivoted.
