# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard with affirmations like 'That's not small stuff, Rosa' and validation of her fears. No labeling, disapproval, or pressure at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, deepening reflections throughout (e.g., contrasting the self-generated hopeful picture with borrowed horror stories). Rosa confirms the reflections land ('When you put it like that... I never noticed').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation, progressively moving from readiness to a concrete first step (talking to Carmen) and then to planning. Each turn advances momentum without wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is genuinely co-constructed ('let's think together'), with Rosa shaping the agenda and requesting information. The counselor follows her lead rather than imposing direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rosa is consistently positioned as expert and decision-maker, and her closing 'I'll decide—not my sister, not the horror stories. Me' demonstrates internalized ownership. Permission-oriented questions reinforce choice over pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within via open questions about what matters most, eliciting Rosa's own reasons (wedding photos, grandkids). Rosa explicitly notes the power of voicing her motivations herself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rosa is explicitly framed as the expert on her own life, and her questions drive the session's agenda. The counselor follows her lead and shares information only when she requests it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk, from 'what's different about that version of you?' to eliciting a first step. Rosa's language strengthens from 'maybe' to 'I don't want to leave here with nothing.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally redirected well through double-sided reflections and the powerful reframe contrasting self-generated hope with borrowed fear. Minor deductions for briefly lingering on fear content and ending with psychoeducation rather than handing meaning back to Rosa.
