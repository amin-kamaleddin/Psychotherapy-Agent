# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally respectful and nonjudgmental, but risk-screening boilerplate and alarmist framing (e.g., 'heavy and could mean dependence') read as subtly judgmental. Few affirmations of Erik's strengths or honesty limit warmth of positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; responses are dominated by information-giving and question lists. Key emotional moments — fear of his father's toe amputation, withdrawal worries — go entirely unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains consistent focus on alcohol reduction, diet, and diabetes, building a concrete plan (labs, two-night trial, wife involvement, follow-up). However, direction is achieved through directive information delivery rather than collaborative goal-shaping.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input and incorporates some of Erik's preferences, but long expert-driven information blocks dominate and the agenda is never jointly negotiated. Erik's best ideas shaped the plan spontaneously rather than through structured exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission and endorses Erik's trial-first approach, but much of the session is prescriptive. Erik's request for reassurance ('I can always go back after, right?') misses a key opportunity to affirm his choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is treated as residing in the counselor's advice; virtually no open-ended questions about Erik's own reasons, values, or confidence. His change talk surfaced largely on his own rather than through skillful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor frequently assumes the expert role with didactic monologues on labs, diet, and craving management. Despite Erik noting the contrast with his doctor, much of the session repeats the advice-giving pattern he disliked.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partially capitalizes on Erik's readiness, and he generates meaningful change talk (two-night trial, asking where to start with diet). However, the counselor never deliberately evokes or deepens it with open questions or scaling probes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'm not an alcoholic', 'beer's been my routine') is mostly bypassed rather than acknowledged and reframed. Missed chances for double-sided reflections, e.g., pairing 'you feel fine' with his father's amputation fear.
