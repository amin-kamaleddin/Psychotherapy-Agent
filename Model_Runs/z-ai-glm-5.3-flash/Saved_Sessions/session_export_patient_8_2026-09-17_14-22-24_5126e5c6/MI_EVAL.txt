# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout ('slips are data, not failure'), explicitly acknowledged by the client ('you didn't just lecture me'). However, warmth is somewhat formulaic, with repeated checklist-style plan recitations rather than deeper personal regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ('Totally understandable to feel twitchy'), but most turns skip reflection and jump to advice or checklists. Emotionally meaningful content — restlessness about cutting back, attachment to friends, fear of failing again — goes unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (nighttime social media use) across the entire session. Every turn advances toward a concrete goal, ending with a specific commitment and a scheduled check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration — the client chooses between options, adjusts times, and selects replacement activities. But the counselor generates all plans and structures while the client merely negotiates details.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices and incorporates the client's preferences (10 PM start, music wind-down, exam mode). Still, the interaction channels her toward pre-built options, and prescriptive reminders slightly undercut client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicits the client's own motivations, values, or ideas; open-ended questions are nearly absent after the first turn. The client's self-generated reasons are acknowledged but never drawn out or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor functions largely as expert prescriber, delivering pre-designed plans, step-by-step instructions, and repeated reminders. The client's input shapes details, but the agenda and solutions originate almost entirely from the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Readiness rulers did elicit movement (3 to 6-7), but change talk otherwise emerges from the client unprompted. No open questions exploring her reasons for change, no elaboration after change talk, no affirmations of stated motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with sustain talk and normalizes it ('That attachment is totally normal'). However, key sustain talk — group chat, exam stress, past failed timers — is accommodated rather than strategically reframed or explored as compatible with change.
