# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard to a guarded teen—normalizing avoidance, affirming self-protectiveness, and respecting her terms ('maybe means maybe'). No judgment or disapproval despite blame and dismissive language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, layered reflections throughout, including the deep reflection about 'feeling stupid for believing someone,' which prompted further disclosure. Reflections clearly landed with the client.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on peer conflict and social reconnection, guiding toward a concrete step and a return visit. Early morning-avoidance exploration was somewhat open-ended, and blame-focused grievances got some air time before redirecting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was genuinely co-constructed, with the client's own ideas shaping the entire plan. The counselor matched her casual register without being patronizing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent autonomy support: 'It's your call, your pace,' honoring 'no promises,' and protecting confidentiality. Explicitly avoided pressure even as the client neared commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never supplied reasons for change—every motivation came from the client. Open questions drew out her own values and solutions, culminating in a self-generated concrete step.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly located expertise within the client ('you know the situation better than anyone'). Her ideas drove the session direction, exemplifying high partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful strategic evocation: selectively reflecting pre-change talk, highlighting hope, amplifying, and eliciting a small commitment. The client's language escalated to 'I'll text back. Maybe' and 'I'm thinking about it.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally did not dwell on sustain talk, validating caution before pivoting to change. Minor deduction for briefly repeating pessimistic framing rather than using tighter double-sided reflections.
