# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Jordan's ambivalence with statements like 'That makes sense.' However, affirmations were formulaic and repeated ('thoughtful and practical'), lacking deep, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections captured Jordan's ambivalence well, but most responses quickly pivoted to information-giving. Emotional dimensions — his guilt about his grandparents ('I'd never forgive myself'), dismissiveness, and flat affect — went largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear focus on flu vaccination and progressed logically from exploration to a concrete plan. The direction felt somewhat pre-set toward vaccination rather than jointly developed, limiting client ownership.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan chose the pharmacy, timing, and check-in schedule, but information flow was largely one-directional, with the counselor delivering expert content in dense blocks. Jordan was positioned as a plan approver rather than a co-creator, and his own knowledge was never invited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menus and preference questions were offered, but the structure consistently funneled Jordan toward vaccination without genuinely holding the decision open. His persistent 'still feels pointless for me personally' was never met with respect for the possibility of not getting vaccinated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew out the client's own motivation — family protection — rather than arguing from external authority. However, once family protection surfaced, evocation stopped; Jordan's personal reasons for self-protection were never elicited, leaving motivation other-directed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered, but the overall stance was expert-driven, with long blocks of clinical information delivered without asking what Jordan wanted to know. He shaped logistics but not the substance of the health discussion.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Desire, ability, and commitment language was effectively elicited and reinforced, culminating in a concrete plan. Missed opportunities to deepen change talk, particularly around Jordan's guilt ('I'd never forgive myself') and his own reasons for self-protection.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not amplified, but softening occurred by omission rather than strategy. No double-sided reflections paired sustain talk with change talk, and Jordan's ambivalence was never explicitly resolved for himself.
