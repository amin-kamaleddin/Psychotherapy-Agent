# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated respect and warmth without judgment, never lecturing even when the patient described online misinformation. Affirmations like 'good question' and 'strong, thoughtful choice' reinforced unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent reflections tracked Elena's emotional state from guarded ambivalence to relief, including family judgment and social pressure. The patient explicitly confirmed feeling deeply understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on vaccine hesitancy and moved purposefully from exploring concerns to a concrete commitment to schedule vaccination. Each exchange advanced toward the target behavior with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-way: the counselor offered menus of options, role-played at Elena's request, and co-created scripts. Elena's preferences substantially shaped each phase of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elena's ownership of the decision was consistently reinforced through choice-offering and respect for her 'I'm not saying I'll change my mind' stance. No persuasive pressure was used; she arrived at scheduling voluntarily.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Elena's values ('What would doing right look like to you?'), producing strong change talk. However, much of the session's momentum came from requested psychoeducation rather than techniques like confidence rulers or elaboration on motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Elena as the decision-maker, with her choices directly shaping the session direction. Slightly reduced by information-heavy segments that were somewhat counselor-directed, with less explicit acknowledgment of her expertise as a parent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions successfully evoked strong change talk ('That's not a risk I want on my conscience,' 'I think I want to schedule her shot'), which the counselor affirmed. Some momentum, however, came from information provision rather than deliberate evocation techniques.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally without reinforcement or debate, and softened naturally as Elena self-corrected. The pivots relied partly on education rather than skilled double-sided reflections.
