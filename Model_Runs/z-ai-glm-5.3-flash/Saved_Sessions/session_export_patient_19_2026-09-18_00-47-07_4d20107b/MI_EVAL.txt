# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with specific and warm affirmations, notably avoiding any shaming reference to the theft or probation context. However, affirmations follow a formulaic template, and unconditional regard is never explicitly stated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurate, with genuine emotional attunement to fear, shame, and family dynamics. Deductions for occasionally interpretive reflections (e.g., 'grieving') and moving on too quickly from emotionally loaded moments without letting them sit.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well-anchored to gambling, debt, and the sustaining lifestyle, with a light agenda menu at the start. Key deduction: after commitment was voiced, the session regresses to re-exploring costs and closes without a summary, plan confirmation, or follow-up; the referral context is also never explicitly negotiated into the agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuine equal-partner exchange from start to finish, with no expert-driven content, psychoeducation, or prescribed homework at any point. The plan was entirely co-constructed from the client's own materials, with the psychiatrist serving purely as reflective structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Every decision point is returned to the client with no pressure-to-comply language, and the client visibly experienced ownership. Slight deductions for a gentle nudge toward public commitment and importing a clinician-supplied timeframe ('this week').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questioning is clearly designed to draw out the client's own reasons, strategies, and commitment, with 'what would it mean' formulations working well. However, evocation is weighted toward reasons and commitment while confidence is left underdeveloped, leaving the commitment resting on desire rather than elicited self-belief.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The strongest domain: expertise is explicitly and repeatedly located in the client, substantively proven by the fact that every plan element originated from Anya herself. The counselor contributed nothing but reflections and questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nearly every turn contains an evocative question that moved the client from preparatory change talk to a dated, specific commitment ('This Friday. The highest-limit one first'). Deductions for never directly building self-efficacy (no scaling or past-success exploration) and for ending with motivation elicited but commitment not consolidated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently defused through double-sided reflections and never argued with or problem-solved directly. Minor deduction for re-invoking the 'hope of escaping debt' rationalization late in the session, subtly re-arming the status quo argument after commitment was voiced.
