# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirmed the patient's worth and efforts ("that sounds like the Sofia who's been getting back up since fifth grade"), separating her identity from the blame she carries. Unconditional positive regard was maintained throughout, even when the patient expressed avoidance and self-criticism.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both surface and underlying feelings—e.g., "It's exhausting when one person's comment can wreck a day you can't control" and naming the ambivalence about the friend ("part of you misses talking to her, and part of you is wary"). The patient responded by deepening her self-exploration.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear focus on reconnecting with the friend as a pathway to making school more manageable, skillfully steering away from avoidance and toward a concrete, patient-generated plan (texting, weekend, quiet moment).

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the patient as the expert: "You know your situation better than anyone," "You know her and how things ended way better than I do." Solutions were drawn entirely from the patient, with the clinician acting as a guide rather than an advice-giver.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Every choice was returned to the patient: "What feels most like you: leading with the truth about the rumors, or keeping it light first?" The clinician reinforced the patient's own decision-making and self-efficacy ("you figured this out yourself").

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Open questions consistently elicited the patient's own reasons, hopes, and coping capacities ("What do you think would help make those 'leave me alone' days happen more often?"). The patient generated the plan, confidence-building strategies, and self-talk independently.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** The interview flowed as a shared exploration, with the clinician building on each of the patient's contributions. The patient's closing remark—"I came in here thinking this was gonna be another adult lecturing me... it feels kinda doable"—demonstrates the success of the partnership stance.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Change talk was systematically cultivated and reflected: "maybe I'd text something small and see," "I think I'm gonna try it." The clinician slowed down to consolidate commitment and used a confidence ruler (1–10), which the patient answered ("a 6... maybe 6 and a half") and then raised herself to an 8 through her own planning.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Sustain talk (avoidance, "it's not up to me," fear of being screenshotted or ignored) was met with reflection rather than confrontation. The clinician highlighted the patient's own counter-statements ("you noticed yourself expecting the worst and questioned whether that fits who she actually is"), allowing doubt to soften naturally.
