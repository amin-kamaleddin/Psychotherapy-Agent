# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently validated the patient's autonomy and perspective, explicitly framing the patient as the expert on their own health.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective statements like 'That pause you just had sounds important' demonstrated deep attunement to the patient's ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation skillfully guided the patient from dismissing vaccination to identifying their own reason (protecting grandparents) and committing to a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open-ended questions like 'What would have to be true for a vaccine to feel worth considering?' consistently invited the patient's input and positioned them as the decision-maker.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized 'my choice, my reason,' and the patient explicitly noted that avoiding a lecture prevented heel-digging resistance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was drawn directly from the patient (e.g., 'I'd feel awful if I gave them something') rather than imposed, with skillful amplification of the grandparents theme.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician worked alongside the patient, following their lead on the grandparents' topic and asking what support they wanted at the end.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effective use of evocative questions ('What would protect them most?') and reflections that deepened the patient's own commitment language throughout.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I don't need it') was reflected without judgment and gently juxtaposed with emerging change talk rather than confronted.
