# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard — validates ambivalence, affirms honesty, and never judges continued use or defensiveness. Client explicitly confirms feeling accepted: 'thanks for not lecturing me.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, deep reflections that capture complex ambivalence and emotional subtext ('carrying secrets is heavy'), often mirroring the client's exact language. Reflections repeatedly landed, evidenced by progressively vulnerable disclosures each turn.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, progressive focus on meth use and harm from opening to close with no topic drift. Each turn advances momentum from the infection to safety strategies, values, and concrete next steps while remaining client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly frames the work as joint ('let's figure it out together') and co-constructs plans from the client's own ideas at his pace. Client confirms the collaborative tone: 'this was more like talking to a person.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced — honors his request to decide by Friday, gives no directives or unsolicited advice, and frames accomplishments as his own. Client's closing ('small stuff, but it's mine') shows internalized ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly all motivation is elicited from the client; every key insight ('being more awake,' 'tired of heavy,' the sister plan) originates with him. Questions systematically draw out desire, need, ability, and commitment rather than arguing for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly locates expertise in the client ('you're the expert on your world'), and the session agenda is shaped substantially by his hesitations and requests. Textbook power sharing throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and develops change talk — the client generates his own three-step plan, states commitment ('that's locked in'), and expresses readiness ('maybe I'll have told my sister'). The counselor reflects and amplifies each change statement rather than supplying her own.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids reinforcing sustain talk, using double-sided reflections and pivoting to change without amplifying minimization ('I'm not some addict'). A point off because a few reflections briefly restated sustain content before pivoting — appropriate technique, but not always a perfect or concise pivot.
