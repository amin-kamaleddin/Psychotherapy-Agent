# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple affirmations validate the patient's competence ('you've just shown me you have plenty of ideas') and expertise about his own smoking. The clinician consistently communicates unconditional positive regard despite the patient's initial hostility.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both surface content and underlying feeling ('being treated like the problem instead of a patient — that's a real sting'). The double-sided reflection of ambivalence demonstrates sophisticated listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a clear focus on the patient's own motivations for change without imposing an agenda. Questions are deliberately sequenced to deepen exploration (dental work → values → first steps).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is framed as joint work throughout ('figure out together, in your style'), and the patient's agenda (the dental appointment) is respected rather than overridden. The patient explicitly notes feeling heard for the first time.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is excellent in content ('your choice and your timing', 'I'm not signing you up for anything today'), and resistance is rolled with skillfully. A minor deduction for continuing to ask questions after the patient twice signaled the session should end ('Now truly, can I go?').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully draws out the patient's own reasons for change — pride, his father's example, his wife's worry — rather than supplying them. Questions like 'what would need to be different this time' surface values-based motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent partnership language ('for us to figure out together') positions the patient as the expert on his own life. Power dynamics are explicitly rebalanced against the patient's history of being lectured.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Emergent change talk is consistently noticed, reflected, and strengthened ('maybe that'd be worth something', 'I could talk to someone... something gentler'). The clinician builds momentum toward a realistic, self-generated next step without pressure.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk and resistance are met with reflection rather than argument, and the patient visibly softens across the session ('this was different', 'I'll not dismiss the idea altogether'). No confrontation or corrective feedback appears anywhere in the exchange.
