# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental responses like 'That doesn't mean you're doing it wrong' and affirmations such as 'that's brave and practical' convey positive regard. Some praise felt formulaic and lacked individualized affirmations of Nadine's strengths as a mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately captured Nadine's exhaustion and desire for reassurance, and normalizing frequent waking eased her fears. Occasionally pivoted to questions or advice too quickly and left deeper guilt material ('something's wrong with her—or with me') unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on improving infant sleep and Nadine's rest throughout, with every exchange advancing toward a concrete plan, log, backup, and follow-up timeline. No topic drift; the session built purposefully toward commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session felt like joint problem-solving, with the plan co-constructed and Nadine's backup ideas incorporated. Several exchanges (dream-feed steps, log format) became clinician-directed instruction rather than drawing on Nadine's own knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently offered choices ('share ideas or brainstorm?', direct feed vs. bottle, check-in timing), letting Nadine drive the plan. Some informational content like safe-sleep and pumping guidance was delivered prescriptively without first asking what she knew.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions about what a working routine and a meaningful win would look like evoked strong change talk ('I can do that,' 'I've got this'). Advice sometimes preempted evocation, and deeper motivations around motherhood and self were reflected but not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Nadine as the expert, asking her preferences on feeds, plans, and follow-up timing, and offering to adjust the partner script. Expert-led instructional moments slightly reduced the collaborative feel.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked commitment language ('That's my plan,' 'I've got this') through questions about goals and wins, and reinforced it by reflecting it back. Limited deepening of hope statements and of confidence talk about her capacity as a mother beyond the plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to helplessness with empathy, normalization, and redirection toward actionable change, avoiding dwelling on sustain talk. Some fears of giving up quickly were not explicitly reframed with double-sided reflections.
