# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently showed unconditional positive regard, validating Erik's defensiveness without judgment or shaming. Erik explicitly noted 'You've not lectured me once today,' confirming the non-judgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, capturing both content and emotion (e.g., the beer as 'the one part of your day that truly belongs to you'). The counselor caught Erik's pivotal admission and checked accuracy ('Did I catch that right?').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor kept a clear, consistent focus on alcohol and diet, building step-by-step from identifying barriers to a concrete plan with follow-up. Every exchange served the change process without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated as genuine collaboration between equals, with Erik's input shaping success criteria, relapse signals, and follow-up timing. The final plan was co-constructed but entirely client-owned.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was exceptional, woven into nearly every response ('it's your experiment to run, not anyone else's'). The counselor honored Erik's caveat about quitting if miserable as legitimate rather than pushing back.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key motivations—better sleep, clear mornings with grandkids—were elicited through open questions rather than persuasion. Erik generated his own insight ('maybe I'm more bothered about this than I let on') and his own plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positioned Erik as the expert ('you know your rhythms better than any doctor or I ever will') and redirected expertise back when Erik deferred. The plan was entirely Erik-designed, with ownership reinforced at the close.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocative questions produced rich change talk, from 'suppose I could try it a few nights' to the pivotal 'maybe I'm more bothered about this than I let on.' Erik left with self-generated commitment to a specific plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I feel fine,' skepticism about the diagnosis) was validated neutrally, never argued with, then pivoted toward change. The reframe of 'failing' as 'information' softened sustain talk while preserving autonomy.
