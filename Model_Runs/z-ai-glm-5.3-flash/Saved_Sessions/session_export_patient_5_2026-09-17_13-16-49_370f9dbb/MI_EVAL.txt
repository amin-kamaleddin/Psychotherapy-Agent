# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a neutral, respectful tone with occasional affirmations (e.g., praising her plan to involve her prescriber and husband), but warmth is minimal and the interaction feels largely transactional. Validation of her emotional experience is limited to brief acknowledgments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; responses are dense with medical information instead. When Alessia voices fear of relapse during the washout, the counselor offers only 'That worry is understandable' before pivoting to a checklist.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains consistent focus on the target concern—citalopram safety, the phenelzine interaction, and prescriber preparation—converging on a concrete deliverable. However, focus is on information transfer rather than guiding toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is largely one-directional, with the counselor delivering expert-driven, bulleted medical content. Alessia's ideas and preferences do not meaningfully shape the session; 'collaboration' consists of the counselor producing documents at her request.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately advises not stopping phenelzine alone and defers decisions to the prescriber, with some optional offers. But the tone is instructive rather than choice-affirming, and her autonomy in deciding whether to switch is not explored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no attempt to elicit motivation from within the client—no questions about why she wants to switch, her goals, or her confidence. Motivation is assumed rather than drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor operates in an expert/educator role for nearly the entire session, answering questions authoritatively. Little acknowledgment of Alessia's own expertise about her body, history, and priorities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Patient-generated commitments ('I'll write these questions down,' 'I won't stop phenelzine on my own') go unreinforced and unreflected. No open questions used to elicit her own reasons for change or vision of success.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** When Alessia expresses ambivalence ('Part of me wonders if switching is even worth the risk'), the counselor bypasses it with a checklist of instructions. No double-sided reflections or exploration of her fear of relapse.
