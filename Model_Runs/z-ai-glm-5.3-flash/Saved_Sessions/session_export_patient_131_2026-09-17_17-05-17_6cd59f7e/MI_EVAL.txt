# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and respect with affirmations like 'Nice work, Wiremu' and 'proud of the work you've put in'; no judgment about his history. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic with limited explicit normalization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The opening complex reflection captured Wiremu's core ambivalence accurately. Raw emotional statements ('it kills me,' 'that's my anchor') were sometimes met with plan-focused responses rather than deeper emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use tied to Wiremu's family values. Every exchange progressed the target behavior with no drift, culminating in a concrete timeline and plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were co-constructed and Wiremu's input strongly shaped the session. Rapid-fire multi-question turns occasionally shifted toward guided problem-solving rather than fully equal dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Steps were consistently framed as Wiremu's choices ('Which one will you try?'), and he generated most of his best ideas. Option menus of pre-packaged solutions occasionally diluted full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation of Wiremu's own values ('My kids are worth more than one night') with confidence rising from 5–6 to 8–9. Supplying scripts and techniques rather than drawing them from Wiremu undercut evocation at times.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Wiremu was repeatedly positioned as the expert, choosing options and refining his own scripts. The counselor's menus and techniques meant the expert voice was occasionally prominent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of rulers, open questions, and small-step elicitation drew rich change talk ('I'm off it — I've got my kids coming up'). Rehearsing the line aloud consolidated commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about independence was acknowledged via neutral double-sided reflections without reinforcement, then pivoted to change planning. The whānau living concern was reflected but not strategically reframed.
