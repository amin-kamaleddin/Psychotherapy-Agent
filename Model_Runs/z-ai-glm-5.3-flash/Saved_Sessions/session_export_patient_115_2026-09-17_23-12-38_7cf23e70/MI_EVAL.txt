# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, including respectful acceptance of Samir's boundary on smoking ('One step at a time... Deal.') and reframing honesty as strength rather than weakness. No labeling or disapproval anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently captured nuance beneath Samir's words, such as the ambivalence around smoking and the insight that 'it's not the cigarette, it's the quiet.' Samir repeatedly responded with elaboration and self-discovery, indicating accurate empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus on asthma-trigger management: from the filter step, to a Friday commitment, to the underlying function of smoking, and consolidation into three named actions. Every exchange advanced understanding or commitment without aimless wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor asked rather than told, built each exchange on Samir's words, and co-constructed the plan, culminating in the mutually negotiated 'Deal.' Samir's playful 'You'll have me sorted before long' reflects his experience of the relationship as equal and non-hierarchical.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit affirmations of Samir's ownership ('That's your call to make') and full acceptance of the plan on Samir's terms, including framing continued smoking during the experiment as an acceptable choice. No pressuring or controlling language appeared.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from within—Samir generated the filter plan, the ten-minutes-of-quiet insight, and his own hypothesis that he may not need cigarettes for calm. His 'I've never thought of it like that' moments are direct evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Samir was consistently positioned as the expert ('You know yourself best here'), and his contributions demonstrably shaped the session—the filter, the timing between fares, the quiet insight—with the counselor explicitly crediting his self-discovery.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and developed, from 'If I give you my word I'll do it by Friday' to Samir's culminating reflection about proving himself wrong 'in a good way.' Strategic reinforcement (highlighting 'learning') strengthened commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged and pivoted toward change without dismissal, notably the double-sided reflection on 'not never' and reframing continued smoking during the experiment as learning rather than failure. Masterful conversion of resistance into exploration.
