# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful throughout with affirmations of the client's competence and honesty, and no judgment of his irritability. Fell short of 5 as some affirmations felt slightly formulaic and the client's opening frustration about the 45-minute wait was never acknowledged or repaired.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures the client's ambivalence and pragmatic worldview. Reflections sometimes praised insight rather than probing the emotional undercurrent — fear about medication and the father's legacy were named but not explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on target behaviors despite resistance and time pressure, guiding steadily to a dated commitment, a self-defined test, and a scheduled follow-up. Every question served the trajectory from abstract resistance to concrete action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine two-way problem-solving with the plan co-constructed from the client's own metaphors and criteria. The counselor's stance occasionally drifted toward admiring observer, offering little of his own clinical perspective for the client to weigh as a partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary — decisions explicitly framed as the client's own, no pushing beyond his chosen pace, and his conditions (trial run, food later) treated as strengths rather than resistance. All planning flowed from the client's own judgments about what fits his life.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never gave advice or reasons to change; all motivations (medication avoidance, father's example, invoicing discipline) were elicited from the client himself. Evocation was consistent and skillful from the first question to the last.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as the expert, and his own ideas genuinely shaped the plan. Occasionally ended exchanges with praise rather than negotiation, and there was no explicit agenda-setting check given the time pressure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully highlighted, deepened, and consolidated change talk via confidence ratings and evocative questions. The client progressed from tentative interest to a firm, dated commitment plus proactive planning of the next behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely converted sustain talk rather than amplifying it, validating feelings then pivoting to solutions without arguing against barriers. Minimal use of double-sided reflections or strategic reframing — pessimistic predictions like Wednesday's walk sometimes passed with only curiosity.
