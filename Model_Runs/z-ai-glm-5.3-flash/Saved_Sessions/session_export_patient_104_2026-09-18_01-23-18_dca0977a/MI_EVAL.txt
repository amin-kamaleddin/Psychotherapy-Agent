# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard was communicated consistently, with Nadine's guilt and self-doubt met with warmth and validation ('that relief is real, and you deserve it'). No judgment or disapproval appeared anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally deep, capturing both surface content and underlying meaning (fear of doing something wrong, guilt, desire to enjoy her daughter). The counselor repeatedly went beyond content to the feelings beneath.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on infant sleep and Nadine's distress, with each exchange advancing the thread toward change. Every turn ended with a question that moved the conversation forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was clearly between equals, with Nadine's ideas (dim/quiet/feed routine, bare-minimum version) forming the actual plan. The counselor contributed developmental knowledge in a genuine two-way exchange of expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was explicitly reinforced ('which of those ideas feels like it might actually fit your family?'), and Nadine designed her own routine. Information was offered without prescribing, pressure, or controlling language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew out Nadine's own motivations and vision ('actually feeling like a person instead of a zombie'). Some psychoeducation was supplied directly, though this was largely justified by her explicit request for straight answers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nadine was consistently positioned as the expert on her baby, and her contributions demonstrably shaped the session direction. Her fussing observation reshaped the sleep plan, and her 'three steps max' request set the plan's simplicity.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change language was actively evoked and developed ('I like that I'm not aiming for perfect anymore'), with concrete commitment language affirmed. Some momentum was counselor-supplied through information-giving before client-generated possibilities were elicited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk about exhaustion and fear of failure was consistently met with reframes rather than reinforcement ('one off night doesn't erase progress'). Doubt was transformed into a sustainable 'good enough' plan.
