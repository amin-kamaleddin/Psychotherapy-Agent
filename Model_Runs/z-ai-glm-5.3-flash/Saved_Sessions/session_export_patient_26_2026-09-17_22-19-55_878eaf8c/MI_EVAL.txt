# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and validating throughout the session. Reframes distrust as protective ('protecting your son, not being stuck') and offers genuine, specific affirmations without any hint of disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately capture Daniela's emotional reality, e.g., 'everything unravels at once' and 'the hope that asking could change everything, and the fear of another letdown.' Reflections deepen understanding rather than parroting content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior—reliable childcare—with every exchange building toward a concrete, client-generated plan. Session flows coherently from identifying the priority to a readiness ruler and behavioral rehearsal with no aimless wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative dynamic between equals—the plan is co-constructed entirely from the client's ideas (meal barter, Saturday morning, trial visits). The offer to practice 'right now, together' positions the psychiatrist as a partner, not an instructor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and ownership, with all solutions originating from the client and suggestions framed as questions. A 5 would require more explicit affirmation of her ownership of the decision even if she chose not to act.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out the client's own motivation and resources—her reasons (son's wellbeing), solutions (meal trade, Saturday timing), and competence all emerge from her, not from the clinician. The rehearsal evokes her own sense of capability.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions the client as the expert ('You know your life best') and her answers directly shape the session's direction. Functions as a guide rather than an advisor throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully evokes and develops change talk from contemplation to rehearsed commitment, including a readiness ruler and live behavioral rehearsal. Client progresses to stating 'I could actually say that Saturday'—a textbook change talk arc.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well through double-sided reflections and reframing distrust protectively, and strategically pivots barriers into problem-solving (e.g., reframing 'begging' into what she could offer). A 5 would require slightly faster pivots, as some obstacles receive extended exploration—though this proved productive.
