# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm and nonjudgmental throughout; validated her experience and never implied she was a bad mother, which the patient explicitly appreciated. Some affirmations were formulaic and did not deeply affirm her strengths beyond surface-level encouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections (exhaustion, comparisons, breastfeeding constraints), but the counselor pivoted quickly to questions and advice. Key emotional content—resentment toward her husband, fear of 'not handling it'—was acknowledged only minimally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical focus on exhaustion, sleep, and partner support, progressing from assessment to a concrete plan, barriers, and follow-up. Occasional clustering of multiple questions and options in one turn diluted focus somewhat.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Checked preferences, offered to role-play, and adjusted the message at the patient's request. However, the overall dynamic felt like expert-to-client delivery, with tips and scripts supplied rather than jointly developed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choice (pick 1–2 items, option menus, invitation to tweak the message), and the patient left with her own plan. Once she chose, the counselor tended to revert to prescriptive instructions about the 'how' rather than co-creating.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Drew out some client-generated ideas (the closed-door strategy) and her own reasons for change. Relied heavily on providing information and pre-written scripts; core motivations like being a good mother and repairing the marriage were never explicitly evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly solicited the patient's input and preferences. However, the counselor often assumed the expert role—prescribing the plan, drafting the message, and supplying scripts—without deeply drawing on her expertise about her baby and marriage.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced concrete commitments ('Nap this morning, pump after her next feed, send him the message') and her 'doable' statement, plus asked about barriers to elicit sustain talk. Could have deepened commitment further with confidence scaling or exploring success.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Responded to doubts with practical reassurance and troubleshooting rather than amplifying them. Did not use double-sided reflections or values-based reframes for key sustain talk like the 'pushing through means handling it' belief.
