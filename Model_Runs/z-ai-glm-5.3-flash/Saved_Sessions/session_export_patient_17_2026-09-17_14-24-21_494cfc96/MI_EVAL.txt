# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Anisa's experience and normalizing her struggles (e.g., the wobbly voice, past failures). Warmth is somewhat generic and checklist-driven, with no deep individualized affirmation beyond her plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections capture her ambivalence well, but the session increasingly shifts to logistical precision over emotional understanding. Key emotional moments—her wobbly voice, her shame ('like being a leper'), and her hope for her daughter—receive little reflective exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with no topic drift; every exchange ties back to the quit plan and craving management. Direction is achieved through clinician-directed advice rather than strategic MI guiding, keeping it from a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Incorporates Anisa's preferences (Monday date, tea swap, combo NRT) and co-develops her scripts, but the counselor's ideas dominate the plan's content. Anisa adjusts the edges rather than shaping the session, and agenda-setting feels scripted.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Honors her chosen date, her own words for her daughter, and offers some choices, but repeatedly leans directive with exact doses, pre-written scripts, and follow-up offers even after she declined multiple times. Never explicitly affirms that she is in charge of next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Does little to draw motivation from within Anisa; the single readiness ruler was never explored ('why a 6 and not a 3?'), and there are no open questions about her reasons or confidence. Her strong motivation emerged despite the counselor's technique rather than because of it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Verbally affirms Anisa's ownership, but behaves in a markedly expert-driven way with prescriptive instructions, scripts, and repeated checklists. Anisa had to set her own pace ('I've got it — I don't want to over-rehearse'), suggesting limited genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nearly all change talk came unprompted from the patient; the counselor's evocation efforts were minimal. The readiness ruler response was unexplored, and advice/checklists replaced open questions about her reasons, hopes, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce or dwell on her doubt, and the repeated reframe of 'slips as information, not failure' is a good strategic pivot toward change. However, sustain talk is sidestepped rather than actively softened—no double-sided reflections or deeper processing of her core fear of being 'just a smoker'.
