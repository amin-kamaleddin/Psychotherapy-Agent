# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard was maintained throughout, including respectful acceptance of Aisha's boundary around alcohol and her expectation of smoking on hard nights. No judgment or disapproval appeared; slips were reframed as useful data rather than failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening tied reflections to her life context (toddler, classes, downtime), especially early in the session. However, emotionally significant statements like 'the cigarette is the only thing that's mine' were met with option lists rather than deeper exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear, consistent focus on the concrete goal of reducing evening smoking, with every turn tied back to the measurable plan (three cigarettes, out-of-reach, tracking, tough nights, structured follow-up). Deflections were gently redirected without losing momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was fully co-constructed, with Aisha's ideas (three-cigarette goal, out-of-reach strategy, mints, notes-app tracking, direct check-ins) visibly shaping every element. The counselor asked rather than prescribed and let the client define the follow-up contract.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options were offered rather than imposed, her rejection of gum and phone calls was respected, and the drinking topic was explicitly left to her timeline. The final plan was framed in her own words as her decision, with no pressure or controlling language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence rulers effectively drew out her core motivation ('my son needs me') and actionable ideas, moving confidence from 5-6 to 7. Mid-session the counselor temporarily shifted from evoking to supplying solutions (lozenges, urge-surfing, stair runs) instead of asking what she thought would work.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha was positioned as the expert, and her preferences demonstrably shaped the final plan. The occasional fall into advice-listing mode (e.g., five numbered alternatives) slightly tempered the power-sharing stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled use of open questions and confidence scaling elicited her own change language ('cutting back... three instead of six') and moved her toward commitment. Some counselor-supplied strategies and only implicit development of commitment language kept this from a perfect score.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally met with acceptance and redirection rather than argument, and slips were proactively reframed as data. However, some suggestions invited discussion against change, and residual sustain talk ('I'll probably still smoke one of the three') was not strategically pivoted into hope or change talk.
