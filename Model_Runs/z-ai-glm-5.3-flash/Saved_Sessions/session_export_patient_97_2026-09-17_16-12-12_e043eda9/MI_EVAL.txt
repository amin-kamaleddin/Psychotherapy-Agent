# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth with affirmations of Mateo's honesty, no judgment about his history. Affirmations were somewhat brief and task-focused, with little normalization of his fear of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opening reflection captured his core tension well, but subsequent turns were mostly operational. Emotionally rich disclosures (boredom as danger, shame about 'soft' coping, fear of failure) were rarely reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on avoiding risky Friday nights with the old crew; every exchange advanced a concrete plan with no topic drift. Session built coherently toward change, including a relapse contingency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely co-created: Mateo chose options, invented his exit line, set the 7:30 time, and proposed the Monday if-then. Initial plans and scripts came from the counselor, and multi-question turns made it feel somewhat guided.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actions consistently framed as Mateo's choice, and his refusals (declining more rehearsal) were respected. Options were often pre-packaged, and rapid question stacks created mild pressure toward immediate commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Mateo's own reasons (girlfriend, future family, boredom as danger) using rulers and open questions. Occasionally answered his own questions with menus, and deeper values exploration was left undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Mateo's choices, but clinician-supplied menus positioned the counselor as plan-generator while Mateo mainly selected. His expertise (e.g., 'bored is dangerous for me') wasn't spotlighted or built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk via values questions, small steps, and confidence rulers, yielding substantial commitments from Mateo. Limited somewhat by frequent multiple-choice prompting that supplied options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not dwell on or reinforce sustain talk, briefly validating and pivoting to next steps. Would be stronger with occasional double-sided reflections linking the pull of the old life to Mateo's own values.
