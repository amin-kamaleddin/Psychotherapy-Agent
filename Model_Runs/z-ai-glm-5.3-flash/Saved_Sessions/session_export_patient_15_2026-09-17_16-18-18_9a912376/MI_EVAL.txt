# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, responding to Andrei's minimization and skepticism without disapproval while validating his autonomy and prior success. Warmth is somewhat formulaic, with limited explicit affirmation of Andrei as a person beyond his behaviors.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections are reasonably accurate, but reflections become sparse and transactional mid-session as the counselor shifts to planning and logistics. Little exploration of the emotional meaning behind Andrei's minimizing, his wife's 'fussing', or drinking to unwind.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on BP-related behavior change with concrete, measurable steps (specific times, days, cuff models, backup plans, follow-up). Direction sometimes becomes checklist-like, prioritizing logistics over resolving Andrei's low intrinsic motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative framing with permission-seeking, option menus, and co-constructed planning; Andrei's answers genuinely alter the plan. Power-sharing is incomplete, as plans and advice are drafted and presented in an expert manner rather than letting Andrei generate solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is repeatedly offered and partial commitments are accepted without pressure. Falls short because advice is frequent and sometimes prescriptive rather than consistently reinforcing Andrei's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is largely supplied by the counselor rather than elicited; there are almost no questions drawing out Andrei's own values, reasons, or confidence. His change talk is mostly compliant rather than self-generated, with no exploration of what matters to him (Elena, unwinding, quitting-smoking history).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission and offers choices, and Andrei's contributions shape the plan. However, the counselor retains the expert role with pre-packaged plans, block-delivered advice, and little acknowledgment that expertise about Andrei's life resides with him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is evoked through open questions and plan invitations, and Andrei produces commitment language that is reflected back. But the counselor never deepens it with follow-ups about Andrei's own reasons, confidence, or vision of success — most change talk arises from counselor-supplied structure.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally not amplified and includes decent double-sided reflections and reframes. However, sustain talk is repeatedly restated nearly verbatim, and the counselor pivots quickly to action steps rather than helping Andrei resolve his ambivalence about alcohol.
