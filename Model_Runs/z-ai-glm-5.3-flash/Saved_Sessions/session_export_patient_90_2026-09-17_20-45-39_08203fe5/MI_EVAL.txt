# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard and explicitly protects Marcus's values ('your dog stays, the bills stay the same'). Past non-adherence is normalized rather than judged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent reflective listening that mirrors Marcus's exact language and goes beyond content to emotional meaning (feeling 'less old, less broken'). Reflections accurately capture his ambivalence from the opening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains laser focus on asthma management while flexibly honoring values, progressing from target behavior identification to success markers and a scheduled follow-up. Each summary consolidates movement toward change with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operates between equals, with no advice offered until Marcus generates ideas and the plan co-constructed ('it all came from you'). Marcus explicitly confirms the partnership: 'You just... let me find my own thing.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support throughout, with choices framed around what Marcus will accept ('works around my life, not against it') and no imposed directives. The closing frames the month as something Marcus controls.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client via the stair visualization, wife narrative, and confidence ruler, all eliciting values-driven motivation. Marcus's closing comment confirms evocation was effective — he expected to be told what to do but found his own path.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is repeatedly and explicitly located in the client ('You know yourself better than anyone'; 'it came from you, not me'). Marcus's own contributions directly shaped the session plan — the hallmark of power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of change talk via open questions, the stair visualization, and the confidence ruler, producing commitment language ('I could give that a real go,' 'I'll see you in a month'). Every major step came from Marcus's own mouth.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged briefly with double-sided reflections then redirected to change, without ever being reinforced. A slight deduction because the dog/finances barriers are restated a few times in summaries before pivoting.
