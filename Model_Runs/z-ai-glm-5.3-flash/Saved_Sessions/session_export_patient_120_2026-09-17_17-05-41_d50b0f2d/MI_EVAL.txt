# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and nonjudgment; normalized pre-pregnancy-awareness drinking ("It's common to have had drinks before knowing; stopping now still helps"), directly countering prior shaming. Affirmations present ("saying it aloud took courage") but somewhat brief and formulaic; unconditional regard independent of progress not explicitly conveyed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening capturing ambivalence (guilt about early drinking vs. determination; fear of losing social support; "silence feels worse than anger"). Reflections accurate but brief, occasionally pivoting quickly to offering options, limiting deeper exploration of guilt, identity loss, and fetal-harm fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol abstinence and the social-pressure barrier. Every turn advanced a concrete goal: friend disclosure, call rehearsal, Saturday timing, backup text, post-call self-care. Session ended with a specific, achievable commitment plan; no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely joint problem-solving: interactive rehearsal, Sofia edited script and text, plan emerged from her own reasoning. "Would it be okay if..." and option menus conveyed partnership. Falls short of 5 because counselor set most agenda structure and supplied most content (scripts, breathing, self-care) rather than co-creating from Sofia's own knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated choices and permission-seeking ("Which feels safest right now?"); accepted Sofia's edits as-is ("Backup text is fine as you wrote it"); respected her decision to postpone telling her parents. Not a 5 because options were counselor-generated rather than client-generated, and ownership of change was not explicitly emphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia voiced determination ("whatever it takes"), hope ("Maybe I can actually do this"), and values, but largely spontaneously. Counselor relied on teaching, scripting, and planning; no classic evocative questions (importance/confidence rulers, "why does this matter to you?"). Change talk not purposefully evoked, deepened, or summarized.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-asking and choice menus supported agency, but stance leaned coach/expert: counselor supplied scripts, plan structure, and self-care ideas while Sofia selected among options. Her ideas (telling Marisol sooner, shortening the text) shaped the session somewhat, but her expertise was rarely explicitly acknowledged or solutions invited from her first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia generated meaningful change talk ("I wanna be responsible for this thing," "Saturday, it's happening"), but mostly from her own momentum. Questions were procedural rather than open questions evoking desire, ability, reasons, or commitment; commitment language was not explicitly reinforced or reflected when it appeared.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ("I can't just cut off my friends," "my head versions always end with her being disgusted") was not reinforced, but also rarely actively redirected. Moved quickly to skills/scripts rather than using double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence; much sustain talk faded on its own.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 3.7/5**

*Strengths: strong direction, autonomy support, and a respectful, non-shaming stance that countered prior judgment; concrete, behaviorally specific outcome (rehearsed disclosure plan with backup). Improvement areas: (1) use open questions and rulers to evoke and reflect Sofia's own change talk; (2) develop double-sided reflections holding ambivalence while pivoting toward her values; (3) invite Sofia to generate solutions first to strengthen partnership and collaboration.*
