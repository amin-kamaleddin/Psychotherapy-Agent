# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance toward the patient's gambling, defensiveness, and minimization, with affirmations communicating positive regard. Missed opportunities for deeper unconditional regard, particularly when the patient first voiced her dreams aloud.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate (naming the tension between the pokies as social lifeline and the risks), but reflective listening largely disappeared once planning began. Emotionally significant disclosures—shame about her mother, fear of being called 'boring,' and first-time voicing of her dreams—went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and built on change talk through 0–10 scaling and structured choice menus, eliciting client-generated commitments like cutting up credit cards and 'don't go to the pub,' with confidence rising from 3 to 6. Could have deepened change talk further by reflecting and amplifying it when it emerged, such as exploring what Egypt symbolizes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk ('it's just a bit of cash') and offered useful reorientation through scripts, but the softening was mostly implicit. Key sustain moments, such as fears of relapse and teasing, were bypassed rather than addressed with double-sided reflections, leaving ambivalence unprocessed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for the patient's input and let her choose strategies, but the session was dominated by counselor-generated menus, scripts, and prescriptive plans. When the patient sought direction ('Which one would you start with?'), the counselor obliged with expert content rather than redirecting to her own expertise.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on gambling reduction and debt with no topic drift, advancing steadily from scaling through a same-night plan, relapse prevention, and a follow-up commitment. Every pivot returned to actionable steps tied to the target behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed steps as choices with no pressure or coercive language despite the probation context, and all decisions (card to Mum, $50 cash, midnight exit) were the patient's own. Could have more explicitly affirmed her ownership of the change process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction largely functioned between equal partners, with fit-checking, accountability preferences, and integration of the patient's edits (softening the text to Mum). However, counselor-drafted menus and scripts meant the balance of idea generation tilted toward the counselor rather than drawing more solutions from her.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation primarily from the patient via rulers and choice-based questions, with her own change talk powering the session ('Egypt before 27,' 'more of a plan than I've had in two years'). Missed deeper evocative opportunities, such as exploring the meaning of her dreams or what drove her confidence rise from 3 to 6.
