# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with a defensive client, validating his perspective and honesty. Falls short of a 5 due to limited normalization of his defensiveness or explicit acceptance of his sustain talk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including a pivotal, accurate reflection on the blackout fear that deepened the session. Some reflections stayed at content-summary level and rarely touched deeper feelings without client prompting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained steady focus on the target behavior while framing it in the client's terms. The session progressed clearly from blame through planning to core values with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was genuinely co-created, with the psychiatrist asking for the client's input and building each question on his previous answers. The client visibly invested, indicating an equal-partner dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced the client's ownership ('That came entirely from you') and offered no advice or directives. All decisions and strategies originated from the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never supplied reasons for change; every motivation was elicited from the client, including success markers, barriers, and the values-based reason to change. Evocation at its best.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly located expertise in the client, whose contributions drove the entire plan. Exemplary power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent evocation of change language through future-oriented questions, with each piece of change talk reflected and amplified. Client moved from grudging admission to concrete commitments and values-based statements — textbook MI.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, pivoting toward change and using double-sided reflections effectively. Some sustain talk (e.g., 'not quitting drinking') passed unaddressed rather than being actively reframed.
