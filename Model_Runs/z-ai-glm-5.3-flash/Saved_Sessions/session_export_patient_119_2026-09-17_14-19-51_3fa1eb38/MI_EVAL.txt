# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations of Selene's honesty and realistic choices. However, warmth feels formulaic and generic, lacking individualized positive regard or normalization of her ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections showing partial understanding, but deeper emotional reflections are largely skipped. Fear of failure, privacy concerns, and skepticism were met with advice rather than reflection of their emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on medication adherence and produced a concrete, time-bound plan. Deductions for repetitive recaps drifting into lecturing and failure to revisit sleep/lifestyle concerns Selene raised.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked which strategies felt doable and adjusted to Selene's preference for starting small. But the dynamic tilted expert-driven, with unsolicited instruction blocks delivered at Selene rather than co-created with her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and acknowledged that slips don't equal failure. However, the heavily prescriptive style and repeating the full plan after Selene said 'I've got it' undermined her expressed preference and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was addressed through external tools rather than eliciting Selene's internal reasons. Her spontaneous motivations (family, health) were never drawn out, elaborated, or reflected back — no evocative questions or confidence/importance rulers used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some input was invited and Selene's choices shaped the plan, but the counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role with long instruction blocks. Selene even pushed back ('I can handle setting an alarm'), suggesting over-instruction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Selene offered meaningful change talk ('I want to stay healthy'), but the counselor rarely evoked or deepened it. No open questions explored her reasons or hopes; the counselor pivoted quickly to practical instructions instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never amplified and was occasionally reframed ('progress, not perfection'). But it was mostly glossed over rather than strategically softened with double-sided reflections or followed by pivots toward change language.
