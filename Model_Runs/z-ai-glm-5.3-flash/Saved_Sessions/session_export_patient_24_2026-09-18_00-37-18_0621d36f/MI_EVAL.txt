# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly affirming Daniel's right to reject the 'drinking problem' label without judgment. Affirmations were specific and genuine ('That's not dumb, that's honest').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was consistently accurate and complex, including double-sided reflections capturing Daniel's ambivalence. Also reflected emotional undertones and validated his skepticism about past failures.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept unwavering focus on alcohol use and its relationship to training goals, building progressively from discrepancy to a concrete experiment. No topic drift; guidance was clear without being pushy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely two-way — every element of the final plan (balcony experiment, tempo run check, sleep markers) originated with Daniel. Questions consistently invited him to co-construct the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced that the choice lives with Daniel, and met his stated limits ('not committing to anything past the experiment') with reflection rather than pushback. Never advised, persuaded, or imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation entirely from Daniel via open questions, letting him discover his own reasons ('the training matters to me more than the drinking does'). The clinician accurately named this as self-generated motivation rather than supplying reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly located expertise with the patient ('You're the expert on your body and your training'), and Daniel's contributions demonstrably shaped the session. Full power-sharing was evident.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and developed change talk, from the hypothetical that produced Daniel's first commitment language to a closing question that elicited a concrete plan directly from him. Consistently reflected and amplified Daniel's own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly, validated, and pivoted toward change without amplification, using double-sided reflections and reframing wariness as honesty. Never argued with Daniel's minimization or dwelled on reasons against change.
