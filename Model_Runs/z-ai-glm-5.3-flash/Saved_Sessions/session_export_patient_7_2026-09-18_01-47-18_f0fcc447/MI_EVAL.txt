# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  clinician  consistently  communicated  unconditional  positive  regard  ('I  won't  make  it  weird'),  respected  the  patient's  autonomy  ('you  get  to  say  what  this  hour  looks  like'),  and  affirmed  the  patient's  perspective  as  valid  throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective  statements  demonstrated  deep  understanding,  e.g.,  'It  sounds  like  carrying  a  weight  alone'  and  'you're  carrying  fairness  for  her  that  nobody's  carrying  for  you,'  accurately  capturing  both  content  and  underlying  emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The  clinician  maintained  focus  on  the  family  conflict  and  the  patient's  feelings  of  blame,  following  the  patient's  lead  rather  than  imposing  an  agenda.  Slightly  limited  movement  toward  concrete  behavior  change  talk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  session  was  explicitly  positioned  around  the  patient's  expertise  ('you're  the  expert  on  your  family'),  with  frequent  invitational  questions  and  shared  exploration  rather  than  direction  or  prescription.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy  was  strongly  supported:  'I'm  not  here  to  make  you  do  anything,'  and  allowing  the  patient  to  define  the  pace  and  content  of  the  conversation  reinforced  self-determination  throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  clinician  skillfully  evoked  the  patient's  own  reasons  and  hopes  ('What  would  they  do  instead,  in  your  ideal  world?'),  drawing  out  change  talk  such  as  'someone  at  home  just  once  saying  hey,  you  did  good'  and  the  patient's  own  recognition  of  what  worked  in  session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  conversation  felt  like  side-by-side  exploration  with  open  questions,  gentle  reflections,  and  responsiveness  to  the  patient's  boundaries,  resulting  in  the  patient  visibly  opening  up  and  offering  feedback  freely.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change  talk  was  elicited  ('maybe  I'd  say  more,'  'just  one  place  where  I'm  not  the  one  getting  blamed'),  though  primarily  in  the  form  of  desires  and  needs;  commitment  and  ability  language  were  less  developed,  which  is  understandable  for  an  opening  session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain  talk  ('nothing's  gonna  change  anyway,'  'she's  been  like  this  forever')  was  met  with  reflective  empathy  rather  than  argument,  and  hope  was  gently  reflected  back  without  minimally  challenging  or  pushing  against  the  patient's  doubts.
