# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental throughout, with specific genuine affirmations ('you turned a problem into a solution on your own'). The dog boundary and Marcus's stress are honored without any disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, emotionally attuned reflections of ambivalence and hope. However, reflections sometimes stay at summary level and the fear/fatigue beneath Marcus's overwhelm ('pile of stuff I'm failing at') is never explored at its emotional core.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on asthma management with a specific weekly commitment secured. Frustration about appointments and the dog boundary are redirected toward actionable change without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-way: the agenda is explicitly invited from Marcus, and the entire plan (inhaler by the dog bowl, bedding washing, delayed filter) is generated by him. The psychiatrist supplies information only on request.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support: 'you get to decide,' 'your call,' and asking what would be most helpful from himself. The plan is framed entirely on Marcus's terms with no pressure about the dog.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nearly textbook evocation — the plan emerged from 'what might that look like?' and motivation was amplified via reflection on his wife. Confidence/ability talk, however, was not deliberately explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Marcus as the expert ('you know your life and your limits better than anyone') and credits him for generating the plan. The psychiatrist acts as a resource rather than director.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilfully evokes and amplifies change talk ('what might that look like?', 'how does it feel having come up with a plan?'), then reflects it back to strengthen commitment. Marcus's sustained change talk is a direct product of this skill.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argues with barriers and redirects well ('what would make it easiest to remember the inhaler?'). But strategic softening is limited — 'I'll probably talk myself out of caring' passes unreflected and the financial barrier is accepted rather than explored for Marcus's own solutions.
