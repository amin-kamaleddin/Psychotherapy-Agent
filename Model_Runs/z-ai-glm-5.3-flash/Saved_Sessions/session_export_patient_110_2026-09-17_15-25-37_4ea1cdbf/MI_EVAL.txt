# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with affirmations present, but affirmations were somewhat generic rather than deeply personalized to Richard's values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing Richard's ambivalence and values; reflections became sparser later as the session shifted to scripts and logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on cholesterol management and moved toward a concrete plan, though focus drifted into appointment logistics rather than staying centered on motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard's input was invited and adjusted to, but the plan was largely counselor-architected; true co-construction drawing on Richard's own expertise was absent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices without pressure and respected Richard's decisions, though options centered on logistics of counselor-provided plans rather than his ownership of the change agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early open questions effectively elicited values and motivations, but the counselor then shifted to advising and missed opportunities to deepen Richard's change talk (e.g., 'Gotta be around for that').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered, but the interaction was largely counselor-driven information delivery, with plans and scripts originating from the counselor rather than being co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged naturally and open questions gave room for it, but the counselor rarely reflected or deepened these moments with evocative follow-ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged but not amplified, and no double-sided reflections were used to pair it with change talk; redirections were neutral rather than strategically evocative.
