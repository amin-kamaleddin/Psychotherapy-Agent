# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated warmth and unconditional positive regard, with no judgment about drinking or ambivalence. Claudia's caution and honesty were met with respect and normalization rather than disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, capturing both surface content and underlying meaning (e.g., the wine 'doing real work'). Double-sided reflections precisely captured her ambivalence and were repeatedly confirmed by the patient.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on evening alcohol use from opening to closing, with each exchange building purposefully toward a concrete plan. There was no aimless drift; every response guided toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully collaborative, with the plan co-constructed entirely from Claudia's own ideas (tea, two-glass boundary, one-evening experiment). The psychiatrist positioned herself as a witness to the patient's process rather than a director.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively reinforced throughout ('on your terms,' 'keeping it small is wise, not weak'), and nothing was prescribed. Her request not to be held to more than she was ready for was explicitly respected and praised.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently drew motivation from the client rather than supplying it, eliciting her own reasons, ideas, and coping wisdom. The 'two bad days' insight, two-glass boundary, and tea ritual all originated from her, with ownership deliberately amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Claudia was explicitly positioned as the expert ('you know yourself best'), and her contributions directly shaped the resulting plan. The psychiatrist repeatedly credited the client's own wisdom over any professional input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked through strategic questions and consistently reflected and affirmed ('That came from you, not me'). Commitment language was strengthened across the session and consolidated in the closing exchange.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was validated through reflection rather than argued against, then pivoted toward change using double-sided reflections. It was never amplified or dwelt upon.
