# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard: 'I don't hear it as being difficult at all,' 'you never have to soften it here.' The client's skepticism, hopelessness, and self-harm disclosure were all met with warmth, and ambivalence was explicitly normalized ('both are okay to bring here').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured the push-pull of ambivalence and the somatic experience of pressure ('gives the feeling somewhere to go'). However, some reflections mirrored rather than deepened emotion, and the shame ('like I'm not allowed to say that') and hopelessness could have been reflected more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on self-harm coping and safer alternatives, progressing coherently toward a concrete behavioral experiment. Minor inefficiency: one question circled back to material already covered (running, ice).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit partnership language ('let's think together, as partners in this') and genuine power-sharing—the final plan (ice test, brief walking) was co-constructed from the client's own ideas. The 'sharing the load' reframe was offered but adopted and shaped by the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice ('not replacing it, just sharing the load,' 'both are okay to bring here') and fully accepted the client's self-defined limits ('I'm not saying I'll stop cutting'). No advice was imposed; options were elicited and her right to test rather than commit was respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Core strategy was drawing out motivation from within—asking 'what feels least pointless to you' and 'what was it about that run that felt different.' All motivation surfaced (running memory, ice idea, 'maybe some nights I could') came from the client, not from counselor-provided reasoning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert ('You know your experience better than anyone,' 'You know yourself best') and invited her own solutions. Session direction—the ice test and walking instead of running—came entirely from the client's contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and amplified change talk by highlighting 'I'd maybe try it' and pausing on 'maybe I don't try anything long enough to know if it works.' The client escalated from 'nothing else has really worked' to volunteering concrete experiments and a commitment ('I could try it once. Like a test').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely avoided amplifying sustain talk—responded to 'cutting is the only thing that helps' with a double-sided reflection and did not argue with 'I'm not saying I'll stop cutting.' However, some reflections repeated sustain content nearly verbatim ('Everything else feels pointless'), briefly re-centering reasons against change.
