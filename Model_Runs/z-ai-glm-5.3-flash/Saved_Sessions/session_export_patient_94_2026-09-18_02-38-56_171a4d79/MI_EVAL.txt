# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently validates the patient's perspective, affirming her caution and research as legitimate rather than reckless. Explicit acceptance is shown through statements like 'your mom's experience especially—that's not abstract, that's personal.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is deep and accurate throughout, capturing both sides of the patient's ambivalence ('you care about breathing well, and you have deep, thoughtful reasons not to trust this'). The clinician repeatedly mirrors her emotional experience, including distrust of the medical system.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear focus on exploring the patient's concerns about inhaler use and moving toward collaborative information-gathering. Every exchange stays anchored to her safety concerns and what would help her reassess.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician works 'with' the patient at every turn, offering choices ('would that feel like a step in the right direction, or something else?') and following her lead on priorities. The joint plan to 'look at it together' embodies the partnership spirit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's right to judge for herself is repeatedly affirmed ('you get to judge for yourself,' 'you can handle unknowns'). The clinician explicitly rejects lecturing and positions the patient as the expert on her own health decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently draw out the patient's own reasons, values, and conditions for change ('what would make this feel doable?', 'what does a trustworthy answer look like?'). Her change talk emerges naturally from being asked, not told.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions about the conversation's direction are made jointly—starting with absorption data, mapping her specific dose, and naming evidence gaps honestly. The clinician frames the work as shared ('I'd like to work on this with you').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicits and reinforces the patient's own movement ('maybe,' 'start there,' 'map it out'), which progresses from pre-contemplation to active commitment. She even anticipates dealing with risk if shown evidence herself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rather than arguing against her resistance, the clinician reflects her sustain talk and diffuses potential confrontation ('Don't apologize—this is exactly the conversation worth having'). Her hostile expectations ('if it's another lecture, I'm done') are met with respect, not pushback.
