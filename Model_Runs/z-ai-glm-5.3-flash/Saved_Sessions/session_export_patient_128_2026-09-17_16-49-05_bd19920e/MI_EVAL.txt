# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains unconditional positive regard throughout, normalizing Tyler's hesitations ('that sounds kinda lame', worry about sounding mean) without any disapproval. Warm, nonjudgmental affirmations from opening to closing helped Tyler feel fully accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently and accurately captured Tyler's perspective, including his ambivalence about drinking and the nuance that firm refusals risk 'killing the vibe.' The counselor echoed Tyler's own language naturally and demonstrated precise understanding of his social context.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear focus on the target behavior (risky drinking) and built a progressive arc: values → what went wrong → strategies → if-then plan → rehearsal → confidence ratings → concrete follow-up. Each turn advanced the change process without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was fully collaborative, with the counselor offering options rather than prescriptions and co-creating the plan. Tyler's ideas (casual phrasing, adding a laugh, texting Marcus) directly shaped the intervention, and the counselor built on each contribution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was repeatedly and explicitly reinforced ('I'm here to support whatever you choose,' closing menu of options). Tyler selected his own drink limit, buddy, phrasing, and confidence ratings, with the counselor never imposing a plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor drew motivation from within Tyler via values questions, discrepancy exploration, and confidence/readiness scaling. Tyler voiced his own motivation ('school's too important,' 'I feel way more ready,' 'kind of a first for me'), demonstrating successful change talk development.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located within Tyler, whose choices substantially shaped the session flow. The counselor followed Tyler's lead each time he redirected (rejecting the sober buddy, preferring the 'saving room for tomorrow' line, requesting rehearsal).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent change talk cultivation through readiness questions, confidence scaling (which climbed from 6 to 8 within the session), and values exploration that elicited Tyler's own discrepancy. Rehearsal structures repeatedly drew out Tyler's self-generated plans and commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally acknowledged sustain talk briefly and pivoted to what Tyler found doable, building refusal skills rather than dwelling on why change is hard. Minor deduction because the counselor engaged with social-pressure content at length rather than consistently pivoting to strengths.
