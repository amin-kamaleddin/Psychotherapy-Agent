# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout; validated Sofia's frustration and thanked her for her safety disclosure. Responded warmly to dismissiveness ("That makes total sense — I won't judge"). Regard occasionally felt formulaic, with few individualized affirmations of Sofia's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections early on, but quickly shifted from reflecting to problem-solving. Vulnerable moments (fear of being left on read, "I think about it at night") were met with templates rather than reflection or exploration of the feelings underneath. Deeper feelings of betrayal and isolation were not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout — clear choice of focus at the start, then systematic progression from coping strategies to role-play, handling being followed, and drafting the friend message. Each segment built purposefully on the last; session never drifted. Clear strength of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration — checked in before practicing and accepted Sofia's rewritten message. However, the session largely followed an advice-dispense-then-rehearse structure; agendas were offered as menus rather than co-constructed, with no joint summary or shared plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices (role-play vs. draft, stronger vs. gentler tone, next steps) with no pressure when options were declined. Heavy advice-giving subtly undermined autonomy — solutions presented as counselor's packages rather than elicited from Sofia.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied primarily on providing scripts, tips, and drafted texts rather than eliciting Sofia's own motivations and ideas. Motivation emerged despite, not because of, counselor technique. No open questions designed to evoke change talk or explore why the friendship matters.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Frequently assumed the expert role with prescriptive advice lists. Sofia's own expertise emerged (her message rewrite, her insight about walking away) but was not explicitly recognized or built upon. Pattern was counselor-generating content with Sofia approving or adjusting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Conditions allowed some change talk ("sending it is better than sitting here doing nothing," "Walking away IS the answer"), largely client-generated during role-play. Missed opportunities to deepen change talk at key insight moments or leverage the friend reconnection as intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ("counselors never do anything," "I've TRIED ignoring it") was not amplified — a strength — but was bypassed rather than softened with double-sided reflections or strategic reconnection to Sofia's own goals.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **3.2/5**

**Summary:** Strong structure and direction, warm tone, and effective use of choice-offering; Sofia engaged genuinely, practicing assertive responses and improving the drafted message. Principal weaknesses lie in the MI "evoking" core: the counselor functioned largely as an advice-giver/coach, empathy was superficial at key emotional moments, sustain talk was bypassed, and change talk was not deliberately cultivated.

**Recommendations:**
1. Slow down and reflect before problem-solving, especially at vulnerable moments.
2. Ask open questions that evoke Sofia's own motivations and solutions before offering advice.
3. Use double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk.
4. Explicitly affirm Sofia's own contributions (message rewrite, insight about leaving on her terms) to build true partnership.
5. Close with a collaborative summary and her own stated next steps.
