# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Warm and nonjudgmental tone throughout, with frequent praise, but affirmations become repetitive and formulaic ('Proud of you'). Compliments like 'This is excellent' feel like hollow cheerleading rather than grounded, unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; the counselor responds to nearly every disclosure with a plan or checklist rather than a reflection. Significant statements — her self-knowledge, loss of momentum, and the emotional weight of making her intention feel 'real' — go unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly anchored to the target behavior (building an exercise routine) with no drift, returning each turn to concrete, actionable steps. Direction prioritizes logistical momentum over consolidating deeper motivation behind the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is markedly counselor-led, with most turns ending in offers to produce plans, scripts, or reminders that the client simply accepts. Her stated self-expertise (what stuck before, sales sensitivity, all-or-nothing pattern) is never drawn upon to shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional autonomy gestures (plan 'tweaks,' adjusting Tuesday after pushback) exist, but the overall style is prescriptive — full plans, scripts, and instructions are supplied unprompted. Client choice is tolerated rather than actively fostered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no evocation occurs; Leila's motivations and 10/10 confidence are volunteered, not elicited. There are no evocative questions about reasons, values, past successes, or hopes — the counselor supplies content instead of drawing motivation from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, driving the session with advice-dense turns, prescriptive checklists, and scheduling. Leila's input is incorporated only reactively after objections rather than actively sought.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client arrives with abundant change talk that the counselor does nothing to evoke, deepen, or reinforce. All change talk is client-generated; there is no elaboration, strategic reflection, or follow-up on her confidence rating.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Barriers (chlorine, gym contracts, distractions, weather) are absorbed into logistics without reframing or double-sided reflections. Her all-or-nothing perfectionism — a core barrier — is never explored, only brushed past with 'small wins matter.'
