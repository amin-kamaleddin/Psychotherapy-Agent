# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, warm, and non-judgmental with genuine affirmations of the patient's efforts and honesty. Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic; deeper individualized regard and normalization of her fears would strengthen this further.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captured her ambivalence, importance/confidence ratings, and social attachment to smoking. Missed opportunities to deepen reflections on her fear of failure and her forty-year morning coffee-and-cigarette ritual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on tobacco cessation throughout, with every exchange advancing the target. The session ended with a concrete, actionable plan and a recap confirming focus never drifted.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally collaborative, with the plan co-constructed around her preferences (goal, timing, check-in schedule). Some unevenness when the counselor delivered detailed psychoeducation in extended monologues rather than jointly exploring her knowledge of NRT.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked permission, offered choices, and let the patient retain control over goal, pace, and social disclosure. Fell short of top marks when some advice was delivered as pre-packaged recommendations, including shortly after she said "don't rush me."

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the patient's own reasons for change and hope via rulers and open questions about past successes. Less deliberate in evoking confidence specifically (rated 4/10), moving to planning structure rather than exploring how she could raise it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited the patient's input and honored her choices at key points, but the mid-session shift toward expert-driven content (gum dosing, morning routine steps) made the overall structure counselor-generated despite her input on specifics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions elicited strong change talk about health, money, and wanting this time to be different, which the counselor reinforced well. Could be stronger by deepening her confidence talk and commitment language before moving to plan logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (social group smoking, keeping friends unaware) was acknowledged neutrally without amplification, but rarely met with double-sided reflections or strategic pivots. Too much of the response was practical problem-solving rather than actively softening ambivalence.
