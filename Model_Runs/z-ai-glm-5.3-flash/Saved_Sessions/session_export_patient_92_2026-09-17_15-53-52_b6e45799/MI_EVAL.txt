# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and no arguing when Marek pushed back. However, warmth felt somewhat formulaic and the brisk withdrawal warning slightly undercut unconditional regard at a vulnerable moment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including an excellent complex reflection on the 'unfair' binge label and Marek's ambivalence. Some reflections felt templated, and deeper emotional exploration (fear beneath bravado) was missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept consistent focus on alcohol use with a logical flow from concerns to guidelines to a concrete, measurable experiment. Occasional drift occurred when broad risk lists opened tangents.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently asked permission, offered menus rather than prescriptions, and co-constructed the experiment around Marek's own parameters. Occasionally defaulted to lecture-style risk lists instead of eliciting Marek's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Honored Marek's control throughout, framed the plan as a low-stakes test, and reinforced his ownership. The unsolicited withdrawal warning introduced a mildly controlling undertone without checking whether he wanted that information.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marek's motivation emerged largely through personalized feedback rather than the counselor drawing out his own reasons and confidence. Key evocative openings (tolerance concern, 'if I got sick') were not explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Marek as the expert, and his preferences clearly shaped the session structure and experiment design. Information was sometimes delivered in packaged expert lists before checking his wishes.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Space was created for change talk — Marek acknowledged the tolerance concern and volunteered a reduction experiment. These moments were not actively reinforced or deepened, emerging more from information-giving than deliberate evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoided arguing with sustain talk and used neutral reflections. However, volunteered risk lists twice amplified defensiveness, and no double-sided reflections or strategic pivots away from sustain language were used.
