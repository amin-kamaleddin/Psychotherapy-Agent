# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirming phrases like 'That took courage' and normalizing ambivalence. However, warmth is somewhat formulaic and repetitive safety warnings interrupt the sense of unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ('That sounds like a meaningful first step'), but emotionally rich statements like 'on autopilot' and 'my brain just says beer' went largely unreflected. The counselor moved quickly to planning rather than deepening understanding of stress and loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, coherent focus on alcohol reduction throughout — assessment, goal setting, concrete planning, and follow-up scheduling were all tightly tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements like asking permission and offering menu options, but the power balance was uneven with counselor-generated plans and checklists. Kenji's own contributions shaped the session only modestly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized client choice — the client picked the day, substitutes, and check-in time. Plans and if-then scripts were largely prescriptive, leaving limited space for the client to shape his own approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied heavily on instruction rather than eliciting the client's own motivations; the one genuine evocative question about exploring what leads him to drink was never followed up. Rich openings like 'the form kind of shifted things' were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and offered options, but much of the session involved delivering instructions and checklists rather than drawing out Kenji's own ideas. The counselor never explicitly acknowledged Kenji as the expert on his own patterns.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Notable change talk emerged ('I can do one day,' 'I kind of care about this now'), but largely by client initiative rather than skillful evocation. Missed opportunities to reinforce, deepen, or elicit confidence behind his statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk and pivoted toward change when the client expressed reluctance ('I wasn't planning on quitting anything'). One mild missed opportunity to acknowledge or reframe his stress-related sustain talk.
