# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, with explicit non-judgment statements and repeated affirmations, though some affirmations felt formulaic and anxiety about records wasn't deeply normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening tracked the client's facts and concerns well, but deeper emotional reflections (fear of being labeled, unease about the referral) were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use, consistently tying discussion to screening results and harm reduction, though slightly constrained by pushing a preconceived A/B/C menu structure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan was co-constructed with menus and client choices (two drinks, naming Maya, rejecting the script), but the fact-gathering opening and expert-delivered alcohol facts limited mutual exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support with explicit repeated choice offerings, acceptance of the client's refusals without pressure, and respect for her ownership of the plan and future contact.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited indirectly by linking facts to the client's values, but the counselor relied heavily on information provision and ready-made options rather than open-ended evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited input and respected decisions, but the rapid multi-question opening felt like fact-extraction and the client explicitly noted being overwhelmed by questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully linked alcohol facts to the client's own values (sax, IB), eliciting genuine change talk and a concrete self-endorsed commitment, though options were somewhat counselor-offered.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and pivoted to values-based consequences, but sustain talk about minimization ('I don't have a problem') was often reflected back rather than softened with double-sided reflections.
