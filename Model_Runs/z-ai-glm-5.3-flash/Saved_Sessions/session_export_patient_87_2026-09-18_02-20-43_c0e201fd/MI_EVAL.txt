# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warmth, respect, and non-judgment ("That's okay," "There's real courage in what you just said"). Affirmations occasionally feel formulaic, and the clinician tends to move toward the hopeful side rather than fully holding Marco's ambivalence with equal warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is a strength—the opening ambivalence reflection, double-sided confidence reflection, and shame-spiral response reached genuine depth, prompting elaboration and disclosure from Marco. Some reflections function as reframes or pivot to strategy before emotional material is fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains exceptionally clear focus on dietary change while building a coherent ladder from behavior to motivation to self-relationship. No drift occurs, and the final consolidation question ties all threads toward a concrete first step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction reads as genuine dialogue between equals, with every turn assigning the expert role to Marco through tentative reflections and open questions. The plan is fully co-constructed from Marco's own material with no assertion of authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ownership is repeatedly affirmed ("You came up with it yourself," the closing agenda-setting menu), and hedged commitment is preserved without advice-giving. However, the self-compassion framing, though adopted enthusiastically, originated with the counselor rather than being fully client-discovered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is built entirely on eliciting motivation from within—Marco self-generates the Sunday prep, fridge note, 'for my kids' meaning, permission to be imperfect, and priority ranking. Confidence rose from six to seven organically, with the clinician's role purely reflective and evocative.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within Marco ("You know your patterns better than anyone"), and the final question hands full agenda-setting control to him. Every strategy in the session was client-generated—exemplary power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist systematically evokes and deepens change talk through questions that elicit progressively more committed language. Marco's arc—from "maybe if there were better options" to "I think I'd actually stand a chance"—is a textbook deepening of commitment, culminating in self-generated commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without reinforcement, and the "what would you want in place this time if it shows up?" reframe treats relapse as information rather than barrier. Slight over-engagement with "week three" and "the catch," and self-critical sustain talk is sometimes met with questions before explicit affirmation, prevents a 5.
