# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, with affirmations like 'That boundary sounds really protective of your son — you're doing something brave.' Normalized Amina's self-criticism without any judgment or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both content and emotional weight, such as 'It sounds like the arguing with your son's father is wearing you both down.' Occasionally reflections served as brief transitions to advice rather than deepening into fuller emotional meaning (e.g., fear of 'looking like the difficult one').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing co-parental arguments and protecting the son from conflict, with every exchange building toward a concrete plan. No topic drift occurred, and the closing recap consolidated the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as genuine equals — Amina drafted her own message, requested the role-play, and proposed the follow-up structure. The counselor actively sought her input at each juncture and incorporated her preferences into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and framed suggestions as options ('pick the tone you prefer,' 'Want feedback, or would you prefer sample responses?'). Immediately respected Amina's decision to defer further role-play practice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Amina's own values and self-generated insights, including her realization that delayed replies protect her son 'from my own temper too.' However, frequent reliance on pre-packaged option menus occasionally shifted from evocation toward provision rather than turning questions back to the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed that expertise resided with Amina by asking about session focus and offering choices, with her contributions clearly shaping the session. Sometimes supplied ready-made scripts rather than drawing wording from Amina herself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked values-based change talk, producing commitment language like 'for my son's sake, I want to try something' and confidence ('it felt... possible'). Could have deepened further with scaling questions or exploring what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged doubts briefly without amplifying them, and the reframe 'that boundary sounds really protective of your son' effectively shifted focus from fear to values. Some sustain talk (fear of plans falling apart) was bypassed rather than strategically resolved with double-sided reflections.
