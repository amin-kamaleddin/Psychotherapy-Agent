# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with a resistant, hurried client, acknowledging time pressure and never criticizing her skepticism. Falls short of 5 due to the repetitive end-of-session recapping that ignored Maria's explicit irritation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered several accurate reflections capturing both surface concerns and underlying values (work, family). Reflections were somewhat formulaic and missed emotionally significant moments, such as her admissions about waking up wheezy and her child hearing her wheeze.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on inhaler adherence despite a rushed client, with clear permission-seeking and efficient movement to a concrete behavioral trial. Falls short of 5 because focus was achieved by compressing exploration of motivation and ambivalence before jumping to the action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adapted the plan to Maria's constraints (phone alarm, coffee maker, phone check-in, text summary) and offered meaningful choices throughout. Early plan proposal was counselor-generated rather than inviting her own ideas, and final recaps were delivered counselor-driven rather than as a collaborative check.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout: every suggestion was gated with permission-seeking, and Maria's conditions (stop if side effects, drop it if no improvement) were accepted without pushback. Consistent affirmations of her ownership ('no lectures, just your decision', 'you're in charge') left her feeling control over the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The weakest area: the session shifted quickly into information-giving and plan-proposing rather than deliberately drawing out motivation from a pre-contemplative client. Maria's motivations (nighttime wheezing, her child) surfaced incidentally and were never amplified or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently treated Maria's input as authoritative, adapting the plan to her constraints and explicitly affirming her decision-making power. Falls short of 5 because the initial trial proposal was fairly counselor-generated rather than co-created from her own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged largely spontaneously from the client rather than through strategic evocation, including her noting increased nighttime rescue inhaler use and her child-related motivation. The counselor missed key opportunities to deepen these statements, moving quickly to plan formation instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled substantial sustain talk (dependence fears, steroid concerns) without arguing, using neutral reflections and permission-based framing to redirect toward a trial. Falls short of 5 because redirection sometimes relied on persuasion toward a specific plan rather than helping Maria resolve ambivalence through her own reasoning.
