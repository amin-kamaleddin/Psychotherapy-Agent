# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Dylan's frustration without labeling or disapproval. Affirmations are somewhat generic, lacking explicit acknowledgment of his strengths or deeper warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate but repetitive, with key phrases like 'angry and stuck, feeling unfairly targeted' repeated nearly verbatim. Surface content was captured but deeper emotional meaning—memory gaps, Jake's mockery—was never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed goal-directed but drifted to the wrong target: conduct-hearing prep instead of the drinking behavior itself. Memory blackouts and patterns of use were never revisited.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Dylan's choices genuinely shaped the session path, and role-play and the email task were jointly enacted. The counselor often supplied pre-written content rather than co-creating solutions from Dylan's own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with consistent choice-offering and explicit permission to disengage or decline further sessions. Framing centered on logistics rather than Dylan's ownership of his drinking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was assumed rather than elicited—no open questions about Dylan's values, concerns, or goals regarding drinking. His brief admission of drinking more than intended passed without exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeated option menus invited input and Dylan's selections shaped the agenda. However, the expert advice-giving stance (scripts, cheat sheets, tactical tips) positioned the counselor as strategist rather than drawing out Dylan's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dylan's spontaneous change talk ('I drank more than I meant to') was a key missed evocable moment with no reflection or follow-up. No questions were asked about his own reasons, desires, or concerns regarding drinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor wisely avoided arguing with rationalizations and offered a practical redirect away from 'everyone does it.' Sustain talk largely flowed unchallenged, with no double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancy-building.
