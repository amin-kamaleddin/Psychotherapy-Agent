# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout; accepted 'I'm not signing up for quitting' without pushback. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and missed personalized affirmation opportunities, such as Linda's self-awareness about all-or-nothing thinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections demonstrated good perspective-taking (e.g., enjoying smoking yet worrying about kids' exposure). Reflections sometimes stayed surface-level and didn't explore the emotional weight of Linda's self-critical failure pattern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, clear focus on smoking with progressive movement from ambivalence to a concrete, values-driven plan (Monday start, garage storage, slip recovery). Excellent directional steering without pushing beyond readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely co-constructed; Linda negotiated details, rejected the door note, and crafted her own cue. Occasionally relied on counselor-generated idea menus with client input invited afterward rather than elicited first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Linda's ownership with choices offered at every turn and explicit statements like 'I'm here to support whatever you choose.' Never pressured toward quitting, fully respecting her stated limit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation clearly drawn from Linda—her reasons, plan details, cue script, and slip-recovery philosophy all came from her. Evocation of deeper desire/importance talk about longer-term change was limited, and some strategies were counselor-supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Linda as the expert, and her contributions materially shaped the plan (husband's reminder, garage storage, Monday start). Occasional lists of counselor-offered ideas slightly shifted toward an advisor role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and built on change talk via open questions and the confidence ruler, yielding commitments like 'Monday. I've got this much, at least.' Deepening of desire/need talk was limited—her 'no plans to quit' wasn't explored further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided amplifying sustain talk, using neutral/double-sided reflections and pivoting toward actionable change. Pivots relied on forward-moving questions rather than explicit reprocessing of ambivalence, and lingering sustain talk lacked a final strategic summary.
