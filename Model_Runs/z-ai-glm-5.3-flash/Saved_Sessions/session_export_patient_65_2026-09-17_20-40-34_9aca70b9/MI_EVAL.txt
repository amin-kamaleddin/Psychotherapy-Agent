# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalizing the client's guarded stance and court involvement without any judgment. Affirmations were specific and genuine (e.g., 'That's you taking charge of your own situation,' 'That's a real insight about yourself'), with no labeling or moralizing throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and emotionally attuned, from the opening reflection capturing the court deadline's weight to the follow-up on being honest with his mother. Each reflection built on the client's exact words and deepened emotional disclosure, culminating in the client's wish to 'breathe around' his mother again.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on the target behavior (completing court-mandated service hours), progressing logically from identifying a site to clarifying paperwork, planning the call, and deepening personal motivation. Each turn advanced the agenda while staying client-centered, with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was a genuine dialogue between equals — the counselor never lectured, and solutions emerged from the client's own reasoning (e.g., 'if I don't have the right paper, the hours don't even count, right?'). Summaries were offered as hypotheses for the client to confirm, and the client actively built on each exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All questions were choice-oriented, and there was no advice-giving or pressure. When the client worried about callbacks, the counselor asked what would remind him rather than prescribing solutions, and explicitly validated his capacity for self-direction ('if it doesn't work, figure something else out').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor masterfully drew out motivation from within — the client's personal insight ('saying it out loud makes it more real'), his value of honesty with his mother, and his vision of success ('a weight off... she wouldn't have that look'). The session traced a clear arc from external compliance to personally meaningful reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly located expertise in the client ('You know your schedule and your situation better than anyone'), and every plan element — calling first, writing down questions, morning calls, reminders — originated from the client. The counselor's role was to reflect and ask rather than direct.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and deepened throughout: highlighting emergent language, reflecting the self-motivating statement 'I don't wanna keep putting it off like I have been,' and transforming external compliance into personal meaning. The final question about telling his mother it's done elicited rich desire and anticipated-benefit talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally handled well — the counselor neither argued with minimization nor dwelled on resistance, and honestly acknowledged the external court pressure to reduce reactance. A few statements (slow callbacks, past avoidance) received practical redirection rather than deliberate softening techniques like double-sided reflections, keeping this from a 5.
