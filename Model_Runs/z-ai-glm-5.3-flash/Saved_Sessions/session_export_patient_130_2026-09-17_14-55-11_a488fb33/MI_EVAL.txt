# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with an externally motivated client, using consistent validation and affirmations like 'good call on timing.' Warmth was somewhat formulaic ('I hear you' used repetitively) with limited explicit affirmation of the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening that accurately captured both sides of ambivalence, e.g., 'you feel fine and resent being pushed, yet you'd like a calmer home.' Rarely explored deeper emotional content (the cousin mention, fear beneath bravado), and reflections sometimes merely restated rather than added meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained good focus on a concrete proximate goal (one calm Saturday-morning conversation) progressively refined. However, the drug use itself—the referral target—was never named or directly explored, and the session stayed within the client's frame of marital communication.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative: co-constructed script language honoring the client's own phrasing and asked permission before suggesting. At times shifted into advice-giving mode (supplying scripts and coaching tips), tilting toward expert direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choice ('Which of those, if any, would you be open to trying?') and accepted refusals without pressure. Fell short of 5 due to little reinforcement of the client's ownership of change and slight nudging to practice despite reluctance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some client-generated motivation—a calmer home vision, tentative commitment ('I'll give it a shot this weekend'), and 'maybe I do care a little.' Evocation stayed confined to relationship communication, with no confidence exploration or discrepancy development around the substance use, and counselor-supplied scripts substituted for client-evoked content.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed that the client's expertise mattered by incorporating his natural phrasing and timing preferences into the plan. Weakened by sometimes supplying the 'right' answers through multiple scripted options without acknowledging the client as the authority on his situation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Modestly evoked change-adjacent talk ('maybe I can head out without a fight for once,' 'maybe I do, a little') and secured a concrete commitment. Change talk was cultivated only around communication skills, never directly around the drug use, with no confidence rulers or exploration of the client's own reasons to change his use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used effective double-sided reflections and avoided arguing with the client's denial, absorbing sustain talk neutrally. The sustain talk was never strategically reframed or used to develop discrepancy, and the core target behavior remained unaddressed.
