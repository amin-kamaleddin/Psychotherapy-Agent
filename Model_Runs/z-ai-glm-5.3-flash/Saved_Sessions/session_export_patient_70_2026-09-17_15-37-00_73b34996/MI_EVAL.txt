# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, affirming Lars's honesty and normalizing his struggles without labeling or disapproval. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and could better convey deeper, individualized unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurately reflects surface content but often pivots quickly to questions or options, missing deeper emotional layers such as Lars's fear of rejection, shame about literacy, and anxiety about the call. Few complex reflections capture the meaning beneath his words.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing reoffending via education/employment, progressing purposefully from exploration to a rehearsed next step. Momentum occasionally slows with long information lists, and the Friday risk plan was dropped without being revisited.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works collaboratively through forms, drafting, and role-play, incorporating Lars's own wording edits rather than imposing formal language. The counselor occasionally slips into expert, information-delivery mode where Lars becomes a chooser rather than a co-creator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Lars's choice, with his preferences directly shaping the session (forms, phone call, casual wording). Autonomy is largely expressed through menu-offering rather than explicitly affirming Lars's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some client motivation and commitment language through open questions, but often side-steps emotional content in favor of practical problem-solving. Little use of evocative questions, rulers, or vision exploration to deepen intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choice and frames help as collaborative, but the counselor retains an expert posture with counselor-generated menus rather than Lars's own ideas shaping direction. Lars's contributions are incorporated but not recognized as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and reinforces change talk, eliciting Lars's own motivations and commitment language ('This actually feels doable'). Misses chances to deepen or amplify change talk through elaboration questions or explicit confidence building.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and offers some reframes, but Lars's fear-based sustain talk is met with reassurance and pivots to options rather than strategic softening. Lacks double-sided reflections honoring ambivalence while steering toward change.
