# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirmations showing positive regard for Jordan's insight and progress. Affirmations are somewhat generic, and emotional experiences like guilt and fear of being left out are not explicitly validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captures Jordan's ambivalence well (e.g., feeling stuck, torn between change and peer pull). Deeper emotional themes—shame, mother's disappointment, fear of caving—are missed in favor of cognitive/strategic reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, clear focus on the shoplifting target behavior with every exchange tied back to the weekend plan. Session progresses logically from exploration to planning with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements exist (Jordan chose the cash strategy and his own exit line), but the counselor drives most content with rapid-fire questions and pre-packaged menus. Jordan largely reacts to proposals rather than co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan's choices drive the plan, with framing like 'pick one or two' and 'whatever feels most important.' Frequent tactic menus subtly narrow perceived options to the counselor's suggestions, and ownership of setbacks/pace is underemphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and role-plays elicited Jordan's own reasoning and coping language. However, motivation is often displaced by counselor-supplied solutions, with little evocation of values, identity, or future vision.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional invitations for input ('Which sounds most doable?'), but the counselor repeatedly supplies pre-packaged tactic lists instead of drawing ideas from Jordan. This tips the stance toward expert-driven and advice-heavy.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled use of scaling questions, evocative prompts, and role-play rehearsal generated concrete commitments and raised confidence from 5 to 7. Much change content is counselor-supplied, and Jordan's own reasons (court, mom) are not deeply developed or summarized to amplify commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (peer pressure, urges, fear of being a 'loser') is neither reinforced nor dwelt upon, but handling is mostly neutral redirection. No double-sided reflections or reframing to strategically resolve ambivalence.
