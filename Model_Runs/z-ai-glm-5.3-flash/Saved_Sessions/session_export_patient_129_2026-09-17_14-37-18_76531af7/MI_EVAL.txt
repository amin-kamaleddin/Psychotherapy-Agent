# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating Tyler's irritation and handling his pushback on safety questions without pressure. Falls short of a 5 due to somewhat procedural warmth and no explicit affirmation of Tyler's worth independent of behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several accurate reflections are offered, but they are brief and quickly followed by menus or advice, limiting emotional depth. Tyler's feelings of being treated like a child and fears of being labeled a 'druggie' are never deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays anchored to the substance use and its family fallout, with menu structures guiding choices well. However, direction narrows to family communication and drifts from exploring the substance use itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menus are offered and permission asked, but the substantive work — scripts, breathing, boundaries — is generated almost entirely by the counselor. Tyler is cast as a rehearsal partner rather than a co-constructor of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is emphasized repeatedly ('I won't push you,' 'Which option do you want?') and Tyler's refusals are accepted. Recommendations are delivered as ready-made plans, with little affirmation of Tyler's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is rarely drawn from within Tyler; questions are informational or menu-based and advice arrives without first eliciting his views. His one self-motivated statement about wanting calm at home is not developed into broader reasons for change regarding use.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional choice among options respects Tyler's input, but the stance shifts to expert-driven advising with prescriptive scripts and recommendations. Tyler's own expertise about his mother is not leveraged in shaping the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is minimal deliberate evocation — the psychiatrist never asks about Tyler's own reasons for change, downsides of use, or hopes for the future. Tyler's sliver of change-oriented language about the conflict being exhausting is pivoted to script rehearsal rather than deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced and is generally redirected, but building scripts around 'I use occasionally, my grades are fine' implicitly amplifies minimizing language. No double-sided reflections are used to hold ambivalence, so sustain talk is sidestepped rather than strategically softened.
