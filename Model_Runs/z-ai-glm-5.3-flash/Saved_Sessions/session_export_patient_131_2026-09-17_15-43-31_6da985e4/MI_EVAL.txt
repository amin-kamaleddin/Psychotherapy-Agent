# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, affirming Wiremu's efforts and accepting his remorse without judgment. Falls short of 5 due to formulaic, repetitive affirmations ('That's huge') and limited normalization of his shame and past relapses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening capturing the core ambivalence, e.g., 'You're torn — knowing a safer home... but you also value freedom.' Reflections tend to be brief content summaries rather than deep explorations of the emotional weight of grief, fear, and fortnightly visits.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every counselor turn advances a clear agenda tied to family reunification and sobriety — rulers, pros/cons, rehearsal, a written agreement, a Saturday move-in date, and obstacle planning. No topic drift anywhere in the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor behaves as a genuine equal: Wiremu declines the offered pros/cons list, generates his own, authors his scripts, and drives the plan, while expertise (template, role-play) is offered only when invited. Exemplary power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Next steps are consistently framed as Wiremu's choice, enabling his ownership ('my choice, not a punishment'). However, frequent multi-option menus feel formulaic and occasionally steer rather than follow, moving quickly toward action-planning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All major motivations and commitments come from Wiremu's own words — 'shame's not a good enough reason to lose my kids,' 'I've got to pick my kids over a good night out.' The counselor never argues for change, instead using rulers, rehearsal, and questions to draw out his values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Wiremu is consistently treated as the expert on his own life, with permission sought before offering help and his choices shaping the session. Falls short of 5 because some turns pack multiple options that slightly steer, rather than letting Wiremu lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled, consistent evocation throughout: rulers elicited strong commitment ('importance? probably a nine'), and confidence-building questions produced concrete planning language and a rise in confidence from 6-7 to eight. Wiremu left with a full action plan and 'no more excuses.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (freedom, shame, Tristan) is effectively redirected via double-sided reflections and pivots to rulers and pros/cons, without amplifying it. Not a 5 because reflections of sustain talk are simple mirrors rather than strategic reframes, and missed chances to affirm the legitimacy of his attachment to freedom.
