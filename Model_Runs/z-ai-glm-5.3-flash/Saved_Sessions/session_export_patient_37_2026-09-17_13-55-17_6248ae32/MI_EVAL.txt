# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validating the client's hesitancy ("It makes sense to feel conflicted and judged") and affirming her courage. However, warmth is somewhat sparse and formulaic; list-like responses can feel impersonal rather than warmly accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections (acknowledging feeling conflicted and judged), but the counselor frequently shifts quickly from reflection to information delivery. Emotional nuances—fear of judgment, anxiety about being "that difficult mom," self-consciousness about a shaky voice—are often met with tips rather than deeper reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on the target behavior—vaccination decision-making and appointment preparation—throughout the session. Every response ties back to questions, scripts, scheduling, or rehearsal. Direction is strong but somewhat checklist-driven, leaning toward task-completion rather than guiding through ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration—the counselor asks the client's preference between options and responds to her requests. However, interaction is uneven; the counselor dominates with pre-prepared lists, scripts, and notes, functioning more as educator/consultant than equal partner. The client's own ideas are solicited late rather than integrated early.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly affirms choice ("it's your choice," "it's okay to ask for written info, time, or a follow-up") and avoids pressuring, emphasizing informed decision-making. Scripts model boundary-setting language. Partly undercut by persuasive framing ("I recommend vaccinating today") and rapid solution-giving that limits the client's room to exercise her own decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to elicit the client's own motivations, values, or reasons for change. Motivation is addressed almost exclusively through information provision. Change talk ("exposing a baby... that sits with me") emerges spontaneously but is not explored, reflected, or amplified. The session's engine is education, not evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers collaboration in places and the client's contributions shape the session, but the counselor frequently assumes the expert role—delivering pre-made lists, scripts, and notes with little exploration of the client's own knowledge or solutions. Heavy pre-scripted content limits genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged organically ("I'll call tomorrow and book the visit"), partly in response to clear education and choice framing. However, the counselor does not actively deepen it—no open questions about her reasons for vaccinating, values as a parent, or confidence ratings. Missed opportunities to consolidate the shift toward scheduling the appointment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Briefly acknowledges sustain talk ("Totally reasonable to question it") and does not amplify doubts. However, relies primarily on informational correction rather than MI techniques like double-sided reflections or strategic reframing. Some responses inadvertently keep attention on reasons not to vaccinate; no deliberate pivoting toward the client's change-oriented statements.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **3.2/5**

**Summary:** This session demonstrates solid structure, clear direction, and respectful autonomy support in addressing vaccine hesitancy. Strengths include nonjudgmental validation, clear psychoeducation correcting misinformation about chickenpox, and skillful provision of self-advocacy tools—the client moved from hesitancy to committing to schedule the appointment. Key weaknesses: the session is heavily information-driven at the expense of core MI techniques—empathy is adequate but shallow, evocation is minimal, sustain talk is neutralized via facts rather than reflective strategies, and collaboration suffers from an expert stance with pre-made materials.

**Recommendations:** Use more open questions to evoke the client's motivations and values as a parent; deepen reflections on her emotional experience; respond to change talk with affirmation and elaboration questions; and co-create materials with the client rather than delivering them ready-made.
