# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor communicates consistent warmth and nonjudgment, affirming Elena's protectiveness and supporting self-compassion ('Don't beat yourself up — one week is a solid start'). Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and repetitive ('You're juggling so much' appears nearly verbatim multiple times), preventing a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of Elena's ambivalence and circumstances are offered throughout, validating her defensiveness as care. However, deeper emotional content like her fear of being seen as hurting her son and her pain about her ex is skipped, and recycled reflections suggest formula over deep attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoke exposure and child health, progressively narrowing to a concrete actionable plan (cardigan, fence spot, bag, check-in) while redirecting tangents. The target behavior shifts from cessation toward harm reduction without explicitly negotiating that reframing with Elena.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is largely co-constructed, with Elena's ideas substantially driving the plan (hook by the door, Sunday laundry, 9pm text, text message content). The very first intervention presented a full menu of expert-derived options before eliciting Elena's own ideas, creating a modest power imbalance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options rather than directives are repeatedly offered, and Elena's refusals (note on door, midweek calls, quitline) are honored without pushing. The early list of five options felt slightly prescriptive in volume, and Elena's own capacity for self-direction is not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions draw out Elena's values and self-generated solutions, and her motivation is clearly client-derived. Evocation is narrow, however—motivation is channeled only into the harm-reduction step rather than developed toward her stated contemplation of quitting, with no exploration of her broader reasons or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena is positioned as decision-maker, and her pushback is incorporated into the plan rather than overridden, with her stating the plan feels 'like something I can actually do.' The counselor retains agenda control in early option-giving and does not explicitly acknowledge Elena's expertise in her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Space is created for Elena to voice change language ('I've thought about quitting,' 'His ears, not my habit. Yet.'), but this change talk is never actively amplified or reinforced. The counselor never reflects back her 'Yet,' asks no evocative questions about benefits or confidence, and steers toward harm reduction rather than her stated contemplation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not argued with, and underlying values (protecting her son, guarding quiet time) are reflected. However, double-sided reflections pivoting toward change are rarely used, sustain talk is sometimes paraphrased back neutrally, and it is accommodated ('That's it for now') without gently re-inviting ambivalence toward quitting.
