# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with genuine warmth, affirmations of strengths (IB, first-chair sax), and repeated confidentiality reassurances. Fell short of 5 due to occasionally formulaic reassurance and no explicit normalization of her ambivalence or blackouts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate summaries captured both content and her confidentiality anxiety, and incorporated her own description of 'fuzzy.' Weaker when she said 'that sounds kind of preachy'—the counselor pivoted to options without reflecting her irritation or fear of being labeled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and harm-reduction from the opening question through a specific behavioral plan. Every turn advanced the target with no meaningful drift off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely between equals—asked permission, offered options, and co-constructed the plan from Isabelle's own ideas (her excuse line, Chloe's text, Marco driving). Fell short of 5 because early turns leaned on structured option-listing rather than drawing the agenda from her first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, repeated autonomy support: menu-based options, permission-seeking before texting, respecting refusals (drink limit) and limits ('five minutes max'), and explicit statements like 'I'll respect your choices.' Choice ownership was consistently reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective evocation (eliciting her 'fuzzy' definition, chosen strategies, and values-linked questions), but motivation was drawn out only superficially. The counselor supplied most substantive content rather than developing Isabelle's deeper values—band pride, academics—as intrinsic reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Isabelle as decision-maker ('Which of those, if any, could you try?'), and her contributions genuinely shaped the plan. Slightly limited by stacked questions offering many options at once, which directed rather than fully followed her agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked commitment and planning language—Isabelle generated concrete strategies ('stopping when I feel fuzzy,' the sax excuse, Chloe's 'you good?' text) and realistic commitment language. Weaker on eliciting deep, values-driven reasons for change; most change talk was situational harm-reduction.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflected sustain talk neutrally ('I hear the memory gaps feel normal to you') and inserted one didactic counter about blackout risks. Missing were double-sided reflections linking her sustain talk to her stated goals, and the counselor's acceptance of 'I'll get off my back' framing implicitly validated resistance rather than resolving ambivalence.
