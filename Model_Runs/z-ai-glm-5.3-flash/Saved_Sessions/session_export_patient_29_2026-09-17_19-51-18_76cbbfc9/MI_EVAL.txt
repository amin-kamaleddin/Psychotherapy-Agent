# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and nonjudgmental regard throughout; David's relapses, hiding spots, and fears were met with normalization rather than disapproval. No labels or shaming, even around past relapse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including accurate double-sided and emotional reflections. Fell slightly short in deeply reflecting the emotional weight of the father's illness and past relapse.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, progressive focus on smoking cessation from triggers through quit date and readiness planning. Each response moved the conversation purposefully forward without drifting or rushing the client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with co-constructed planning and inclusive language. The clinician briefly shifted into an informational expert role when answering the medication efficacy question rather than first exploring David's perspective.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions—including the quit date and medication use—were consistently framed as David's to make, with options presented without pressure. No coercive language appeared anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was built on eliciting David's own motivations and solutions, which he generated throughout (walk, coffee idea, bad-day plan). The clinician never argued for change or supplied motivation, culminating in David's own accountability structure.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** David was consistently positioned as the expert, with every key plan element coming from his own mouth and actively credited by the clinician. The plan was so fully his that David himself asked whether it sounded like a real plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocative questions drew out rich change talk—David's vision of clean mornings, his 'nobody actually cares' realization, and 'somebody's got to go first.' Change statements were reflected back and amplified, ending in commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubts and fears were generally validated briefly and pivoted to collaborative problem-solving. The opening dwelt slightly long on sustain talk, and the white-knuckling worry received a somewhat informational rather than evocative response.
