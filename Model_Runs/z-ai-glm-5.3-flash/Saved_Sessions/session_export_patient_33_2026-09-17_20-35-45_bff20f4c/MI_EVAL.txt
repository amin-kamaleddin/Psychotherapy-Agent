# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, normalizing Diego's honesty about needle reuse and explicitly countering anticipated shame. Affirms his courage in showing up and naming 'Don't leave me alone' as an act of trust.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and emotionally attuned, e.g., the double-sided reflection naming the 'exhausting' dual burden of relief and fear, which Diego confirms. Also validates both practical and shame-based barriers and his self-awareness about deflecting with humor.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, purposeful focus on harm reduction throughout, progressing from motivation to rehearsal to problem-solving to concrete action planning. Ends with two specific, time-bound commitments and a follow-up plan with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely works as equals — 'let's figure it out together' — with the housemate script coming entirely from Diego and the counselor facilitating rather than dictating. Information offers are immediately paired with invitations for Diego's judgment on feasibility.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforces Diego's ownership ('you're the expert on what'll work'), offers choices, and frames the exchange visit as low-commitment. Quitting is never pushed, and the closing check-in respects his capacity rather than assuming compliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn out rather than imposed, opening with 'What made you decide to show up?' and letting Diego supply his own reasons. The rehearsal elicits Diego's own words, and every plan element originates from him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Diego as the expert, and client choices shape the session (step order, porch setting, conditions for success). Asks 'How could I help you prepare?' rather than prescribing, consistently returning agency to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk, highlighting 'ten out of ten ready' and asking evocative questions that build toward concrete, client-generated commitments. Each change statement is reflected back and built upon.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well with double-sided reflections that honor ambivalence without amplifying it, and accepts 'I'm not quitting' without argument. Minor refinement: could pivot slightly more briskly toward Diego's expressed desire for safety at points.
