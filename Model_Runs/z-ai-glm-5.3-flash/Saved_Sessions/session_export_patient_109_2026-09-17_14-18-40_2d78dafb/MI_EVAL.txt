# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'small steps reduce pressure' and 'be kind if it goes differently.' Rafael felt supported and not lectured, but affirmations were brief and rarely highlighted his strengths beyond the plan itself.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Feelings were acknowledged ('feeling silly is normal'), but reflections stayed surface-level. Emotional content like fear of repeated failure and feeling wiped out was met with logistics rather than deeper reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout: every exchange tied back to returning to the gym, progressing logically from barriers to a concrete, achievable Tuesday 6 PM step with backup plan and follow-up. No drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rafael actively shaped the plan (choosing Tuesday, one session instead of three, shorter texts) and the counselor adapted. However, a full pre-made plan was presented early, tilting toward the counselor generating solutions that the client then adjusted.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice and permission-granting: options offered, renegotiation to one session accepted without pushback, and 5-minute walk backup framed as acceptable. Falls short because solutions were proposed first, then consent sought.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness rulers elicited importance (7-8) and confidence (4) ratings, and minimal framing drew out commitment. But deeper reasons for change were never explored, and the confidence rating of 4 was not mined to expand the client's own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission was repeatedly asked and Rafael's input shaped the plan. Still, the counselor often took the expert role with detailed prescriptions rather than first drawing on what Rafael already knew or would try.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers effectively elicited change talk ('I'll try it Tuesday... it's a start'), but it wasn't deepened—'it doesn't feel impossible anymore' passed without reinforcement, and the confidence rating of 4 went unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Doubt ('part of me still wonders if I'll actually follow through') was redirected with planning rather than normalized or met with double-sided reflection. Ambivalence was handled by pivoting to steps rather than skillfully resolving it.
