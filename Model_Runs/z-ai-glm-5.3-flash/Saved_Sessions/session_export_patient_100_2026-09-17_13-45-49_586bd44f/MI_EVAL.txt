# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, non-judgmental stance, reassuring Maya she was not in trouble and clearly explaining confidentiality. However, warmth felt thin and transactional, with a vulnerable disclosure about her drinking going unaffirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several accurate reflections appeared (e.g., naming her frustration, embarrassment, and social worry), but they were brief and quickly redirected to advice or option menus. Deeper emotional content—shame, fear of her mother's yelling, anxiety about being watched—was acknowledged once then not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained reasonable focus, moving coherently from rapport to anxiety management, confidentiality clarification, and repairing trust with her mother, including appropriate safety screening. Some drift into general stress-management occurred, but the thread back to the referral concern was largely maintained.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session's substance consisted of counselor-generated scripts and menus that Maya merely accepted or modified, with limited genuine co-construction. Maya's input mostly shaped tone rather than content, and her substantive pushback on scripted language was not used to build the plan from her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered and confidentiality explained in a supportive, non-punitive frame, and Maya's chosen focus was honored. Autonomy was undercut, though, by pre-written scripts and prescribed commitment terms imposed from outside; Maya's self-revision of the script was a strong autonomous moment that came despite counselor behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions were largely supplied by the counselor rather than elicited, with few open questions exploring Maya's own reasons, hopes, or vision for rebuilding trust. Her spontaneous admission about increased drinking—a powerful evocative opening—was not developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor frequently assumed the expert role, drafting scripts for Maya to memorize and prescribing specific commitments. Maya explicitly pushed back that the line 'sounds kind of scripted,' revealing the counselor-generated content did not fit her life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maya spontaneously generated meaningful change talk, including admitting her drinking had increased and expressing a desire to rebuild trust, but the counselor missed nearly all of it. Her admission was met with a safety checklist rather than reflection or deepening, and no open questions evoked her own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maya's sustain talk ('It's just what everyone does at parties') was not reinforced and the counselor did not argue with it. However, few deliberate strategies were used—no double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with her own change statements, leaving it mostly bypassed rather than strategically softened.
