# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'That's momentum you built yourself' and normalizes guarded behavior. Affirmations feel somewhat formulaic and the mandated nature of the session is not explicitly validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening including complex double-sided reflections on ambivalence. Reflections sometimes track surface logistics rather than the emotional weight of Julian's fear of failure and judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains steady focus and progressively narrows toward concrete action (placement, bus route, Tuesday call, documentation). Some exchanges circle logistical details rather than deepening intrinsic motivation or linking to larger values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely between equal partners, with Julian experiencing the session as his own work. The counselor sometimes steers toward a pre-figured structure and does not explicitly negotiate the agenda at the start.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Control is repeatedly handed back to the client ('That's up to you — this is your time and your plan'), with no directive advice-giving. Every next step originates from Julian, and his capacity to self-direct is affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits Julian's reasons, preferences, and solutions, with change talk increasing across the session. Evocation of personal meaning is limited — the work stays anchored in external compliance rather than intrinsic values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client from the outset, and every plan element (food bank, Tuesday, shift question) is client-generated. The counselor affirms Julian's ownership directly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently evokes and reinforces change talk through open questions and highlighting client-generated momentum. This produced genuine client planning ('Maybe I'll ask the food bank how many shifts they'd give me') and self-motivating language ('now I actually have, like, a plan').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor validates doubts without amplifying them, e.g., 'That's a real concern, not you making excuses.' Could have more deliberately converted Julian's worries into additional commitment language.
