# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with normalization of the visit and affirmations, though warmth was somewhat formulaic and affirmations focused on task performance rather than Laura's character strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic validation was present, but reflective listening was thin — Laura's fear about the drunk-driver incident and social worries ('the weird sober one') were skipped over in favor of immediate practical advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed well-focused on alcohol-related ride safety with concrete next steps, though there was minor drift into generic safety advice and a slightly scattered early agenda question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative moments (role-play, option selection), but the counselor dominated with expert-driven checklists and didn't build the plan from Laura's own existing strategies and resources.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura was given choices among options, but the overall style was advisory and directive, offering solution menus rather than first exploring her own ideas and ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A few open questions and role-play practice drew out some content, but the counselor never explored Laura's values, reasons for staying safe, or confidence — most material was supplied rather than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor occasionally asked for Laura's input, but the dominant expert stance with rapid-fire advice lists meant Laura's own expertise drove the useful content without explicit acknowledgment.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged largely from Laura's intrinsic motivation ('I'd want to help my friends too', 'I'll talk to my mom tonight'), but the counselor reinforced rather than deliberately evoked or deepened it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** No resistance was amplified, which was good, but the counselor missed chances to reframe Laura's mild disclaimers ('I was expecting a lecture') into autonomy affirmations or use double-sided reflections for her ambivalence about fitting in.
