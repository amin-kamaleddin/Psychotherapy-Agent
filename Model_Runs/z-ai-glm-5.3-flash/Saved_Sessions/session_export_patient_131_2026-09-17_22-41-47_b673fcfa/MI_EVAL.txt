# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard throughout, with no judgment even around drinking triggers and past failures. Cultural identity (kōrero, eh, whānau) was honored respectfully, and worth was consistently affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections capturing both content and emotion, such as 'structure without feeling locked up' and 'pride and fear.' Wiremu's repeated deepening self-disclosure confirms the reflections landed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful focus progressing logically from envisioning the ideal living situation to barriers, anchors, and a concrete commitment. Every question advanced the arc with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session unfolded between equal partners, with curiosity-driven questions and each exchange built on Wiremu's contributions. The kōrero plan, uncle as anchor, and final commitment were all his ideas developed jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was actively fostered, e.g., 'what would support need to look like for it to feel like your choice?' The entire plan originated from and was owned by Wiremu, with no pressure or persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reasons for change were drawn entirely from the client, eliciting his own insight about family accountability and 'counting myself as someone worth keeping promises to.' A hallmark of expert evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Wiremu designed his entire support plan himself, which the psychiatrist explicitly named and affirmed. No advice, prescriptions, or claims of expert authority appeared.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional evocation and reinforcement of change talk, from envisioning questions to naming commitment language. Wiremu's language escalated from 'worth a try' to 'one call, this weekend... Done.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled skillfully with double-sided reflections and pivots toward change, reframing shame as responsibility. A few moments around pride and shame could have been summarized or explored slightly more explicitly before reframing.
