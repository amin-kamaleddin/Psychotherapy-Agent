# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance, explicitly normalizing slips with 'no guilt' framing. However, warmth felt somewhat formulaic, with limited individualized affirmation of Jonathan's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was solid and accurate, capturing Jonathan's overwhelm and desire for simplicity. Deeper emotional exploration was missed, notably when Jonathan disclosed fear about his rising numbers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, consistent focus on medication adherence and glucose checking throughout, with no topic drift. The session ended with a well-specified behavioral plan including time, tone, fallback, and check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several decisions were genuinely co-constructed (snooze fallback, flexible routine, telling his wife on his terms). However, collaboration was occasionally superficial, with the counselor presenting pre-packaged options rather than jointly generating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor regularly offered choices and affirmed Jonathan's control, respecting his boundary about his wife. Mildly directive moments—such as recommending specific alarm labels and default structures—kept it from a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions and open prompts effectively drew out Jonathan's own reasons for change, and confidence rose from 3 to 4-5. Evocation was somewhat mechanical, and the significant fear disclosure was not explored to deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered choices and asked for input, but repeatedly steered toward pre-formatted solutions on his own terms. Jonathan's key insight about quitting when things 'feel like rules someone else made for me' was affirmed but not leveraged into co-creating the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The scaling question effectively drew out conditions for change, and Jonathan made meaningful self-motivational statements, including his fear disclosure. The counselor did not consistently deepen or reflect this change talk back, nor explicitly elicit commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully acknowledged reluctance without dwelling and neutralized the all-or-nothing pattern with no-guilt framing. Some responses echoed sustain talk content without strategic reframing, and reluctance was occasionally summarized rather than pivoted toward change.
