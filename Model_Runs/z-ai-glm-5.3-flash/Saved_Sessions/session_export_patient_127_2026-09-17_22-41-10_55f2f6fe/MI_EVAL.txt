# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and warm with no judgment regarding the client's criminal history. Affirmations are frequent but occasionally repetitive and formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures both sides of ambivalence and the client's feelings about Fiona. Could probe deeper emotional undercurrents such as fear of relapse or shame about his prison record.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus guiding the client from values toward a concrete action step and contingency planning. Every exchange moves progressively toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ideas are co-developed and the counselor asks rather than directs, with the client's own suggestions shaping the plan. Does not explicitly check accuracy of reflections or jointly negotiate the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support throughout ('your goals, your life, your call'; 'no commitment'). Change is framed as entirely the client's decision, with no pressure or persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertly draws motivation from within using vivid evocative questions (e.g., imagining Fiona's face). The client articulates his own reasons, future vision, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located with the client, and his own ideas drive the session. Less than full marks because agenda-setting is not explicitly handed to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation ('What would it take for you to properly decide?') produces strong desire, need, and commitment talk. Confidence language could be deepened further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (cost, boredom, feeling shut out) is acknowledged briefly without reinforcement, using double-sided reflections to pivot toward change. Doubts could occasionally be redirected more rapidly.
