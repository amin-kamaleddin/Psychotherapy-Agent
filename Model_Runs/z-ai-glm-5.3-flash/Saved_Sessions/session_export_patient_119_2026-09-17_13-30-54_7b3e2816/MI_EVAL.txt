# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with affirmations of Selene's reasons for change and normalization of imperfect adherence; she explicitly noted she expected a lecture and didn't get one. Falls short due to formulaic, repetitive plan recaps that feel mechanical rather than individually attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections were present, but reflections were sparse and the counselor asked rapid-fire assessment questions the client experienced as overwhelming. Deeper emotional content like her fear of lab results and dread of her husband's nagging was acknowledged briefly but not explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed firmly focused on medication adherence and closed with a concrete, specific behavioral plan. However, direction was achieved through task focus rather than strategic MI direction, never exploring the underlying ambivalence about importance and confidence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor showed responsiveness by incorporating Selene's feedback, but frequently delivered multi-part advice blocks without inviting her ideas first. The repeated one-sided recaps resembled a checklist handoff more than joint planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The 'trial, not a forever overhaul' framing strengthened Selene's sense of choice, which she voiced directly. Not a 5 because options were presented as a counselor-chosen menu and closing recaps edged toward instructing rather than affirming ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nearly all change strategies originated from the counselor, with almost no open questions to evoke change talk or confidence. Selene's motivation surfaced spontaneously but was acknowledged rather than developed or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered choices and asked permission at times, but the overall stance leaned expert-driven, supplying solutions rather than drawing out Selene's own ideas. Selene was never explicitly named as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The trial framing elicited commitment and desire language, but the counselor missed opportunities to deepen it. No reflections, amplification, or evocative follow-ups when Selene shared meaningful change talk about her kids and wanting improved numbers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and the trial framing reduced its weight, but it was handled mostly by moving past it rather than actively softening it. Responses to ambivalence were psychoeducational rather than double-sided reflections or evocative questions.
