# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing Jordan's frustration with the court referral and offering affirmations like 'Nice work' and 'That's meaningful.' Affirmations remain somewhat brief and process-focused rather than deeply personalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate reflective listening, capturing Jordan's ambivalence, peer-pressure concerns, and fear of the post-step-out moment. Reflections occasionally stay at surface level and do not deeply explore the emotional weight of Jordan's guilt or fear of social rejection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains exemplary, consistent focus on reducing shoplifting throughout — moving logically from triggers to readiness scaling, strategy selection, a concrete plan (mall ~1pm Saturday, preset text, waiting outside), and contingency planning, with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Operates as an equal partner — Jordan rates his own readiness, co-constructs the Saturday plan, and decisions about strategies are clearly his. Collaboration is slightly tempered by a structured, menu-driven approach where the counselor drives the sequence of questions and option lists.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently asks permission, offers menus rather than prescriptions, and lets Jordan select strategies and lines to rehearse — Jordan explicitly recognizes ownership ('it's mine'). Occasionally supplies a large volume of pre-packaged suggestions, which slightly reduces space for Jordan's own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits motivation from within using rulers and open questions, drawing out Jordan's own insights and self-generated solutions (cousin support, 'wait outside' plan). Sometimes fills space with suggestions before Jordan has fully articulated his motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Jordan as the expert, asking which options sound doable or natural, and Jordan's contributions genuinely shaped the session (chose the phone-call line, cousin as support, 'wait outside' plan). Some interactions lean toward a menu-driven format where the counselor supplies most of the option content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evokes change talk through readiness and confidence rulers, drawing out meaningful commitments like the delay strategy, the cousin text, and 'maybe that's proof I can actually do this.' Could deepen further by explicitly reflecting Jordan's evolving commitment language back to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges Jordan's sustain talk (peer influence, urges, 'one small change feels pointless') without reinforcing it, then pivots to actionable low-barrier options. Some responses engage extensively with sustain-talk content without always pairing it with double-sided reflections reconnecting Jordan to his motivations.
