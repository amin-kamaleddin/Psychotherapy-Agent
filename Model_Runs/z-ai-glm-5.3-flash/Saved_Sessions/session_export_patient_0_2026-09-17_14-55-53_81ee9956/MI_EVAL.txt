# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance, honoring Adrian's boundary of safer use without disapproval or labeling. However, affirmations of Adrian as a person were rare, and warmth sometimes felt procedural rather than personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were adequate but stayed at the logistical level, missing key emotional disclosures like the fear the infection caused and shame about telling mates. The counselor never explored the underlying boredom or social function ('something to do on weekends') that Adrian repeatedly emphasized.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Focus on methamphetamine-related risk was consistent, but the agenda narrowed prematurely to harm-reduction logistics without renegotiating the broader referral concern about use itself. Much of the session became resource-finding rather than progressive movement on the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor largely worked as a partner—asking permission, offering menus, and letting Adrian set the agenda and select options. Collaboration was uneven, though, as repeated unsolicited expert instruction blocks shifted the dynamic toward expert consultation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support throughout: repeated choice offerings, explicit honoring of the safer-use boundary, respect for privacy needs, and no pressure even when Adrian defended continuing use. Adrian's stated willingness to return is direct evidence of its effectiveness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation was the weakest domain—the counselor's default was supplying information rather than eliciting Adrian's own motivations, values, or ideas. Almost no open questions probed why the infection mattered, longer-term hopes, or how use fit into his life.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing was present through permission-seeking and choice offerings, but the sessions frequently shifted into expert/information-delivery mode with long instruction lists Adrian did not request in that depth. Expertise about drugs, risks, and strategies resided mostly with the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was successfully evoked in the harm-reduction domain, including meaningful commitment-adjacent language like 'this is me doing something about that, in my own way.' However, the counselor never evoked change talk about the use itself—reducing, evaluating impact, or future goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing with sustain talk and steered clear of power struggles, but largely accepted it without strategic pivots or double-sided reflections. Adrian's 'kind of makes it seem more serious than it is' was a prime ambivalence moment that was skipped over in favor of logistics.
