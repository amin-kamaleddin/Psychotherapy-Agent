# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, even when Amina was dismissive ('It's not really any of your business'). The psychiatrist responded with warmth and validation without judgment or siding against anyone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately captured both surface content and underlying meaning (control, autonomy, feeling blamed). These reflections were pivotal in drawing Amina out of one-word answers into substantive disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintained coherent focus on household conflict, building logically from rapport to coping to timing of communication. Excellent stage-appropriate direction, though no concrete change goal was yet anchored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative, with Amina's ideas (headphones, privacy hour, calm-TV moments) shaping the session's substance. The psychiatrist framed questions as joint inquiry and never imposed an agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy explicitly and repeatedly supported ('I'm not here with an agenda'), with no advice-giving or pressure anywhere. This directly counteracted the powerlessness Amina described at home.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly evoked the client's own ideas and observations—every key insight of the session was hers. Not yet developed into explicit change-oriented motivation or confidence that change is possible.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise consistently located within the client ('You're the expert on your own life'), and Amina's contributions genuinely shaped the session direction. Power-sharing at the highest level.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked tentative change language ('Maybe if people just... knocked') and amplified it ('That's you knowing what you need'). Change talk remains early and was not yet deepened into commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent sustain talk (hopelessness, 'it won't change anything') was consistently met with reflective reframes toward her strengths rather than reinforcement. Some bursts received only brief acknowledgment rather than an active strategic pivot.
