# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard; never judges or lectures, even when Ethan minimizes or hedges, and validates his ambivalence ('Both of those things can be true at once'). Warmth feels genuine and tailored to an adolescent's sensitivity about judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are consistently accurate and capture emotional meaning, e.g., 'The habit part feels trickier than the people part' and the two-sided reflection of the Jake's conflict. Distilled Ethan's social-anxiety core and his own insights back to him precisely.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus on Ethan's weekend drinking from first reflection to closing — moving from ambivalence to triggers to concrete plans and relapse prevention. No topic drift, with pacing that guides toward change while remaining client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative; agenda co-constructed through questions, and every client contribution (Marcus as ride, holding a drink) became part of the plan. Ethan's ideas genuinely shaped session content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently affirmed — the clinician never prescribes, asks for Ethan's input at each step, and celebrates 'You built your own plan.' Ethan's closing remark that he expected a lecture but instead feels okay directly evidences experiencing freedom of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates from the conviction that motivation resides in Ethan, systematically eliciting his own reasons ('beats sitting around getting trashed'), his definition of a win, and his confidence, then deliberately reflecting the change talk back. Developmentally attuned to an adolescent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Ethan as the expert ('You're the expert on your own life'), and all plan elements originate from him. Clinician contributions limited to reflecting, affirming, and strategic questioning — the standard for substantial client influence.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematically evokes and reinforces Ethan's change language, flagging his own reasons and commitments ('Saturday basketball locked in,' 'remembering it and not being in trouble'). Open questions reliably produced desire, ability, and commitment talk plus coping plans for triggers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced; double-sided reflections keep change salient, and minimization is reframed as realism rather than argued with. Scored 4 because the 'even if it wasn't perfect' framing of Ethan's 'beer at Jake's, whatever' slightly amplifies the status quo instead of pivoting toward his stated win of remembering the weekend.
