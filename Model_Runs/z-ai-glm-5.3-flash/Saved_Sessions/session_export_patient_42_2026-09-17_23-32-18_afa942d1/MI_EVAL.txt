# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, including explicit non-judgment ('I want you to know there's no judgment here') even when the patient reveals self-protective avoidance. Warm affirmations reinforce the client's own efforts without any disapproving language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing subtle meanings like the smoke being 'part of the connection itself' and the cooler-moment sting. Each reflection lands, as evidenced by Elise deepening her disclosure after every one.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on smoking cessation, progressing logically from social ambivalence to deeper avoidance insight to concrete next steps. Every response steers back to the target behavior without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is fully collaborative, with the plan co-constructed from Elise's own material (Sandra, the lake trip, the doorway script) rather than prescribed. Questions explicitly invite her take ('I'd love your take,' 'What feels most doable about it?').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice is repeatedly affirmed ('You get to set the pace'), and no advice is imposed. The counselor even returns her question ('is that enough?') to her own judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client through evocative questions about what quitting would make possible. Elise generates her own reasons (her niece, the lake trip) and her own strategies (texting Sandra, 'I'll catch you after').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions Elise as the expert on her friendships and pace, and her contributions genuinely shape the session direction. The Sandra plan, lake trip motivation, and doorway script all originate from her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor selectively reflects change talk and asks evocative questions, producing strong self-generated commitment language ('I'd like to be at the top next time,' 'I could text her tonight'). The client's own words carry the momentum of change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of being the odd one out, break-room pressure, keeping change 'impossible' as self-protection) is acknowledged with validation but never reinforced. Double-sided reflections and pivots toward possibility transform the feared doorway moment into problem-solving.
