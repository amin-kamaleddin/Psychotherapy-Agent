# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful tone with affirmations like 'That's a strong, doable plan' and effective normalization. Affirmations were somewhat generic and didn't explicitly acknowledge Marcus's ambivalence about finger-pricking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Marcus's ambivalence and emotions (e.g., 'stomach-turn' about the boss text). Reflections were often brief and pivoted quickly to problem-solving, missing chances to explore the emotional weight of the diagnosis.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on diabetes self-management throughout, with every exchange advancing concrete action steps. No topic drift; each turn tied back to actionable behavioral goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were co-constructed from Marcus's preferences, with permission-seeking and joint negotiation of check-in timing. The counselor dominated practical problem-solving with solution menus rather than eliciting Marcus's own strategies first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed choices as Marcus's (timing, method, tone, check-in) and offered options without pressure. Some reliance on pre-drafted scripts limited the client's authorship of his own words.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The confidence ruler effectively evoked Marcus's own solution (telling his wife) and strong self-generated change talk. Deeper exploration of intrinsic motivation—values, hopes, and personal reasons for managing his health—was limited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus's contributions directly shaped the plan, and power-sharing was evident in preference questions and tailored drafting. The counselor frequently supplied ready-made solutions before Marcus generated his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation produced commitment language ('I can do that much,' 'whole to-do list') via the confidence ruler and preference questions. Relied heavily on the ruler, with less exploration of Marcus's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk but rarely actively softened it, often moving past hesitation ('might chicken out,' finger-pricking aversion) with planning. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections, e.g., reframing his anxiety as evidence of how much this matters to him.
