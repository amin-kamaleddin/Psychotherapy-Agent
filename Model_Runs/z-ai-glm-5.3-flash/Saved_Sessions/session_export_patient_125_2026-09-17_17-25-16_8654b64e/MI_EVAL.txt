# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Tariq's anger without criticism even when he partially admitted fault. Affirmations were brief and formulaic, lacking deeper individualized expression of positive regard for his worth beyond compliance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening at key moments, accurately capturing Tariq's core fear of one 'no' undoing weeks of progress and his longing for family connection. When Tariq revealed physical anxiety during rehearsal ('my chest got tight'), the counselor moved quickly to scripts rather than deepening emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on the target concern of restoring family contact and preserving compliance until release, with every exchange tied to the phone privilege issue. Deducted for not explicitly summarizing the overarching goal and for directing energy toward administrative tactics rather than the broader behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative flavor with co-drafting the request, shared rehearsal where Tariq directed the roleplay, and checking in after each practice. However, the counselor authored all scripts and strategies while Tariq mainly approved or tweaked, so collaboration operated at the level of choosing rather than creating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered genuine choices (four action options, delivery route, roleplay firmness) with no pressure, persuasion, or coercion. Autonomy support was slightly diluted by direct recommendations ('I'd recommend giving it to your caseworker') and supplying ready-made options rather than inviting Tariq to generate his own paths.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation clearly resided in Tariq—his love for family, pride in staying clean—yet the counselor did little to draw it out, relying on a didactic, script-supplying approach. No evocative questions, confidence exploration, or values clarification; Tariq's self-motivational statements passed without reflection or expansion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited Tariq's choices and checked his readiness, conveying some power sharing, but the session became heavily script-driven with the counselor supplying virtually all content. No explicit acknowledgment that Tariq is the expert on his own coping, his relationships with staff, or what will work in that facility.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tariq generated meaningful spontaneous change talk (staying clean for his mom, holding it together for the birthday call, 'maybe it won't wreck me if it actually happens'), but the counselor rarely reflected or amplified it. No deliberate evocative questions or strategic deepening of commitment language; change talk emerged from roleplay dynamics rather than counselor skill.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk and officer-blame ('he has it out for me,' 'he'll just find another reason') were neither reinforced nor effectively softened; validating the unfairness built rapport but did not pivot toward Tariq's agency. No double-sided reflections, reframes, or gentle redirection linking his compliance history to his power to influence outcomes.
