# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'You're taking concrete, kind steps — that's brave.' Falls short of a 5 because affirmations are somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate dual-sided reflections capturing Elise's ambivalence between belonging and health. Emotional depth around her fear of exclusion ('would my friends even hang out with me the same way?') was not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation throughout, advancing steadily from exploration to a concrete plan with no topic drift. Session progressed through importance rating, first step, confidence rating, scripts, backup plan, and social support.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative — the counselor asks before suggesting and the final plan (coffee, script, gum, backup plan, Maria as ally) is fully co-constructed through dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: 'It's your choice,' options offered as a menu, and all commitments elicited voluntarily via 'Will you...?' questions. Elise's own compassionate framing ('one slip isn't the whole thing gone') was affirmed rather than counselor-imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from Elise, who generated her own reasons, plan, and self-talk. Withheld from 5 because several strategies (fidget, delay/breathing, gum) came from the counselor's menu rather than being evoked from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elise positioned as the expert, with her ideas directly shaping the plan. Some strategies originated from the counselor's suggestions rather than being primarily client-generated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance and confidence rulers that moved Elise from 'maybe a five' to confidence 7, culminating in commitment language ('I want to try this'). Early change talk was not deepened as strategically as it could have been.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about losing belonging was acknowledged and redirected ('How might you keep the sense of belonging if you changed your smoking?'). The counselor sometimes restated sustain talk rather than actively softening it with double-sided reflections.
