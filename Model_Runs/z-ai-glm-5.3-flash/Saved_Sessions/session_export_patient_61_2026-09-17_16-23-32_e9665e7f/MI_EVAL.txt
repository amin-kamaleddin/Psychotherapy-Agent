# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental; Jonathan's skepticism and self-criticism are met with validation. However, affirmations are brief and formulaic, with little explicit recognition of his personal strengths beyond the closing compliments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately track Jonathan's overwhelm, frustration, and ambivalence (e.g., 'exhausted and skeptical'). Reflections tend to be brief and pivot quickly to planning, leaving deeper emotional meaning like fear of repeated failure underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains an unwavering focus on one target behavior—a 10-minute post-dinner walk—with every turn advancing a concrete plan (nights, cue, backup, alarm, spouse script). No drift at any point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans are genuinely co-constructed; Jonathan picks the nights, cue, backup, and spouse script, and notes leaving with 'more of a plan than I walked in here with.' The counselor's dense, pre-formatted menus and recaps occasionally make it feel like Jonathan is selecting from options rather than shaping direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is explicitly supported throughout, and Jonathan's refusal to over-commit ('I'm not making any promises') is accepted without pushback. Autonomy is occasionally undercut by rapid-fire optional suggestions (alarm times, labels, scripts) and pushing for a confidence number.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler is a genuine evocative move and some client motivations emerge ('feel less like crap,' 'not as hopeless'). Much motivational content is supplied by the counselor's summaries and option menus rather than drawn out through open-ended exploration of Jonathan's values and reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jonathan is consistently positioned as the decision-maker and his contributions clearly shape the plan. The menu-driven offers are somewhat pre-scripted, with little explicit acknowledgment of Jonathan's own expertise as the primary resource for change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocation produced real commitment language ('I can try it this week,' 'ask me Friday') and confidence rising from 5–6 to 7. The counselor sometimes supplies ideas before Jonathan generates them, reducing client-driven change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jonathan's considerable sustain talk is never argued with, and some reframing occurs (walk as an experiment). But the counselor repeatedly answers objections directly rather than using double-sided reflections or deliberately pivoting away, keeping focus on reasons against change.
