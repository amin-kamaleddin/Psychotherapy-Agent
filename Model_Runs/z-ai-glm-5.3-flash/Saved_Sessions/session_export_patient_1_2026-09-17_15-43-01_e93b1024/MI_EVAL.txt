# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicates unconditional positive regard and validates the patient's choices without judgment, even when he defends cigar use. Warmth feels genuine, individualized, and consistent throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and capture both content and feeling. However, they sometimes stay surface-level and do not deeply explore the emotional role of the cigars or the patient's defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear focus on the target behavior from the opening and guides the session logically from ambivalence to a concrete one-week plan. Every exchange moves the target forward without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed from the patient's own preferences, and information is shared only after explicit permission. The power dynamic is genuinely equal, with the patient openly negotiating terms.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently supported through permission-seeking, honoring the 'no promises past one week' boundary, and accepting harm reduction over abstinence. The patient's final remarks reflect successful ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions effectively draw motivation from the patient, who generates his own reasons and plan. Some motivation remains external (wife, dentist pressure), and intrinsic values-based reasons are not fully developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within the client, and the plan incorporates his own words, choices, and even his wife's role. The patient explicitly notes the session exceeded his expectations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation elicits substantial change talk ('maybe two or three a week instead of five', confidence of 7), which is then built into a concrete plan. Deeper exploration of the patient's reasons for change is missing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by reflecting neutrally and asking permission before sharing information, which visibly softened resistance. Sustain talk is acknowledged but not always strategically reframed with double-sided reflections.
