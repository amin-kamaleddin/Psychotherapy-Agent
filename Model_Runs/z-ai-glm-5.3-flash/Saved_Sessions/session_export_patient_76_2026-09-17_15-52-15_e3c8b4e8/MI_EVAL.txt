# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, meeting the self-harm disclosure with acceptance rather than alarm. However, some warmth feels formulaic, and the client's exhaustion and effort in showing up were not deeply affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several accurate reflections captured the client's experience, but many responses were bundled multi-question blocks that rushed past emotional content. The hopelessness and the meaning of cutting as her only relief were only superficially explored before pivoting to safety logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing self-harm and building a concrete safety plan, with each element followed through and summarized. Focus was briefly diluted by repeated safety check-ins and resource lists.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The safety plan was genuinely co-constructed, with the client's actual choices filling each slot. The balance still tilted toward the counselor, who generated most options rather than eliciting the client's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and fully respected the client's boundaries and limits. Could be stronger by explicitly affirming her ownership of the change process rather than primarily offering menus of options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions were largely informational rather than evocative, drawing little motivation from the client herself. Her hopelessness and tentative openness were prime material for evoking her own reasons for change but were never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited input and incorporated the client's choices, but heavy use of pre-structured menus and multi-question barrages kept the counselor as agenda-setter. The client remained largely passive rather than contributing her own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerged incidentally through structured safety-plan prompts rather than through strategic evocative questions. Tentative openness about the blades was never deepened into exploration of her own motivations or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was validated without amplification, but there was minimal active pivoting toward change. Statements like 'nothing else has worked' were moved past into logistics rather than resolved through double-sided reflections or strategic redirection.
