# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard from opening to closing, normalizing ambivalence and affirming the client's honesty without any judgment or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures both content and emotional nuance (e.g., 'two truths at once,' 'embarrassment underneath'), with the client repeatedly confirming understanding by elaborating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior throughout, narrowing systematically from problem recognition to a concrete, sequenced action plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative interaction where every plan element builds from the client's own ideas and the counselor asks before suggesting, co-constructing a harm-reduction plan rather than imposing abstinence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively fosters choice and ownership, reframes slips as information, and celebrates client self-direction with no persuasive or controlling language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation and all solutions are drawn entirely from the client via skilled open questions, with zero advice-giving—the counselor only reflects and amplifies the client's own material.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise within the client ('You're the expert on you'), and the client's closing comment that the plan 'feels like mine' confirms genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk through open questions, highlighting the client's desire and confidence language, culminating in a self-generated commitment ('one thing, Tuesday').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged warmly without argument or reinforcement, using double-sided reflections effectively. Minor refinement possible: redirections toward change could be more consistently strategic rather than gentle neutral acknowledgment.
