# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, normalizes ambivalence, and avoids shaming despite the court-referred context. Falls short of 5 because affirmations are brief and functional rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers accurate surface reflections (e.g., 'peer pressure plus being broke'), but frequently jumps from reflection into prescriptive advice. Most notably misses Jordan's poignant disclosure of isolation ('That's kind of sad when I say it like that').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps firm focus on the target behavior and builds progressively toward a concrete goal, Friday plan, and role-play. Falls short of 5 because the structure is counselor-imposed and focuses on tactics more than values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is largely expert-driven, with advice bundles dispensed before exploring Jordan's own ideas. Jordan mostly selects from pre-set options rather than co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers menu choices (role-play vs. commitment, which exit action to use), showing some respect for choice. However, the plan's content is counselor-designed, with no explicit reinforcement that Jordan owns the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Supplies reasons and solutions externally rather than eliciting Jordan's own motivations, with no open evocative questions or confidence/importance ratings. Reads closer to CBT-style skills training than MI evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nearly every response is a bulleted list of prescriptive instructions originating from the counselor. Jordan's own ideas rarely shape the plan despite occasional opportunities to choose among options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged and was channeled into a seven-day commitment plus rehearsal, but this was largely spontaneous rather than skillfully evoked. The counselor rarely uses open questions to deepen Jordan's change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responds to sustain talk with double-sided reflections ('wanting the stuff and wanting to stop can both be true') and pivots to action. Loses a point for extended engagement with peer-pushback logistics, keeping attention on friend pressure.
