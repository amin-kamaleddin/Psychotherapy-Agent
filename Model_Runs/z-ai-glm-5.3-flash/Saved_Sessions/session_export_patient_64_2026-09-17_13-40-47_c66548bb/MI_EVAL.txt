# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, acknowledging Jordan's skepticism and normalizing his ambivalence without disapproval. Affirmations feel somewhat generic ('nice job choosing it') rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Jordan's ambivalence and his grandmother-focused values. The emotional meaning behind 'She'd never know either way, but I'd know' is never explored or reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear and consistent focus on the vaccine decision, moving from ambivalence to a concrete time-bound plan with follow-up. Direction is achieved through prescriptive planning rather than guiding Jordan's own change process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan's preferences shape the plan (pharmacy option, if-then format), but the counselor repeatedly hands down pros/cons lists, menus, scripts, and tips. True co-construction, asking for the client's own ideas first, is largely absent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices, frames the plan as low-pressure, and accepts Jordan's 'no promises' boundary without coercion. Undermined somewhat by rapid-fire pre-packaged options and tips, with no explicit affirmation that ownership rests with Jordan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Identifies and reflects Jordan's own motivation (protecting his grandmother) and elicits some client-generated change talk. Motivation is never expanded through open questions about his values or relationship with his grandparents; the counselor leans on information and planning instead.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites Jordan's input and lets him choose his next steps, but the interaction is dominated by counselor-generated option menus. Jordan contributes, yet the counselor remains the primary ideas-source, keeping the power dynamic uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicits change talk around protecting family and consolidates it via if-then planning. Misses chances to deepen it with evocative questions at moments like 'Maybe I surprise you' or 'I'd know.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Redirects sustain talk toward family-protective motivation and normalizes uncertainty, but never uses double-sided reflections to link Jordan's self-protection doubts to his family values. Sustain talk is tolerated more than strategically softened.
