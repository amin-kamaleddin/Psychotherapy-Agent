# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, including non-judgmental acceptance of the patient's enjoyment of smoking and his relapse-friendly backup plan. Warmth, humor matching, and respect were consistent across the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, capturing both surface content and underlying meaning at every patient turn. The reframe of the past gum failure as a 'how-to gap' rather than a personal failure was an especially empathic moment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, steady focus on reducing daytime smoking while respecting the patient's evening boundary, with each turn advancing the plan. It concluded with a concrete, committed plan: 4mg fruit gum, chew-park technique, ten pieces, starting today.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully collaborative, with advice offered as questions and the plan co-constructed at every step. Every plan element—gum type, daily limit, backup, partner disclosure—originated with or was ratified by the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was reinforced at nearly every turn: 'you get to decide,' 'your call on who knows,' and choices offered rather than imposed. The relapse-friendly plan and confidentiality request were accepted without pushback, powerfully communicating ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited from within rather than supplied, with open questions drawing out the patient's own reasons and self-efficacy. The patient's own shift to commitment language ('I actually kind of want to see if it works') demonstrated mastery-level evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located with the patient through questions like 'what feels realistic for your workday?' Information was offered with permission and framed as options, and the patient's ideas visibly shaped the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked, named, and amplified, with the patient's own motivations reflected back throughout. His change talk clearly escalated over the session, culminating in commitment to start today.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged without judgment and pivoted toward change, with the trial-run framing effectively defusing fear of failure. Occasional double-sided reflections ('both feelings are valid,' 'evenings stay yours') gave brief equal weight to the status quo, slightly sustaining ambivalence.
