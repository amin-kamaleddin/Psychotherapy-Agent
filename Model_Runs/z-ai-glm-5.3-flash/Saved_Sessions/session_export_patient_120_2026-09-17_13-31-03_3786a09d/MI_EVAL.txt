# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Neutral and nonjudgmental with some normalization ('it's normal to grieve'), but affirmations of Sofia's courage, honesty, and maternal responsibility are sparse and formulaic; tone feels clinical rather than warmly accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal — emotional statements like 'the guilt just hits me like a truck' and fear of losing friends receive no reflection before pivoting to risk facts and advice. Most client emotional content goes unexplored and unvalidated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on stopping alcohol during pregnancy with concrete sub-goals (Marisol text, refusal scripts, Friday plan), and Sofia ends with clear momentum. Deducted because focus is achieved through directive task-completion rather than connecting to Sofia's own values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine collaboration exists — tailoring the Marisol text to Sofia's voice and a useful role-play she requested and shaped. But the session is fundamentally counselor-led, with prescribed plans and tasks; Sofia's own expertise about her social world is rarely solicited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A genuine strength — repeated menu-based choices ('Which feels most helpful right now?', 'Which feels most like you?') and reinforcement of Sofia's ownership ('I'm choosing'). Falls short because constant menu-driven advice implicitly positions the counselor as the source of solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little motivation is drawn from within the client — no open questions about Sofia's reasons, hopes, confidence, or values-discrepancy. Her powerful change talk ('I don't wanna let my kid down') surfaces on its own and is never elicited, reflected, or deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly assumes the expert role, delivering prescriptive advice lists even when Sofia expresses ambivalence ('that's not that simple'). Sofia's ideas are rarely explored and the counselor's expertise dominates the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged largely from the client via skill-building and role-play, and the counselor responded well to the 'I'm choosing' moment. However, there is little deliberate evocation, no commitment-strengthening questions, and change talk is never deepened or reflected.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A strength in not reinforcing sustain talk — fears about losing friends are reframed ('You can keep your friends without drinking'). But sustain talk is never strategically softened with double-sided reflections pairing it against Sofia's strong maternal motivation, and some responses invite further sustain talk.
