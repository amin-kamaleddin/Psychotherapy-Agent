# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, nonjudgmental regard ("no judgment, either way"; "I believe you can do this, at exactly the pace you choose"). No labeling, disapproval, or lecture-like tone; validates the patient's ambivalence rather than judging it.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including the accurate double-sided reflection of ambivalence. However, one turn nearly mirrors the patient's speech verbatim (retelling the father's story), reading as parroting rather than a deepened reflection; the emotional layer of fear from the father's decline is never explicitly named.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on cholesterol management (medication adherence and activity) from start to finish; every question ties back to the target behavior, ending in a concrete, time-bound follow-up plan.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Work is framed as joint ("figure out together whatever's gotten in the way"), and the follow-up plan honors the patient's preference. However, the sequence is somewhat counselor-steered (readiness ruler, father question), and there is no explicit agenda negotiation at the start.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced ("you get to decide the pace"; "at exactly the pace you choose"). No directives or pressure; the patient explicitly notes the decision feels like his own.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within the client — his retirement goals, his father's regret, and what feels manageable are all articulated by the patient himself and then amplified by the counselor rather than supplied.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in the client ("You're the expert on what would make these changes doable"). The plan (walking first, medication, talking with his wife) is entirely shaped by the patient; he confirms effective power sharing ("thanks for not just telling me what to do").

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked via open questions, exploration of the father's story, and a readiness ruler; the counselor deeply reflects the patient's own commitment language. Culminates in strong change talk: "I'll start the medicine... Maybe I start this week."

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled well via double-sided reflection rather than argument, and reframed into values-based motivation. However, sustain talk is mostly absorbed rather than actively redirected, and the minimization is never explicitly reframed as a strength (e.g., confidence).

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** 4.7/5
