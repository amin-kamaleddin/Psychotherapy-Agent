# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with no judgment of the patient's hesitancy or reliance on an online blog. Strong affirmations protected her self-worth, notably reframing her questioning as thorough parenting when she felt foolish.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were consistently accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both content and underlying feelings (protectiveness, fear, nervousness). Complex, double-sided reflections went well beyond parroting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every turn maintained focus on the vaccination decision and progressed logically from identifying trustworthy information through to booking the dose and planning for the husband. No topic drift; transitional questions guided momentum while keeping the patient's agenda central.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Permission was sought before providing information, and the session structure was co-constructed around the patient's stated preferences. Her ideas directly shaped the session's course, exemplifying equal-partner collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was repeatedly reinforced — her preference not to be told what to do was validated, explicit choices were offered, and no rush or pressure was applied. The final decision was clearly attributed entirely to her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from the client through evocative questions about trust, meaning, and her own read of the data. Her own reasons (protectiveness, her daughter's vulnerability) became the engine of change, and the shift was explicitly credited to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing — the patient chose discussion order, requested take-home materials, and was handed the expert role regarding her husband. Data-delivery segments were somewhat clinician-led, and the clinician could more explicitly name her as the expert on her family's context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was evoked repeatedly through open questions and reinforced when it appeared, producing a clear shift to commitment language. Reinforcement was solid, though a few opportunities to deepen her own verbalized motivation were passed over in favor of information delivery.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged and paired with change talk in double-sided reflections without argument. However, the CDC data delivery and some statements about vaccine protection leaned persuasive rather than eliciting the patient's own resolution of her doubts.
