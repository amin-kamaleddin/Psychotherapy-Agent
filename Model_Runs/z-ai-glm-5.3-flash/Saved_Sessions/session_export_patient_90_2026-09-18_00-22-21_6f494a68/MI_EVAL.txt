# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard is explicit and sustained throughout, with validation of both sides of ambivalence, specific affirmations, and zero judgment or pressure. The client directly confirms feeling regarded: 'you didn't just hand me a list and shove me out the door.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Deep, accurate reflective listening that captures meaning beneath the client's words, e.g., the reframe 'you're guarding what little room you have left,' which the client confirms landed. Reflections consistently touch the emotional core: fear, attachment to his dog, and fairness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician keeps focus on what the client wants to change without pulling toward an agenda, guiding gently from overwhelm to a concrete two-week plan the client owned.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership: 'You know your home, your routines, and your body better than anyone,' and 'I'd honestly like to figure that out together.' Every plan element came from the client, and the clinician repeatedly emphasized choice ('entirely your call,' 'no wrong answer').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently honored — tracking method and follow-up plan are explicitly left to the client, and change is framed 'on your terms.' The clinician avoids any instruction, pressure, or correction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician draws out the client's own motivations and solutions ('what do you think could actually work?'), yielding client-generated change talk and self-motivational statements like 'I can do two weeks.' The client's own 'why' — sleeping through the night — anchors the plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician works with rather than on the client, crediting all ideas to him and inviting shared problem-solving about the dog, wife's involvement, and follow-up. The client's closing remarks ('not another lecture... an actual plan') verify felt collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open-ended questions skillfully evoke desire ('I do want to breathe easier'), ability ('those don't sound impossible'), and commitment ('I've got this'). The clinician reinforces change talk with affirmations and reflections rather than evoking sustain talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ambivalence is met with non-judgmental validation and reframing ('Of course you hit a wall there' regarding the dog) rather than arguing, defusing resistance while keeping the client engaged.
