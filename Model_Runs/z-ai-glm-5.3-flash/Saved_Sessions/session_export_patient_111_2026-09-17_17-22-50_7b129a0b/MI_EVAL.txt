# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicated respect and unconditional positive regard despite Richard's defensiveness, never arguing or judging, and framing slips as 'information, not failure.' Warmth was steady but somewhat functional and repetitive rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured surface content but bundled multiple statements together and missed the emotional core — Richard's tears about his wife and his fear of losing her were acknowledged but not deeply reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking, progressing logically from scaling to a concrete, time-bound experiment (Monday, shed + timer + ashtray removal) with a scheduled follow-up. Direction was somewhat technique-driven, and the plan was not explicitly tied back to Richard's core value of his marriage.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed decisions as joint choices and co-constructed the plan around Richard's own ideas (shed, 10-minute delay, his confidentiality preferences). Occasionally delivered prescriptive strategy menus rather than drawing out Richard's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Richard's ownership ('you're in control of this,' 'it's reversible'), honored his check-in format, timing, and confidentiality preferences ('I won't share details with your wife unless you want me to'). Avoided coercion entirely, and Richard's growing self-direction reflects this strong support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within Richard through open questions and scaling, eliciting genuine client-generated change talk ('if skipping one cigarette makes her stop crying... that'd be worth more than the smoking ever was'). Evocation was somewhat tool-driven, and the relational motivation was not developed into the change plan itself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Richard as the decision-maker, and his contributions (no fanfare, not telling his wife, 7 AM logistics) clearly shaped and were honored in the session. At times drifted toward list-like advice-giving rather than fully eliciting Richard's own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked Richard's own commitment ('maybe I could skip the one with my morning coffee'), his relational motivation, and a concrete plan, reinforced with affirmations. Relied on structured tools (scaling, experiment framing) and did not always amplify the deeper change talk about his marriage.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rolled with resistance without arguing, neutrally reflected ambivalence, and pivoted to forward-looking questions ('What would need to happen to move your importance rating?'). Occasionally repeated sustain talk verbatim without double-sided reflections pairing it with Richard's relational motivation.
