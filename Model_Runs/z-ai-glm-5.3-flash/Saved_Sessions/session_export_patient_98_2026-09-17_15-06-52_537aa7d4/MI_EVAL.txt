# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations ('clear and honest — not whiny'). Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and lacked explicit normalization of relapse/setbacks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent, accurate reflections of frustration, ambivalence, and the loss of evening walks with his wife. Emotional depth was sometimes skipped—shame about 'complaining too much' and fear of disappointing his wife deserved fuller exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior throughout, building from frustrations to a specific, testable, time-limited plan. Every response tied back to the behavioral goal without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine equal partnership—the note was co-constructed from Mateo's own language and the stop rule was chosen by him. Occasionally the counselor generated scripts and options first, with the client refining rather than originating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed everything as the client's choice, with repeated choice-offering and a time-limited trial frame that reduced pressure. His ownership was evident ('I'll try it,' 'I've actually got a plan here').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation via goal elicitation, confidence rulers used twice, and rehearsal. Could be deeper with more exploration of the client's underlying values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Power sharing was genuine—client contributions materially shaped the final plan and he was repeatedly asked which option felt safest or most helpful. Fell short because the counselor sometimes supplied scripts and stop rules himself rather than drawing them from the client first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Active change talk cultivation through eliciting goals, double confidence rulers, and aloud rehearsal, producing clear change talk ('maybe it's worth a shot,' confidence 6). Could deepen with open questions about why change matters to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never amplified; double-sided reflections and the 'just testing it out' trial frame softened fear of failure. Some sustain talk (feeling like 'complaining too much') was not directly validated and reworked.
