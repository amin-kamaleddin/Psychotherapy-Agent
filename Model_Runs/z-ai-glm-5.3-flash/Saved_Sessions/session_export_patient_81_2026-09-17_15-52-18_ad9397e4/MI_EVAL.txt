# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with affirmations like 'That's a strong, brave move' even when the client is defensive. Warmth is somewhat formulaic and lacks explicit normalization of his exposure anxiety and fear of losing community.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures the core ambivalence between the scene's importance and money concerns. The deeper emotional layer—fear of becoming 'separate' and quiet shame about weekday use—is acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on reducing meth-related spending, progressing logically toward a concrete plan. However, weekday use—the highest-risk behavior—is allowed to be deferred across several turns rather than gently kept on the table earlier.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine equal partnership throughout: options are co-constructed, the client freely selects and modifies, and he designs his own experiment ($150 prepaid, rent transfer, mutual text to Marcus). The final plan is authentically co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently reinforced—advice is framed as options the client can accept, reject, or tweak, and his rejections (e.g., the walk, the one-night cap) are honored without pushback. The plan is framed as wise and reversible.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and the confidence ruler effectively drew client-generated reasoning and insight ('maybe things crept further than I noticed'). Evocation is not continuous—the counselor sometimes proposes solution lists rather than eliciting ideas, and emotionally significant disclosures are not mined for motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lukas is treated as the expert and clearly shaped the session, designing his own experiment. At times the counselor supplies lists of solutions before the client generates ideas, slipping slightly into a suggestions-first mode.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully elicited ('Saying that sounds a bit worse than I thought') and the confidence ruler drew a self-identified weak spot. However, significant change talk moments (e.g., abandoning script practice—'Maybe that says something') are not deepened further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without arguing—community values are reflected while pivoting to workable options, and rejected suggestions are respected. Weekday use is repeatedly deferred, leaving sustain talk around it unaddressed.
