# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, warm, and non-judgmental tone throughout, with repeated affirmations of Ingrid's caution ('that's a careful, safe move'). However, affirmations became formulaic and repetitive, limiting individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was notably sparse, with almost no reflections of feelings; emotional cues like Ingrid's relief and ambivalence went unreflected. No complex reflections were used — interactions were largely transactional Q&A.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus: the interaction concern was answered precisely, followed by structured planning of washout, warning signs, checklist, and a confirmed Thursday 2pm phone follow-up. Every exchange moved toward safe medication management without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The checklist was co-constructed based on Ingrid's requests, with scheduling negotiated around her constraints. Power tilted expert-driven on clinical content, with didactic information delivery and no checking of what Ingrid already knew.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: the opening explicitly framed support for client choice, and decisions were consistently offered as options (now vs. schedule, phone/video/in-person, stop St John's Wort now vs. Thursday). No pressuring language; Ingrid owned every decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor informed and organized rather than drawing out the client's own motivations, reasons, or confidence. No open evocative questions were asked, and Ingrid's spontaneously offered change talk ('better to be safe than sorry') was never developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ingrid's input substantially shaped the session — her requests determined the checklist content, and the counselor repeatedly invited additions and preferences. Held at 4 because the counselor retained the expert role without inviting Ingrid's own medication knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some space for change talk was created (safety commitments, decision to stop St John's Wort, commitment to Thursday consultation), but opportunities to deepen it were missed. No commitment-strengthening or importance/confidence explorations were used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk (time pressure) was handled well by offering flexible alternatives (phone format, 20 minutes). The St John's Wort redirect was decent, but the counselor never acknowledged the underlying reason for use (self-treating low mood).
