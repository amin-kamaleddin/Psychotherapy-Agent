# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations tied to Tariq's efforts. However, warmth felt somewhat formulaic, and affirmations focused on task performance rather than deeper unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening that accurately captured Tariq's emotions, especially the family-connection motive. Reflections remained somewhat repetitive and rarely deepened beneath the initial anger to explore underlying anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session focused and logical, progressing from venting to role-play to a written request. Focus centered on a concrete sub-problem rather than the broader behavioral goals of emotion regulation and compliance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration appeared in the role-plays, but power-sharing was uneven as the counselor designed the plan, scripts, and letter largely unilaterally. A stronger approach would invite Tariq to co-author wording and strategy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered menus of options early, supporting choice, but the session's middle became directive with pre-written scripts and templates handed over. Tariq's pushback was accommodated, yet the default mode was advice-giving rather than reinforcing ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tariq self-generated meaningful change talk (his mother's struggles, protecting his release), but the counselor did little to evoke this systematically. Motivation was often bypassed in favor of supplying techniques.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed responsiveness by dropping the apology line and shifting to a written request per Tariq's input. However, the counselor frequently assumed the expert role, supplying scripts and step-by-step advice rather than drawing solutions from Tariq.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged mostly from the client and was reflected, but not deliberately evoked or deepened. Open questions about Tariq's own reasons, hopes for release, or vision of success were absent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged Tariq's blame and sustain talk without reinforcing or arguing against it, pivoting toward skillful coping. Redirects were largely procedural rather than strategic MI softening, with no double-sided reflections linking sustain talk to change goals.
