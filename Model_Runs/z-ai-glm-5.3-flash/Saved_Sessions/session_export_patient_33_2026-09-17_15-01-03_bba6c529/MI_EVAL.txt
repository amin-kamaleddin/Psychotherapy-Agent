# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental, explicitly disarming Diego's fear of lecturing and validating harm reduction and his disclosure as brave. Affirmations, however, remain brief and generic rather than personalized to his strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and capture Diego's perspective on control, safety goals, and his boundaries. Mostly surface-level summaries; deeper emotional reflections on months of avoidance, fear of judgment, and disclosure anxiety were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on concrete harm-reduction goals, closing with Diego articulating a specific plan. Direction is sometimes reactive rather than strategic—rarely proactively summarizing progress or setting up next steps and barriers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine joint work, especially the role-play where Diego scripted his own responses and the counselor took the housemate's part. Some information (SSP description, harm-reduction list) was delivered in didactic blocks rather than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary throughout—repeated explicit choice points about pace, topic, and content, with Diego's input genuinely shaping the session. His 'not quitting' boundary was accepted fully without pressure or persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Diego's own goals and reasons, with his readiness ('10 out of 10') emerging from within rather than persuasion. Missed opportunities to systematically build on change talk—exploring confidence, setbacks, or longer-term vision.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego's expertise and choices genuinely shaped the session, including selecting the opening script and first steps. The counselor still supplied most of the concrete content (scripts, resource lists) rather than drawing more from Diego.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions elicited strong commitments ('I'll actually do it this week,' 'Ten out of ten') and a concrete plan. Change talk was not deepened or reinforced with targeted follow-ups or strategic summaries of commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally without amplification, and the 'not quitting' boundary was respected without arguing. No double-sided reflections were used to actively resolve ambivalence, and treatment options were touched despite being excluded, albeit gently.
