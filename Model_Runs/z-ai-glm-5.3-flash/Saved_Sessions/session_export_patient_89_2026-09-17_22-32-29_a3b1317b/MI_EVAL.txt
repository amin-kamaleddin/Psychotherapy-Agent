# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard with no judgment about weight or past failures, and affirms the client genuinely ('that excitement is worth paying attention to'). Client explicitly feels accepted, saying 'thanks for not giving up on me.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are consistently accurate and transformative, reframing fear into motivation and noticing subtle affect. Client responses ('that's kind of been my problem, hasn't it?') show he feels deeply understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on physical activity from opening question to concrete, measurable commitments (walk tomorrow, dancing research, check-in in two weeks). Each turn advances progressively toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opens with 'work together,' uses partnership language throughout, and co-constructs the plan entirely from the client's own ideas. Even suggestions are offered only after seeking permission.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces client choice and ownership ('you decide what pace makes sense,' 'this plan is yours'), framing the follow-up call as an option rather than a directive. Client internalizes this: 'it's my plan, my call.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivations come from the client — his kids, father's heart attack, love of walking and dancing — with expertly evocative questions and no advice-giving or persuasion. The client articulates his own theory of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the client as the expert from the first sentence and sustains it throughout; the client's contributions demonstrably shape the session. Client's closing insight confirms expertise was located within himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open evocative questions elicit the client's own motivations, and every change statement is strategically amplified. Change talk escalates from 'maybe a short walk' to a strong commitment: 'I'll make this work.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids reinforcing barriers and effectively reframes 'all-or-nothing' thinking and 'what's the point' into insight and motivation. A couple of exchanges linger on time barriers at moderate length before redirecting toward change.
