# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms the patient's autonomy and expertise, explicitly valuing ambivalence as legitimate. Statements like 'I don't hear pathetic; I hear someone carrying this alone and coming anyway' convey deep unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both surface content and underlying emotions, including shame, self-punishment, and loneliness. The clinician repeatedly names and validates the patient's internal conflicts with precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is skillfully steered from hopelessness toward concrete, patient-generated steps (early shower timing, looking up NA meetings, writing an affirmation). Each pivot builds logically on the patient's own disclosures without imposing the clinician's agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently defers to the patient's expertise ('you know your evenings better than anyone') and builds exclusively on patient-identified solutions. Every plan element originates from the patient, with the clinician serving as guide rather than director.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly honored: 'whatever you decide is completely yours,' 'yours to keep or drop, no promises attached.' The clinician carefully avoids pressure, even reframing the patient's self-imposed commitments to remove failure risk.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions effectively draw out change talk about meaning, accountability, and self-worth, eliciting the pivotal insight that 'changing would matter more than hating myself.' The clinician evokes the patient's own reasons and capacity rather than supplying them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operates as genuine collaboration, with the clinician actively soliciting the patient's ideas at every turn and co-constructing the final two-part plan. The patient's closing statement — 'it's mine. I didn't walk in here with anything, and now I've got two things' — confirms the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician strategically develops nascent change talk into strengthened commitment, moving from 'maybe showing up is something' to a self-authored coping sentence and concrete plan. Patient-generated change talk escalates naturally across the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (self-punishment, 'things never last with me,' fear of failure) is met with double-sided reflections that honor it while amplifying the contrary voice. The clinician never argues against resistance, instead letting the patient's own counters — 'changing would matter more' — do the work.
