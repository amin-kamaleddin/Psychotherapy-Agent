# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, including affirmation of the client's smoking milestone and full respect for his pride and concerns about his wife 'policing' him.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured key emotions, such as wanting teamwork rather than policing. However, some nuances, like his worry about being 'annoying' in front of his son, could have been reflected more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus on party drinking from early on, moving logically from exploration to rulers, strategies, plan details, and rehearsal, culminating in a concrete plan tied to an upcoming event. Every question advanced the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with consent-seeking before brainstorming, role-play, and rehearsal, and all plan elements co-constructed with the client's contributions shaping the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client selected his own drink limit, signal, and decline line, reinforcing ownership. The strategy and decline-line lists were somewhat prescriptively presented rather than eliciting the client's own ideas first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from the client's own reasons, history of quitting smoking, wording of scripts, and rising confidence (7 to 8). The counselor elicited rather than instilled motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client's expertise drove the session: he chose the number, signal, decline line, and wife script, with the counselor positioning him as the source of solutions by linking his smoking-cessation playbook.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of rulers, open questions, and linking past success evoked strong commitment talk, including 'Three drinks max feels right' and testing the plan at his sister's birthday.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was mild and not reinforced, with the counselor steering toward change. However, it was not actively reframed or addressed with double-sided reflections.
