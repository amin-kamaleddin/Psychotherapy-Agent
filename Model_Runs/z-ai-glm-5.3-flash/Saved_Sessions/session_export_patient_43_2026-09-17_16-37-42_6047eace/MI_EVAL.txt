# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, accepting even potential failure ('If you cave, that's fine') and honoring confidentiality. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and lacked individualized acknowledgment of Eric's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Eric's ambivalence and perspective, including mixed signals from his wife. Reflections were sometimes followed too quickly by questions or offers, leaving less room to explore the emotional layer beneath the minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained focus on smoking and moved toward a concrete next step with agenda choice at the start. Frequent menu-offers created some drift, and the path forward was driven more by counselor suggestions than a values-based collaborative goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed from Eric's preferences (coffee, gum, timer), with the counselor positioned as a resource rather than an authority. The final plan reflected Eric's own wording, rules, and conditions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly reinforced throughout ('you're in charge', 'I won't nag you', accepting no promises). The counselor explicitly honored Eric's conditions, including no messages and confidentiality with his wife.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Eric's own motivations via rulers and open questions, eliciting spontaneous reflections. Sometimes answered his questions with psychoeducation instead of eliciting his own theories and reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine power sharing was evident: 'you're in charge', 'you set the rules', and Eric's contributions directly shaped the plan. The counselor repeatedly returned decision points to Eric, including how to follow up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked change talk through rulers and a behavioral experiment, moving Eric from importance 2 to a committed first step. Missed opportunities to deepen change talk when Eric showed curiosity about delaying.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged with neutral, double-sided reflections rather than argument, avoiding reinforcement of minimization. Reflections sometimes restated sustain talk at length without an intentional shift toward developing discrepancy.
