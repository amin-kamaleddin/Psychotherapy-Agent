# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard from the opening and maintained it throughout, explicitly honoring Linda's externally motivated entry and her ambivalence without judgment. Affirmed her honesty about ambivalence as a strength and personalized warmth rather than formulaic responses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Made consistently accurate reflections capturing both content and emotional nuance, such as reframing smoking as 'your break, your friend almost' and reflecting the generational pattern fear. Reflections were varied and deepened the conversation rather than repeating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, progressive focus on smoking while moving purposefully from engagement through eliciting change talk to a concrete first step and follow-up. Every reflection and question tied back to the target behavior with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned between equals—opening with what Linda found useful, soliciting her hypotheses, and co-constructing the plan entirely from her ideas. Even the check-in was framed as optional and defined by her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively and repeatedly reinforced Linda's autonomy ('that's completely your call,' 'no promises attached') and validated the narrow scope she set for herself. Choice points were genuine, with Linda defining limits, selecting the experiment, and choosing the follow-up day.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from within the client rather than supplying it, most powerfully by evoking Linda's personal reasons beyond her children (breathing, money, hypocrisy) after she noted 'Nobody ever asks me that.' All change talk and the plan originated from Linda.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned Linda as the expert ('you know what this road looks like from the inside') and let her shape the session direction. The plan—target, strategies, terms, and follow-up—was entirely client-generated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully moved Linda from 'no plans to quit' to self-generated reasons, strengths, and solution ideas, with each change talk moment reflected and amplified. The session culminated in client-generated commitment language and her first-ever image of herself not smoking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk, accepting it without judgment and pivoting back to what mattered, and Linda's sustain talk visibly decreased. However, pivots relied on 'at the same time' constructions rather than strategic double-sided reflections, and some sustain statements (e.g., 'I still want that drive-home smoke') passed without a softening response.
