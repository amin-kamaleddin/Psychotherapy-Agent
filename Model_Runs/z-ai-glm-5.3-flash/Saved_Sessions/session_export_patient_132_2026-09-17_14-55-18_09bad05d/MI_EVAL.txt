# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveys warmth and respect (e.g., 'That makes sense,' 'Baby steps are wise') without judgment of her dieting history. However, affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic, missing opportunities for deeper, more individualized recognition of her courage and self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture her ambivalence and desire for sustainability (e.g., 'the dieting cycle sounds exhausting and demoralizing'). Emotional depth remains somewhat surface-level — the shame behind her 'constant failure feeling' and her fear of the scale are not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains an exceptionally clear focus on a specific target behavior — reducing food guilt via one mindful, unlabeled dinner — carried through to concrete planning. Every exchange advances the goal, including skillfully folding her weight-loss ambivalence into the experiment frame.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed and the counselor repeatedly checks preferences. That said, offering menus of pre-formatted experiments and scripts at times drives the structure rather than letting the client's own ideas generate the intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced ('I'll follow your lead,' 'It's totally okay to keep going to the gym'), and the client explicitly notes the plan 'doesn't feel punishing.' No pressure or persuasion occurs anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good evocative questions were asked and the client generated hope and commitment language. However, the session relies heavily on counselor-supplied options and scripts rather than deeply exploring and amplifying the client's own reasons, values, and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the decision-maker, with her choices (dinner, routine-plus-one-meal, involving Sara) substantially shaping the plan. Limitation: the counselor often presents pre-formulated menus rather than truly open co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was elicited indirectly and a commitment emerged ('Wednesday dinner. I can commit to that'). But opportunities to deepen her own change statements (e.g., why sustainability matters, what makes this different from past attempts) were largely missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally with a good reframe about goals coexisting as information-gathering. However, her fear of losing control was accommodated rather than gently explored, and no double-sided reflections were used to resolve ambivalence.
