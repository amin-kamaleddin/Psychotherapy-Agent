# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, affirming the client's strengths and protective choices without any disapproval of drinking or smoking. Falls short of a 5 because the warmth feels brief and formulaic, with little explicit normalization of slips or struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate early reflections (e.g., 'That sounds exhausting'), but reflections are often superficial and quickly pivot to questions or advice. Deeper emotional content—distrust of her mother, isolation, and her insightful 'it all connects' observation—is largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains strong focus on childcare reliability and drinking, progressing logically to concrete steps and ending with specific commitments and follow-up. Falls short of a 5 because direction is sometimes achieved by fast-tracking to solutions, and the smoking concern the client raised was dropped without collaborative prioritization.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some choices and Daniela's input shapes the plan, but the counselor supplies scripts, agreements, and coping plans in long advice monologues. Daniela mostly selects from the counselor's menu rather than co-creating solutions, though her own barter idea goes unexploited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Provides choice menus and lets Daniela pick her own steps, but steers strongly toward pre-selected solutions and repeatedly offers to draft texts and plans. Little explicit emphasis on Daniela's ownership, and permission is not sought before dense advice blocks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dominant mode is providing solutions rather than eliciting Daniela's own motivation; scaling responses are shallow with no follow-up on why importance is high or how confidence could rise. Most change talk is volunteered by the client, and rapid pivots to scripts foreclose exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites Daniela's preferences at several points and her choices shape the plan, but the counselor frequently assumes the expert role, generating all content with limited curiosity about Daniela's own ideas. Her resourcefulness in proposing the meal exchange is not highlighted as expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses evocative questions and scaling that successfully elicited change talk (importance 9, 'I know I can do it some'), but does not deepen it. Missed opportunities include exploring what made successful nights work and what would move confidence from 5 to 6.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and offers brief validating reframes, but the handling is mostly passive. Substantial barriers and doubts are not addressed with double-sided reflections or linked to her demonstrated capacity for change; change language is tacked on rather than helping her resolve ambivalence.
