# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with consistent affirmations like 'That's excellent — you did the work' and 'This is a brave, specific plan.' Praise is somewhat generic and outcome-focused, slightly limiting deeper unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ('That sounds really heavy and stressful') are present, but emotionally rich disclosures like Hana's tears of relief are met with logistics and next steps rather than deepened reflection. Empathic understanding is inconsistent and shallow relative to the material offered.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on approaching the certification exam; every response advances the graded exposure plan from 10-minute bursts to a 25-question timed practice with follow-up checkpoints. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana's choices (burst length, coping phrase, schedule) shape the plan and were honored, but the default mode is prescriptive numbered plans delivered rapidly. Power-sharing occurs at decision points rather than continuously.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice-offering ('pick or tweak one,' 'guide the session live or check in afterward?') supports Hana's ownership. Falls short of 5 because advice is often pre-packaged, making choices feel like selecting from the counselor's menu.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some drawing on Hana's internal resources ('You're doing the hardest part — starting'), but motivation is largely scaffolded by techniques and plans. Evocative material like her work presentation success appears spontaneously and is not deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices are offered and Hana's input is respected, but the overall stance leans expert-driven, with plans arriving as numbered prescriptions before her ideas are explored. Her own expertise is incorporated but not deeply leveraged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana spontaneously produces strong change talk ('I want that weight off my shoulders more,' 'maybe I'm really doing this'), but the psychiatrist rarely deliberately evokes or deepens it. Questions tend to be logistical rather than motivational.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('part of me is already predicting I'll hit snooze,' 'that voice is loud') is met with reassurance and practical tips rather than double-sided reflections or strategic reframing. It avoids reinforcing sustain talk but lacks active softening.
