# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with strength-based reframing of self-labels and unconditional acceptance of outcomes. Limited to one explicit affirmation, with more frequent and varied affirmations needed, especially around moments of vulnerability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, emotionally attuned reflections tracking both content and feeling, explicitly confirmed by the patient. The clinician lets vulnerable moments be processed rather than rushing to fix.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus and coherent arc from ambivalence to a concrete, accountable plan. The middle portion circles somewhat before landing, slightly loosening directional momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructed session in which the patient proposed each element of the plan and visibly reshaped its direction. Equal power balance with jointly negotiated accountability.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy affirmed repeatedly with the 'have to' vs. 'choose to' framing and explicit permission to proceed at the patient's own pace. No advice-giving, persuasion, or coercion anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All substantive insights and motivation emerged from the patient's own words, prompted by skillful open questions. The clinician never supplied reasons for change or their own plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located within the client ('You'd know better than I would'), and the entire plan originated from the patient's own reasoning. Patient confirms ownership: 'this actually feels like mine.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions targeting prior success and the pivotal 'when I have to' vs. 'when I choose to' question evoked sustained self-generated change and commitment language. The plan was driven entirely by the client's own ideas.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided reinforcing sustain talk, holding ambivalence without amplifying the avoidant side. Strategy relied mostly on reflecting and redirecting rather than actively reframing defeat statements like 'my brain's lawyer wins a lot.'
