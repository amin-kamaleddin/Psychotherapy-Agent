# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, unconditional regard throughout, with genuine affirmations ('that's not nothing, Erik') and acceptance of even Erik's less flattering impulses (the spite-candy-bar image). No judgment or conditional regard appears anywhere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate, capturing Erik's ambivalence, internal logic, and subtle distinctions (e.g., support vs. being watched). Deepens understanding with well-timed exploratory questions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Crystal-clear focus on exercise and diet targets with a purposeful arc from exploring the comparison barrier to concrete plans. Smooth, well-timed transitions with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely egalitarian — the psychiatrist asks rather than tells, and all plans (gym anchors, Tomás ask, snack strategy) are self-authored by Erik with the clinician offering only curiosity and structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan originates from Erik's own ideas and is framed as his choice; no prescribing or persuasion. Erik explicitly articulates ownership: 'when she's just alongside me, it feels like mine.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is systematically drawn from Erik's own experience — back-pain victory, empty-gym pride, hockey accountability precedent. By session's end he has generated three concrete commitments entirely from his own reasoning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik is consistently positioned as the expert and his contributions drive the session. Docked slightly because a few key frames ('gift to yourself,' 'support vs. being watched') were clinician-generated rather than emerging from Erik.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions about past successes and strengths consistently elicit concrete commitment language ('I'll do it tonight, probably,' 'that feels small enough to actually stick'). Change talk is then amplified through reflective affirmation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and skillfully reframed, with barriers met by drawing out Erik's own solutions. Slightly below top marks because the comparison/sustain-talk theme occupied several early exchanges before pivoting.
