# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance maintained throughout, with validation of Oliver's ambivalence and affirmations of his kindness. However, affirmations risk feeling formulaic and could be more individualized to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing Oliver's stuckness, guilt, and desire for independence. Reflections became thinner and more transactional once the session shifted to scripting and rehearsal, leaving emotional undercurrents under-explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear and consistent focus on the boundary-setting goal, with every exchange moving toward action—clarifying outcomes, scripting, role-play, and a concrete plan. No topic drift; even humor was folded into the boundary work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative, with permission-seeking, options, and joint role-play incorporating Oliver's edits. Falls short because most scripts and tactics were delivered by the counselor rather than co-authored from Oliver's own phrasing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized Oliver's choice and ownership, and he genuinely drove the agenda. However, directive tips and provided scripts at times edged toward prescription rather than reinforcing his autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some early evocation of Oliver's motivation and reasons, but the bulk of session content consisted of counselor-supplied scripts and contingency replies. Client-generated language and solutions were present but inconsistently expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed expertise as residing with Oliver, who shaped the session's focus through choices about rehearsal and content. Short of a 5 because the counselor supplied most scripts and tactics prescriptively rather than co-creating them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforced Oliver's commitment language and used rehearsal to strengthen determination ('I feel more ready than I have all month'). Limited by the counselor introducing many specific scripts rather than drawing them primarily from Oliver.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely avoided dwelling on sustain talk, acknowledging guilt briefly before pivoting to outcome-focused questions and practical steps. Effectively minimized negotiation space without amplifying reasons against change.
