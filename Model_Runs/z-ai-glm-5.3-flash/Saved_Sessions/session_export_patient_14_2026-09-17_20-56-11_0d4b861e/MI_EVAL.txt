# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained respect and non-judgment toward a resistant, irritable client, with effective affirmations and normalization of the client's efficiency-driven framing. Fell short of full marks only because warmth was somewhat formulaic and setbacks weren't normalized in advance beyond a single 'no judgment either way.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening throughout, consistently capturing the client's ambivalence, values, and content. Reflections stayed mostly cognitive and strategic, missing opportunities to explore the emotional layer beneath the client's irritability and his admission that the issue had been 'hanging over' him.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus in a time-pressured session, with every exchange moving progressively from exploring tension to a concrete, self-owned commitment. Questions consistently served forward momentum without drifting from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative framing ('a puzzle worth solving together'), with every plan element offered as a question open to modification and client logistics adjustments flexibly accepted. The session concluded with a co-constructed, client-owned plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, explicit support of client choice—no obligation framing, honoring boundaries ('lunches only, no regime'), and repeatedly locating ownership of the change with the client. The closing emphasized his ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client rather than supplied—no lecturing on diet or health risks, with questions eliciting his own reasons and solutions. The client's shift from resistance to voluntary rebooking demonstrates evocation at a high level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly and repeatedly located with the client, and every element of the plan (Sunday prep, calendar entry, combined appointments) came from his own contributions. Client input genuinely shaped the session's direction—the hallmark of strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, systematic evocation and amplification of change talk, from eliciting a small realistic change to explicitly naming commitment ('that 'maybe' about booking the next one yourself? I heard it'). The client's language grew steadily from 'it's a start, I suppose' to 'I'll give it three weeks' and voluntary intent to rebook.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification, validated briefly, then pivoted toward change—e.g., reframing 'lunch is the weak spot' as already time-free. Sustain talk progressively diminished across the session as the client moved toward commitment.
