# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental, honoring the client's no-lecture request and framing slips as information rather than failures. Affirmations were somewhat sparse and formulaic, lacking individualized acknowledgment of her strengths (honesty, motivation rooted in her daughter).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captured her perspective, including her core frustration at being told rather than heard. Reflections stayed at a summary level and did not touch the emotional core — fear of failure, shame, and the weight of the failed cold-turkey attempt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking cessation and reached a concrete, time-limited plan (one-week experiment, gum, if-then plan, Monday check-in). Momentum came partly from counselor-driven structure rather than the client's own, and logistics consumed significant session space.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed — the client chose the experiment, the coffee substitute, the Monday start, and the check-in method. Early bulleted menus positioned the counselor as idea generator, with the client tweaking a largely counselor-designed structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: repeated permission-seeking, offered choices, honored boundary requests, and framed slips as the client's own information to use. The client's closing comment ('nobody's yelling at me for once and you're not taking the breaks away') is direct evidence her autonomy was respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Drew out some of the client's own motivations (daughter, keeping breaks) via scaling questions, but much change content came from counselor-offered menus. Rich opportunities went unexplored — why the daughter matters, the meaning of 'weirdly okay,' and the unexamined confidence rating of 4.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and offered choices, but frequently slipped into an expert stance with bulleted option lists and prescribed gum instructions. The client's ideas shaped execution more than direction, with little explicit recognition of her as the expert on her own habits.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited change talk through scaling questions and reinforced her self-generated idea of keeping the break without the cigarette. Key moments were under-exploited — 'I feel weirdly okay about this' and the confidence rating of 4 were never deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well — no lecturing, validated frustration, respected non-negotiables like breaks and untouched weekends. Some sustain talk ('it never sticks,' second-week collapse) was acknowledged but could have been met with more strategic reframing or double-sided reflections.
