# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates warmth and nonjudgment throughout, avoiding shaming about self-harm and staying present when asked. Falls short of 5 because warmth is somewhat formulaic and repetitive, with little individualized affirmation of Maria's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes strong metaphorical reflections like the dropped plate and capturing Maria's need to be heard, but early reflections are shallow. Emotional core issues—fear that 'this is forever,' shame about being a burden, pain around her mom—are reflected only briefly before redirecting to safety checklists.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, appropriate focus on suicide risk with direct assessment of ideation, plan, means, and connection, rarely drifting. Withheld from 5 because direction takes a checklist form rather than strategic movement toward resolving ambivalence about living.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maria co-authored the text to Dani, chose where to place pills, and set the agenda for talking rather than skills. Falls short due to early question-battery style risk assessment and clinician-originated solutions like the safety plan and grounding options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames actions as Maria's choice with options like 'Which feels most doable?' and 'What would you like the message to say?' Maria edited the counselor's draft and acted on her own terms, though rapid-fire stacked requests can feel pressuring despite optional framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Maria's own motivations—asking about reasons to wait elicited the powerful statement about her mom, and Maria generated her own plans. Evocation is intermittent, with much of the middle session consisting of counselor-proposed menus rather than eliciting Maria's ideas, and deeper motivations explored only briefly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly asks permission and offers choices, and incorporated Maria's insight that she needs listening rather than skills. However, expertise conveyed is mostly the counselor's—the drafted text example was clinician-provided and safety-plan content is clinician-directed, making the stance more directive than fully power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully amplifies Maria's emerging reasons to live, reflecting her mom as a reason to wait and reinforcing concrete steps like moving pills and texting Dani. Missed opportunities to deepen change talk—not exploring what made 'a tiny bit less' possible, and commitment language never explicitly elicited or consolidated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue against sustain talk and occasionally reframes gently, but does not actively redirect toward change. Acknowledgments like 'it makes safety steps feel pointless — I hear that' don't pivot, and menu questions sometimes invite more hopelessness rather than using double-sided reflections linking pain relief to reasons for living.
