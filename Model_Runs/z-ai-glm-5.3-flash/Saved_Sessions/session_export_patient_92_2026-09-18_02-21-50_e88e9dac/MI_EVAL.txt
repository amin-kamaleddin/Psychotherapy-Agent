# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician opens with explicit non-judgment and affirms the client's expertise, remaining warm and validating throughout. However, explicit affirmations of the client's strengths are sparse and the warmth is slightly formulaic rather than individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing both content and emotional meaning, including a well-executed double-sided reflection in the final turn. Deeper probing of underlying feelings (e.g., fear of losing social identity, the 'little voice' of worry) would be needed for a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a consistent focus on alcohol use with every visible turn tied to the topic, progressing logically from eliciting alcohol's role to evoking change talk. A 5 would require visible consolidation, such as a change talk summary and a collaboratively chosen next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The work is consistently framed as joint, with open-ended questions and the client's contributions visibly shaping subsequent reflections; the clinician never lectures. Deeper power-sharing through explicit collaborative agenda-setting would elevate the score.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a clear strength, with explicit ownership statements ('it's your call entirely') and no coercive language anywhere. A 5 would require more frequent affirmations of the client's demonstrated capacity, such as linking his past smoking cessation to his ability to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nearly every question elicits the client's own reasoning, with no counselor-supplied reasons for change or lecturing, directly evoking internal motivation. What limits a 5 is that elicited change talk is not systematically deepened or consolidated, and some evocative opportunities go unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly positions expertise with the client, and the client's own contributions demonstrably steer the session. A 5 would require more active fostering of power-sharing, such as inviting the client to set the agenda or co-author next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is actively and skillfully cultivated through well-timed questions that drew out first hesitant acknowledgment and rich client-generated desired outcomes. However, commitment language is not deepened (e.g., confidence/readiness rulers), and missing psychiatrist turns limit verification of consistency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Abundant sustain talk is handled well, with the clinician avoiding arguments, using double-sided reflections, and effectively pivoting toward change-oriented language. The pivot isn't perfectly consistent, as some sustain talk is reflected somewhat neutrally before redirecting.
