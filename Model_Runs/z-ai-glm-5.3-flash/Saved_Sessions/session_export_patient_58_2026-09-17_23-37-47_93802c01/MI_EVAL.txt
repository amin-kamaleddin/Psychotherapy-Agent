# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor validates Jamal's resentment about the involuntary referral from the very first lines and affirms his honesty throughout without ever judging his late nights or past failures. Warmth is genuine and individualized, not formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture both content and emotional meaning, e.g., 'it's not really about the clock — it's about walking in calm instead of stressed' and naming the trade-off between his downtime and a morning that doesn't suck. The counselor also reflects deeper meanings like his mother backing off when he proves himself and his fear the talk might go sideways.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every response ties back to punctuality while the session progresses logically through motivation, vision, barriers, supports, and commitment. The MI arc is purposeful with no wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly hands expertise to Jamal ('You know her better than I do') and functions as a thinking partner rather than a director. Summaries reflect back Jamal's own words rather than the counselor's agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor never pushes or advises, framing the plan as 'something you built, not something handed to you' — Jamal himself says 'it's got to be mine this time,' showing the autonomy framing landed. Even follow-up choices are left to Jamal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question draws out Jamal's own reasons rather than supplying them, and his motivation visibly evolves from 'so people will get off my back' to 'it's got to be mine this time' to setting both alarms tonight. Affirmations strategically consolidate his self-generated insights.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Jamal is positioned as the expert throughout, and the entire action plan (log-off alarm, alarm across the room, talk with mom) comes from him, not the counselor. The counselor offers no unsolicited advice, only questions that help Jamal build his own plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The pivot question ('What would it look like if this were more for you?') shifted Jamal from compliance to intrinsic motivation, and follow-up questions elicited increasingly specific commitment language ('I'm gonna set both tonight... so it's locked in'). Each change statement was affirmed before deepening.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('Nights are my time,' 'why even bother') is met with neutral or double-sided reflections and then pivots to possibility, while honoring Jamal's autonomy. Minor deduction because the mother-pressure narrative is explored somewhat long before redirecting.
