# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warm, nonjudgmental regard; patient's skepticism and near-refusal are validated rather than criticized, with affirmations like 'that makes total sense' and 'you did the hard part.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening throughout, including double-sided reflections of ambivalence and naming her emotional need to be 'informed rather than scared.' Reflections track her shifting mood and end with a meaningful reflection on her ownership.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful focus on medication understanding and adherence, building step-by-step toward a concrete plan (dosing, juice swap, safety card) with summaries keeping the target behavior front and center.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Work is explicitly framed as joint ('let's look at what this medication is together'), and the patient's own ideas shape the final plan, demonstrating equal-partner collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is repeatedly reinforced ('Would swapping to orange juice be an acceptable trade-off... or does that still feel like losing something?') with no pressuring language; her decision is framed as her own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the patient's own values—understanding, routine, being informed—and her stated reasons are developed into genuine commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently attributes expertise to the patient ('You know your body and your life best'), and her contributions substantially shape the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked through targeted questions and reinforced by reflecting back her commitment statements ('I'll take it at night,' 'I'll switch to orange juice,' 'I'll call Dr. Ferris').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is briefly acknowledged then redirected toward collaborative problem-solving—skepticism, grapefruit attachment, and fears are reframed without ever reinforcing reasons against change.
