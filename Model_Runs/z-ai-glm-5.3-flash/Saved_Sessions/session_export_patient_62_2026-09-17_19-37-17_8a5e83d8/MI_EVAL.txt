# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth with strong affirmations that counter Jordan's self-disparagement ('That's not pathetic, that's knowing what matters to you'). Warmth occasionally risks sounding scripted, and the opening jumped to a solution-focused question before fully receiving his discouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening throughout, including accurate double-sided reflections ('part of you calls it pointless, and part of you does it anyway'). Empathy for the shame around his sister was opened but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus from the opening reflection on stuckness to a concrete one-week behavioral experiment, with each turn building on the last. No aimless drift; guidance was strategic while keeping Jordan in the driver's seat.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative—Jordan's own ideas, metaphors, and words ('skipping the breaks that actually call me,' 'too stubborn to quit') shaped the session's themes and final plan. Inclusive language and questions rather than directives throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly affirmed Jordan's ownership ('That's a plan you built, not one I handed you') and accepted his skepticism ('you don't have to be convinced yet, just willing to test it'). Minor moments where suggestions were offered rather than fully evoked.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from within—eliciting Jordan's own definitions of success, coping resources, and criteria for progress, with change talk increasing to commitment language ('I can try one week'). Occasionally came close to leading, e.g., proposing the prepared answer for his sister rather than evoking Jordan's own solution.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Jordan as the expert ('You know your situation better than anyone') and credited him for all insights and the final plan. The entire one-week plan was built from Jordan's own material—exemplary partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's greatest strength—skillfully evoking and amplifying change talk from minimal signals ('I wish something would go right') and developing it into concrete commitment language by session's end. Reframing sustain talk ('skipping the breaks') back to Jordan was especially effective.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Persistent sustain talk was generally acknowledged briefly and neutrally before pivoting to change-oriented questions, avoiding amplification. Could maintain fuller neutrality when resilience-based sustain talk ('running on empty') emerged.
