# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validating Marisol's autonomy and never pressuring removal of the piercing. Warmth was somewhat mechanical, with checklist-style and scripted responses limiting depth of positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some accurate reflections early on, but they were thin and repeated, and deeper emotional exploration was skipped in favor of menu options. A key vulnerable moment ('I kind of brushed it off') received no reflective response.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept focus on the piercing and oral health concern and converted the client's defensiveness into a concrete monitoring plan. Early session energy was partly spent on dentist-communication side-quests rather than the oral-health behavior itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaboration existed, with client choices steering the session, but the counselor often delivered monologue-style advice blocks. The dynamic was more 'counselor proposes, client accepts' than genuine co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: repeatedly offered choice, accepted the refusal to remove the piercing, and normalized slips, with a plan built around preserving her choice. Could be stronger by explicitly affirming her ownership of the decision to monitor her own health.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicited the client's own motivations or values, relying mostly on closed menu questions and missing open questions about what matters to her. Motivation emerged spontaneously; the counselor capitalized on it but did not actively draw it out or deepen it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used some collaborative framing, but frequently fell into a menu-offering, advice-delivering pattern that positioned the counselor as the solution provider. Little explicit acknowledgment that Marisol is the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Responded well when the client volunteered change-relevant material, leading to concrete commitments (monthly checks, reminder, shorter post). This success was largely accidental rather than strategically evoked through open questions about her reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided arguing with sustain talk, instead pivoting to harm-reduction options compatible with keeping the piercing, which naturally softened resistance. Missed opportunities to actively reframe or double-side reflect the sustain talk to develop ambivalence.
