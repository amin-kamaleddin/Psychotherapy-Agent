# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently acknowledged the patient's autonomy ('it's yours to make') and respected his self-knowledge without judgment or pressure, even when noting ambivalence about the diet.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates deep empathic understanding, accurately reflecting both sides of the patient's experience (pride in progress vs. lingering discouragement) and mirroring his own insights back to him.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on exercise motivation and diet change throughout, skillfully narrowing from social comparison discouragement toward concrete behavior change strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the patient as the expert ('you know yourself better than I do'), asking rather than telling, and letting the patient generate his own solutions like the belt reminder and snack drawer plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is entirely the patient's own—'actually mine, not something I'm doing because someone told me to'—and the clinician reinforced this ownership repeatedly without prescribing actions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation of change talk: the patient articulated his own insights about immediate payoffs, his fear of accountability, and his readiness ('I could talk to her tonight'), rather than the clinician persuading him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative rapport with balanced exchanges, genuine curiosity, and shared problem-solving; the clinician followed the patient's lead while gently steering toward change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician systematically drew out the patient's own reasons for change (back pain, sleep, momentum, wife's support) and highlighted change talk moments ('that's you recognizing what matters').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ambivalence ('discouragement wins more than I'd like,' fear of saying it out loud) was validated rather than challenged, and consistently paired with reflections on the patient's strengths and self-efficacy.
