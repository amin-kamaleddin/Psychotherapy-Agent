# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with good humor about Andrei's waiting-room complaint and several affirmations ('Nice plan,' 'good call'). Affirmations were somewhat generic, and his vulnerable disclosure about the scales was not explicitly honored or normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong early reflections ('You're juggling nonstop... totally fair') but reflective listening largely disappeared afterward, replaced by rapid-fire advice. The counselor gave no acknowledgment when Andrei disclosed that the scales hitting fifteen stone 'stung'—a significant empathy failure at the session's emotional peak.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Excellent focus maintained despite time pressure, moving cleanly from importance to behavior, plan, success measures, and follow-up. Direction was achieved largely through counselor-led agenda-setting, and the final emotional disclosure relevant to the target was deflected rather than incorporated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Polite interaction with repeated preference questions, but the counselor generated virtually all content while Andrei merely selected and edited. Andrei's own expertise ('I know the theories of exercise and a balanced diet') was never drawn upon.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine choice points and the one-week experiment framing supported client choice, and his boundaries were respected. However, counselor turns were dense with prescriptions that controlled the 'how' of change rather than eliciting Andrei's own strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain: after one good opening question, solutions and motivation were largely supplied by the counselor rather than elicited. Andrei's vulnerable admissions about caring about his weight begged for open exploration but were met only with administrative closure.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice-based questions invited some input, but the session was driven by the counselor's pre-formed menus of snacks, reminders, and backup plans. Andrei's practical expertise (identifying Tesco as the real barrier) emerged despite, not because of, the approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening question and success-measure framing successfully evoked change talk ('I'd like to get hold of the weight'; 'I can do this'). However, promising change talk like 'maybe I'll think about the walking thing next' was left unreflected and never deepened or summarized as commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('no time,' 'food is just fuel,' 'first thing to drop') was acknowledged but not consistently softened or reframed. No double-sided reflections paired his barriers with his own stated desire to change, and pivots were directive plan-fixes rather than MI-consistent redirection.
