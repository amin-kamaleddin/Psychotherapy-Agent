# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing resistance and affirming the client's caution. Warmth is somewhat brief and transactional, and normalization of slips appears only at the end.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of values and frustration, such as validating the smoking breaks as a reset. Deeper emotional content like family guilt and fear of failure is never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains tight focus on the smoking behavior and progressively moves toward concrete action, from assessment to commitment and logistics. The only minor weakness is bundling four questions in the opening turn.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed, with the client selecting the gum, car change, start day, and reminder format. The counselor repeatedly checks in before finalizing each step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently honored through framing without commitment to quit, offering choices, and respecting the client's boundaries and communication preferences. No pressure toward quitting is applied.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client's own reasons and self-efficacy are effectively drawn out through open questions. The counselor occasionally slips into the expert role with prescriptive tips rather than first eliciting the client's ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions the client as the expert, with his contributions substantively shaping the plan. Some advice-giving turns are delivered before inviting the client's own strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and reinforces change talk, moving the client from desire to specific commitment. Prompts about next steps and barriers strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with acceptance rather than argument, and reframed toward manageable change. However, the counselor sometimes mirrors sustain talk without pivoting back, and misses double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
