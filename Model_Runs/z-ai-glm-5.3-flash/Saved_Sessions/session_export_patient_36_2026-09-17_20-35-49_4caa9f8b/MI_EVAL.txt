# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard with frequent genuine affirmations ('Did you hear what you just said?', 'That came from somewhere deep in you'). Normalizes imperfection, honors the client's pace, and frames partial success (3 of 7 mornings) as worthwhile.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that captures both content and emotional meaning, mirroring Elena's key phrases ('deliberate instead of accidental,' 'the pause is where the choice lives'). Reflections reach her deeper value of self-worth, not just surface behavior.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus on the target behavior (protecting morning self-time), moving purposefully from identification to planning and follow-up. Each turn advances the goal, ending with a concrete commitment and check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully egalitarian: frames the check-in as something to 'look at together' and lets Elena design the tracking method and success criteria. Her ideas drive the plan while the counselor provides structure and scaffolding.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offers options without prescribing and frames success on the client's terms ('not to grade yourself,' 3 of 7 as acceptable). Elena's closing statement that the plan 'belongs to me' is direct evidence of effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn almost entirely from the client; the counselor never argues for change or supplies reasons. Questions elicit Elena's own deeply held values, and all commitments are self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Elena as the expert on her own life, and her contributions substantially shape the session. The coffee ritual, phone-in-drawer plan, and notes-app tracking all originate from her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes rich desire, ability, reason, and commitment language ('Starting tomorrow,' 'I think I can hold that line') through open questions. Reinforces change talk by reflecting it back and amplifying it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled the limited sustain talk well — pivoting away from rejected options (journaling, walks) without arguing and reframing skipped mornings non-judgmentally. Slightly below 5 because low client ambivalence meant skill in softening resistance under real pressure wasn't fully demonstrated.
