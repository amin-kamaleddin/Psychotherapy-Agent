# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, accepting Jamal's wish to try the plan without telling his mom. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic ('That's a solid plan,' 'That's great') and lacked individualized warmth or normalization of his ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that incorporated Jamal's own words (snooze habit, external pressure, confidence ratings). Reflections rarely touched deeper emotional content, such as his shame about teacher comments and his desire to prove himself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus with no topic drift — every exchange tied back to punctuality. The session moved expertly from barriers to importance, to a concrete one-week trial with contingency planning and a scheduled check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuine partnership of equals — Jamal contributed the phone-by-the-door idea, tracking method, reward, and check-in preference, and the final plan was unmistakably his own, refined together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice-offering ('Which sounds best to try this week?') and respect for Jamal's rejection of telling his mom and daily check-ins. Some counselor-generated elements (preset options, reward suggestion) and no explicit reinforcement of Jamal's ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reliably drew motivation from Jamal via scaling questions and forward-looking prompts, eliciting intrinsic reasons ('For me, not just 'cause everyone's nagging'). Evocation leaned procedural, with lighter exploration of values and identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jamal's input consistently shaped the plan, including his alarm strategy and 3-day check-in preference. The counselor generated most of the option menus rather than eliciting Jamal's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance (6) and confidence scales (5→7) evoked meaningful change talk ('Not this time. It's just a week'; 'part of me wants to do this for me'). The intrinsic change talk was not deliberately deepened before moving to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of slipping back, phone scrolling) was acknowledged neutrally and not reinforced, keeping the session solution-focused. Concerns were often bypassed via quick pivots to action rather than met with double-sided reflections.
