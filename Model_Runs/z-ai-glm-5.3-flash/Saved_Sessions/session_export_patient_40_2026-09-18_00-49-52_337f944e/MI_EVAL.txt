# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, unconditional positive regard throughout, with affirmations and normalization of relapse and shame; no judgment or disapproval appeared.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening captured Elias's emotional core each turn, including subtle content beneath his words and mirroring his own language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, progressive focus: relapse, ambivalence, plan-building, hardest step, and a concrete commitment with follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative; the plan, action step, and meaning of the call all originated from Elias, with the psychiatrist acting as a thinking partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership consistently reinforced ('in your words,' 'completely yours'), with no advice imposed and Elias setting the terms of his action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation elicited from within—family, identity, self-respect—through questions that drew out and developed Elias's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in Elias ('You know your life best'), and he genuinely shaped the session by generating the plan and strategy.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk skillfully evoked and amplified at every stage, reflecting Elias's own reasons—kids, brother, self-worth—back to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged and gently reframed with ambivalence paired with movement; however, the core ambivalence about intrinsic motivation was nudged past rather than fully worked through.
