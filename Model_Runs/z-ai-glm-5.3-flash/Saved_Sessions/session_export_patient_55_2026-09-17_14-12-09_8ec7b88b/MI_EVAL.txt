# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a polite, nonjudgmental tone (e.g., 'Totally okay to be unsure') despite the client's deflection. However, when the client said she felt like she was 'in trouble,' the counselor failed to explicitly validate or normalize that feeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal, limited to brief surface acknowledgments before pivoting to checklists and advice. The counselor never reflected the emotional layer—her feeling of being accused, defensive relief, or the meaning behind her minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The referral target (alcohol use) was raised once and immediately dropped when the client deflected, never returned to with curiosity or collaborative exploration. The session drifted to essay planning, largely chosen by the client to avoid the target topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session was expert-delivery rather than equal partnership: the counselor supplied a full weekly plan, thesis, and paragraph template without first eliciting the client's own strategies. The client's 'I already kind of make lists' was largely ignored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor consistently offered menus of options and asked for the client's preferences throughout. However, autonomy support coexisted with heavy unsolicited advice, and framing avoidance of the alcohol topic as equally valid undermined genuine informed choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was assumed to reside in the counselor's advice; no open questions explored the client's values, her relationship with alcohol, or her goals beyond logistics. When the client shared her own ideas, the counselor responded by supplying structure rather than drawing out her thinking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offered choices about session content and scheduling, conveying some partnership. But the actual content (Pomodoro plans, essay templates) was delivered prescriptively with little incorporation of the client's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was almost no effort to evoke the client's own motivations—no exploration of values, goals, or ambivalence around alcohol. The client's 'This was actually more useful than I expected' went unreinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor accepted the client's substantial sustain talk ('I'm not a big drinker,' 'everyone my age does it') by prematurely agreeing the risk was low and abandoning the topic. No double-sided reflections, discrepancy development, or exploration of the gap between the screening and her self-report.
