# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance despite sarcasm and defensiveness, with affirmations like 'Good on you for trying this small step.' However, warmth felt somewhat formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured Luis's frustration, but the counselor shifted quickly to checklists and action plans, missing the emotional core of his resistance — feeling judged, shame about being forgetful, and defensive pride. Reflections became sparse in the latter half.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stayed clearly focused on asthma management and adherence, closing with a concrete, trackable plan and safety guidance. Direction was achieved through counselor-driven structuring rather than collaborative focusing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Luis's input shaped the plan and his rejections were accepted, but the counselor typically presented options first and invited selection second, with multiple unrequested recaps creating an uneven power dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were consistently offered and refusals accepted without pressure or lecturing. A higher score would require more explicit reinforcement of Luis's ownership of the change process and his own expertise.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was supplied rather than drawn from the client. Key openings were missed, such as exploring what 'What's it even doing for me?' meant to him or the significance of his morning chest tightness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were repeatedly offered and client rejections accepted, but the interaction felt like menu-delivery of pre-packaged options rather than drawing out Luis's own ideas, despite signals of his self-education about asthma.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged (e.g., 'I can do one week'), but the counselor rarely deepened or reinforced it, pivoting instead to logistics and safety instructions rather than exploring Luis's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and was gently redirected, with low-burden options effectively defusing resistance. Softening was done via quick solutions rather than double-sided reflections or strategic use of the sustain talk itself.
