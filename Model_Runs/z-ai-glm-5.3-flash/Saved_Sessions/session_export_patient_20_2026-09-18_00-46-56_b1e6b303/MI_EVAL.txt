# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated unconditional acceptance, responding to ambivalence and resistance with warmth and curiosity rather than judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was deep and accurate, including reflections on the emotional meaning of family comments and the patient's fear of deprivation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintained a clear focus on drinking behavior while following the patient's lead, balancing steering with non-attachment to a specific agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was highly collaborative, with the clinician inviting the patient to generate ideas and self-assess rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized the patient's choice and self-direction ('on your terms,' 'you know your life best'), reinforcing ownership of the change plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician used open questions to help the patient discover his own motivations, such as the distinction between social and solo drinking and the value of sister's comments stopping.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as the expert on his own life, consistently asking what felt doable to him rather than directing the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully evoked commitment language ('I could try it for a couple of weeks,' 'small's the point') through open questions targeting desire, ability, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('drinks work,' 'nothing hits the same') was met with reflection rather than confrontation, allowing it to dissipate naturally and pivot to change talk.
