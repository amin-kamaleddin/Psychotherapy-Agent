# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth throughout, including honoring the client's explicit decision not to quit without judgment or pressure. Falls short of a 5 because affirmations are somewhat formulaic and not deeply individualized to Linda's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate surface-level reflections capture her ambivalence between enjoying smoking and concern for her kids. However, reflections are brief and repetitive, and deeper emotions—her guilt, fear of failure, and defensiveness—are never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Steady focus on smoking and its visibility to the kids, progressing logically from rulers to a concrete action plan with implementation questions. Momentum stalls at behavioral logistics, as the 'not quitting' framing is never revisited to build toward the larger change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed from Linda's own ideas (coat pocket, fridge note, kettle), with her input sought at each step. The initial six-option list was counselor-generated, making one segment more expert-driven despite her free filtering of it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: every step framed as Linda's choice, with permission-seeking, explicit honoring of her refusals (no brainstorming, no alarms, no quitting pressure), and no directive language anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Importance and confidence rulers drew out Linda's own reasons, including her concern for the kids. Evocation was inconsistent, however—much of the session shifted to counselor-provided options and logistics, with missed opportunities to explore what would raise her ratings or what the kids' future means to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated permission-seeking and genuine deference to Linda's choices and boundaries shaped the plan throughout. The expertise-driven option list and only a brief acknowledgment of her stated boundary kept this from a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers successfully elicited some change talk ('The kids part worries me'), but these moments were not deepened—no follow-up on why her rating wasn't lower or what would move it higher. Focus stayed on logistics rather than amplifying her motivations or connecting change to her values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never argued with or confronted sustain talk, respecting Linda's position. However, sustain talk was often met with neutral restatements rather than strategic double-sided reflections pairing her enjoyment of smoking with her hopes for her children.
