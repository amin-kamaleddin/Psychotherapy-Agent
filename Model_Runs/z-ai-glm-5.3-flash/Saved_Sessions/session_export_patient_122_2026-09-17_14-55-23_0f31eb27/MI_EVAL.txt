# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone maintained throughout, even when Sofia shared vulnerable material. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and lacked explicit validation of her self-worth independent of her plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were consistently accurate and concise, tracking both content and worry (e.g., the week-three fear). They tended to summarize rather than deepen emotional nuance, missing opportunities to explore discouragement from past failed diets.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on a single concrete target behavior: one 10-minute Saturday walk. Every exchange built the plan forward, ending with a fully specified, actionable behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Sofia selecting, tweaking, and rejecting elements while the counselor invited revisions and adapted in real time. Decision-making was fully shared.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was exemplary, with repeated choices and explicit handing over of control ('It's completely up to you'). The counselor honored her requests about nudge format and what to avoid in the Sunday reply.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn effectively from Sofia's own ideas (walking-buddy routine, podcast reward) and confidence rulers raised her confidence from 5-6 to 7. Some direct counselor-generated suggestions (habit-stacking, reminder options) slightly tempered this.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sofia was consistently treated as the expert, and her contributions genuinely shaped the session. She picked 'ten minutes for me,' adjusted the walk time, and designed the nudge format, all incorporated without override.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Importance and confidence rulers actively evoked change talk, tracking confidence from 5-6 up to 7 with clear commitment language ('I can do one week'). Could go further by deepening change talk (e.g., what lasting past week three would mean to her).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('diets don't stick,' 'week three usually gets me') was acknowledged without amplification and redirected toward practical solutions like the if/then backup and 'small counts' reframing. More explicit double-sided reflections would strengthen this further.
