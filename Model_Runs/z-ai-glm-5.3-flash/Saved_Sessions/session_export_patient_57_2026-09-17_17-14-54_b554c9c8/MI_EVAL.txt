# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with respectful validation throughout. However, praise is somewhat formulaic and lacks explicit affirmation of Jamal's personal strengths beyond his utility to the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections show good perspective-taking, but reflections become sparse and are replaced by advice-giving. The emotional core of Jamal's ambivalence—fear of social exclusion and team belonging—is never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on avoiding alcohol at post-game gatherings and channels toward concrete, Friday-specific action. Falls short of a 5 because the plan is never linked back to Jamal's deeper values like college prospects.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Role-play is co-constructed at Jamal's request with genuine check-ins. However, the counselor drives strategy selection and delivers corrective feedback in a coach-like manner, with Jamal's own contributions improved upon rather than built from.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language and menu-based options at the start, but the session becomes increasingly directive. The counselor supplies the best lines and scripts the Marcus conversation, leaving Jamal's plan substantially counselor-authored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler and requests for Jamal's own lines produce client-generated change talk with confidence rising from 4 to 8. Yet most strategies originate from the counselor and Jamal's own reasons for change (college scouts, team spot) are never developed into values-based motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission is sought before offering ideas and Jamal's input is invited, showing some power sharing. But nearly every useful strategy and script is counselor-generated and handed to Jamal, rather than drawing out his own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of a confidence ruler with repeated elicitation of Jamal's own strategies, and his change talk grows substantially (confidence 4 to 8). Missed opportunities remain to reflect his motivations back and elicit deeper commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal's sustain talk about group pressure is acknowledged but not strategically softened with double-sided reflections. The counselor quickly pivots to problem-solving, sidestepping rather than resolving the ambivalence.
