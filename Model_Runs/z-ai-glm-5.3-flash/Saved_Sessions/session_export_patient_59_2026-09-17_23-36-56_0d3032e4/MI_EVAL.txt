# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout despite the client's dismissive presentation. No judgment about the marijuana possession or minimization; honesty and even 'corny' ideas were affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate complex reflections captured both content and emotional nuance, including the frustration of the referral and the vulnerability around his mother. Emotional cores like the fear of his mom's hurt were reflected precisely.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained purposeful focus, moving step by step from frustration to goals, obstacles, past strategies, intrinsic motivation, and a concrete action plan. Every question built toward change-relevant territory despite the client's resistance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Work was framed as joint problem-solving ('let's figure this out together'), with the agenda emerging from the client's own priorities. Pushback was rolled with non-defensively, keeping the interaction on equal footing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership was reinforced throughout ('I'm not here to lecture you,' 'It's your idea'), and all solutions originated with Jamal. The counselor explicitly attributed ideas to him rather than claiming credit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation: no advice or persuasion, with strategic questions moving the client from external compliance to intrinsic motivation (his mother's disappointment) and self-generated commitment ('decide before I go, not while I'm there').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned the client as the expert on his own life, and his contributions (phone reminder, pre-decision strategy, mother motivation) drove the session. Each question built directly on the client's previous statements.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and deepened through open questions about goals, past successes, and motivation, culminating in the client's own concrete plan. Motivation was consistently drawn from the client rather than supplied by the counselor.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification, and the counselor appropriately never argued against minimization. A few reflections briefly restated sustain talk (e.g., the annoyance of proving himself), where a quicker pivot toward change language would help.
