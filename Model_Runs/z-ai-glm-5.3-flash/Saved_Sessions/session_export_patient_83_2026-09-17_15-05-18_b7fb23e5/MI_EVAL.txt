# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and validation of Mai's frustration ('That's a reasonable concern'). Affirmations feel somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized to her specific situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening that tracks Mai's concerns ('cautious about another pill and worried about muscle problems'). Reflections are occasionally brief or bundled with multiple questions, limiting deeper emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, unwavering focus on the target behavior — understanding and adhering to the statin — with no topic drift. The session converges on a concrete, actionable plan Mai explicitly commits to.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaboration via offering options and permission-seeking, but the counselor often delivers bundled answers positioning themselves as the knowledge source. Solutions are presented as finished products rather than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices and respects Mai's decisions without pressure. Heavy focus on scripts and checklists occasionally tips toward directing rather than reinforcing her ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good evocative questions ('What worries you most?'), but change was driven mainly by counselor-delivered education. Deeper personal motivations were never explored, and no rulers or elaboration techniques were used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai's input influences the session, but the counselor functions more as an expert consultant than a partner. Rarely explicitly acknowledges that Mai knows her own routines and life best.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits strong commitment language ('I'll start tonight... no reason to put it off anymore') and affirms it. Misses strategic follow-ups to deepen change talk; much momentum came from psychoeducation rather than deliberate evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without amplification, but opportunities for double-sided reflections pivoting toward change are missed. Mai's shift came largely through information delivery rather than deliberate MI technique.
