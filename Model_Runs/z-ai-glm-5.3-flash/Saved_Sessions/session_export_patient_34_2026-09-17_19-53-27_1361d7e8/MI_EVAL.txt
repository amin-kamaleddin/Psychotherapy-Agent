# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard even when Dylan was defensive and blame-shifting. Explicitly refused the lecturing stance, validated honesty, and accepted Dylan's 'not there yet' on drinking without judgment or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including an accurate capture of Dylan's core grievance about being singled out and reflections tying ideas to his values. Fell short of top marks because early reflections stayed at surface content and didn't probe the emotional undercurrent (later-revealed stress about consequences and parents) until Dylan volunteered it himself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained tight, steady focus on the conduct incident and alcohol-related risk throughout, with every exchange tied back to the target behavior. Skillfully redirected deflections rather than abandoning focus, progressing logically from engagement to a concrete, client-owned plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine exchange between equals: asked permission before probing, framed the session around Dylan's perspective, and built the plan from Dylan's own material. No advice was imposed; Dylan visibly shaped the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy repeatedly and explicitly reinforced ('you get to decide what this hour is for,' 'that's yours to decide, whenever or never'). Never pressured Dylan on drinking changes and kept the commitment small and self-chosen.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from Dylan rather than supplying it, eliciting his record-protection values, concrete plan, peer-connection reasoning, and anticipated feelings of relief and pride. Every motivational statement came from Dylan's mouth — evocation at its best.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Dylan as the expert on his own life, crediting ideas to him and asking permission and confirmation. The session's direction was visibly driven by Dylan's contributions (leaving early, the 'guy who dips at midnight' identity, peer conversations).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and deepened change talk, transforming a dismissive 'be smarter about which parties' into a client-owned, identity-based plan. Used evocative questions that produced commitment-adjacent talk ('relieved... like I dodged something'), each reflected back and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided arguing with sustain talk, instead pivoting to Dylan's own values and explicitly accepting his 'not there yet' stance without reinforcing it. Could improve by more consistently using double-sided reflections to pair sustain talk with the change-oriented content Dylan had just offered.
