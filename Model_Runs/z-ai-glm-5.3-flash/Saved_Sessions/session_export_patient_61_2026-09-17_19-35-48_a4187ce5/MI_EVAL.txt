# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard is maintained throughout—resistance, sustain talk, and habits are met with curiosity and affirmation rather than judgment. The closing reinforces acceptance regardless of outcome.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and emotionally attuned, including a model double-sided reflection on ambivalence ('worn out by the sheer size of it' vs. 'some part of you showed up anyway'). Deeper meanings, such as fear of his father's fate and shame with his wife watching, are skillfully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session progresses logically from overwhelm to concrete, self-authored steps, with skillful returns to the target behavior. The one gap: glucose monitoring—the core medical behavior—is never explicitly re-linked to the wins before closing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every major direction (the lunch idea, the Sarah conversation, autonomy framing) originated with the client and was co-developed rather than prescribed. No advice or expert-driven directives were given at any point.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is actively reinforced throughout ('on your terms,' 'you get to set the pace here,' no promise required). The clinician offers a menu of acceptable outcomes and explicitly honors the client's wish to lead the change himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within the client via open questions and affirmations, never argued for. The payoff is Jonathan's spontaneous shift: 'it stopped feeling like their list... started feeling like mine.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly locates expertise in the client ('You're the expert on your own life,' 'You know her better than anyone'). The session is built around the client's own contributions rather than the clinician's agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is evoked and systematically developed—elicitng the client's own idea, naming his self-generated reason, exploring past successes, and deepening mobilizing language ('what changed for you just now?'). The client's spontaneous shift to 'mine' shows the evocation worked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('just gonna be another thing I fail at') is met with reframing rather than argument, and fear of disappointment is transformed into a concrete plan. The clinician explicitly sets the imposed list aside and closes on the client's own steps.
