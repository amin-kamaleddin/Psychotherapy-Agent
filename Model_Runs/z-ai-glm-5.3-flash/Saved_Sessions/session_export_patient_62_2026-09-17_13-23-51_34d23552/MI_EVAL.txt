# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and respect without judgment — responds to passive ideation screening with concern rather than alarm, and affirms the client's experiences. Warmth is occasionally procedural (e.g., the final checklist), slightly diluting unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic understanding is conveyed, but reflections are sparse and formulaic, moving quickly to advice-giving. Deeper feelings — fear of hoping again, repeated rejection, the meaning of being cared about — are rarely reflected with emotional depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on a concrete target behavior with logical progression from safety screening to a specific, time-bound commitment. Falls short of a 5 because direction sometimes stems from the counselor's task-completion agenda rather than the client's articulated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mixes genuine collaboration (resume bullet built from the client's real experience) with expert-driven dispensing of templates, step lists, and keyword tips. Often feels like homework assignment rather than joint exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices and fully accepts the client's self-imposed limit ('Just one'), with the commitment genuinely originating from the client. Would need more emphasis on the client's control over the overall change process, not just menu options within the counselor's framework.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes the client's competence (training new hires, fastest zone) and draws out self-generated commitment. However, much of the session supplies solutions rather than using open questions to draw out the client's own reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some true collaboration — the client's contributions genuinely shape the plan — but the counselor frequently slips into an expert stance with pre-packaged products. The client is more a consumer of the counselor's materials than a co-creator.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively builds on brief change talk through small doable steps and co-developed resume content, eliciting strong commitment language ('one shot's more than zero shots') and a concrete 9am plan. Evocation relies more on offering option menus than on open questions drawing out the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids reinforcing pessimistic narratives and gently pivots to achievable action, allowing the client to counter his own sustain talk. However, rarely uses double-sided reflections to acknowledge and strategically reframe the pessimism rather than sidestepping it.
