# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone even when the patient minimized his cigar use, validating his perspective without labeling or confrontation. However, warmth felt somewhat procedural, with limited individualized affirmation of the patient's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening that accurately captured the patient's skepticism and pragmatism. Deeper emotional content (wife's pressure, pride, financial stress) was acknowledged only lightly and never explored for meaning or feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (cigar use and throat irritation) throughout every exchange, with no drift. Slightly weakened by the heavy logistics emphasis, which consumed time better spent exploring ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The tone was collaborative and the patient's answers directly determined the plan. However, the counselor supplied nearly all ideas, templates, and scripts, functioning as plan-provider while the patient picked from menus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choice and never pressured quitting, explicitly respecting the patient's 'not cold turkey' stance. Autonomy support was delivered through counselor-constructed menus rather than inviting the patient to generate his own options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Most questions were logistical rather than motivational, and the patient's own reasons for change (cost concern, wanting proof for himself) emerged spontaneously without being drawn out or expanded. Motivation resided more in the counselor's options menu than in elicited client language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed that the patient's input shaped the session, and his preferences (voice-to-text, 8 PM reminder) determined the plan. The counselor's pre-formulated options and materials slightly limited genuine co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client-generated change talk emerged (openness to knowing, cost concerns, conditional willingness to cut back), but the counselor missed key opportunities to reflect and amplify it. No commitment or confidence language was explicitly elicited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing with the patient's minimization but did little to actively redirect it, offering options and scripts instead of double-sided reflections. Sustain talk was tolerated rather than softened.
