# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations and reframing of David's self-criticism. However, validation of the emotional difficulty of past failed attempts and social pressure remains limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate but sparse, surface-level reflections are present (e.g., acknowledging the torn feelings about medication options). The emotional weight of repeated failures, work stress, and social fear is not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation, progressing logically from assessment to medication, quit date, refusal skills, and follow-up. No topic drift occurs.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Agenda-setting, option choices, and interactive role-play reflect genuine joint work. The score falls short of 5 because the psychiatrist often pre-supplies strategy menus rather than eliciting David's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is repeatedly affirmed through preference questions about options, quit date, and strategies. More explicit reinforcement of David's ownership and self-direction would elevate this.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers, evocative questions, and preference prompts draw out substantial client-generated motivation and change talk. The strongest change talk ('I actually think I can do this') is not deepened or fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and preference questions show some power sharing, but the interaction is largely information-heavy and prescriptive. David's ideas are incorporated, often only after the psychiatrist has supplied the framework.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocative openers, rulers, and choice-based questions successfully elicit rich change talk, including commitment to a quit date. Peak change talk is not consistently deepened with strategic reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubts about side effects and social refusal are met with balanced information and solution-focused redirection rather than amplification. Explicit double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence are absent.
