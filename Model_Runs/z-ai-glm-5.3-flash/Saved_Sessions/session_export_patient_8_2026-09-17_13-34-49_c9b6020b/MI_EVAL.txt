### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections were present, but reflective listening was inconsistent and often shallow. The patient's fear of loneliness—the emotional core of the session—was met with practical fixes rather than a deeper reflection of her isolation concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed well focused on the target behavior and progressed from exploration to a concrete, time-bound plan with defined follow-up. However, direction was achieved largely through agenda-driven advice-giving rather than guiding, with only one true agenda-setting question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction read as genuine teamwork with co-constructed plans and exemplary collaborative language ('we'll troubleshoot together'). However, nearly every solution originated from the psychiatrist, with the patient in the role of accepter/adapter rather than co-generator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered options and framed the plan as a patient-owned 'small experiment,' with the patient retaining full control. However, there was little explicit reinforcement of the patient's own authority, and the rapid-fire strategy menus sometimes functioned as soft persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain: the psychiatrist supplied motivation through solutions and reassurance rather than drawing it from the patient. There were no evocative questions, rulers, or values exploration, and the patient's self-generated insight about her own shift went unacknowledged.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing: choices were offered, permission asked, and the patient's contributions genuinely shaped the plan (e.g., three check-windows). It fell short because the psychiatrist drifted into an expert role with strategy lists in nearly every turn.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged (e.g., 'maybe a time limit thing would be okay', 'I'll actually send that group message tonight'), including a pivotal patient-voiced shift from 'not a problem' to a plan. However, the psychiatrist rarely reinforced or deepened these moments, and no open questions were designed to elicit desire, ability, reasons, or need for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not amplified, but it was also not actively softened—instead it was accommodated by repeatedly negotiating the requested change downward (60→30→15 min, grayscale). No double-sided reflections were used to develop discrepancy using the patient's own stated costs.
