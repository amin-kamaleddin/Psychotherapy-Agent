# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard with no judgment or disapproval. Mai's skepticism about her doctor was validated rather than dismissed, and concerns were treated as solvable questions rather than faults.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured both content and emotional undertones throughout the session. Reflections occasionally shifted quickly into questions or agenda-setting rather than dwelling deeper on Mai's frustration and anxiety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on the target behavior of safely starting the statin, structuring the conversation around Mai's own stated priorities. Each concern was sequentially resolved with explicit loop-closing and no drift off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly framed the interaction as joint work, co-constructing agendas by asking Mai to rank priorities. Acknowledged limits of their own knowledge, leveling the power dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently reinforced, with Mai's decisions framed as her own ownership. Offered menu-based choices and transparent, accurate information rather than pressuring adherence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew motivation from within, eliciting Mai's own reasons and confidence. Some educational content was delivered didactically before being anchored to Mai's motivations, and there was less explicit elicitation of her personal values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Mai as the expert on her own body, schedule, and habits. Her contributions visibly shaped the session, driving the medication-timing discussion and teach-back.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked commitment language, directly leading to Mai's statements of readiness and change talk that were reinforced through teach-back affirmations. Did not always deepen change talk with follow-up evocative questions after Mai volunteered it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly and redirected constructively, with the muscle concern used to provide clear red-flag criteria. A few reflections restated the concern without an immediate strategic pivot, though this was minimal.
