# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicated consistent warmth and non-judgment with affirmations like 'wanting boundaries doesn't make you unkind' that directly countered Oliver's guilt. Falls short of 5 because affirmations were somewhat formulaic and missed chances to deeply normalize his conflict-avoidance as an understandable pattern rather than a flaw.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opened with an accurate complex reflection and captured the emotional core of his guilt ('your kindness wants to prevent harm'), tracking his humor and ambivalence well. Reflections sometimes pivoted quickly to scripts and questions rather than lingering on deeper emotional undercurrents like his fear of his voice 'going squeaky' or his loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on the target behavior — the assertiveness conversation and move-out date — with every exchange (scaling question, scripts, four role-play rounds) building directly toward Oliver asserting boundaries. Session progressed logically from confidence assessment to rehearsal to concrete plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative with genuine check-ins ('Want to try a quick role-play?', 'Which of those feels most doable?') and a plan built around Oliver's stated preferences like the Saturday sit-down. Fell short of 5 because the counselor drove all the content by supplying every script and pushback line, making collaboration more counselor-led than fully egalitarian.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected Oliver's choices by asking what outcome felt acceptable to him and which replies felt most doable, and implicitly accepted his adaptations of scripts into his own words. Autonomy support was diluted by heavy scripting and prescribing the plan (date, Saturday prep, materials) rather than explicitly returning ownership of words and timeline to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from within via the confidence ruler, exploring his worries, and having him voice boundary statements himself, producing powerful client-owned insights ('It's not rejection, it's... completion'). Some motivational language was scaffolded by counselor-provided scripts rather than consistently elicited de novo; more open evocation would strengthen his own motivational foundation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Incorporated Oliver's input at the margins (his 'no with a plan' preference, Saturday timing) but repeatedly took the expert role by supplying scripts, canned replies, and structured steps. The client largely rehearsed the counselor's words rather than generating his own phrasing and strategy, limiting genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk throughout: the confidence ruler elicited a concrete plan, and role-play rehearsals produced vivid commitment language ('The date is firm,' 'Helping isn't the same as housing you forever,' 'I reckon I can actually do this'). Consistently having Oliver say boundary statements aloud turned abstract intention into ability and commitment talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled guilt-driven sustain talk well with reframes like 'guilt is normal; being kind and setting boundaries can coexist,' channeling fears into rehearsal so that 'the guilt's still there... but it didn't grab the wheel.' Not a 5 because the counselor occasionally supplied fear-based pushback lines itself, keeping sustain-oriented content in the room, though in a controlled exposure format.
