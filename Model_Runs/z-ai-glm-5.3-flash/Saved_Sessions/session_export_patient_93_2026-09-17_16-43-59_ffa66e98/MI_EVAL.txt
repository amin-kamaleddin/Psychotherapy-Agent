# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with affirmations like 'That sounds like a sensible, realistic start' even when Margaret minimized her drinking. No judgment or disapproval appeared at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Margaret's ambivalence and values (independence, pub nights). Reflections stayed mostly at the content level; deeper emotional exploration (fear after the fall, feelings about her son's concern) was limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained clear, consistent focus on alcohol reduction from opening to close, with no drift despite minimization attempts. Every exchange tied back to drinking, falls risk, and medication interactions, culminating in a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative language and check-ins were used consistently, and the plan was co-constructed from Margaret's own ideas. The counselor occasionally slipped into advisory mode (safety instructions, drink suggestions) without first asking what Margaret already knew.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong emphasis on choice throughout — offering options rather than directives and never pressuring toward abstinence. Margaret explicitly recognized this: 'you didn't lecture... my way, mind' and 'tell him it was my idea.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was effectively drawn from within by linking change to Margaret's own values, producing genuine change talk. The counselor could have evoked more confidence/commitment language explicitly (e.g., a confidence ruler) rather than filling space with options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently positioned with Margaret, whose contributions shaped the plan (Tuesday choice, 'bucket' glass insight, sticker preference). At times the counselor supplied options in a more expert-driven mode, and power-sharing could be more explicit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions ('What would a manageable first step look like for you?') elicited genuine change talk, which was reinforced through summaries. Occasionally offering concrete options limited Margaret's opportunity to generate her own ideas.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was abundant but largely not reinforced, using neutral reflections and linking change to her values to pivot toward change. Reflections occasionally restated the minimization without strategic double-sided reflections or gentle reframing.
