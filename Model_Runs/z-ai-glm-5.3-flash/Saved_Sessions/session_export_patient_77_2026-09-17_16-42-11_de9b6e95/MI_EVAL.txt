# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive with well-timed affirmations of Liyana's courage, and no judgment even when concerning behaviors were disclosed. Some affirmations felt slightly formulaic, and normalization of her discouragement as universal could have deepened unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that captured both content and emotion, including the loneliness ('even when I come home, I still feel alone') and ambivalence about eating more. Some reflections were double-barreled, and emotional pain around the teasing and years of feeling alone could have been explored more deeply before pivoting to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus from the opening question through to a concrete three-part plan with follow-up. Every exchange advanced the goal, ending with Liyana summarizing the plan herself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed—Liyana chose the breakfast intervention, negotiated the weighing schedule, and designed her own response to teasing. Portions were somewhat didactic (plateau mechanisms, meal ideas), and rapid multi-question turns occasionally dominated the floor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were repeatedly framed as Liyana's ('pick one that feels most like you'), and her negotiation to keep weekly weighing was accepted. Frequent closed-ended and menu-driven questions sometimes steered rather than purely supported her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions effectively elicited her motivation, but much momentum came from counselor-supplied content (psychoeducation, meal ideas, scripted lines). Few open questions invited Liyana's deeper 'why' or her own theories about the plateau.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor positioned Liyana as the decision-maker, accepting her preference to talk to her mom herself and letting the breakfast choice emerge from her preferences. Several exchanges were info-heavy and delivered in expert mode without first eliciting her own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of rulers drove visible confidence growth from 4 to 8 and strong commitment language ('One week. I can do one week.'). Confidence gains were partly driven by counselor task-listing rather than open evocative questions or elaboration after commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('why even bother') was acknowledged with empathy and redirected away from scale dependence. However, no double-sided reflections were used, and the honest 'one week may not change the scale' lacked a strong counterbalancing reframe, making softening inconsistent rather than strategic.
