# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with a defensive teen; Marcus responded with increasing openness. However, warmth felt somewhat formulaic and lacked explicit affirmation of Marcus's personal strengths or values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made accurate but inconsistent reflections and moved quickly to options and logistics instead of exploring the emotional side—peer belonging, feeling misunderstood, or irritation at the referral.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use, guiding the conversation from defensiveness to a concrete commitment and scheduled follow-up. Minor drift into logistics and redundant check-in instructions rather than consolidating change talk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaved largely as an equal partner, co-creating a plan shaped by Marcus's choices. Reliance on pre-packaged lists rather than soliciting Marcus's own ideas first limited true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was regularly affirmed through choice-offering at nearly every turn and accepting Marcus's rejections without pushback. Mild advisory pressure and structuring of choices kept it short of a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evoked brief ambivalence and a commitment, but didn't explore or amplify those moments—most motivation was negotiated around a harm-reduction menu rather than drawn from Marcus himself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly conveyed expertise resides with the client, and Marcus's choices genuinely shaped the session. Counselor-generated menus and Marcus's 'Is that what you wanted to hear?' suggest a lingering agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged ('this weekend I could try it') but was largely a byproduct of menu-based options rather than strategic evocation—no elaboration, confidence scaling, or deepening of Marcus's stated intent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or confronting, which is a strength, but largely ignored or glossed over sustain talk. Phrases like 'social drinking is normal' risked reinforcing minimization, and no double-sided reflections were used.
