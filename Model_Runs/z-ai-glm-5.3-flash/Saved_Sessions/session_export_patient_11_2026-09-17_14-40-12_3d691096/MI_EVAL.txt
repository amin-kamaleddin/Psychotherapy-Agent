# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with frequent affirmations and validation of worries. Affirmations lean slightly generic and are tied to plan elements rather than deep individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate but brief, surface-level reflections; often transitions to advice quickly. Emotional weight of the jar moment, the all-or-nothing self-criticism, and past discouragement was never reflected at a feeling level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the physical activity goal throughout, with every turn tied to scheduling, cues, backup plans, and follow-up. Ended with concrete commitments, tracking, and contingency strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and offered menu-style options, but the core workout plan was delivered unilaterally with only tweaks invited. Read more as competent coaching than equal-partner co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly framed next steps as client's choice (tracking style, timing, backup plans), and honored her request to hear 'one session is still a win.' Slightly undercut by initially prescribing the full plan before consultation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Excellent evocative opening and confidence scaling question, but evocation was front-loaded. Client's self-generated motivation ('strong enough to open the jar') was packaged as a script rather than explored for its meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Began with permission-asking and invitations to customize, but the middle shifted into an expert-driven posture with a fully formed circuit plan. Little explicit acknowledgment that the client knows what works for her body and life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good evocative questions early yielded strong client change talk, but it was never strategically deepened. Commitment statements were met with agreement and advice rather than elaborating questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal and handled well: the couch trap was reframed as a strength, and the all-or-nothing pattern channeled into a harm-reduction backup plan. Effective redirection, though implicit rather than via deliberate double-sided reflections.
