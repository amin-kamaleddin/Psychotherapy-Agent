# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard with no disapproval of the client's dieting history or weight concerns. Both sides of her ambivalence, including her scared voice about self-trust, are validated and given space.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate complex and double-sided reflections capture both content and emotional meaning throughout, and the client repeatedly confirms feeling understood. Reflections land and the client builds on them productively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains steady focus on moving from dieting toward sustainable, self-directed health behaviors, with every exchange building momentum. Each question advances toward change without rushing, while tying back to the client's core pattern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operates as a genuine partnership between equals, with inclusive language and explicit deference to the client's expertise. Power-sharing is demonstrated through repeatedly crediting the client with insights she generated herself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice and ownership are actively reinforced throughout, with no advice-giving or persuasion anywhere in the session. All ideas and decisions originate with the client, including the plan's design and pacing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently elicits the client's own reasons, strategies, and self-compassion rather than supplying them. Her arc from 'I don't know what that looks like yet' to designing and committing to a self-generated plan demonstrates highly effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located within the client, who drives the session — the energy-tracking plan, habit stacking, and self-compassion strategies all originated from her. The counselor highlights and celebrates this ownership each time.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoke vision, values, and commitment, with change talk strategically reflected back and amplified. This produces progressively stronger client change talk, including 'this feels like something I could actually keep doing. That's new for me.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well — reflected neutrally and redirected to the client's own values without arguing or reinforcing it. A 5 would require slightly more explicit pivoting of the language itself toward change.
