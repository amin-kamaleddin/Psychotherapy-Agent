# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and nonjudgment throughout, honoring the client's request to avoid a lecture and using accepting language. Warmth remained somewhat procedural and task-focused rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured the client's frustration, pill side effects, and preferences. Reflections stayed surface-level and did not explore the emotional meaning of 40 years of smoking or his vulnerability about needles.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Focus drifted almost immediately from smoking into dental scheduling logistics, with most of the session devoted to booking details. No sustained movement toward the target behavior occurred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sought consent and incorporated client preferences into the plan. However, the counselor unilaterally generated the option menu and the session functioned more as intake coordination than two-way dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered genuine choice, including the explicit option to defer smoking talk, and fully respected the client's decisions. The client confirmed experiencing real freedom of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was rarely elicited from within; the importance question was never deepened and the conversation centered on counselor-provided information. The client's spontaneous offer of brief pre-procedure abstinence went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and solicited preferences on scheduling and plan choices. The session was largely driven by the counselor's care-coordination agenda rather than drawing on the client's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The importance ruler was asked in a stacked multi-question opener and never followed up. The client's spontaneous change talk about going an hour or two without a smoke was a clear missed opportunity for affirmation and deepening.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and the counselor explicitly agreed to defer cessation discussion, which respected the client but left ambivalence unaddressed. No double-sided reflections were used to strategically reframe sustain talk toward change.
