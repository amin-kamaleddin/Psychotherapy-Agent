# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with affirmations like 'Nice work picking specific days' and accommodation of Farid's preferences. Warmth is somewhat brisk, with stacked questions limiting deeper personal affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections early and late, but most turns quickly shift into multiple questions, crowding out reflective listening. Farid's ambivalence and self-blame about laziness were only lightly explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary focus throughout, moving steadily from barriers to a concrete plan (specific days, calendar block, phone rule, backup option, check-in). Every turn advances toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely co-constructed—Farid chose the walk, shaped the reminder text, and set check-in timing. Early brainstorming was counselor-generated, and some turns read as protocol application rather than two-way problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice-offering ('if you'd like,' 'want any tweak?') with Farid exercising clear ownership. Stacked questions occasionally pressured responses, and explicit reinforcement of his capacity to direct his own change was limited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reliably drew motivation from Farid via rulers and evocative questions, with his own reasons emerging naturally. Evocation skewed toward logistics rather than deepening his values and the personal meaning of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Farid as decision-maker, and client contributions clearly shaped the plan. Early option-presenting was somewhat expert-ish, and rapid questioning reduced genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocation of change talk through rulers, eliciting prior successes, and having Farid articulate his own plan; confidence rose from 4 to 7. Evocation remained largely procedural rather than values-based.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and offered neutral reframes, but did not strategically resolve ambivalence. Self-doubt and obstacles were handled with contingency planning rather than double-sided reflections linking to Farid's capabilities.
