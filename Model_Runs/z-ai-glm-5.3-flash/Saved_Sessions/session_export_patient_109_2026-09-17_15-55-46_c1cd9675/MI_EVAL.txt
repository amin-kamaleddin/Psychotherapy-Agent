# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone and acknowledges Rafael's fears without criticism. Affirmations are somewhat generic ('Nice plan,' 'really solid') and rarely tied to his specific strengths, like his self-awareness about the lying-in-bed negotiation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captures both content and emotion, e.g., 'You're torn — you care about getting back... That makes sense.' Deeper emotional content — fear about his dad's health, feeling out of place at the gym, discouragement from repeated one-week attempts — is acknowledged but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains clear, consistent focus on returning to the gym with no topic drift, moving logically from barriers to a concrete, testable Saturday plan. Every exchange advances the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is largely a joint enterprise with genuine co-creation of the if-then plan and text wording. Several key ideas (clothes, backup plan structure, workout menus) originate from the counselor rather than being elicited from Rafael.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly and repeatedly reinforces Rafael's ownership ('You're in charge of what we focus on,' 'pick one'), and all plan elements are chosen by Rafael. Help is always offered as options, never directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively draws out Rafael's reasons, past success factors, and his own solutions, producing strong self-generated insights. When Rafael voices worry about feeling out of place, the counselor sometimes jumps to solutions rather than continuing to elicit his motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rafael is repeatedly positioned as the expert and his contributions genuinely shape the session. At times the counselor moves quickly into prescribing solutions (full multi-step plan, three workout options) before Rafael fully articulates his own preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance/confidence scaling, questions about past success, and envisioning, with confidence rising from 4–5 to 8.5 plus commitment language. The counselor misses chances to deepen change talk after it emerges, e.g., elaborating on 'that version of me anymore.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently redirected toward change without being dismissed, via reframing, the backup plan, and if-then coping. Some redirections are more procedural (plan logistics) than strategic softening of the underlying ambivalence about repeated failure.
