# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validates Dylan's feelings throughout. However, warmth is largely procedural, with no affirmations of Dylan's honesty or willingness to engage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflects Dylan's frustration and desires several times, but reflections are brief summaries of stated facts rather than deeper exploration of meaning. Emotional content like his hearing anxiety and roommate conflict goes unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, concrete agenda with specific next steps for the conduct process. Direction is task-focused on process management rather than the underlying behavior change, which is narrower than ideal MI direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works 'with' Dylan throughout, incorporating his edits and co-creating materials. Falls short because the counselor frequently generates content unilaterally rather than inviting Dylan to create his own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices and frames advice with consent, respecting Dylan's decisions. Some turns deliver highly prescriptive content and scripts, and ownership of change is never explicitly returned to Dylan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies on information-giving and document preparation rather than eliciting Dylan's own motivations, supplying answers and scripts instead. The core evocative opportunity—the discrepancy between his claimed innocence and willing cooperation—is never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly asks Dylan what he wants and lets him shape the plan. However, much communication is expert-driven content delivery, with little drawing on Dylan's own expertise about his situation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Dylan admits drinking, accepts an educational sanction, and says 'having a plan feels better than being angry,' but these shifts arise spontaneously. The counselor never explicitly explores his reasons for change or what compliance would mean for him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue with or reinforce Dylan's heavy sustain talk, gently redirecting toward action. There is little strategic softening—no double-sided reflections—and pivots are practical rather than motivational.
