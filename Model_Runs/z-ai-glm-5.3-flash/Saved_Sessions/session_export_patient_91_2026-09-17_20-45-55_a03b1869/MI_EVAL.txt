# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental warmth throughout, with affirmations like 'That's a genuine step' and acceptance of relapse risk met non-punitively. Kept from a 5 by occasionally formulaic warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, often complex reflections (e.g., 'the problem was never you — it was the size of the pile') show strong understanding. Emotional depth around fears of bad numbers and the wife conversation remains light.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session stays focused on blood sugar monitoring from start to finish with progressive momentum. The diet detour is handled as an extension of the same self-management focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative; the agenda genuinely emerges from Marcus's own contributions (evening anchor, ranges question, soda idea). Frequent 'What comes to mind for you?' probes shape the session jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with statements like 'it's yours to shape at your pace' and offering choices (asking doctor vs. learning together) that Marcus controls. No pressure around diet or the wife conversation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from Marcus himself through textbook evocative probes rather than lecturing; he articulates his own reasons and strategies. This is a standout strength.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus is treated as the expert ('You know your life and schedule better than anyone'), and strategies come from him. The clinician mirrors and evokes rather than instructs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation of DESIRE, ABILITY, NEED, and commitment language, deepened with skillful questions ('What do you think changed for you?'). Change talk is reflected and reinforced, closing with elaborated commitment ('I want that').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but never amplified, efficiently redirected toward change (e.g., fear of failure reframed as 'bouncing back'). A few sustain statements are left standing without a gentle reframe.
