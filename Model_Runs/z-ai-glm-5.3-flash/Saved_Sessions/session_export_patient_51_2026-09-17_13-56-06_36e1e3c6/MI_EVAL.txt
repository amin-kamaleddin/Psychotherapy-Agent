# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Polite, nonjudgmental tone, but warmth feels formulaic; the counselor fails to acknowledge Ingrid's time pressure or affirm her proactive safety-seeking when she says she must dash.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no reflective listening; client disclosures about the medication switch concern and her time constraint are never reflected, and responses are purely informational.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Stays on topic with the client's medication concern, but no collaborative goal or MI momentum develops, and the repetitive safety monologues during the closing exchange reflect poor session management.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is a one-way information exchange with no joint agenda-setting or elicitation of the client's own ideas about managing the medication transition.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately defers to the prescriber without coercion, but autonomy is supported only implicitly, with no explicit affirmations of client ownership or permission-asking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no effort to draw out the client's own motivations, values, or readiness; natural change talk goes unexplored and the only exploratory question is a closed factual one.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes an expert, directive role throughout; the client's contributions and offer to write things down do not visibly shape the interaction beyond direct answers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ingrid shows natural change talk about safe medication use, but the counselor never evokes, reflects, or deepens it, asking only a single closed question.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoids reinforcing sustain talk, but does not actively redirect client language toward change since almost no client exploration occurs.
