# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with genuine affirmations ('you should feel proud') and non-pathologizing safety screening. Deducted for repetitive, near-verbatim closing recaps that felt scripted rather than personally attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate ('that constant grind would leave anyone wiped out'), but the counselor quickly pivoted to solutions and checklists. The emotional core of Jordan's hopelessness, loneliness, and shame was acknowledged only briefly and never explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on financial stress and the landlord issue, moving logically from distress to safety to a concrete task and commitment. Slight deduction because the task was never connected back to Jordan's larger stated desire to 'not feel stuck anymore.'

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing in practice: Jordan chose the target, payment split, communication medium, and timing, and the text was drafted in his preferred tone. Falls short of 5 because the counselor proposed the plan structures (checklist, breathing protocol, reward) rather than consistently eliciting Jordan's own strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support through repeated choice offerings (options A/B, text vs. call, tone preference) and respect for Jordan's refusal of a scheduled check-in without pushing. Closing safety instructions were somewhat repetitive and directive, though appropriate given context.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain: the counselor relied heavily on offering solutions, scripts, and tips rather than open questions drawing out Jordan's own motivations, hopes, and past successes. Jordan's change talk emerged spontaneously and was channeled into tasks rather than evoked and expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices that honored Jordan's input, but slid into an expert/task-manager role with detailed step-by-step plans and limited invitation for Jordan's ideas. The counselor rarely explicitly acknowledged Jordan as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan progressed from wishful statements ('I wish something would go right') to concrete commitments ('I'll send it tomorrow morning') and self-generated insight ('it's more than I did last month'). However, the counselor rarely amplified or reflected this change talk back and did not deliberately elicit confidence or hope language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not reinforce Jordan's pessimism and delivered one effective reframe (framing the task as a 'tiny experiment — not a promise it'll fix everything'). However, no double-sided reflections paired sustain talk with Jordan's desire for change, and some sustain talk was simply bypassed.
