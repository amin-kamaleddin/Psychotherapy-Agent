# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone with effective affirmations ('You did that calmly — nice work') and validation of Tariq's frustration. Warmth was occasionally brief and task-focused rather than deeply personal, keeping it from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflections accurately captured the patient's emotional experience ('I hear how frustrated and hurt you are...'). Deeper exploration of the underlying anxiety about release and separation from family was limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on the target behavior — managing frustration to regain phone contact — with a concrete plan established. Deducted slightly because the early agenda (supervisor/grievance route) was dropped without explicit negotiation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration in role-play and planning, including co-developing the fallback line and timing. The power balance tilted when the counselor handed over a polished script rather than inviting the patient to build his own words first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered meaningful choices ('Which option feels most useful?', 'Would you like me to play the officer or coach you?'), and the patient made key decisions. Advice around the script was fairly directive and didn't explicitly affirm his ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and open questions ('What might trip you up tomorrow?') drew out useful patient insight and his own motivations ('My mom's worth more than winning some argument'). Reliance on offering solutions and coaching outweighed consistent evocation of his values and ideas for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for the patient's input and offered choice of role-play format, supporting partnership. The stance shifted toward expert-driven coaching with scripts and detailed instructions, and the patient's own expertise was invited only late in the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective confidence scaling (rising from 5 to 8) and space for self-generated motivation ('I can hold it together for one phone call'). Momentum was largely driven by the coaching agenda rather than deepened with evocative questions about why the call matters.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about the officer and unfairness was not reinforced and was briefly validated before redirecting. However, little strategic softening occurred — no double-sided reflections or reframing of blame toward the patient's own agency — so sustain talk was sidestepped rather than resolved.
