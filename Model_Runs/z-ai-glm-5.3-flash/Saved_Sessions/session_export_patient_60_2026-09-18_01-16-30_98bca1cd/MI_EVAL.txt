# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Showing unconditional positive regard and respect for the patient's autonomy. The clinician avoids judgment and affirms the patient's perspective throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reflects the patient's feelings accurately, including frustration, stakes around the internship, and social pressures. Reflections demonstrate deep understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully steers the conversation toward change talk about protecting the record and reducing risk, while following the patient's lead. Focus stays on what matters to the patient.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is explicitly framed as the patient's own ('your call, your plan'). The clinician acts as a partner rather than an expert directing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes the patient's choice and control, e.g., 'That's you talking, not me' and 'handled it your way.' No pressure or persuasion is used.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits the patient's own motivations and strengths (chem comeback, internship goals) through open questions. Change talk emerges naturally from the patient rather than being supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions invite shared problem-solving ('part of what we could work on together here'). The tone is side-by-side rather than directive.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effectively draws out desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment talk ('not putting myself in the crosshairs,' 'it was my choice'). The patient articulates a concrete plan he designed himself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses reflective responses to neutralize sustain talk about unfairness and peer pressure without arguing. The clinician 'comes alongside' ambivalence ('both things are true, you don't have to pick one').
