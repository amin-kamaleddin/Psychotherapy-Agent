# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirmed the patient's strengths (resilience, problem-solving) and autonomy throughout. Affirmations were genuine and specific, and the patient was repeatedly positioned as the expert on her own life.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflected both the difficulty of the situation and the patient's ambivalence in her own words. Reflections captured emotional content, such as the fragility of her childcare setup and fear of being a burden.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintained a clear focus on building practical childcare solutions (neighbor arrangement, Head Start). Every exchange moved toward identifying concrete, achievable steps without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently invited the patient's own ideas ('what do you imagine would help most?') rather than offering advice. Solutions were generated by the patient, with the clinician serving as a collaborative sounding board.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient was reinforced as the decision-maker throughout, e.g., 'You know yourself well' and 'You did that yourself.' The clinician never prescribed a course of action but supported the patient's self-generated plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicited change talk through open questions about goals and meaning ('what would it mean for you if you got clear answers?'). The patient voiced strong motivation, hope, and personally meaningful reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was markedly conversational, with the patient elaborating freely and doing most of the talking. Question-answer sequences gave way to shared exploration, and the patient even verbalized feeling 'ready'—a sign of strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician used open questions, reflections, and affirmation to draw out extensive change talk about desire, ability, reasons, and commitment. The patient articulated specific intentions (Thursday call, weekend conversation with neighbor) that the clinician reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of rejection, exhaustion, history of avoiding calls, past slips with drinking) was met with reflective validation rather than argument or reassurance. The clinician acknowledged ambivalence as normal and let the patient develop her own counter-responses and buffers.
