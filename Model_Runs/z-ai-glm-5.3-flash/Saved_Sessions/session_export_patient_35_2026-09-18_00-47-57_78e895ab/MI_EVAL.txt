# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering unconditional positive regard throughout. The psychiatrist reframed defensiveness as protective love, countered guilt without judgment, and explicitly honored Elena's conditions ('no lecture, either way').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate, deep reflective listening that captured core meanings, e.g., 'those cigarettes are one of the few things that belongs just to you.' Reflections went beyond content to underlying emotions like guilt, exhaustion, and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking and its link to her son's health while every exchange moved the conversation forward — from ambivalence to a concrete experiment and planning for her highest-risk day (Thursday). The closing check about potential obstacles demonstrated deliberate directional skill.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative: ideas were presented as bids for reaction ('Which of those sounds like you, even a little?') and the plan was co-created rather than prescribed. The final 'Deal?' exchange formalized an equal, negotiated commitment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with explicit statements like 'I'm not here to take that away from you' and 'that's completely your call.' Elena's disclaimer ('that's not me quitting') was accepted without pushback, and her closing 'on my terms' confirms choice landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A model of evocation — the psychiatrist never argued for change but drew motivation from Elena herself, whose own words carried the motivational weight (the 'long exhale' parallel, the Thursday plan, the unprompted commitment to report honestly).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was repeatedly located in Elena ('You know your life and your routines better than anyone'), and her contributions genuinely shaped the plan. Her closing comment ('this felt different') confirms the power-sharing landed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation of change talk via open questions that drew out Elena's own motivation ('maybe if quitting felt less impossible,' 'I could maybe try that once'). These moments were selectively reinforced and translated into a concrete commitment plus proactive planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently met with acceptance rather than argument and gently pivoted toward change (reframing 'not quitting' as caring for her son and herself). The minor limitation was occasionally reflecting sustain talk back in full rather than strategically downplaying it — defensible MI practice but not maximal pivoting.
