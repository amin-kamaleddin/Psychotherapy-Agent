# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor is polite and nonjudgmental, normalizing slips and reframing the plan as an experiment. However, warmth feels formulaic, with little specific affirmation of Camila's honesty or courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; major emotional disclosures (fear of being the 'boring one,' 'that felt weird just to say') go unreflected. Her final vulnerable admission that drinking to meet people 'isn't even working' received no empathic response.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on alcohol reduction—assessment, cut-down plan, refusal scripts, high-risk planning, weekend limits. Focus is consistent, though it favors logistics over collaboratively clarifying values and goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Collaboration is superficial—multiple-choice menus and transactional questions replace co-construction. Camila does most of the integrative thinking while the counselor dispenses tactics.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options are offered and Camila shapes key plan elements (weekday abstinence, 4-drink weekend limit). But advice is delivered rapidly in prescriptive bullet lists with little emphasis on her ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Almost no effort to draw motivation from within—the counselor never asks what cutting back means to her or why it matters. Motivation-rich material (missing the gym, texting her ex, the drinking not working) was offered by the patient and left unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction becomes expert-driven, with plans and scripts delivered as bullet-point menus. Camila's own ideas (weekday abstinence, driving herself, telling Dana) are raised by her rather than elicited or built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Camila offers spontaneous change talk ('I can actually commit to that,' 'I miss the gym') but the counselor neither evokes nor deepens it. No open questions about reasons for change, importance/confidence scaling, or elaboration of her motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant sustain talk ('I don't think I'm an alcoholic,' 'my social life would disappear,' 'a lot of rules just to have fun') is bypassed rather than addressed. No double-sided reflections, reframing, or validation while pivoting toward change.
