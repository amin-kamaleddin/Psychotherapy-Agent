# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated the client's overwhelm, even normalizing his external motivation. However, the repetitive closing instructions felt slightly patronizing, and warmth remained somewhat generic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections acknowledged the client's overwhelm, but deeper feelings—diabetes as a 'second job', wanting people 'off my back'—were never explored with emotional depth. Relied on scripted-feeling advice rather than complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on one concrete target behavior (pillbox by the coffee maker) throughout the session. A specific follow-up check-in was established; direction was arguably over-maintained but clearly met.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The 'experiment' framing and option choices showed partial collaboration, but the counselor supplied nearly all content and structure. Repetitive instruction-giving at the end signaled a one-directional, expert-driven dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice ('You're in charge', 'No alarm, no extra reminders') and normalized failure. However, re-prescribing the same plan repeatedly without re-checking the client's preferences slightly constrained perceived choice late in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied by counselor structure rather than drawn from the client; few open questions explored his reasons, hopes, or confidence. Client-offered change talk was not evoked further—a significant missed opportunity given his precontemplative presentation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and incorporated the client's ideas (coffee maker, fridge cue), partially honoring his expertise. But the overall stance was expert-driven, with the counselor generating options and repeating instructions five times, limiting the client's role in shaping the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The small-step approach elicited modest change talk ('maybe my numbers might settle down', 'it'd be kinda nice if something felt doable'). Missed chances to deepen these moments—pivoting back to instructions instead of exploring them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('no promises', 'so everyone gets off my back') was acknowledged without reinforcement, and external motivation was normalized. However, no double-sided reflections were used to actively soften ambivalence and redirect toward change.
