# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations ('That's a meaningful first step'), and Natalia explicitly thanked him for not lecturing her. However, warmth was somewhat formulaic, with frequent pivots to checklists and instructions rather than deep, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured her situation and ambivalence, including her protective morning ritual and difficult evenings. Emotional depth was limited, though—missing chances to explore her loneliness as a solo parent, fear of failure, and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with no topic drift. Every exchange moved toward a concrete action plan: triggers identified, 4 mg gum selected, Thursday set as the target day, coping line, relapse plan, and Friday check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan emerged from Natalia's stated constraints (solo parenting, couch time, lighter Thursday) and the check-in format was negotiated with her. However, instructional monologues on gum technique and dosing were delivered expert-to-novice rather than co-explored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced her ownership through menu options rather than directives, framing the plan as 'an experiment, not a verdict.' Natalia directly confirmed its effectiveness: 'Like I'm actually choosing this instead of being pushed.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively anchored the plan to Natalia's own values and elicited her commitments and motivational language ('it's one evening, and for him, I can do one evening'). Evocation was front-loaded, however, fading mid-session as practical instructions took over.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited her input and her choices genuinely shaped the plan (couch breathing, gum, Thursday/Friday timing). Yet the counselor frequently slipped into an expert/instructor role with detailed NRT dosing and structured lists, diluting the sense that expertise resided within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions successfully evoked Natalia's own commitments ('Thursday. I'll actually do it', 'maybe Friday too') tied to protecting her son. Some evocation relied on menu-listing, and her value statement about not wanting to be 'the mom who always smelled like smoke' was not amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged her need to cope ('cigarettes do both. That makes sense') before pivoting to doable options, and the 'experiment, not a verdict' framing softened her fear of failure. Double-sided reflections were not used, and sustain talk was at times simply moved past rather than strategically reframed.
