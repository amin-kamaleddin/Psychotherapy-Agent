# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with personalized affirmations of Mateo's effort and honesty. His fear of being 'written off' was treated as legitimate, though affirmations occasionally felt slightly effusive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including a double-sided reflection capturing Mateo's full ambivalence and unspoken shame. Reflections consistently moved beyond content to meaning, mirroring his own words and emotional shift from dread to agency.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on Mateo self-advocating with rehab therapists about pacing and pain, with every exchange advancing that goal. No topic drift; open questions guided momentum without steering toward predetermined content.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor operated as a genuine equal partner — the advocacy script was entirely Mateo's language and the plan was co-constructed from his ideas. Success criteria were defined by Mateo, not the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change was consistently framed as Mateo's own ('Thursday sounds like it's yours to shape now'), with every next step offered as a choice. No advice-giving, persuasion, or pressure anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All substantive ideas — pacing, the script, the card, wife's support — originated from Mateo, with questions drawing out his own reasoning rather than supplying content. The counselor explicitly affirmed that motivation and expertise resided with the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power sharing was actively fostered, with Mateo positioned as the expert from the outset and his contributions directly shaping the plan. Asking rather than telling at every decision point kept expertise clearly with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength: change talk was elicited and deepened at every turn, producing strong commitment language ('I'm committed to this,' 'I'm not going anywhere'). The counselor strategically reflected and reinforced Mateo's self-perceptions of capability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced; double-sided reflections acknowledged barriers while pivoting toward change, and fear of quitting was reframed as commitment. Mateo's language shifted almost entirely toward change by the session's end.
