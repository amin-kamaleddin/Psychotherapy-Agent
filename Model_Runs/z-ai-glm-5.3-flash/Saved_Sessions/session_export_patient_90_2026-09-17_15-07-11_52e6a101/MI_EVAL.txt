# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming, with respectful acceptance of the dog boundary. Affirmations were somewhat generic and deeper validation of the patient's emotional exhaustion was limited, keeping it short of a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections (e.g., 'stress tightens your chest') and acknowledgment of the dog boundary. Much of the session favored problem-solving over understanding, missing chances to reflect the patient's overwhelm, shame, and his wife's fear at an emotional level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on asthma management, consistently returning to inhaler adherence, refills, and monitoring without being derailed by the dog boundary. Occasionally became a checklist of logistics rather than deeper exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong menu-asking early ('Which feels most doable?') with genuine co-creation of the plan. The back half became directive, with rapid-fire advice, a ghostwritten text, and pharmacy scripts while the patient mostly said 'yeah.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Honored the patient's choices (alarm strategy, cue time, declining the written text for his wife). However, the style was advice-dense with few explicit choice-affirming statements and no permission-seeking before giving advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective confidence ruler and future-visualization questions drew out commitment language and reasons for change, but spontaneous change talk ('something's gotta give,' ER fear) went unexplored. Motivation was harvested rather than deliberately evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for the patient's input and let his answers shape the plan. Frequently slipped into an expert role with unprompted options and detailed tips, implying expertise resided mainly with the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and 'what would you notice' questions effectively elicited commitment language ('one week, I can commit'). Key change talk like 'my asthma's been bad enough that I know something's gotta give' went unrefined and unreinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectfully sidestepped the dog issue and validated hassle concerns without reinforcing sustain talk. Lacked active reframing or skillful redirection of sustain talk toward change.
