# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout; patient noted the non-lecturing tone and counselor expressed consistent positive regard. Affirmations were somewhat generic ('Nice choice,' 'Great') rather than individualized to the patient's strengths and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections, but most turns focused on logistics and checklists rather than deep reflective listening. The counselor rarely explored the patient's ambivalence, attachment to the coffee ritual, or underlying skepticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the jitters target and progressed logically to a concrete, time-limited plan with follow-up measures. Fell short of a 5 because the plan was never connected to the patient's larger health concerns (e.g., blood pressure) or original reason for attending.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered options as a menu and let the patient actively co-design the plan, including suggesting his own addition (afternoon tracking). Collaboration was uneven, with counselor turns often long monologues stacked with suggestions and the scaling formula feeling somewhat scripted.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly and explicitly reinforced the patient's choice and ownership ('It's completely up to you') and normalized slips without pressure. The patient confirmed the lack of pressure helped, making this a consistently strong demonstration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evoked confidence effectively via scaling questions (rising from 4 to 7), but the patient's own deeper reasons for change were barely explored. The most meaningful self-motivational statement ('maybe I'd trust myself with bigger changes') emerged spontaneously but was not deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently solicited the patient's input and let his answers shape the plan's specifics. Counselor turns were long and multi-question, sometimes taking the agenda-setting lead rather than genuinely co-constructing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions effectively evoked change talk, with confidence rising from 4 to 7 and clear commitment language ('I'll give it a shot'). Change talk was channeled into one small behavior, missing chances to deepen the patient's own reasons or connect to his health concerns.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argued with or reinforced sustain talk, instead pivoting to small experiments and reflecting it neutrally ('That makes sense'). A 5 would require strategic double-sided reflections linking sustain talk to the patient's stated health concerns to build discrepancy.
