# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance toward Timur's criminal history and ambivalence, with warm affirmations of his progress. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and tied mainly to task compliance rather than deeper unconditional regard for him as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurately captured the core ambivalence and fear of being treated like a criminal, but reflections were brief and quickly followed by advice. Deeper emotional content—shame, self-label of laziness, and quiet hope—went largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior, with every turn tied to a concrete first step (visiting the community centre). The session progressed logically from importance to confidence to contingencies to commitment, with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine joint problem-solving on logistics and backup plans, but the counselor increasingly co-opted the content by writing texts, one-liners, and step-by-step instructions. Timur's pushback ('I'm not stupid') suggests the help felt patronizing rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed actions as Timur's choice, and he exercised genuine choice—declining drafts, choosing to walk, deciding when to send the text. Falls short of 5 because persistent offers of scripts and reminders despite repeated refusals subtly pressured compliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and open probes elicited useful insights, including Timur's realization about Fiona waiting at the door. However, motivation was evoked mainly around appointment logistics, leaving his identity struggle and deeper values largely untapped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial power-sharing through permission questions and input on logistics, but the interaction became increasingly expert-driven with drafted texts and contingency plans. Timur's comment 'you've written enough for me today' signals he felt over-scaffolded.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked change talk through importance/confidence scaling and commitment invitations, drawing out meaningful language like 'maybe now' and 'better odds than I've given anything in a while.' Evocation was heavily task-oriented, with less exploration of Timur's own deeper reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor wisely did not amplify Timur's sustain talk about mates' money, laziness, and fear of rejection, but also rarely engaged it strategically. No double-sided reflections or validation of doubts; ambivalence was sidestepped rather than softened.
