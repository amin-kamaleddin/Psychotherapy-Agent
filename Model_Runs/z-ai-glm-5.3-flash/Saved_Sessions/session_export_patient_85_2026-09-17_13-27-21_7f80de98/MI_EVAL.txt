# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout; validated frustration and affirmed efforts; safety screening was respectful and non-alarming. Warmth is somewhat formulaic — endings repeat nearly identical script-like advice blocks, reducing individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Good reflective listening early on; accurately reflected discouragement after weight regain and acknowledged "favorite comforts are the hardest to change." Tracked emotional shifts from frustration to hope. Weaker moments: near-verbatim repeated advice near the end; did not explore emotional weight of past "failure" or self-criticism in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☒ 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on the target behavior (juice consumption/weight habits) from the start. Each turn ties back to the concrete plan; session progresses logically from exploration to a specific, measurable action plan with a Thursday check-in. Strong directional skill.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Invitational language and check-ins before moving forward; final plan integrates Marco's own ideas with counselor suggestions. Collaboration is genuine but uneven — pre-packaged strategy lists rather than joint brainstorming tilt the exchange toward the counselor's agenda at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly frames choices as Marco's ("Would you like to explore...?", "Pick a day/time that works"). No pressure or coercion; ownership affirmed. Large unsolicited advice menus in nearly every turn somewhat undercut autonomy by positioning the counselor as the source of solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☒ 1 ☐ 2 ☒ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Motivation resided within Marco (he arrived with confidence of 10); plan choices and confidence were elicited indirectly. Minimal deliberate evocation: no open questions about his reasons for change, no scaling question posed, no values exploration, no commitment statements elicited. Much of the content is counselor-provided advice rather than client-generated motivation drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☒ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Partnership conveyed at times ("make a plan together"), but the predominant pattern is counselor-generated strategy lists. Marco independently proposed the most impactful ideas (diluting, wife not buying juice), yet the counselor did not explicitly affirm Marco as the expert on his own life or ask what he thought would work before offering options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Marco produced substantial change talk; counselor responded well by inviting one change, reinforcing the plan as "solid, realistic," and anchoring a concrete check-in. Missed opportunities to deepen change talk — did not ask what keeps him motivated, did not explore his confidence of 10, did not elicit explicit commitment language. Marco generated much of the change talk himself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☒ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** When Marco said "boy, do I love that OJ," the counselor validated but immediately pivoted to a six-item strategy menu. Sustain talk was briefly acknowledged rather than reflected, amplified, or strategically resolved; no double-sided reflection linking his love of OJ to his desire to lose weight. Rapid pivot to advice limited exploration of ambivalence.

---

### Overall Global Ratings Score: **3.8/5**

**Summary:** Solid MI fundamentals — strong direction, good empathy and acceptance, consistent autonomy-supportive language, and a concrete, client-shaped plan. Key growth areas: rely less on unsolicited advice menus and evoke solutions from the client; use double-sided reflections around ambivalence; deepen change talk (confidence scaling, values, commitment language); affirm the client's expertise explicitly; and vary/personalize reflections rather than repeating script-like advice blocks at closing.
