# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warmth and unconditional regard were consistent, with client worry and past failures validated rather than judged. Occasional effusiveness ('I love that you said...') risked feeling performative, keeping this below a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate, complex, and emotionally attuned, e.g., capturing both evening willpower fatigue and the pantry barrier. The counselor tracked subtle meaning-making shifts, such as reframing the wife's watching as support.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on diet and activity while progressing logically from barriers to a concrete, client-owned action plan with follow-up. Each counselor turn advanced the agenda without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was largely egalitarian, with the counselor seeking the client's evaluation and co-constructing steps. Occasionally the counselor led through strategically stacked question sequences, slightly reducing mutual pacing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice was imposed; the counselor consistently elicited options ('what feels like something you could actually pull off') and affirmed ownership even around setbacks. The client's closing statement that 'this feels different because it's mine' confirms successful autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from the client rather than supplied, with questions like 'What do you think made those weeks work?' evoking his own insight about structure versus willpower. All plan content originated from the client, and his confidence rose through his own reasoning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was repeatedly located in the client ('You know your family's rhythm best'), and the plan emerged entirely from Marco's contributions. His closing summary ('I figured out the structure thing myself') confirms genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully selected and amplified, from 'I want it to be different' to explicit naming ('That's real change talk, Marco'). Evocative questions produced rich commitment language and a confidence shift from six toward seven.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was reflected once then redirected toward client strengths and success evidence, which was effective. However, deliberate double-sided reflections to actively soften ambivalence were used less than optimally—doubt was more often bypassed than resolved.
