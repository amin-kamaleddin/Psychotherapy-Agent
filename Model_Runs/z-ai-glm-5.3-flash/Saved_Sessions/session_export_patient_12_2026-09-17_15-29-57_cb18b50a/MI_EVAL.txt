# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and nonjudgment throughout, including affirming the nine days as 'real strength, not failure' and respectfully handling the safety check. Warmth occasionally felt formulaic, and Amira's fear of being a burden was not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing her helplessness, skepticism, and torn ambivalence. Reflections became less frequent later as the counselor shifted to option lists, missing exploration of her deeper hopelessness and isolation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on relapse prevention throughout, with the safety assessment inserted smoothly without derailing focus. Slight deduction for drifting into logistical detail (text wording) rather than returning to the core change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Amira's input and genuinely co-created the final text from her own words and edits. However, early problem-solving (pre-written texts, coping lists) was delivered unilaterally before collaborative exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language ('no pressure,' comfort with sharing, honoring her 'just a week' limit without pushing for more). Deduction because option menus were counselor-generated rather than open-ended, subtly steering toward preferred solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good evocative questions early ('What would someone who actually stuck around do?') that drew out meaningful client material. Evocation tapered mid-session as the counselor shifted to prescribing solutions, and her core motivation ('tired of being the only one who cares') was never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership markers existed (offering tweaks, role-play, choices), but the counselor dominated with expert-generated content—three text templates and a long coping list—before Amira articulated her needs.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amira offered change talk and eventually commitment to a concrete step, which the counselor affirmed. However, change talk was not systematically deepened—no elaboration questions or confidence/importance scaling—and much of the plan's energy came from the counselor's suggestions rather than being evoked from Amira.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor never reinforced sustain talk and reframed the relapse positively. But Amira's doubt and fear of being ignored were often met with more problem-solving lists rather than double-sided reflections or strategic pivots using her own change language.
