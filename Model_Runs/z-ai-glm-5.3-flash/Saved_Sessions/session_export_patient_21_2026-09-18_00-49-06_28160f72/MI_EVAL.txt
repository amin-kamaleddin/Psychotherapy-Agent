# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms the patient's perspective and autonomy, treating her as the expert on her own life ('you know your rhythms better than anyone'). There is no judgment or criticism anywhere in the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections capture both the patient's hopes and her ambivalence accurately ('a real pull in both directions'). The clinician repeatedly demonstrates deep understanding of her energy concerns and fear of overcommitting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on finding a sustainable activity plan that fits the patient's life, moving steadily from exploration to a concrete, patient-generated plan (two nights, twenty minutes, husband as partner).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician acts as a guide rather than an authority, explicitly avoiding pushing ('this wasn't about pushing you into some big thing'). All ideas and solutions come from the patient herself, with the clinician offering questions and reflections only.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient defines success on her own terms ('keeping your word to myself'), and the clinician reinforces her choice of a small, realistic plan. Explicit support for her right to decide pace, size, and structure of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician draws out the patient's own motivations, past successes, and reasons for change ('what made it stick for you?'). Change talk is consistently elicited rather than supplied by the clinician.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are genuinely exploratory ('I'm curious what you make of it all') and the clinician defers to the patient's expertise about her routines. Decision-making is shared throughout, with the patient authoring the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions effectively evoke strong change talk, including commitment language ('I'll tell him tonight, before I talk myself out of it') and the patient articulating her own desire, reasons, and plan. The clinician responds with reflections that strengthen this talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ambivalence about low evening energy and fear of 'flaking out' is met with dual-sided reflections that validate both sides. The clinician gently reframes worries into plans without dismissing concerns.
