# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout ('I'm here to listen without judgment,' 'thanks for being honest'). Affirmations were brief and formulaic, without individualized affirmation of Matteo's strengths or honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate content summaries were present, but reflections were mostly long compound summary-question hybrids rather than emotionally attuned ones. The vulnerable disclosure about the father ('he lost a lot — my mom mostly') was never reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol in the context of the ankle injury and moved logically from patterns to a concrete plan. Repeated topic-choice offers and confidence-checks somewhat slowed the progression toward consolidating commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent permission-seeking and a genuinely co-constructed final plan built on Matteo's ideas. However, the interaction was asymmetric — multi-question stacks and counselor-generated option menus steered the client through a checklist rather than purely facilitating his problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, repeated autonomy support: 'support your choices,' frequent permission-seeking, and framing that quitting is not the goal. Matteo freely modified suggestions without pushback, but the counselor continually added its own tactics rather than reinforcing his ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions elicited valuable client-generated material (dad history, self-designed plan). But the counselor often supplied reasons and tactics itself and failed to develop the richest motivational material — the father — with deeper follow-up.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good permission-seeking and choice-offering showed respect, but the counselor repeatedly offered pre-packaged option menus instead of drawing out Matteo's ideas first. Matteo generated most of the best strategies himself, suggesting the counselor could have trusted his expertise more.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of importance/confidence rulers and evocative questions produced meaningful change talk and a commitment-level statement, with confidence rising from 6 to 8. Fell short of 5 because the dad-related motivation wasn't deepened and option menus sometimes pre-empted Matteo's own ideas.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it's normal,' 'my friends all do this') was acknowledged respectfully and paired with change talk via double-sided reflections. Occasionally sustain content was re-listed in summaries without redirect, and ambivalence wasn't explicitly affirmed while steering forward.
