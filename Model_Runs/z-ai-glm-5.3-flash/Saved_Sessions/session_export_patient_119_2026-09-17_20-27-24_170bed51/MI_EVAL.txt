# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, with no judgment even when the client minimized her condition. The client explicitly noted 'you not lecturing me' and 'nobody's done that before.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Deep, accurate reflections such as reframing avoidance as self-protection, which the client confirmed with 'I never thought of it like that.' Reflections consistently captured both content and emotion, moving the client forward.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on medication adherence, progressing logically from routine gap to strategy, barriers, support, refill, and consolidation of learning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The entire plan originated from the client's own ideas, with the counselor offering suggestions as possibilities rather than prescriptions. The counselor also responded directly to the client's request for help with the refill.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced ownership and choice ('That's all you,' 'You've got everything you need'), and returned responsibility to the client even when asked to help with the refill. The client's closing remarks reflect internalized self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client, who generated all strategies, articulated her own reasons, and offered strong self-motivational statements like 'I'm not her, I get to do it different.' The counselor never argued for adherence or cited medical risks.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert on her own life, and the client recognized this ownership ('The coffee maker thing—it's mine'). Client contributions substantially shaped the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk via open questions, exploring what made the difference, and a confidence ruler. The counselor highlighted commitment language, and the client's change talk escalated from tentative to strong self-motivational statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk, exploring the meaning behind 'it's not a big deal' rather than arguing. The question about the downside of making it a bigger deal briefly invited extended sustain talk, though it was strategically used and skillfully resolved.
