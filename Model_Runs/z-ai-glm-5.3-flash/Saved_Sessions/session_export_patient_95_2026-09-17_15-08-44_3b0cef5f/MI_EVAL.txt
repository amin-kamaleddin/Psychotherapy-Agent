# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm,  nonjudgmental  stance  throughout,  with  Maria  explicitly  noting  'You're  not  rushing  me...  That's  rare.'  However,  affirmations  were  somewhat  generic  and  missed  chances  to  deeply  affirm  her  courage  in  voicing  suicidal  thoughts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid  reflective  listening  captured  her  exhaustion,  pain,  and  themes  like  'worksheets  feel  hollow,  and  what  matters  is  your  sister.'  Reflections  were  sometimes  brief  and  checklist-like,  and  the  emotional  weight  of  her  shame  and  despair  was  not  deeply  reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained  clear,  appropriate  focus  on  safety  and  crisis  management,  moving  toward  a  concrete  micro-plan  (text  to  sister,  grounding  pause).  Session  was  heavily  weighted  toward  immediate  safety  logistics  rather  than  connecting  to  broader  change  goals  or  reasons  for  living.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked  permission  before  questions,  co-constructed  the  text  message  in  Maria's  own  words,  and  handed  over  the  agenda  ('What  would  you  like  me  to  do  right  now?').  Short  of  5  because  the  counselor  often  supplied  options  and  drafts  rather  than  eliciting  Maria's  own  ideas  first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently  framed  everything  as  Maria's  choice  and  honored  her  rejection  of  worksheets  and  grounding  without  pushback.  Persistent  option  menus  sometimes  felt  like  controlled  choice  architecture  rather  than  open  ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Key  evocative  moments  —  asking  what  calms  her  led  to  her  discovering  her  own  coping  resources  ('I  didn't  realize  those  were  things  until  you  asked').  Much  of  the  session  relied  on  counselor-generated  options  and  safety  checklists  rather  than  drawing  out  Maria's  own  reasons  for  living.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Explicitly  positioned  Maria  as  decision-maker,  and  her  edits  genuinely  shaped  the  plan  (modifying  the  text  to  her  own  words).  Rapid-fire  option  menus  felt  scripted  and  service-provider-like,  with  little  acknowledgment  that  Maria  holds  expertise  about  her  own  suffering.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited  meaningful  change  talk  —  Maria  identified  coping  resources  and  made  a  commitment  ('I'll  try  the  text  thing.  Tonight').  Did  not  consistently  deepen  this  with  strategic  reflections  or  evocative  questions,  moving  quickly  to  safety  logistics  rather  than  amplifying  her  reasons  for  living.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did  not  argue  with  or  reinforce  sustain  talk,  and  continued  engaging  rather  than  panicking  when  Maria  restated  suicidal  thoughts.  Little  active  softening  —  no  double-sided  reflections  holding  both  her  despair  and  her  connections,  and  sustain  talk  was  met  with  pivots  rather  than  gentle  reframing.
