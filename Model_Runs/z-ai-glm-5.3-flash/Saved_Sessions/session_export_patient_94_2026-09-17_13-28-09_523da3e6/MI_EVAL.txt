# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental toward a rushed, skeptical client, with affirmations like 'Thanks — I appreciate the directness' and 'you did well.' Warmth remained somewhat brief and formulaic, missing a chance to affirm her courage in admitting the hour-long rescue inhaler delay.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validated her skepticism and time pressure accurately, but reflections were sparse. Key disclosures ('I always downplayed it because I didn't want the lecture') received no emotional validation, only a pivot to the next tip.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on asthma/inhaler adherence despite deflection attempts ('I don't have anxiety'), progressing logically to a concrete appointment plan. Early direction was somewhat mechanical, with momentum depending heavily on Maria's own disclosures.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative elements like menu-based options and script tailoring offers, but the plan was largely counselor-generated and delivered to her rather than co-created. Repeatedly pushing the same script after initial decline undermined the expert-partner balance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly affirmed her choice ('It's your body and time') and accepted her refusal of rehearsal without protest. Occasionally edged toward persuasive education rather than fostering intrinsic ownership, and her successes weren't framed as her own accomplishments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged mainly in response to factual risk information rather than skillful open questions ('What would make this worth it for you?' was never asked). The script questions did position her own voice as the vehicle for change, which earns some credit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and elicited preferences, but much of the session was counselor-led information delivery, and Maria's research/knowledge wasn't substantively integrated. Her ideas (e.g., being on her phone all day) could have shaped the plan more directly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong change talk emerged ('maybe I'll surprise you and actually start the inhaler') but was never explicitly reinforced, deepened, or reflected back. No confidence/scale questions were used, and evocation was largely incidental to information-giving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk and effectively defused resistance through validation ('Totally reasonable to feel wary'). However, no double-sided reflections or strategic pivoting were used — ambivalence was bypassed rather than actively worked with.
