# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, affirming Daniel's question and framing change as a low-risk experiment rather than demanding abstinence. However, explicit affirmations of his honesty and insight were largely absent, keeping warmth somewhat clinical.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate at key moments (e.g., validating his surprise and pride in his running) but followed a formulaic validate-then-pivot-to-information pattern. Missed opportunities to deepen emotional understanding of his ambivalence and attachment to beer as a coping mechanism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained unwavering focus on the alcohol-reduction goal with no topic drift. Every exchange moved concretely toward a fully specified, measurable 4-week experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed from Daniel's own resources (Garmin, tempo loop, NA beers, notes app). Collaboration dipped when the counselor shifted into expert-lecture mode and supplied pre-made option lists rather than brainstorming jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Daniel's choice ('you don't have to quit,' framed as a self-directed experiment), producing strong autonomy language ('Data decides, not feelings'). Slightly constrained by directive structure, pre-formulated menus, and rapid-fire question stacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some client motivation (confidence ratings, his own rationale and weak-spot identification), but much of the persuasive weight came from counselor-delivered physiology lectures rather than evoked values. Missed chances to explore meaning behind his training goals and amplify his change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clearly positioned Daniel as the expert, asking which options felt doable and letting his ideas shape the plan. Occasionally defaulted to expert-mode information delivery and pre-formatted choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supported meaningful change talk through confidence ratings and planning questions, but relied heavily on structured tools rather than open evocation. Missed opportunities to deepen motivations when Daniel volunteered them ('the numbers thing sold me').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk skillfully without arguing, using the 'test, not punishment' reframe to convert skepticism into engagement. Some minimizing statements (weekends don't count) were acknowledged but left unexplored rather than gently resolved.
