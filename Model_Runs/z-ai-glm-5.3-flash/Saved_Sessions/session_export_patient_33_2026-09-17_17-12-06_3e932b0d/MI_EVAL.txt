# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a nonjudgmental stance with unconditional support and brief affirmations, but warmth was minimal and formulaic. Missed validating the emotional weight of Diego's disclosures about the 'escape' and telling his housemate.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was sparse and shallow; the session was dominated by lists and instructions rather than reflections of meaning. Diego's rich emotional statements ('the escape stops working if everyone knows') went unreflected and unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept tight focus on harm reduction with collaboratively sequenced concrete steps and a written plan Diego left with. Deducted for narrowly behavioral targeting that never connected to Diego's broader concerns (stress, escape).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission early and offered menus of options, but the middle shifted to one-directional advice delivery of long lists and scripts. The late-session role-play was a genuinely collaborative, client-centered move, though it stood alone.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine choice offered at the outset and Diego's non-quitting stance was accepted without judgment. The latter half became prescriptive with detailed do/don't instructions and little re-affirmation of Diego's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Beyond the initial importance ruler and one open question, there was almost no effort to evoke Diego's motivations, values, or confidence. His best material ('a month ago I wouldn't have bothered') surfaced spontaneously and was never drawn out or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role, delivering instructions and checklists rather than acknowledging Diego's expertise about his own use. The interaction pattern was largely 'patient asks, counselor instructs.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Diego generated strong change talk almost entirely on his own (10/10 readiness, housemate plan), but the counselor did little to deepen or reinforce it. No evocative questions, reflections of commitment language, or affirmations tied to his stated readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Diego's clear sustain talk ('I need that escape') was neither reinforced nor skillfully softened. No double-sided reflections acknowledging the function of use; ambivalence was passed over in favor of returning to checklists.
