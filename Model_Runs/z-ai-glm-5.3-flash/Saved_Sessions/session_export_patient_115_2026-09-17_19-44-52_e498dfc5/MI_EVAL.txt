# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and non-judgment, with affirmations of Samir's efforts and validation of the cigarettes' meaning. Falls short of exemplary because some affirmations are generic and there is no explicit normalization of relapse or struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong throughout, accurately capturing both content and emotion. One notable slip: the clinician voiced Samir's own words back as if speaking in his voice ('I just reach for the pack'), and deeper feelings around his father's death went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on asthma management and progresses logically from triggers through ambivalence to a concrete plan and barriers. Lacks explicit agenda-setting and tighter structural summaries at transitions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan originates entirely from Samir (cab filters, brother, glovebox note), with the clinician reflecting and amplifying rather than prescribing. Samir's closing comment that he expected a lecture but left with a plan confirms the collaborative stance was genuinely felt.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly and repeatedly reinforced ('that's your call to make, nobody else's'), and the clinician's 'not yet' stance is honored without pressure. Menu options are offered while leaving every choice to the client, with no coercive language anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation is elicited from Samir himself—his reasons, fears, solutions, success markers, and coping strategies—rather than supplied by the clinician. His final statement ('my head's clearer than it's been in a while') demonstrates authentic, self-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located in the client ('You're the expert on your life here'), and Samir shapes the session substantially—choosing the focus, defining success, and designing his own plan. This is high-level power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and deepened across the session through questions about day-to-day differences, middle steps, and barriers. Samir responds with increasingly concrete commitments ('I could look into the cab stuff this week') and success markers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is well-handled via double-sided reflections and gentle pivots ('what would have to change for not yet to start shifting toward someday?'). However, some reflections restate the sustain position at length without an immediate pivot, and the 'not yet' ambivalence was reflected more than actively softened early on.
