# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validating Jamal's frustration and affirming his honesty. However, warmth feels somewhat formulaic and repeatedly re-asking commitment questions despite Jamal's expressed irritation shows some insensitivity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections captured Jamal's exhaustion and dual motivation, but reflections became repetitive plan summaries rather than deepening emotional understanding. Emotionally significant material like his shame about classmates watching and fatigue with conflict was never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear focus on punctuality throughout, building a concrete behavioral plan and scheduling follow-up with no topic drift. However, direction became over-focused on micro-logistics at the expense of consolidating motivation and values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements existed, but the counselor supplied most solutions rather than co-constructing the plan from Jamal's own ideas. Jamal's resistance to repeated readiness questions suggested the counselor was not fully attuned to the shared pace of the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly framed choices as Jamal's, offered options rather than directives, and reinforced his ownership of the plan. The repeated commitment-seeking questions subtly pressured rather than trusted his stated autonomy, and several plan elements were clinician-supplied.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicited meaningful change statements through open questions and a confidence ruler, but frequently shifted from eliciting to prescribing. Jamal's potentially powerful comment about not wanting everyone to look at him was never explored or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices and input requests were offered, but the session was largely counselor-driven with the plan architecture, contingency plans, and scripted responses coming from the clinician. Jamal shaped details but not direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicited some change talk through open questions and a confidence ruler, generating statements like 'For me, too' and 'I'm the one who has to deal with it.' However, these rich intrinsic motivation statements were left unexplored rather than strategically deepened or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about maternal nagging and doubts was acknowledged neutrally but never actively pivoted or softened. No double-sided reflections were used to resolve ambivalence, and the counselor shifted quickly to logistics rather than engaging with Jamal's doubts.
