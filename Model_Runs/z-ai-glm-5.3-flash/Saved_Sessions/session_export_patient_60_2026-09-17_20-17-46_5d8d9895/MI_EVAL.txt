# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout despite defensiveness, with affirming statements and a shame-free close. The inaccurate 'lowest this week' reflection imposed a narrative on the client, undermining unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflections like the 'unlucky moment' reframe and the double-sided ambivalence reflection were confirmed by the client. Deducted for the unrepaired empathic failure ('your lowest this week') and occasionally mirroring minimizations rather than deepening emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistent focus on alcohol use and the conduct incident, building progressively toward client-owned goals. The close was abrupt without a negotiated action plan, and the inaccurate crisis pivot derailed direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked with rather than on the client, visibly building on his contributions (Sunday plans, pre-party eating). Occasionally steered too visibly (the 'tailgate version' summary) and closed unilaterally rather than collaboratively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength: consistently affirmed choice ('that's your call'), never imposed advice, and attributed all ideas to the client. Especially effective in countering the coercion pressure inherent in the mandated context.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from within, moving the client from 'nothing needs to change' to self-generated commitments. Deducted for uneven early evocation and missing opportunities to explore confidence/importance and strengthen commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as the expert ('on your terms, not the school's'). Slipped occasionally into persuasive framing and assumed unwarranted expertise about his inner state.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk from a pre-contemplative client, eliciting tentative ideas, a success memory, and self-generated strategies. However, commitments were not deepened into specific plans or consolidated before session end.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively rolled with resistance without arguing, using a double-sided reflection to surface ambivalence. Some early reflections simply mirrored minimization, and the 'everyone was doing it' excuse went unreframed.
