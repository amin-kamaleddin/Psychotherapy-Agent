# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, including the affirming opening and noncritical role-play as Mads. Few individualized affirmations of Lukas's specific strengths and no normalization of his ambivalence kept this from being exceptional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections tracked both financial stress and social identity concerns (e.g., 'You're torn...'). Missed opportunities to reflect deeper emotional layers such as his embarrassment, fear of losing belonging, and vulnerability at session end.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed well-focused on rent debt, work reliability, and concrete next steps, progressing coherently from ambivalence to action. Substance use itself was largely shelved, meaning the core target behavior received less direct attention.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally framed as joint problem-solving with offer of choice and responsive role-plays, and Lukas's input shaped the plan. Counselor often pre-wrote menus and scripts rather than eliciting Lukas's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens with client choice ('You're in charge') but frequently supplies pre-packaged plans and directives. His firm boundary ('my weekends are mine') passed without affirming his control over the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and plan choices did draw out meaningful self-motivating statements and rising confidence. Evocation was often displaced by advice-giving, with little exploration of why change matters to his values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited input and role-played at Lukas's request, honoring his agency. Interaction often slipped into expert-prescriptive mode, supplying options and step-by-step actions without co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and planning prompts produced real commitments and honest self-reflection. Important change talk ('saying it out loud, it does sound worse than I thought') was not explored or amplified — the counselor pivoted to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Weakest domain: substantial sustain talk ('my weekends are mine,' 'a sober night sounds horrible') went effectively unchallenged. The response 'keeping your social life and fixing rent aren't mutually exclusive' partially validated and reinforced the status quo around weekend use.
