# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and nonjudgment with effective normalization ('feeling overwhelmed doesn't mean you're not cut out for this'). Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and generic rather than individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflections capture the client's exhaustion and self-doubt ('You're exhausted and doubting yourself — that makes total sense'). Deeper emotional content—fear of not being cut out for parenting, guilt about asking her partner—is acknowledged but not consistently explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on sleep deprivation and steadily moves toward a concrete, time-bound plan with a follow-up check-in. Slightly over-full turns with multiple suggestions and stacked questions dilute focus at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative elements exist (asking permission, offering options, tailoring scripts) but the power balance tilts expert-ward with rapid-fire advice. Functions more as consultation than equal-partner problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choice ('reassurance, a simple night strategy, or shift ideas?') with no pressure or coercion. Falls short of 5 because the framing offers a menu of the counselor's suggestions rather than reinforcing the client's own capacity to generate solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions largely come from the counselor rather than being drawn out of the client; questions are mostly informational or choice-based rather than evocative. The client's own motivations surface spontaneously but are not elicited or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission before advising, which is good practice, but the session is largely expert-driven with the counselor supplying most strategies and scripts. Rarely highlights the client's expertise as a parent or invites her own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Heavy reliance on giving advice rather than eliciting the client's own motivation; nearly every turn ends with a concrete suggestion and a closed choice question. Client-generated change talk ('I can try tonight') is not strategically evoked, reinforced, or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced and worry about handing off feeds is briefly normalized, but no double-sided reflections or deliberate reframing of self-doubt and guilt are used. The counselor tends to move straight to practical solutions rather than resolving ambivalence.
