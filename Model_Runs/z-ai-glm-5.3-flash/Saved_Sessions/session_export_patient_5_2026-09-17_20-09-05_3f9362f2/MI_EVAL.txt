# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, unconditional positive regard throughout, with her caution and thoroughness consistently affirmed as strengths ('that care you take in getting the facts right says something good about how you look after yourself'). No judgment or disapproval appears anywhere in the session, and the regard feels genuine and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including the complex reflection 'you're not refusing the medication, you're refusing to be left in the dark.' Reflections mirror both content and emotional nuance and deepen rather than repeat the client's experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on understanding and safely managing the citalopram/phenelzine situation, with every turn advancing the agenda toward concrete, committed action. There is no aimless drift; momentum builds steadily from concerns to a written question list and contact plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is fully collaborative ('let's figure this out together'), with the question list co-constructed from the client's own words. Her choices — starting with the pharmacist, adding the waiting-period question — directly shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are repeatedly framed as the client's ('wherever you land with this,' 'who feels like the right first person to ask?'), with no directive or pressuring language. Even suggestions are offered collaboratively, and her final commitment comes entirely from her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation of the client's own motivations ('what would tell you it went well?'), drawing out 'I'd rather know than not know.' However, evocation mostly confirms existing motivation rather than developing deeper change talk through values exploration or confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within the client ('that's your own wisdom, not something I gave you'), and her contributions substantially define the session's plan. Questions explicitly defer to her judgment throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and reinforces change talk ('does deciding feel possible then?' elicited 'then I could actually decide'), culminating in a firm commitment to call both pharmacist and doctor. Could be deepened with explicit confidence exploration, such as scaling questions or anticipating obstacles on the calls.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (not knowing enough, nervousness about bad news) is acknowledged but not amplified, instead reframed as strength and channeled into concrete steps. Occasional validation of worry is slightly generous, but reasons against change are never reinforced.
