# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with a resistant client, apologizing for over-questioning without defensiveness. Missed chances to offer specific affirmations after her disclosure about family arguing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and validating, including naming the client's irritation ('fed up with questions'). Missed an opportunity to reflect deeply on the emotional weight of living with the arguing, pivoting to option-offering instead.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session eventually landed on coping with household conflict, but the first half was directionless with repeated topic menus. Offered three divergent options rather than converging on the emerging goal of 'getting away'.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided an authoritarian stance and shared decision-making implicitly. However, constant multiple-choice questions shifted all conversational labor to the client, who explicitly found it burdensome ('This is a lot of questions').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced client ownership: 'You're in control here' and 'You can use me however you'd like.' No advice was forced, and options were offered without pressure throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Never lectured or persuaded, and the client eventually volunteered her own concern ('getting away from it'). Evocation was largely passive—no open questions tied to her values or hopes after this key statement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly handed control to the client, respecting her expertise. Power-sharing was passive, however, and the client's contribution ('getting away') wasn't built into a co-created plan; her 'You seriously don't give up' suggests the counselor's agenda sometimes overrode her stated 'I'm fine'.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina volunteered early change talk ('getting away from it... where do I even go'), supported by an invitation to say more. The counselor did not capitalize with evocative follow-ups about what getting away would look like or provide.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing disengagement ('I'm fine') and gently normalized without dwelling. Repeatedly mirroring ambivalence back ('you don't want to share, and that's okay') risked cementing the status quo; no double-sided reflection linked her discomfort to her desire to get away.
