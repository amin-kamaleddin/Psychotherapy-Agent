# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Brief empathic acknowledgment at the start, but no affirmations of Tyler's honesty or values; tone becomes transactional and checklist-like.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is nearly absent—one initial empathic statement followed by no reflections on Tyler's ambivalence, fears, or values; the session reads as advice-giving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session focuses on conduct-hearing prep rather than the target drinking behavior; no behavioral focus for change is ever established.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The dynamic is expert-to-client: the psychiatrist drafts statements and scripts while Tyler edits. No shared agenda-setting or joint exploration of his drinking.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional options and comfort-limiting statements are offered, but the overall stance is prescriptive—caps, sober-buddy plans, and scripts are handed to the client rather than generated by him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No open questions elicit Tyler's own reasons, confidence, or readiness; the sole evocative offer comes only after he has voiced the discrepancy himself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist assumes the expert role throughout—explaining processes, dictating statement structure, and coaching delivery—without positioning Tyler as the expert on his own drinking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tyler spontaneously voices meaningful change talk (education matters, 'I'd rather not find out the hard way again'), but the psychiatrist never reflects, elaborates, or deepens it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively amplifies sustain talk by scripting minimization lines ('not my typical pattern,' 'I don't consider myself someone with a drinking problem') that Tyler then had to correct.
