# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates consistent respect and nonjudgment, validating Kevin's annoyance at being referred and never disapproving of his drinking. Falls short of 5 due to warmth that is somewhat brief and transactional, with few individualized affirmations of his strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture Kevin's ambivalence and validate the social role of drinking (connection, stress relief, confidence). However, key emotional moments are under-reflective — e.g., moving past Kevin's fear that cutting back means 'bailing on' his friends rather than exploring it deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use throughout, anchoring on the referral and injury and progressing logically from assessment to a concrete plan. Minor drift with safety screening and generic advice, and steps could be tied more explicitly to Kevin's stated values (friendship, confidence).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly frames work as joint ('We can do a brief decisional-balance together'), seeks permission, and co-constructs the plan with Kevin's input shaping key elements. Falls short of 5 because the counselor dominates with pre-drawn menus and scripts rather than eliciting Kevin's own strategies first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary reinforcement of Kevin's ownership throughout: presenting options rather than directives, honoring his preference to try it alone, and framing slips as 'information, not failure.' Kevin's choices (skip two rounds, his own line 'I'm pacing myself') directly shaped the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The pros/cons exercise drew out Kevin's own concerns (fuzzy memories, the ankle, cost) and his eventual 'I can do that,' but much content is counselor-supplied. Confidence is never directly assessed, and the discrepancy between Kevin's embarrassment about the injury and his continued drinking goes undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses collaborative framing and permission-checking, and Kevin's contributions did influence the plan. However, power sharing is uneven — long expert-style lists (eight assessment questions, strategy menus, scripts) position the counselor as advisor, crowding out Kevin's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicits change talk through direct questioning and the decisional balance, drawing out Kevin's own concerns and commitment-adjacent language ('I can do that'). However, change talk is not deepened with strategic reflections; the counselor quickly pivots to supplying solutions instead of exploring what success or confidence would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoids arguing with sustain talk and validates its legitimacy ('Those are clear and valid reasons'). Yet the counselor sometimes amplifies sustain talk by listing pros of drinking and cons of cutting back directly, with little active pivoting back toward Kevin's own motivations.
