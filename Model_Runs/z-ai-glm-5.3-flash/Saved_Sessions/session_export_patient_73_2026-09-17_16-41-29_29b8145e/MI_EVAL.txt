# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful tone throughout with no judgment of Linda's smoking history or past failures; affirmations like 'brave step' and 'solid steps' land well. However, affirmations are somewhat generic and repeated, and the emotional weight of her fear and discouragement is not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens with an accurate complex reflection capturing the warning's impact, the social embeddedness of smoking, and the readiness/confidence gap. However, empathy stays summary-level — Linda's emotional core (fear of illness, repeated failure, 'I don't want to feel like a failure again') goes unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on cessation and narrows into a concrete, manageable first target that Linda endorses. Deduction for the second half shifting into administrative logistics repeated across three turns, trading motivational momentum for task focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A genuine two-way exchange — Linda's preferences (patch+gum, morning target, check-in format) clearly shape the plan, and permission is asked before advising. Minor deductions for the psychiatrist driving the agenda and answering her own questions with full solution lists.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks permission before sharing options, invites Linda to choose among them, and accommodates her preferences for check-in logistics and 'celebrating first.' Deductions for prescriptive strategy lists once permission was granted and no explicit reinforcement that change is Linda's choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation genuinely resides with Linda — her own reasons, plan choices, and hopeful statements — and evocative questions about past partial successes are used well. But evocation is front-loaded and under-developed; the session drifts into advice and logistics, and Linda must ask the psychiatrist to remind her of her own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently signals expertise lies with Linda, asking what feels doable and letting her preferences determine the plan. Falls short by answering her own 'what would people suggest?' question with a prescribed list and controlling call logistics without much client input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges (health, money, 'this time can be different') and rulers are used, with some reinforcement of change talk. Missed opportunities to deepen it: Linda's own reasons aren't reflected, 'what makes this time different' goes unexplored, and the confidence ruler is scored but not developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (routine, social group, fear of failure) is acknowledged without being amplified, and the psychiatrist pivots effectively to evidence of capability ('three weeks with the patch'). Pivots are somewhat abrupt — social loss and fear of failure aren't explored before redirecting, and the unrealistic six-month timeline goes unaddressed.
