# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard; ambivalence explicitly normalized ('neither is wrong'), with no labeling or disapproval anywhere in the session. Affirmations are specific and behaviorally anchored rather than generic praise.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening throughout, including complex reflections that reach beneath content to emotion (e.g., naming embarrassment about starting over weaker). Reflections consistently captured both sides of the patient's ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on exercise re-engagement from the opening question onward. Each response steered the conversation productively toward the patient's own solutions and concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced patient autonomy: 'Those are your solutions, not mine' and 'You know your patterns best.' The plan was entirely patient-generated, with the psychiatrist evoking rather than advising.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized the patient's expertise over his own life and framed choices as his own ('You know yourself best here'). The closing question about self-kindness further reinforced that regard does not hinge on perfect performance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions drew out the patient's own motivations, past successes, and solutions ('what helped you keep going?'). The patient articulated change talk and a full concrete plan in his own words.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The tone throughout was that of working alongside the patient rather than directing him. Questions invited the patient's perspective at every decision point.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Successfully evoked sustained change talk, including commitment language ('I could tell you I'd try that') and self-motivational reasoning ('twenty minutes is technically... something'). The patient left with specific commitments of his own making.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Each expression of doubt ('doesn't feel like enough,' fear of failing in front of his wife) was met with dual-sided reflections rather than argument. The patient verbally resolved his own ambivalence each time.
