# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and responds calmly to Tyler's dismissiveness without lecturing or moralizing. Affirmations are sparse and somewhat generic, keeping this below a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate at the level of content and surface feelings (frustration, wanting mom to back off). Rarely explores the deeper emotional meaning behind Tyler's need for acceptance or feeling unheard.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session stays focused but drifts: it opens on substance use, then pivots to mother-conflict communication coaching and never returns. The negotiation skills are never linked back to the substance use target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acts as an equal partner—asks what Tyler wants, co-constructs the role-play, and lets Tyler's ideas drive the plan. Frequently supplying ready-made scripts tips some exchanges toward expert instruction rather than joint discovery.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers explicit choice (agenda, role-play vs. coaching, which scenario to practice) and accepts Tyler's refusal to set any substance-use goal without pressure. No coercive or persuasive language at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Early evocative questions ('what could go sideways?', 'anything that worries you?') show good intent, but Tyler's deflections are not pursued or deepened. Motivation for change regarding substance use itself is never elicited; rulers, values exploration, or looking-forward questions are unused.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions Tyler as the expert—menu-based options and Tyler's choices genuinely shape the session agenda. Falls short of a 5 because transitions are slightly leading and Tyler's own expertise about his substance use is never tapped.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk about the target behavior is essentially absent and not pursued; openings from risk questions are dropped when Tyler deflects. The only change-adjacent language ('maybe it's worth it') concerns mother negotiation, not substance use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with sustain talk and doesn't reinforce it, but also doesn't actively soften it. Misses chances for double-sided reflections and lets the 'everyone does it' minimization pass without gentle exploration.
