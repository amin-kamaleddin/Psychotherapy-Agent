# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates warmth and unconditional positive regard, responding to Nabil's vulnerable admissions without judgment and honoring both sides of his ambivalence. He never labels or shames Nabil for his hostility toward staff.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and layered, including the double reflection on anger masking a desire to be heard and precise reflections of Nabil's own words. Each response builds on prior statements, including subtle reflections on the 'strange comfort' of the panic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains progressive focus on anger in medical settings, moving from trigger to internal mechanism to values to a concrete strategy. Guiding questions like 'which one lights the fuse first?' direct without coercing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a genuine joint inquiry, using curious, invitational language and positioning the counselor as a companion in discovery. Strategies emerge from Nabil's own insights, and his reactions confirm he experiences the session as interactive and self-directed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are consistently framed as Nabil's, with no prescriptive advice or pressure. Minor deductions for the closing question subtly steering toward two counselor-flagged options and a phrase edging toward persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited from within Nabil—his business skills, his own observations, and most powerfully his values about his children. The pivotal father question evoked the session's deepest motivation ('I want to be him'), which was deliberately reinforced.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor actively locates expertise within the client, building the intervention from Nabil's own professional experience and values. The 'checking with Dave' strategy came entirely from Nabil and was amplified without added prescriptions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and deepened, moving from blame to 'the anger doesn't get me seen any faster' to the desire statement 'I want to be him, I actually do.' The counselor slowed down to reinforce this commitment language and elicited concrete next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections honor complaints while pivoting to agency, and the counselor neither argues nor dwells on system blame. One point off because a few reflections give substantial airtime to the blame narrative before pivoting, though arguably necessary rapport-building with an angry precontemplative client.
