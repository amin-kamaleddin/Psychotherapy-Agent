# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental throughout, with affirmations like 'Coming here shows you want change' and validation of Rosa's sustain talk without disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate summaries captured Rosa's ambivalence and specific cues, but reflections were often followed by question batteries, and the emotional core (smoker identity, fear of failure) was not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with no topic drift; every exchange built progressively toward a fully specified action plan with a concrete Monday start date.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong joint enterprise with permission-seeking and co-construction of the plan (Rosa picked the day, dose, routine, and support person), though rapid multi-option advice blocks made some exchanges feel like guided selection rather than open co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options presented as menus ('Which sounds doable?') with Rosa's choices accepted without pushback, but the counselor missed explicitly reinforcing that the motivation and plan originated from Rosa herself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of rulers and open questions drew motivation from within ('What would move your number up?'), but deeper reasons for change were never fully explored and hope statements were met with logistics rather than amplification.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong permission-seeking and genuine incorporation of Rosa's ideas (2 mg gum, porch coffee, 'light' script), but occasional drift into expert/advice-giving mode, with Rosa twice saying 'You'd know better than me.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective ruler use moved confidence visibly from 3-4 to 7, and Rosa produced commitment and mobilization talk ('Monday, I do this'), but the counselor sometimes answered change talk with logistics rather than deepening it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fears (weight gain, withdrawal, friends' reactions) were briefly acknowledged then reframed into actionable coping plans rather than amplified, with sustain talk declining markedly as the session progressed.
