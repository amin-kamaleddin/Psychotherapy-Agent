# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental regard throughout, with specific genuine affirmations ('That takes guts to name out loud'). Ambivalence and doubts were normalized and respected rather than corrected.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently captured both content and underlying meaning (e.g., 'the worry underneath the word yet'). The counselor showed deep understanding of the practical and emotional texture of the patient's life.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session built clear momentum toward a concrete weekly plan while keeping the patient in the driver's seat. However, the final exchange reopened ambivalence exploration after the patient had already summarized her plan, circularly revisiting earlier ground instead of consolidating and closing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every step was co-constructed, with the counselor asking rather than telling and incorporating the patient's half-ideas into the working plan. The patient's words ('that's a fair challenge') confirm she was an active, equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine options were offered and the plan was explicitly the patient's ('easy, yours, this week'). The patient confirmed full ownership: 'it works because it's small and it's mine.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client via evocative questions rather than lectures or advice. The patient's own reasons emerged ('I did it,' not letting her sister down), and she noted 'hearing myself say it' made it real.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('You know yourself best'), culminating in a textbook power-sharing closing question. The entire experiment originated from the patient's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoked desire, ability, and commitment language, culminating in 'I'll text her tonight.' The counselor recognized and deepened commitment statements rather than moving on prematurely.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged honestly and reframed toward strengths without reinforcement. However, some doubts (cheating, public failure) were reflected at length, and a quicker pivot after acknowledgment would have been ideal.
