# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician maintained unconditional positive regard with a guarded, sarcastic adolescent, responding to testing ('you gonna tell my PO?') without defensiveness. No labeling, shaming, or disapproval anywhere; affirmations were genuine and specific.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were complex and accurate, consistently capturing emotional subtext (resentment of adult control, feeling the charge 'blocked the path'). Jamal's progressively warmer responses ('thanks for not just lecturing me') confirm the empathy was felt, not just performed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong directional structure linking the charge to Jamal's values (money, independence) with a logical session progression. Score is 4 rather than 5 because marijuana use and future risk were never directly explored — the focus stayed on consequences and compliance rather than the target behavior itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was a genuine exchange of equals: agenda was co-created, Jamal's ideas drove the action plan, and even the closing was negotiated around his readiness. No expert-driven advice appears anywhere.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated explicit affirmations of Jamal's ownership ('that's your call,' 'that's yours to carry, not mine') with zero pressure or moralizing. His volunteered thanks and 'maybe' commitment show he experienced genuine choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All change talk traced to Jamal's own words, drawn out via strategic future-vision, values-prioritization, and goal-setting questions. Near-exemplary evocation with a resistant, precontemplative adolescent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located within Jamal ('you know the streets and the employers around here better than I do'), and the entire action plan originated from his cousin's example. The closing credited him with doing most of the work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk escalated visibly across the session, from 'I could just ask him' to a concrete self-set goal ('I talk to my cousin... Maybe I actually apply'). Each instance was reinforced with affirmations and reflections, and the goal was returned to Jamal's ownership.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimization and resistance were never argued with; sustain talk was neutrally reflected and redirected toward Jamal's goals, letting him define acceptable terms. One missed opportunity to explore the charge's consequences more directly for discrepancy-building before moving quickly to solutions.
