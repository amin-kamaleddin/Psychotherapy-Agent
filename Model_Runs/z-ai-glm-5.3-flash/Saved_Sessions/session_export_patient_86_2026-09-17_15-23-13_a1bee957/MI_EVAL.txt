# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance maintained even during client pushback, with consistent 'test not failure' framing. However, affirmations of Marco as a person (his humor, resilience) were limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured Marco's frustration and pride, and the patch vs. ritual distinction echoed his insight well. The second half shifted to informational responses, leaving Marco's emotional relationship to smoking and his post-surgery fatalism unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear behavioral focus and converged on a concrete plan (10-minute delay experiment, urge ratings, cleaning). The mid-session information dumps (NRT, whitening, vaping) briefly diluted the momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration with negotiated agendas and a co-designed experiment the client noted felt different from prior clinicians. The clinician-led option menus and recaps, however, kept the architecture somewhat expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength: repeatedly reinforced client ownership ('support whatever choice you make'), honored his 10-minute boundary explicitly, and never pressured or extended the goal beyond what Marco set.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco's dilemma, patch interest, and self-designed experiment emerged from him, which is good. But evocation was inconsistent — heavy reliance on providing information rather than drawing out his reasons, values, or confidence; no scaling questions used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Marco as the expert, letting him select the focus and design the experiment on his terms. Prescriptive option lists at times took an informational lead rather than drawing plans primarily from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively built on Marco's own openness and evoked client-generated commitments (patch interest, 10-minute delay, urge ratings, booking a cleaning). Missed opportunities to deepen commitment language via importance/confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully rolled with resistance — accepted the 10-minute limit, framed continued smoking as 'nothing's lost,' avoided arguing. Option menus briefly amplified the difficulty frame triggering the 'boot camp' pushback, and sustain talk wasn't used in double-sided reflections.
