# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental; validated Linda's discouragement and reassured her that her six-month timeline wasn't silly. Affirmations of her strengths and persistence were sparse and formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening, accurately summarizing Linda's ambivalence and fears about failing again. Reflections were sometimes brief and quickly pivoted to advice, leaving deeper emotional content (shame, fear of the patch, social anxiety) unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on tobacco use throughout, moving purposefully from exploration to a concrete first step and follow-up plan. No topic drift; forward momentum toward change was a clear strength.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission before summarizing and offered menu-based decisions, with the plan co-selected by Linda. However, the session took an expert-prescriptive character, with Linda mostly approving rather than co-creating goals, scripts, and follow-up structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and let Linda select the delay plan, appointment time, and role-play, with Linda explicitly voicing ownership. Autonomy was somewhat undermined by dense unsolicited advice and permission-seeking only after proposing menus.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation came largely from counselor-provided information rather than being drawn out from Linda. Few open questions elicited her own reasons, hopes, or confidence, and her motivational statements ('it gives me a bit of hope') were not reflected back or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices between options, showing some power sharing. Much of the session was advice-driven, with long blocks of expert content delivered before Linda's own ideas shaped the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged ('It's for my health and the money'), but was mostly client-generated rather than skillfully elicited. The counselor rarely deepened it with evocative questions or follow-up reflections, shifting quickly to options and plans.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk and offered an effective reframe of slipping as learning rather than failure. Softening was largely passive, however—statements like smoking being 'part of my life' passed without strategic double-sided reflections or redirection.
