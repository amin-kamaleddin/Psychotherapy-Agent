# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with boundary honoring (alcohol deferral) and occasional affirmations, but positive regard is thin and formulaic, with little individualized warmth or affirmation of Aisha's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; emotionally significant disclosures like her fears for her health and her need to decompress were met with generic responses or planning advice rather than reflections of feeling or meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on evening smoking reduction and produced a concrete plan with a follow-up. Points lost because the topic emerged somewhat by accident and the fatigue/sleep concern was dropped without closure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha chose her moments, substitutes, and reward, but the clinician repeatedly delivered pre-packaged instruction lists without checking her input, making the interaction feel more like lecturing than joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong choice support: reduction rather than quitting was respected, alcohol was deferred, and the craving script was declined without pushback. Falls short of 5 due to heavy prescriptive advice implying the clinician knows best.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician defaulted to dispensing solutions rather than eliciting Aisha's own reasons and ideas. Her powerful motivators (her son, cancer fears) emerged spontaneously and were never explored or deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing through choice questions and letting Aisha pick her plan details, but the overall stance was expert-driven with the session's direction set by the clinician's checklist rather than the client's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful evocative moves (cut-down question, confidence scale) elicited strong change talk ('I want to be around for my son'), but it was never reflected back, deepened, or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and the alcohol boundary was respected, avoiding escalation. However, minimizations were simply accepted or deferred, missing chances for double-sided reflections to gently acknowledge ambivalence.
