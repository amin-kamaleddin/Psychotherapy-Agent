# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated warmth and unconditional positive regard, validating Elena's worry, tentativeness, and avoidance without any judgment or pressure. Her hesitation ('I don't really want to get into all the contraception stuff') was met with acceptance rather than disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, notably capturing Elena's ambivalence ('You're holding two truths: you dodged this time, and you're scared about next time'). However, emotional exploration stayed somewhat surface-level — the fear that 'keeps me up at night' was pivoted away from fairly quickly toward menu options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus throughout, progressing from the pregnancy scare to the fear to a concrete, actionable deliverable (the booking text). Even when Elena asked for more time, the counselor kept the target behavior in view without pressure, helping Elena recognize that leaving without something concrete would lead her to push it aside again.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated as a genuine equal partnership — Elena's answers drove the agenda at every juncture, from processing the fear to drafting doctor questions to the booking text. The counselor drafted materials at her request and invited her edits, exemplifying co-constructed problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported through repeated choice menus, 'you're allowed to take time' framing, and explicit acceptance of tentativeness ('It's okay to be tentative about booking'). The counselor never pressured or moralized, and Elena's ownership was reinforced at every step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Elena's own motivations and values ('a baby right now would just derail everything') and built self-efficacy through rehearsal. The rating is held back because the counselor occasionally led with pre-formed information and scripts rather than consistently drawing content and reasoning from Elena herself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena was repeatedly framed as the decision-maker ('What would you like from me in this moment?'), and her contributions clearly shaped the session's direction. The stance slipped momentarily when the counselor delivered a high-volume list of contraceptive options in one turn, leaning toward an information-giving expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk — the invitation to name one small, concrete step elicited 'if there was something easy... that might not be the worst thing?' and culminated in strong client statements ('moving toward this instead of running from it'). Not a 5 because the counselor sometimes supplied the change directions rather than drawing them from Elena.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I do what I can,' 'Can I just think about it?') was consistently met with acceptance and gentle redirection, never argument, and a double-sided reflection effectively softened ambivalence. Some responses simply accommodated the delay without strategic reframing toward change.
