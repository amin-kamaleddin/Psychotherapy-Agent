# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental, and supportive throughout, with affirmations and explicit normalization of setbacks. Praise occasionally feels formulaic, and warmth is sometimes crowded out by rapid advice-giving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional good reflections ('failing feels heavy'), but the counselor repeatedly bypasses emotional content to jump to strategies. The patient's fear of failure and ambivalence are never deeply explored or reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tightly focused on the target behavior from start to finish, ending with a concrete commitment (Tuesday, 10 minutes, check-in). Direction was largely counselor-driven rather than collaboratively negotiated, and moved to solutions before consolidating motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient contributed real ideas (Tuesday start, telling his wife, car-seat idea) that the counselor incorporated. Still, the overall stance was expert-driven, with the counselor dispensing plans, scripts, and checklists in nearly every turn.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong 'experiment' framing, 'no judgment' language, and affirmation of the patient's scaled-down choice, plus a low-pressure fallback option. However, most advice was prescriptive without checking whether the patient wanted it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no evocative questioning after the opening; the counselor relies on external strategies and pep talks rather than eliciting the patient's own reasons, ideas, or confidence. Motivation emerged despite, not because of, the counselor's technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked which days were realistic and adopted the patient's suggestions, but the overall dynamic remained plan-writer and recipient rather than true partners. Power-sharing was uneven throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerged and was supported by the low-pressure, scaled-down plan the patient embraced. Missed opportunities to deepen it — never exploring what feeling like himself again would mean or reinforcing his commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk was never amplified, but reframing failure and offering the backup walk lowered the stakes somewhat. Double-sided reflections and direct exploration of the fear of failing again were absent, leaving skepticism unprocessed.
