# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating David's skepticism and closing with genuine affirmation ('you did great advocating for yourself'). However, affirmations were somewhat generic and lacked individualization of David's specific strengths, and his ambivalence was never explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy was present but inconsistent—an accurate early reflection captured the core concern ('you don't want to risk feeling worse when you're okay now'). Most responses quickly pivoted to information and option menus, and the emotional core (distrust of rushed medicine, fear of weakness, family rationalizations) was never deeply explored or reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on the cholesterol medication decision across the entire session, with every response tied back to the target behavior—question list, trial script, monitoring instructions, and phone note. The session progressed logically from skepticism to a concrete, actionable plan with no drift off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative elements existed—David chose options, adjusted wording, and influenced the plan. However, the interaction was structurally counselor-led, with the counselor generating the questions, scripts, and plan while David served as approver and rehearser rather than co-creator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices rather than directives ('Which would be most helpful right now?', 'Want to rehearse that now or tweak the words?'), and the trial-period framing reinforced David's control ('it's my body'). Falls short of 5 because early responses delivered expert information before establishing choice, and the menus were counselor-constructed rather than client-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided lecturing and motivation did emerge from David himself ('I might actually fill the prescription'), but this was incidental rather than cultivated through evocative questioning. No open questions about David's own reasons for change, no confidence rulers, and no exploration of how medication fits his values (family, work, longevity).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David's voice was honored selectively ('pick a different wording that feels more like you'), and his choices did shape the plan. The counselor nonetheless retained the expert role throughout—diagnosing what David needed—without acknowledging David as the expert on his own life, body, and work demands.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively moved David from resistance ('Maybe down the line, but—') to commitment ('a trial with a real check-in? That I could live with'; 'I can do that'), aided by the concrete script and rehearsals. However, change talk was elicited through practical tools rather than deliberate evocative questions, and David's change statements were never reflected back to deepen them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David produced substantial sustain talk ('I'm not even certain I need it,' 'the whole thing's overblown') that was never reinforced, which is positive. However, there were no double-sided reflections to strategically soften it, and early listing of side-effect facts inadvertently kept attention on the risk narrative.
