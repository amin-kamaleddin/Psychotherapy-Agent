# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with no disapproval of Lars's history, literacy struggles, or drinking; however, affirmations like 'Nice' and 'solid plan' are brief and formulaic rather than deep, individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture both practical and emotional layers (e.g., paperwork as a wall, boredom and mates pulling him back), but deeper emotions — shame about reading, fear of 'ex-con' judgment, wanting to be a different dad — go unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout: every exchange ties back to reducing reoffending via education/employment, moving from vague aspiration to a specific time-bound plan (Thursday 10 appointment, forms, script, hand-in) with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is substantially shaped by Lars's ideas (20-minute task, same-day hand-in, burgers, fridge checklist), but the counselor often supplies the menu of options rather than eliciting Lars's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice is verbally affirmed, but the pace is controlling — pushing confidence scaling repeatedly, delivering the script after Lars declined to call, and structuring the plan before full agreement; Lars twice has to set his own pace ('one step at a time, yeah?').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence rulers and 'which feels most doable' questions do elicit some change talk, but much of the session relies on counselor-supplied scripts, checklists, and rewards; core themes like the mechanic dream, the kind of dad Lars wants to be, and his need for action/stimulation are never evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong partnership opening, but the session becomes increasingly directive with counselor-constructed steps, verbatim scripts, and dictated checklists; options funnel Lars toward the counselor's preferred plan rather than genuine co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of the 'biggest difference' question, confidence rulers, and 'what would raise your confidence' elicited strong change talk ('I don't wanna end up going back to prison,' 'something solid for once'); stops short of 5 because Lars's stated motivations aren't explored more deeply or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of the office, embarrassment about Donna) is acknowledged briefly then redirected to doable options without reinforcing hopelessness; however, redirection occasionally jumps to solutions rather than using double-sided reflections, and the boredom/mates ambivalence is never directly resolved.
