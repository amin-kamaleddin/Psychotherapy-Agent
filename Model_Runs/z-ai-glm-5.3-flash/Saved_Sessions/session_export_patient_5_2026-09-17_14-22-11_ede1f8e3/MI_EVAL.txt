# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and affirming of Alessia's caution ('You're right to pause', 'you handled this very responsibly') with no judgment about her near-miss. Held back from a 5 due to warmth that was somewhat brief and formulaic, with repeated closing reminders rather than deeper individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Validated Alessia's caution adequately ('I'm glad you caught it', 'That sounds like a strong plan') but reflections of her emotional experience were minimal. Missed opportunities to reflect her stated feelings of worry, relief, and responsibility, as the session stayed heavily focused on information delivery.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focus was maintained unwaveringly on the citalopram–phenelzine interaction and safe next steps, with every turn reinforcing the same concrete plan. No drift occurred; each response tightened the plan and ended with clear action steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration occurred in co-constructing the message to the prescriber, with Alessia's input shaping the draft. However, the overall pattern was expert-consultant delivering instructions rather than two equal partners exploring together, with her ideas rarely shaping the session beyond the message draft.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices where possible ('Would you like that?', 'If you'd like, I can review your doctor's reply') and affirmed her responsible handling. Safety-critical content was appropriately directive given serotonin syndrome risk, but this imperative tone left little room for emphasizing her decision-making capacity.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Never asked Alessia about her own reasons for medication safety, her confidence, or what matters most to her health. Her volunteered motivations ('safety matters to me', 'I don't want to make a mistake') were missed opportunities, met with reminders rather than evocative questions or reflections.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited collaboration at key moments ('How would you like to proceed?') and incorporated Alessia's contributions into the message draft. Much of the session remained expert-driven with imperative safety instructions, with little explicit acknowledgment that expertise about her life and decisions resides with her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** This was primarily a medication-safety consultation, with change talk not actively evoked or reinforced. Alessia generated her own commitments ('I'll call today'), but the counselor did not reflect, deepen, or build on these statements, and open questions about her reasons or confidence were absent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal given the safety-focused nature of the encounter, and the counselor appropriately did not amplify barriers. However, there was little active redirection toward client-generated motivation, with most responses pivoting to safety instructions rather than helping her articulate her own reasons for adhering safely.
