# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and never judged Elise's smoking or ambivalence. Affirmations were genuine and individualized, and her sustain talk was treated as legitimate and understandable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were highly accurate, capturing Elise's ambivalence, her fear of feeling left out, and the social awkwardness she called 'the scary part.' Listening consistently incorporated her exact concerns and emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on smoking reduction throughout, progressing logically from exploration to commitment to concrete planning. Every turn — rulers, plan, refusal script, role-play — advanced the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully collaborative, with Elise's contributions shaping the plan and the role-play driven by her request to practice the 'pushy' version. The counselor actively sought her input at every decision point and role-played the friend at her request.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All options were presented as choices with no pressure or coercion, and Elise made every decision herself. The counselor explicitly supported her authority even over the wording of the provided text message.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from Elise herself — her coffee rationale, her own refusal line, and her confidence criterion were self-generated. No lecturing or arguing for change; rulers and role-play elicited her own reasons, with confidence rising from 4 to 5.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor positions Elise as the expert, co-develops the plan from her ideas, and asks permission before role-play. Occasional multiple suggestions (gum, coffee, exit options) somewhat led the content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance and confidence rulers, 'one point higher' questions, and behavioral elicitation. Elise progressed to explicit commitment language and a self-generated plan, with confidence rising from 4 to 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor rolls with sustain talk by reflecting the dilemma without amplifying it and reframes the past failed attempt as informative. However, some questions could invite sustain talk, and neutral rather than double-sided reflections were mostly used.
