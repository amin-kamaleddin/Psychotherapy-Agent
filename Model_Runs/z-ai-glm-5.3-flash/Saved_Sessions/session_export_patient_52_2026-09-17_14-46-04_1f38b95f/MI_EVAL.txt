# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance with validating affirmations ('It makes sense it feels normal given your family traditions'). Affirmations mostly praised the plan's quality rather than Irina's personal strengths as a mother navigating enormous stress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered consistent, accurate reflections that captured her ambivalence about the evening drink as both a needed break and a source of guilt. Reflections sometimes summarized content rather than probing deeper emotions like her guilt over wine vs. playtime and her fear of losing the last thing that is 'hers'.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on the target behavior throughout, with every turn connecting back to the evening drinking and potential drifts (smoking, childcare) tied back to the alcohol goal. The session progressed coherently from exploration to a specific, measurable plan with a Thursday check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was genuinely co-constructed — Irina generated the delay, bottle placement, sister backup, exam-night plan, and dinner insight while the psychiatrist only refined rather than dictated. Her shift from 'I don't know what you want me to change' to 'Deal?' confirms a negotiated contract between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly reinforced choice at every decision point, offering options as menus rather than prescriptions and framing slips as 'information, not failure'. Her closing comment that the psychiatrist 'just trimmed it' rather than taking it away is direct evidence the autonomy support landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully drew motivation from Irina herself, using open questions and the confidence ruler to elicit her own reasons and solutions. Missed exploring deeper values (the parent she wants to be, culinary school hopes), and change talk was sometimes answered with logistics rather than deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Irina as the expert, asking which options felt most doable and building on her own ideas for every major plan element. A 5 would require more explicit naming of her expertise in her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk with the paired likes/worries question and the confidence ruler that moved her from 6.5 to concrete confidence-building steps. The moment 'If I get through this week even most nights with one drink, that's a win, right?' was a prime opportunity to amplify with an evocative question about what success would mean.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled minimization and defensiveness with neutral, empathic reflections rather than argument, and the 'trimming, not taking away' framing softened her fear of losing everything. Reflections sometimes gave equal weight to both sides without steering toward change, and her 'I'm not promising anything' was absorbed rather than explored or reframed.
