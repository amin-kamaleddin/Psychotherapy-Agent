# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Consistent respect and nonjudgment: "That's honest and useful," "no judgment, just info," and "information, not failure." Never criticized Marco's smoking despite major health events. Falls short of 5 as affirmations are somewhat formulaic; deeper affirmation of strengths (resilience, self-knowledge) is missing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Good reflective listening — "smoking feels like part of you, you've tried things that didn't stick, and nagging made it worse." Accurately captures ambivalence and history. Reflections tend to be brief and summative; misses opportunities to explore emotional meaning behind "I've survived worse" and his resignation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking throughout. Every exchange ties back to the target: log, triggers, patch trial, GP consult, partner support, follow-up. Gently redirects tangents (wife, parking meter) to concrete next steps. Logical progression: exploring → rulers → options → small-step commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Largely equal footing: "we'll look for one small, realistic change," "we'll review it together — no judgment." Marco visibly shapes the plan (simplified log, chosen dates, edited text, rejecting reminders). Not a 5 because most options and scripts are pre-packaged rather than jointly generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Clear strength. Everything framed as optional and Marco-owned: "Would it be OK...," "low-pressure," "pick the tone that fits you," "I won't push." Respected his choice to consult GP first; no pressure on cancellation joke. Outcome — Marco volunteering to call the surgery — demonstrates effective support of self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Opens with evocative open questions and uses rulers appropriately for precontemplation. However, evocation is superficial: rulers not followed by "why that number" probes, and the counselor quickly shifts to providing options and scripts. Marco's own reasons (coffee, Den) emerge but are not developed into deeper personal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Some partnership elements: "Which of those sounds tolerable to try? Can I help you plan it?" and Marco shaping plan details. But solutions are largely counselor-supplied (experiments, log columns, drafted texts, GP script) and choices are constrained; agenda-setting remains counselor-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Standard evocative tools used (open questions, importance/confidence rulers), eliciting mild engagement ("not the worst idea"). However, rulers are not capitalized on — no "Why a three and not a zero?" or "What would raise it?" — and spontaneous change talk at the end is not deepened. Good tools, weak follow-through.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk consistently reflected without amplification. Fatalism met with neutral summaries and low-pressure pivots; explicitly reframes logging as "information, not failure" and commits not to push. Rolls with resistance effectively. Missing: double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk; low rulers accepted without gentle reorientation.

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 3.9/5**
