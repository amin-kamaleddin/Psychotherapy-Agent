# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently non-judgmental and respectful ('I'm here without judgment,' validating Tyler's anger). Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and lack individualized warmth or explicit normalization of his ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers accurate reflections of both content and feeling, e.g., 'you feel judged and like her trust evaporated overnight.' Empathy falters late—Tyler's worry about effort being pointless gets no reflective response, and the emotion beneath his anger is only briefly touched.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session anchored to substance use and parent conflict, channeling resistance into a concrete, achievable step. However, direction narrows prematurely onto parent-management tactics, and the core target behavior is never revisited.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes collaborative moments like letting Tyler adapt wording into his own voice, but the structure is mostly counselor-proposes, Tyler-reacts. When Tyler declines rehearsal, the counselor responds with more tips rather than renegotiating the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirms Tyler's choice ('You don't have to decide anything now') and accepts his boundary about friends without pushback. Falls short because frequent scripts and plans subtly steer toward counselor solutions, framing change as externally motivated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies heavily on supplying scripts, plans, and tips rather than eliciting Tyler's own motivations or values, with almost no open questions. The one evocative question is a forced choice; Tyler's motivation emerges despite, not because of, the counselor's technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine partnership moments exist (asking what would be helpful, inviting Tyler to tweak wording), but the counselor repeatedly slips into an expert role with pre-packaged solutions. Tyler explicitly critiques this: 'Those sound like something a therapist wrote.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk arises spontaneously from Tyler rather than through skillful evocation, and the counselor captures but rarely deepens it. He misses the pivotal opening when Tyler shares that being seen as a 'screw-up' is what 'actually sucks.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk neutrally without confrontation, but never reframes or redirects it. Statements like 'I'm not saying I have a problem' are never met with double-sided reflections; the counselor works around sustain talk rather than softening it.
