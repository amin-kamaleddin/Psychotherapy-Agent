# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warmth and unconditional positive regard were consistent throughout, with statements like 'you're not broken' and 'That's you protecting your effort.' No judgment or disapproval was present, even when the client expressed fear, loneliness, or doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, e.g., 'the scale has been drowning all of that out,' and the counselor slowed down on emotionally significant moments. Deep attunement was shown to an emotionally vulnerable adolescent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus on the weight-loss plateau was clear, culminating in a concrete commitment to weekly sleep tracking and a check-in. Slight drift occurred near the close when exploring the Aina/loneliness theme without consolidation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor co-constructed meaning and offered psychoeducation about plateaus only when directly asked, crediting the client's own noticing. All decisions about tracking, timing, and disclosure were made jointly with the client holding final say.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently reinforced with 'Totally your call' and genuine choices about timing and disclosure. Every plan element originated from the client, with no persuasion or pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from the client through open questions, evoking the tracking plan, timeline, and powerful desire language ('I'm fighting for this'). Advice was given only on request (plateau explanation).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned the client as the expert, asking which changes 'would you actually want to track' rather than prescribing. The sleep-tracking plan, phone note, and mom idea all came from the client and were built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and amplified — naming the shift from 'nothing's gonna work' to planning, and deepening nascent statements with follow-up questions. This elicited strong commitment language ('This week, I think... at least I'd have proof I tried things differently').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally reframed effectively, such as transforming the scale from 'a verdict to just one piece of information.' However, some fears (journal failing, telling Aina) were pivoted away from quickly rather than fully honored with slower, validating reflections.
