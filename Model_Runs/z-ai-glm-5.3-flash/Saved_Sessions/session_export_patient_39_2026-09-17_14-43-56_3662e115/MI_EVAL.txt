# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout ('This is a safe, nonjudgmental space'), and Elias's discouragement is never judged or minimized. To reach a 5, affirmations would need to be less formulaic and the self-critical inner voice more explicitly normalized as understandable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening is demonstrated early, including an accurate double-sided reflection capturing both the beauty of the outing and the frustration of his physical limitations. However, deeper emotional content — the loneliness of the 'four walls and the TV' and shame of 'feeling useless' — is acknowledged but not explored at depth before pivoting to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus on increasing activity via group attendance, guiding steadily from exploring the past attempt to a specific next action (the 10 a.m. call). Every exchange advances toward a concrete, self-authored plan with barrier anticipation and contingency planning; no topic drift occurs.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine two-way collaboration is evident — Elias's ideas (tambourine, phone script wording, seat by the door) directly shape the plan, and the counselor adopts his phrasing rather than imposing one. The counselor even takes the supporting role-play part at Elias's request, and the written note is co-created from his own spoken words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is explicitly supported ('I'm here to listen and support your choices'), and the counselor honors Elias's 'just once' boundary without pushing for more. It falls short of a 5 because rapid-fire, stacked questions and pre-generated option lists at times crowd his ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within Elias — questions like 'What part of that experience mattered most to you?' elicit his core value of belonging, and all reasons for change are his own words, not counselor-supplied. Emerging change talk is expertly captured and consolidated into commitment through the written self-contract and confidence ruler.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elias is repeatedly positioned as the expert and decision-maker, with his own ideas substantially shaping the final plan and the script being essentially his words. The score falls short of 5 because some early option lists (e.g., tapping, percussion, singing) are counselor-generated before fully exploring his ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and reinforced, with Elias's language shifting from ambivalence to commitment ('I'll do the call tomorrow morning,' 'Once. That's the deal I'm making with myself'). The counselor captures and consolidates emerging commitment through rehearsal, written notes, a confidence ruler, and a verbal self-contract.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (the 'old man shaking a toy' voice) is acknowledged without reinforcement, and the counselor engages the inner critic directly while Elias ultimately resolves it himself ('sitting home wasn't better'). Points are lost because the counselor sometimes moves quickly to brainstorming rather than using double-sided reflections to fully soften the emotional weight.
