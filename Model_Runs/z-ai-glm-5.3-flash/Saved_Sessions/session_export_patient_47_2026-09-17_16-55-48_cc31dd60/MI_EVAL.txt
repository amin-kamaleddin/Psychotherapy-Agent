# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used nonjudgmental language and affirmed Evelyn's efforts, respecting her choices throughout. Warmth was somewhat generic and formulaic, with affirmations that lacked individualization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Produced some decent reflections (e.g., the two-sides reflection about the pub and creeping drinking), but reflections were sparse overall. The counselor rarely explored the emotional meaning of the fall, her son's worry, or fear of losing her social life, moving quickly to planning instead.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol reduction from opening question to closing plan, with no topic drift. The session progressed logically toward a concrete, well-structured change plan including tracking and a scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was largely co-constructed, with Evelyn choosing the alternating strategy, tracking method, decline line, and check-in time. However, key tactics were initially counselor-generated rather than drawn out from Evelyn, making some exchanges feel counselor-supplied.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Evelyn's choice, offered options rather than directives, and respected her refusals (keeping pub nights, declining video chat). Occasionally slipped into prescriptive advice blocks and repeated directives without checking her perspective.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler was well used, but motivation was elicited only superficially. The counselor filled space with practical advice rather than deepening Evelyn's own reasons for change (feeling steadier, easing Danny's worry).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for Evelyn's input at key moments, but several turns slid into expert mode with rapid tactic lists delivered before exploring her own ideas. The counselor supplied most of the solutions, limiting genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Evelyn's change language, including her commitment to a month-long experiment and concrete steps. The confidence ruler was a skillful evocation, though her motivations (son's worry, feeling steadier) could have been amplified more.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue with Evelyn's sustain talk ('seven a week isn't a crime') but also did not strategically soften or pivot it. Early double-sided reflection was helpful, but later turns mostly ignored sustain talk rather than guiding ambivalence toward change.
