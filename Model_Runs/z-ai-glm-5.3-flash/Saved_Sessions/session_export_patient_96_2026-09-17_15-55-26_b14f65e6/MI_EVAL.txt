# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating her boundary when directly challenged about a hidden agenda. Falls short of 5 because affirmations were somewhat formulaic and lacked deep, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate but often truncated summaries ending in 'got it.' Missed emotional opportunities to explore her feelings about being judged or the piercing's meaning as self-expression.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained steady focus on the target behavior and narrowed to a concrete, time-bound plan (jewelry swap, dentist check, optional follow-up). Direction was achieved by supplying content rather than guiding her to clarify her own goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission, offered choices, and let Marisol dictate the session's terms, creating genuine equal footing. The actual plan content was counselor-generated, however, rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor's strongest domain — consistently reinforced her ownership through option framing and transparently denied any hidden agenda. Her closing comment ('You didn't try to talk me out of it') is direct client evidence that autonomy support landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Conveyed that the decision rested with Marisol but did little to actively draw motivation from within her. Her budding motivation arose organically and was never deepened through evocative questions about importance or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing was present via permission-seeking and option menus, but the session became largely an advice-delivery exchange. Marisol never generated any part of the plan herself, and her expertise was not acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged ('I'd actually be into that,' 'I'll send the piercer text tonight') and was responded to reasonably, but it was largely a byproduct of practical framing rather than deliberate evocation. The counselor did not deepen or consolidate her 'I'll think about it' commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing strong sustain talk, pivoting instead toward actionable protective steps and preventing reactance. At times sustain talk was simply accepted without a strategic reframe or double-sided reflection.
