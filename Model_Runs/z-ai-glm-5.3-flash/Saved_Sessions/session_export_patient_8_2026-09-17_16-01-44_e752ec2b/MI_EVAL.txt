# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with affirmations like 'That was a brave step to reach out,' but warmth occasionally felt formulaic and strengths were rarely affirmed beyond the initial compliment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early ('You're torn—sleep matters more, but your phone is how you relax'), but reflections thinned out later and the emotional weight of her academic fear was never deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, consistent focus on reducing nighttime scrolling and improving sleep, with every exchange tied back to the target behavior and the concrete 3-night experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely joint—her edits shaped the plan and the counselor asked permission before drafting messages—but the counselor sometimes moved quickly into prescribing steps and ready-made texts, tilting idea generation toward the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong repeated ownership language ('once you pick one,' 'it keeps control with you') and the plan was scaled to Amina's comfort; she left stating it felt like 'a choice instead of a punishment.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions were consistently drawn from Amina via rulers, open questions, and planning prompts, resulting in self-generated motivation and change ownership residing with her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amina was positioned as the decision-maker ('Which of these feels most doable?') and her contributions were built upon, but the counselor's repeated menus of options and drafted messages edged into expert/fixer territory.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematic use of importance and confidence rulers with rationale probes reliably produced change talk, including 'my grades slipping freaked me out' and her self-generated plan to push toward seven nights.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections validated her connection and stress-relief reasons without amplifying sustain talk, but episodes like 'I'll probably fail night one' were sometimes met with fixes rather than strategic reframing.
