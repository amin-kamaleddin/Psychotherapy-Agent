# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard toward a court-mandated client with no judgment or moralizing about the shoplifting. Warm, affirming statements throughout, closing with genuine allegiance ('I'm with you on this').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captured Jordan's core ambivalence, including his self-consciousness. Reflections occasionally stayed at surface content, missing opportunities to explore deeper emotional material like shame, fear of exclusion, and the mother relationship.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior from first question through final rehearsal, progressively moving Jordan from contemplation to planning. Frequent stacking of 3-4 questions per turn created scatter and a hurried feel that diluted the direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely egalitarian exchange with Jordan's own ideas forming the substance of the plan, and decisions co-constructed rather than prescribed. Multi-part question barrages set the pace unilaterally, and supplied example options sometimes led the client rather than eliciting his ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Jordan's ownership of every decision with choices offered and left to him, without pressure or directive advice. Jordan's confidence rise from 5 to 7 was self-attributed, and the counselor reinforced this self-driven progress.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully drew out Jordan's own motivations rather than supplying reasons to change, using textbook ruler questions with follow-ups. Jordan's change talk expanded measurably over the session, from uncertain guilt to confident commitment, with self-generated rewards and reframes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Jordan as the expert on his own life, with every plan element originating from him. Occasional rapid-fire, multi-barreled questions shifted the interaction toward a task-driven agenda rather than fully shared pacing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematically evoked and developed change language through importance and confidence rulers, tracking movement from 5 to 7 with Jordan attributing the increase himself. Concrete planning questions and rehearsal prompted strong commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally reflected sustain talk briefly and pivoted toward change without amplifying it, and Jordan's own counter-sustain talk emerged naturally. Some suggestions echoed sustain themes (e.g., 'fear of getting caught is a strong motivator'), and double-sided reflections pairing sustain with change talk were never used.
