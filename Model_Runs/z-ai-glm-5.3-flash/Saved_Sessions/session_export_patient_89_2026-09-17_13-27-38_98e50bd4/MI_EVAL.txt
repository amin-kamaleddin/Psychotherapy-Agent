# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect and nonjudgment maintained with brief, generic affirmations. However, little validation of Marcus's discouragement, and the tone is more task-focused than warmly accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is nearly absent after one early acknowledgment. Emotional disclosures (yo-yo dieting despair, love of dancing, the closing shift to hope) are never reflected back or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, unwavering focus on the target behavior with progressive narrowing toward a concrete plan. The only limitation is that focus is achieved through directive, prescriptive style rather than MI-consistent guiding.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is markedly unequal, with the counselor functioning as an advice-dispenser using menus, checklists, and predetermined structure. No joint brainstorming or negotiation of the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some offering of choices (picking days, options for classes vs. routine), but the counselor designs the entire plan, cues, and backup structure. Autonomy is nominally respected, but ownership of the change process is not consistently reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to draw out Marcus's own motivations; the rich motivational material was generated by the client despite, not because of, counselor technique. Motivation is treated as something to be engineered via structure rather than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role, dominating nearly every turn with bullet-point instructions. Marcus's contributions shape some content, but the counselor drives virtually all decisions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No deliberate evocation of change talk; the client's own motivation ('I'm kind of excited,' 'I can do this') is met with prescriptive planning rather than deepening. The confidence scale is the only evocative technique, and its follow-up jumps to problem-solving instead of exploring the 'why.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is neither amplified nor actively softened; the counselor bypasses doubts rather than resolving ambivalence. No double-sided reflections or reframing of past failures as useful data.
