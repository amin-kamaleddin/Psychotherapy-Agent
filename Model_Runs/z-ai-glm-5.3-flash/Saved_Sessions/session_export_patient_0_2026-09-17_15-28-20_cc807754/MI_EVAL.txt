# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, thanking Adrian for his honesty and accepting his refusal to quit with a harm-reduction framing. Warmth reads somewhat clinical, and his shame about friends not knowing 'the whole story' went unvalidated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but formulaic, capturing content rather than feeling or meaning. Emotional disclosures — his shame about secrecy and the weight of the infection — were met with problem-solving rather than emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session produced concrete behavioral steps (reminder, single-use implements, test strips, a trial period), but focus drifted into harm-reduction logistics. Little movement occurred on Adrian's ambivalence or relationship to use itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor seeks permission, offers choices, and co-constructs a plan genuinely shaped by Adrian's preferences (discreet reminder, gas-station purchase). It falls short of full collaboration because the counselor pre-formats options rather than eliciting Adrian's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The strongest domain: autonomy is consistently reinforced through menus rather than directives, accepting the 'trial, not quitting' framing, and normalizing imperfection ('If you forget, no sweat'). Adrian retains full ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler and commitment questions elicit some motivation ('I can do a trial,' 'I'd rather not gamble'), but the session is heavy on counselor-supplied options. The counselor never explores why risk reduction matters to Adrian personally or develops his intrinsic motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-asking and option-giving support power sharing, but the counselor tends to supply the ideas, menus, and plans, keeping the expert role. Adrian's contributions shape the plan somewhat, but he is rarely positioned as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler and concrete planning questions evoke some commitments ('I'll try it this weekend,' interest in test strips). However, evocation targets harm-reduction compliance rather than Adrian's own motivations about his use itself, missing deeper exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor commendably avoids arguing or pushing quitting when Adrian minimizes ('I've got it figured out'). But there is no active softening — no double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with his concerns, leaving ambivalence unworked.
