# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirming statements ('I'm really proud of you') and normalization of missed sessions. Kept from a 5 by formulaic encouragement ('You've got this' repetitions) and little explicit affirmation of Aisha's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent — Aisha offers rich emotional content (feeling 'gross,' all-or-nothing thinking, gym self-consciousness) that is met with advice and checklists rather than reflections. Only minimal acknowledgments like 'that sounds frustrating' without genuine perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on restarting exercise with logical progression from motivation to plan, backup rules, and follow-up. Falls short because direction is achieved through prescriptive structuring, and underlying body-image concerns are never explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The plan is presented as a finished expert product rather than co-constructed, with Aisha mostly accepting and adapting details. Some accommodation on logistics (the 5pm check-in) prevents a lower score.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive elements exist (asking before drafting the plan, offering text template options). However, the overall style is prescriptive with fixed rules and directives, and the counselor never explicitly affirms Aisha's decision-making authority.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor never asks why change matters to Aisha or what it would give her, despite her arriving with strong intrinsic motivation. Motivational content is counselor-supplied rather than evoked, missing key opportunities to develop her own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, delivering unrequested advice lists before asking anything and prescribing plans and rules. Aisha's expertise is never invited ('What has worked for you before?').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha generates abundant spontaneous change talk, but the counselor does not strategically evoke or deepen it with open questions or amplifying reflections. Questions remain logistical rather than motivation-focused.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (self-consciousness, fear of flopping again) is not reinforced and the 'data, not failure' reframe helpfully softens all-or-nothing thinking. However, no double-sided reflections are used, and sustain talk is skipped over rather than strategically pivoted from.
