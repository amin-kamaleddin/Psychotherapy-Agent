# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician communicates clear nonjudgment and respect ('there's no judgment here. You get to decide') and affirms the client's insights. Warmth is somewhat formulaic and there's no explicit normalization of the client's minimization or defensiveness, which would elevate this to a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurate, including a well-crafted double-sided reflection capturing the client's ambivalence and skillful use of the 'Mostly, anyway' slip. Falls short of 5 because emotional undercurrents (guilt about his father, his wife's disapproval) aren't explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on smoking and alcohol, moving progressively from ambivalence to values to a concrete behavioral experiment and a specific high-risk situation (Dean's stag do). Each response builds toward the target behavior without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction reads as two equal partners—the clinician shares his own relevant experience, asks rather than tells, and the plan is co-discovered rather than delivered. The client actively problem-solves, showing the session direction was genuinely shared.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is reinforced consistently and explicitly ('You get to decide what, if anything, feels worth looking at'), and the clinician never prescribes. All commitments are framed as the client's own choice, reflected in his final ownership ('let's see how it goes').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client—questions like 'what would that noticing part be worried about?' elicit his values-based motivation around his son. The client ends with spontaneous commitment language, strong evidence of skilled evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly positions the client as the expert on his own patterns and asks what he makes of his own attendance. The plan comes entirely from Thomas, demonstrating full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation throughout: open questions led the client to articulate his own reasons and commitment language, reinforced with a strategic summary tied to a concrete upcoming test (the stag do). This is textbook change talk cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is reflected without reinforcement and pivoted toward the client's values, with a skillful reframe of minimization ('a door left cracked open'). Occasionally sustain talk ('not up for negotiation') is simply echoed back without active redirection, preventing a 5.
