# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with warm openers ('here to listen and support you, not judge') and normalized Lukas's ambivalence. Warmth felt somewhat formulaic and repetitive, limiting individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the core conflict between loyalty to friends and protecting his performance. Reflections were often brief summaries followed by option menus, with deeper emotional and identity stakes left unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on alcohol use in peer contexts and its link to athletics, with concrete steps and a follow-up plan tied to Dino's party. Some drift occurred from re-presenting the same menu of options rather than advancing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing: Lukas selected strategies, edited scripts, and co-constructed the final plan. Idea generation was counselor-heavy, as Lukas mostly modified pre-packaged menus rather than originating ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength — the clinician renounced control, offered choices, accepted edits and refusals to over-commit, and honored Lukas's real-world follow-up plan. Lukas's sense of ownership was evident in his closing commitment to return after Dino's party.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client motivation was elicited through open questions about goals and worries. However, the clinician quickly shifted to supplying solutions rather than developing Lukas's own reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lukas was consistently treated as the expert, with his contributions genuinely shaping the plan (the casual text, the buddy strategy). Repeatedly supplying pre-packaged scripts slightly undercut the message that expertise resides within him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Capitalized on Lukas's performance concerns and affirmed his commitment language ('I'll give it a real shot'). Much change talk was selected from counselor-generated menus rather than openly evoked or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue with Lukas's minimization ('everyone experiments a bit') and accommodating it softened resistance. Missed chances to redirect sustain talk toward change via double-sided reflection linking his experimentation with his athletic priorities.
