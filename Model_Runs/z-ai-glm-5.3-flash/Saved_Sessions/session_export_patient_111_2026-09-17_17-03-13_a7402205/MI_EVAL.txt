# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, never criticizing Richard's smoking or minimization, and affirmed his self-made plan. Few explicit affirmations of his strengths or honesty kept this from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured Richard's core ambivalence about ritual and identity. Missed opportunities for deeper emotional reflection, especially around his fear of losing his wife and shame about withdrawal irritability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation, gently redirecting deflections and progressing logically from ambivalence to a concrete, actionable plan without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Richard choosing mornings, format, and framing. Occasionally delivered pre-packaged expert content (pharmacotherapy, chew-and-park) before eliciting his own knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: reinforced Richard's ownership at every step, embraced his 'experiment' framing, and respected all his conditions (no cold turkey, no doctor talk, short texts).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out Richard's own motivations, moving him from importance 2 to 'maybe a bit for me, if I'm honest.' Some peak moments (e.g., 'I'd pick her') were underdeveloped, and the final third became more advisory than evocative.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Richard as the expert with choice-based questions and let him shape the plan on his terms. Moments of advice-delivery mode slightly undermined power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked substantial change talk from a precontemplative client, culminating in a voluntary commitment. Strongest change talk (e.g., 'I'd pick her') could have been deepened and amplified more.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently rolled with resistance using reflective acceptance and strong double-sided reflections pivoting toward change. Practical suggestions occasionally dwelt on barriers and techniques rather than Richard's own change reasons.
