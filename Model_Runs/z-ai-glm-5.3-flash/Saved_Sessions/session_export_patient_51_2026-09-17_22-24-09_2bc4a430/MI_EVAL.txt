### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with frequent, specific affirmations. Held at 4 as affirmations occasionally feel formulaic and redundant goodbyes pad the closing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture the core tension between time limits and wanting to get it right. Reflections sometimes stay at the logistical surface, missing emotional cues like 'that's usually where I give up.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus under time pressure, maintaining the citalopram/phenelzine safety target throughout and adapting immediately to the client's urgency. Converges on a specific, actionable plan with minimal drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — the plan is visibly co-constructed from Ingrid's own ideas (Thursday bundling, written question, afternoon call). Answers plainly when asked for the bottom line, without lecturing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong choice-giving ('calling today, or having the Thursday question ready?') with the plan entirely Ingrid's construction. Slight pressing on a same-day check-in after she'd said today was impossible keeps it from a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicits the client's own motivation and reasons for change. Confidence is never explored and commitment language isn't consolidated, though time constraints make this a defensible trade-off.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Ingrid as the expert on her own life, and her contributions genuinely shape the session. The closing becomes somewhat monologue-like with repeated affirmations rather than two-way exchange.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits and reinforces change talk, which grows visibly from 'I do want to get this right' to a concrete plan. Evocation is somewhat passive — no scaling or confidence exploration to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with double-sided reflections and quick pivots to what's possible. One mild pressing moment after Ingrid said today was booked is a small misattunement, though recovered.
