# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect, confidentiality, and safety planning are present, but affirmations feel formulaic and the referral context is never normalized. Missed chances to affirm Isabelle's honesty, coping, and four years of saxophone commitment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal—only one modest summary is offered. Isabelle's confusion, resistance ('I've got things pretty handled'), and dismissiveness about safety are never explored empathically.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No clear target behavior; critically, the referral reason (elevated alcohol-risk screening) is never addressed at all. The conversation wanders from a generic checklist to sleep advice the client never identified as a concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is dominated by a rapid-fire question checklist and unsolicited expert advice; Isabelle's confusion about the session's purpose goes unaddressed. The plan is handed to her rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A genuine choice and permission to skip questions are offered, but the session quickly becomes prescriptive ('Try this for a week'), leaving Isabelle compliantly deferring ('Whatever you recommend'). Her ownership of the plan is never reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions are treated as residing with the counselor, not the client. Isabelle's values and expertise (saxophone, IB goals) are never drawn on as change resources, and advice dominates.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor positions herself as the expert, delivering prescriptive recommendations with little client input. Isabelle's existing strategies (music as a break) are not built upon, and the one offered choice is quickly overridden.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Almost no effort to evoke change talk—no open questions about Isabelle's own reasons, desires, or confidence for change. Her mild commitments arise from compliance, not skilled evocation, and are never deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I sort of do something like that already', 'I've got things pretty handled') is simply bypassed rather than acknowledged or redirected. No double-sided reflections are used to work with ambivalence.
