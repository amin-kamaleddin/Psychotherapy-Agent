# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with strong affirmations ("that's real courage," "you are the expert on your body") and no judgment about her avoidance. Fell slightly short of 5 due to occasionally formulaic affirmations and limited explicit normalization of her fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including a skilled double-sided reflection balancing her caregiving role with her body's signals, and accurately identifying the phone call as the feared step. Some emotionally charged moments were moved past quickly rather than deepened.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, unwavering focus on seeking medical care, with each response advancing momentum from fear to a concrete plan. Even self-compassion exploration directly served follow-through on the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Full partnership framing throughout, with the script co-developed but client-owned and options (e.g., the receptionist idea) offered for her evaluation rather than as directives. The client's request for help with wording was met with collaborative support.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was consistently reinforced ("you get to choose what fits") with no pressure or coercion. The plan was entirely client-authored, and the counselor explicitly highlighted the client's use of "ready" rather than "forced."

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from the client via open-ended questions about readiness, needs, and hopes. Key insights (the pediatrician analogy, self-compassion) came from the client herself, skillfully developed throughout.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit, consistent power sharing ("that's your voice, not mine," "you are the expert on your body"), and the client's stated needs shaped the entire plan. The counselor repeatedly credited her authorship.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, sustained evocation — the counselor highlighted the client's own change talk ("ignoring it doesn't make it stop") and drew out concrete commitments (phone script, text to sister). DARN and commitment language came almost entirely from the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (panic, fear of judgment, worry about backing out) was acknowledged without amplification and redirected toward change, with a double-sided reflection early on. Some reflections restated her fears at length, slightly extending focus on the negative.
