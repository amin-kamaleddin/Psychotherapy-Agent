# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor remained nonjudgmental and accepted Kareem's stated enjoyment of smoking without pushback, but warmth was formulaic ('You've got this' repeated). No affirmations of Kareem's honesty or insights were offered despite clear opportunities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial reflections were decent, but the session quickly shifted to advice-giving checklists. Deeper material — his wife's pressure, Dave's teasing, his guarded self-confidence — was never reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on workplace smoking and produced a concrete plan: a one-week patch trial starting Monday, trigger strategies, and a scheduled check-in. A relative strength, though maintained through task-orientation rather than collaborative goal clarification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked permission and let Kareem pick a start day and script, but problem-solving followed a counselor-generates-solutions pattern with prescriptive checklists. Kareem's own ideas surfaced late and organically rather than being co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Participation was repeatedly framed as optional and Kareem's 'not promising anything beyond the week' boundary was accepted without pressure. The low-stakes experiment framing itself respected autonomy, though options were often bundled into prescriptive lists.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was never elicited — no questions about why quitting matters, what his wife's concern means, or what a successful week would feel like. Kareem's own motives (wife, workplace pressure, self-respect) emerged unprompted and were passed over.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional invitations for input ('What feels most doable right now?') were present, but the overall stance was expert-driven with rapid solution lists. Kareem's knowledge of his triggers emerged only because he volunteered it, not because it was drawn out.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Kareem offered meaningful change talk ('I can quit,' 'I don't see why I couldn't stick to it,' 'maybe the patch works better than I'm expecting') that was never reinforced or deepened. No open questions about importance or confidence; change talk landed on deaf ears.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not amplified or argued against — 'I like smoking' was met with a neutral reflection, which is good. However, sustain talk was also never reframed or used as a bridge to change talk; redirection relied on practical advice rather than resolving ambivalence.
