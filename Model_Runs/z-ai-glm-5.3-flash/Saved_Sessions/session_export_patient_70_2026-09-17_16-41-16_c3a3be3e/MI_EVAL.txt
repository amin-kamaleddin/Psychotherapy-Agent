**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, including honoring the request not to mention prison history and reassuring him he won't read aloud or be judged.

### Empathy

**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Lars's shame, worry, and motivation (e.g., forms feeling humiliating, fear the entrance test would trip him up). Reflections were sometimes brief and could explore deeper emotional themes like guilt and fear of failure more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained crystal-clear focus on course enrollment as the route to employment and staying out of trouble, with every turn advancing concrete steps (forms, Thursday 2pm appointment, polytech call). No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong joint problem-solving with a genuinely co-created plan (bringing forms, joint scheduling, co-constructing what to say to the polytech). Occasionally supplied options rather than drawing them from Lars.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered genuine choices (2pm vs 4pm, text vs call, test-free courses vs supported testing) and honored Lars's decisions, including his wish about prison history. Affirmed his ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within via questions like 'What makes that important to you?' and 'What would help you actually take that step?' Could have explored values ('be a different dad') and confidence more.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Lars as the expert, asking permission and letting his input shape the plan. Slightly less than 5 because detailed solution menus were sometimes offered before eliciting his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk

**Comments:** Successfully evoked substantial change talk ('I'd feel more confident about staying out of trouble,' 'I don't wanna end up going back inside,' 'Not doing that this time').
