# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout and normalized mandated attendance without disapproval. Affirmations were somewhat sparse and formulaic, lacking deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections like 'wanting change but getting pulled back is exhausting' showed adequate but shallow understanding. Responses often pivoted quickly to structured options rather than deepening exploration of family dynamics, loneliness, or fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on the 7-day no-contact goal with minimal drift. However, direction was achieved through task-oriented logistics rather than connecting actions to Mateo's deeper values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo's preferences actively shaped the plan (muting cousin, blunter text, mom as backup). The power balance occasionally tipped, with the counselor providing detailed scripts before Mateo fully articulated his own thinking.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly honored Mateo's choices, including his preference to mute rather than block his cousin and accepting 'no promises' without pushing. Rapid-fire suggestions and pre-written scripts sometimes left him reacting to the counselor's ideas rather than generating his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited (staying out past a week), but the counselor rarely drew out the 'why' behind change or linked goals to Mateo's vision of family and stability. Most energy went into supplying practical tools rather than evoking internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently presented Mateo's own ideas as central, with his contributions actively shaping the plan. The counselor leaned toward ready-made solutions and scripts rather than explicitly acknowledging Mateo's expertise in his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evoked initial change language through the 'one small thing' question, but did not strategically deepen it with evocative follow-ups. Most subsequent exchanges shifted into practical planning rather than amplifying motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and gently redirected doubt toward concrete steps. However, when Mateo expressed ambivalence ('no promises,' 'might not even remember'), the counselor responded with logistics rather than double-sided reflections linking back to his values.
