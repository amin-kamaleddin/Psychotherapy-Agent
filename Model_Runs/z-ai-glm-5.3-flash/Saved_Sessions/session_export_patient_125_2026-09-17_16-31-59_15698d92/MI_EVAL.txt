# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicated respect and affirmed Tariq's compliance, frustration, and coping strengths without judgment. Warmth was sometimes brief and task-focused, moving past vulnerable moments rather than deeply accepting the emotions expressed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening captured both emotion and context, e.g., the frustration of losing privileges before release. Reflections were occasionally skipped in favor of solutions, notably missing the guilt and fear in Tariq's statement about his mother crying.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on family contact, frustration management, and release preparation, with logical session progression. Focus was sometimes scattered across many parallel options, and some agenda transitions were counselor-driven rather than collaborative.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used 'we' language, checked in before acting, and built on Tariq's contributions, which he explicitly appreciated. The counselor frequently supplied the plan structure and did the drafting himself, limiting genuine shared agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed Tariq's choice and pace, honoring his rejection of breathing exercises in favor of yard laps. Sometimes framed help as his own actions ('I can submit...') rather than reinforcing Tariq's agency and decision-making capacity.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out authentic, client-generated motivation around family connection and going home clean. Missed opportunities to deepen the strongest change talk, particularly exploring confidence and the meaning of staying clean for his mother.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Incorporated Tariq's input, keeping the letter in his voice and following up on his self-generated ideas. However, a directive, menu-offering stance and self-appointed execution roles tilted power toward the clinician rather than drawing plans from Tariq's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and reinforced Tariq's own motivations, including 'going home clean' and not hurting his mother. Did not deepen the strongest change talk with evocative follow-ups or explicitly assess and strengthen his confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Briefly acknowledged Tariq's blame and distrust of staff but then moved to solutions rather than strategically rolling with resistance. Sustain talk was largely sidestepped instead of softened with double-sided reflections or reframing toward Tariq's agency.
