# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout; Mai's skepticism is affirmed rather than judged. No disapproval or labeling anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture both content and underlying meaning, e.g., her desire for autonomy and proof. Reflections occasionally shift quickly into questions/education, and her frustration with Dr. Ferris's communication is acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange ties back to the target behavior of understanding and adhering to the statin, moving progressively from concerns to a concrete plan. No drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative framing ('figure out together') with frequent solicitation of Mai's input that visibly shapes the session. Educational segments are delivered somewhat monologically rather than actively negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of Mai's ownership and choice, with options offered and decisions left to her ('a plan you actually understand and chose for yourself'). No pressure or coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Mai's own reasons and confidence language, which are then amplified ('proof helps motivation'). Several turns default to information delivery and practical closed questions rather than deeper development of desire or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mai is repeatedly positioned as the expert on her own life ('You know your body well'). The counselor retains the informational expert role at times, offering suggestions rather than fully co-constructing them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation and reinforcement of Mai's commitment language ('Orange juice is fine — I can do that'). Evocation is less deliberate mid-session, where the counselor slips into educational/asking mode.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged non-defensively and pivoted toward solutions Mai readily adopts. Some sustain talk (distrust of the doctor) is never explicitly reframed, and double-sided reflections are not used to link back to her own motivations.
