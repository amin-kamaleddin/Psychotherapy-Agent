# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with acknowledgments of the client's fears and affirmations of her efforts. However, affirmations were somewhat generic and ambivalence was not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client's core conflict between fear of side effects and fear of breathlessness, plus validation of her appointment anxiety. Reflections were sometimes brief, missing chances to explore the emotional depth of her worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerged largely self-initiated, and the counselor affirmed these steps. However, there was little deliberate evocation—no open questions exploring desire, ability, reasons, or need for change, and no deepening of commitment language.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced, and a helpful reframe shifted the client's thinking about the inhaler's purpose. Softening was mostly passive, however—no double-sided reflections, and repeated urgent-care warnings may have amplified fear rather than resolved ambivalence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The stance was largely expert-driven, rapidly supplying question lists, a pre-drafted note, tracking templates, and checklists without inviting the client's input. The client's role was mostly to accept or slightly trim the counselor's proposals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on the target behavior with a concrete, actionable plan. Direction was driven by premature problem-solving and task lists rather than MI-consistent focusing on the client's own goals and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive elements existed, such as offering options and honoring her preference to skip the script. Much of the session was directive, however, with the counselor prescribing durations, exercises, and checklists rather than asking the client what she wanted to do.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration occurred through check-ins and the client's feedback modifying the plan (e.g., a simpler log). Still, the counselor generated nearly all content, and decisions were presented rather than co-constructed through exploring her ideas first.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort was made to elicit motivation from within the client; after the opening question, there were almost no open-ended motivational questions. Motivational movement came from psychoeducation and practical tools rather than evocation, contrary to the MI spirit.
