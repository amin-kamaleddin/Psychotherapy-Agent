# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout: rationalizations are met with curiosity, stress is validated ('That pressure is real'), and affirmations like 'trying counts' are framed as meaningful. No labeling or disapproval of her smoking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, emotionally deep reflections, e.g., reframing the fear of slipping as fear of being a hypocrite, which produced a genuine client insight. Reflections consistently capture both sides of ambivalence and advance the conversation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus on smoking and its impact on her son from opening reflection to closing plan, with steady forward movement from ambivalence to a concrete, dated plan. No aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving: every element of the plan (outside rule, car strategy, Saturday timing, tea ritual, sister as support) came from the client. The counselor's summary reflects back what the client built.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of client choice and ownership: conditionality preserved ('if you decided to'), menu-style support options, and explicit framing of motivation as hers ('it's yours, not your doctor's'). No coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within the client rather than supplied, with no lecturing about health risks. Skillful evocative questions ('What part of you feels most ready?') yielded strong self-motivating statements by the end.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is treated as the expert on her own life; the counselor deliberately elicits her ideas rather than supplying them. Her contributions directly shaped the entire plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open evocations produced progressively stronger change talk, from 'I'd want to be around for him, long term' to 'the wanting-it part finally feels bigger than the five minutes.' Change talk is reinforced with specific affirmations and named as the shift that matters.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is effectively redirected rather than argued with, and fear of failure is reframed into problem-solving. Minor deduction: sustain talk is occasionally reflected directly ('a whole day feels impossible'), though always within clinically sound double-sided reflections.
