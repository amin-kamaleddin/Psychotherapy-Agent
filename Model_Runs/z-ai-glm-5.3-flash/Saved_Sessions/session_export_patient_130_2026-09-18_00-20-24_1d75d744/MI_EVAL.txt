# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard throughout; explicitly refused to judge or label ('you don't have to pick a label today') and normalized setbacks ('you're allowed to try something and have it not land'). Affirmations were genuine and specific, crediting Viktor's own insights.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured ambivalence and deeper feelings accurately. Minor lapses: one reflection trailed off mid-sentence requiring Viktor to prompt, and a question momentarily assumed the plan had already been attempted.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on the target behavior with strong momentum from denial to a specific testable plan (weekends only, honesty with Maria, home by eleven). One timeline error ('What made it harder than you expected?' before Wednesday occurred), and drug use itself could have been tied back to more explicitly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership: the agenda was handed to Viktor immediately, questions were exploratory rather than advisory, and the entire plan emerged from his own reflections. The closing 'Deal?' extended collaboration into a negotiated next step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit and consistent autonomy support: 'That's your design, not mine or hers,' 'You're the expert on your own life,' and no prescriptive advice. Viktor's right to frame his use as non-problematic was respected at every juncture.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within Viktor—his own reasons (peace at home, keeping his word, Maria's 'quiet, disappointed thing') were elicited rather than supplied. His self-directed insight ('She's been asking me to be straight with her for months') demonstrates highly effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Viktor was repeatedly framed as the expert ('You're the expert on your own life,' 'That's yours'), and the entire honesty-focused plan originated from his reflections. The counselor's role was consistently to draw out rather than instruct.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematic evocation of change talk through open questions that moved Viktor from denial to commitment language ('Just being honest. That I can do.'). Change talk was amplified and reflected each time it appeared, showing a clear evolution in his framing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective double-sided reflections held both sides without arguing, and 'I'm not here to argue with that' prevented debate. However, Viktor's recurring minimization and rumination about Maria lecturing were sometimes left standing without strategic reframing toward change.
