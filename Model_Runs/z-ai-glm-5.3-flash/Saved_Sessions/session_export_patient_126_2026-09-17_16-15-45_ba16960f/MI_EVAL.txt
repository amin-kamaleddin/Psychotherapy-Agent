# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with affirmations and no disapproval, even when Thomas minimizes risks. Slightly held back by somewhat formulaic regard and limited explicit validation of his initial dismissiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections tracked Thomas's minimizing stance without confrontation. However, reflections occasionally stayed surface-level and missed deeper emotional material like his guilt about Sunday mornings and avoiding his wife's hints.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, clear focus on drinking behavior with every exchange tied to the target, despite repeated minimizing detours. Culminated in a concrete, testable plan (three-drink limit, refusal line, wife conversation).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely equal interaction with permission-seeking and a co-constructed final plan from Thomas's own ideas. The middle section (guidelines, bulleted tips) was more practitioner-delivered than jointly developed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated affirmations of choice and permission-based advice-giving. Some option menus were presented prescriptively, slightly reducing Thomas's space to generate his own strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong use of rulers, values-based questions, and open exploration, with Thomas generating his own reasons and plan. Leaned on standardized tools rather than deeper exploration of his ambivalence and his wife's prior hints.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Thomas was consistently positioned as decision-maker and his contributions visibly shaped the plan. Weakened by episodes of quick advice-giving with bulleted option lists.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation via importance rulers, values linkage, and confidence scaling, producing strong commitment language and a self-generated plan. A 5 would require deeper exploration of Thomas's own motivations beyond structured questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally and redirected toward values, with the 'slip' framed as info, not failure. Some moments (e.g., fear of being 'the boring one') received tips rather than strategic double-sided reflections.
