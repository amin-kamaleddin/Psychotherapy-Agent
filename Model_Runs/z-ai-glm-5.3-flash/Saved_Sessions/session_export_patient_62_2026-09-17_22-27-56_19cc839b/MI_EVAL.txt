# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, normalizing hopelessness and validating Jordan's self-criticism ('the couch wins') without any hint of disapproval. Warmth is genuine and individualized throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and emotionally attuned, tracking both sides of ambivalence and even subtle shifts in Jordan's voice. Reflections deepen meaning rather than merely repeating content, e.g., framing the comparison to others as 'lonely as much as exhausting.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus, moving progressively from hopelessness to stable work to a concrete resume/application step and barrier planning. Each exchange advances toward the target behavior without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor frames exploration as a joint effort ('together with you,' 'you and I figured out one small thing') and explicitly credits Jordan for insights. The interaction is genuinely egalitarian.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced ('on your own terms,' 'no promises attached'); the counselor never prescribes solutions. Jordan generates the goal, step, barriers plan, and reminder note himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client through questions like 'what's kept you showing up?' and 'what got you the callbacks?' Jordan articulates his own strengths, goals, strategies, and commitment language without the counselor ever arguing for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions Jordan as the expert on his own life, crediting him for all insights and plans. Jordan's contributions visibly shape the session direction, including the resume plan and fridge note.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful questions like 'what would make this week's version of you look back and say that was worth doing?' elicited commitment language ('I could actually do that,' 'prove myself wrong'). Change talk is amplified and reflected each time it emerges, culminating in a client-authored reminder note.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently met with double-sided reflections and pivots toward strengths ('you haven't fully given up'). Occasional reflections briefly restate hopeless themes before redirecting, which is validating but slightly extends focus on hopelessness.
