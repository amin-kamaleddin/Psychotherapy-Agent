# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor was respectful and non-judgmental with a warm opening and closing, but affirmations were generic and praise for Dante's specific strengths (his honesty, his insight about 'one miss turns into a week') was missing. The safety screen in the first message felt mechanical rather than caring.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections captured Dante's ambivalence well, but the counselor stopped reflecting once it shifted into planning mode. Emotionally meaningful statements ('committing to it out loud makes it real,' 'I leave these things feeling guilty') were passed over in favor of logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear focus on re-establishing exercise and successfully guided the session to a concrete, achievable plan. However, it moved to action-planning very quickly given Dante's contemplative stance, and final exchanges became repetitive re-capping rather than forward movement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative moments existed ('want to lock in those times or pick others?') and Dante's input shaped the final plan, but the counselor positioned itself as the expert dispensing behavioral techniques rather than co-creating solutions. Repeated unsolicited instruction-giving at the end undermined the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were autonomy-supportive elements (choice of options, 'experiment, not perfection' framing that Dante liked), but the counselor prescribed exact times, alarm labels, timers, and breathing exercises unprompted. It kept repeating instructions even after Dante explicitly signaled he had the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocation was largely absent — Dante's rich wells of motivation (past identity as 'the guy who never missed workouts,' his youngest's enthusiasm, his dislike of guilt) were never explored. The counselor supplied its own rationale and pre-packaged solutions rather than drawing out his reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor solicited Dante's input and incorporated his adjustments, but dominated concrete planning with a pre-built menu of techniques and protocols. Dante's own solutions came from him, yet the counselor never explicitly credited or built on his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Dante generated meaningful change talk ('I could actually do that,' 'I'll report back'), but the counselor rarely amplified it and never explicitly asked why change matters, what success looks like, or how confident he felt. Change talk emerged more from Dante's personality than skillful evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dante voiced significant sustain talk about self-sabotage ('I talk myself out of it,' 'part of me is already thinking about Wednesday going wrong'), but the counselor skipped over these doubts and responded with more tactical instructions. No double-sided reflections or validation of his fear of failing again were offered.
