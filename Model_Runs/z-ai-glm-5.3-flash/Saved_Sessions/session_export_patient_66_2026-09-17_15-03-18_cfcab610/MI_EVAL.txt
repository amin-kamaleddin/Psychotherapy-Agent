# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with explicit validation of Kareem's resistance and his choice to keep the morning cigarette. Affirmations were somewhat generic, missing chances to affirm specific strengths like his insight into all-or-nothing thinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was consistently accurate, capturing Kareem's ambivalence and perspective well. Empathy remained more cognitive than emotional, with limited exploration of the emotional meaning of the morning ritual or his underlying frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on workday smoking and systematically moved from vague frustration to a specific if-then plan with slip-recovery rules and accountability steps, all within one visit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Kareem choosing the delay tactic, backup strategies, reminders, and slip rule, and permission sought at each step. Early option generation relied on pre-packaged lists, making initial collaboration slightly counselor-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, repeated reinforcement of Kareem's ownership ('It's your choice whether to quit'), honoring his insistence on keeping the morning cigarette and never pressuring beyond what he chose. All change was clearly self-selected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within — Kareem articulated his own reasons, insights, and solutions, including his self-identified all-or-nothing pattern. His transformation from 'planning to tell you off' to a self-generated plan exemplifies effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kareem's contributions consistently shaped the session: his preferences determined the goals and the delay tactic became the centerpiece. However, early structured option lists slightly steered the agenda rather than fully drawing it from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked: open questions moved Kareem from 'I'm not gonna quit' to committing to cut workday smokes. Confidence scaling three times showed measurable progression (5→6→7), and questions about obstacles elicited sophisticated self-generated coping plans.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well by reflecting underlying values rather than arguing, and it diminished markedly as the session progressed. The counselor repeatedly validated keeping the morning cigarette without exploring whether that compromise might undermine long-term progress.
