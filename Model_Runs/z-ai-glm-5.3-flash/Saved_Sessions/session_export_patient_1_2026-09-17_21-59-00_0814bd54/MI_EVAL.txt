# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers unconditional positive regard, affirming Ahmed ('a fair-minded guy') even while he minimizes and deflects. Judgment-free statements like 'It makes sense that this feels like a big deal being made out of nothing' convey genuine warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate, capturing Ahmed's comparisons, emotions, and deeper meanings ('those cigars aren't just a habit—they're your way of closing out a hard day's work'). The final reflection 'Every ordinary Tuesday' mirrors his exact language, demonstrating deep attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear focus on cigars and the throat/gum irritation throughout, with no meaningful drift despite deflections to dust, stress, and his wife's nagging. The session culminates in a concrete, testable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor co-constructs the 'fair test' concept based on Ahmed's own standard of evidence and invites his hypotheses. All key decisions—timing, accountability, follow-up—came from Ahmed with the counselor facilitating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is reinforced from the opening line ('that's completely your call') and repeatedly emphasized ('your own test, your own timeline'). Ahmed explicitly notes 'if it's my idea, my test... that feels different somehow,' confirming successful self-directed change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic open questions elicited Ahmed's own standards for evidence and unlocked deeply personal motivation—his father's throat cancer death and his daughter's 'bad smoke' comment. He connected this material himself, saying he 'never put that together... until right now.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions Ahmed as the expert ('You know your habits and body better than anyone'). Ahmed substantively shaped the session, designing his own two-week test, involving his wife, and planning pre/post dental checks.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is evoked skillfully, culminating in strong client-generated commitment ('if I say it out loud, I can't back out,' 'That matters more than a cigar after work. It has to.'). Strategic questions progressively deepened Ahmed's reasons from precontemplation to concrete planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently acknowledges and pivots sustain talk rather than confronting it, e.g., reflecting the dust theory before asking 'What's your own sense of what's going on?' Ahmed himself concludes 'Dust doesn't explain everything, I suppose'—with no arguing or reinforcement of anti-change reasons.
