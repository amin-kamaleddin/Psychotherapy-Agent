# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated the client's mandated status, with the client explicitly noting feeling heard. However, affirmations were formulaic and lacked personalized warmth or recognition of the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections captured the weight of new freedom and expectations, but empathy was inconsistent. Emotionally significant disclosures — fear of looking like an idiot, self-criticism, dreams of a family — were repeatedly skipped to return to task logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session kept a concrete focus on work-seeking and returned consistently to the action plan. However, direction was narrowly task-focused, never exploring the core discrepancy between the client's hopes (family, own place) and current patterns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor generated virtually all plan content while the client merely approved or chose between options. Collaboration amounted to multiple-choice menus rather than joint problem-solving built from the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice menus and 'want any tweaks?' offered superficial autonomy, but the overall stance was controlling — prescribing exact scripts, breathing tactics, and pushing to send texts even after the client said he would. Ownership of change was never explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Solutions were treated as residing in the counselor, with almost no open questions eliciting the client's motivations, values, or confidence (e.g., 'What makes this time different?'). Motivation surfaced spontaneously but was never deliberately drawn out or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed an expert/fixer role, supplying drafted texts, phone scripts, and step-by-step instructions without inviting the client's input. The client's own expertise (external accountability helps, self-talk patterns) was never integrated into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offered strong change talk ('I want this one. Family, my own place'), but the counselor rarely reflected, amplified, or deepened it, pivoting instead to action steps. Change talk appeared by chance, not through skillful evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant sustain talk ('one slip-up and I'm back where I was,' 'I dunno if it'll last') was simply ignored, with no double-sided reflections or reframing. Ignoring rather than reinforcing is preferable, but there was no active softening of ambivalence.
