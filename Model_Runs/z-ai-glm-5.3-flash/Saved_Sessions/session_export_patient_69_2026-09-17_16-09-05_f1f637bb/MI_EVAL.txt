# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental, validating reluctance and responding to disclosures without disapproval. The opening briefly referenced negative caseworker notes, which felt slightly stigmatizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured both content and Kieran's avoidance patterns. However, empathy stayed somewhat cognitive — deeper emotional disclosures (sleep loss from money stress, 'maybe I don't have anything together') were not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally focused: every exchange stayed oriented toward one client-chosen target (sending an emergency rent assistance inquiry). The session progressed logically to a concrete, realistic plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction: Kieran edited the message tone and caseworker note, and the counselor accepted every change without defensiveness. The final plan was explicitly created together, with each contributing their expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength: genuine menus of options were offered throughout, with explicit permission to opt out. Kieran's boundaries — including keeping private disclosures out of the caseworker note — were honored without pushback.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation was evoked mainly through concrete choice questions rather than drawing out Kieran's deeper personal reasons and confidence. The plan relies partly on counselor-supplied tools and extrinsic motivation (caseworker credit).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly positioned Kieran as the expert, and his choices visibly shaped the session (rejecting goal-talk, choosing rent aid, vetoing role-play). Point lost because the counselor drove most option-generation rather than eliciting Kieran's own solution ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Preference-based questions successfully evoked change talk ('rent aid. Let's try that one,' 'I'll send this today'). Missed chance to deepen Kieran's own reasons (rent stress keeping him up at night) before he closed the topic off.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was reflected briefly and redirected into actionable plans rather than argued against; fatalism was met with contingency planning. Ambivalence was pragmatically managed but not fully explored or resolved.
