# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with normalizing of the referral and appreciation for honesty, but warmth is somewhat formulaic and pivots quickly to checklists rather than affirming Matteo's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effort to understand (group pressure is real), but meaningful emotional content about peer pressure and fear of looking 'soft' was never reflected back or explored; reflections are minimal.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on alcohol with steady guidance toward a concrete, actionable plan and follow-up, though direction remains narrowly tactical without linking to broader values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (menu options, permission, joint rehearsal), but repeated bullet-point advice dumps create an advice-giver dynamic, and Matteo mostly receives and complies rather than shaping direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice-offering is consistent, slips framed as learning, and Matteo noted the non-pressuring approach; deduction because the 3-drink goal and strategies were largely counselor-proposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence scaling and role-play elicited Matteo's own words and commitments, but most reasons and strategies come from the clinician, and Matteo's own motivations are never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-asking and check-ins show some power-sharing, but an expert stance dominates with unrequested tactic lists and prescribed goals, and Matteo's own expertise is rarely elicited or credited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation of commitment via confidence scaling, limit-setting, and role-play rehearsals, with confidence rising from 6 to 7; Matteo's own reasons for change were rarely reflected or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk and minimization ('dumb luck,' 'not trying to quit') were neither amplified nor actively softened—no double-sided reflections or reframing, just bypassed in favor of harm-reduction tips.
