# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, with no judgment or labeling. Actively counters Farid's harsh self-labeling ('lazy') and reframes his persistence as determination.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and deep, capturing both content and emotional weight ('that fatigue isn't just physical'). Reflections consistently land, as shown by Farid's responses of surprise and recognition.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains unwavering focus on increasing physical activity from first utterance to last. Every exchange builds progressively from exploration toward a concrete, specific plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly frames the work as joint ('let's think ahead together, as partners'). The emerging plan is entirely co-constructed from Farid's own input, with no authoritarian or advice-giving stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Farid's ownership ('You get to decide whether that trade-off is worth it; there's no wrong answer'). All ideas originate from the client, with autonomy affirmed even amid his doubts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully draws out Farid's own motivations rather than supplying them—linking the walk to his brother and reframing his 'laziness.' Every major insight and commitment originates from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Farid as the expert on his own life. The Saturday walk, involving his brother, and the 'ten minutes' strategy all come from Farid, with the counselor reflecting and building on them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes progressively strengthening change talk, from 'thinking about working out' to 'maybe this Saturday I'll text him' to 'I can do ten minutes.' Each evocative question is followed by reflection and deepening rather than moving on prematurely.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids amplifying sustain talk and uses effective double-sided reflections; sustain talk is mostly paired with or pivoted toward change. Score is 4 because a few moments (e.g., 'Both of those feelings make sense' after the accountability trade-off) reflect sustain talk without an explicit pivot toward change.
