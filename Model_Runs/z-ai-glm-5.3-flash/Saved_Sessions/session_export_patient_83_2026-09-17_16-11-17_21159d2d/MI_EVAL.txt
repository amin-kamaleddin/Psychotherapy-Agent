# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with Mai's concerns validated. However, warmth is somewhat procedural, focused on logistics rather than deeper affirmation of Mai as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic accurate reflections were present but largely factual rather than emotionally attuned. Missed opportunities to explore Mai's anxiety, frustration with Dr. Ferris, and the meaning of giving up her grapefruit juice habit.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior of understanding and adhering to the new statin with no drift. Falls short of 5 because the session was information-heavy without collaboratively setting self-determined goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor acted as a partner, soliciting Mai's input on labs, timing, and plan details, which substantially shaped the session. The interaction remained somewhat transactional, and Mai's own motivations were not woven into a shared change plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support via permission-seeking, offering choices, and honoring Mai's decisions without pressure. Falls short of 5 as autonomy statements were mostly permission-requests and logistics rather than reinforcing Mai's ownership of her health.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort was made to elicit Mai's own motivations, values, or reasons for change; the session was dominated by education and logistics. The opening 'hopes or worries' question was never followed up, making this the weakest domain.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mai was consistently positioned as the decision-maker with permission-seeking and incorporation of her preferences into the plan. Stops short of 5 because the structure was question-answer-educate rather than genuinely co-constructed exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai generated meaningful change talk, but the counselor did not actively evoke, reflect, or deepen it. No open questions explored confidence or benefits, and expressed motivation was not developed into commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai's mild sustain talk about her grapefruit habit was handled with clear corrective education rather than MI strategy. No double-sided reflections or acknowledgment of the difficulty of changing a cherished habit.
