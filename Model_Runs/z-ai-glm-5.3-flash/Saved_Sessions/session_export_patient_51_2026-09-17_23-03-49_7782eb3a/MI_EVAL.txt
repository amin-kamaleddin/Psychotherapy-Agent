# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth despite time pressure. Frequent affirmations ("Your caution just proved its worth," "That's real self-advocacy in a busy day"). No judgment about the patient's limited engagement; constraints validated. Held at 4 due to occasional formulaic warmth and limited exploration of the patient as a whole person beyond the practical task.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening throughout — e.g., reflecting being "unsettled" yet "glad I asked," and the concern about undoing stability. Reflections captured both emotional and practical sides. However, reflections stayed largely at the pragmatic surface; deeper emotional themes (depression, treatment uncertainty, stress of juggling work/family) were not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Clear, appropriate focus on medication safety and the citalopram/phenelzine question the patient identified. Focus maintained despite time pressure, with concrete action steps and a reinforcing closing summary ("keep everything steady, name serotonin syndrome, raise the two weeks"). Not a 5 because focus was narrowly defined by the patient's brief agenda, with little broadening toward longer-term treatment engagement — though arguably respectful of context.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Genuine two-way exchange despite constraints. Invitations to collaborate ("we could sort it out together"), understanding checks ("Does that match what you were expecting?"), and co-constructed follow-up planning. Stops short of 5 because core medical content was delivered somewhat unilaterally, and the structure leaned more question-answer than mutual exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Exemplary. Repeated affirmation of the patient's ownership: "You lead on what matters most," "You know your day," "You know best how you want that conversation to go." Advice framed with choice; safety instructions emphasized prescriber supervision rather than directives. Patient left visibly strengthened in autonomous decision-making ("I'm not making any changes on my own") with a self-shaped plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Opened evocatively ("what do you think could realistically work for you right now?") and drew out practical priorities and coping style. However, evocation remained confined to logistics and preferences rather than deeper motivations, values, or confidence regarding treatment. Missed opportunities for importance/confidence exploration — fitting restraint for an informational stage, but intrinsic motivation went largely untapped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the patient as expert on her own life ("Your insight into your own routine matters most here"). Her contributions genuinely shaped the session — her preferences drove the plan. Falls short of 5 because information delivery was necessarily clinician-driven, and there was limited acknowledgment of her broader expertise about her own treatment experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Effective evocative questions drew out commitment statements ("I'll mention it at my appointment today," "I'll mention the two weeks myself"). However, evocation was mostly logistical rather than motivational — little exploration of the patient's own reasons for managing medication safely, hopes for treatment, or deeper health motivation. Plan-oriented but shallow.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (time constraints, "not looking to overhaul anything") was accommodated rather than challenged ("Short and practical — let's work within that"), reducing friction without arguing. However, no active pivot toward the value of deeper engagement or resolution of the convenience-vs-thoroughness tension; accommodation was appropriate but passive.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **3.8/5**
