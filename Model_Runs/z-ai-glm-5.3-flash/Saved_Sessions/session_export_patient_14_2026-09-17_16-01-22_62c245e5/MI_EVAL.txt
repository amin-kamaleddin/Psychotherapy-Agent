# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with affirmations and no labeling despite the client's initial irritation. Warmth is somewhat brief and formulaic, and deeper normalization of the client's self-criticism was missing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured both content and emotion (e.g., 'needs to feel effortless and tied to what you already do'), and the closing summary was faithful. Fell short of exploring the emotional weight behind his self-criticism and the 'waste of my morning' admission.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful focus on weight/lifestyle change throughout, moving logically to a concrete, measurable plan (Sunday 9:00 stairs test, standing calls, calf raises). Each turn built toward the target behavior without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was co-constructed line by line, with client ideas (headset cue, Sunday timing, stairs test) visibly driving direction. The counselor offered rather than prescribed and checked in before advancing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Options were always offered as menus, and client rejections (phone alarm, meeting-title notes) were accepted without pushback. Confidence was repeatedly measured and respected with no coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the client's own solutions and intrinsic driver ('numbers work for me — the accountant in me'). Leaned toward practical/logistical questions rather than deeper personal values about why the change matters.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located in the client, who shaped every element of the plan including the cue, timing, and outcome measure. Genuine, consistent power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Commitment language developed progressively (d desire → commitment → plan), reinforced by confidence scaling and commitment-strengthening questions. Relied heavily on logistics and scaling rather than amplifying the client's deeper personal motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly then pivoted with acceptance, and the 'track days completed, not perfection' rule directly addressed all-or-nothing thinking. Lacked explicit double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
