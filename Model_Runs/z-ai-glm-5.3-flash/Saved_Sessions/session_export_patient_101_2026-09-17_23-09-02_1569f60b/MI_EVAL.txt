# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance with strong affirmations ('that's real insight') and normalization of imperfection. Falls short of 5 only because warmth is occasionally formulaic and normalization of slips/self-criticism comes late in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening throughout, capturing emotional nuance as well as content — from the dual-sided opening reflection to mirroring 'a check-in instead of a punishment.' Reflective accuracy is consistently strong.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior (reducing drinking to protect contraceptive reliability), with every exchange advancing toward a concrete, measurable plan with a timeline. Skilled steering keeps each response tied to the behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely equal interaction — the roommate accountability, low-stakes dinner, and Sunday check-in all originated from the client. Session content and outcome were co-constructed rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of client choice: 'your rules, your pace' and the closing 'Saturday is you picking up the pen.' No advice imposed; all strategies self-generated, and ownership of both success and setbacks is explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn entirely from the client, with no arguing for change. Questions like 'what would a win look like to you?' elicited her own reasons and confidence, and her closing 'you helped me figure out my own plan' is direct evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership with expertise clearly located in the client, whose contributions shaped the entire plan. Falls short of 5 because open questions subtly steered the sequence (e.g., prompting toward 'someone you could tell' before she generated the idea).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk skillfully evoked and deepened, eliciting strong commitment language ('I'd rather try something,' 'I get to choose differently. Starting Saturday'). The counselor reinforces and solidifies it through reflections and by inviting her to articulate her plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally redirected with neutral, reorienting reflections ('what might help it stick?'). Loses a point because some reflections briefly restated her difficulties without double-sided pivots back toward her reasons for change.
