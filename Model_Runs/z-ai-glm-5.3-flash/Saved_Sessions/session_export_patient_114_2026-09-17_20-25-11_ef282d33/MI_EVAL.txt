# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, with genuine affirmations and explicit welcome of anticipated setbacks ('screw-ups are welcome here too; they're information, not failure'). The client confirmed feeling un-judged despite arriving expecting a lecture.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally attuned, precisely capturing Ryan's core dilemma between protecting himself and fitting in. Ryan's responses ('when you put it like that...') confirmed he felt deeply understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on the target behavior with each exchange building momentum from strengths to values clarification to concrete planning and success metrics. The session progressed logically from contemplation toward commitment with a follow-up scheduled.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction operated fully between equal partners, with the counselor asking rather than telling at every juncture. The client's ideas directly shaped the session's content, including the follow-up plan he himself proposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Ryan's ownership of all choices, with every safety strategy originating from him rather than imposed advice. Setbacks were framed as 'information, not failure,' and he was proactively invited to revise the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every meaningful change element — the buddy system, crew-safe reframe, Sunday check-in, and 'two wins a month' metric — originated from the client's own words. His surprise at being asked his opinion confirmed motivation was elicited, not installed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned expertise within the client ('You know your world better than I do'), and the plan's components all came from Ryan. He noted this power-sharing differed from his expectations of being lectured.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked strong change talk through strategic questions about values and commitment, reinforcing each instance with affirmations highlighting client ownership. Ryan progressed from desire language to explicit commitment ('willing to try the plan').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk and redirected effectively with double-sided reflections, but some moments (e.g., 'nobody wants to be that guy') were moved past quickly rather than deeply explored. More explicit double-sided reflections could have deepened ambivalence resolution.
