# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validating the patient's frustration without labeling or disapproval. Affirmations were specific but brief, and ambivalence about the long-standing habit was not deeply normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including the nuanced complex reflection that the daughter's worry feels like a threat to her quiet breaks. Some reflections stayed at surface-level summaries and the counselor did not reflect her expressed shame about feeling like a liar with her daughter.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifted from smoking cessation to daughter-communication coaching for the entire second half. The counselor never returned to smoking change talk or linked the role-play back to the quitting goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was permission-seeking and responsiveness to feedback, but the role-play coaching tipped into counselor expertise with scripts, tips, and evaluations. The patient's final sentence was largely a condensation of the counselor's words rather than a co-constructed product.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently respected autonomy, mirroring 'I'm not ready to quit' without pushing and offering choices throughout. It fell short of a 5 because ownership of the change process was never explicitly affirmed and the pivot to scripted coaching edged toward expert direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplied content (scripts, coping tips, communication strategies) rather than evoking the patient's own motivation for quitting. The one strong evocative question about the breaks was not followed up, and emergent change talk was left undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor positioned the patient as decision-maker and honored her request not to make a big plan, but the menu-driven structure felt formulaic. When offering scripted lines, the counselor shifted into expert mode rather than eliciting the patient's own words first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged but was largely client-generated rather than deliberately cultivated. The counselor missed a key opportunity to deepen 'Maybe I'll think about that part' and never asked about her own reasons for quitting or importance/confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing with sustain talk and did not lecture, which is a strength. However, extensive exploration of smoking's rewards was not counterbalanced with reasons for change, and there were no double-sided reflections or strategic redirection toward change language.
