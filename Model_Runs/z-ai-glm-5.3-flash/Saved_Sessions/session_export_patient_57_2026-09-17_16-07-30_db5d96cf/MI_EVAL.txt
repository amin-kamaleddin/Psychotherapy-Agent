# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout; affirmed Jamal's honesty and validated team loyalty without labeling. Acceptance was somewhat undermined by the abrupt confidentiality disclosure at the end, which created distress without sufficient emotional containment beforehand. Earlier, proactive scaffolding of confidentiality limits would demonstrate fuller unconditional regard.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections consistently accurate — captured the peer-pressure dynamic, fear of looking "soft," and worry about eligibility. Empathy break occurred around confidentiality: Jamal's fear of coach discovery was met with a rules-based disclosure rather than first deeply reflecting his fear. Reflective listening sometimes formulaic, repeating 0–10 confidence prompts even after Jamal noted the numbers hadn't changed.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on reducing alcohol use at team events: pressure situations, refusal strategies, ally recruitment, contingency planning. The final plan (text Marcus, nonalcoholic drink, Tyrell backup, exit option) is concrete and behavior-focused. Minor drift when Jamal raised the coaches/confidentiality concern, though this was legitimately connected to the referral context.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options were offered rather than prescribed, and Jamal's own wording was adopted. However, the counselor dominated the agenda with a repetitive question loop — confidence scales asked at least three times and role-play offered three times — which Jamal explicitly flagged. A collaborative partner would have shifted toward Jamal's expressed priority (the AD/coaches question) earlier.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated choice offerings ("Which feels most doable?", "Which would you rather use?") and explicit acceptance of Jamal's preference for casual texting over provided scripts. Change framed as Jamal's plan and decisions. Weakened by continued role-play pressure after refusal and by pivoting to the coaches/AD plan without first letting Jamal decide what he wanted from that conversation.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited Jamal's own ideas — Marcus as ally, his natural texting style, Tyrell, Gatorade, Uber backup. But evocation was somewhat mechanical: heavy reliance on the same confidence ruler and structured option menus rather than open questions drawing out deeper values (what the team means to him, who he wants to be). Jamal's best motivation came from his own statements rather than skillful evocation; identity-based motivation was underdeveloped.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuinely positioned Jamal as expert at times ("Tell me your side," "Your casual text idea fits you better"). However, repeatedly pushed role-play despite two declines, and asked near-identical questions about confidence numbers and next steps multiple times, showing limited attunement to client cues. Client expertise shaped content, but the agenda remained counselor-driven.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance/confidence rulers and elicitation of concrete ideas. Jamal produced meaningful change talk: "texting him tonight," confidence rising from 4–5 to 8, adding Tyrell, and "this was more useful than I thought it'd be." Missed opportunities to deepen — e.g., not exploring what "I know that it's bad for me" means to him specifically, and not reflecting his self-initiated commitment as a strength.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal offered considerable sustain talk ("everyone does it," fear of looking soft, worry Marcus gets pulled in, exit plan "feels like already losing"). Reflections were accurate but neutral; no double-sided reflections or reframes used to resolve ambivalence. Sustain talk was acknowledged but not strategically softened — e.g., peer loyalty could have been leveraged *for* staying sober via Marcus.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** 3.6/5
