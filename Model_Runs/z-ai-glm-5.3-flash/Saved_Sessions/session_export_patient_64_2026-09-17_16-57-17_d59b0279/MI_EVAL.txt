# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Jordan's concerns about side effects, work, and cost. Affirmations are sparse and formulaic, lacking explicit affirmation of Jordan's strengths like his loyalty to his grandparents.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures both sides of Jordan's ambivalence and his practical concerns. Reflections stay at the content level and never explore the deeper emotional theme, such as the guilt implied in 'that'd be on me.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains an exceptionally clear focus on flu vaccination from the opening question through the concrete close, with every exchange moving toward an actionable outcome. The session culminates in a commitment with a specific plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor behaves largely as an equal partner, offering agenda choices and co-editing texts based on Jordan's input. At times the counselor supplies plan structures and checklists for Jordan to select from rather than inviting him to generate ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly frames everything as Jordan's choice, asks permission before sharing information, and pivots immediately when Jordan declines role-play. Some message blocks (side effects, safety, logistics) are delivered as fairly directive bundles rather than with the same choice-based framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently draws motivation from within Jordan, asking what matters most and what a 'real reason' would look like, producing self-generated commitment and a readiness jump from 2–3 to 6. Jordan explicitly credits the non-lecturing approach as making it easier to say yes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan is repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker, and his contributions substantially shape the session. Information sharing is at times dense and checklist-driven, slipping slightly into expert-delivery mode.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of open-ended questions, the readiness ruler, and the pivotal question about protecting grandparents elicits Jordan's own desire and commitment language. His conclusion that 'there's no good excuse left' demonstrates highly successful change talk cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan's resistance is acknowledged with neutral, brief reflections and redirected toward family protection, with no arguing or disagreement. Some reflections repeat sustain talk nearly verbatim without double-sided pivots toward change, and his final 'I still don't think I need it' goes unaddressed.
