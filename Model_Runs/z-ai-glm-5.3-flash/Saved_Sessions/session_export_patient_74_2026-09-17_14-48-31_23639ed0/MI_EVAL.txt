# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, honoring the patient's boundary around not quitting. Affirmations, however, are generic ('that makes sense') and lack individualized recognition of her strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly and accurately reflects the patient's core conflict between protecting her kids and valuing her after-dinner cigarette. Reflections tend toward content summary and miss opportunities to capture deeper emotional nuance like her guilt and fear for her daughter.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking and children's exposure throughout, progressing logically from exploration to planning. Never explicitly confirms a shared goal, and the target quietly narrows to exposure reduction without a collaborative decision point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaves as an equal partner, offering options as menus and incorporating the patient's own ideas (jacket, one-week trial, sweater layering). Occasionally delivers dense expert-driven checklists rather than letting ideas fully emerge from the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongest domain: every decision point returns choice to the patient, and her boundaries (no reminders, no husband involvement, no quitting talk) are honored without protest. Consistently reinforces that she owns the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses a confidence ruler and evocative questions that draw out incremental commitment, but focuses on logistics rather than the patient's deeper values. Fails to explore the discrepancy she voices between being a good example and enjoying her cigarettes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly solicits input and lets the patient's choices shape the plan, which she genuinely drives. At times delivers informational blocks without checking whether she shares that framing, tipping slightly toward an advisory role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes incremental change talk ('I'll give it my best shot,' 'a whole week of trying — that I can do') via confidence rulers and cue-based planning. Change talk stays limited to harm-reduction steps rather than deepening motivation about quitting itself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently pivots away from sustain talk without reinforcing or dwelling on barriers. However, the patient's 'I'm not quitting' statements are sidestepped rather than strategically softened with double-sided reflections or reframing.
