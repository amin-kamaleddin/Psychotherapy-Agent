# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and non-judgmental throughout, with genuine affirmations of Erik's progress. Affirmations occasionally remained general and could have conveyed more explicit unconditional regard when Erik was hardest on himself (e.g., calling his treadmill effort 'pathetic').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captured both sides of Erik's ambivalence and mirrored his own metaphors ('competing against the old Erik'). Reflections sometimes functioned as transitions to questions rather than fully exploring emotional pain, such as when Erik described turning progress into 'another way to judge myself.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus on target behaviors—gym consistency, self-comparison, and diet—with steady forward momentum. Guided progressively toward a concrete plan (writing the note tonight, telling Anna at dinner) without ever imposing it.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently invited Erik's ideas, and every strategy (note, headphones, Anna's role) emerged from the client. Erik actively refined his own plan mid-session ('Maybe I combine them'), demonstrating a genuinely co-constructed approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ownership of decisions was consistently reinforced ('That's you designing something that fits your workout, your life'), with options offered as choices rather than directives. No persuasive or controlling language appeared, and Erik responded with self-directed commitments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited entirely from within Erik via questions like 'What does that version of you want?', drawing out his core values and self-generated problem-solving. His strongest change talk, including refusing the sundae, developed from the counselor's obstacle-focused questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located within Erik ('That's your expertise about yourself showing'), and all strategies originated from him. The counselor never offered unsolicited advice, positioning themselves as a thinking partner rather than a director.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoked rich desire, need, and commitment talk ('He's more confident... keep the weight coming off slowly'; 'I'll tell her tonight'). The counselor consistently reflected and amplified Erik's change language rather than substituting their own.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was largely not reinforced; discouragement was acknowledged briefly and pivoted toward strengths and solutions ('and yet you keep showing up'). However, some sustain talk—fear of using the note against himself and the diet as a 'weak spot'—received brief acknowledgment without strategic reframing.
