# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with validated concerns about peer perception, but few individualized affirmations of Jamal's own insights.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections early on, but the session shifted heavily into advice-giving and the core fear of losing team belonging was never deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, clear focus on reducing alcohol use in the team-party context, ending with specific, measurable next steps and a follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Role-play was genuinely collaborative, but earlier option lists and pre-written scripts made the counselor function more as a skills coach than an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Jamal's choices and let him pick scripts and role-play formats, though long prescriptive lists subtly steered toward the counselor's plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied by the counselor rather than elicited; almost no evocative questions about Jamal's own reasons, values, or desired outcomes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission before offering options, but the middle session was expert-driven and never leveraged Jamal's genuine expertise about his teammates.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The readiness ruler elicited a 4/10 but was never followed up with 'Why a 4 and not lower?'; most change language came from the counselor's agenda.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with neutral acceptance rather than reinforcement, but there was little active redirection, such as double-sided reflections pivoting to Jamal's performance concerns.
