**Comments:** Fully collaborative: "together we could explore both," "Which feels most important to start with?" Plan co-constructed from David's own ideas (break-time changes, telling his wife). Expert-role redirect when asked "What would you pick if you were me?"

#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of client ownership: "it's your head and your job on the line." Information offered transparently ("straight numbers"); all decisions (medication, date, strategies) originate from David. No pressure or coercion present.

#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from the client rather than lectured: father's death at 61, blood pressure scare, wife's birthday, and self-realization about "waiting for perfect conditions." Evocative questions produced unprompted commitment language and a self-generated action plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located within the client ("you know your body and your workdays better than anyone"). David's contributions substantially shape session direction—the hallmark of a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation-to-commitment arc: eliciting visions ("what would it look like if it worked?"), deepening reasons, and inviting commitment. #### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk generally redirected effectively—reframed "waiting for a calm stretch" into client's own realization; side-effect worries acknowledged then returned to client decision-making. Not a 5 only because the side-effect exchange was explored at moderate length rather than more rapidly pivoted, though it served informed choice.
