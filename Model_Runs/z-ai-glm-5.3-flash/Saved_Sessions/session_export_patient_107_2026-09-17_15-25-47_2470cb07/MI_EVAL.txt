# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; the client explicitly noted he expected a lecture and appreciated there wasn't one. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and not individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening, including double-sided reflections capturing ambivalence. Reflections stayed somewhat plan-focused rather than exploring deeper emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on evening caffeine throughout, with logical progression from exploration to psychoeducation to a concrete experiment, barrier planning, and follow-up date. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Joint planning with menu options and negotiation, producing a client-endorsed plan. However, the framework (ratings, scales, experiment structure) originated from the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent autonomy support: repeated choices, explicit permission to slip without judgment, and acceptance of the client's preconditions without pushback. No pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited doability judgments and curiosity, but core motivations, values, and the personal meaning of outcomes were never explored. Much content came from the counselor rather than the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and preference questions were present, but the agenda was largely coach-driven, with the client selecting from counselor-generated options rather than co-creating ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evocative questions successfully elicited commitment and ability language ('that I can manage', 'proper go'). Missed opportunities to deepen or amplify change talk once it appeared.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was frequent but handled well via double-sided reflections, scaled-down options, and no arguing. Some skepticism was merely restated, and 'numbers will come back the same' predictions went unreframed.
