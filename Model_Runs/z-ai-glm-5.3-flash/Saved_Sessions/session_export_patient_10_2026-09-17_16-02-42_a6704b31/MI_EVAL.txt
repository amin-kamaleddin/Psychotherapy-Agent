# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental; framed childcare as the barrier rather than blaming the client. Affirmations were somewhat generic and brief rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured content but stayed at surface level, missing the emotional weight of shame, disappointment, and the meaning of her kids being her 'why.' Session moved quickly to problem-solving rather than reflecting feelings at depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on the childcare barrier with logical progression (barrier, options, rehearsal, text, backup plan). Momentum was often driven by the psychiatrist's rapid-fire task lists rather than collaboratively paced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-way — Amina's ideas (childcare swap, sister backup, interview prep) drove the plan. Fell short of 5 because the psychiatrist tended to present option menus and ready-made scripts rather than building on the client's answers first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and reinforced Amina's ownership of her plan and confidence. However, frequently supplying pre-drafted texts and scripts borders on doing the work for the client rather than trusting her own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out Amina's own motivations ('the kids are my why') and had her generate her own plan steps. Lacked deliberate, strategic evocation such as exploring how success would feel or querying the discrepancy between past avoidance and stated determination.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Amina as the expert, offering choice points and letting her preferences shape the session. Supplied much of the actual content (scripts, replies) rather than primarily drawing solutions from her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited strong commitment language ('I'm not doing that again,' 'I'll be in that room') and a confidence rating of 10. Rarely deepened change talk with evocative follow-ups, relying instead on offering concrete options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and never reinforced, with effective reframes pivoting toward change. However, redirections were somewhat abrupt — problem-solving came before validating reflections, making pivots less gentle.
