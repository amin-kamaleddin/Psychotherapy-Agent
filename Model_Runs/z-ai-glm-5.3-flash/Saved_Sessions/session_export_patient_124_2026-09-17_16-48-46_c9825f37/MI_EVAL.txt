# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with warm affirmations ('That's excellent,' 'I believe you can do this') and a shame-free slip plan ('notice without judging'). Could be elevated to a 5 with more individualized affirmations of Sofia's specific strengths, such as her honesty and creative privacy workaround.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening, accurately capturing Sofia's ambivalence ('takes the edge off... but you're worried about money, morning fog') and validating her readiness rating. Reflections were occasionally brief and bundled with multiple questions, limiting deeper emotional exploration of her anxiety and family pressures.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained an exceptionally clear, unwavering focus on the target behavior (marijuana use) across every turn, with each response tied to the concrete action plan. Questions consistently moved the session forward, culminating in a specific, measurable two-week experiment with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely a joint enterprise, with rules offered as options and Sofia's contributions (her own refusal lines, note titles, check-in preference) substantially shaping the plan. Several turns delivered counselor-generated content (breathing scripts, line templates) with limited space for Sofia's input, making power-sharing strong but not fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Sofia's ownership through menus of choices, 'I can help explore options if you'd like,' and 'I'm here either way,' while the slip plan explicitly removed shame. Her confidence rising from 6 to 7 and her own 'I'll actually try' reflect genuine autonomous commitment rather than compliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within via readiness and confidence rulers, values-connection questions, and choice-based prompts that elicited Sofia's own reasons (fog, finals, cost, mom's expectations). Occasionally shifted to supplying information and tactics rather than consistently deepening Sofia's own reasons and hopes for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly conveyed that expertise resides with Sofia ('Which rule feels most doable?', 'Which one fits you best?'), and the plan was clearly co-created rather than prescribed. Some turns were advice-dense with lists of lines and tactics, slightly tipping the balance toward the counselor as solution-generator.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked change language through readiness/confidence rulers, values questions, and choice-based prompts, with the confidence re-rating (6 to 7) skillfully capturing mobilization. Limited deepening through evocative follow-ups (e.g., 'What would feeling less foggy mean for you?') and some reliance on closed questions kept this from a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying Sofia's substantial sustain talk, responding with neutral acknowledgment and pivoting toward experiments, supported by a constructive double-sided summary. A few sustain statements were passed over rather than gently reframed, and solutions were sometimes supplied too quickly instead of letting Sofia resolve ambivalence herself.
