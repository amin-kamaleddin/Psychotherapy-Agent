# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a non-judgmental, normalizing tone throughout, but repeated formulaic safety checks (despite no indication of distress) felt generic, and there was little individualized affirmation of Anya's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections were present, but key emotional moments were bypassed — her worry about the fuzzy night, her question 'does that one night mean something?', and her fear about the school record were all met with information or deliverables rather than reflective exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed broadly focused on alcohol screening and party drinking, but the opening drifted into generic mood screening and the exam-stress topic was dropped without closure. Momentum came largely from the client, with the counselor re-offering the same two deliverables.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration was genuine on the concrete plan (two-drink limit, Priya as buddy, emoji signal), but much of the session followed an advice-giver dynamic with lists of pre-packaged content, making power-sharing uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear strength: choices were repeatedly offered (menus, drafting options), Anya's edits to the plan were accepted, and there was no pressure to self-label as problematic. Falls short of top marks because tip-delivery dominated over helping Anya own her reasons for change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was rarely drawn from within the client; the default move was supplying tips, plans, and drafts. Golden opportunities — like her question about how to know when something 'feels off' — were missed for exploring her own instincts, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was real power-sharing in offering menus and co-authoring the plan, but the advice-heavy structure with pre-made plans and a pre-drafted school letter positioned the counselor as expert rather than drawing on Anya's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerged ('that fuzzy night did freak me out,' 'I'd pace myself more') but was never reflected, deepened, or reinforced — no evocative questions, confidence rulers, or values exploration. The change talk developed incidentally rather than through skillful cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anya's sustain talk ('everyone does that,' 'I'm not an at-risk kid') was tolerated but not strategically managed — normalizing responses validated it without pivoting, and no double-sided reflections were used to resolve her ambivalence.
