# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone, affirming Selene's self-awareness and normalizing slips. Affirmations were occasionally brief and formulaic, not deeply individualized to her broader strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured Selene's ambivalence, chaotic mornings, and desire for self-proof. Deeper emotional layers — her fear of letting others down and history of failure — were noted but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on medication adherence throughout, with every exchange advancing the concrete goal. The session concluded with a specific, measurable, time-bound plan without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was a genuine joint product shaped substantially by Selene's own ideas. However, the counselor sometimes offered pre-formatted option menus (pillbox, sachet, alarm) rather than consistently drawing solutions from her first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforced Selene's ownership of the plan, respected her explicit preferences (no nagging, no scare tactics), and never pressured. Selene's closing remark — 'You didn't lecture me' — confirms she experienced genuine choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence scaling, barrier exploration, and open questions effectively surfaced Selene's own motivations (proving it to herself, staying healthy for family). Missed opportunities to deepen emerging motivation — e.g., her statement about self-trust and believing the ticks matter — limited the score.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly solicited Selene's input and her contributions genuinely shaped the plan. Occasional solution menus leaned toward advice-giving rather than fully eliciting her own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation of change talk produced several commitment statements ('I'm ready,' 'I'll give it an honest go'). Emerging motivation was sometimes left unamplified — the counselor moved to logistics instead of deepening moments like her wish to believe the ticks matter.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ('does it really matter if I miss one?,' 'I half-expect to fail') was generally not amplified but was often bypassed rather than strategically addressed. Double-sided reflections linking ambivalence to her stated values (health for her family) were underused.
