# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard. Explicitly disarms judgment ("I'm not here to judge you or side with anyone"), validates defensiveness, and affirms identity claims ("someone who thinks things through") without labeling. Affirmations occasionally feel slightly performative ("I love how practical that is"), but regard remains warm and nonjudgmental throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening. Complex reflections capture surface and underlying feelings (e.g., "unfairly watched and labeled… underneath that, it was embarrassing"). Occasionally leans on interpretation ("I love how…") over pure reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior across every turn. Session progresses coherently from referral event → ambivalence → conditions for change → concrete experiment with Jake → success image → follow-up plan. Each turn advances the change process; final plan is specific and time-bound.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine equal partnership; plan (texting Jake, post-party check-in) generated entirely by the client. Reframes are sometimes so polished they slightly steer the conversation (e.g., "Those feel like your only two options right now"), though client responses confirm real power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook-level. Ownership repeatedly returned to the client: "this one's yours to weigh, at your pace," "No promises, no programs—that's yours to decide." Choice-offering question about follow-up timing; no pressure, persuasion, or coercion anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element originates from the client—Jake idea, "reason ready," Sunday-morning benefit, success criteria, check-in timing. No advice or prescriptive content. Questions like "what would make the difference for you?" are pure evocation; concentrated more in the latter half.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located with the client ("You know your world best"); client's contributions (teammates, buddy plan) genuinely redirect the session. Slightly short of 5 because contributions are amplified rather than allowed to substantially restructure the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation across categories: desire/ability ("what would make the difference for you?"), reasons (Sunday-morning clarity, playing better), and commitment-adjacent language ("I'll text him," "I'll come find you"). Change talk reflected and deepened effectively—near-exemplary.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled without arguing or reinforcing; double-sided reflection after minimization is skilled, and "I'm not saying I'm quitting" is reframed as a conditional plan. However, some sustain talk is reflected back fully without a strong pivot ("two nights a month feels pretty normal to you"), and discrepancy between drinking and stated values is not developed as directly as possible.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.5/5**

Strong MI fidelity with a defensive adolescent expecting a lecture. Standouts: autonomy support and direction (5s), with excellent change talk cultivation and evocation. Weaker areas: softening sustain talk (3), where minimization is sometimes reflected without strong pivots or developed discrepancy, and partnership/collaboration (4), where polished reframes slightly outrun the client's contributions. Overall a well-executed MI session with minor room to deepen discrepancy work and more fully honor sustain talk before pivoting.
