# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout with strong validation of her anger and fear, and reassuring confidentiality about her crying. However, explicit affirmations of her strengths (persistence over four years, self-awareness) were lacking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured her frustration and fear of dismissal. Reflections occasionally stayed transactional—her shame about her son and isolation were disclosed but not explicitly reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus stayed firmly on the target problem, with each turn advancing concrete steps (diary, referral email, follow-up timeline, safety advice). A closing summary naming the emerging behavioral focus was missing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drafts were co-edited, her corrections incorporated, and she chose which lines to use—decisions were genuinely co-constructed. Deducted because the counselor tended to supply ready-made solutions rather than drawing out her own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support, notably affirming her choice to send the email immediately rather than follow the suggested plan. Advice, while permission-seeking, was somewhat prescriptive in content.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk surfaced largely incidentally rather than through intentional evocative questions; no open questions explored her hopes, reasons, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aizada was consistently positioned as the decision-maker, offering menu choices and inviting edits, and her contributions clearly shaped the session. Some content (diary fields, drafts) was supplied without first eliciting her own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged mostly spontaneously and was indirectly elicited through option-offering; key moments like 'I shouldn't be hiding from him' were not deepened, and no confidence/importance scaling was used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided argument and deftly sidestepped psychological framing when she rejected it, pivoting to practical action. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections acknowledging her sustain talk alongside her change talk.
