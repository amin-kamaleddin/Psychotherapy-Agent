# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, non-judgmental tone with repeated affirmations of the client's cautious instincts. However, affirmations became repetitive, and re-offering materials after closure slightly undermined respect for the client's stated wishes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some early reflections showed understanding of the client's goals and worry, but the session was dominated by information delivery. The client's expressed distress at nearly starting a dangerous combination was not explored or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong focus on the client's stated goals and a critical safety issue, producing concrete action steps. The final third became repetitive after the client signaled closure, diluting momentum without truly wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was predominantly expert-driven, with long informational monologues and counselor-generated options. The client's contributions were praised but did not shape the session's direction, leaving her mostly an information recipient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive framing and no pressure on medication decisions, but the advice-heavy structure and four repeated unsolicited offers after the client declined edged toward controlling the interaction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A good evocative opening question was not sustained; motivation and safety guidance were provided from the expert position rather than elicited. No exploration of the client's confidence, reasons, or own strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role with dense informational blocks and rhetorical offers rather than genuine invitations for the client's expertise. The counselor's agenda dominated throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client volunteered change-oriented statements (e.g., planning to call her prescriber and hold the medication), which were affirmed but not deepened. No evocative questions were used to amplify commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was little sustain talk, and the counselor did not reinforce any resistance, redirecting worry toward action. The criterion was not strongly tested in this low-ambivalence context, limiting demonstrated skill.
