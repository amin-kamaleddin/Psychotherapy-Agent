# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, framing screening as routine care and validating Marek's enjoyment of drinking. Affirmations were somewhat generic ('Nice,' 'sensible') and lacked deeper unconditional regard or normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured Marek's ambivalence, social identity, and worry about mates' teasing. Reflections were somewhat formulaic ('that makes sense') and the emotional layer beneath his rationalizations wasn't deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use, guiding from awareness toward a concrete, measurable one-weekend plan. Some extended psychoeducation and option-listing slowed momentum, and the plan could have been tied more explicitly to Marek's stated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed the session as joint work, with permission-seeking before education and a co-constructed final plan from Marek's own preferences. Pre-packaged menus of tactics with brief invitations at times made the exchange feel more like advice-dispensing than genuine collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support — Marek's ownership was explicitly reinforced throughout ('totally your choice,' 'no promises'), advice was offered conditionally, and his refusal to commit or reject options was honored without pushback.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened with evocative questions and elicited some priorities and change talk, but much of the session relied on the counselor supplying information and option lists. Marek's key motivations (health, social identity) were not deeply explored, with no follow-up probing or confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Marek as the decision-maker, and his own ideas substantially shaped the final plan. Option lists were fairly prescriptive and the counselor occasionally drifted into an advisor role rather than drawing plans out of the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked change language, eliciting meaningful commitments ('I've got something to think about,' 'I'll give it a shot') and deepened them by exploring implementation and barriers. Moved too quickly to option-listing rather than eliciting more of Marek's own reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk and responded with acceptance rather than confrontation. However, sustain talk (e.g., 'I'll change if I get sick') was accepted without strategic reframes or double-sided reflections to shift ambivalence toward change.
