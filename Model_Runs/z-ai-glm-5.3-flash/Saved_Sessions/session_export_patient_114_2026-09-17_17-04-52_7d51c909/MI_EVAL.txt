# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard—validating frustration, normalizing peer pressure, and explicitly removing label pressure ('You don't have to label anything'). No judgment or shaming appeared anywhere, even when Ryan minimized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening, capturing Ryan's ambivalence between social life and protecting his GPA/parents' trust. Deducted for question barrages (up to five stacked questions in one turn) and never directly reflecting the humiliation Ryan expressed about the arrest.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior throughout with zero topic drift—every turn tied back to alcohol-related risk. Guided progressively toward a specific, actionable plan (3pm text, reminder, rehearsed refusal lines) that Ryan recapped himself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was largely co-constructed—Ryan originated the study group idea and selected/refined options while the counselor role-played the peer. Slipped briefly into a consultative cadence mid-session, offering interventions in rapid succession rather than fully joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice and ownership, which Ryan clearly perceived ('I'm not letting this plan fall apart'). Multi-option menus, while choice-affirming, sometimes felt counselor-generated, and Ryan's autonomy could have been affirmed more directly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Ryan via goal-oriented open questions, scaling, and value-discrepancy exploration, producing genuine change talk ('Importance? Probably like a 7'). Evocation was strongest in the first half; the latter shifted toward plan execution with fewer opportunities to develop deeper motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Ryan as the expert ('Which one feels most doable?'), and his contributions substantially shaped the session's plan. Rapid-fire question style in a few turns occasionally took over the conversational steering rather than following Ryan's lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through confidence/importance scaling and discrepancy exploration ('How would staying out of trouble support your goals?'), and rehearsed value-based refusal lines. Strongest change talk moments ('I care about my grades') were not deepened with affirmations or elaboration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing ('one bad night doesn't mean I've got a problem') and pivoted to future-oriented questions. Sustain talk was sometimes mirrored back in summaries rather than actively redirected toward change.
