# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; normalized Jordan's frustration about being judged and affirmed his care for his grandparents. However, affirmations were brief and somewhat formulaic ('that makes sense'), with no individualized affirmation of his autonomy or character.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were adequate but brief and formulaic, often immediately followed by multiple-choice menus. Key emotional material—resentment of being controlled, fear of being 'the guy who brought it home,' dinner-table conflict worries—was touched once but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed anchored to the vaccination decision with a clear progressive arc from resistance to planning. Score lowered because direction was driven by the counselor's menus rather than strategic MI direction, with early momentum slow to develop.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—information was offered on request, reactions checked, and a plan co-created (script choice, timing, role-play). But ideas flowed mostly from counselor to client; Jordan shaped details, not session structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy messaging throughout ('You choose the pace and topic'), with no pressure or lecturing. After Jordan's deferral, the structured menus occasionally felt like steering, and there was little explicit affirmation of his ownership of the final decision at the close.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Best move was following the grandparents thread, drawing out genuinely client-generated motivation. Otherwise the session relied on counselor-provided information, scripts, and menus, with no open questions inviting Jordan's own reasons, confidence exploration, or solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan was repeatedly asked what he wanted to focus on, but near-constant numbered menus meant the counselor structured the agenda. When Jordan deferred ('whatever you think is useful'), the counselor offered more options rather than exploring his ambivalence about ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively capitalized on the grandparents theme, eliciting authentic change talk ('that's not nothing,' 'maybe I could get it before my next visit') and moved Jordan to conditional planning. Missing: confidence/importance scaling, deepened commitment language, and a firm plan by session's end.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without endorsement, avoiding argument, but repeated back multiple times. No double-sided reflections paired sustain talk with emerging change talk, and ambivalence was managed rather than actively resolved.
