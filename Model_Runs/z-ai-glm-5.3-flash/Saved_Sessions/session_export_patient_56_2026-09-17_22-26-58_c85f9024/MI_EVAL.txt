# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates warmth and unconditional positive regard, with genuine affirmations like 'You've given a lot of thought to this' and 'That's real self-awareness.' No judgment or disapproval appears anywhere, even when the client admits to failing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately tracks the client's core insight about small present-moment habits, including the emotionally attuned response to 'rough day, but I'm glad to be here.' Reflections occasionally stay at summary level rather than deepening into the emotional meaning beneath moments like 'snapping' or 'quiet dinners in a bad way.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear, consistent focus on presence and family values with no topic drift. Each question builds strategically toward a concrete, client-generated one-week experiment with a defined review plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is largely an exchange between equals, with 'us' and 'together' framing and a plan built entirely from the client's own ideas. However, the counselor never explicitly checks whether her agenda matches the client's, which would demonstrate fuller power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces the client's ownership, offering choice in the review plan, respecting her judgment about whether the car-pause is even worth changing, and validating her self-defined success measure as 'a wiser one.' The experiment is proposed softly and the client freely shapes its terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor's primary technique is eliciting the client's own wisdom — every key insight, including 'deciding before the engine starts,' came from her. Some turns close with affirming summaries rather than further evocative questions, and confidence/ambivalence about the new plan is never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positions the client as the expert, and her contributions genuinely shape the session — the plan emerged entirely from her ideas. Some evaluative summaries ('That's a real shift in how you measure your life') frame rather than co-create, and there's no explicit agenda negotiation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful questioning ('what have you noticed already helps you come back?') elicited the client's toolkit and commitment talk ('I'll notice how the evenings land'). The counselor stops short of 5 because there's no explicit exploration of confidence or importance regarding the new plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal given the client is in maintenance, but where it appeared ('I fail and walk in scattered'), the counselor responded well by reframing failure as a 'safety net' and asking 'or is that even worth changing?'. Little opportunity existed to demonstrate active pivoting under ambivalence.
