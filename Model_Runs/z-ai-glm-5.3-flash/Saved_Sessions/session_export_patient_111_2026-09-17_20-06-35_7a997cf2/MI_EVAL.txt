# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, with a warm, nonjudgmental tone even amid patient irritability and sustain talk. No labeling, disapproval, or pressure was present at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including the insightful distinction between the ritual and the substance ('the cigarette itself, or that quiet time?') that unlocked the patient's key realization. Reflections tracked emotional nuance without distortion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, progressive focus on smoking change, moving deliberately from engagement to a concrete, patient-paced plan. Each question served the target behavior without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative, with the plan's key elements (porch coffee, slip protocol, follow-up) originating from the patient and shaped jointly. The closing follow-up was framed as an invitation the patient accepted and modified himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively reinforced ('being here is your choice,' 'your own terms'), echoed back by the patient as 'my terms, my pace.' The psychiatrist never prescribed and accepted cutting back rather than quitting without pushback.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client, never argued for by the psychiatrist. By the end, the patient articulated reasons partly his own ('maybe a little for me'), evidencing internal rather than imposed motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located within the client ('those are your ideas, not mine'), and the psychiatrist explicitly refused the expert role when asked 'Would that count?'. All major plan elements demonstrably originated from the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic open questions evoked and deepened change talk, from a tentative 'cut back some' to concrete commitments ('I'd try it. For her. And maybe a little for me'). The summary amplified commitment language and the session ended with the patient proposing his own follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with double-sided reflections rather than argument and never reinforced, with effective reframing of the patient's attendance as meaningful. A few early reflections briefly gave air to status quo reasons (routine attachment) before pivoting.
