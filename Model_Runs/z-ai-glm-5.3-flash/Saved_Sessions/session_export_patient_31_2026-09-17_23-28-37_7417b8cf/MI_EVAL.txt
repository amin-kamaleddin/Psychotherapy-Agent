# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout — evening cigarettes were framed as 'a real choice, not a failure,' and slips were normalized rather than judged. The client's closing thanks for 'actually listening instead of just lecturing' confirms this was experienced.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were highly accurate and deep, including meaning-level statements like 'it's the stillness... the one moment all day that belongs to you.' Reflections captured both content and feeling without repetition.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focus stayed on the target behavior with clear strategic momentum, following the client's redirect toward daytime replacement. Each exchange advanced the plan: cravings timing → strategy → success criteria → start date → follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely collaborative, with every plan element co-constructed from the client's own ideas and the clinician asking rather than telling. When the client corrected course, the clinician immediately followed, keeping his agenda in the driver's seat.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was repeatedly reinforced ('wherever you land on this, you set the pace here,' 'your way, not mine'), and permission to keep evening cigarettes was explicitly honored. Slips were framed as data, not failure, with no pressure toward full cessation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from the client's own values, strategies, and self-knowledge rather than supplied — the clinician never cited health risks or external reasons. His unprompted closing openness to eventually changing the evening cigarettes is the clearest evidence motivation was evoked, not installed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('you're the expert on what that break does for you'), and all plan elements originated from David. The clinician contributed reflections and open questions, not prescriptions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully elicited progressively stronger commitment language, culminating in a concrete, scheduled plan. The clinician reinforced David's change talk by highlighting it in his own words ('opening a door you'd said was closed').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and legitimized rather than argued with, and the clinician consistently pivoted toward the client's stated yes ('you're saying yes to something: the daytime part'). The score falls short of 5 because sustain talk received generous airtime during the early ritual exploration, though this ultimately served engagement.
