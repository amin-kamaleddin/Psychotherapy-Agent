# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive with affirmations for journaling, rehearsing, and planning, though praise is somewhat formulaic and brief. Affirmations are often followed immediately by additional questions rather than individualized recognition of Samuel's specific efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections exist but are sparse relative to questions, and emotional content is often glossed over — Samuel's fear of folding at work and defensiveness with his wife go unreflected. Multiple stacked questions dominate many turns and crowd out deeper listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus maintained throughout — every turn ties back to smoking reduction, progressing logically from data review to planning, rehearsal, and contingency planning. Culminates in a clear, specific behavioral plan with fallbacks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed, with Samuel's preferences shaping every decision (script choice, walking strategy, rain contingency). Still, the counselor retains more conversational control than a fully equal partner through stacked questions and frequent suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames choices as Samuel's own — he sets the goal, picks strategies, and decides timing, with no coercion. However, autonomy is somewhat undercut by prescriptive scripts and ready-made fallback wording rather than letting Samuel fully generate his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reliably draws motivation from within through rulers, rehearsal, and open questions, with Samuel articulating his own reasons ('I can only control what I do with the urge'). Counselor sometimes answers for him by generating scripts and listing strategy menus.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mostly treats Samuel as the expert on his own life and incorporates his choices throughout. However, the counselor frequently slips into a coaching role — supplying scripts, strategy menus, and exact wording — which dilutes power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits change language via confidence rulers (6→8), open questions, and behavioral rehearsal that produced genuine commitment talk ('Nervous but ready'). Points deducted because the counselor sometimes supplies scripts and strategy menus, reducing purity of evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without reinforcing it and occasionally pivots toward change ('That shows you can change, even at work'). But repeated obstacle questions invite more sustain talk, and no double-sided reflections are used to resolve ambivalence.
