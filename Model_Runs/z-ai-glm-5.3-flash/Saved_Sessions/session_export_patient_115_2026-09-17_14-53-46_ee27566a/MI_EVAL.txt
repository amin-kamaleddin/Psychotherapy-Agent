# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with explicit safety-netting and affirmation of Samir's efforts like cutting smoking. Missed an opportunity to affirm autonomy over his choice to keep weekend drinking/smoking without any undertone of disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of frustration, fear of nighttime symptoms, and validation of partial progress throughout. Reflections were often diluted by bundled questions and planning content, leaving deeper feelings (wife's worry, financial strain) underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on asthma control and trigger reduction, with every exchange tied to inhalers, cab dust, or medical follow-up. Successfully steered from assessment to a concrete, measurable two-week plan aligned with Samir's goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated choice points and genuine incorporation of Samir's constraints (cost, time, customer perceptions) into the final plan. Uneven at times, with counselor-designed packages presented first rather than built from his ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language and graceful acceptance of Samir's refusal to commit to quitting or to take the doctor script immediately. Rapid-fire menus and detailed prescriptions occasionally narrowed his perceived options, and 'continued smoking will limit gains' bordered on mild pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions genuinely evoked Samir's motivation and doable commitments, but much of the session involved counselor-supplied information and advice. His own reasons ('I just want to breathe properly again') were elicited incidentally rather than deliberately explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early on Samir was positioned as the expert, but the counselor frequently shifted into expert-prescriber mode with a pre-built five-point plan and tracking template. Samir shaped choices within the counselor's menu, limiting true power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful scaling questions elicited strong change talk (importance 9, confidence rising to 7) and commitment language ('I'll start with the wipes and windows'). Change talk was not always deepened—for example, 'I just want to breathe properly again' was followed by assessment rather than amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk, redirecting focus to what Samir could control rather than arguing about smoking and drinking. A double-sided reflection could have better honored his ambivalence, avoiding the defensive reaction to the honest warning about smoking.
