# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with a defensive adolescent, using warm, age-appropriate affirmations like 'Nice choice' and 'You're taking control without quitting your friends.' Falls short of 5 due to somewhat formulaic praise and a missed opportunity to affirm Malik's honesty when he stated he wasn't ready to promise anything about quitting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening throughout, capturing both peer-belonging and mother-conflict themes in complex reflections. Kept from a 5 by occasionally transitional reflections and lack of deeper exploration of the emotional layers beneath Malik's dismissiveness, such as the stress of home surveillance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on substance use, peer influence, and parental conflict, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete, rehearsed plan. The focus centered on family negotiation rather than the substance use itself, and no bridge was set for revisiting cannabis use next session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely co-constructed, with Malik selecting the script, editing the text, and inventing his own check-in line. Collaboration was uneven, however, as the counselor often presented finished products (scripts, templates) rather than inviting Malik to draft his own wording first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Malik's choice ('Which step feels easiest?'), framed the plan as revocable, and accepted his boundary about not promising to quit without pressure. Falls short of 5 because the supplied scripts sometimes pre-empted Malik's own authorship and the counselor never explicitly affirmed that the cannabis decision remains entirely his.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Malik via open questions, a confidence ruler (7→9), and rehearsal, culminating in self-generated commitment ('Alright, I got this'). Evocation was channeled exclusively toward the parent relationship, never eliciting Malik's own reasons to reconsider use or exploring his 'wake-up call' comment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Started well by positioning Malik as the expert, but the counselor shifted into an advice-giving role mid-session, supplying numerous scripts, templates, and tips rather than first eliciting Malik's ideas. Malik's contributions shaped the final plan, but the volume of prescriptive content and lack of explicit acknowledgment of his expertise kept collaboration lukewarm.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through the 'What would you want to be different?' question, confidence rulers (7→8→9), and a commitment invitation leading to actual rehearsal of a message and check-in line. Limited by the absence of any attempt to evoke change talk about the cannabis use itself, leaving Malik's 'wake-up call' remark unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk by not arguing with Malik's minimization and instead redirecting to his values, and by accepting his 'not ready to promise' boundary while pivoting to honest-communication scripts. Not a 5 because double-sided reflections linking Malik's stated concerns (grades, a friend in trouble) back to substance use were never used, missing discrepancy-development opportunities.
