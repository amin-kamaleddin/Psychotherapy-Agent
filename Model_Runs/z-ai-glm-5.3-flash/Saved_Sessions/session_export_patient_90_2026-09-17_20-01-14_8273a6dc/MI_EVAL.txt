# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, including respectful handling of Marcus's refusal to rehome his dog ('he's family'). No judgment, labeling, or disapproval appears anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently and accurately captured Marcus's perspective, from the 'full plate' overwhelm to the emotional significance of his wife hearing him breathe at night. Even his final ambivalence and shift toward commitment were precisely reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful focus on asthma management throughout—moving from ambivalence to a doable behavior, a cue-based plan, success markers, and a concrete two-week follow-up. No topic drift; even the dog discussion was integrated into the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, building on Marcus's own idea (inhaler next to the dog food) with 'we' language and verification ('Did I get that right?'). Marcus explicitly experienced ownership: 'this one's mine. I set it up.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong support for choice and respect for Marcus's limits, including accepting his condition and validating that he's 'not there yet' on bigger changes. Could be strengthened by explicitly reiterating Marcus's freedom to modify or abandon the plan at follow-up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from Marcus via open elicitation ('what do you think might actually work for you?'), surfacing vivid intrinsic motivations (sleep, the hill, his wife's worry). The session ended with his own commitment: 'I'll be honest about what I notice.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned Marcus as the expert on his own life and credited his contributions substantially. Marcus clearly experienced ownership of the plan he designed himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and deepened through prompts like 'What's one thing in your day that feels most doable to tweak?' and success-imagery questions. The counselor reinforced Marcus's self-generated plan and his own change language ('I could try that,' 'that's the plan').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never amplified—the dog's non-negotiable status was honored without argument, and overwhelm was met by keeping the plan small rather than challenging it. Double-sided reflections acknowledged barriers while foregrounding change.
