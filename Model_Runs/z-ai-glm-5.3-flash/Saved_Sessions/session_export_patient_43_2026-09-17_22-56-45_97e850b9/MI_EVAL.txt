# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout despite Eric's initial defensiveness, explicitly validating his honesty and normalizing his hesitancy. No disapproval or confrontation was present at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently captured emotional undercurrents, such as smoking as valued personal time and the unspoken fear connected to his father's illness. Reflections were fresh, complex, and often double-sided.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on smoking and progressed logically from ambivalence to concrete next steps. Minor deduction: such careful pacing meant the agreed-upon option review wasn't completed in-session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session operated between equals, with the counselor inviting Eric's agenda and adopting his language ('three tests,' 'on my own terms') as shared working framework. Information was offered only with consent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly and actively supported ('on your own terms and timeline,' 'you'll be the judge'), and Eric responded strongly to this framing. No pressure or imposed urgency was present.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within rather than supplied, moving Eric from 'I feel fine' to 'a real maybe' in one session via well-timed evocative questions. The counselor consistently credited Eric for his own progress.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in Eric ('you're the expert on your own life') and sustained throughout. His own contributions shaped the session structure, with the 'three tests' becoming the working framework.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked progressively stronger change language, from 'I feel fine' to 'a real maybe, not a polite one,' using forward-looking questions about the cough and what would make a method stick. Change talk was consistently reinforced as Eric's own doing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally acknowledged sustain talk without amplifying it and pivoted effectively toward change. Minor deduction: some double-sided reflections restated sustain talk content more than necessary, keeping reasons against change in the air.
