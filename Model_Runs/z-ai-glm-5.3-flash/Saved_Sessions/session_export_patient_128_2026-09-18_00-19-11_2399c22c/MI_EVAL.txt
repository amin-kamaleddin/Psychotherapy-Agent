# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates warmth and unconditional positive regard with no judgment or labeling of Tyler's drinking. Specific, genuine affirmations and normalizing of ambivalence ('both of those things can be true at once') signal full acceptance, which Tyler explicitly confirms at the close.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Deep, accurate reflective listening throughout, capturing both content and emotional nuance (e.g., the core ambivalence about drinking as 'college fun' vs. trouble with police). Notably tracked Tyler's moment-to-moment shift from 'that's awkward' to 'actually, not that hard' with no inaccurate reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains tight focus on the target behavior—risky drinking on big party nights—throughout the session. Wisely narrows from unrealistic abstinence to the specific high-risk pattern, with each exchange building progressively toward a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames the session and follow-up as genuine joint problem-solving ('we look at that list together... wins and the parts that didn't hold'). Tyler's own language mirrors the collaborative frame, indicating he internalized it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly reinforces Tyler's ownership and choice ('You're the expert on your own life here') without prescribing solutions. Accepts Tyler's rejection of total abstinence and offers a genuine closing menu of options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every strategy—Jake as check-in, eating first, the three-drink limit, the Sunday scorecard—originated from Tyler's own answers, with the counselor never giving advice. Evocative questions culminated in Tyler's spontaneous self-diagnosis: 'maybe it's a fixable one.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly places expertise with the client, crediting every plan element to Tyler ('every piece of it came from you'). Tyler's closing comment that he 'talked myself into figuring it out' is direct evidence power-sharing was genuinely experienced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and immediately amplifies change talk ('I can't keep risking that for some dumb chugging contest'), and uses a confidence ruler to elicit nuanced self-assessment. Success-criteria questions generated vivid, self-motivated change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally handles sustain talk well with effective double-sided reflections and respectful pivots toward change rather than confrontation. Minor deduction: some reflections gave equal airtime to both sides without a strategic tilt toward change, and recurring sustain talk about drinking as fun went unaddressed with a weighted reflection.
