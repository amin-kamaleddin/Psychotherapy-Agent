# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout; validates Luis's constraints and small commitments. Warmth occasionally reads formulaic, with limited individualized affirmation beyond his practical efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are consistently accurate, e.g., mirroring the 'extra laundry' and 'walking pharmacy' frustrations. Sometimes brief and practical, missing deeper emotional content like the fear behind his ER visits.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on asthma self-management—every response ties back to adherence, inhaler placement, symptom rules, or follow-up. Session progresses steadily from ambivalence to a specific written plan despite sarcasm and deflection.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The final plan is entirely co-constructed—Luis modified or rejected options freely (text not call, direct wording, two-line check-in) and the counselor adapted without resistance. Genuine two-way problem-solving throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Luis's choice at every decision point, offering options rather than directives and allowing rejection of suggestions. His ownership of the final plan ('I did my part today') reflects successful autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong evocative opening questions, but once planning began the session shifted to logistics rather than actively deepening Luis's own motivations, hopes, and values. Much of his motivation surfaced incidentally rather than through exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly invites Luis to choose ('which sounds most doable?'), and his contributions substantially shape the plan—the mug spot, text wording, PCP contact, and text-over-call preference. More explicit acknowledgment of his expertise would raise this.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully draws out commitment language ('the mug thing and the rule—I can do those,' 'maybe I'll show up to the next appointment') and reinforces Luis's self-generated knowledge. More explicit elicitation of his own reasons for change and deeper desire/ability language would strengthen this.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids amplifying sustain talk but does not strategically soften it—dismissive statements like 'Oscar hasn't made me die yet' and the ER comment receive no double-sided reflection or gentle reframing. Redirects to logistics rather than resolving ambivalence.
