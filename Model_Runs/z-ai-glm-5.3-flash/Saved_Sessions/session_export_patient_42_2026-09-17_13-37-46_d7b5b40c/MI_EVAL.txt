# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'Thanks for being honest' and 'don't judge it.' However, affirmations and instructions become formulaic and repeated verbatim, diluting individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections ('torn: health concerns vs the social comfort'), but the counselor pivots quickly to checklists and planning. Elise's core concern about loneliness and losing 'instant friends' is never deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus leading to a specific target (morning coffee cigarette) and defined check-in time. However, direction occasionally outruns the contemplation-stage client, prioritizing rapid action over consolidating motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration: Elise chose the target cigarette, delay length, and check-in time. But the session structure is largely counselor-driven with bulleted prescriptive plans and Elise in a passive, agreeable role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-affirming moves ('Which option appeals to you most?'), and Elise appreciated not being pushed. Still, the plan's specifics (doses, breath counts, reduction targets) were heavily counselor-prescribed with limited space for her choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor rarely drew out Elise's own motivations, values, or hopes; the readiness score was never explored. Motivation came primarily from counselor suggestions and education rather than the client's own language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role, prescribing numeric goals and techniques while Elise mostly agreed. Her central concern about work-break social smoking was deferred rather than integrated as her expertise guiding the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal change talk was elicited or deepened; the readiness ruler answer ('four or five') was never explored, and her own reasons for quitting were never asked. Movement came mainly from the counselor's plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately did not argue with sustain talk ('instant friends,' 'the other fourteen are still mine'). However, few strategic pivots or double-sided reflections used the social-bonding theme to build discrepancy.
