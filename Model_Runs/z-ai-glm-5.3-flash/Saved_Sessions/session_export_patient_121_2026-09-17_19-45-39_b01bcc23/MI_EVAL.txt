# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout; Sofia's defensiveness, blame-shifting, and dismissiveness are met with patience and validation, never judgment. Her request not to 'make it a whole thing' is explicitly honored with genuine warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures both surface content and underlying hurt. Falls short of 5 because the years of being blamed since fifth grade are acknowledged but not deeply explored or emotionally validated before moving to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on school attendance and peer connection from the first reflection, with each exchange building toward a concrete one-week trial and follow-up. Tangents are gently steered back to actionable change with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative: the counselor asks rather than tells, and every plan element originates from Sofia's input. Obstacle planning is co-developed and the follow-up is framed as a shared check-in rather than an assignment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently affirmed—'not promising anything' is accepted as fair, and the one-week trial is set entirely on Sofia's own terms. The counselor never pressures and even accepts 'half a disaster' as an acceptable outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within, eliciting Sofia's intrinsic reasons like 'it won't be on me for once' and not wanting to eat lunch alone. She ends the session with her own plan, coping strategies, and commitment to return.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client ('You know your school and your people better than I do'). The entire plan comes from Sofia's tentative ideas, which the counselor structures rather than supplies—textbook power-sharing with a guarded teen.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional evocation of desire, ability, commitment, and planning language, with change talk strengthening progressively ('I could try it for like one week'). Each change statement is reflected, affirmed, and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through double-sided reflections and affirming self-respect rather than arguing. Some sustain talk (rumors, distrust) is engaged fairly fully before redirection, though this actually supports rapport and ambivalence resolution.
