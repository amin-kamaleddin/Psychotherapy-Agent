# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect and brief affirmations ('saying no out loud matters') are present, but warmth feels thin and formulaic, with little deeper affirmation or normalization of Anya's fear and vulnerability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections appear ('stressful even if it feels just a laugh'), but emotionally rich moments ('I'd probably cry,' 'my heart's going') are skipped in favor of scripts and tactics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on gambling and finances with concrete forward movement, ending in a completed bank role-play and a 90-day commitment. Direction is task-oriented rather than addressing emotional drivers of gambling.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor acts like a coach drilling scripts and tactic lists rather than an equal partner; Anya's ideas shape tasks but not session direction, with no joint agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menus and choices ($500 limit, temporary, 90 days) affirm some choice, but framing is prescriptive and Anya's hesitations are accommodated without honoring them as meaningful self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Anya's own motivations (Egypt, motherhood, self-respect) are introduced by her and never evoked, reflected, or linked to behavior; the counselor offers techniques instead of drawing out her reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor positions itself as the advice-giver, supplying scripts and option lists; solutions are handed to Anya rather than co-constructed, with little acknowledgment of her expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Role-plays elicit genuine change talk ('I'm skipping the pokies,' 'all four... that's the whole point'), but the counselor rarely deepens or reinforces it, missing moments like 'most adult thing I've done in years.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('just a bit of cash,' 'like admitting this is forever') is mostly ignored or glossed over; no double-sided reflections, and the pivotal temporary-vs-permanent ambivalence goes unexplored.
