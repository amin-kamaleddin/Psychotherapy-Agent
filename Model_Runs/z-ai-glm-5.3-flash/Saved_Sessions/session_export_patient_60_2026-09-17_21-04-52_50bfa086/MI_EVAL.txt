# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, from the opening 'I'm not here to judge you' to affirmations of Jason's self-awareness. Zero instances of disapproval, labeling, or moralizing even during minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including reflections capturing unfairness and emotional costs like faking the laugh at the inside joke. These reflections landed powerfully, with Jason responding 'when you say it like that, it kind of hits.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on Jason's alcohol use and the incident, guiding progressively from the event to ambivalence to concrete future planning. Every exchange circled back to the target behavior without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Model of collaborative inquiry between equals—asking permission, following Jason's agenda, and shared humor. Jason's closing comment confirms the collaborative stance was experienced firsthand.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices consistently located in Jason, explicitly disclaiming pressure ('No program, promise') and accepting his limits on commitment. His pacing plan emerged entirely voluntarily and he internalized ownership ('That's mine to figure out').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from Jason's own values—being 'the fun one,' remembering the funny stuff, saving money. His spontaneous realization about why he goes out was entirely self-generated and genuinely evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Jason was positioned as the expert on his own life throughout, with no advice or expert prescriptions given. His internalized conclusion ('That's mine to figure out, I guess') demonstrates effective power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully moved Jason from sustain talk to self-generated change talk and commitment-adjacent planning (eating beforehand, counting drinks, water between drinks). Strategic open questions like 'anything you wish went a little differently?' produced values-based motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mostly avoided reinforcing sustain talk using strategic double-sided reflections and allowing Jason to hold his view of the referral. Early reflections briefly amplified sustain talk before redirecting, though this proved strategic for rapport.
