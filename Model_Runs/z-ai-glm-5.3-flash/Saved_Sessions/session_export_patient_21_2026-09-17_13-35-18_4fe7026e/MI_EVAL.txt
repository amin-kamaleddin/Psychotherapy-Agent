# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Supportive stance with encouragement ('good plan,' 'You've got this') and endorsement of self-compassion, but warmth is generic with no individualized affirmations of Camila's strengths, such as her existing walking routine or insight about overcommitment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; responses are dominated by advice and checklists. Rich emotional openings around her fear of failure and self-criticism ('I'm my own worst critic') went unexplored and unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on increasing physical activity, producing a specific measurable plan (20-minute walk, Saturday 9 AM, if-then backup, structured check-in). Point deducted because direction was driven by prescriptive momentum rather than collaborative goal clarification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is notably unequal: counselor prescribes strategies and task checklists while Camila mostly complies. No joint brainstorming or 'we' language; her expertise about her own routines is never explicitly drawn upon.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Camila's choice to postpone the strength routine is respected and she controls timing and check-in method, but the rapid-fire imperative checklists repeated multiple times convey pressure rather than genuine choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to draw motivation from Camila; nearly all content originates from the counselor. No open questions exploring her reasons, values, or what more energy would mean, and her spontaneous motivations are never amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role with directive bursts of strategies; Camila's remark 'You're good at this, aren't you?' signals the expert-driven dynamic despite her contributions shaping the logistics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good concrete commitment questions elicited meaningful change talk ('doable,' 'exciting,' specific timing), but the counselor never reflects, reinforces, or deepens it—change talk was captured, not cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mild sustain talk about being wiped out and fear of failure is not amplified or dwelt upon, but also never strategically softened—the counselor sidesteps it rather than addressing her ambivalence about overcommitment.
