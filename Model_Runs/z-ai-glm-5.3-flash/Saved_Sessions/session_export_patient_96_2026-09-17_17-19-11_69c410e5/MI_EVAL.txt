# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental, affirming the piercing as meaningful and explicitly supporting her choice. Affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures her core theme of autonomy and identity. Misses opportunities to reflect deeper emotions, such as her mother's rejection or her nervousness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session drifts entirely away from the referral target, becoming family communication coaching with no attempt to reconnect to piercing/oral health concerns. Good client-centered process, but poorly focused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine teamwork: options offered rather than prescribed, scripts co-constructed and adapted by the client, and her choices visibly shape the session. Marisol is empowered throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematic menu-based choices at every decision point and explicit statements supporting her ownership of the decision. Never pressures her toward removing the piercing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Early open questions elicit good identity material, but the session shifts to supplying scripts, lines, and techniques. Strategies and solutions come from the counselor rather than the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Marisol as the expert, with menu options and her choices shaping the session direction. Occasionally tips toward a coach-like stance when offering scripts and tips.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no effort to evoke change talk about the target behavior; the counselor misses her opening ('maybe I'll get sick of it') and never links oral health to her values. Change talk that emerges concerns family communication, not the referral.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Doesn't argue with or reinforce sustain talk, pivoting respectfully instead. However, sustain talk is sidestepped rather than actively softened — no double-sided reflections or reframing to re-engage the health concerns.
