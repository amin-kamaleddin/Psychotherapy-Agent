# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout; the patient explicitly noted 'you didn't lecture, and that counts for a lot.' His resistance and hesitancy were accepted without disapproval, and slips were normalized as 'not a failure.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that integrated the patient's prior statements (grandkids, medication nightmares, patch rash, lecture fatigue). The patient explicitly validated the reflections as fair and noted nobody had asked what he'd tolerate before.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking cessation from the opening question while respectfully honoring the dental context, building progressively to a concrete, time-limited plan with side-effect exit criteria and a scheduled 2pm follow-up call.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative in tone — options offered as a menu, the pharmacist script tailored to the patient's input, and follow-up details co-negotiated. However, the scaffolding (two-week trial, option list, follow-up cadence) was clinician-proposed, with the patient selecting within it rather than the plan emerging from mutual brainstorming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated permission-seeking and choice-framing handed control to the patient, who decided on gum, set his own conditions ('if it's anything like the pills, I'm done'), chose the follow-up modality and time, and dictated what he wanted asked. Refusals and vaping hesitation were accepted without pressure, with a clear exit route offered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked the patient's own motivations (grandkids) and his own solution (gum, based on his cousin's success) rather than lecturing. Missed opportunities to deepen evocation: no confidence ruler, no exploration of what success would look like, and limited amplification of change talk once the plan was set.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Centered the patient's lived experience of side effects ('what would feel acceptable to try?') and negotiated follow-up logistics with him. The option menu and two-week trial structure were largely clinician-designed, with the patient choosing among options rather than co-creating the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and developed change talk — the opening question drew out the grandkids as intrinsic motivation, and acceptable-option questions moved the patient from 'I'm not promising anything' to a concrete commitment: 'two weeks of the gum, then I'll tell you straight if it's rubbish or not.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally acknowledged resistance briefly before pivoting to autonomy and options, and normalized slips effectively. However, some reflections repeated sustain talk verbatim (side effects, lectures); a double-sided reflection weighted more heavily toward change would have been stronger.
