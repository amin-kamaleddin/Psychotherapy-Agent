# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard — normalizing ambivalence, validating the worry about seeming 'needy,' and reframing nervousness with hope. No judgment was detected at any point in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured Aisha's feelings and insights, including her fear of failure. Empathy was occasionally delivered briskly, moving quickly to option lists rather than deeply exploring body-image distress and discouragement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on resuming exercise from the opening question through a concrete, time-bound plan. Every turn advanced the process with no topic drift, tying each element back to Aisha's schedule and preferences.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely shared power, with Aisha's input repeatedly redirecting the session and plans to 'tweak together.' However, frequent long lists of pre-formatted options at times positioned the counselor as the idea generator, with Aisha mostly selecting rather than co-creating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced choice and ownership — asking permission before offering ideas, letting Aisha select every plan element, and supporting her decision to send the text alone. The check-in standard was set by Aisha herself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Aisha through rulers, open questions, and exploring past success, so her own reasons and solutions drove the plan. Occasionally reduced by supplying ready-made options and self-compassion scripts instead of asking what might work for her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Aisha as the expert with questions like 'Which feels most doable this week?' and her own ideas shaped the final plan. Score fell short of 5 due to a somewhat menu-driven approach (five routines, five accountability options) rather than deep co-construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk via importance/confidence rulers, surfacing past success, and solidifying concrete commitments (10-minute walk, if-then plan, 3:30 alarm, accountability check-in). Aisha's closing statement — 'I'm leaving with an actual plan' — reflects highly successful cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded well to minimal sustain talk (fear of failure, 'I'll chicken out') with the if-miss rule, micro-wins, and reframing skips as 'info, not judgement.' However, the counselor jumped straight to solution lists rather than briefly exploring or reflecting the fear of failure before pivoting.
