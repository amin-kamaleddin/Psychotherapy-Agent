# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard ('I appreciate your honesty,' 'not something I'm grading'), even when Adrian tested boundaries ('do I pass some test of yours?'). No judgment or disapproval appeared despite defensiveness and minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Adrian's ambivalence and concerns well. However, deeper emotional content—shame about the infection, fear of fentanyl, and relief at avoiding a lecture—received limited explicit reflective acknowledgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained an unwavering focus on the target behavior from the opening question through harm-reduction planning to the concrete commitment to order test strips before Saturday. Every exchange advanced the behavioral focus without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—Adrian's choices (rejecting nurse check-ins, online-only, DanceSafe) directly shaped the plan. However, some stretches, particularly the product recommendations and checklist, became information-delivery driven rather than eliciting Adrian's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: repeatedly affirmed choice, explicitly rejected pass/fail framing, accepted refusals and limits ('No promises'), and presented options without pressure. Adrian's own conclusion ('this wasn't as bad as I expected') reflects the success of this non-controlling stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within via the readiness ruler and open questions, eliciting Adrian's own reasons and progressively stronger change talk. Evocation focused narrowly on harm reduction, however, without deepening Adrian's emerging ambivalence about use itself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Adrian as the decision-maker ('Your call — what would be most helpful right now?'), and his contributions genuinely shaped the plan. A 5 would require more explicit acknowledgment of Adrian's expertise about his own life and social context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through readiness scaling, moving Adrian from a 2 to concrete commitments ('I'll order before Saturday'). Evocation relied mostly on harm-reduction logistics rather than deeper exploration of Adrian's own broader reasons and confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided reinforcing or arguing against sustain talk, instead redirecting toward Adrian's own concerns (infection pain, unknown cuts). However, some sustain talk ('I'm not making some big deal out of this') was allowed to stand without a double-sided reflection.
