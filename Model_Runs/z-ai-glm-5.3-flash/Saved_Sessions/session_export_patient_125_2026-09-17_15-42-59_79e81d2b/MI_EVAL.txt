# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and affirming stance with a frustrated, guarded client ('You're showing real restraint and focus'). However, warmth is somewhat procedural; the psychiatrist does not explicitly normalize or validate the emotional weight of the family separation and his father's birthday.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, e.g., accurately capturing both emotion and family motivation ('angry and hurt... what matters most is calling your dad'). Missed opportunities to reflect the deeper vulnerability beneath the anger, such as fear and loneliness about release and separation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional, unwavering focus on regaining phone contact with his father and managing frustration. Every element (rulers, note, delivery plan, backup script, debrief) ties back to this target, ending with a fully specified plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative: preferences elicited, confidence ratings used to shape the plan, client edits incorporated, and joint debrief time agreed. Falls short because decisions are often presented as counselor-generated option packages rather than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicit choice-offering ('use as-is, tweak it, or practice?') and acceptance of Tariq's script edits. Undermined somewhat by the volume of pre-written suggestions per turn, which carries a prescriptive flavor; ownership could be reinforced more explicitly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocation via importance/confidence rulers, with change talk escalating from the client himself (importance 10, confidence 6→9; 'at least I did it right this time'). Sometimes shifts from evoking to equipping by supplying scripts and coping techniques rather than eliciting the client's own strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client expertise is acknowledged and his choices shape the plan, but the counselor frequently slips into directive, expert-driven mode—supplying pre-written scripts and stacking multiple suggestions per turn. Power-sharing is present but uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance/confidence rulers produced escalating commitment language ('that's the deal I'm making with myself'). Some evocation is more procedural than motivational, with the counselor quickly supplying scripts instead of deepening the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects to options, but relies on topic changes rather than strategic techniques. No double-sided reflections or acknowledgment-and-reframing of the grievance; the client arrived at insight about his own attitude largely unprompted.
