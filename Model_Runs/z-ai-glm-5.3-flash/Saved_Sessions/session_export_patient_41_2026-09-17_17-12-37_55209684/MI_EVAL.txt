# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with genuine affirmations of Elira's effort and self-awareness. However, acceptance occasionally borders on cheerleading ('Nice,' 'really good') and does not fully normalize the difficulty of relapse or her avoidance pattern.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early, accurately naming her tight-stomach panic and her brain 'not following' if-then plans. Later turns shift quickly to logistics, leaving her significant loneliness disclosure ('I don't have anyone I'm close with') unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (attending after practice) with every exchange advancing a concrete, time-bound plan for Thursday. Logical progression from smallest step to contingency planning to in-the-moment rehearsal, with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collegial tone with frequent input-seeking and joint step-by-step plan building. However, the interaction is largely counselor-led question followed by client selection, and Elira's own insights were accepted but not built upon as an equal partner's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames choices as Elira's and honors her edits (shortened text, two alarms, declining the friend option) without pressure or persuasion. The menu-based format constrains genuine choice to counselor-defined options, limiting deeper autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses scaling questions and lets Elira choose her own coping scripts and self-talk ('Five minutes counts'). Much of the session remains didactic—teaching breathing mechanics, providing scripts, and drafting texts—conveying that expertise lies with the counselor rather than drawing motivation from within Elira.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Elira as the decision-maker ('Which feels most doable?'), but the option-heavy style undermines power-sharing. Nearly every turn offers pre-packaged menus, and virtually all plan content was counselor-generated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through scaling questions, confidence-building, and rehearsing counter-sustain statements, eliciting notable commitments ('I can try. This week.'). Points lost because change talk is evoked mainly for plan-building rather than exploring Elira's deeper values and 'why.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('my brain just doesn't follow it,' 'feels kind of silly') is consistently met with acceptance and gentle reframing, including the effective 'leaving moment' rehearsal script. Redirection tends to be practical rather than reflective, without double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence while tilting toward change.
