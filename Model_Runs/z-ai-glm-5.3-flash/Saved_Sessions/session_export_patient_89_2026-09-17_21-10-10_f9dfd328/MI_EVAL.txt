# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained warm, unconditional positive regard throughout, never judging past failures or weight and normalizing slips ('We're not chasing perfection here'). Consistent affirmations of Marcus's strengths were offered without any disapproval or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, from the opening double-sided reflection to the closing reflection mirroring Marcus's own metaphor about shoulders down and leaving with something genuinely his. The counselor captured both content and emotional shifts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity, with each exchange advancing toward a concrete schedule, relapse-prevention rule, and specific follow-up commitment. No topic drift occurred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions were fully co-constructed — the bounce-back rule and follow-up plan were developed jointly, and Marcus supplied all session content (walking, dancing, family involvement). The exchange felt genuinely between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly affirmed Marcus's ownership and choice, asking rather than prescribing and explicitly validating that small movement counts. The session ended with success attributed entirely to Marcus ('you did the work of making it happen'), with no coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within Marcus rather than imposed, exemplified by the metacognitive question about what made this conversation feel different — prompting Marcus to articulate his own insights. No advice-giving or external reasons for change were imposed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly located expertise in Marcus ('You're the expert on your own schedule') and all plan content came from the client, with questions consistently handing the pen to him. The counselor's contributions shaped the session minimally.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions ('What would feel small enough to actually survive a hectic week?') systematically evoked Marcus's own commitments and visible excitement, which the counselor reinforced by pausing on it. Marcus moved from discouragement to concrete commitments ('Tuesdays and Thursdays, lunch trail. I can do that.').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Doubt and anticipated failure were consistently reframed as strengths and insight rather than dwelt upon ('You know your own history well enough to see the trap coming, and that's a real strength'). Setbacks were collaboratively transformed into a bounce-back plan.
