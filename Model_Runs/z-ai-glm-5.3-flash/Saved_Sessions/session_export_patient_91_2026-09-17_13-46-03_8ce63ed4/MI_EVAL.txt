# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with normalization of missed days. Explicit affirmations of Marcus's strengths were sparse and formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate understanding of his overwhelm, but reflective listening was limited; self-critical statements and feelings were not explored before pivoting to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Crystal-clear focus on morning blood sugar checking with no topic drift; every exchange built toward a concrete, achievable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options were offered and Marcus's preferences shaped details, but solutions were largely counselor-generated rather than co-created; his expertise about his routines was rarely elicited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice-offering and patient decision-making throughout, with Marcus owning the plan. Could be strengthened by affirming his capacity for self-direction during moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions came predominantly from the counselor; no open questions explored Marcus's own reasons, values, or confidence, and his motivation emerged largely on its own.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices and check-ins conveyed some power-sharing, but many responses were prescriptive and expert-driven, with limited exploration of Marcus's own ideas before proposing solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerged thanks to concrete, manageable steps, but it was never deliberately evoked or deepened (no confidence rulers or open evocative questions) and was only briefly affirmed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Perfectionism was gently reframed and sustain talk was not reinforced, but doubts about snoozing and forgetting were not strategically addressed with double-sided reflections or confidence-building.
