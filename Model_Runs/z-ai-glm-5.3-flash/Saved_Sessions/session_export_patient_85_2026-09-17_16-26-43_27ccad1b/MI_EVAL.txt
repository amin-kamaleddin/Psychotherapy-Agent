# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with effective affirmations like 'You've already shown you can change' and normalizing setbacks as 'information, not failure.' However, warmth was somewhat brief and formulaic, with emotional disclosures (e.g., his father's diabetes) receiving only passing acknowledgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on that accurately captured Marco's emotional arc and values. Deeper emotional content—fear of his father's path and the 'hitting me right in the chest' grandkids moment—was not consistently reflected with depth, as reflections pivoted quickly to strategy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the target behavior (reducing juice) with zero topic drift; every turn advanced the agenda from exploration to a concrete, time-bound plan with a scheduled Wednesday check-in. Exemplary MI direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session operated as joint problem-solving, with Marco's suggestions (sparkling water placement, tally jar, grandkids script) meaningfully shaping the plan and the check-in co-designed. Docked slightly because the initial strategy menu and script offerings tilted information flow toward the counselor rather than trusting Marco's demonstrated problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Advice was consistently permission-based, with Marco's preferences shaping every plan detail (tracking method, wife script, check-in time). The counselor respected his stated limits ('no reminder needed') without pushing extra support, resulting in genuine self-directed ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Values and scaling questions effectively drew out Marco's intrinsic motivation, including his powerful statement about his father and his pride in past success. The extensive counselor-supplied strategy list reduced evocation, and exploration of the father's story was cut short.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission before advising and invited Marco's choices ('Which of these feels most doable?'). However, the five-to-six item expert-driven strategy menu and offer to draft scripts slightly undermined Marco's expressed confidence ('I got it covered').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Readiness and confidence scaling questions effectively elicited strong change talk ('A 10... the walking proved I can stick with stuff'), and values questions tapped his family motivation. Some strategies were supplied rather than drawn out of Marco first.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled Marco's minimal sustain talk ('boy, do I love that OJ') well by not amplifying it and pivoting to temptation planning and coping strategies. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or reframing juice as the treat rather than the default.
