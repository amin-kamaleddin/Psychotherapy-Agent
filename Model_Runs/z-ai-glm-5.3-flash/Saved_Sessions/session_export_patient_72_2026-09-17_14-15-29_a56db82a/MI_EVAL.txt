# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and respects Leila's preferences (no contracts, no swimming, trust your gut). However, praise is generic ('Love this,' 'Great') rather than individualized to her specific strengths like her self-awareness about past burnout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is entirely absent — the counselor never restates, paraphrases, or reflects Leila's statements. Emotionally significant moments (her fear of flaking, nostalgia for her active self, saying the plan out loud 'makes it feel more real') pass without acknowledgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear, consistent focus on establishing an exercise routine and secures concrete commitments (Tuesday 6pm gym tour, Saturday 9am walk, Monday bag packing). The weakness is that direction takes the form of rapid-fire task lists rather than guiding Leila to clarify her own next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is periodic two-way exchange ('Want to rehearse now or wait?') and the role-play emerges somewhat collaboratively. However, plans, workouts, and checklists are delivered as finished products rather than co-constructed, and Leila's own contributions are built on only lukewarmly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive elements exist: options are offered, consent sought before resources, and Leila is told to trust her gut. But much of the session is prescriptive, with no explicit statements reinforcing that the choice and change process are entirely hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor makes little effort to draw out Leila's own motivations — her reasons for change are volunteered, not evoked. Questions are logistical (gym features, schedules) rather than motivational, with no open questions about values, no scaling questions, and no exploration of what success looks like.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks some collaborative questions and defers to Leila's judgment, but the overall stance is expert-driven. Leila's self-knowledge ('I'm a decent judge of places') is never explicitly acknowledged or drawn upon; solutions are counselor-generated and Leila reacts to them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions do elicit commitments (specific days/times), but Leila's abundant change talk is self-generated and rarely reflected or deepened. A prime opportunity — 'A month ago this all felt impossible, and now it just feels like errands' — receives no reinforcing response.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoids amplifying Leila's doubts (fear of flaking, rain worries) and moves toward solutions. However, there is no active softening — no double-sided reflections, affirmations addressing her self-doubt, or reframing of her worry in light of her 10/10 confidence.
