# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated respect and non-judgment, and Elena explicitly noted she did not feel judged or lectured. However, warmth was at times procedural — responses often pivoted quickly to facts or options rather than lingering with individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately validated Elena's unease and protective instincts. But deeper emotional cues — her fear of blame, family tension, and worry about seeming a 'difficult parent' — were never reflected back, as many responses shifted to information delivery.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor kept the session clearly focused on moving Elena from hesitancy toward informed engagement with vaccination, staying on topic throughout. It fell short of a 5 because the agenda was counselor-driven rather than collaboratively anchored to Elena's values, with no summaries or joint goal-setting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options were offered, role-play was co-selected, and Elena's questions shaped some content. However, the exchange was asymmetric — the counselor supplied lists, scripts, and talking points while Elena mostly received and rehearsed, with limited elicitation of her own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly framed the decision as Elena's, offered choices, and avoided pressure or directives, which Elena internalized ('If I'm going to decide...'). More explicit affirmation of her capacity for self-direction would be needed for a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked about Elena's concerns and preferences, drawing out some of her own reasons. But most persuasion was carried by counselor-provided facts, with no confidence rulers or deliberate deepening of statements like 'I can protect her and still ask questions.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing occurred through choice-offering and inviting input. Still, the session was heavily counselor-directed in content, with little acknowledgment that Elena holds expertise about her own family and partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged ('Maybe that's not being difficult', 'the risks are worse than I thought', 'I feel ready now'), partly through educational reframing. However, the counselor never deliberately evoked or deepened it with open questions or reflective amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced, and her minimization of chickenpox was gently reframed with balanced risk information. But softening relied on persuasion and facts rather than MI-consistent techniques like double-sided reflections or rolling with ambivalence.
