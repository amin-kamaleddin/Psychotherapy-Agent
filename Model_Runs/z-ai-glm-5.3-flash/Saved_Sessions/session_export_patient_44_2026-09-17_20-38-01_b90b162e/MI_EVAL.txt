# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicates warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, normalizing ambivalence (e.g., 'Both make sense,' 'Both of those things are true') and never criticizing Erik's struggles with comparison or diet.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture both progress and emotional pain, such as the opening reflection on gym comparisons and 'you'd rather answer to your word than to a candy craving,' showing deep understanding of emotional undercurrents.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focus on exercise and dietary change is maintained with smooth, progressive transitions—from gym comparisons to the written note, to diet, to Wednesday planning, and visualizing the next session—building momentum toward action without rushing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a genuine exchange of equals; the plan is fully co-constructed from Erik's own materials (treadmill math, wife Anna, the stairs idea), with no expert-driven advice anywhere in the transcript.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces Erik's ownership through permission-style questions and affirmations, never prescribing or pressuring; 'I'm not promising I'll be perfect' is met with affirmation rather than correction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every strategy emerged from Erik's own experience, and evocative questions about identity and future drew out his deepest motivations ('That's the guy I'm trying to be'), with motivation visibly growing across the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Erik is positioned as the expert ('You've essentially designed your own strategy, Erik'), and his contributions demonstrably shape the entire intervention, with the counselor only facilitating.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and amplified through questions like 'What's worked, even a little?' and 'what would beating the old Erik in the kitchen look like?', culminating in strong client commitments and concrete plans.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged briefly and reframed via double-sided reflections (e.g., honoring 'what's the point?' frustration while redirecting to 'What's worked, even a little?'), never dwelled upon or dismissed.
