# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful tone with affirmations and no judgment (including gentle humor about the wife). Affirmations were somewhat generic and repeated, lacking individualized depth and normalization of setbacks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate content-level reflections on frustration and pacing preferences, but reflections rarely deepened. The emotional weight of Mateo's discouragement ('some days I just don't see the point') was never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on rehab activity/pacing throughout, progressing logically to a concrete weekly plan. Occasional question-stacking (3-4 questions per turn) slightly diluted focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan was genuinely co-constructed from Mateo's own ideas (20/10 rule, softer fridge note wording, backup plan). Collaboration was uneven at times, with counselor-dominated menus, templates, and expert-tinged framings.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit, consistent choice support at every step ('Which feels most doable?', accepting edits to the note and backup plan). Mateo directly confirmed the benefit: 'Thanks for not just telling me to push harder.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good use of open questions and a confidence ruler that drew out genuine motivation ('one good week... maybe I'm a seven'). However, evocation was inconsistent — many turns shifted to advising and pre-written scripts rather than exploring Mateo's own reasons and values in depth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative framing with real client input shaping the plan, but the interaction was heavily question-driven with pre-formatted menus and templates. Little explicit positioning of Mateo as the expert on his own pain and body.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and the confidence ruler effectively evoked change talk, but spontaneous change statements ('I'll give it a fair shot', 'Better than giving up entirely') were not deepened with reflections. No commitment language about importance or readiness was elicited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about pain and discouragement was acknowledged and reframed into 'small win' solutions without argument. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk to explicitly resolve ambivalence.
