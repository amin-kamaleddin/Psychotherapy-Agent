### Acceptance

**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with frequent genuine affirmations. The restaurant relapse is treated as information, and David's self-criticism is never reinforced.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that produced genuine 'aha' moments, including capturing the functional meaning of smoking. Falls short of 5 because emotional nuance was under-explored—David's shame about feeling 'weak' moved past quickly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on smoking cessation with each turn advancing the target behavior. Transitions build logically toward a concrete one-week plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership with inclusive language and David's answers driving the session. David's comfort in checking 'Does that sound sensible?' reflects his experience of the counselor as a collaborator, not an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is regularly affirmed and David makes all key decisions. The combination medication option was introduced before fully eliciting his preference, and ownership of potential setbacks wasn't explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions are drawn almost entirely from the client through skillful questions. Both the plan and reasons for change are genuinely David's own.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly conveys that expertise resides in the client, and David's contributions genuinely shape the plan. The final medication discussion leans slightly toward clinician guidance before David's preference was fully articulated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully elicit increasingly strong commitment language: 'One week. I can commit to that,' 'I want every advantage this time.' The final plan is almost entirely self-generated.
