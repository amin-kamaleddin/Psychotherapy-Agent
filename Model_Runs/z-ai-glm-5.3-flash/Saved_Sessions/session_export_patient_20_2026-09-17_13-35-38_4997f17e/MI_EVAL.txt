# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, honoring Brandon's conditions and minimization without confrontation, though affirmations of his strengths were limited and somewhat formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, but emotionally rich material (3am waking, anxiety, the Thanksgiving fight, drinking as reward) went largely unexplored as reflections became sparse later in the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout—every exchange tied back to alcohol use, with a logical progression from assessment to baseline tracking to a concrete reduction plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Brandon's ideas (tracking, two-beer goal) incorporated, though substantial expert-delivered content (risk lists, templates) kept it from full collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength: repeated menus of options, permission-seeking, honoring Brandon's 'no interventions' condition, and accepting his self-generated two-beer goal without pushing further.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation emerged from within (Brandon's own rationale and goal), but largely spontaneously—the counselor relied on information-giving rather than open evocative questions about values or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-asking, choices, and adopting Brandon's ideas showed partnership, but heavy counselor-delivered instruction created an expert stance Brandon explicitly had to negotiate against.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited meaningful change talk (tracking, two-beer goal, commitment, gratitude), though rarely deepened it with evocative questions or importance/confidence exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never confronted—a strength—but was sidestepped rather than softened with double-sided reflections, and the quick risk psychoeducation may have invited defensiveness.
