# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard from start to finish — judgment explicitly disavowed and the session substantiates it. Margaret's minimization is never corrected, her valued routines are protected, and affirmations are individualized rather than generic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with excellent double-sided and complex reflections, including mirroring Margaret's own metaphors. One notable lapse: a near-verbatim repeated question at the second planning turn, which Margaret noticed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear therapeutic focus on drinking behavior and its consequences while staying tied to Margaret's own concerns. Excellent steering back to the fall and independence without confrontation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor follows Margaret's lead, using her own words and metaphors, and invites rather than directs ('what do you think would help...?'). The plan emerges from the patient rather than being imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly emphasizes that change is Margaret's choice ('nobody's going to shame you into anything') and protects what she values (the pub). The plan is entirely drawn from Margaret's own ideas, with permission-based, optional framing throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently draws out Margaret's own motivations rather than supplying them — the fall-to-independence connection comes from the patient. Affirmations reinforce that reasons 'came from you, not from your doctor or your son.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor positions Margaret as the expert on her own life, explicitly deferring to her judgment ('I'd never suggest giving that up'). Questions and reflections build jointly on her answers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled use of open questions and reflections that elicit Margaret's own reasons (the fall, independence) and her specific commitments. Change talk is consistently reflected and strengthened across the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Margaret's minimization and resistance are met with double-sided reflections rather than argument or correction. The counselor neither ignores nor confronts sustain talk, allowing change talk to emerge naturally.
