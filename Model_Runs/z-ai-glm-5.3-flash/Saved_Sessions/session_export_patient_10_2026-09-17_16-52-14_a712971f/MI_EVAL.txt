# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed respect and warmth, affirming Amina's courage and resilience without judgment, even around her distrust of the system. Regard occasionally felt formulaic, and the emotional toll of repeated barriers was not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured Amina's core distinction between her capability and external barriers. Reflections were sometimes brief, with missed opportunities to explore deeper emotions such as her shame about hiding the childcare issue.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained exceptionally clear and consistent focus on the target behavior, moving systematically from barriers to a specific scheduled action with scripts, contingencies, role-play, and a written plan. Masterful direction that was never coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint, with Amina's input actively shaping the script, timing, and take-home materials. Collaboration was somewhat uneven as the counselor tended to supply solutions rather than drawing them out of Amina, at times feeling more like coaching than equal problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Amina's ownership of choices ('Which feels best,' 'Pick one and I'll tweak it') with no pressure or persuasion, and supported her 'prior commitments' framing without judgment. Her selections of script, timing, and plan format demonstrated that choice genuinely resided with her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did elicit some motivation (her confidence rating, past successes, and commitment), but a substantial portion of session content—scripts, backup lines, checklists—originated from the counselor. The prescriptive, skills-training style superseded evocation despite Amina's already-high motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed that expertise largely resided with Amina, whose contributions shaped the session's direction and materials. Somewhat limited by heavy provision of ready-made scripts, which periodically shifted expertise back toward the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced Amina's change talk ('I know I can do the work,' confidence of 10) and channeled it into a concrete commitment ('Thursday. I'll do it.'). Rarely deepened change talk through strategic follow-ups, and much of the session relied on counselor-provided scripting rather than client-generated motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce Amina's sustain talk about voucher waitlists and system distrust, but also did little to actively soften or reframe it. No double-sided reflections or deliberate pivots toward her change language were used; the sustain talk was largely left unaddressed.
