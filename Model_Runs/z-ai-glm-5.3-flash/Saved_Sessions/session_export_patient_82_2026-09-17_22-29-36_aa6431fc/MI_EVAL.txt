# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, with explicit reassurance ('no judgment here') when Lukas disclosed drinking. Rapid trust-building is evident in the teen's honest follow-through.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures both surface content and deeper meaning — peer dynamics, the coach's 'nobody's watching' voice, and the teen's identity concerns. Reflections are accurate and consistently deepen the conversation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on alcohol use and its interface with athletics/academics from the opening question onward, progressing logically through values, peer influence, limits, refusal strategies, and relapse prevention. Each turn advances the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership of equals — the psychiatrist asks rather than tells, and Lukas shapes the plan, check-in schedule, and 'pre-game read' metaphor. The psychiatrist adapts to every client contribution.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced ('what would help you most...'), options are offered, and the client's rejection of talking to his coach is accepted without pushback. No persuasion or pressure at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is entirely client-generated through evocative questions about his goals, past successes, and limits, culminating in his own commitment ('nothing during the season'). The psychiatrist never supplies reasons to change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client expertise is explicitly located within Lukas ('everything you're walking out with today came from you'), and the entire plan is client-generated with further client-driven refinement at the end.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and systematically developed, producing commitment language ('I think I can pull it off... saying this makes me feel like I actually might'). Each change-talk utterance is affirmed and reflected back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally handled well via double-sided reflections and redirection ('I love that question'), but is sometimes validated neutrally rather than actively reframed toward change. Sharper strategic pivoting would reach a 5.
