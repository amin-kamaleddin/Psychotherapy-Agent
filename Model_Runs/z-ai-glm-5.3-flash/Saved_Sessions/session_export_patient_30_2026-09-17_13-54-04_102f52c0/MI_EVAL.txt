# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a neutral, respectful tone with no judgment or labeling. Early validation ("That makes sense — being told to start medication when you feel fine can feel unnecessary and frustrating") shows basic respect. However, little warmth or explicit affirmation of David as a person; session shifted quickly to technical advice rather than unconditional positive regard for his feelings about being coerced into the appointment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited and superficial. After one initial reflection, reflections largely ceased. When David shared his vivid fear ("my buddy... could barely get through a round of golf"), the counselor responded with medical facts instead of reflecting the fear. Missed empathic openings included his self-doubt ("Sounded like a whiny kid?") and pattern of caving ("I'd probably just cave like usual").

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on the target behavior and moved toward a concrete outcome: a negotiation script, a 3–6 month lifestyle trial plan, measurable goals, and a commitment to raise it at his next appointment. Clear and productive, though it pursued a counselor-defined agenda (assertiveness scripting) without checking whether it matched David's most pressing concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration — the role-play exchanges were genuinely interactive, with David practicing in his own words and receiving tweaks. However, the session was largely counselor-directed: dense question lists, pre-written scripts, and clinical checklists delivered lecture-style with little back-and-forth agenda negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly protected David's choice: presented options (lifestyle first, low dose, different statin, monitoring), asked "Which would be most helpful right now?", and framed the script as a way "to protect your choice." No pressure applied; conditional commitment ("no promises") was accepted. A 5 would require more explicit reinforcement of David's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely imported rather than evoked. Change-promoting content (risk numbers, trial plans, scripts) came from the clinician. Almost no open questions probed David's own values, health goals, or the meaning of his father's history. His motivation ("That part I actually like") emerged despite, not because of, counselor technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally honored David's expertise — offering a menu of options and letting him shape his own words in role-play shows some power sharing. But the overall stance was expert-driven, delivering dense clinical recommendations with little inquiry into what David already knows or believes.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Few deliberate efforts to evoke motivation. David spontaneously produced meaningful change talk ("If I could just try the diet thing first, that'd be my preference"; "At my next appointment. We'll see."), but these were not reflected, affirmed, or deepened. Counselor supplied scripts and information rather than asking evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and constructively channeled resistance into advocacy skills (doctor-conversation scripts). However, much of the session centered on side effects and monitoring, keeping attention on barriers. No double-sided reflections or strategic reframing (e.g., using his dad's longevity or preference for quick action) were used to soften ambivalence.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **2.9/5**

**Summary:** The session was information-rich, well-focused, and autonomy-respecting, producing a tangible, useful outcome (a negotiation script and a concrete plan). However, it fell short of MI spirit: heavily advice-driven and educative rather than evocative. Empathy was minimal after the opening; multiple emotionally significant openings were missed. Change talk emerged spontaneously but was never affirmed or deepened. **Recommendations:** slow down, reflect feelings and ambivalence before offering facts, ask open questions eliciting David's own reasons for protecting his health, and specifically affirm his demonstrated capability (his practice scripts were genuinely good).
