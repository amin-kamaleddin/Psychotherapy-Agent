# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and affirming throughout, with good regard for Daniela's autonomy. However, affirmations became formulaic ('You're doing great' repeated) and praise was often paired with directive instructions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good early reflections ('really overwhelming'), but emotionally significant disclosures—fear of failing again, distrust of family, 'you earned it' thinking—were rarely explored in depth. Reflections were frequently replaced by solution lists.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, efficient focus on concrete stressors with a defined action plan and one-week follow-up; no aimless drifting. Slightly scattered across multiple behaviors (neighbor, smoking, drinking, Head Start) rather than prioritizing one.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some inviting questions and incorporation of Daniela's own ideas (barter idea, Head Start), but the dominant pattern was counselor-generated scripts and checklists with Daniela in an approving role. Genuine joint problem-solving was largely absent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated choices offered ('Pick whichever feels most helpful') and Daniela's decisions were accepted without pushback. Advice-heavy script delivery subtly limited her sense of authorship over solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied heavily on supplying tools and reasons rather than drawing motivation from Daniela. No open-ended evocative questions; her motivations (healthier for her son, finishing school) surfaced spontaneously but were never elicited or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for input on priorities and preferences, but quickly assumed the expert role with pre-made scripts and step-by-step instructions. The session was largely counselor-driven with Daniela reacting to solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged naturally ('text her tonight before I talk myself out of it,' 'I want to be healthier for him') but was not deliberately evoked or deepened. No open questions on reasons, importance, or confidence; the counselor supplied plans instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Barriers (trust, daycare costs, past quitting patterns) were noted without amplification, which is good, but were also left mostly unaddressed. No double-sided reflections or strategic reframing beyond one line ('slips are info, not failure').
