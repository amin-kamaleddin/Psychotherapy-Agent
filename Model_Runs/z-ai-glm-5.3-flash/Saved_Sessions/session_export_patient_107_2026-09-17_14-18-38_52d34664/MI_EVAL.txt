# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout; the client felt accepted ('Appreciate you not making this into some huge deal'). Falls short of 5 due to few individualized affirmations of the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was sparse; ambivalence about work performance and doubts about tracking were met with protocols rather than reflections. No complex or feeling reflections, and the meaning coffee holds for the client went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on the target behavior with a concrete agreed plan and brief safety guidance. Weakened by direction being expert-driven rather than toward an internally motivated client goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction was largely expert-to-recipient with pre-built plans and checklists; the client's ideas were merely absorbed into the counselor's framework. No joint agenda-setting or co-construction of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive elements (BP cuff 'not required,' accepting the client's gentler plan, no pressure to quit cold turkey). However, imperative language saturated the session and plans were offered before readiness, without affirming that change is the client's choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation and ideas were almost entirely supplied by the counselor rather than drawn out; virtually no evocative open questions or discrepancy development. The client's spontaneous change talk was met with logistics instead of deepening.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor assumed the expert role, delivering dense prescriptive protocols that prompted the client's 'Whoa, that's a lot of options!' The client had to negotiate his own gentler version, showing the plan was not collaboratively shaped.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk was client-generated, not evoked; the counselor failed to reflect or deepen statements like 'maybe the jitters thing shows up more than I think.' No open questions or importance/confidence scales were used to elicit change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor never argued for change or amplified sustain talk, but also did not strategically pivot it. Performance concerns received practical coping tips rather than double-sided reflections linking health concerns to the client's values.
