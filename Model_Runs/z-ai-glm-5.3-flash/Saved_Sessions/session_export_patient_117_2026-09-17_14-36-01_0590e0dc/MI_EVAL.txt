# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout; affirmations normalize Samuel's ambivalence. However, affirmations are brief and formulaic, lacking individualized recognition of his specific strengths (e.g., self-directed goal, resourcefulness in journaling).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections and validation in places, and building the trigger map from his journal shows careful listening. Misses reflecting the emotional weight of guilt, the loss of his smoking buddy Ray, and the fear that work stress is permanent—reflections favor structure over emotion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on smoking reduction with excellent progressive momentum (journal → trigger map → urge-surf practice → if-then plan → quantified reduction schedule). Every exchange builds toward concrete change with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine choice-points at most decisions, and the plan was co-constructed with Samuel's modifications (fidget, deferred NRT). Falls short of 5 because many offers were pre-defined binary menus and the final plan reads as counselor-authored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked for preferences, honored the choice to defer NRT, and adapted to workplace constraints. Some responses edged toward prescriptive instruction-giving with little explicit language that Samuel owns the outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited Samuel's views and goals, but superficially—no confidence/importance rulers, no values exploration (daughter, health), and motivation arrived mostly unprompted from the patient. The counselor's role was more directive-planning than evocative.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered meaningful choices that shaped the session's direction. Held at 4 because the menu-driven structure feels somewhat mechanical, and Samuel's 'you're the expert' comment went unaffirmed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged largely from the patient's own journaling rather than counselor cultivation. No evocative questions, confidence rulers, or strategic reflection of his commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally and folded into the plan without being reinforced. However, little active pivoting or double-sided reflection linking his doubt to his demonstrated success—sustain talk was tolerated rather than strategically softened.
