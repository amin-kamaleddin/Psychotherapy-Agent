# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout despite Dylan's defensiveness, with validation of frustration and affirmation of his social values; no judgment or lecturing at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, layered reflections throughout, including skillful reframing ('get smarter about where you are') and moment-to-moment tracking of shifts in Dylan's language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the alcohol-related conduct risk, guiding purposefully from incident to stakes to a concrete plan with self-defined success criteria; no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor acts as a genuine equal partner, inviting Dylan's ideas at every turn and co-constructing the agenda entirely from Dylan's own material and solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of Dylan's ownership and choice, with explicit crediting ('That's your own motivation doing the work') and no pressure or persuasive directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited rather than supplied; Dylan's transformation from externalizing blame to 'I can do that' with a self-generated plan demonstrates masterful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was repeatedly located within Dylan ('You know your friends and yourself better than anyone'), and the plan emerged entirely from his own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful open-ended questions evoked Dylan's own plan, commitment language, and confidence, deepening change talk and producing concrete next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally redirected effectively toward Dylan's values, though a few neutral reflections of sustain talk were not always paired with pivots toward change.
