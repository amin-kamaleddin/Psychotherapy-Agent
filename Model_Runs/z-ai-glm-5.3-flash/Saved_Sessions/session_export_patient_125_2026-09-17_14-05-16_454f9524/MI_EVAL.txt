# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating frustration without criticizing the patient's hostility or history. Warmth was somewhat procedural (repeated safety checks felt mechanical) and lacked explicit affirmations of the patient's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some accurate reflections (e.g., about the missed call before his dad's birthday), but advice-giving and task drafting dominated. Key emotional material — fear of relapse, his father's distance, his mother's worry — was passed over rather than deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on phone privileges, family contact, and compliance, progressing logically from complaint to plan to rehearsal. Deducted because the core behavior change (anger management over the final three weeks) that the patient himself raised went unexplored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drafts were co-edited, approval sought before finalizing, and the role-play was rehearsed interactively, leaving the patient with a plan he co-authored. Collaboration was somewhat asymmetric, with the counselor proposing and the patient ratifying.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered menus and permission-seeking ('pick one or tell me what to change') and accepted edits without resistance, never pressuring or moralizing. Choices were confined to counselor-defined options rather than fostering the patient's ownership of the overall plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively linked the grievance to discharge readiness and amplified intrinsic motivations (his mother's pride, his release in three weeks), with motivation visibly strengthening across the session. Evocation was largely implicit, relying on the patient's spontaneous momentum rather than deliberate open questions about his values and hopes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some choices, but the session was largely counselor-driven, with the counselor generating nearly all content while the patient approved or tweaked. Missed opportunities to draw on the patient's own expertise about what has helped him stay calm before.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully built on the patient's emerging change talk ('I want to walk in there calm... not some kid whining about his phone'), which grew noticeably across the session. Evocation was somewhat passive, with few explicit open questions to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided debate entirely, redirecting sustain talk about the unfair officer and futile grievances toward agency and action, and the patient self-corrected his role in the incident. Sustain talk was ignored rather than strategically reflected; a double-sided reflection acknowledging the grievance's legitimacy would have been stronger.
