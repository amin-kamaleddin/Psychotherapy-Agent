# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and honors Dylan's concerns about record language. Lacks explicit affirmation of his strengths (e.g., his reliability, willingness to follow through), making the stance feel somewhat procedural.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens with a decent empathic frame, but reflections are repetitive ('I hear you' nearly every turn) and deeper emotional content is missed. Key disclosures, like not remembering the night, go unexplored, and repeated questions convey incomplete listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear focus on the conduct violation and concrete next steps (mitigation letter, roommate statement, deadline). Focus centers on administrative resolution rather than the underlying drinking behavior, and the memory-gap disclosure was not incorporated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-drafts documents, invites corrections, and adopts Dylan's wording edits, so he clearly influences the session. Collaboration centers on paperwork rather than shared exploration, and repeated questions weaken the exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly frames choices, asks permission, and repeatedly affirms Dylan's control ('I won't submit anything without your okay') while being honest about limits. Information-dense, rapid-fire turns sometimes leave Dylan reacting rather than genuinely weighing options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions are almost exclusively logistical; the one values question early on is never developed. Dylan's motivation comes from self-interest, not anything the counselor deliberately elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Dylan as decision-maker and adopts his preferences. Some exchanges drift toward case management, with offers reading like a menu of services rather than genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dylan offers change talk ('I guess I could do that,' 'I came in ready to just argue my way out of it') that goes unrecognized and unreinforced. No open questions evoke his own reasons for change; effort is largely logistical.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Abundant sustain talk (denial, 'everyone does it,' turning 21) is not reinforced but also rarely redirected. No double-sided reflections or values-based reframes; the counselor sidesteps sustain talk by staying procedural.
