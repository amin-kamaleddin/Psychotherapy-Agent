# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, validating Kareem's resistance as understandable and never judging or pushing. The client explicitly confirmed this: 'you're not nagging me... you just asked me a question and actually listened.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally deep, capturing both surface content and underlying meaning (e.g., the daughter at 4 pm, the son copying the cough). The double-sided reflection about the cough was textbook-quality and each reflection elicited progressively deeper disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained clear focus on smoking cessation while guiding steadily from ambivalence through reasons to a concrete plan and next step. Emotional material like the cough was tied back to behavior, with no drift from the target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with the client owning behavior change and the clinician offering practical support ('you'd sort me out with those, right?'). Scored slightly lower because explicit joint weighing of options (e.g., patch strength/dosing) was cut short at session end.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was the explicit spine of the session: 'whatever you decide, it stands,' 'not for anyone else's approval. Just yours,' and asking permission before offering patch information. Kareem directly responded by claiming the decision as his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key motivation — the daughter, the cough, being at breakfast, quitting on his own terms — was elicited from the client rather than supplied. Kareem's 'it doesn't sound like much of a trade at all, does it?' demonstrated self-persuasion occurring in real time.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently positioned Kareem as the expert ('since you know your workday best,' 'you've basically sketched the whole path yourself') and handed agenda control back with 'What would be most useful from me?' The client's engagement visibly deepened in response.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout domain: evocative questions systematically pulled progressively stronger change talk, culminating in a self-generated plan (skip morning cigarette, patches for afternoons, honest follow-up). The clinician reinforced with affirmations and deliberately avoided taking ownership of the plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well without amplification, using double-sided reflections and brief acknowledgment before pivoting to change ('Thank you for trusting me with that'). Minor deduction because sustain talk was occasionally reflected back somewhat fully before pivoting.
