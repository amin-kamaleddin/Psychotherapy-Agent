# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, reassuring Nadine ('You're absolutely not failing'), normalizing her experience, and affirming her request for support as reasonable. A warm, nonjudgmental tone was maintained throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured Nadine's exhaustion and fear of failing, and validated her concerns. Some reflections could have gone deeper into the emotional weight of mother comparisons and the isolation of sleep deprivation, as many turns shifted quickly to information-giving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on infant sleep and Nadine's sleep deprivation throughout, with each turn advancing toward concrete action. The session ended with a specific tonight plan, a drafted note, and a scheduled Thursday check-in with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan and note were genuinely co-constructed, with Nadine setting parameters and adding content. Early turns were somewhat expert-driven, with menu-style options offered before fully exploring Nadine's own knowledge and preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly presented genuine choices and deferred to Nadine's judgment on strategies, note content, and follow-up timing. Her selections directly shaped the plan, and her self-directed decisions were affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions like 'What's one small, doable step you can take right now?' elicited commitment language and Nadine's own change narrative. Much of the middle session was information-delivery, reducing the proportion of client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor validated Nadine's expertise (wet diapers, weight gain) and let her shape the plan and the note. However, the counselor drove much of the information delivery with menu-style options rather than fully drawing out Nadine's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions effectively elicited commitment language ('Let's do the tonight plan,' 'I'll attempt drowsy-but-awake') and a concrete self-generated step. Opportunities for deeper change talk were limited by heavy information-giving at times.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledged sustain talk without amplifying it, reframed nursing-to-sleep as acceptable rather than a failure, and redirected toward realistic, small steps. Some sustain talk (e.g., 'I can't handle anything complicated') was met with information rather than strategic reframing toward confidence.
