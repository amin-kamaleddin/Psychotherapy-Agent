# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental throughout with several affirmations ('That's a strong insight,' 'That 8 is meaningful'). Affirmation of Aisha's vulnerable self-consciousness was implicit until the close, keeping this from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured both motivation and discouragement ('You sound worn out by this...'). Reflections sometimes stacked multiple questions, and the emotional pain behind 'I feel gross' and self-consciousness was not deeply explored until the session ended.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Crystal-clear focus on restarting exercise from first turn to last, with every exchange progressing the goal — rulers, barriers, specific step, schedule, reminders, fallbacks, tracking, rewards, and follow-up. No topic drift and logical movement from motivation to concrete action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was largely co-constructed from Aisha's preferences (walk, tracking, keeping the family phrase private), with check-ins before finalizing. Some counselor-provided menus of options gave a slightly uneven power-sharing dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated choice points ('Which feels most helpful?', 'Anything you want to add or adjust?') and respect for Aisha's decision to keep the family line private. Some turns subtly steered by presenting counselor-generated options rather than anchoring solutions in her own capacities.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Classic evocative techniques — importance/confidence rulers, exploring past helpers and barriers — drove a confidence rise from 6 to 8 with strong client commitment language. The counselor occasionally supplied reasons and solutions himself rather than drawing them out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha's input was invited and her contributions shaped the plan, but several turns were quite directive: supplying example steps, offering the half-X idea, drafting the check-in structure, and stacking questions. Little explicit acknowledgment that Aisha holds the expertise on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation via rulers, eliciting past helpers, and re-assessing confidence after planning (6 to 7 to 8), yielding strong desire and commitment language ('I actually feel ready now'). Minor deduction for supplying some change-relevant content himself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it didn't stick,' fear of the guilt spiral) was not dwelt on or reinforced; the half-X fallback and 'reset without guilt' framing effectively preempted all-or-nothing thinking. Redirects were solution-offered rather than drawn from the client, keeping this from a 5.
