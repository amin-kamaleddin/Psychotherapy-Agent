# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, normalizing ambivalence and reframing relapse as information rather than failure. Affirmations of strengths were limited beyond noting Sofia's curiosity about the numbers.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections consistently and accurately captured Sofia's perspective, including the dual costs/benefits of use and the money drain frustration. Emotional depth was limited — anxiety, family expectations, and fear of needing it more than expected were not explored further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on marijuana use throughout, progressing logically from function to costs to a concrete behavioral experiment with success criteria and follow-up. No topic drift; session culminated in measurable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plan was genuinely co-constructed through option sets and Sofia's own choices (Sunday, notes app, no reminders). Equal-partner teamwork evident in offering help while letting Sofia shape every element.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced with menus of options, 'your choice,' and 'no pressure' language. Sofia's no-promises stance was accepted without pushback, and relapse was framed as informational.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and a readiness ruler elicited some motivation (cost, next-day anxiety), but the counselor relied heavily on pre-constructed menus rather than Sofia's self-generated reasons. Key discrepancies — family expectations, being first in family to attend college, values and goals — were never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia was positioned as the expert with genuine power-sharing over topic, plan, and privacy decisions. The counselor offered somewhat more concrete tips and advice than a fully client-expert stance would.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk skillfully evoked through open questions, a readiness ruler, and menu-based options, leading to tentative commitment ('maybe a Sunday,' 'tracking the money couldn't hurt'). Change talk was not deepened with evocative follow-ups connecting changes to Sofia's values or goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('not a big deal,' 'not addicted') was acknowledged nonjudgmentally and the counselor quickly pivoted toward what Sofia wished worked differently. Some minimization, such as the fear of 'needing it more than I thought,' passed without a reflective reframe.
