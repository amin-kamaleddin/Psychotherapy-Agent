# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone with explicit affirmations and successfully avoided lecturing, which the client appreciated. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and generic rather than reflecting deeper, individualized regard for her specific values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid, accurate reflective listening around peer pressure, awkwardness, and her preference for texting her mom. Emotional depth was limited, with missed opportunities to explore feelings behind her comments on awkwardness and her parents' reaction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent, consistent focus on alcohol-related safety and refusal skills with no topic drift. Each turn built logically toward a concrete, actionable outcome including refusal lines, exit strategies, and the 'pineapple' code word plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session had a genuinely collaborative feel with menus of options, frequent check-ins, and a co-constructed safety plan incorporating the client's real details. Fell short as the counselor tended to supply final polished versions (e.g., rewriting her text) rather than letting her contributions stand.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed everything as the client's choice and validated her preferences without any pressure or control language. Autonomy was slightly diluted by frequent provision of ready-made answers and scripts rather than trusting her to generate her own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Included some evocative moments, such as inviting her to draft her own text and choose lines that 'feel like you.' However, relied heavily on providing scripts and tips rather than open questions exploring her own values, reasons, and confidence regarding alcohol.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked for the client's input and incorporated her contributions into the plan. The approach was noticeably advice-heavy, with tools, scripts, and lines predominantly generated by the counselor rather than drawn out of her own wisdom.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicited self-protective commitment language and reinforced ownership of her strategies. However, rarely deepened this change talk with evocative questions about her values or reasons for staying alcohol-free, relying instead on supplying tools.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled minimal sustain talk well, never reinforcing the status quo and consistently pivoting toward skills and safety planning. A notable sustain-flavored concern ('What if they keep pushing?') was skillfully normalized and redirected, though ambivalence about her parents' reaction was left unexplored.
