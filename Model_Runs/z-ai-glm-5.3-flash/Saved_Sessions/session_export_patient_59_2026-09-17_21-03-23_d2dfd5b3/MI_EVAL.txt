# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, even during dismissiveness ('yeah, whatever') and guarded moments. Non-defensive responses to Jamal's pushback honored his autonomy and vulnerability without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, progressively deeper reflections moved from surface frustration to meaning ('carrying the label while others skate') and captured emotional shifts. Reflections elicited Jamal's vulnerable disclosure about his mother.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good logical progression from frustration to values to a concrete step, but the marijuana use itself was never directly revisited — focus shifted entirely to employment and probation compliance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided: Jamal's direct question was engaged honestly, and all ideas (basketball, uncle's job) came from him. His shift from adversarial to cooperative tone demonstrated real partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy explicitly and repeatedly reinforced ('it's your call,' 'you don't have to promise me a thing'). No advice imposed; all next steps originated from Jamal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every motivational resource — basketball, the job, pride in his mom — was drawn from Jamal's own words via open questions. No lecturing despite multiple invitations, moving him to a concrete self-generated step within one session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Jamal positioned as the expert ('you're the expert on your own life'), and his contributions directly shaped session direction. His behavioral shift from passive to volunteering goals shows power-sharing was real.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful progressive evocation of desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment, culminating in a concrete step ('actually call my uncle this week'). Change talk was mirrored and amplified in reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk generally met with empathic reflection rather than agreement or argument, then pivoted to change questions. However, early reflections dwelled on unfairness themes for a couple of turns, and 'it's not about the one bag' risked affirming minimization.
