# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout with appropriate safety screening, but affirmations are generic and late, missing Amina's specific strengths. No explicit positive regard for her anger or struggles with her temper.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** One good early reflection, but the counselor then shifts almost entirely to bullet-point advice. Emotionally charged disclosures ('that just breaks my heart,' 'it's about respect,' 'that stung') are never reflected or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on co-parenting communication and the screen-time conflict, offering a menu of options early and following the client's chosen thread. Points deducted because direction is driven by the counselor's practical agenda rather than Amina's stated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Structured expert-to-client, with bullet-point advice preceding client requests and transactional check-ins. The role-play is a collaborative bright spot, but Amina's ideas rarely shape the counselor's approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dominant mode is prescriptive directives rather than options she owns, despite occasional choices like script vs. role-play. Her genuine autonomous insight about controlling her own household goes unnarrated and unaffirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amina's intrinsic motivation and values are abundant, but the counselor makes almost no effort to draw them out—no evocative questions, confidence scaling, or eliciting her own solutions. Virtually all content is counselor-supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes an expert/advice-giving role for most of the session, delivering dense prescriptive lists before being asked. Amina's own expertise about her ex-husband surfaces but is never acknowledged or built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerges naturally from the client but is never deliberately evoked or deepened—no exploration of hopes, confidence, or values. Her role-play success ('I got it out') is a missed prime opportunity to develop her sense of capability.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about her temper and her ex's dismissiveness is bypassed rather than strategically softened. The counselor responds to her doubts with more strategy lists instead of double-sided reflections.
