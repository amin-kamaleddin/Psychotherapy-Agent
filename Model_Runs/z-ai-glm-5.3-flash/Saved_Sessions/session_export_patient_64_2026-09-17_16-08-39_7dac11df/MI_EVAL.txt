# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained consistent respect and warmth, affirming Jordan's autonomy without judgment even during initial resistance. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and the checklist-heavy closings felt more procedural than deeply warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured Jordan's perspective accurately, including the nuanced autonomy concern about getting the shot only because others wanted him to. The counselor missed deepening the emotional moment when Jordan expressed guilt about potentially infecting his grandmother.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained excellent focus from the opening question through to a concrete commitment, with every exchange tied back to the target behavior. The session progressed logically from exploration to decision to action planning with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely a two-way exchange, with the counselor asking permission before sharing information and building the plan from Jordan's own logistics. Some exchanges tipped toward counselor-driven advice, such as the lengthy prescriptive checklist delivered before Jordan requested that detail.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently reinforced through explicit choice, flexible options, and framing the plan as Jordan's own decision. His closing remark about appreciating 'no lecture' confirms he experienced no pressure or control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew motivation from within the client, letting Jordan select his own reason and eliciting his intrinsic motivation around protecting his grandparents. The resolution was genuinely self-generated, though the emotional core of his motivation could have been developed more systematically.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positioned Jordan as the expert, and his contributions visibly shaped the session, including redirecting the entire plan toward his grandparents. Pre-packaged checklists and offers to draft messages occasionally fell short of fully co-creating every element.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evoked change talk through targeted questions, the readiness ruler, and the 'what would move you from a 4 to a 6-7' prompt, producing commitment language like 'it's happening.' Moving more quickly to logistics rather than deepening each change-talk moment kept this below a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged but not strategically softened, with early resistance met only with neutral reflection. Later sustain talk about personal risk was largely left unaddressed rather than met with double-sided reflections linking his self-reliance to family protection.
