# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard ('gracias por ser sincera,' 'no es fracaso; es información'), which directly justified Lucía's request to be treated the same if she relapses. No labeling or disapproval despite the client's dismissiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captured both frustration and underlying values (e.g., wanting to be present for her daughter). However, reflections were sometimes brief and formulaic, missing deeper exploration of her prior failed attempt, workplace pressure, and fear of mockery.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking cessation with a logical flow from exploration to planning to commitment. The closing summaries repeated the plan nearly identically several times, adding redundancy without new momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was fully co-constructed, with Lucía selecting and modifying every element (pill, tea, 3pm break, social script). Her comment 'por fin alguien no me sermoneó y por eso estoy intentando' is direct evidence of successful collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of client ownership through menu options, normalizing relapse as information, and suggestion-framed advice with no coercive language. The client's own words ('no me voy a castigar,' 'trátame igual') show she experienced genuine choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from the client rather than lecturing, using open questions and a confidence ruler to elicit her own reasons and commitment language. Relied on a standard technique set without deeper exploration of core personal values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong power sharing — the client's contributions substantially shaped the session, from choosing the intervention option to the storage place and social script. Repeated preference questions treated Lucía as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation produced concrete change talk: her daughter's reaction, a confidence rating of 5, self-generated plan elements, and commitment language ('Eso nada más prometo'). The question '¿Cuál te suena más manejable?' effectively drew out choice language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced, with effective pivots toward client strengths and choices. However, reflections sometimes restated sustain talk without double-sided pairings, and stress and social smoking themes were not explicitly reframed.
