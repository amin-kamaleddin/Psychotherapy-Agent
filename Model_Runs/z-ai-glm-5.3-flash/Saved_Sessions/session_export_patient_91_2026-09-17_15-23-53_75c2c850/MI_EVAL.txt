# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and non-judgmental, opening with validation and normalizing Marcus's uncertainty. Affirmations were somewhat generic and formulaic rather than deeply individualized reflections of his specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening accurately captured Marcus's ambivalence and fear of slipping. Reflections rarely explored deeper emotional content, such as his self-critical shame about not sticking with things.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior of daily blood sugar monitoring with no drift. Every turn tied back to the plan, and repeated confidence ratings progressively clarified goals and next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus's own contributions substantially shaped the final plan, and the counselor checked in frequently. However, the counselor tended to supply structure (templates, alarm texts, scripts) rather than co-creating it, tilting toward counselor-led problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed choice and respected Marcus's pacing, with Marcus exercising meaningful choices (gentle alarm, paper log, fridge placement, reward). Menus of options were sometimes presented rapidly in an advice-heavy way, slightly reducing the sense that solutions came from Marcus himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Marcus's own motivation, including his desire to show his doctor results and his checklist-driven persistence; confidence rulers tracked growing self-efficacy (6→8). Missed opportunities to explore deeper reasons for managing diabetes and develop his 'doing it right, not guessing' value.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for Marcus's input on choices, honoring his expertise. Much of the session shifted into the counselor providing concrete solutions, positioning the counselor as plan-provider rather than deliberately drawing out Marcus's ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently evoked and reinforced change talk through doable-step questions and confidence rulers, generating substantial commitment statements from Marcus. Missed chances to deepen his 'seven filled days would feel like a win' statement with commitment-strengthening questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Briefly acknowledged sustain talk but tended to respond with advice rather than strategic softening techniques. Self-critical statements were met with reasonable reframes but not double-sided reflections, and the contingency plan was repeated multiple times instead of actively reframing reasons against change.
