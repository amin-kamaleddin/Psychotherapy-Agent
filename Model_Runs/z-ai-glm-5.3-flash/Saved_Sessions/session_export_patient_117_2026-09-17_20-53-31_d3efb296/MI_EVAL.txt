# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth and unconditional positive regard, normalizing ambivalence and honoring Samuel's request to keep the car slips private ('That makes sense. I won't say a word'). No judgment or criticism is expressed anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both surface content and undercurrents (e.g., 'That worry about feeling like a failure again — that weighs heavy'). The psychiatrist also reflects the shame underlying the hidden car slips and past relapse secrecy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains tight focus on tobacco reduction: journal patterns, lunch cigarettes, car/desk urges, stress management, and a concrete plan (five lunch days, honest journaling, breathing exercise, follow-up accountability). Each response advances the target behavior without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is fully collaborative, with options offered as a menu and Samuel selecting ('The breathing thing, maybe'). The plan is co-constructed ('That's the deal, right?') rather than prescribed, and Samuel's own ideas shape every element of it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced: 'What feels most doable for you to test this week?' and 'We can tweak it next time based on what your journal shows.' The psychiatrist explicitly respects Samuel's pacing about telling his wife ('One win at a time') without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within by asking what helped, what a win would look like, and how it feels to have more tools than expected. This evoked Samuel's own insights about stress as the real trigger and his shift from external compliance to internal ownership.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is repeatedly located within Samuel: 'That's you doing this, not the journal, not your wife, not me,' and 'You know yourself.' Samuel's own contributions (toothpick, journal honesty, breathing) substantially drive the session's plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and deepens change talk ('What would make this week feel like a win,' 'What would arguing back sound like in your own words?'), producing rich client-generated language like 'arguing back with it once' and 'that feels real now.' This change talk is consistently reflected and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (urge at the desk, 'just one, you've earned it,' fear of failing, secrecy) is acknowledged and pivoted toward change, e.g., reframing 'it's worth a shot anyway' and 'we're not starting from zero.' The secret-keeping discussion is handled well with a client-generated backup plan; some sustain talk ('it hits fast') is explored in detail before redirecting.
