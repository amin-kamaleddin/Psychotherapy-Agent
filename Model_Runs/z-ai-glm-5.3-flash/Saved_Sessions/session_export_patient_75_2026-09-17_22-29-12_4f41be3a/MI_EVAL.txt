# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance with statements like 'There's no judgment in that; it's human' and validates the client's concerns without shaming. Affirmations occasionally feel slightly formulaic and could more explicitly acknowledge vulnerable disclosures like skipped doses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, consistent reflective listening throughout. The reflection 'the exhausting part wasn't the inhaler, it was hiding the doubt' captured the emotional core of the client's experience more precisely than she had articulated herself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on asthma management and medication adherence across every exchange. Even tangential fears (steroid risks) were channeled productively toward a concrete physician conversation and sustainable routine.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative, with the final plan co-constructed entirely from the client's own words. The counselor consistently sought her perspective before offering direction, modeling the 'partner' relationship the client explicitly wanted.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames decisions as the client's own and avoids prescribing solutions, attributing change to her ('That's a real change, and it came from you'). Falls short of 5 due to absence of explicit permissive statements ('it's up to you') emphasizing ownership of outcomes including setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key insight and element of the plan originated from the client, with no persuasive counterarguments offered. Evocative future-focused questions elicited deep motivation talk ('relieved,' 'I want to actually build the habit properly this time').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary power sharing — the nightstand idea, written questions, and husband involvement all originated from the client and became the treatment plan. The counselor explicitly framed expertise as residing within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and amplified commitment language, spotlighting 'I think I want to try that' and building toward strong change talk ('I'm ready to stop making it harder than it needs to be'). Consistently attributed solutions and change to the client herself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated steroid fears and doubts as legitimate concerns, exploring rather than confronting them ('What worries you most about it?'). Sustain talk was sometimes reflected back extensively without always strategically pivoting back toward change.
