# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating Tariq's frustration and affirming his compliance history. However, warmth was occasionally formulaic and acceptance was briefly expressed before pivoting to solutions, limiting its depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were accurate reflections early on, but the psychiatrist quickly moved from reflection to problem-solving, missing deeper emotional content. Vulnerability beneath the frustration—fear about his father, his mother's stress, anxiety about release—was touched but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained an exceptionally clear focus on restoring family contact while preserving Tariq's release timeline. Every exchange advanced this concrete goal, and the session never wandered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative elements like asking permission before role-plays, and Tariq's contributions (the mom line) were incorporated. Still, the power balance tilted toward the counselor, as the problem analysis, letter, scripts, and coping strategies all originated from the psychiatrist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered some choices and framed the plan as optional, but much of the session involved telling Tariq exactly what to do and say. Tariq's 'I'll try it your way' signaled the plan was counselor-generated rather than client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The motivation came largely from the counselor, who supplied the note text, exact sentences, and coping strategies rather than eliciting Tariq's own reasons and solutions. Early evocative intent quickly became prescriptive coaching.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed some respect for Tariq's input by asking questions and offering choices, but the session was largely expert-driven. Tariq's own ideas barely shaped the plan, with little acknowledgment that he holds expertise about what will work with staff.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged from Tariq and the psychiatrist opened with questions about desired outcomes, but the psychiatrist did not consistently reinforce or deepen this talk. Tariq's strongest commitment statements received brief affirmations without exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively did not dwell on sustain talk, briefly normalizing Tariq's frustration and redirecting toward constructive action. This protected the change focus, though normalization was sometimes brief and moved past feelings too quickly.
