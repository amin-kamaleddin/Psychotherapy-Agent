# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and non-judgmental with a guarded teen — no labeling, respectful stance throughout, and affirmations without patronizing praise (e.g., "it's your idea, not mine").

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening tailored to an adolescent, accurately capturing his irritation and reframing the puking-kid story around his own values; slightly below top marks as some reflections could go deeper.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear strategic momentum from normalization to risk identification to a concrete plan, but the session lacks an explicit closing summary or collaboratively agreed next step, and one turn was a placeholder.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are genuinely open and co-constructed; no unrequested advice is given, and solutions emerge entirely from Marcus with the psychiatrist acting as a thinking partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids prescriptive advice and reinforces ownership, but never explicitly affirms Marcus's freedom to choose his next steps (no 'whether you talk to Jake is up to you' statement) or hands off ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates from the assumption that motivation resides within Marcus — his speech shows a clear arc from "it's not a big deal" to "he'd probably be relieved," produced by consistently evocative, non-advising questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly locates expertise within Marcus, whose own contributions (excuse plan, Jake backup, casual framing) genuinely drive the session and shape the intervention.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout domain — skillfully elicits progressively deeper change talk from a precontemplative teen, moving from ambivalence about getting caught to commitment-flavored language ("he'd do it," "wouldn't hurt to actually say it out loud"), with each piece reflected and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Abundant sustain talk is never reinforced; it is met with brief acknowledgment followed by a pivot toward Marcus's own protective values or ambivalence.
