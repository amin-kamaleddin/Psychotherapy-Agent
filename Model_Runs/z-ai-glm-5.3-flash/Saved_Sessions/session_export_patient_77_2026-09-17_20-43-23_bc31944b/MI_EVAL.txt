# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and non-judgment, notably reframing 'I hate that I even think about that' with 'It's human.' Autonomy repeatedly affirmed ('That's totally allowed', 'You picked Friday yourself').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including a skilled double-sided reflection on trying versus it 'not counting.' Deeper emotions like loneliness were validated briefly rather than fully explored before pivoting to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session progressed logically from what works, to food adjustments, non-scale tracking, a concrete check-in plan, and a Friday action. Clear guiding structure with a summarizing, feasibility-checking close.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided—nearly every plan element originated from the client, and the counselor built on her ideas. Ended with a collaborative feasibility check ('too much, or actually manageable?').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit support of choice throughout ('no pressure, nobody watching', 'small, yours, doable'). Success criteria were framed as the client's own definition.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rich change talk was elicited from within, including the desire to be 'seen, not looked through' and her insight that self-imposed invisibility held power. Skillful questions deepened her own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently located expertise in the client ('you figured that out yourself'). All three plan elements were client-generated and explicitly credited to her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation of client-generated motivation ('I could try it once', 'I want people to look at me and not look through me'). Reinforced client insights by directing attention back to them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk and reframed scale-focused discouragement toward non-scale victories. Occasionally engaged with barrier framing rather than actively pivoting with double-sided reflections.
