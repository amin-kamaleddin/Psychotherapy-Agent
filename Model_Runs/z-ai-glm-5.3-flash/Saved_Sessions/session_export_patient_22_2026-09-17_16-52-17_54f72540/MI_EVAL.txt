# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with affirmations of progress and acceptance of Camila's harm-reduction goal. Falls short of a 5 because warmth is somewhat formulaic, with few individualized affirmations of her strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures Camila's core ambivalence, and the counselor later recognizes the social piece as her real fear. Early reflections are dense summaries bundling many elements, and emotions like loneliness and fear of losing her place in the group are never explicitly explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing weekday drinking from opening question to concrete action plan. Every exchange ties back to the target behavior with no topic drift, progressing from exploration to confidence ratings to rehearsal to commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely works as a partner through joint problem-solving, role-play, and incorporating Camila's own ideas (preemptive text, yoga reward) into the plan. Several strategy menus are presented top-down before eliciting her suggestions, making some moments feel advisory rather than equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: 'I'm here to explore options and support whatever you choose,' repeated offers of choice, honoring her preference not to over-plan, and acceptance of her harm-reduction goal. The change plan is entirely her own, as Camila explicitly acknowledges.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently draws motivation from Camila herself via rulers, open questions, and rehearsal rather than persuading. Her confidence rose from 5 to 8 in one session, and she articulated her own reasons and commitment ('Thursday, I got this').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Camila as the expert, letting her choose strategies, design her own script, and set the follow-up topic. At times slips into an expert role, delivering prescriptive lists of scripts and backup plans before she has generated her own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation of change talk through importance/confidence rulers, open questions, and concrete commitment invitations. Camila's strong change talk ('If I'm telling you I'm doing it, that means I probably actually will') is tracked, deepened through rehearsal, and locked in via reward and follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids dwelling on reasons against change, pivoting quickly toward solutions when barriers arise. The approach is solution-jumping rather than strategic softening — sustain talk about peer pressure is acknowledged but never fully explored with double-sided reflections, leaving some ambivalence bypassed.
