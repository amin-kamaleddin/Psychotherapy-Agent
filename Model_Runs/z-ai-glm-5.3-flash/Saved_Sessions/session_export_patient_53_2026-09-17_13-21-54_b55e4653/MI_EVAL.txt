# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with good normalizing statements and no labeling of Isabella's avoidance. However, affirmations were somewhat generic and could be more individualized to her specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening was solid, accurately capturing Isabella's perspective, and her self-criticism was gently addressed. However, reflections were often brief and quickly paired with advice, and the loneliness Isabella mentioned was never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor kept a clear focus on avoidance of community activities and built toward a concrete next step. The middle section drifted into social-skills coaching, narrowing focus away from Isabella's ambivalence and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offered Isabella choices about session direction and acted as an equal roleplay partner, with her answers visibly shaping the session. However, practical tips were delivered in a menu-like expert manner rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforced choice with no pressure or coercion, and Isabella's selections directed the session. To reach a 5, the counselor would more explicitly affirm Isabella's ownership of the change process itself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited through an effective open question and roleplay, moving Isabella to 'it doesn't feel totally impossible anymore.' However, the counselor often supplied content (scripts, tips) and used no rulers or values exploration to draw motivation from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Isabella chose the topic, strategy, and practice format, and her contributions clearly steered the session. However, the counselor assumed an instructional expert role when listing tips and scripts, partially offsetting the power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively evoked change talk, moving Isabella from sustain talk to 'maybe I could actually do this' through an effective small-change question and roleplay. However, emerging change talk was not deepened or reflected, and the session shifted toward skills coaching rather than continued evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with accepting, normalizing reflections rather than amplification, but it was bypassed more than actively softened. Her doubt ('I don't know if I'd actually do it') went unaddressed, leaving the ambivalence unresolved.
