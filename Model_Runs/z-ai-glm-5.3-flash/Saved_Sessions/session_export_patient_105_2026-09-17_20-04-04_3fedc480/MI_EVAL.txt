# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth and unconditional positive regard, e.g., 'It doesn't sound dumb to me' and affirming courage ('Trying something new when you've felt like a failure before takes real courage'). Sustain talk is received with respect, with no judgment or labeling anywhere in the transcript.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate and often go beyond surface content to underlying meaning, such as 'that replaying-everything-wrong part sounds exhausting' and 'it's hard to tell where their voices end and yours begins.' Naomi repeatedly confirms their accuracy, indicating deep understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on social anxiety in professional settings and progresses purposefully from the specific incident to coping options to commitment and a concrete follow-up plan. Each question builds on the last, guiding without dragging.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely between equals, with permission-oriented questions and invitations like 'what do you make of it?'. Naomi's answers directly determine the session's path, and no expert-driven directives appear.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced ('which one feels most doable for you?'), and the counselor never prescribes—Naomi generated the plan, selected the strategy, and defined success on her own terms. Framing like 'you don't have to do it perfectly to learn from it' further supports ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the client, never supplied; the 'trusted other' question elicited her own arguments for change, and 'whose voice does it sound like?' evoked deep self-understanding. The result is Naomi spontaneously concluding 'I want to try.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly conveys that expertise resides within the client ('that's your expertise talking'). Naomi chose breaks over rehearsal and chose self-talk as the follow-up focus—power-sharing at the highest level.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk, from asking which idea feels doable to using the 'what would you tell another photographer' technique. Change language is amplified ('That's not someone who's stuck—that's someone moving'), culminating in Naomi's commitment: 'I want to try.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged well, with a skillful double-sided reflection on hope and fear, and reframing of worry as courage. However, some sustain talk is reflected back at length (e.g., extended reflections on self-criticism), leaving room to pivot more briskly in places.
