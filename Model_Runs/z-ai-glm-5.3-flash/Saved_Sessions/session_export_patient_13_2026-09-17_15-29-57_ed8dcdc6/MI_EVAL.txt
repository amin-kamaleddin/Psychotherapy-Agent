# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warmth and nonjudgment were communicated consistently, with the client herself noting she didn't feel judged. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and lacked individualized affirmation of Anara's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections consistently captured the emotional core, including her fear and shame, and validated rather than argued. Reflections were occasionally brief and quickly pivoted to options lists, missing chances to deepen emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear focus was maintained and the session built logically toward a concrete, achievable plan. The target behavior of seeking medical care was deliberately deferred, and the session closed without any follow-up linkage in that direction beyond Anara's vague 'maybe.'

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The work was framed as joint, with the final plan emerging from Anara's preferences and invitations for input at each decision point. Collaboration was limited by reliance on pre-packaged option menus rather than soliciting Anara's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a clear strength: choice was explicitly affirmed at every juncture, refusals were honored without pressure, and the plan was built entirely at Anara's pace. Her request not to be pushed on the doctor topic was fully respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine evocation of preferences and self-efficacy occurred, but motivation for the core issue — seeking medical care — was never drawn out. Intrinsic motivation was not elicited; the counselor relied on option selection rather than exploring Anara's own values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Anara was positioned as decision-maker throughout, and her choices substantially shaped the plan. Options were mostly counselor-generated menus rather than co-created, and her expertise was implied more than explicitly named.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change language was elicited ('maybe the writing thing?'), but the counselor never explored Anara's own reasons for seeking care or her hopes. Small change statements were not deepened with reflective amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without arguing or amplification, with skillful pivots to doable steps. However, double-sided reflections linking her awareness of the problem to change were not used, and ambivalence was mostly sidestepped rather than strategically resolved.
