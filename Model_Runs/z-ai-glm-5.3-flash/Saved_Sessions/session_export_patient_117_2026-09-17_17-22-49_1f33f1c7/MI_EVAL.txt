# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations ('you already cut two cigarettes a day') and normalizes slips and partial success. Warmth is somewhat technique-driven, with few individualized affirmations beyond Samuel's journaling follow-through.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates solid reflective listening, accurately capturing Samuel's ambivalence about his wife's involvement and his desire for self-proof. Reflections tend to stay summary-level and pivot quickly to questions, leaving deeper emotional content (nervousness, all-or-nothing thinking, fear of blame) under-explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains an exceptionally clear focus on morning smoking reduction, with every exchange moving toward a concrete, behaviorally specific plan. Gently redirects when conversation drifts and ends with a coherently summarized plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session is genuinely co-constructed — Samuel shapes the plan, and the counselor incorporates each adjustment immediately. Power-sharing is slightly uneven early on, with a barrage of assessment questions and pre-formed scripts rather than consistently eliciting Samuel's own wording first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly and repeatedly reinforces Samuel's ownership ('I'm here to support whatever you choose'), offering menus of options and letting Samuel select and tweak. Affirms that partial success counts, fully supporting a self-directed change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out motivation via the importance ruler and forward-looking questions, eliciting strong self-generated change talk ('it's my idea, my pace'). Relies mainly on goal-logistics questions and rarely explores Samuel's deeper values or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats Samuel's contributions as central, adopting his script tweak verbatim and anchoring the plan to his choices. The opening message feels somewhat protocol-driven, and the counselor offers advice menus more than drawing plans purely from Samuel.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evokes change talk through the importance ruler, concrete goal questions, and commitment rehearsal, producing strong responses ('I want to try some morning changes', 'I can do that'). Lacks confidence rulers and deeper evocation of Samuel's values, with limited deepening of his optimism.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids amplifying doubt, using reassurance, reframing slips as data, and aligning with Samuel's rejection of all-or-nothing thinking. Tends to ignore rather than strategically pivot doubt statements, missing opportunities for double-sided reflections to actively resolve ambivalence.
