# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental tone with validation of Ethan's resistance and no shaming. Falls short of 5 due to generic, formulaic affirmations rather than individually tailored expressions of positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections, but reflective listening is sparse and the session is largely question- and advice-driven. Emotionally significant statements (tired of feeling like garbage, fear of caving, the ride scaring his mom) went without deep or attuned reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on alcohol use and progresses logically from risk assessment to harm reduction and refusal skills with no drift. Not a 5 because direction was somewhat prescriptive, driven toward a counselor-determined plan rather than Ethan's own stated priority.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration through permission-seeking, preference invitations, and incorporation of Ethan's ideas (Marcus, the head-nod signal). However, the overall structure is expert-led, with the counselor supplying the questions, tips, and scripts while Ethan mostly approves and tweaks.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choice and permission, and accepts Ethan scaling goals down. Weakened by counselor-authored tip lists delivered before Ethan's own ideas were explored and little affirmation of his ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation through inviting Ethan's own refusal line, which produced strong client-generated material. Overall superficial and inconsistent — no exploration of values, importance/confidence scaling, or development of discrepancy between belonging and disliking consequences.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative gestures exist and Ethan's contributions shaped the plan, but expertise largely resides with the counselor via tips, scripts, and structured plans. This is counselor-generated content with client buy-in rather than genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ethan produced meaningful change talk, but mostly from role-play and his own reflection rather than deliberate evocative techniques. The psychiatrist did not systematically reinforce or deepen change statements, moving quickly back to advice and logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and was occasionally lightly reframed, but was often met with more information or planning. No strategic double-sided reflections to help Ethan resolve ambivalence between belonging and his dislike of consequences.
