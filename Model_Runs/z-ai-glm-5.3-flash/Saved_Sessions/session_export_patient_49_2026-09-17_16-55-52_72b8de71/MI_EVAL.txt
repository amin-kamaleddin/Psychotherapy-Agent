# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with frequent specific affirmations and acceptance of client defensiveness without correction. Deeper, more individualized positive regard (e.g., affirming courage, normalizing social fears) would reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, but the session shifted to rapid-fire advice, leaving Hana's emotional concerns (fear of being 'the boring one,' fear of slipping Friday) largely unreflected. Most responses were strategy lists rather than understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, unwavering focus on smoking cessation with a logical trajectory from ambivalence to workplace target, refusal skills, and a concrete Friday plan. Every turn tied back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructed: goals, supports, and the refusal script came from Hana's own words and choices. Repeated strategy dumps tilted the exchange toward expert-driven problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was explicitly reinforced and the client's autonomous decisions drove the plan. Advice menus were dense and directive, with little emphasis that ownership of outcomes belongs entirely to Hana.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence scaling and role-play effectively elicited change talk and self-motivating statements, but the clinician supplied more reasons and tactics than were evoked. Core motivations (teeth, social belonging) were never deeply explored with open questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Power sharing was erratic: Hana's choices were incorporated, but lengthy clinician-generated tactic lists dominated multiple turns, positioning the counselor as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence scaling (5→7), role-play, and rehearsal skillfully drew out concrete commitment, desire, and self-motivating statements. A 5 would require deeper exploration of the client's own values-based reasons rather than provider-generated tactics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about social smoking and cold turkey was appropriately not amplified, but pivots to solution lists were abrupt. Concerns were neutralized but not actively softened with double-sided reflections or reframes.
