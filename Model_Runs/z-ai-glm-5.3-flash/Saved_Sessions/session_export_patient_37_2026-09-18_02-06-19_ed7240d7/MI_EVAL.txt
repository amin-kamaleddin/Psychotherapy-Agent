# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard; Elena's hesitancy and distrust are validated rather than corrected. Standout moments include the reframe that her ambivalence comes from 'being a caring mom' and acknowledgment of her feeling judged for asking questions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and complex, including a strong double reflection and checks on understanding. However, the emotional layer—Elena's fear, isolation, and relief—is often left unexplored in favor of the cognitive structure of her dilemma.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on the vaccine decision with a clear clinical arc from values to evidence to next steps. Every turn advances the goal, ending with a concrete, achievable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed, with Elena originating its core and the psychiatrist refining it. Permission is sought before educating, and agendas are negotiated rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Pressure is explicitly disclaimed, permission asked, and choices offered; her 'no' to on-the-spot commitment is fully accepted. Elena herself confirms the mechanism: 'I'm not being pushed, so I don't have to push back.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from Elena's own processing, with values elicited and her key shift credited to her own thinking. But the most persuasive moment came from counselor-delivered data, and deeper techniques (elaboration, scaling, developing the 'she's safe' value) are absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elena is positioned as expert and evaluator of the evidence, with real choices shaping the session. Her contributions demonstrably alter the session's direction, and she notes the psychiatrist is the first person to ask what she thinks.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is evoked strategically through values, readiness, and next-step questions, and reinforced well. However, it is collected rather than developed—no elaboration, confidence exploration, scaling, or consolidation of commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully—pharma distrust is met with validation plus independent sources, and the 'both things are true' framing softens her chickenpox experience. But the side-effect worry was deferred rather than worked with, leaving residual ambivalence unresolved.
