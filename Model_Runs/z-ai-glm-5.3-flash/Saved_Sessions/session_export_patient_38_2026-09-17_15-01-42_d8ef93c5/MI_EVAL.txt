# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard was maintained throughout, with no judgment or pressure even when the patient tested boundaries. The patient explicitly credited the nonjudgmental approach: 'Thanks for not pushing me today.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening accurately captured the patient's fear, uncertainty, and frustration. Reflections stayed somewhat brief and did not deeply explore the emotional core of her lack of control or fear of the future.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session had a clear logical arc from emotional processing to concrete next steps (handout, clinic list, possible call). A stronger summary of change themes and a collaboratively named goal would have strengthened direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient's choices consistently shaped the session, and the question list positioned her as decision-maker. The two dense information-giving turns briefly shifted into a more expert-driven mode.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was exemplary — every choice point was offered as an option and 'no decisions' boundaries were respected without pushback. The patient's tentative self-directed commitment ('maybe I'll call in a week or two') followed directly from this.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and permission-based menus elicited the patient's own motivations without lecturing or unsolicited arguments for change. Deliberate evocative techniques (elaboration, importance/confidence rulers) were not used, so some motivation surfaced spontaneously.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session direction was guided by patient input, with choice points and permission-seeking before information. The dense, lecture-like information blocks limited her influence on pacing until she steered the conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective conditions for change talk emerged ('I never want to feel that again,' 'the lighter periods thing is sitting in my head,' 'maybe I'll call'). Opportunities to actively deepen change talk, such as exploring 'saying that out loud is weird' or using scaling questions, were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently met with acceptance rather than argument, preventing resistance from escalating. Sustain talk was tolerated rather than strategically reframed (e.g., no double-sided reflections linking her fear to readiness for change).
