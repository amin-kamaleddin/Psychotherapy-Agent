# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with consistent affirmations of Marcus's choices and validation of his fear of failure. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic, and his frustration with past failures could have been normalized more warmly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens with a strong empathic reflection, but then shifts to questions and advice-giving with few deep reflections. Emotionally significant disclosures (father's heart attack, fear of failure, expecting a lecture) received logistics and planning rather than reflection of their emotional weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity, with every turn moving the plan forward toward the concrete Tuesday family walk. No topic drift, and the session concludes with firm commitments and a scheduled check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options are offered as choices and Marcus's own contributions (Tuesday, the 5:30 calendar block, texting his wife) shape the plan. However, the counselor structures nearly every step, tilting the power balance somewhat toward the counselor during planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choice, asks permission before advising, and respects Marcus's decisions (e.g., declining the assistant text). Rapid-fire stacked questions and a pre-packaged option menu slightly constrain space for Marcus's own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The success question and confidence ruler drew out genuine change talk, but much of the session's content (options, reminder texts, cues, if-then framing) was counselor-supplied rather than elicited. Marcus's strongest motivations (dancing, family support) surfaced spontaneously and went largely undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission before offering options, but the middle of the session is counselor-driven, following a 'counselor proposes, client approves' pattern. Client-generated strategies like the calendar block and texting his wife deserved more explicit credit and expansion.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and reinforces change talk through the success question and confidence ruler, eliciting strong commitments ('an 8 now that I've said it out loud'). Sometimes supplies plan content rather than letting Marcus generate more of it himself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Does not dwell on barriers, pivoting skillfully from gym objections and past failed programs to feasible options and reframing walking as legitimate exercise. The derailer question invites barrier talk but is structured into productive if-then problem-solving.
