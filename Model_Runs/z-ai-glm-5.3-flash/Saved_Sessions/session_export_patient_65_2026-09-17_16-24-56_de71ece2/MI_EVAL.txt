# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone toward a guarded, court-mandated client, with warm supportive statements. Positive regard leaned on formulaic encouragement ('That's a solid plan') rather than individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opening reflection accurately captured logistical stress, but empathy stayed surface-level. When Julian expressed fear of getting in more trouble, the counselor pivoted to practical scripts rather than reflecting the emotional weight of legal consequences.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's strongest domain — unwavering focus on completing court-ordered community service with zero topic drift. Each turn advanced a concrete, time-bound plan ending in a Thursday 3:30 check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Functioned more as efficient case management than an equal partnership, with the counselor supplying scripts, checklists, and structure while Julian mostly acquiesced. His own knowledge was only used to fill the counselor's template rather than shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered genuine choices (check-in format, script tweaks) and respected his refusal to share his case number. However, options were largely about logistics within a counselor-prescribed path, edging toward a controlling style despite polite framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Solutions and motivation were supplied from outside rather than drawn from the client; his purely external motivation ('don't end up back in court') was never explored. The one open question in the opening was effectively answered by the counselor's own menu of options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor adopted an expert, directive stance — drafting scripts multiple times and delivering checklists — while Julian never shaped the session's direction. The closing recap read more like a discharge summary than a co-constructed plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no deliberate effort to evoke or develop change talk; no open questions probing personal values or what finishing would mean for him. Preparatory statements arose from the logistics agenda rather than skillful evocation of intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce Julian's avoidant talk and occasionally validated it without amplifying resistance. However, no strategic double-sided reflections were used, and sustain talk about not wanting more sessions was accommodated rather than leveraged motivationally.
