# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard with affirmations like 'you're already collecting evidence against it' and 'that care for others is part of who you are.' No judgment anywhere; sustain talk is met with acceptance rather than correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that captures emotional nuance, such as 'treating my own time like it matters' and 'Sunday mornings are your safe container.' Occasional reflections add interpretation rather than staying with the client's meaning, and some emotional layers (fear of sister's reaction) could be reflected more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on extending self-respect beyond Sundays, with each response building momentum from noticing the routine to a concrete next step (Tuesday call with sister). Session never wanders aimlessly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaves as an equal partner, asking rather than telling and building jointly on Elena's material. Occasionally offers somewhat leading framings (e.g., 'what would that say about the person you're becoming?') that shape the agenda beyond fully egalitarian exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership is consistently supported—the entire plan is Elena's idea, with no advice-giving or pressure. The counselor explicitly positions Elena as the authority on her own relationships and choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Draws motivation entirely from within the client—every major insight, from self-trust to the guilty-yes distinction to the Tuesday plan, originates in Elena's words. Consistently elicits and expands client motivation rather than supplying it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly conveys that expertise resides with the client ('you know your life best,' 'you know your relationships best'). Elena's contributions clearly shape the session, with the counselor following her lead rather than prescribing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through open questions that produce rich client statements ('Maybe it starts with one small no this week'). Follow-up questions deepen these statements rather than settling for surface affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Neither argues against sustain talk nor dwells on it, using double-sided reflections like 'part of you worries about the 'selfish' label, AND you've recognized that a guilty yes was never really giving.' Most redirections are gentle neutral reflections rather than active strategic reframing.
