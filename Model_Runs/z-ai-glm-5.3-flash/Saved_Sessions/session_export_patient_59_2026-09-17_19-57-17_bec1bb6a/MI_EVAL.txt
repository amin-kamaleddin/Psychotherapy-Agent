# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout — affirms Jamal's honesty repeatedly, normalizes reluctance and frustration, and explicitly declines to moralize about marijuana. Even his defiant honesty ('I'd be lying to your face') is treated as valuable rather than resistant.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening that deepens over the session, notably capturing that the phone represents trust and being seen as grown. Falls short of 5 because some earlier reflections summarize rather than fully explore the emotional layer (e.g., feeling labeled 'a felon').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear purposeful focus building from validation to a self-generated action plan, anchored on Jamal's own concerns. Not a 5 because the mandated target behavior (marijuana use) is never directly revisited, leaving the link to future behavioral focus implicit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine exchange between equals — the counselor offers no advice, accepts Jamal's corrections gracefully, and the entire plan was client-generated. Power is visibly shared across every turn.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary support of choice: explicitly honors Jamal's disclaimers ('that's fine, that's your call') and boundary-setting without pushback. Never pressures or prescribes; Jamal's growing ownership reflects internalized choice rather than compliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Zero advice or persuasion; every movement comes from questions drawing out Jamal's own motivations (freedom, trust, respect). Deepens change talk with meaning-focused questions, culminating in Jamal's spontaneous recognition that trust is recoverable.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Jamal as the expert ('You know her better than I do') and hands agenda-setting to him from the opening question. The counselor attributes the entire plan to Jamal's own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic open questions ('what does less drama look like?', 'what changed for you just now?') elicit substantial change talk from a precontemplative client, including a concrete self-generated first step. Change talk is reinforced with affirmations and deepened with follow-up questions about meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully rolls with sustain talk without amplifying it — never argues about marijuana, instead pivoting to Jamal's self-identified 'actual drama' (mom, phone, freedom). Explicitly disclaims needing to agree weed is a problem, defusing the debate entirely.
