# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental regard throughout. Validated the patient's experience, normalized slips as 'information, not proof you can't quit,' and explicitly countered her shame spiral.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered accurate, complex reflections capturing both content and emotional nuance, including her fear of failing again. Fell short of a 5 because deeper emotional themes (shame, the mother/music connection, daughter's eye-rolling worry) were noted but not explored with further reflective depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, unwavering focus on smoking cessation and converged on a concrete, specific plan (playlist, breathing, gum, alarm, text to daughter, wine strategy). Every exchange built momentum toward tonight's experiment with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plans were genuinely co-constructed, with the psychiatrist proposing options and incorporating every patient modification (music, evening cigarette, tea swap). Questions like 'Want to cue it now?' positioned them as equal partners.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly and explicitly supported patient choice through menus with the decision returned to her ('Which option feels more doable and kind to yourself?'). Never pressured or prescribed, even avoiding the promise framing that historically triggered her failure spiral.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently drew out the patient's own motivations via rulers and open questions, producing her reasons (daughter, feeling 'gross', 'my time, my lungs, my choice'). Some responses slipped toward providing content (suggested text wording, playlist phrasing, coping menus), mildly pre-empting her generative process.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated the patient as the expert on her own life, seeking permission and asking what felt most supportive. Her contributions (evening cigarette, music option, reminder phrase, tea substitution) substantively shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk through importance/confidence rulers, reflections of her motivations, and amplifying her confidence shift ('that's real progress'). Asking 'What would nudge your confidence up?' directly produced stronger commitment language, ending with clear commitment and optimism.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively softened sustain talk with strategic reframes ('an experiment, not a promise'; slips as 'information, not failure'). Occasionally invited elaboration on barriers, which briefly amplified them, though always redirected back to solutions.
