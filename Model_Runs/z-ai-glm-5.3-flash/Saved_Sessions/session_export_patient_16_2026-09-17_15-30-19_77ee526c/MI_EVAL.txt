# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance toward a mildly defensive teen, with respectful openers and affirmations, even when the client minimized risks. Falls short of 5 as affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, with little explicit normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections about relaxation, friends, and school concerns, but reflections stayed simple and quickly pivoted to option lists. Emotional nuances like fear of losing basketball, anxiety about Sarah's teasing, and stress as a trigger were acknowledged only superficially.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on vaping and its consequences, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete, self-generated plan with follow-up. Some momentum was counselor-driven via option lists, slightly diluting collaborative focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative feel with joint plan-building, shared role-play, and mutual refinement ('we'll tweak if needed'). Not a 5 because most content was framed as expert-supplied menus Anika selected from rather than fully co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with consistent choice-offering ('Your choice what to do with any ideas,' 'Which do you prefer?') and no pressure or coercion anywhere. Anika fully owned the plan, and the counselor reinforced her ownership through confidence ratings and self-generated steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within—basketball goals, confidence scaling (7→9), triggers, and alternatives—producing strong change talk. Misses 5 as the counselor moved quickly to providing options early on and deeper intrinsic motivations beyond external stakes were never fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Anika as the expert, and her contributions genuinely shaped the session—she chose the goal, selected refusal lines, and generated the key step of leaving the pen at home. Falls short of 5 because the counselor predominantly supplied ideas (option menus, scripts) rather than first eliciting her solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked motivation through pros/cons exploration, confidence ratings (8, then 9), and practical step elicitation, yielding strong change talk ('I play better when I'm not high,' 'can't cave if I don't have it on me'). Falls short of 5 because deepening centered on scripts and logistics rather than her deeper reasons for change, with the counselor sometimes supplying options instead of waiting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue against sustain talk and acknowledged its legitimacy, but offered little strategic softening—no double-sided reflections pairing sustain with change talk. The rapid pivot to menu-driven scripts kept focus on logistics rather than resolving ambivalence.
