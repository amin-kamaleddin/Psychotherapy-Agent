# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, affirming Jamal's efforts and validating his difficult position without labeling or criticizing his drinking or minimization. Falls short of a 5 as the warmth feels somewhat formulaic, with rapid pivoting to strategy lists that can seem like problem-managing rather than genuinely regarding the whole person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately capture surface content, but the counselor repeatedly bypasses Jamal's deeper emotional disclosures about the stakes of losing the team and the 'picking them or the team' dilemma. Responding to his vulnerable admission that 'my voice got smaller' with 'That's useful info' is particularly tone-deaf.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays firmly anchored on alcohol use at team parties and refusal skills with no topic drift. However, the direction is tactical rather than motivational, narrowing quickly to refusal scripts without consolidating a broader change goal or linking to Jamal's stated stakes of season and college.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are genuine collaborative elements, including permission-seeking and letting Jamal's choices shape role-play scenarios. However, the counselor dominates content with scripted lines and option menus, and when Jamal offers his own strategy (talking to a captain privately), it isn't integrated, making power-sharing superficial.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offering strategy choices and asking permission before role-play superficially supports autonomy, but the rapid-fire menus with immediate 'which feels most doable?' pressure feel like a compliance exercise. Jamal's explicit ambivalence ('I don't know if I want things to be that different') goes unacknowledged, and there are no explicit statements affirming that change is his decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The approach is fundamentally prescriptive, with motivation assumed rather than drawn out—strategies, scripts, and justifications are counselor-supplied while Jamal selects from menus. Jamal's own motivations (the season, college prospects, team identity) surface spontaneously but are never explored, and his values and vision of success are never asked about.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and letting Jamal choose scenarios give him some ownership, but the dominant pattern positions the counselor as the idea source and Jamal as the selector. Jamal's insight about team culture (e.g., Tyler not roasting him in front of everyone) is met with another option list rather than being built upon as the client's own wisdom.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited—Jamal acknowledges the harm, generates his own lines, and reports rising confidence (5 to 6.5) during role-play, aided by confidence scaling. However, the counselor never deepens this talk or connects the strategies to Jamal's own goals, missing prime opportunities to amplify his stated stakes into sustained motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal's substantial sustain talk is often reinforced rather than softened—his statement that it's 'not like it's a real problem' is immediately met with harm-reduction strategies that implicitly accommodate the status quo. The counselor never examines the 'it's how the team bonds' rationalization or pivots Jamal's own words toward change, with most forward movement being counselor-provided content.
