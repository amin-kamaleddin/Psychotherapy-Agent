# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental welcome throughout with no disapproval of the drinking; coach role-play affirmatively praised Jamal's honesty. However, warmth was somewhat formulaic, with few affirmations of his specific strengths like his loyalty or courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate simple reflections captured ambivalence ('torn between staying on the team and pressure to fit in'), but reflections were brief restatements. Rich emotional material — basketball as identity, fear of parents, loyalty to teammates — was passed over in favor of moving to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed well-focused with clear agenda-setting, appropriate safety screening, and a concrete outcome achieved via a rehearsed coach conversation. Minor drift occurred during the pros/cons deliberation about disclosing, though it was clinically relevant.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options were offered and Jamal's script edits were accepted, with role-play co-constructed at his pace. However, the counselor generated most content (scripts, pros/cons, replies), leaving Jamal selecting from a menu rather than co-creating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeated choice-offering at each decision point, and Jamal selected and modified the script. Yet there was subtle pressure toward disclosure — the counselor framed waiting as riskier — and scripted answers replaced exploration of what Jamal thought would be honest.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor's default was supplying answers rather than drawing out Jamal's own motivation; the one scaling question elicited good material that was never explored. Intrinsic motives — love of basketball, discomfort with lying, loyalty values — were never evoked, leaving Jamal externally motivated at session's end.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal's input was solicited and options rather than prescriptions were offered, respecting some of his expertise. But the counselor often slid into the expert role, providing scripts and coach norms and dictating the structure of the disclosure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A well-timed scaling question elicited high importance (9/10) and honest low confidence (5/10). The low confidence with 'the whole team's doing it' begged for deeper exploration, and the change talk that emerged stayed compliance-based rather than being developed into intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced, but it was handled passively — repeated concerns ('everyone was doing it,' 'he might not even find out') were reflected neutrally without strategic reframing. The pros/cons list actually amplified sustain talk, and opportunities to link Jamal's team identity to reasons for change were missed.
