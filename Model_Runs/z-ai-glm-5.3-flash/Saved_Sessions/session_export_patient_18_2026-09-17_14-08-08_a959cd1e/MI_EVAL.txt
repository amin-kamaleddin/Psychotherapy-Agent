# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, normalizing party pressure and validating Anya's experiences. However, warmth occasionally felt formulaic, such as the rapid-fire safety spiel in the opening message.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections like acknowledging the sting of the 'buzzkill' jab showed good attunement. However, the counselor frequently moved quickly from reflections into advice lists, and key emotional disclosures ('It felt sketchy,' 'Like I'm choosing it, not just avoiding it') went unreflected and undeveloped.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on Anya's self-identified goal of navigating parties, moving systematically through refusal skills, delivery practice, and exit planning tied to the concrete upcoming party. Minor deduction for tangential safety content that exceeded the needs of a lower-risk teen and diluted focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor framed the work as joint practice, offering role-play options and co-constructing an exit plan using Anya's real-life details (her sister, the Grandma code word). Deduction because early interactions were directive, with pre-packaged strategy lists that felt more like instruction than two-way problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support throughout, with repeated choices offered (role-play modes, firmer practice, text vs. rehearse) and flexible responses to Anya's preferences. Her closing insight — 'Like I'm choosing it, not just avoiding it' — directly evidences fostered ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some of Anya's motivation was elicited, including her own refusal lines and code word, but the counselor relied heavily on supplying content rather than drawing it out. Few open questions explored her own reasons for not drinking, her values, or her confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered choices about practice modes and invited Anya's reactions, supporting shared decision-making. However, reliance on expert-supplied lines and tips positioned the counselor as the advice-giver rather than leveraging Anya's own expertise, which produced her best material.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor created opportunities for Anya to voice strong change talk, including her commitment to never again riding with impaired drivers and her realization that she is 'choosing it, not just avoiding it.' However, these moments were not strategically deepened, and there were no confidence or commitment questions about the upcoming party.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal, and the counselor's skill focus successfully redirected the session toward Anya's goals rather than deficit discussion. Slight deduction for not explicitly acknowledging or reframing her mild ambivalence ('I don't feel super pressured, but it's awkward') toward her stated values.
