# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveyed respect and warmth, affirming the client's expertise ('You know yourself better than anyone') and offering a genuine apology for errors. However, the repeated 'RESPONSE 1'/'RESPONSE 3' slips disrupted the respectful connection and required the client to ask multiple times for clarification.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong, accurately capturing both sides of ambivalence and deeper motivations (e.g., the father-related health concerns). The communication breakdowns temporarily undermined the sense of being understood, though recovery was skillful.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained unwavering focus on coffee consumption and progressively built momentum toward resolving ambivalence, linking afternoons, blood pressure, and the upcoming checkup. Even the broken responses did not derail the therapeutic trajectory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor actively invited the client's ideas and let their answers shape the plan, embodying equal-partner inquiry. The two non-answers ('RESPONSE 1', 'RESPONSE 3') created one-sided moments that forced the client to ask for plain communication, though recovery was good.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership were consistently reinforced, with the counselor never prescribing change and explicitly framing the checkup as optional. The client's decision not to change anything yet was met with validation rather than challenge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from within the client through open questions, eliciting personal reasons like the father's cardiac history and a desire for 'peace of mind.' No advice or counselor-supplied reasons for change appeared anywhere, and reflections skillfully amplified the client's change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly located expertise in the client ('You're the expert on your mornings') and credited the client's own solutions. The client's ideas substantially shaped the session direction, a hallmark of excellent power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions consistently elicited the client's own change language, including health motivation, family history, preparatory language, and desired outcomes. The final explorations of 'what would it feel like' deepened commitment language expertly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement and generally redirected via neutral or double-sided reflections toward the client's own reasons for change. Some reflections validated the pro-coffee position extensively before pivoting, though this was largely strategic and effective.
