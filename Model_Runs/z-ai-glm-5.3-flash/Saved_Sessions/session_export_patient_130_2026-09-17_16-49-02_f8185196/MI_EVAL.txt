# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout and affirmed Viktor's reliability and control without dismissing his defensive posture. The warmth was somewhat formulaic, and the counselor missed chances to explicitly normalize his reluctance about being pressured to attend.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Viktor's sense of control, his pride in responsibility, and his frustration at being pushed. However, reflections rarely reached the emotional layer beneath the bravado, such as his fear of losing the marriage or the hurt behind 'nothing I do counts anyway.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained consistent focus on the drug use and its relational consequences, converging on a concrete, testable plan with follow-up scheduled. The opening four-part compound question diffused focus, and early exchanges circled somewhat without clear momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was genuine collaboration early on, and the final plan was co-selected, but the power balance tilted counselor-ward mid-session with unilateral option menus, scripts, and tactics. Viktor's comment that the script 'sounds a bit like something off a card' suggests the plan felt partly counselor-authored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly affirmed Viktor's choice ('It's your call,' 'I'll support what you choose') and framed the plan as 'you taking control without giving everything up,' aligning with his autonomy values. The counselor was fairly directive in supplying scripts and tactics, and did not reinforce his ownership when he hedged about her reaction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some authentic change talk was evoked through scaling questions and prompts about what matters most in his marriage. However, evocation was inconsistent—the counselor frequently filled space with advice and scripts rather than developing key motivational material like 'I love her' and 'we used to do stuff.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited Viktor's input and asked permission to brainstorm, but shifted toward an expert stance mid-session by generating most solution options, scripts, and tactics unilaterally. Viktor shaped the plan somewhat, but the ideas largely originated from the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicited meaningful change talk—Viktor admitted 'I don't want to lose her,' proposed a small step, and committed to three concrete actions with a confidence rating of 6-7. Early evocation was somewhat scattered, and deeper change talk about 'we used to do stuff' could have been developed before moving to advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not reinforce sustain talk and offered some reframes affirming Viktor's values, but used no deliberate double-sided reflections to pivot from sustain talk to change talk. Substantial session time was spent on his rationalizations without strategic redirection, and advice-giving sidestepped rather than resolved the ambivalence about friends' pressure.
