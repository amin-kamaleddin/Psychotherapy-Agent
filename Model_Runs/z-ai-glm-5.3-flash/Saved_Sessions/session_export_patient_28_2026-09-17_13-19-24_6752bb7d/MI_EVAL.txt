# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with affirmations and normalizing statements that conveyed positive regard. Kept from a 5 by occasional formulaic warmth and repetitive closing checklists that felt mechanical rather than individually attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections ('losing the built-in cues and a workout buddy is huge') captured Dante's situation well. However, vulnerable disclosures like 'It's the keeping-going part I stink at' and 'all or nothing' were met with practical tips rather than reflection of the underlying shame, and his closing relief went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on re-establishing exercise from start to finish, with concrete steps agreed (Tue/Thu/Sat walks, dishes cue, 5-minute fallback, Friday check-in). Weakness was that focus sometimes took a prescriptive form rather than collaboratively guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine power-sharing (invitation to plan, menu of options, Dante shaping choices), but the style was largely transactional—present solution, seek approval, repeat—with stacked closed questions and a consultant-pitching feel rather than equal co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy-supportive moments included offering options, asking 'Which option sounds doable?', and framing Tuesday 'as an experiment, not a test.' But unsolicited solutions piled up, the repeated checklists ignored Dante's cue to ease off ('you sound like my wife'), and his ownership of the decision was never explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain: the core method was counselor-generated advice (habit-stacking, fallback rules, rewards) rather than drawing motivation from Dante. His own motivations and insights surfaced spontaneously but were left underdeveloped, with almost no open evocative or scaling questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menu options and 'Which option sounds doable?' gestured toward client expertise, but the plan structure, strategies, and check-in criteria were largely counselor-generated, with Dante mostly approving or tweaking rather than positioned as the architect of his own plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dante offered rich change talk spontaneously ('the dishes thing — that's the piece I was missing'; 'I feel... a little something'), but it was rarely reflected, amplified, or deepened, and was typically met with more advice or logistics. No evocative follow-ups or confidence rulers were used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('feels a bit pointless,' 'I can do one good week') was neither judged nor reinforced, which is a strength. However, it was mostly bypassed with new practical suggestions rather than addressed through double-sided reflections or the client's own ambivalence work.
