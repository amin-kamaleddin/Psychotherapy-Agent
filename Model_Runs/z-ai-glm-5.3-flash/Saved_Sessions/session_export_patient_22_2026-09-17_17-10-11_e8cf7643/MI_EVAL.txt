# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard — validates the self-referral as 'hard and brave,' never challenges minimizations with judgment, and affirms Camila's limits ('keeping Saturdays is totally reasonable'). No labeling or conditional regard appears anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate throughout ('three days wrecked — sounds really striking') but stay mostly at the level of facts and logistics rather than deeper emotions. Missed opportunities include the sting of friends' criticism, fear of social loss, and exhaustion with hangovers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains exceptionally clear focus, moving purposefully from mapping a typical week, to one specific experiment, to rehearsal, to fully specified details (start Wednesday, preset text, home by 10, log, check-in). Every response ties back to the target behavior with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed — Camila selects, personalizes, and rehearses, reflected in her closing statement of shared ownership ('I can actually do this'). However, the 'quick check' and bulleted option lists occasionally make the counselor the agenda-setter, offering three to four suggestions where one collaborative question would suffice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly reinforced via structural choices, experiments framed as selectable options, and explicit endorsement of Camila's limits. Camila's closing reflection — that she expected pressure to quit entirely and instead felt free to design her own approach — confirms genuine choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently draws motivation from Camila (desire questions, confidence ruler, rehearsal), with her generating her own rationale and commitment language. Deduction: the counselor sometimes pre-empts evocation by supplying refusal scripts and coping moves before asking open questions that would have let Camila generate solutions herself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Camila's contributions (the humorous pushback line, phone-note log, Wednesday reward dinner) substantially shape the final plan, with the counselor repeatedly asking 'which sounds more realistic/feels most natural.' Deduction: the middle section leans toward expert content delivery, shifting at times from power-sharing to advice-giving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully and consistently evokes change talk — the opening question elicits desire language, structured options prompt Camila to generate her own rationale, and rehearsal leads to her strongest commitment statement ('I actually feel more ready than when I walked in... I can actually do this'). The counselor repeatedly has Camila do the motivational work herself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids reinforcing sustain talk, pivoting from 'I don't wanna stop altogether' to her own goal of control and reframing as 'protecting weekdays while keeping weekends.' Minor deduction: no double-sided reflection is used to acknowledge the social-loss fear directly, and ambivalence beneath 'Sofia's gonna give me grief' is handled with scripts rather than exploration.
