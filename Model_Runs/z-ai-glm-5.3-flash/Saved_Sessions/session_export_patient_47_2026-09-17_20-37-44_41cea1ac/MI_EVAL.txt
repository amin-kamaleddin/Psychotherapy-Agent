# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with warm, specific affirmations (e.g., praising her parenting and her own-terms commitment). Slightly formulaic at times and could normalize ambivalence more explicitly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately capturing both sides of Evelyn's ambivalence and her son's concern. Missed opportunities to deepen emotion, such as her fear about the fall.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on alcohol use throughout, steering every exchange back to the target behavior. The session progressed logically toward a concrete, self-generated plan with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided interaction: asked permission, sought her input, and the plan emerged from her own ideas. Would benefit from more explicit framing of the work as shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, repeated emphasis on her choice ('whatever you decide,' 'on your own terms'). Evelyn explicitly said the plan felt like her own, and slips were normalized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never lectured; all motivation came from Evelyn herself through skillful open questions. Her closing remark — 'I've talked myself into more than I expected' — is direct evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly located expertise in the client ('You know your life best') and the plan was entirely hers. Could more explicitly name her as the expert on her own change and negotiate the agenda openly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation of change talk through open questions, eliciting concrete commitments (slower glasses, skipping Tuesdays, telling her son). Each change statement was reflected and built upon.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully used double-sided reflections and never argued with minimization, pivoting back to her own reasons. Some reflections restated the sustain side without consistently tilting toward change.
