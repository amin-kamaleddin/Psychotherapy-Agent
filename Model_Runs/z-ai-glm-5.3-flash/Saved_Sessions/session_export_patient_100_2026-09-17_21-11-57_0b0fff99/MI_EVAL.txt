# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard was communicated throughout, from opening affirmations to the non-judgmental closing ('it's information, not a grade'). The client explicitly expressed relief at not being lectured, confirming she experienced the non-judgmental stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing ambivalence, the party as the real test, and deeper stakes (Sundays, safety, mom's trust). Maya built on reflections rather than correcting them, indicating strong understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on alcohol use and moved purposefully from ambivalence to a specific commitment. Each turn advanced the target behavior without drift while remaining client-paced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit teamwork language ('let's figure it out together') and co-constructed problem-solving, with Maya supplying the ideas and the counselor structuring the process. Next steps were initiated and owned by the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change was framed as fully optional ('what would need to change, if anything') and evaluation pressure was explicitly removed at the close. The plan was entirely Maya's own, with an open door to report failure honestly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key element of change — reasons, strategies, timeline — came from Maya herself, with the counselor eliciting rather than supplying motivation. Her arc from 'I don't know if anything needs to change' to a firm commitment demonstrates masterful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned the client as the expert ('You know those situations better than anyone'), and the entire plan was generated by Maya. This reflects textbook power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully elicited Maya's own desire, ability, and commitment language ('that feels doable,' 'I'll text Dani tomorrow, I promise'), and the counselor amplified her progress before sealing a concrete commitment for Saturday.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections effectively balanced sustain talk with change talk, and doubts were validated then pivoted to collaborative problem-solving. Minor deductions for extended discussion of social pressure and a missed opportunity to soften Maya's nervousness about Saturday before finalizing the commitment.
