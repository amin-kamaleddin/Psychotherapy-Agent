# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **4/5**
**Comments:** Consistent respect and nonjudgment throughout. Early affirmation ("that's a really honest description") and later ("Nice work — that took courage"). No labeling or shaming of avoidance. Warmth somewhat formulaic; limited explicit normalization of setbacks keeps this from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **3/5**
**Comments:** Accurate understanding of the anxiety-avoidance cycle and good summary of the in-session exposure experience. Reflections sparse; frequent pivots to advice rather than reflecting feelings (embarrassment, self-criticism, "less hopeless" shift). Empathic effort adequate but inconsistent and shallow.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5/5**
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on avoidance behavior. Steadily advanced action agenda: tiny first step, in-session 2-minute practice, if-then reminder, timer label, tracking note. No drift; ended with defined follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **3/5**
**Comments:** Surface-level collaboration — preferences invited, wording tweakable — but interaction largely counselor-directed ("Quick things we can try," "Here's the exact text") with patient in a compliant role. Ideas not co-created; could have invited patient-generated steps and solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **4/5**
**Comments:** Menus offered and permission asked throughout ("Which feels more doable?", "Want me to wait?"). No coercion; patient's choices (option B, reminder #2) shaped the plan. Short of 5 because autonomy expressed mostly as choosing among counselor-generated options, with little affirmation of patient ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **2/5**
**Comments:** Rarely drew motivation from within. Session predominantly advice- and technique-driven. No open questions exploring patient's own reasons, values, or hopes; the volunteered confidence rating (4–5) was never explored. Change talk emerged from task success, not intentional evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **3/5**
**Comments:** Frequent choice-offering and incorporation of patient preferences, but counselor retained the expert role throughout — all ideas and plans originated from the counselor. Patient contributions incorporated reactively rather than shaping the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **3/5**
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk generated ("I know I can... even if my head keeps telling me I can't," "it feels a tiny bit less hopeless") via structured achievable tasks and doability questions. However, counselor rarely reflected or amplified this talk (no elaboration questions, no confidence scaling).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **3/5**
**Comments:** Considerable sustain talk voiced ("I still end up putting it off," "you'll do it today and then never again"). Neutral-to-adequate acknowledgment ("Totally understandable to feel skeptical") without amplifying. No double-sided reflections, reframing of doubt, or deliberate pivots to successes; redirection done mainly by moving to the next task.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **3.3/5**

**Summary:** Strong direction, structure, and nonjudgmental tone with effective scaffolding of a behavioral experiment and concrete follow-up plan. Areas for growth: evoke rather than prescribe (explore reasons, values, and the 4–5 confidence rating); deepen empathy with more emotionally attuned reflections; strategically soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections and affirmations; and share power more genuinely by inviting the patient to co-create steps and solutions.
