# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Mai's frustration and responsibility. However, warmth was somewhat procedural and task-focused, lacking deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections, but emotional nuances like feeling like 'just another number' and fear of lifelong medication were glossed over. The session was dominated by information delivery rather than exploring feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on statin understanding and adherence with no topic drift. Misses a 5 because focus was maintained through information-giving rather than MI-consistent guiding.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission before drafting and offered tweaks, but the counselor supplied nearly all content while Mai primarily received and approved it. Mai's expertise about her own routines was not drawn upon to co-create the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and menu options with no pressure or coercion, and supported Mai's decision to consult the pharmacist first. Autonomy support relied mainly on service menus rather than explicit affirmations of her ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Information and motivation flowed largely from the counselor through lecture-style education rather than being drawn from Mai. Her spontaneously expressed motivation ('preventing a heart attack beats dealing with one') was not explored or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered options and asked permission, but the counselor was positioned as the information expert delivering checklists and drafted materials. Mai's own expertise was not explicitly integrated into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged naturally from Mai but was driven by her own readiness, not deliberate MI technique. The counselor missed opportunities to reinforce, reflect, or deepen her commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai's doubts about lifelong medication were acknowledged neutrally but not skillfully managed. No double-sided reflections or strategic pivots toward her own change statements were used.
