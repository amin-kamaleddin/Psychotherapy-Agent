# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Jordan's exhaustion and normalizing fear of getting hopes up. However, affirmations were brief and formulaic rather than deeply individualized, with no explicit statement of unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate but thin, quickly pivoting to advice and practical options instead of exploring emotional weight. Core themes of failure, rejection, and shame were acknowledged superficially but never deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on employment/financial strain from start to finish, with crisp summaries and concrete commitments (alert tonight, agency Monday, 30-minute limit). The weakness is that direction was channeled almost entirely into task execution rather than the motivational dimension.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were genuine collaborative moments—offering choice and incorporating Jordan's work history—but much of the session was the counselor delivering advice packages with Jordan in a reactive role. Jordan's 'I'll try it your way' signals an uneven dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offered options rather than directives and respected Jordan's choices, including rejecting breathing exercises. Autonomy support was somewhat undermined by rapid-fire prescriptive instruction lists, and the plan felt more like the counselor's than Jordan's own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied primarily on providing information and instructions rather than drawing motivation from Jordan, with almost no open questions evoking reasons, confidence, or hope. The session reads more like brief practical case management than MI, confirmed by Jordan's 'I'll try it your way' framing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partnership elements existed (choice, readiness checks), but the counselor frequently slipped into an expert stance with option lists and ready-made scripts without eliciting Jordan's ideas first. Jordan's closing line reflects the power imbalance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan produced meaningful change talk, but the counselor rarely paused to reinforce or deepen it, responding with task plans instead of elaboration or commitment-strengthening questions. Momentum came largely from Jordan himself rather than skillful evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification and occasionally reframed, which is adequate. However, the counselor did not use double-sided reflections pairing doubt with Jordan's stated desire to change, leaving sustain talk to dissipate on its own.
