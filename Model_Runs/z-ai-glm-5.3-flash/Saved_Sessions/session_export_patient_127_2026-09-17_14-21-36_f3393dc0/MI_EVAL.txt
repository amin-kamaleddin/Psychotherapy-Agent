# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirms progress ('22 days is real progress'), softening suggestions when Timur pushes back. However, affirmations are somewhat generic and checklist-driven rather than deeply personalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is sparse; the counselor pivots quickly to advice and planning rather than reflecting Timur's statements. Core emotional material (comparison to mates, feeling 'unaccepted,' calling himself 'lazy') is never reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on reducing recidivism and building pro-social routines, with concrete commitments and effective follow-up on the walk. Focus occasionally narrows too much onto relapse-prevention mechanics at the expense of deeper motivational work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative moves exist (asking what Timur wants to focus on, menu choices), but the counselor frequently defaults to delivering plans and checklists before Timur has finished exploring. Plans are counselor-structured with Timur filling in details.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine autonomy-supportive moments (no pressure on the text, respecting refusal of scripts), but relentless prescribing ('Do these three quick things now') exerts pressure. Timur repeatedly has to assert autonomy himself; autonomy is permitted more than fostered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no open questions exploring Timur's reasons, values, or vision; questions are predominantly logistical. His richest motivational material (tea on the porch, being 'the bloke Fiona fell in love with') emerges unprompted, not from skillful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor honors Timur's expertise at key moments (e.g., not pushing scripts after 'Don't write it for me'). Still, the overall style is expert-driven with unsolicited checklists and directive instructions that shape the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Timur generates meaningful change talk ('that bloke's still in here somewhere,' 'once I truly decide, I stick to it'), but the counselor never reflects, deepens, or amplifies it. No evocative questions about his reasons or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids reinforcing sustain talk and steers toward action, but sustain talk ('I'm lazy,' 'people like me are unaccepted') is largely ignored rather than acknowledged and reframed. The two-week trial reframe is good but counselor-generated, with no double-sided reflections.
