# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout ('Both things are true,' 'It's your call, at your pace'), honoring the client's skepticism, traditions, and autonomy even in a high-risk scenario. No disapproval, labeling, or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, consistent reflective listening, including a double-sided reflection capturing both sustain and change talk ('That last line carries a lot of weight... And I hear something shifting too'). Reflections catch emotional nuance beneath surface content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior while guiding a logical arc: ambivalence → values → envisioning → obstacles → success criteria → coping strategies → follow-up. Secures a concrete commitment and accountability structure within a client-led frame.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operates between equals—plans are co-constructed ('yours and Marco's'), the check-in schedule and accountability questions are client-generated, and client solutions are built upon rather than replaced by expert recommendations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support with phrases like 'without anyone deciding for you' and 'You're the expert on your life'; information is provided only on request and framed as 'real information, not scare tactics.' Never pressures or persuades, fitting the client's autonomy-driven presentation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivational elements—the client's values, success definition, coping images, and self-accountability plan—come from her own words via open questions. The client's statement 'I just needed to get there myself' is direct evidence that evocation, not persuasion, drove the change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise within the client ('You're the expert on your life') with affirmations that follow genuine client contributions that shaped the session. The client confirms the power shift: 'I came in expecting to be told what to do... I just needed to get there myself.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions progressively evoke stronger change talk, moving the client from 'Maybe' to 'That feels doable' to 'It's the best thing I can do today, for the baby.' The counselor names and deepens change talk rather than moving on.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with sustain talk, instead holding both sides ('Both things are true') and reframing fear of change by decoupling cultural connection from alcohol. Sustain talk diminishes markedly as a result.
