# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with genuine affirmations of partial progress ('That's real progress,' 'That's a strong insight'). No judgment about the patient's smoking, drinking, or minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective understanding of fears and triggers (flare-ups while driving, breaktime ritual), but some reflections stay brief. The deeper all-or-nothing self-criticism is praised rather than deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus on reducing smoking in the cab with no topic drift. Every exchange advances the goal through confidence ratings, concrete steps, barriers, and success markers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed through iterative back-and-forth, with the patient's own ideas (trunk, seeds, wife's nudges) adopted and built upon. Menus of options replace directives, demonstrating genuine two-way problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced; the patient freely rejects suggestions (gum, patch, walks) without pressure. The counselor endorses his pacing and lets him define success markers and relapse recovery himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the patient via ruler questions and open prompts, eliciting his own reasons (tight chest, less coughing) and deep insight about the all-or-nothing pattern. No lecturing or arguing for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient is positioned as the expert, choosing and shaping every plan element while the counselor offers options and asks him to evaluate. His contributions substantially steer the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled, consistent evocation through open questions, confidence rulers, and commitment-specific prompts. The patient responds with strong change talk and rising confidence (5-6 to 7 to 8).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (dust is unfixable, brother's miserable quitting) is never reinforced and is redirected toward achievable steps. However, explicit double-sided reflections to honor ambivalence—especially around the brother comparison—could deepen this handling.
