# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance despite the client's anger and criticism, validating complaints without endorsing hostility. Deeper, more individualized affirmations of the client's character and efforts would be needed for a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflections captured both emotions and meaning (e.g., frustration, feeling disrespected, job pressure). Reflections occasionally stayed at content level, with less exploration of underlying vulnerabilities like fear of job loss or his wife's concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus, moving purposefully from venting to a concrete action plan (email, follow-up, reception script, breathing tool, follow-up appointment) within one session. Each exchange advanced toward action while respecting the client's pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner, soliciting input on recipients, examples, deadlines, and tone, and adjusting drafts accordingly. However, the counselor drafted emails and tools largely alone, making the client more of an approver/editor than a true co-author.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options rather than directives, let the client set the tone and pacing, and accepted his decision to defer the anger topic. The client's growing ownership ("I'll send it," "I'll report back. Deal?") reflects this well-supported autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently drew motivation from the client himself, eliciting his own solutions (early appointments, professional tone from his negotiation expertise) and reasons (protecting his job). Deeper elicitation of personal values and confidence would strengthen this further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned the client as the expert, building the plan from his own ideas and existing strategies. Slightly tipped toward clinician output by drafting substantial content himself rather than co-authoring more with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk, moving a precontemplative, angry client to concrete commitments ("Early slots and the email," "I'll send it"). More deliberate reinforcement and reflection of the change talk itself would deepen this.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not argue with the client's sustain talk or externalized blame, instead redirecting to client-chosen action while validating reasonable concerns. Missed opportunities included double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence explicitly.
