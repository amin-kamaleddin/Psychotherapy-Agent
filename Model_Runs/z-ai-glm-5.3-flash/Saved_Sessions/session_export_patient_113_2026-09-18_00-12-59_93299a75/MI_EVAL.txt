# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard despite blunt resistance ('I'm not here to pile on,' 'you don't owe me agreement'). Never judged the dismissiveness and validated the client's self-knowledge throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections consistently landed, capturing both content and emotional undertone (the 'gaps' concern, frustration with nagging). Client confirmed feeling understood ('nobody's asked me that before,' 'you didn't lecture me').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained steady focus on the charging behavior with no topic drift. Each exchange built progressively from barriers toward a concrete, client-generated plan including relapse planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — every substantive idea (dinner timing, visible charger, sticky note) came from the client. Counselor acted as a curious partner with no expert-driven stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly affirmed choice and pace ('you don't have to promise anything,' 'or would you rather just see how it goes?'). Falls short of 5 only due to fewer explicit statements reinforcing the client's ownership of the decision and outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never lectured; motivation was drawn from the client, who articulated his own reason for change (the data gaps) and solutions. Closing insight ('I actually heard myself') provides direct evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert ('You know your days better than anyone,' 'That's your expertise talking'). The entire plan direction was determined by the client's contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully highlighted pre-change language and used strategic questions to evoke motivation. Client progressed from 'I don't wanna' to 'I could maybe do some nights' plus concrete problem-solving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it, using double-sided reflections and 'you said you feel fine, and that matters. At the same time...' pivots. Even the 'whatever' mentality was reframed into relapse-prevention planning.
