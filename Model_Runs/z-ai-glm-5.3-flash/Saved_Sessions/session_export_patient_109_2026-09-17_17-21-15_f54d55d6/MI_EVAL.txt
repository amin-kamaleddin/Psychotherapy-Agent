# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'That's a smart, practical step' and 'showing up is the goal.' However, warmth feels somewhat formulaic, with generic praise rather than deep, individualized regard for Rafael's discouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections show decent understanding ('packing the bag cuts out the usual excuse') but are sparse relative to questions. The counselor rarely explores the emotional core of Rafael's ambivalence — his exhaustion, discouragement, and self-defeating self-talk — missing opportunities for deeper reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus on building a concrete exercise routine throughout. Every exchange advances the target, moving efficiently to a specific, measurable commitment (Tuesday, 20 minutes, 7pm text, bag in car) without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Inclusive language ('Which would help you most right now?') and Rafael's choices shape most of the plan. However, the pace and volume of counselor-supplied material (plans, texts, reminders) mean Rafael mostly accepts rather than co-creates.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offers choices and immediately honors Rafael's request to write his own text to his wife. But prescribed content (workout plan, reminder wording) and less reinforcement of Rafael's long-term self-direction slightly weaken autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early evocation is genuine — eliciting what fitness means to him and letting Rafael generate most plan elements. The final third becomes counselor-driven, with leading yes/no questions ('Sound good?') seeking assent rather than drawing out Rafael's own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rafael's contributions shape the plan (Tuesday, wife, bag in car), but the counselor drifts toward an expert stance by supplying the workout plan, text, if-then, and coping phrase. Solutions are offered before eliciting Rafael's own ideas, tilting expertise away from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation through values questions, small-change requests, confidence scaling, and linking the plan to Rafael's own values, yielding strong change talk ('that feels doable'). Slightly limited by repeated re-scaling rather than deepening desire and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids reinforcing sustain talk and pivots quickly toward solutions, such as moving from 'I can't get back in the gym' to past success. However, excuses like 'talking myself out of it' are acknowledged but not reframed, with little use of double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
