# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, including an honest confidentiality clarification that Marcus explicitly appreciated ('you didn't treat me like a little kid'). Warmth was sometimes brief and instrumental, quickly pivoting to practical steps rather than affirming Marcus's personal qualities more fully.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections consistently captured both the appeal of drinking and Marcus's ambivalence (e.g., fitting in vs. getting caught). Deeper emotional content, such as the implicit fear in 'dying in Diego's car is worse,' was acknowledged but not explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use and immediate risk reduction, yielding a concrete, feasible weekend plan. However, the target subtly shifted from alcohol behavior change to harm-reduction only, and Marcus's 'I'm still gonna party' went unexplored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely co-constructed, with Marcus selecting and adapting options into his own words (his 'chill' text version became the plan). At times the counselor produced dense, list-heavy, multi-question turns that tipped toward an expert/coaching cadence.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength — choices were explicitly offered at every step, confidentiality was framed around Marcus's control, and he was never pressured to quit. Marcus's response ('you didn't treat me like a little kid') confirms he experienced genuine choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine evocative moves were made (open questions, readiness and confidence rulers), but elicited motivation remained largely logistical. Opportunities to explore Marcus's values and personal stakes (e.g., why safety matters to him) were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly conveyed that Marcus's expertise mattered, asking what felt doable and inviting tweaks so plans fit his style. Deduction because early safety suggestions were counselor-generated and multiple options were supplied rapidly rather than drawing ideas from Marcus first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk via rulers and open questions, with modest commitments ('cash and Jay,' promising the text). Key moments of personal stake ('it'd be on me too,' 'wasn't as bad as I thought') were not deepened or amplified, as the counselor pivoted to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification or debate, and Marcus's 'I'm not quitting drinking' was strategically accepted with a pivot to keeping things social. Minor deduction because harm-reduction framing gave considerable airtime to reasons for maintaining the status quo.
