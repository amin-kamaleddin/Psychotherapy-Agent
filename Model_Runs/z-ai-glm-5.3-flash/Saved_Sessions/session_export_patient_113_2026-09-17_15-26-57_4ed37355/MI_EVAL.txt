# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental, calmly accepting Rowan's refusals and accusations without defensiveness. Affirmations were somewhat generic, lacking individualized affirmation of his strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate simple reflections showed decent perspective-taking, but rarely reached the deeper shame and fear ('admitting something's wrong with me'), staying mostly at surface content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session drifted from the health behavior (charging the device) into boundary scripts to deflect the sister, inadvertently coaching avoidance with no link back to change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor sought Rowan's preferences and tailored scripts, but the agenda was never jointly negotiated and content was largely counselor-supplied.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support: permission requested before every suggestion, options consistently offered, and Rowan's control repeatedly affirmed even when he accused the counselor of trickery.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely drew out Rowan's own motivations or reasons; questions were mostly closed, and his meaningful disclosure about what charging signifies was quickly bypassed in favor of offering scripts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing through asking permission and inviting input on wording, but the counselor retained the expert role, generating all ideas and strategies rather than exploring Rowan's own.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Small openings (agreeing to role-play, keeping scripts) were not deepened into change talk about the device itself; no open questions explored his motivations, confidence, or values around the target behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was reflected non-judgmentally without arguing, but no double-sided reflections or reframing were used, and the session inadvertently strengthened avoidance by coaching scripts to shut down those encouraging the behavior.
