# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful tone with frequent, specific affirmations targeting the patient's strengths and insight. However, praise occasionally feels effusive or formulaic, and there is little explicit normalization of her struggles with relocation or past quit-failure cycles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, capturing meaning beneath the patient's words and moving beyond content to underlying motivation. Depth is limited in places—the emotional weight of her self-criticism and shaken self-trust after the move is not explored, and some reflections merely restate her plan.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains crystal-clear focus on physical activity from start to finish, with nutrition explicitly deferred to the patient's own timeline. Each turn advances toward a concrete plan culminating in a well-defined commitment with a built-in follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative interaction with explicit partnership language like "let's make it concrete together." The patient's preferences directly shape the session's direction, and plan elements are co-developed rather than clinician-imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient decides the activity type, timing, duration, success criteria, and nutrition timeline, with no advice-giving or pressure. Her statement that the plan "feels like it's mine, not something handed to me" demonstrates the autonomy-supportive stance landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every strategic question draws out the patient's own reasons, values, and coping strategies, with no external motivation supplied by the clinician. Asking her to define success in her own words is a hallmark evocative technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer explicitly positions the patient as the expert on her own life, ceding decision-making power throughout. The walking plan, timing, and success criteria all originate from her, exemplifying power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout domain—the interviewer skillfully evokes and deepens change talk through open questions about fit, barriers, and self-defined success. The patient responds with strong commitment language ("I'm actually ready for this") that is consistently reflected and reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk around nutrition difficulty and relapse fear is handled well—validated without amplification and reframed compassionately ("grace for the missed days"). The score reflects the mild nature of the sustain talk, offering few opportunities to demonstrate advanced pivoting from significant resistance.
