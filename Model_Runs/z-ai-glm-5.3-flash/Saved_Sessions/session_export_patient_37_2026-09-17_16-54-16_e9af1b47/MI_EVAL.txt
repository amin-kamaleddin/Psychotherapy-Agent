# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard—validated Elena's desire for a nonjudgmental comparison, normalized her fears, and never judged her reliance on internet sources. Client explicitly confirmed feeling accepted rather than lectured.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captured core ambivalence and named her feelings throughout. Reflections were occasionally repetitive and deeper emotional exploration was sometimes bypassed in favor of delivering information.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on the vaccination decision throughout, with every turn tying back to clarifying concerns and building a concrete plan. Session progressed logically from ambivalence exploration to a clear commitment with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Elena's input on format and direction, and co-constructed the plan around her stated preferences. Data-delivery segments temporarily became more educator-driven with fewer collaborative check-ins.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered options, respected Elena's declining role-play, and presented information neutrally without pressure. Her spontaneous commitment demonstrated she arrived at her own decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early open questions elicited Elena's values, but the decisive shift came mainly from receiving statistics rather than from drawing out and deepening her own motivations. Little elaboration on change talk, and no confidence exploration or evocation strategies were used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Elena as the decision-maker, offering choices and tailoring resources to her needs, and she clearly experienced the session as partnership. Some information delivery was one-directional and her expertise was implied rather than explicitly affirmed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions successfully invited change talk ('I'm leaning toward wanting her protected,' 'Let's do it'). However, change talk was not deepened when it emerged—the counselor jumped to logistics rather than exploring what shifted or her confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement and effectively redirected via double-sided reflections and accurate information. The seizure-fear statement was answered with data rather than a deeper empathic reflection before problem-solving.
