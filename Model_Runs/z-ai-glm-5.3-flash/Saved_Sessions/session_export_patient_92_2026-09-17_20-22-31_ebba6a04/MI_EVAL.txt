# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent respect and non-judgment, immediately owning missteps ('Fair enough — that came out pushy') and offering genuine affirmations. Warmth occasionally felt strategic, with limited explicit validation of the difficulty of his social-pressure concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured Marek's defensiveness and ambivalence, and reflections on Sunday mornings and family health history landed accurately. Some inserted reframes rather than pure reflections, and his worry about friends' teasing was never explored emotionally in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on weekend drinking from opening reflection to final concrete plan, with each question building progressively. Even the social-pressure tangent was tied back to how change would fit with his drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently invited co-exploration, and Marek's own ideas shaped the plan. Power was uneven at moments — the counselor steered toward a trial before Marek signaled readiness, requiring Marek to reassert control.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: the counselor acknowledged the push, stated 'it's your life and your pace,' offered options without prescription, and resisted commitment escalation. The final plan was entirely client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from the client, who articulated his own reasons (father's health, 'can't argue with my own numbers') and generated his own strategies. Some evocation was leading, and the counselor voiced Marek's commitment language himself at the close rather than eliciting it fresh.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicit power-sharing was present ('You're the expert on your own life') and Marek's ideas shaped the session direction. A few questions were subtly leading, with the 'one weekend like Petr' trial originating from the counselor, and the counselor put words in Marek's mouth in the final exchange.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited change talk through questions about Sunday mornings, imagining change, and identifying what felt 'most doable,' producing client-generated commitments. Change talk was sometimes evoked leadingly, with the counselor voicing 'Maybe I try it this Saturday' on Marek's behalf.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally acknowledged without amplification, pivoting instead to what matters to the client. Early on, the counselor invited sustain talk by asking if the screening 'just missed the mark,' though partially redeemed by double-sided framing.
