# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed the patient's efforts as a parent and her self-designed plan without judgment. Affirmations felt genuine and specific (e.g., 'you're designing support on your own terms') rather than generic praise.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly demonstrated deep understanding by reflecting the emotional core of the patient's experience, such as evenings being her only downtime and the fear of losing her quiet moment. Reflections captured both surface content and underlying meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintained a clear focus on the patient's own change talk around cutting back, gently steering away from defensiveness and toward a concrete plan. Each exchange built momentum toward her self-generated goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The approach was fully collaborative, with the clinician positioning the patient as the expert on her own evenings ('you know your evenings better than anyone'). All ideas (tea, week trial, telling her sister) came from the patient, with the clinician supporting rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient explicitly stated 'it matters that it's my plan, my way' and 'you didn't push,' confirming strong autonomy support. The clinician emphasized the patient's ownership of every element of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully evoked the patient's own motivations, values (her son), and solutions through open questions like 'what tipped the scales' and 'what would tell you it went well.' The patient articulated her reasons for change in her own words throughout.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue functioned as a genuine partnership, with the clinician exploring and building on the patient's ideas rather than offering advice or a pre-set agenda. The tone was side-by-side problem-solving, evident in the co-development of the week-long trial.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently noticed, reflected, and deepened change talk ('maybe I could cut back — three instead of six'). This led to strong commitment language, including a concrete plan, a support person, and a 'deal' to report back honestly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ambivalence ('I'm not saying quit,' readiness of 5/10) was met with acceptance and reflection rather than confrontation, allowing sustain talk to soften naturally. The clinician reframed quitting forever into a manageable one-week trial, dissolving resistance without pressure.
