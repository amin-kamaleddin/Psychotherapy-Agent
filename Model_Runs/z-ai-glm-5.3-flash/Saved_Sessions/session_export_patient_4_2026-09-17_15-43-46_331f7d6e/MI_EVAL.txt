# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, but acceptance stayed shallow — no affirmations of the client's courage, symptom diary, or four years of feeling dismissed, and her fear of cancer was not normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One strong early reflection on frustration and fear, after which the counselor largely stopped reflecting. Emotionally significant material — fear of being labeled 'hysterical,' dread of 'another year of not knowing' — went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Focused and well-organized, but directed toward the client's biomedical agenda (referral advocacy) rather than the therapeutic target of the stress-symptom relationship, which emerged only accidentally.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client shaped and approved the final agreement and scripts, but the working mode was largely didactic — counselor-produced tools with client approval — more a consultant-client model than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear strength: choices were consistently offered, the psychologist referral was kept optional, and the care agreement awaited her approval, though options were counselor-constructed menus rather than open exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and insight came almost entirely unprompted from the client (the wedding observation, husband's absence, 'maybe I'd listen later') without a single open question designed to draw out her own reasons, confidence, or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role — supplying verbatim scripts, role-playing the doctor, and drafting the agreement unilaterally — with little inquiry into the client's own ideas about change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client spontaneously offered notable change talk ('I barely felt the pain at the wedding,' 'maybe I'd listen... later') that was never reflected, affirmed, or deepened — the session's biggest missed opportunity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never amplified and the counselor avoided arguing, but the session was organized around sustain-talk goals with missed chances for double-sided reflections linking her biomedical certainty to her own stress observations.
