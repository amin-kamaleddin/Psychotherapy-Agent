# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with a safety check and affirmations ('That was a big step—well done'). However, warmth is brief and quickly replaced by task-focused advice, making positive regard feel somewhat transactional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; the counselor uses rapid question batteries and advice lists instead of reflecting Naomi's disclosures. Her vulnerability about shame and fear of freezing up is largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on seeking medical evaluation, progressing logically from assessment to a booked appointment and role-play. The goal was largely counselor-chosen rather than collaboratively derived from Naomi's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative elements like offering menu choices and role-play at Naomi's request, but the power balance is uneven. Much 'collaboration' is limited to choosing among counselor-generated options rather than co-creating a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers genuine choices among options, but advice is dense and directive with complete scripts and medical workup details. Naomi's autonomy statements ('I can figure out the insurance text myself') go unaffirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor rarely elicits Naomi's own motivations or confidence, relying on closed informational questions. Solutions are provided rather than drawing out Naomi's capacity to find her own.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor frequently adopts an expert role, delivering unprompted lists of labs, medications, and verbatim scripts. Naomi's ideas do influence direction somewhat, keeping it above a 1, but expert-driven instruction dominates.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Naomi's change talk emerges from her own engagement with the concrete plan and is affirmed, but the counselor rarely deliberately evokes it. Change talk is harvested rather than cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Naomi expresses frequent sustain talk ('I might still chicken out', 'I don't totally believe it'll fix things') that the counselor largely ignores. Doubts are bypassed in favor of the next task rather than softened with double-sided reflections.
