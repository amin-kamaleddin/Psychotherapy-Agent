# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, normalizing Linda's ambivalence and affirming her honesty. However, the affirmations were somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized, and unconditional regard was not explicitly stated regardless of whether Linda changes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening was demonstrated, accurately capturing Linda's core ambivalence and responding well to her emotional peak. Reflections were sometimes brief or followed by multiple questions, and deeper emotional themes (fear of being a bad mom, her daughter's fear) were not fully explored before moving to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on the smoking behavior, moving logically from ambivalence exploration to a concrete first step and parent-child script. The advice-heavy option menu momentarily lost momentum, and concrete steps could have been more consistently tied to Linda's stated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and joint plan development were present, but the counselor delivered a dense, expert-style menu of five strategies and largely structured the session. Linda's contributions shaped the final plan but not the overall session architecture, keeping the interaction closer to counselor-guided than fully egalitarian.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently framed everything as optional and client-chosen, respected Linda's 'not ready to quit' stance, and did not pressure her. Some turns bundled many questions and options at once, subtly pressing toward action, and Linda's self-direction was not consistently affirmed in moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor drew out Linda's own reasons and plan elements through rulers and open questions, but relied heavily on providing content (option menus, scripts) rather than evoking motivation. Linda's powerful self-recognition ('it does feel like choosing') was not actively developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing occurred through choice-offering and inviting Linda's input, but the mid-session pivot to an expert-driven options menu shifted away from partnership. Linda's expertise about her own routines was underutilized and not explicitly built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Standard MI tools (rulers, open questions) were used and Linda produced some change talk, but the counselor did not strategically deepen it. When Linda offered emergent self-recognition, the counselor pivoted to logistics rather than evoking and amplifying it, with no commitment-strengthening questions used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not reinforce sustain talk and acknowledged ambivalence neutrally, but Linda's sustain talk was mostly met with acceptance plus advice-giving. No double-sided reflections were used to pair sustain talk with her change talk, and opportunities to pivot toward change language were missed.
