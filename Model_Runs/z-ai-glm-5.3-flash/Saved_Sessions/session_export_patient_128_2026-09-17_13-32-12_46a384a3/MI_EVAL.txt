# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with respectful responses even when Tyler deflected or minimized. However, affirmations were somewhat generic and the rapid pivot to fixing the situation may have subtly undermined unconditional regard for Tyler's own pace.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial reflections were accurate, but the session quickly shifted to advice-giving. Deep emotional content—shame, fear of parental notification, identity concerns about friendships—was met with practical solutions rather than reflective understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed connected to the alcohol issue without aimless wandering. However, focus shifted toward hearing logistics and damage control rather than sustained exploration of the drinking behavior itself and its conflict with Tyler's goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly supplied material for Tyler to adopt, positioning him as a consumer of expertise rather than a co-creating partner. Tyler's own ideas, such as Marcus keeping him in check, were never developed collaboratively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered some menu choices and respected Tyler's deflection of the grounding exercise. However, prescribing a full statement and step-by-step plan meant the overall structure was answer-provision rather than reinforcing Tyler's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was assumed rather than elicited—the counselor never asked about Tyler's own reasons for changing despite his volunteering rich material. Tyler rehearsed counselor-generated change language rather than generating his own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor largely assumed the expert role: drafting the statement, listing outcomes, supplying scripted answers, and prescribing the plan. Tyler's contributions were incorporated only erratically, with minimal exploration of his own knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Tyler generated genuine change talk ('Maybe I do have to own some of it,' 'setting a drink limit, that I could actually do') but the counselor rarely recognized or reinforced it. No evocative questions were used, and pre-written statements supplied the change language for him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor didn't reinforce sustain talk but also didn't actively work with it, either ignoring it or jumping to solutions. No double-sided reflections were used to pair sustain talk with Tyler's stated values, missing key opportunities to resolve ambivalence.
