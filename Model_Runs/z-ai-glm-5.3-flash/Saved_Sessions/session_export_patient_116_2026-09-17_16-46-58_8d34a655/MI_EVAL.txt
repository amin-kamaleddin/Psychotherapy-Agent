# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, acknowledging Samir's frustration without criticism. Some affirmations felt formulaic ('Good choice', 'Nice job') and lacked deeper validation of his emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong reflective listening, accurately capturing Samir's core concern that pills feel like a 'daily label' and recognizing his humor-deflecting style. Did not explore the emotional weight beneath the deflection (what 'something's wrong with me' means to him)." 

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained exceptionally clear focus on medication adherence from the opening line, with every exchange tied to the target behavior. Gently but persistently steered past deflection and humor to produce a specific, actionable plan (Thursday, 10 AM medication review call)."

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-created the plan from Samir's own preferences and invited his input on next steps. Frequent rapid-fire offering of counselor-generated options (scripts, role-play, alarms, voicemail templates) occasionally shifted toward solving problems for him rather than with him.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Samir's ownership with explicit acceptance of his non-commitment and framing the final plan entirely as his choice. Options were presented as menus, and his rejections (pillbox, coffee) were honored without pushback.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effort was made to draw out Samir's perspective, but motivation was largely generated through counselor-supplied menus rather than evoked from within. No open questions about his values, health hopes, or confidence, leaving evocation superficial.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated Samir's contributions as impactful, with his rejections directly shaping the plan and power-sharing language present ('on your terms', 'we can pick one step together'). Drove most of the agenda with pre-packaged options and occasionally stacked multiple suggestions at once.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked gradual change talk, moving Samir from resistance to 'maybe simplifying them' and a concrete commitment (Thursday 10 AM call). Change talk was largely elicited via menu options rather than drawn from Samir's own motivations and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initially handled resistance well by reflecting it rather than arguing, but then pivoted rapidly to solutions before fully exploring the sustain talk. Ambivalence ('I'm not promising anything') was acknowledged but never strategically resolved or reframed.
