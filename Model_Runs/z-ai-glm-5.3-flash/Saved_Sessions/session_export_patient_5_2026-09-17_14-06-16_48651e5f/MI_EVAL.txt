# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout the session. Falls short of a 5 due to few individualized affirmations of Alessia's strengths, such as her proactive safety-checking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Responses are predominantly informational blocks with almost no reflections of the client's feelings or concerns. Brief acknowledgments like 'Good question' are generic rather than demonstrative of understanding her perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on safe medication management and the washout plan without wandering. It is functional but information-focused rather than change-process-focused, keeping it from a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely a one-way, expert-to-layperson transfer of information, with the counselor supplying all knowledge, drafts, and next steps. The client's good ideas (check-ins, husband's support) are not built upon collaboratively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers rather than imposes, and the client's decisions remain her own. However, directive instructions dominate without explicit affirmations of her ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is virtually no evocation — the counselor never asks about the client's motivations, values, or confidence. All content is supplied rather than drawn from the client, missing obvious opportunities like 'How confident are you in this plan?'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes an expert role, generating all content and templates with little solicitation of the client's own ideas. Her contributions are acknowledged but do not shape the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk (e.g., 'I'll hold off entirely and contact my doctor') emerges from the client's own initiative, not counselor evocation. Commitment statements go largely unnoticed rather than reinforced or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was minimal sustain talk to address, and normalizing expected withdrawal symptoms was appropriate. However, mild worries were not actively pivoted toward the client's own strengths, resulting in neutral handling.
