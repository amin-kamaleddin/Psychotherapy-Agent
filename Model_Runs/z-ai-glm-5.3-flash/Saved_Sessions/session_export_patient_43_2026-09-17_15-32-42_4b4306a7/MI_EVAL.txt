# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with repeated validation of Eric's honesty. Warmth occasionally felt formulaic, and his defensiveness about his wife's pressure was acknowledged but not deeply normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, generally accurate summaries and reflections captured Eric's perspective well. Empathy was more cognitive than emotional—feelings beneath his minimization were rarely explored, and stacked questions reduced space for deeper understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking from start to finish with no topic drift. Every exchange moved progressively toward a highly specific, concrete change plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Real collaboration in refining plan details (Eric chose delaying, set 30 minutes, Monday start, alarm). The overall structure and tools were largely counselor-generated, with Eric reacting to a pre-shaped agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong reinforcement of ownership: permission-seeking before advice, options as choices, and explicit respect for Eric's non-commitment ('not quitting quitting'). Advice was consistently gated and Eric's doubts honored without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited, but evocation stayed superficial—the importance rating of 3 was never explored ('why a 3 and not a 2?'). Session momentum came largely from counselor structure rather than Eric's intrinsic motivations, a key gap for a precontemplative client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good permission-seeking and choice-offering, but the session became increasingly counselor-driven (delay tactic, duration, tracking, check-in). Eric's own expertise shaped the plan less than it could.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of rulers and open questions elicited genuine change language ('I could try it and see if the cough changes'). Missed opportunity to deepen the low importance rating before moving quickly to action planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was reflected neutrally rather than reinforced, but there was little strategic softening. No double-sided reflections, and summaries echoing sustain talk weren't pivoted toward Eric's emerging concerns (the 'ten years' comment, year-long cough).
