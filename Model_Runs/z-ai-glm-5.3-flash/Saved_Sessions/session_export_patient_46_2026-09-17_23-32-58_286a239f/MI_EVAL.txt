# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with no judgment, even when Ethan was defensive or embarrassed. Explicit validation ('I respect that honesty,' 'zero shame in that') and a judgment-free close reinforce acceptance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently captured both content and feeling (e.g., the connection/stress ambivalence, the hangover and grounding distress) and landed so well that Ethan built on them with his own insights. Strong evidence of accurate, generative reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every psychiatrist turn advanced the alcohol-use target — from social role of drinking, to consequences, to peer pressure, to a concrete harm-reduction plan and follow-up. Focus stayed unwavering without becoming coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative: Ethan generated all plan elements while the clinician reflected, affirmed, and invited ('figuring things out together'). The client explicitly recognized he 'figured out my own stuff,' confirming genuine power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly reinforced Ethan's ownership ('I'm not here to tell you to stop,' 'That plan came entirely from you') and let him define success on his own terms. No persuasive or controlling language anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was systematically drawn from Ethan himself — dislike of hangovers, the Jake realization, the cousin memory — rather than supplied by the clinician. Each question built on his prior statements, deepening change talk into a self-made plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located in the client ('You know your friends and these situations better than anyone') while the clinician avoided the expert role. The resulting plan was entirely Ethan's own — the hallmark of high partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation from the opening question onward, with Ethan's change talk escalating ('I could drink something else' → 'I can actually do that'). Each instance was reinforced with affirmations and reflections, culminating in a concrete self-authored plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was effectively redirected and even converted into productive insight (fear of mockery vs. Jake's experience). Minor growth area: some sustain talk was explored at moderate length before pivoting, without always explicitly affirming the legitimacy of Ethan's attachments to drinking.
