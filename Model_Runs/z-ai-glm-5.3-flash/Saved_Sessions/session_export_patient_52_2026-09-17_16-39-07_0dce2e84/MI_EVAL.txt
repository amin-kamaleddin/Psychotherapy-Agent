# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing her exhaustion and explicitly honoring her request to avoid a lecture. Affirmations were sparse and somewhat formulaic, with limited affirmation of her strengths and courage in voicing guilt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured her situation and validated both sides of her ambivalence, including her guilt and her 'off switch' need. Reflections sometimes transitioned too quickly into menus and questions, leaving emotional material (fear of her mother's path, guilt) under-explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use as stress coping from the opening and converged on a concrete plan with scheduled follow-up accountability. Discussion of exhaustion and childcare briefly expanded without explicitly tying each thread back to the alcohol goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Agendas were negotiated, the plan was co-constructed from her own successful experience, and the childcare email was drafted together—she noted the plan 'feels more like mine.' Option menus retained some expert-driven framing, and she explicitly asked 'What have you got?' rather than being evoked for her own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supported her ownership: 'pick one to try,' 'you can tweak it anytime,' and honoring her rejection of whole-night abstinence by adjusting the plan. She was never pressured toward a prescribed outcome and the plan was explicitly framed as hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence scaling question and the closing reflection on how skipping the second glass felt were effective evocative moves. However, her reasons for change were never deliberately explored, the confidence rating was not followed up, and most content was supplied via menus rather than drawn from within her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Irina as the decision-maker ('Which of these feels doable this week?') and closed by reinforcing that the plan is hers. Pre-packaged option menus positioned the counselor as idea-generator, and her own expertise (the neighbor walk) was not built upon until late.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used a confidence scale and menu of options to generate meaningful change talk ('If I tried one instead of two, that I could probably manage'). Missed key chances to deepen it—her confidence rating of six was never explored, and her change talk ('half my stress is gone') went unreflected and unaffirmed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce her sustain talk and occasionally used double-sided reflections pairing her needs with her concerns. Redirection was inconsistent—some menu options directly invited further sustain talk, and reframes pivoted passively rather than strategically toward her son-focused motivations.
