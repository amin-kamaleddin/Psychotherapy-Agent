# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Viktor's feeling of being singled out and never challenging his denial directly. Warmth is somewhat formulaic ('I appreciate your honesty' repeated) and lacks individualized affirmation of strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Viktor's ambivalence and both sides of his struggle (valuing weekends vs. noticing real costs). Reflections are occasionally mechanical, and deeper emotional content like his feeling of being pressured or controlled is missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on use and relational consequences, progressing logically from ambivalence exploration to a concrete two-week behavioral trial. Pacing briefly outstripped Viktor's readiness (his 'Whoa, slow down' reaction) and suggestion-heavy moments pulled focus from his own cues.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative — asks permission, offers choices at each decision point, and co-constructs a plan built on Viktor's own ideas and preferred 'low-admission' phrasing. Occasionally slipped into advisory mode with dense preset option menus rather than drawing out his solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with consistent no-pressure language, immediate backing off when Viktor rejected change suggestions, honest refusal to promise outcomes, and allowing Viktor to frame the commitment on his own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Relies more on structured exercises and menu-driven framing than on open questions eliciting Viktor's own motivations and values. His key motivation — peace at home to end the pressure — was stated but never developed with depth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions Viktor as the decision-maker, with his preferences genuinely shaping the session agenda and plan. Slightly below 5 because preset option menus were offered rather than primarily drawing solutions from Viktor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicited some change talk ('I'd like us to stop arguing so much') and drew out costs through the pros/cons exercise. These statements were never deepened with elaboration, importance/confidence questions, or reinforcing reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids amplifying sustain talk, using neutral double-sided reflections pairing sustain statements with change-relevant observations and steering to experiments Viktor controls. Missed a strategic reframe when Viktor insisted 'doesn't mean I agree there's a problem,' and early on briefly kept focus on the wife's concerns.
