# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with no labeling or disapproval. However, affirmations leaned formulaic ('I love this/that') and were repeated frequently, reducing their individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening that consistently incorporated Leila's own words ('me time,' trial pass). Reflections occasionally slid into problem-solving, and deeper emotional layers (embarrassment, 'I freeze up') went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on re-establishing exercise from the opening line through the close, with no topic drift. Every exchange advanced concrete action: two committed goals, a scheduled check-in, a rehearsed script, and a route plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session felt largely joint: the counselor asked before suggesting, let Leila select options, and co-constructed the plan. Some practical planning (alarms, water bottles, loop structure) was delivered as counselor-generated packages rather than built from Leila's own problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice ('Which feels most realistic to try this week?') and honored Leila's decisions. Advice lists, though offered with permission-seeking framing, front-loaded counselor solutions before Leila's own ideas were exhausted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evoked motivations early and used a confidence ruler, but much of Leila's change talk flowed from her own readiness rather than skillful evocation. The counselor shifted toward logistics rather than developing the meaning behind 'strength,' 'energy,' and 'me time.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned expertise within Leila at times, but slid into an advice-heavy expert stance with unprompted tip lists and a turn-by-turn route plan. Leila's contributions shaped the plan, but the overall balance tilted toward counselor-as-advisor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively invited planning, commitment, and confidence scaling, and Leila generated abundant change talk. Missed strategic follow-ups (e.g., elaboration after 'I'm confident I can do it'), and some momentum came from counselor suggestions rather than evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept barrier discussion proportionate and solution-focused, converting obstacles (weather, sales pressure) into contingency plans. Minor deduction for counselor-initiated barrier questions that invited sustain-adjacent talk rather than framing around strengths and past successes.
