# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations of David's attempts and confidence growth. However, affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic, with little explicit normalization of past failed quit attempts or his fear of snapping at his wife.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections early on and a decent empathic acknowledgment of the role-play feelings, but the psychiatrist frequently skips emotional content in favor of tips and logistics. David's deeper worries about work stress and the hovering wife scenario go unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on smoking cessation throughout, with every exchange advancing the goal — from importance questions through medication selection, quit date, rehearsal, and concrete next steps. Loops are explicitly closed and momentum is maintained toward the agreed quit date.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are genuine collaborative elements like offering to make a plan together and the role-play check-ins, but the power balance is uneven, with the psychiatrist supplying nearly all strategies and scripts. David mostly selects among pre-generated options rather than co-creating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David's choices are acknowledged at key decision points, but the communication style is heavily prescriptive with long unsolicited advice blocks and pre-packaged scripts. There's little explicit emphasis that David owns the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited at key moments, including the confidence ruler, which produced David's spontaneous confidence increase. However, the default mode is supplying information and advice rather than consistently drawing out David's own reasons, ideas, and capacity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asks for David's input on medication and strategy choice, showing some power sharing. Yet the overall stance is expert-driven, with David cast primarily as a recipient of strategies rather than a source of expertise about his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Active evocation through the importance question, confidence ruler, and the 'what would raise your confidence' question, which directly produced change talk. Held at 4 because the change talk is rarely deepened with follow-up reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David's sustain talk about stress and past caving is acknowledged but not strategically softened. No double-sided reflections pairing his doubts with his motivation, and responses jump to practical tips rather than helping resolve ambivalence.
