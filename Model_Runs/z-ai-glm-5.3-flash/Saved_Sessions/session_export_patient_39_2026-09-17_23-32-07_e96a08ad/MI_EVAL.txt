# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with plentiful affirmations and validation of Elias's discouragement and pride. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and recycled (e.g., 'You know your body better than anyone' repeated three times), preventing fully individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several accurate complex reflections, including a strong reflection of helplessness and wounded pride about the chair. Empathy was undercut by near-verbatim repetition of the opening reflection, and the emotional sting of feeling foolish was acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The target behavior stayed in focus and progressed to a concrete commitment, but the session stalled mid-way with re-asked questions, prompting Elias to note 'We've circled this pretty good already.' The close restated the opening reflection rather than summarizing and consolidating the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions were co-constructed and questions consistently invited Elias's perspective with no directives from the clinician. Collaboration was somewhat passive, though — the clinician followed a template rather than actively weaving Elias's ideas into a shared, negotiated plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support: no pressure or prescriptions, 'no promises' tolerated, and Elias's right to decline explicitly honored. Elias visibly internalized this, stating 'It's my choice, this. Nobody's shoving me back there.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within — Elias articulated his own reasons, solutions, and insights, including the strong evocative question about protecting himself vs. belonging again. Evocation circled the same themes rather than deepening into values or explicitly exploring confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elias was repeatedly positioned as the expert, and all ideas (chair exercises, phone call, written questions, wife's involvement) originated from and were credited to him. The clinician's fixed agenda and the circling limited fuller dynamic power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and reinforced, mobilizing toward a concrete commitment (calling tomorrow with his wife present). Limited question formats and no confidence scaling or deeper value elaboration kept it from a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification through double-sided reflections pairing it with change talk. The redirection strategy was repetitive, and moments like 'No promises' were passed over rather than gently reframed.
