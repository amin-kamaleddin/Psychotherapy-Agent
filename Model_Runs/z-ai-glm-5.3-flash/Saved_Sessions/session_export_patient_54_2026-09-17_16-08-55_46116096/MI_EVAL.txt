# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental stance despite the patient's initial defiance. Explicitly offered "nonjudgmental" information, validated the patient's independence ("You're right to want straight facts and to decide for yourself"), and honored family tradition rather than criticizing it. No labeling or lecturing. Praise was competent but slightly formulaic ("That's a strong, doable plan"), limiting personalization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening — captured the core ambivalence (family ritual/independence vs. the "what if" about responsibility) and the identity-vs-safety tension. Reflections were accurate but occasionally functional rather than emotionally deep; the frightening "what if something went wrong because of me" moment received only brief acknowledgment rather than fuller exploration of guilt or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on drinking during pregnancy from open to close, with each turn advancing toward a concrete plan: evidence → options → scripts → checklist → follow-up. No drift. Only limitation: occasionally jumped to offering tools before exploring the patient's own ideas in depth, slightly compressing patient-led exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-way session: menu-based options, patient preference solicited at each step ("Which of those would you like me to draft first?"), and plan content shaped by the patient (coworker scripts, "driving" line, dessert rewards). Patient explicitly noted the plan "felt like my idea."

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength. Repeatedly deferred choice: "I'll support whatever you choose," "which would you prefer?" Patient's closing statement — "It never felt like someone taking my choices away... it felt like me deciding" — is direct evidence the stance landed. Only minor gap: occasional slightly directive tips (e.g., "put sparkling where the wine is"), though framed as optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited self-generated motivation ("having a healthy baby... that's bigger than a glass of wine"; "I'm the one in charge here"). The evocative stance (experiment framing, honoring tradition) drew motivation from within rather than lecturing. Relied more on supplying options/materials than systematically developing the patient's reasons; no exploration of confidence or what success would look like.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated the patient as the expert on her own life — her cultural knowledge shaped the non-alcoholic wine and Nonna-toast strategies; her work context shaped social scripts; her preference shaped follow-up. Power-sharing evident throughout. Fell short of 5 because the clinician supplied most concrete tools (scripts, checklist) rather than first drawing out the patient's own problem-solving ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited commitment language ("that's probably the best thing I can do today," "one month? Yeah, I can do that," "it has to be my decision") via "What feels most doable to you?" and the low-pressure one-month experiment. However, change talk was rarely reflected or deepened when it emerged (e.g., after "that's bigger than a glass of wine"), and no explicit confidence/ability questions were asked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and never amplified sustain talk; evidence discussion was factual, preventing the confrontation the patient dreaded. But sustain talk was only briefly acknowledged ("balancing identity and safety") before pivoting to solutions — missed chances for double-sided reflections or exploring the meaning of the family tradition. Sustain talk was neutralized rather than skillfully transformed.

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 4.2/5**
