# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout; humor and self-criticism are normalized rather than corrected. Affirmations, while frequent, are somewhat formulaic and occasionally lean toward praise rather than deeply individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening captures ambivalence and emerging meaning. Exploration occasionally stays at the content level, with deeper emotions like guilt and fear acknowledged but not fully probed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on one target behavior with steady progression from meaning to commitment to planning. Falls short of a 5 due to the lack of explicit agenda-setting, summarizing, or negotiated next steps at the close.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving with the tone of equals working on a shared puzzle; the patient's ideas drive the plan. However, the counselor retains control of every transition, so conversational handoffs aren't fully balanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice and ownership are repeatedly reinforced and no content is ever prescribed—all strategies are the patient's own. Could be deepened by affirming ownership of the outcome to protect against all-or-nothing self-judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation: every major insight is self-generated, with no advice or persuasion offered at any point. The patient's motivation is fully mobilized by session's end, moving from ambivalence to concrete commitment and rehearsal.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Powerful partnership statements explicitly locate expertise in the patient, whose contributions genuinely shape the working plan. Power-sharing is slightly counselor-steered in the pacing of questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The standout strength of the session: open questions reliably evoke commitment language, which is strategically reflected back. Having the patient rehearse the conversation aloud produced a pivotal readiness moment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged with skillful double-sided reflections and consistently pivoted toward the patient's own resolve. A 5 would require more active reframing of guilt as a signal of his values rather than a threat.
