# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard from the opening line disarmed Erik's expectation of a lecture, with zero judgment despite his minimization and defensiveness. Repeated affirmations ('That's real change talk, Erik, and it's yours') left Erik explicitly noting he wasn't told off.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and layered, capturing deep meaning such as the pints representing 'mates and belonging,' which Erik explicitly confirmed. The clinician validated his social fears and honored ambivalence without pathologizing it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained steady focus on alcohol, diet, and diabetes from start to finish, progressing logically to a concrete plan and a two-week follow-up. There was no drift; every exchange moved the agenda forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operated between equal partners, with Erik's ideas (driving, pacing pints, enlisting his wife) forming the entire plan. Even the follow-up was framed collaboratively, and information was offered only when Erik directly asked.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently reinforced client ownership and refrained from prescribing despite Erik's repeated requests for direction. Erik left feeling fully in control, even asserting boundaries with his wife ('it's my thing, not hers to manage').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every element of the plan originated from Erik, elicited rather than supplied by the clinician. His transformation from expecting a row to leaving with a self-generated plan demonstrates successful internal elicitation of motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Erik was consistently positioned as the expert ('you know your routine better than I do'), and the clinician skillfully deflected the expert role each time Erik asked to be told what to do. Erik internalized this: 'you made me do the thinking.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was systematically evoked, named, and amplified, progressing Erik from 'maybe' to a concrete commitment and self-affirmation ('I'm leaving with a plan'). The clinician explicitly credited Erik's own problem-solving as his own.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged but never reinforced, using reflective pivots toward Erik's own expressed desire ('and yet something in you said I'd like to not end up worse off'). His resistance visibly decreased as change talk took over.
