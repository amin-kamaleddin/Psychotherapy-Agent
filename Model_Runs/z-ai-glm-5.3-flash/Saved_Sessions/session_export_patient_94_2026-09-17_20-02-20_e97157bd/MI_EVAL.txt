# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard, even validating Maria's decision to decline the daily inhaler. Individualized warmth using her research and stubbornness as strengths, never judgmental.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, emotionally attuned reflections throughout, including a deep reflection on the frightening night and a double-sided reflection holding her ambivalence. Nuanced understanding of her relational history with providers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on asthma management and inhaler adherence with no topic drift, moving progressively from pre-contemplation toward commitment language. Closing summary tied the arc together purposefully.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership of equals, explicitly named and enacted — asking rather than telling and co-constructing the rehearsal. Maria's own conditions and ideas became the entire action plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit reinforcement of Maria's ownership of every decision, embracing her conditions rather than negotiating them away. Advice framed as optional, with the plan entirely hers and the clinician as consultant.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from within Maria — her family, self-advocacy, and her own solutions — rather than arguing or lecturing. Her closing recognition that the session surfaced her own questions and trial idea reflects evocation at its best.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise repeatedly located within Maria, and her contributions demonstrably shaped the session direction and action plan. Role-play offered as a tool at her service, exemplifying power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic open questions moved Maria from pre-contemplation to elaborated change talk and self-authored commitment ('try it for a month or two... if I notice problems, I stop'). Reinforced tentative change talk by slowing down and consolidating with a closing commitment question.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk and reframed resistance as self-advocacy, with effective redirects. Occasionally gave the sustain position full voice (validating her 'no daily steroid' stance), which was respectful but time-costly.
