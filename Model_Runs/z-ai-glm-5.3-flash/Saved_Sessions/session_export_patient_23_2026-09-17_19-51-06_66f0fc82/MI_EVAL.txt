# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicates unconditional positive regard with no judgment, even when Claudia admits to heavier drinking. Claudia explicitly notes feeling safe: 'I don't have to defend myself here.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and consistent, e.g., capturing the wind-down ritual's emotional role and synthesizing her ambivalence (ritual AND feeling less rotten). Reflections build on each other and deepen understanding rather than repeat.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear focus on reducing alcohol, guiding progressively from evoking motivation to concrete planning and a two-week follow-up. Each turn advances the target behavior without drift, with a final summary recapping the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operates between equals — the counselor asks rather than tells, and Claudia's own insights (bottle trigger, chamomile, notes) shape the plan. The closing 'we'll figure out the next step together' encapsulates the collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces Claudia's ownership ('This is your plan, your pace') and offers choices rather than imposing advice. All strategies originate from Claudia, and perfection is explicitly not required.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is effectively drawn from within, with Claudia's own solutions and intrinsic reasons surfacing and being reflected back. However, importance and confidence were never explicitly explored (e.g., scaling questions), making evocation strong but not maximally systematic.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is clearly positioned in the client — 'You know your life and what you need better than anyone' — and insights are repeatedly credited to Claudia. The plan is co-constructed from her observations rather than prescribed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The opening question immediately elicits change talk ('two a night feels doable'), and Claudia generates her own plan, which the counselor affirms as 'your design.' Deeper commitment language or confidence scaling would have strengthened this further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids amplifying reasons against change, pivoting to practical triggers and validating her modest goal without pushing stricter guidelines. More explicit double-sided reflections could have resolved residual ambivalence about low-mood drinking.
