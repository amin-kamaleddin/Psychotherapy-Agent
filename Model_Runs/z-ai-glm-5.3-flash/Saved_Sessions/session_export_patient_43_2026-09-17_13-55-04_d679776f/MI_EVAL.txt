# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respect maintained without judgment, but warmth is minimal and formulaic. No personalized affirmations of Eric's honesty or willingness despite skepticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections early on, but empathy is superficial. Eric's fear of failure and need to decompress are never explored in depth—responses quickly pivot to advice lists.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on smoking with a concrete target behavior and follow-up plan. Direction is clear but achieved through directing rather than collaborative guiding.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor proposes, lists, and prescribes while Eric modifies mainly through resistance. No joint agenda-setting before jumping to a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine autonomy framing (reversible, 'data not pass/fail'), but repeated pushes of declined options (checklists, reminders) after Eric says no.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is supplied by the counselor rather than drawn from Eric; no evocative open questions. His own reasons surface incidentally, not through skillful inquiry.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Expert role assumed throughout—rules prescribed, checklist pushed despite refusal. Eric's contributions shape the plan only after pushback.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk comes mostly from Eric himself, not evocation. The readiness ruler answer of 2 was never explored—a classic missed opportunity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with counter-argument and information delivery rather than double-sided reflections. Benefit lectures risk reinforcing defensiveness in a pre-contemplative client.
