# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard from the opening line ('you're not in trouble here') with consistent warmth, affirmation, and zero judgment even when the client denied a problem.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured her ambivalence and identity, but some reflections favored affirmation over deeper exploration of emotions like fear or grief tied to her cousin's story.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear progressive structure moving from denial to a self-generated action step, though the target behavior was addressed somewhat indirectly via values and future planning rather than named goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully egalitarian interaction—client's answers demonstrably shaped each step, and she explicitly experienced the session as a genuine two-way conversation ('Thanks for not just reading the questionnaire at me').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy repeatedly reinforced ('What matters most is that it's yours'); no advice or pressure, and every plan element originated from the client with ownership confirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful evocation—the counselor supplied no reasons to change; all risk markers, motivations, and the concrete plan came from the client, who moved from precontemplation to commitment in one session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent power-sharing framing the client as the expert ('Those came from you, not me'), with her contributions visibly steering the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of client-generated change talk culminating in a concrete, self-authored plan (phone note, specific memory, friend check-in) deepened by follow-up questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided arguing with sustain talk, transforming 'That's not me' into identity-based change talk; one double-sided reflection briefly restated sustain talk, though risk was well mitigated.
