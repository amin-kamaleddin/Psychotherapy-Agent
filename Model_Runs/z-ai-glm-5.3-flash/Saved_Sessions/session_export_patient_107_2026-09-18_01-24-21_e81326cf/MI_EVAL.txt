# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, owning the premature push ('that's on me') without defensiveness and normalizing the client's ambivalence. Affirmations are warm and specific, locating motivation within the client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including complex reflections capturing both sides of ambivalence and the deeper emotional layer around his father. Reflections genuinely advanced self-understanding, as the client himself noted.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on the coffee/tea experiment, with every response advancing toward change. Gently redirects defensiveness and excuses with targeted questions that keep momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functions between equal partners throughout; the client's ideas (tea, Thursday, calendar, accountability) drive the session while the counselor facilitates. The final agreement is explicitly co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced ('no promises — that's completely yours to decide'), and the counselor explicitly apologized after getting ahead of the client's readiness. Even the accountability arrangement is client-designed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully draws motivation from within, evoking the client's own criteria and surfacing the powerful father/blood-pressure motivator he'd never voiced. The client's closing reflection — expecting to defend his habit but leaving with a plan — is direct evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively fosters power sharing, with the client's contributions visibly shaping every element of the plan. Explicitly affirms client ownership ('it came entirely from you') and rebalances power after the early push.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and develops change talk, progressing the client from 'maybe next week sometime' to a specific self-authored plan with built-in accountability. Selectively reflects commitment language and connects it to deeper 'why' motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally handles sustain talk well with double-sided reflections, but occasionally reflects it at length without a strategic pivot. Excuses like the slammed week are acknowledged but not consistently redirected toward change.
