# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and nonjudgmental regard, including validating David's courage in seeking help and rehearsing the refusal line. Affirmations remain somewhat surface-level and could more explicitly honor David's own strengths and past quit efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but stay at the content level, missing key emotional cues like David's shame after the restaurant relapse, his fear of mood effects, and his anxiety about saying the quit line aloud. These moments were met with information or tasks rather than deeper emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on smoking cessation, with medication, quit timing, social triggers, and refusal skills all connected to the target behavior. The medication discussion briefly shifts into didactic territory, diluting the MI trajectory before focus is recovered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session feels genuinely joint, with co-created plans, in-character rehearsal, and David shaping the agenda at each step. Several turns take the form of bulleted advice lists, keeping power-sharing slightly uneven at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is consistently offered and David's preferences drive each activity, with no pressure or coercion. During medication counseling, however, recommendations are presented in an expert framing with less emphasis on David's ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively draws out David's own reasons, refusal lines, and coping insights, and the role-play elicited strong commitment language. Deeper evocation of David's intrinsic motivations and more explicit reflection of his change talk would elevate this further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David is repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker, and his contributions genuinely shape the session. The counselor rarely explicitly names David's expertise about his own triggers, and some turns are expert-delivered information blocks.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open choice questions and behavioral rehearsal effectively evoked strong client change talk, including 'I'm serious this time' and 'Stepping away changes the whole thing.' Some commitment language passed without being deepened through reflection or amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about alcohol and 'one won't hurt' thinking is tolerated but not actively softened, with the counselor defaulting to practical solutions rather than double-sided reflections or strategic pivots. This is the weakest area and a clear target for skill development.
