# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance ('no judgment if you miss it,' 'data, not failure'), but some responses felt generic and scripted with repeated safety reminders, slightly diluting individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections ('a huge pile of shoulds'), but emotionally significant moments—fear of failure, feeling 'pointless,' the impact of the 187 reading—were met with checklists and advice rather than reflection of the emotional weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on glucose checking with a concrete, achievable next step that was followed up on successfully; direction was largely tactic-listing rather than strategically connected to broader values and change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered menus of options and shared next steps, but interaction was dominated by pre-packaged advice bundles in checklist style, functioning more like expert consultation than joint problem-solving built from Jonathan's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support via experimental framing ('one check as an experiment'), no-pressure language, and acceptance of Jonathan's choices about pace and family involvement; prescriptive how-to instructions occasionally edged toward controlling the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicited Jonathan's own motivations, values, or reasons for change; no open questions explored what mattered to him, leaving motivation dependent on external pressure and a single numeric shock rather than the counselor's technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for Jonathan's input at key decision points, but frequently reverted to expert-driven content delivery with lists of tips and directives, and did not clearly credit or build on Jonathan's own contributions (e.g., the coffee cue).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The experimental framing successfully evoked change ('one check, tomorrow, before coffee'; 'what now?'), but Jonathan's emerging change statements ('that's more than I did last week,' 'maybe that's worth knowing again') went unaffirmed and unexplored, missing chances to amplify motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with resistance and accepted external motivation without reinforcing it, but made little strategic use of double-sided reflections or reframing to actively resolve ambivalence—more neutral than skillful.
