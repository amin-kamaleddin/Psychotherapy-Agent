# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Jordan's discouragement and skepticism without labeling. Affirmations felt somewhat formulaic at times, and despair was quickly reframed before being fully explored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected Jordan's core experience, including validating that his barriers felt practical rather than psychological. Reflections were sometimes cut short in favor of offering options, and the emotional weariness beneath 'I can't get a break' wasn't deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on a concrete, achievable target with no topic drift. Ended with a clearly defined commitment: one resume bullet, drafted Thursday, reviewed at 7 PM, modest tone, no buzzwords.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan's choices were honored, but the power balance tilted toward the counselor, who supplied the draft bullet, formula, and unsolicited tips. The session felt more like presenting options for approval than genuine joint brainstorming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong support: repeated menus of options, respected Jordan's preference to work solo and decline reminders, and never pressured past his stated limits ('I'm not promising anything'). Occasionally steered toward the resume option and supplied too much advice content rather than letting Jordan own the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client motivation emerged ('I'd like to not feel stuck anymore'), but most momentum came from counselor-generated solutions. Missed chances to explore the meaning behind 'not feeling stuck' or the competence theme Jordan raised when describing training new hires; questions were mostly logistical.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan shaped the session by choosing option (a), solo work, timing, and tone, all accommodated by the counselor. However, frequent expert/directive slips — drafting the bullet and offering formulas/tips — nearly overwrote Jordan's own contribution ('under a week').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively moved Jordan from hopelessness ('I wish something would go right') to firm commitment ('I'll have it done') using low-threat options and reflective reinforcement of commitment language. Limited because much of the change content was counselor-generated rather than client-evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged Jordan's substantial sustain talk without arguing, but simply moved past it with task offers rather than strategic softening. No double-sided reflections were used, and Jordan's pivotal turn ('doing nothing's clearly not working either') went unamplified.
