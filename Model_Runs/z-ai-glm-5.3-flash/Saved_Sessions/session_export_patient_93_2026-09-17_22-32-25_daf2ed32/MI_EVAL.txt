# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates consistent, genuine unconditional positive regard ('That matters, and so do you, Margaret, exactly as you are'). No judgment about her drinking; her values and resistance to quitting are warmly accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections capture her ambivalence and elevate her insight ('the loneliness does it, more than the craving'). Reflections become repetitive in closing turns, and deeper emotional material (grief for Arthur, fear from the fall) is touched but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing alcohol intake throughout without drifting off-target. Closing turns repeat near-identical summaries rather than advancing toward follow-up planning or success criteria.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction functions between equal partners with invitational questions and jointly generated, client-authored problem-solving. Margaret's expertise visibly shapes the session direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly supports autonomy through genuine options and acceptance of her refusal to quit entirely. Attributes change fully to her agency: 'a plan you built, not one handed to you.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently draws out Margaret's own reasons and plans rather than supplying them. Missed opportunities to develop confidence/commitment language, e.g., linking her past success quitting sugar or scaling confidence for the first Tuesday.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within the client, and all key solutions (Tuesdays/Thursdays plan, Dorothy's calls, hiding the bottle) originate from Margaret. Never prescribes; decision-making stays firmly in her hands.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk, leading Margaret to generate her own concrete plan. Commitment language ('I'll give it a month, proper go') is summarized but not deeply amplified or consolidated with confidence-strengthening strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argues with or reinforces her minimization ('Seven drinks a week isn't much'), instead pivoting briefly toward change. Lacks explicit double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk.
