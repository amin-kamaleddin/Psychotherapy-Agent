# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tone was respectful and nonjudgmental, with some normalization ('one night doesn't make you an alcoholic'), but warmth was limited and regard felt conditional on cooperation with the mitigation plan. Few genuine affirmations of Ryan's honesty or ambivalence were offered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal; the counselor relied on batteries of closed information-gathering questions rather than reflecting feelings or meaning. Emotional material (couldn't sleep, 'why me?', fear of being labeled an alcoholic) went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the conduct hearing and preparation, a legitimate and consistent agenda. However, direction was toward case management rather than the clinical target of alcohol use, which was repeatedly redirected back to paperwork.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction resembled a case-management consultation, with the counselor generating the statement, witness note, checklist, and email template while Ryan approved or tweaked. Ryan's request to make the statement 'sound like me' was honored, but agenda, content, and pacing were overwhelmingly counselor-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered menu-style choices ('Which would you prefer?'), respecting some client choice. However, it prescribed remedies and answered Ryan's question about whether the alcohol class would actually help him with a directive, pragmatic answer rather than supporting his own evaluation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The counselor provided answers and finished products rather than eliciting Ryan's own motivations, concerns, or relationship with alcohol. All meaningful change-relevant motivation ('education matters a lot to me,' 'once is a mistake, twice is a pattern') emerged unprompted from Ryan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role, designing nearly all deliverables with Ryan's contributions limited to revisions of counselor-generated material. Little was done to elicit Ryan's own ideas about what would work for him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ryan spontaneously offered rich change talk about valuing education and not wanting to repeat the mistake, but the counselor did nothing to evoke, reinforce, or deepen it. No open questions linked Ryan's drinking to his values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** While the counselor did not actively reinforce sustain talk, it failed to roll with or strategically soften Ryan's minimization ('it was one party,' 'I don't think I do have a problem'). Early reassurance actually validated the minimization instead of developing discrepancy.
