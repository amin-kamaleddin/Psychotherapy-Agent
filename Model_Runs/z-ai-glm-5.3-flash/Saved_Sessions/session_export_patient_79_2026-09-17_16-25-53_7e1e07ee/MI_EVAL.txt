# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent respect and nonjudgment toward a resistant, sarcastic client, affirming small efforts without criticism. Fell short of 5 because affirmations were functional rather than deeply personalizing, with little explicit normalization of his ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected core concerns including exhaustion with complexity, fear of looking fragile at work, and desire for privacy. Reflections occasionally served as preambles to advice-giving rather than deep emotional exploration of feeling judged or dismissed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively narrowed from broad frustration to a concrete plan: brush → one practice puff, inhaler by toothpaste, one week trial. Advice-giving at times shifted from guiding to instructing, and the broader target of overall asthma management was left unaddressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative with permission-seeking and plan elements drawn from Luis's own life and routines. Power-sharing was uneven, as Luis mostly selected among counselor-provided options rather than co-creating ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support, accepting his preferences to skip the demo, avoid phone reminders, and decline check-in calls. Some options were pre-packaged by the counselor and autonomy statements sometimes functioned as gentle steering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicited motivation through open questions and a confidence ruler that drew out Luis's own solutions. Evocation sometimes narrowed quickly to logistics, missing deeper motivations like his wife's concern and self-image as not fragile.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Luis as the expert via permission-seeking, choices, and letting him shape the plan. The counselor still drove the agenda at points by introducing the ruler, if-then menu, and check-in question.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked commitment, ability, and willingness statements, plus a concrete 6/6.5 confidence rating with a solution. Did not deepen Luis's strongest change talk ('maybe I'll keep it up') with follow-up reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with neutral or double-sided reflections rather than argument, with quick pivots to low-burden options. Accommodated some sustain talk without strategic reframing, e.g., accepting deferral of controller medication and follow-up without a bridge.
