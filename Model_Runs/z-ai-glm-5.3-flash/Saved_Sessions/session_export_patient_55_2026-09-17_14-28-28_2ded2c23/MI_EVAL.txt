# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect maintained and nonjudgmental tone even when Isabelle minimized risks ("That's completely okay," "Thanks for being honest"). However, warmth was formulaic; affirmations were generic ("nice job," "That sounds sensible"). Her closing fear about parental notification was not met with a reassuring, accepting response.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal; responses rarely reflected her feelings or perspective. Ambivalence ("Why does everyone make it such a big thing?") was met with agreement rather than deep reflection of her self-image as a high-functioning student. Repeated pivots to questions or advice lists instead of exploring meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Once alcohol was disclosed, focus stayed connected to the referral concern. However, the first half drifted across study tips, sleep, and Pomodoro; the counselor did not proactively name the screening's alcohol focus even after Isabelle asked directly why she was called in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Session was predominantly counselor-driven: rapid-fire question batteries, bulleted advice, and pre-packaged plans. No joint brainstorming or eliciting her ideas; content originated entirely from the counselor. Isabelle's role was largely to answer questions and accept.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered and Isabelle successfully negotiated smaller commitments (one Pomodoro trial, no text template), without coercion. However, interaction was largely counselor-proposed plans for her to accept/tweak; no explicit message that decisions about drinking are hers, and her confidentiality worry went unaddressed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Almost no effort to draw out Isabelle's own motivations or values. No open questions linking drinking to music, academics, or independence. Motivation appeared only as reluctant compliance; information and reasons for change were pushed rather than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Surface-level menu choices ("Which would you prefer?") showed partial partnership, but expertise was clearly counselor-positioned. The 3-step plan and summaries were delivered expert-style; the plan was essentially the counselor's with minor tweaks. No acknowledgment that she knows best what would work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little deliberate evocation of change talk. Commitments ("A limit of two is fine," "I'll give one or two a shot") emerged from checklists, not strategic MI questions. No importance/confidence scaling or open evocative questions; change talk was compliance, not internal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ("It's not a problem," "Everyone does it") was occasionally deflected with "Totally fair," then followed by advice-giving. No double-sided reflections acknowledging her strengths alongside risk concerns; ambivalence never explored or resolved.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **2.2/5**
