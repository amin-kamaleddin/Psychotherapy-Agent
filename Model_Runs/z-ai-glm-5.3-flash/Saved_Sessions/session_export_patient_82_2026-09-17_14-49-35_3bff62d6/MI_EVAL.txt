# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, respect, and non-judgment, including appropriate handling of confidentiality limits and validation of ambivalence. Slightly formulaic repeated phrases ('That makes sense,' 'totally understandable') kept it from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong accurate reflections, e.g., capturing the tension between 'everyone does it' and Lukas's unease. Reflections occasionally mirrored exact wording rather than deepening the identity conflict, and were sometimes paired with immediate advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior, progressing from disclosure to a concrete micro-plan (driving, a phrase). Lukas twice signaled wanting to slow down and the counselor continued offering options, slightly overriding his pacing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative — asked before advising, checked preferences, and built the plan from Lukas's own idea. Some turns tipped advice-heavy with counselor-generated bulleted lists and scripts rather than even co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: options presented as menus with explicit client choice, no pressure, and full respect for Lukas's request to keep things low-key and reach out on his own. He chose his own pace, plan, and mantra ('my night, my choice').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked identity-based motives and client-generated change talk (driving plan, 'my night, my choice') from a hesitant adolescent. Frequent supplying of options/scripts and missed confidence/commitment exploration prevented a 5.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly framed Lukas as decision-maker ('What feels most helpful to talk about?', 'Which feels most doable?'), and his input shaped the session. The counselor generating most of the solution menu slightly undercut client expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evoking questions ('What would you most want to be different?', 'smallest, most doable step?') elicited meaningful change talk that was reflected and reinforced. At times offered solutions himself and missed chances to deepen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged Lukas's substantial sustain talk ('everyone does it,' 'it was one party') but with neutral rather than strategic double-sided reflections pivoting to change. Occasionally accommodated sustain talk without gently testing it against his values.
