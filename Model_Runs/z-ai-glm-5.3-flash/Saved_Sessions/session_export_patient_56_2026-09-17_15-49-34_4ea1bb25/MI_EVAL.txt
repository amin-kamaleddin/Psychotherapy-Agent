# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with affirmations and no judgment. The client's family-centered values are fully respected and her fears normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate, capturing both content and ambivalence, including her assertiveness concerns. Reflections are sometimes brief, moving quickly to questions rather than deepening her guilt about 'letting the team down.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful focus on protecting family dinners by leaving on time from opening to concrete end product. Each exchange advances the goal, with no topic drift, ending in an actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative session with role-play, feedback, and co-constructed scripts and email. The client's ideas shape every element of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Permission-seeking, offered rather than imposed tweaks, and client choices throughout reinforce ownership. Her final 'I feel ready' reflects autonomous commitment rather than compliance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within the client via confidence rulers, reflection questions, and role-play. She generates her own rationale and culminates in autonomous commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as author of the plan through open questions and performance feedback. Slightly reduced because the counselor supplies sample phrasings and drafts the email, limiting her sole contribution.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation through confidence scales (7 rising to 8+), role-play, and co-drafted commitments. Change talk grows from tentative to 'I feel ready.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Guilt and fear of seeming less committed are acknowledged and reframed without amplification. Barrier discussion, while productive, occupies substantial airtime.
