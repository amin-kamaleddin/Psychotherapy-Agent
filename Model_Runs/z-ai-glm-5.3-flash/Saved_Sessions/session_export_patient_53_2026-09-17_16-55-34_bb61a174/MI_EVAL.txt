# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with normalized anxiety and no disapproval despite Isabella's avoidance. Affirmations ('that's brave,' 'that's real progress') are somewhat formulaic and could be more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of ambivalence ('you want someone to talk to, but going somewhere new feels hard'). Depth is occasionally limited, with insufficient exploration of loneliness and past bailouts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every turn ties back to the concrete target: texting the support worker for a Tuesday 10 AM coffee. The session moved from vague openness to a specific plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Most turns present pre-packaged solutions (scripts, times, techniques) with Isabella passively agreeing ('I guess,' 'okay'). True two-way idea generation appears mainly in her voice-note proposal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong choice language ('what feels doable right now,' backup options she selected from). Pacing of suggestions and rehearsal pressure at times narrowed options, with limited affirmation of her ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened well with an evocative question and used confidence rulers effectively (4→5). However, the session largely supplies solutions rather than drawing out Isabella's own ideas and reasons for connection.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Advice-dense interactions (scripts, exit lines, specific times) were mostly complied with rather than co-created. Her contributions were incorporated, but power-sharing was inconsistent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence rulers, specific commitments, and eliciting her backup ideas increased change talk ('I'll do one of them'). Change statements could be more reflected and amplified rather than immediately problem-solved.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I might chicken out,' 'I always talk myself out of things') was neither reinforced nor strategically softened. Pivoting to next steps was functional but lacked double-sided reflections or reframing of past cancellations.
