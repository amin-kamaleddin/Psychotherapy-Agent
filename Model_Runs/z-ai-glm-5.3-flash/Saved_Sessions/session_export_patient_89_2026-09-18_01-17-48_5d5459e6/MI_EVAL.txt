# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, genuine respect and positive regard throughout, with specific individualized affirmations and no labeling or judgment. The patient's history of quitting was treated as understandable rather than a character flaw, with his expertise repeatedly honored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, emotionally attuned reflective listening, including complex reflections capturing ambivalence ('part of you is tired... another part lights up') and emotional core ('it's the story you tell yourself after one miss'). Subtle content like 'old tape' and 'how it goes wrong for me' was tracked closely.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus maintained with a coherent arc from barriers to concrete first step to relapse prevention. Fell short of 5 because the clinician followed the patient's momentum rather than proactively shaping measurable specifics (duration, exact days, feasibility of walks).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership of equals, with the final plan fully co-constructed from the patient's own ideas and even the follow-up schedule his decision. The clinician served as a reflective collaborator rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy actively reinforced — no advice-giving or persuading at any point, with every plan element originating from the patient. Ownership was explicitly highlighted, directly countering his fear of imposed plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from within via skillful open questions and amplified discrepancy, yielding strong self-motivating language ('this time feels different, because it is'). The patient left with his own reasons, rules, and supports.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise clearly located in the patient, who authored every element of the final plan. The closing summary explicitly credited his self-designed approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk skillfully evoked and developed, with strategic self-selection questions ('which one feels most likely to actually happen?') drawing out commitment language. The closing question yielded a rich self-generated commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk consistently acknowledged then pivoted away from, using double-sided reflections and inviting the patient to build his own counter-story to the 'old tape.' Focus remained on possibility and small wins rather than past failures.
