# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treats Derrick with respect and warmth despite his defensiveness and illegal behavior, validating his reluctance without judgment. Falls short of a 5 because affirmations are sparse and generic, with little explicit positive regard for Derrick as a person beyond his behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Efforts to capture Derrick's perspective are present but reflective listening is limited, with checklist-style question batteries and advice lists dominating. Key emotional material — his isolation during sobriety, the breakup, and fear of losing his only friends — is never deeply reflected or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on drinking and impaired driving with safety checks and concrete next steps throughout the session. Only misses a 5 because focus drifts prematurely into abstinence metrics that Derrick never agreed to, triggering resistance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction occurs in the roleplay, the text to his cousin, and the negotiated weekend checklist, but much of the session is pre-formed plans delivered for Derrick to react to. The best collaborative moments stem more from Derrick's initiative than the counselor's equalizing stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are real autonomy-supportive moments, including asking permission, offering choices, and accepting Derrick's smaller-step counter-proposal. However, directive pre-packaged advice and unrequested abstinence targets are experienced as pressure, and the counselor rarely emphasizes that Derrick controls the outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation largely came from Derrick himself; the counselor's evocation was superficial, with a readiness ruler never followed up ('why a 4 and not a 2?') and Derrick's own insightful questions answered with script lists. His own reasons for change — isolation, self-respect, work — were left largely unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine power-sharing exists, and Derrick's ideas did meaningfully shape the plan (ride fund, DD role, keys with cousin). But the clinician frequently slips into expert-driven mode with prescriptive lists and imposed numeric goals, requiring Derrick to push back to regain ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Derrick generated substantial change talk (DD role, ride fund, 'calling tomorrow, for real') that the psychiatrist capitalized on at times. However, change talk was never systematically deepened with elaboration questions or complex reflections, and jumps to prescribed targets provoked pushback.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist wisely avoids arguing with Derrick's sustain talk and cleverly reframes socializing via the DD plan. Missed double-sided reflections and the introduction of abstinence-focused targets briefly amplified sustain talk ('you're sliding that in there like I agreed to quit').
