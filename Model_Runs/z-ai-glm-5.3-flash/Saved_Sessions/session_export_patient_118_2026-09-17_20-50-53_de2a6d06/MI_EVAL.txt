# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains unconditional positive regard throughout, affirming the patient's resistance as reasonable rather than problematic. Even when the patient pushes back or deflects to dental concerns, the counselor responds with consistent warmth and respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, layered reflections captured both surface content and emotional undercurrents, prompting the patient to admit "it bothers me more than I let on." The patient explicitly validated the counselor: "you actually listened when I said the pills were a disaster."

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session progressed coherently from past failed attempts toward a personalized plan, flexibly linking the smoking and dental agendas. A point is withheld as some drift into general stress discussion occurred before reconnecting to the smoking target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor asked permission, co-constructed rather than prescribed, and extended the collaborative stance to dental planning. The patient's own ideas directly shaped the session's content and outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership were consistently reinforced ("You're not signing up for anything today, and I respect that completely"). The patient's repeated emphasis on "my terms" shows he perceived and valued this support, moving toward change voluntarily.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from the patient, who articulated his own reasons (his mother's gum disease, the walking and garden plan). His closing remark that he "ended up talking myself into... thinking about it properly" confirms the evocative approach succeeded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned the patient as the expert on his own life ("Sometimes you're the best expert on what might fit your life"). The entire stress-management and garden/walking plan emerged from the patient's own answers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful open questions ("what do you think might work if you ever decided to cut back or quit?") generated substantial change talk ("maybe I'll give the walking a fair go this week," "someday, on my terms"). The counselor deepened it with a textbook follow-up: "What shifted, if anything?"

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was redirected without argument—e.g., honoring the dental focus while gently pivoting with a bounded question. A point is withheld because sustain talk received fairly full airtime in early exchanges before redirection occurred.
