# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, unconditional positive regard throughout, with no judgment about literacy struggles, criminal history, or ambivalence. Asking for help was reframed respectfully as 'a bloke taking his future seriously.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, capturing core ambivalence from the opening reflection onward. Lars repeatedly confirmed feeling understood ('Yeah, you've got it,' 'when you put it like that').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on the target behaviors (peer pressure, alcohol-fueled boredom, education/employment), advancing steadily to a concrete plan. Even exploratory questions served self-efficacy for the change plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear partnership explicitly named ('let's be partners on this'), with plan content co-constructed and mostly authored by Lars. The informal, warm register reinforced egalitarian rapport.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lars chose his own timing and methods, and was consistently offered choice. Slight drift into persuasion at closure ('I don't reckon you'd trade that...') and 'Sounds like you've already decided' kept this from a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within Lars via questions eliciting his own resources, values, and self-talk. His commitment language strengthened progressively, culminating in a fully self-authored plan and 'Hold me to that, eh?'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was positioned within Lars, who generated the fridge note, Wednesday timing, and 'rip the plaster off' script himself. The counselor affirmed and built on these rather than substituting advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions effectively evoked strong commitment language ('maybe I could give it a crack,' 'Wednesday it is'). Change talk was deliberately highlighted and amplified, and past coping successes built confidence talk organically.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally reframed rather than amplified, though sometimes reflected at length before redirecting (e.g., the mates' pull and guilt). More strategic use of double-sided reflections could have resolved ambivalence faster.
