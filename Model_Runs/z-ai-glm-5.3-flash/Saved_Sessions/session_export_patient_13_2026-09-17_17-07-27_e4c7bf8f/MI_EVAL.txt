# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations and normalization of fear, but warmth occasionally feels formulaic and is quickly followed by task lists.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections, but deeper emotional content — fear of bad news, shame, pattern of giving up — is met with logistics rather than reflective exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on seeking medical care and moves progressively toward a concrete Thursday plan, though direction is heavily action-oriented rather than balancing exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anara's choices shape the plan, but the pattern is mostly counselor-creates-resource, client-accepts-or-tweaks, with little joint brainstorming or inquiry into her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices and reassurance that setbacks are recoverable are consistent ('thank you for not pushing me'), but the counselor tends to propose option menus rather than invite client-generated steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplies reasons, scripts, and solutions rather than eliciting Anara's own motivations and confidence; almost no evocative open questions or scaling — a core MI shortfall.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing in content choices, but the counselor frequently assumes the expert role, dispensing tips and checklists without drawing out the client's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is captured and affirmed, and Anara's self-efficacy grows, but momentum is largely client-generated with little deliberate evocation through open questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced and avoidance is briefly reframed, but fears about failing and chickening out pass without double-sided reflections or reattribution toward capability.
