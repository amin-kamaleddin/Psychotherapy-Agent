# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, normalizing the relapse and praising three months of sobriety. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and the counselor moves quickly to tools rather than fully sitting with shame and self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections are present, but the counselor repeatedly jumps over emotional material to deliver strategy lists. Responses to deep shame and fear of judgment feel scripted rather than reflecting the emotional weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays focused on relapse prevention, but direction is expressed through rapid-fire agenda items (triggers, coping, access, backups, check-ins) that overloaded Elias. Focus is maintained but not strategically paced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Decisions are framed as options and Elias's objections shape the plan, but the interaction remains counselor-proposes/patient-negotiates. True co-creation occurs only when Elias insists on it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices and accepts Elias's refusals and modifications. Undermined by prescriptive content (ready-made texts, the 30-minute rule) and end-of-turn questions pushing immediate action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplies reasons, steps, and scripts rather than eliciting Elias's own motivations. Missed opportunities to explore the meaning of his sobriety, his reasons for stopping, or his vision of the brother conversation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for Elias's preferences and accepts his pushback, but the dominant mode is expert-driven with pre-made scripts Elias must edit. His own suggestions are adopted but not treated as the primary source of solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elias offers meaningful change talk ('I don't want to feel that way again,' 'I can do one thing') but it is rarely reflected or deepened. No open questions evoke his own reasons or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about uncertainty and fear of disclosure is acknowledged without arguing, but is followed by rapid redirects to problem-solving. Missed chances for double-sided reflections linking his fear of disclosure with his desire to avoid shame.
