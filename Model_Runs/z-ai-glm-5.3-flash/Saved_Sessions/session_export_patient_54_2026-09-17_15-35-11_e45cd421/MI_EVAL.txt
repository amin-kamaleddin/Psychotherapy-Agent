# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly opened with 'listen without judgment and support your choices' and maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout. Accommodated the client's request to remove blame-oriented language from the handout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflections consistently captured the client's frame, including her family context and uncertainty. The mid-session reflection of her core ambivalence ('torn between ritual and baby') showed strong emotional depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained steady focus on alcohol use in pregnancy with no drift, progressing logically from ambivalence to commitment and a concrete plan. Session ended with specific, time-bound steps (boxing wine, first mocktail tomorrow).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plan was genuinely co-constructed — client proposed the mocktail swap and partner support while the counselor contributed structure. Her closing comment ('I came in ready to argue, and now I'm leaving with a plan') evidences successful partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced client control ('it has to be my call') and offered menus of options rather than directives. The non-controlling stance directly enabled the client's shift from resistance to self-chosen commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from the client herself — her values ('the baby wins every time'), confidence rating, and her own solutions. All change talk originated from the client; the counselor never lectured.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Asked permission before sharing information and solicited the client's input on every plan detail (wording, format, recipes). The final materials reflected her specifications and edits.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The well-timed values question ('preserving the ritual or avoiding risk?') elicited decisive change talk ('the baby wins every time'). The counselor then reinforced commitment language and helped operationalize the client's own solutions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce or debate sustain talk, redirecting instead toward values and choice. However, relied mainly on pivoting away rather than using double-sided reflections or explicit reframing to actively soften it.
