# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations of Oliver's generosity and courage. However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic and tied to task completion, with deeper emotional material (guilt, self-criticism) left unexplored or unnormalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate reflections but recycles the same 'torn between guilt and space' reflection rather than deepening it. Key emotional moments — Oliver's fear of sadness and his insight that courage follows action — go unreflected as the psychiatrist moves quickly to scripts and tools.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the target behavior, with every exchange tied to setting the date, rehearsing the script, and planning delivery. The session progresses logically from ambivalence to commitment to a concrete action plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative elements exist — Oliver adapts scripts, selects dates, and shapes the final notice. However, the psychiatrist frequently presents menus of pre-made options rather than jointly generating ideas, and uses yes/no questions that steer rather than share the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers genuine choices and invites Oliver to select dates, wording, and delivery methods. But the session is heavy with prescriptive advice (scripts, checklists, techniques), positioning the psychiatrist as the source of solutions with little explicit messaging that the change belongs to Oliver.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely draws out Oliver's own motivations, values, or reasons for change; strong commitment language emerges spontaneously rather than from deliberate evocative questioning. Most session content — scripts, tools, checklists — is supplied rather than elicited, contrary to MI's spirit that expertise resides within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Positions Oliver's voice as central and co-creates wording he adopts as his own ('That's mine now'). Still, the overall stance leans expert-driven, with Oliver mostly selecting from offered options and his own ideas not explicitly credited as his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates good conditions for change talk through role-play rehearsal, eliciting strong commitment language ('My decision's firm', 'I think I can actually do this'). Misses chances to deliberately deepen it, however — e.g., exploring what reclaiming his flat would mean or using confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges Oliver's guilt and ambivalence neutrally without reinforcing the status quo, but offers little active strategic softening — no double-sided reflections or reframing of guilt as evidence of his kindness. Sustain talk is largely bypassed rather than resolved.
