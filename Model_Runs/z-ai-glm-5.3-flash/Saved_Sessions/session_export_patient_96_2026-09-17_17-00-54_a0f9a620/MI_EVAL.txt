# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with warm affirmations of Marisol's choices and autonomy. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and repetitive, and there was little explicit normalization of her precontemplative resistance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate reflective listening captured surface content (e.g., 'fiddling is automatic'), but reflections stayed brief and shallow. Deeper exploration of her feelings about authority figures and her surprise at caring about her gums was missing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained unwavering focus on reducing oral health risks, with every exchange advancing toward concrete action. Two real steps were completed in-session (reminder set, text sent).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner, co-constructing a plan from Marisol's own ideas and preferences. A dense advice list early on and repeated offers to draft messages were subtle expert leans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: every step was framed as Marisol's choice, her refusal to remove the piercing and to accept drafting help was respected, and no pressure or lecturing occurred. She explicitly credited the non-lecturing approach for her engagement.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within via the confidence ruler, 'what might move you up one point,' and eliciting her own rationale ('keeping my teeth is part of keeping the piercing'). At times the counselor supplied options and rationales rather than letting Marisol articulate reasons herself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Marisol as the expert, respected her boundaries ('I'll leave the draft help to you'), and let her contributions shape the plan. Early prescriptive menu options leaned slightly expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited change talk from a precontemplative client through open questions and a confidence ruler, producing concrete commitments and in-session actions. Reliance on menu-based options limited deeper reflection and development of Marisol's own change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk neutrally and pivoted toward harm-reduction without arguing. However, sustain talk was not actively resolved with double-sided reflections, and her repeated 'I'm not taking it out' was never leveraged into commitment language.
