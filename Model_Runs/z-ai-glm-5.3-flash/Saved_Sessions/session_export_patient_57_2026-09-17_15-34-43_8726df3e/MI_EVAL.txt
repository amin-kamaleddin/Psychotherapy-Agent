# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, accepting Jamal's rationalizations without criticism. However, affirmations were somewhat generic and formulaic ('nice concrete plan,' 'strong work') rather than individualized to his character strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Jamal's core ambivalence between belonging and protecting his team spot, including his desire for a 'quiet win.' Reflections were occasionally repetitive, and deeper emotional content — fear of losing standing or cracking under pressure — was acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained exceptional focus on the target behavior (avoiding team drinking games) with no topic drift. Every turn built toward a concrete plan: scaling questions, a specific exit time, named supports, a scripted exit line, and role-play.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session functioned as joint problem-solving, with Jamal drafting his own texts and exit line while the counselor structured the process and joined in role-play. The counselor frequently generated the full option lists himself rather than eliciting Jamal's ideas first, making the counselor the primary source of ideas at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Everything was consistently framed as Jamal's choice, with option menus offered rather than directives, and his rejections (the reward, the corny comeback) were immediately honored. His self-set confidence of 8 and closing 'I'll let you know how it goes' reflect genuine ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, importance/confidence rulers, and 'what would raise it?' probes successfully drew out Jamal's own motivations and self-generated insights. A significant portion of concrete strategies came from counselor suggestion lists, and deeper personal values behind protecting his team spot were not explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jamal was repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker, and his preferences (role-play over drafting texts, rejecting the reward) clearly shaped the session. The counselor often supplied full option lists himself rather than eliciting Jamal's own ideas first, slightly undermining the principle that expertise resides within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Standardized MI tools were skillfully used: importance scaling elicited a 4, and confidence scaling tracked movement from 6 to 7 to 8, with Jamal generating his own commitments ('I can do that this weekend'). Much of the concrete content (exit strategies, options) was counselor-generated rather than evoked, limiting deeper self-motivational elaboration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about peer pressure was met with empathic reflection followed by solution-focused redirection, and Jamal's corrections of suggested options were incorporated rather than defended. The counselor at times provided long option menus that implicitly kept barriers salient, preventing a higher score.
