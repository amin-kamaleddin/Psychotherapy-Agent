# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone with effective normalizing ('ambivalence is normal, doesn't mean you're lazy') and repeated affirmations. Affirmations are somewhat generic ('That's a solid start'); more individualized regard for his honesty and self-awareness would raise this.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening throughout, capturing his core concern (energy, work eating plans) and fear of talking himself out of it. Reflections sometimes shift to plan summaries rather than exploring the discouragement beneath 'I've bailed on myself enough times.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on increasing physical activity; every exchange ties back to the target behavior, from opening questions through concrete planning to next steps. Guides toward change each turn while staying grounded in Farid's own goal of energy and small steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely power-shared plan: Farid proposed the train backup, chose Deshawn as accountability, picked the calendar note, and set Thursday's condition himself. The clinician consistently invites his ideas rather than prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirms choice ('What feels most important to you,' 'Which day/time feels most doable') and accepts his decision to wait on Thursday. A few permission-based but directive framings ('Are you willing to schedule the alert now...?') slightly steer rather than purely reinforce ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully draws motivation from within via rulers and open questions, eliciting Farid's own change philosophy ('Small is doable'), accountability insight, and success signal ('fog lifting'). Client-generated motivation drives the plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently treats Farid as the expert on his own life, and his contributions genuinely shape the plan. Falls short of 5 because some advice blocks ('A clear plan might help: block 10 minutes...') are somewhat prescriptive rather than eliciting his solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, skillful evocation using readiness and confidence rulers, plus questions about moving from 5 to 6 and what follow-through would look like. Farid responds with rich change talk, and confidence visibly rises from 5 to 7 during the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well without amplifying it, effectively reframing the 'laziness' doubt and responding to ten-minute doubts with benefits. Some sustain talk (fear of failing twice in one week) is absorbed into logistics rather than actively softened with double-sided reflections.
