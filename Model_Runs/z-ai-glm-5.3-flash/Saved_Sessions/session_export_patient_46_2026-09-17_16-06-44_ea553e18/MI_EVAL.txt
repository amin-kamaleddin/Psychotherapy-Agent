# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 ☐ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard and explicit non-judgment ("Thanks for being straight about it"; "straight answers are useful, no judgment"). Counselor never scolds or labels despite Ethan's minimization ("not an alcoholic"). Warm, respectful tone throughout with a defensive adolescent — particularly difficult.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 5 ☑ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening — "vomiting and your mom's disappointment sting — and you're tired of getting in trouble" accurately weaves ambivalence together. Slightly below 5: early reflections lean toward summary/paraphrase rather than deeper emotional reflection; Ethan's "that kinda stung more than I expected" invited deeper affective exploration that was summarized instead.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 ☐ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Remarkable focus for an adolescent session. Every exchange moves toward the target behavior — from description, to ambivalence, to rulers, to a concrete time-anchored plan (activities, ~11 exit, safe-text, practiced refusal lines, Sunday check-in). Gently redirects rather than lets the session wander.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 ☐ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Genuinely two-way: safety plan co-constructed from Ethan's own suggestions (Marcus, soda/Monster, gaming); Ethan drafts the Sunday text himself and runs both sides of the role-play. Decisions (leave time, check-in method, backup plan) are negotiated, not prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 ☐ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Choice explicitly and repeatedly reinforced: "Which of those (or something else) feels most doable"; "How would you prefer to check in — a quick text, a message here, or a short visit?" Options offered as menus, not directives. Counselor respects Ethan's one-weekend framing without pushing for more.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 ☐ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Motivation drawn almost entirely from Ethan — no lectures or persuasion. Rulers and open questions elicit his own reasons and solutions; he moves from "it's not a big deal... importance 4" to "not puking Sunday morning and not getting grounded? That'd be a win," and self-generates the backup plan. Textbook client-owned motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 ☐ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Expertise consistently located in Ethan: "Which of those feels most doable," "what would YOU try this week?" His ideas (bail to Marcus's, casual text wording, leaving by 11) substantially shaped the plan and were folded back in. His contributions genuinely drove the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 ☐ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Excellent, systematic evocation: importance/confidence rulers plus "If you wanted to move from a 4 or 5 up to a 7, what would need to change?" Ethan responds with sustained change talk ("tired of getting in trouble," "I don't like when I get sick"), deepened by "what would make a non-drinking hangout actually feel fun?" and solidified via "which of those would you try this week?"

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 5 ☑ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Generally avoids amplifying sustain talk with brief, non-reinforcing acknowledgments ("Rating a 4–5 makes sense"). However, "What do you like most about drinking?" invited elaborated sustain talk ("It's just fun to drink with my friends") without consistent double-sided reflections to pivot back; "I'm not saying I'm quitting" was allowed to stand without a gentle reframe. Effective but not maximally skillful at softening.
