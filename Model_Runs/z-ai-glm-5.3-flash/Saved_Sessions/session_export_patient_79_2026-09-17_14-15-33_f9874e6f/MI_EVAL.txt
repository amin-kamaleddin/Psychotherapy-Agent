# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains basic respect and non-judgment, but warmth is formulaic and affirmations of Luis's efforts are largely absent. Misses the chance to affirm his vulnerable disclosure about the night-time attack.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal, with surface acknowledgments quickly pivoting to advice. Luis's job demands, fear of the night attack, and distrust of his doctor's records go unexplored, with no complex reflections capturing meaning beneath his sarcasm.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well focused on asthma self-management and ends with a concrete, actionable plan. However, direction is pursued through premature problem-solving rather than clarifying Luis's own goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor dispenses multi-step plans in an expert-driven manner, with collaboration appearing mainly as damage control after pushback. Luis's input is honored but the agenda and solutions originate almost entirely from the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers menus of options and Luis ultimately chooses the script, preventer trial, and role-play. Framing is nonetheless pushy and advice-dense, with Luis noting 'You said this was simple' and 'I'm not promising anything,' indicating perceived pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Luis's motivations emerge spontaneously (fear of night attacks, desire for fewer appointments and better sleep) but are never elicited or developed. The counselor supplies reasons and tactics rather than drawing out the client's own.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role, prescribing tracking, plans, scripts, and techniques. Luis's ideas shape the plan only after he objects, with little indication his own contributions drive it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerges from the client but is never deliberately evoked, reflected, or amplified. No open questions about his reasons for change, and no importance or confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('too much for me right now,' distrust of doctors) is met mainly by reducing task load rather than strategic redirection. No double-sided reflections link his stated hopes against his doubts.
