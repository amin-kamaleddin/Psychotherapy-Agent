# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirmed Linda's honesty and ambivalence. Warmth was somewhat formulaic and brief, missing deeper affirmation of her strengths as a mother and normalization of her 30-year struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered accurate early reflections (enjoying smoking vs. worry about the example set), but most responses quickly pivoted to advice-giving. Significant emotional disclosures — the sting of her daughter's comment and breaking the generational chain from her mother — went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking in the context of protecting the children, progressing coherently to a concrete plan (Saturday talk, moving the pack, delaying the cigarette). Minor drift occurred through repeated delivery coaching without explicitly renegotiating the shifting focus between cessation and harm reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was co-constructed from Linda's own ideas, options were presented as choices, and role-play was done jointly at her request. The counselor nonetheless drove content through expert advice menus rather than letting Linda's ideas shape each direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, asked permission, and respected Linda's stated limits without any pressure to quit. Autonomy support was somewhat undercut by pre-formatted advice menus in nearly every turn and failure to affirm her ownership of self-generated insights.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist supplied reasons, scripts, and rationales rather than drawing motivation from within the client. Powerful material (breaking the chain from her mother, feeling like a hypocrite, 'hearing myself now') went unexplored — no importance/confidence scaling or evocative questions were used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for Linda's preferences and offered choices, supporting some power-sharing. However, the session was heavily advice-driven, positioning the counselor as the content expert while Linda's contributions mostly shaped logistics rather than conversation direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda volunteered meaningful change talk ('maybe that lands with her,' 'I'm hearing myself now'), but the psychiatrist did not reflect, amplify, or strategically deepen it. Questions focused on logistics rather than eliciting reasons, importance, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally ('it's normal to feel quitting is huge after 30 years') but not actively softened. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections linking her strong feelings for her children to her doubts about quitting.
