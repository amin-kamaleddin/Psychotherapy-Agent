# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Non-judgmental tone was maintained and Dylan noted the absence of lecturing, but repeated offers of scripts after explicit refusals implied the counselor knew better. Little affirmation of Dylan's genuine strengths or existing harm-reduction efforts limited positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections were minimal and quickly followed by advice, checklists, and scripts. No complex reflections explored Dylan's frustration, record concerns, or the tension between social identity and the conduct process.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the conduct referral and asked early about Dylan's goals, but direction was almost entirely logistical rather than oriented toward the target behavior of underage drinking. The drinking itself was never named or explored as a focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was predominantly counselor-directed with scripts, checklists, and menus of counselor-authored options. Dylan's preferences were acknowledged but then overridden with re-offers of the same products.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Explicit affirmation of choice was present with no pressure or coercion about drinking. However, persistent offers to draft scripts and plans after repeated refusals eroded autonomy by positioning the counselor as the doer.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Reasons, strategies, and solutions came almost exclusively from the counselor with virtually no open questions eliciting Dylan's motivations or values. The single moment of client-generated motivation was met with a prescriptive list rather than evocative follow-up.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly occupied the expert role, offering pre-made products rather than inviting Dylan's own ideas. Dylan had to push back multiple times to reclaim ownership of his own statements and plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dylan's spontaneous change talk ('being smarter about where') was met with a list of safety rules instead of reinforcement or expansion. No open questions evoked desire, ability, reasons, or commitment for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor did not actively reinforce sustain talk, which is positive, but also made no effort to soften or redirect it. No double-sided reflections or use of the record concern to build discrepancy; sustain talk remained dominant and unaddressed.
