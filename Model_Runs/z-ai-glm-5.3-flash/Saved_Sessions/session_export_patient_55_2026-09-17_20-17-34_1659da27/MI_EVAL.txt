# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with no judgment or arguing; pushback was met with respect and warm, individualized affirmation throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflections including a skillful double-sided reflection of ambivalence. Slightly limited by empathy occasionally serving as a strategic pivot rather than an end in itself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The referral concern (alcohol risk) was dropped entirely after the client deflected to stress, with no gentle query or collaborative acknowledgment of the screening flag. Excellent focus was maintained on the chosen target, but the original clinical target was silently replaced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was genuinely co-constructed — the experiment design, timing, and success criteria all came from the client. The clinician invited and accepted client direction throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership was consistently reinforced ('you're steering this, not me'), advice was never imposed, and outcomes were framed as information for her to gather rather than pass/fail judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Values-based questions and the scientist framing drew out the client's own motivations and counterarguments, culminating in 'I guess I did kind of talk myself into it.' No persuasive arguments were supplied by the clinician.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located within the client ('the real expert on your own life,' 'the scientist of your own life'), and client contributions genuinely shaped the session. A model demonstration of partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook progression from desire/ability talk to a full commitment ('Thursday, stop at 11, see how Friday feels'), with change language explicitly named, mirrored, and reinforced via values-based evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well through double-sided reflections and a brilliant reframe of skepticism into client-designed experimentation. Minor deduction for occasionally engaging the cost-benefit debate directly rather than pivoting away.
