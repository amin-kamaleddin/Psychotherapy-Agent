# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance, including explicitly committing to not raking Marcus 'over the coals' if he struggled. Affirmations were somewhat generic, which prevents a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured Marcus's ambivalence, including his frustration with past failures and his motivation for his kids. However, reflections were often brief and rarely deepened the emotional meaning behind his repeated discouragement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained an exceptionally clear focus on increasing physical activity, with every exchange advancing toward a concrete plan including implementation details, obstacle troubleshooting, and follow-up arrangements. No topic drift occurred at any point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated between genuine equals, with the counselor co-creating the plan and incorporating Marcus's creative additions like the dance-break rain fallback and dog-spotting contest. Marcus's own ideas substantially shaped the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforced Marcus's ownership through choices, permission-seeking, and inviting him to shape the check-in style, without any pressure or prescribing. Marcus's closing reflection on leaving with a plan he might actually stick to demonstrates genuine autonomous commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew motivation from within, eliciting Marcus's core reason (his kids), enjoyable activities, confidence rating, and own problem-solving. Deeper evocation of Marcus's values and hope, beyond logical persuasion about small steps, would distinguish a 5.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor positioned Marcus as the expert, asking about his barriers and preferences, and plans were built from his own ideas like walking, dancing, and family involvement. Option menus, while collaborative, occasionally steered rather than fully letting Marcus generate ideas himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evoked commitment and planning through open questions, option menus, and confidence scaling, drawing strong change talk from Marcus including excitement and immediate action. Some opportunities to deepen change talk, such as exploring why small steps would succeed where past attempts failed, were left unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** When Marcus voiced sustain talk about past failures and time barriers, the counselor acknowledged it neutrally and pivoted toward doable change without dwelling on failures. Slightly more use of double-sided reflections balancing sustain and change talk would raise this to a 5.
