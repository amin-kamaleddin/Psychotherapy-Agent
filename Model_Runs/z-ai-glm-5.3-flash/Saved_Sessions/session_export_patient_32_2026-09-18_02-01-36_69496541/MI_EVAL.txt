# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed the patient's perspective, including his belief that he doesn't have an alcohol problem, without judgment or argument. Validating statements like 'That's real' and 'that honesty counts for a lot' appear throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections repeatedly captured both surface content and deeper meaning (e.g., framing Friday nights as belonging and history, not just drinking). The therapist also acknowledged the emotional weight of the failed six-month sober period.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintained a clear focus on safer drinking/riding behavior and license recovery without being coercive. Each exchange moved the patient incrementally toward a self-generated plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was entirely patient-authored, with the therapist acting as a curious guide ('What's your gut say?', 'What feels like the right next step for you?'). The patient repeatedly generated his own solutions (ride money, arriving at ten, gym first).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist consistently emphasized that choices belonged to the patient ('that's a plan you built yourself') and normalized imperfection ('Friday one won't be perfect'). No pressure or directive language was used.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully drew out the patient's own change talk ('I can't keep tempting luck,' 'getting my license back matters more'). The therapist elicited and highlighted the patient's own reasons rather than supplying them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The back-and-forth rhythm was highly balanced, with the therapist building on each patient statement. The interaction felt like joint problem-solving rather than expert-led advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist explicitly flagged change talk ('That's you doing real thinking out loud,' 'that's not nothing') and reflected it back to strengthen commitment. This helped the patient move from precontemplation to concrete commitment ('Friday one, see how it goes').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The therapist accepted sustain talk (e.g., 'just beer with the guys,' 'I don't think I'm some alcoholic') without arguing, and worked around it by identifying values (friendship, license). Slightly less emphasis was placed on directly reframing the 'not an alcoholic' identity statement, though it was handled respectfully.
