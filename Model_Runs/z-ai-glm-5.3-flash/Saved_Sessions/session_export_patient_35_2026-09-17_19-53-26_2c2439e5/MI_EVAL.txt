# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly accepting Elena's refusal to promise and normalizing her fears and shame without any judgment. Affirmations were genuine and individualized, such as recognizing that Elena came up with the walking idea herself.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was consistently accurate and increasingly deep, capturing her ambivalence from the opening reflection onward. Complex reflections, like reframing the cigarette as a smaller, faster version of the break she already knows works, were immediately confirmed by Elena.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on smoking cessation and stress management, progressing logically from eliciting a doable alternative to a concrete step and follow-through. Each exchange moved forward without drift, and the closing question kept momentum toward the change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element originated with Elena, including the walking, ten-minute increments, phone alarm, and coworker check-in. The clinician never prescribed, and Elena explicitly recognized the plan as feeling like something 'mine.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforced Elena's ownership by accepting her refusal to promise and explicitly de-emphasizing premature commitment. There was no advice-giving or pressure, and Elena repeatedly framed the plan as her own choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from Elena: her own reasons, confidence, self-discovery, and commitment. Questions like 'What does that tell you about yourself?' elicited self-affirming conclusions, producing escalating change talk culminating in her articulating her deeper fear and readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly framed Elena as the expert on her own life and asked genuinely exploratory questions. Elena's contributions visibly drove the session and were built upon rather than redirected.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions systematically evoked Elena's own ideas, and her language strengthened progressively from 'I've thought about it' to 'I want to try.' The clinician consistently reflected and amplified her change talk rather than supplying it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced and was often reframed effectively, such as turning the cigarette break into a version of a known-working break. However, some acknowledgments of her fear of quitting received extended airtime without an immediate pivot back to change, though this was handled respectfully.
