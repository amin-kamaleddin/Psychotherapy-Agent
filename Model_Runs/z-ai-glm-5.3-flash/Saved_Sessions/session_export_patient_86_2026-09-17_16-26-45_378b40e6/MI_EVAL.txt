# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout ('I'll back you either way,' 'slips aren't failure'), with no lecturing or judgment despite the client's guardedness. The client even echoed this support back, demonstrating its impact.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured the client's ambivalence, routine, and subtle distinctions (wanting a cigarette vs. reflex). The clinician validated his honesty and his partner-related motivation throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on smoking, moving from assessment to a concrete, time-limited experiment (porch coffee swap starting tomorrow, one-week trial, 10-minute delay backup, 3-day check-in at 10am). No topic drift; recaps consolidated the plan effectively.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing: the client selected and designed the experiment and negotiated check-in logistics. Occasionally the clinician supplied suggestion lists (thermos, alarm, gum, deep breaths) rather than drawing ideas from the client first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding autonomy support with repeated explicit affirmations ('I'm here to support whatever you decide') that were consistently honored, including accepting the client's deferral on gum without pushing. The client tested this commitment and the clinician's reliability validated his ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful open-ended evocative questions elicited genuine self-generated motivation (partner, curiosity about testing himself, want-vs-reflex insight), moving readiness from 2 to 3. Pivotal moments—the partner's quiet acceptance and 'half of it was just going through the motions'—were left underexplored, and some later turns offered solutions instead of evoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's choices clearly shaped the plan (experiment design, call preferences, check-in agenda). Score falls short of 5 because some ideas were delivered in a slightly advice-list format rather than fully elicited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective techniques like 'what would move you from 2 to 3' drew out authentic change talk and concrete plan details. However, the clinician did not deepen or strategically reflect the client's strongest change statements back as motivational material.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('surgery didn't do it,' 'can't see myself changing') was acknowledged without reinforcement and diminished over the session. Double-sided reflections counterbalancing sustain talk with the client's own change talk would have strengthened this.
