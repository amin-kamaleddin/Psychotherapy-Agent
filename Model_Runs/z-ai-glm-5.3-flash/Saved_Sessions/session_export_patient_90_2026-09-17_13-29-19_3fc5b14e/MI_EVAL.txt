# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Marcus's dog boundary without pushback and offering affirmations. Warmth felt somewhat procedural, with quick pivots from emotional disclosure to practical triage limiting depth of unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered adequate reflective statements acknowledging exhaustion and frustration, but most responses quickly shifted to information-giving. Emotional core themes (guilt over skipped appointments, fear of betraying the dog, worry about his wife) were acknowledged but not explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created good opportunities for change talk through manageable choices, and Marcus generated genuine, client-driven commitments ('I can't keep going like this,' 'Two weeks... Deal?'). Could have deepened this by eliciting Marcus's own reasons for change and confidence levels.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk about the dog, instead reframing toward alternatives (med changes, HEPA filters, limiting pet access) and redirecting barriers into small concrete steps. At times redirection was abrupt, moving to problem-solving before ambivalence was fully acknowledged.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally invited Marcus's input and co-created a plan, but much of the session was advice-heavy with expert-to-client option lists. Marcus's own expertise regarding his dog, routines, and finances was incorporated only lightly rather than actively drawn upon.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session wandered across billing, home stress, the dog, and asthma without an integrative summary to consolidate focus. Eventually crystallized around the bedroom-exclusion experiment and morning tracking, but early billing discussion pulled focus from the health behavior goal.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices rather than directives, explicitly respected Marcus's boundary on the dog, and framed the plan as a self-run 2-week test. Would be stronger with explicit statements that outcomes and decisions rest entirely with Marcus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a collaborative stance—offering options, engaging in role-play at Marcus's request, and using 'we'll decide together' language. Fell short of top marks because the counselor dominated with expert content rather than jointly generating strategies from Marcus's own ideas.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Relied primarily on information-giving and structured suggestions rather than drawing motivation from within Marcus. His motivations emerged organically but were not evoked through targeted questions about what matters most or how confident he felt.
