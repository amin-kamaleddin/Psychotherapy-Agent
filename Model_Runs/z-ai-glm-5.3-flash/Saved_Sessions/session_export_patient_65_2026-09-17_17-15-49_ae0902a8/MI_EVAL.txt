# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with multiple affirmations and no criticism of the client's guardedness or avoidance. However, affirmations were somewhat generic and task-focused rather than reflecting deep, individualized positive regard for Julian's values and strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections consistently and accurately captured Julian's stated concerns, including his stress about fitting in hours and uncertainty about logistics. Reflections remained mostly cognitive/logistical, with little exploration of the emotional weight of being court-mandated or his anxiety about phone calls.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained excellent focus on completing and documenting court-mandated community service hours from start to finish, with a logical progression of steps. The session closed with concrete commitments: a 7pm text, Thursday follow-up, and Wednesday 1pm meeting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor framed the work as joint and co-constructed materials, with Julian revising the text message and summarizing the plan in his own words. However, the counselor generated nearly all tools and templates, leaving Julian operating mostly as an approver rather than a co-creator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently offered choices and accepted Julian's edits without pushback, reinforcing his ownership. Autonomy support was somewhat paternalistic, as Julian selected among counselor-generated options rather than generating his own, with little explicit reinforcement of his capacity to succeed independently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor almost exclusively asked logistical questions and never elicited Julian's own motivations, values, or reasons for change — a critical gap for a pre-contemplative, externally motivated client. Motivation that emerged was incidental to action planning rather than deliberately evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered some choices and incorporated Julian's contributions, but frequently assumed the expert role by supplying fully drafted texts, templates, and scripts with minimal elicitation of Julian's own ideas. The session's structure and solutions were largely counselor-generated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Julian made preparatory change talk (agreeing to steps, self-efficacy statements, expressing relief), but this was largely driven by the counselor's action-oriented structure rather than evocation. Missed opportunities to explore what completing the hours might mean personally or to use confidence rulers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor did not dwell on or reinforce Julian's avoidance and uncertainty, instead pivoting quickly to solutions without arguing or confronting. A stronger response would have reframed the procrastination disclosure into a strength or change opportunity rather than simply engineering around it.
