# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, normalizing ambivalence and never labeling or pressuring. Withholds a 5 only because explicit affirmations are limited early on.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Precise double-sided reflections accurately capture both sides of Elias's ambivalence, and follow-up questions track his lived experience throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every turn stays anchored to the target behavior of increasing activity with no topic drift, while acknowledging barriers as they arise.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently asks rather than tells, treating Elias as the expert on his own body and limits. Strong collaborative stance, though agenda-shaping evidence is mostly implied rather than explicit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change is framed as Elias's own exploration at his own pace ('a bit more manageable,' 'if and when it feels right'). An explicit acknowledgment of his right to choose would elevate this further.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Answers are consistently drawn from Elias himself, eliciting his own motivations ('that feeling again,' 'proud'). Sustained evocation is well demonstrated, with room for even deeper commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician positions Elias as the expert, offers no prescriptions, and highlights 'you proposed it, not me.' Power-sharing is evident but could be more explicit throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open evocative questions generate desire, ability, and commitment language ('I'd like that feeling again,' 'I want to'). Stops short of deepening commitment language in some moments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (body won't keep up, fear of broken promises) is acknowledged briefly then pivoted toward possibility. Light reflection is effective, though strategic reframing is only occasionally used.
