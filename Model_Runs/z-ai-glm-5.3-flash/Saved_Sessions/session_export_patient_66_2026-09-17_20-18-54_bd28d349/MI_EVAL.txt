# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout ('wherever you are with this right now is completely okay'; 'you're worthy either way'). Client explicitly notes he expected a lecture and didn't get one.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, layered reflective listening; double-sided reflections capture ambivalence precisely and repeatedly elicit new disclosure (kids, mornings, self-efficacy). Checks understanding and reflects the emotional core (the ten quiet minutes).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking in the context of the workplace policy from opening reflection to closing invitation. No topic drift; ends with a specific collaborative plan to reconvene.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely joint exploration with permission-seeking and building on the client's own ideas. The plan remains entirely client-defined; slightly more explicit co-construction of a follow-up plan would reach 5.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice consistently reinforced ('it's your call, not the company's or mine'; 'my door's open'). Accepts the client's statement that he'll still smoke tomorrow without pressure; client shows internalized ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All reasons for change originate from the client via open, evocative questions. Moved from 'I'm not gonna quit' to self-generated change talk and self-identified motivation ('the idea's sitting with me more').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in the client ('You know yourself better than anyone here'); never prescribes and asks permission. Client confirms experiencing power-sharing ('You seem like you'd actually help, not just nag').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk skillfully evoked and developed, progressing from 'maybe I'd think about it more seriously' to commitment-adjacent language. Ability talk amplified by highlighting the client's prior successful cutback.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well with double-sided reflections and avoids arguing. Several reflections restate sustain talk, giving it repeated airtime; earlier summarization of the change side would resolve ambivalence faster.
