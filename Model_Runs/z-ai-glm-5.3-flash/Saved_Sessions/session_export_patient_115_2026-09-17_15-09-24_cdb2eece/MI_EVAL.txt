# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, with affirmations of Samir's progress and honesty. No lecturing or disapproval despite continued smoking and drinking, which the client explicitly appreciated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captured the fear of nighttime attacks and the meaning of evening cigarettes. Reflections were sometimes formulaic and did not explore deeper emotional layers such as resignation and family dynamics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on the target behavior of asthma control, with every response tied to concrete steps and measurable indicators. No drift whatsoever across the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed with joint anticipation of obstacles, and the client recognized shared ownership. The counselor sometimes generated options ahead of the client rather than waiting for his input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: consistently offered choices, respected the client's limits without pushback, and framed tools as optional. The client's closing comment confirms he felt he was choosing the change himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew motivation from the client through scaling questions and open exploration, letting Samir reason himself into the experiment. Stayed at the behavioral-experiment level rather than evoking deeper values-level motivation around work, family, and identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing with agenda and plan choices negotiated, and Samir's own contributions substantially shaped the plan. The counselor supplied most of the solution menu rather than eliciting Samir's own ideas more often.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent and effective use of scaling questions and small-step prompts that elicited genuine commitment language and client-generated insights. Deeper reinforcement of commitment language, such as exploring what success would mean, was missing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk and pivoted toward client-chosen experiments rather than debate. However, no double-sided reflections were used, and the counselor repeatedly offered options for the most resisted behavior instead of building first on high-confidence wins.
