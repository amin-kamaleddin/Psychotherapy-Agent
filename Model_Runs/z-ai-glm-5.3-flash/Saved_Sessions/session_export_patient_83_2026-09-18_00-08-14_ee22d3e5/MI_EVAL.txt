# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard is consistently conveyed ('However you're feeling is completely okay,' 'no judgment either way'). Mai's skepticism and frustration are validated without any judgment or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately tracks Mai's emotional shift from frustration to confidence. Reflections sometimes repeat her own words rather than probing deeper emotional meaning behind her distrust of unexplained care.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains unwavering focus on understanding and deciding on the new statin. Every exchange ties back to the target behavior, moving logically from education to a concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently frames the work as joint ('Let's look at this together') and Mai's preferences directly shape the plan. Equal partnership is demonstrated throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly reinforced: 'whether this feels right for you at all,' 'on your own terms,' and 'this plan is yours — you built it.' No coercive or pressuring language appears.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor draws motivation from Mai by eliciting her questions and reflecting her values back to her, with change talk emerging naturally. More deliberate evocative techniques (scaling, deeper values exploration) would elevate this further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mai's expertise and contributions are repeatedly affirmed ('you kept asking until it made sense') and her questions set the agenda. Falls slightly short because the educational content is initially counselor-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked ('what would make you feel ready') and amplified when it emerges ('You've tied this to what matters to you'). Could be strengthened with explicit confidence/importance ratings or deeper commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mai's sustain talk is validated rather than argued against, with skillful pivots toward autonomy and information needs. Redirection relies on implicit moves and education rather than double-sided reflections to actively resolve ambivalence.
