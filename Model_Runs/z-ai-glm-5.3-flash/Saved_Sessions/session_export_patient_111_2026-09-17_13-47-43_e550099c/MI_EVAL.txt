# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a nonjudgmental, welcoming stance despite Richard's hostility and minimizing. However, warmth was sparse and mechanical, with few genuine affirmations of his honesty or 25-year marriage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered an accurate initial summary reflection, but dominated the session with advice-giving rather than deeper reflections. Key emotional moments like 'she's scared, isn't she?' were glossed over instead of reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session abandoned the target behavior (smoking cessation) almost immediately, shifting entirely to marital communication scripts. No exploratory questions about Richard's own smoking, health, or readiness were asked.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative elements included menu-based choices and incorporation of Richard's script edits, and the role-play was engaging. However, the default mode was dispensing bullet-pointed advice, with power-sharing responsive rather than proactive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices at the outset and invited practice/tweaking, which supports agency. But frequently gave directives and never affirmed Richard's stated boundary about not promising to quit in an autonomy-respecting way.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicited almost no motivation from within Richard; all change-oriented statements were spontaneously generated, not evoked. No evocative questions, importance/confidence rulers, or exploration of his own reasons for quitting were used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Quickly shifted into an expert/advisor role delivering scripts and tactic lists with minimal solicitation of Richard's own ideas. Failed to treat Richard as the expert on his marriage and smoking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Richard produced meaningful change talk ('I don't want to lose her,' 'she's scared, isn't she?') that was repeatedly missed. Redirected to communication scripts instead of reinforcing or deepening these moments, and change talk about quitting itself was never evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ('Doesn't mean I'm quitting,' 'not like flipping a switch') was simply bypassed rather than softened. No double-sided reflections or exploration of the 'for her, not for me' ambivalence Richard explicitly raised.
