# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and warmth, with individualized affirmations of the client's honesty, self-knowledge, and effort throughout. No judgment or disapproval was present, and ambivalence was normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and tracked the client's content and values well. However, most reflections restated content rather than exploring deeper emotional experiences (e.g., the feeling of being 'stuck' for months).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on establishing a physical activity routine with progressive momentum from motivation to a concrete follow-up plan. There was no topic drift, and each exchange moved the plan forward while remaining client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely two-directional, with the client's ideas driving session content and the clinician's summaries building on her contributions. Inclusive language ('we'll figure out together') and a co-constructed follow-up plan demonstrated full collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was reinforced throughout without any controlling statements—options were offered rather than prescriptions, and ownership was consistently returned to the client. The client directly validated this approach: 'you let me figure out my own way of doing this.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently drew motivation from the client rather than supplying it, using evocative open questions and reflecting her own motivation back. The client left with self-generated goals, coping strategies, and confidence—the clearest evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was consistently treated as the expert on her own life, with every plan element originating from her. The psychiatrist explicitly located expertise within the client, who acknowledged it: 'Thanks for not just telling me what to do.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked at nearly every turn—explicitly named, reinforced, and strengthened through evocative questions. The client responded with sustained desire, ability, commitment language, and a 10/10 confidence rating.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never amplified and was consistently reframed as barriers to 'outsmart.' Barrier discussion, however, occupied considerable airtime, with occasional echoing of barrier details rather than quicker pivots back to change.
