# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout — framing the plan as 'just an experiment' and validating Daniel's skepticism without labeling his drinking as a problem. Falls short of 5 due to somewhat instrumental warmth and few genuine affirmations of Daniel as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening, accurately capturing Daniel's ambivalence and the emotional meaning of the Friday beer ritual. Some reflections stay brief, with quick pivots to information-giving or option menus rather than deepening exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear and consistent focus on alcohol use in the context of marathon training, with every exchange tied to the target behavior. Progressed steadily to a concrete, time-bound experiment without becoming coercive despite deflections.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong joint problem-solving — Daniel shaped the plan's days, tracking method, and check-in schedule. Somewhat uneven, as dense pre-packaged option lists and the max-4-drinks suggestion occasionally shifted toward expert-driven advice until Daniel reasserted preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary reinforcement of Daniel's ownership: repeatedly emphasizing choice, accommodating his refusal to commit to a numeric limit, honoring his check-in preferences, and permitting the experiment to 'fail.' Consistently framed change as Daniel's decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful opening question evoked value discrepancy and drew out Daniel's own motivations and insights (flat Tuesday runs, wanting to see the data). Evocation was front-loaded, with later exchanges shifting to providing information rather than consistently eliciting reasons, confidence, or vision.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively positioned Daniel as the expert with questions like 'Which of those feels most doable?' and his contributions genuinely shaped the plan. Reliance on pre-packaged menus and counselor framing (the 'cliff' suggestion) kept it from a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited change talk via the training/discrepancy framing and low-threshold experiments, yielding commitments like Thu–Sun alcohol-free and tracking. Missed chances to deepen change talk when it appeared (e.g., 'part of me wants to see') and sometimes supplied options himself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing, but little strategic softening — no double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with Daniel's own change talk (e.g., 'hoping to prove you wrong' vs. double-checking the math). Often met with neutral acknowledgment plus new information or accommodation.
