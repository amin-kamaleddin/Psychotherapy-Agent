# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful tone with genuine affirmations of Erik's progress and no judgment about his struggles. However, affirmations occasionally feel formulaic and setbacks like relapse or caving to cravings are not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections about back pain progress and wanting to feel like himself again, but reflections are brief and quickly pivot to questions. Deeper emotional content—self-comparison discouragement, self-criticism, and fear of quitting—goes unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains consistent focus on exercise consistency and craving management, progressing logically from goals to barriers to concrete commitments. Some drift occurs from stacked questions and the diet topic is only briefly addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative tone with permission-seeking and co-developed plans shaped by Erik's own ideas. The counselor sometimes supplies solutions before exploring Erik's own, tilting toward expert-driven problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers meaningful choices and lets Erik select which changes to adopt. At times somewhat directive with pre-packaged tweaks and scripts, and Erik's ownership of the plan is not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Erik's own motivations through values questions and confidence rulers, connecting plans to his stated goals. Evocation is occasionally replaced by advice-giving, and the emotional meaning behind his motivations goes unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik's input is requested on choices, but the counselor frequently offers pre-packaged solutions and drives the agenda with rapid-fire questions. Erik's own good ideas are not explicitly acknowledged as expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence scaling and concrete planning questions successfully evoked strong change talk, moving Erik from a 6 to an 8. Change talk is rarely deepened with follow-ups about why the number wasn't higher or what success looks like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'll cave,' 'the couch wins') is not reinforced, but the counselor pivots to problem-solving rather than strategically softening it. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections, e.g., when Erik said he 'won't be perfect'.
