# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations of Yasmin's honesty and self-awareness; her weight-loss desire was accepted without correction or disapproval. Warmth was somewhat formulaic ('I really like this plan,' 'I love how practical'), and affirmations focused on plan adherence rather than deeper affirmation of Yasmin as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate summary reflections captured Yasmin's ambivalence, fatigue with crash diets, and fear of restriction, including a strong reflection framing the plan as 'a one-week experiment to support energy, not rules.' Reflections were often bundled with multiple new questions or advice, diluting emotional depth; the shame spiral was acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (sustainable, non-diet health changes) from the opening values question through the closing check-in plan. The session progressed logically from values to goals to first step to contingency planning, with strong recap summaries that kept momentum without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used inclusive language and co-constructed the plan from Yasmin's preferences, with genuinely joint decisions (eggs, shake backup, Sunday prep, Wednesday 7pm). Frequent pre-formatted option lists (e.g., 'Quick options: 1) ... 2) ... 3) ...') made the psychiatrist the agenda-setter of available choices, partially offsetting full equality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every intervention was framed as an offer Yasmin could decline ('Would you like to start there?', 'Want me to suggest a first small step?'), and the plan was explicitly grounded in her values. Her request that the midweek check-in ask about energy rather than weight was adopted without hesitation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivation from within using the values question and confidence rulers twice (6 to 8), with Yasmin's own reasons driving the plan. Evocation was occasionally replaced by suggestion-giving (protein gram targets, specific foods, script wording), and the pivotal statement 'maybe I'm done looking for one' was not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Yasmin as the expert, and her contributions genuinely shaped the session (eggs, shake backup, Sunday prep, evening check-in all came from her). However, frequent multiple-choice menus subtly reclaimed the expert role and constrained her space to generate her own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk through the values question and confidence rulers, yielding strong client statements ('I want something more sustainable,' 'this feels like something I'm doing for myself, not to myself'). A missed opportunity to deepen the spontaneous 'maybe I'm done looking for one' with a reflection or affirmation before moving to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of 'cheating,' all-or-nothing thinking, attachment to weight loss) was consistently acknowledged and gently reframed without reinforcement, and the weight-loss desire was not argued against. Sometimes met with a quick pivot to logistics rather than a deliberate double-sided reflection connecting her ambivalence to her own change reasons.
