# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and respect, with explicit autonomy validation and no judgment despite the client's defensiveness. Warmth occasionally feels formulaic, such as the boilerplate opening and generic crisis screening.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captured the client's perspective, e.g., the smoking ritual as his 'private mine' and the autonomy-vs-relationship conflict. Emotional depth was sometimes reflected only briefly before pivoting to practical suggestions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus, pivoting from resistance to the relational motivation and anchoring in concrete targets (script, Sunday date, compromises, one-month reassess). Early menu options slightly scattered focus before the relational theme emerged.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration occurred mid-session when the client co-developed his message and set terms. However, the counselor's default was directive—providing numbered options, step-by-step plans, and pre-written scripts rather than letting the client's ideas shape the content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicit autonomy affirmations were present ('You don't have to want to quit') and the client's refusal of experiments was respected. The prescriptive scripts and structured plans at times constrained the client's sense of authorship.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from the client himself, producing genuine change talk ('She means more to me than these cigarettes ever did') and a shift from forced attendance to volunteering to return. Evocation relied heavily on the relational channel and was less systematic in exploring health concerns or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frequently asked for client input and incorporated his contributions, but repeatedly slipped into expert-driven delivery of scripts, tips, and templates. The client's words were often handed back polished rather than drawn from him, limiting genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and amplified change talk, transforming the relational motivation into a concrete plan (Sunday conversation, script rehearsal, one-month reassess). Missed opportunities to deepen the client's ambivalence about quitting itself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided arguing and reframed defense of the ritual into relationship-focused work. Early prompts like 'tell me one thing you like about smoking' and experiment menus briefly invited sustain talk and irritated the client.
