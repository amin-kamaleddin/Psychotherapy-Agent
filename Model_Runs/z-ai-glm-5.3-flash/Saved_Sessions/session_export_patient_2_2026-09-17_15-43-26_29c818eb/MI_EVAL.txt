# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, immediately honoring Aisha's 'no lecture' boundary and accepting outcomes regardless. Explicit affirmations of her strengths were sparse and somewhat generic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening softened Aisha's initial guardedness and accurately reflected her exhaustion and need for evening wind-down. Deeper emotional content — grief about her aunt and fear for her son — was acknowledged only indirectly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on a specific behavior — evening smoking reduction — with every exchange building toward an actionable plan (three cigarettes, hidden pack, tea, timer, sister, follow-up). No aimless wandering; the alcohol topic was appropriately deferred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely shared, with Aisha's contributions central and the counselor explicitly requesting her input at each decision point. Counselor turns were information-dense, and a moment where Aisha sought validation was affirmed rather than turned back to her own judgment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent use of choice-oriented language ('Would you like...') and no pressure when Aisha hesitated, letting her take visible ownership. Some turns packed multiple suggestions and advice into long lists, which felt slightly leading.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation through open questions and scaling that drew out Aisha's own goal and solutions, with confidence rising from 5 to 7. Deeper emotional motivations (aunt's death, son's dependence) were never fully developed as her own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan was genuinely co-constructed with Aisha's ideas (tea, keeping the 'treat' smoke, texting sister) integrated into the final plan. Counselor occasionally supplied pre-packaged option menus and unsolicited gum dosing info, drifting slightly into expert mode.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Well-timed open and scaling questions elicited Aisha's own plan and mobilizing change talk, and her confidence rose during the session. Her deeper motivation (motherhood, cancer fear) was not explicitly deepened or reflected back as her reason for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue against sustain talk but also missed chances to reframe it. Aisha's minimization ('colleagues are worse') and alcohol deflection were left unexplored rather than gently challenged with double-sided reflections.
