# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, with genuine, specific affirmations (e.g., praising Leila's boundary-setting in the role-play) and an explicit non-judgmental stance ('we'll troubleshoot without judgment').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing Leila's motivators and frustrations. Reflections become sparser during the planning phase, missing deeper exploration of her people-pleasing pattern and the emotional weight of her inactivity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on the exercise goal throughout, progressing coherently from ambivalence to a fully specified action plan with concrete commitments, backup plans, and a scheduled check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed around Leila's schedule and preferences, with role-play at her request. Many solutions were offered as pre-packaged advice rather than brainstormed together, making some exchanges feel more like coaching.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, explicit autonomy support throughout ('Whatever you choose is yours'), with the counselor repeatedly asking Leila's preferences and reinforcing her self-owned commitment rather than controlling the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective evocation at the start via values questions and a readiness ruler, but the counselor shifts to telling — supplying scripts, tips, and reminders — rather than continuing to draw out Leila's own reasons, confidence, and strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Leila's contributions were valued at key moments, but the counselor slipped into an expert role for much of the session with detailed scripts and tips Leila did not initially request. Asking for her own ideas before supplying scripts would better honor client expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good evocative opening that prompted change talk ('I want to feel stronger,' '9/10 ready'), but the counselor then moved to advice and logistics instead of deepening her motivation. Much of Leila's commitment language was self-generated rather than skillfully drawn out.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without argument and channeled toward actionable solutions like indoor backups and boundary scripts. Minor deduction for the extensive scripting around sales pressure, which dwelt on the barrier rather than pivoting back to Leila's strengths.
