# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard is maintained throughout, with genuine, individualized affirmations and no judgment about the patient's habits, failed diets, or medication reluctance. Even resistance ('Still doesn't settle the pill question') is respected rather than challenged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and layered, capturing content and emotional nuance—from the core dilemma ('trade one problem for another') to deeper identity concerns around pride and being 'a guy with health problems.' Reflections consistently move beyond surface facts to meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains tight focus on the target behavior, moving stepwise from ambivalence to a concrete, self-generated plan with clear success criteria and commitment. The medication question is held as a defined future decision point without letting the session drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operates between equal partners: the clinician asks rather than tells, co-constructs success criteria, and offers genuine options. No expert-driven advice is given; the patient's ideas shaped the session's path and final plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's ownership of the medication decision is explicitly and repeatedly affirmed ('That decision stays yours'), with no pressure or coercion. His 'not yet' is framed as a legitimate choice, and the entire plan is patient-designed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within the patient—his relationship with Maria, his pride, his own reasons for change—rather than supplied by the clinician. Strategic questions let him build his own case for change, culminating in self-generated commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently positions the patient as the expert, with every strategy emerging from his own life (Maria, Ray, walking at seven). His own insight—'Maybe that's where I've been going wrong'—illustrates genuine partnership rather than prescription.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions systematically evoke desire, ability, reasons, need, and confidence language, moving the patient from dismissive reluctance ('Sure. Fine') to strong commitment ('I'll tell her tonight'). The confidence question directly deepened self-efficacy ('Maybe better than decent').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged briefly and redirected effectively, and anti-medication positions are honored without arguing. However, a few reflections restate sustain talk (e.g., 'the medication question stays open'); tighter pivoting toward change language would earn a 5.
