# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms the patient's plan. However, affirmations are somewhat generic and deeper unconditional regard is not explicitly conveyed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely reflects the patient's emotional content; responds to expressions of discouragement or excitement with tips and checklists. There are almost no reflective listening statements of feelings across the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains consistent focus on the target behavior with concrete, structured next steps and no drift. Points lost because the emphasis is on rapid plan-building rather than deepening motivation, with repetitive checklist recaps adding structure but not therapeutic direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers options and some content comes from the patient (walking, dancing, family support), but most turns deliver pre-packaged plans. The counselor holds the expert/plan-builder role while the patient mostly agrees.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices and frames the backup plan as the patient's own, with no pressure or coercion. Somewhat undermined by prescriptive instructions delivered without negotiation and a lack of explicit affirmation of the patient's ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some elicitation of the patient's own motivations (walking, dancing, family time) occurred. However, there was little open-ended exploration of reasons for change, confidence, or values — much content was supplied rather than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers menu options and asks the patient to choose, sharing some decision-making power. The overall stance remains expert-driven, prescribing specific steps rather than drawing expertise from the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforces and operationalizes the patient's change talk (walking, dancing), with commitment language escalating appropriately. Points lost because the counselor assigns rather than evokes, using few open questions to elicit the patient's own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and the counselor pivots to small, feasible habits. However, no double-sided reflections are used to resolve ambivalence, and forward-looking doubt receives only brief normalization.
