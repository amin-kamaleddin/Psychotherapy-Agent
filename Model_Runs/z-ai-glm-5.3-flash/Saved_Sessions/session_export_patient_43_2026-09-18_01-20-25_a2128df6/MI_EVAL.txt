# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout; client's function of smoking, skepticism, secrecy, and explicit boundary ('don't push it') all accepted without judgment or correction. No labeling, disapproval, or condescension anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate complex reflections and impressive tracking of the client's language shifts, but the session opens with a notable misattunement ('drinking' instead of smoking) that the client had to correct. Emotional depth around the wife tension and underlying fear beneath minimization was touched but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear, purposeful focus on smoking cessation while honoring the client's terms, progressively moving from 'what's the rush' to concrete planning. Each exchange builds deliberately toward the client's own next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently works with the client rather than on him, framing insights as the client's own discoveries and inviting him to shape the plan. The final framing strategy ('curious, not signing up') was co-constructed and genuinely his.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly and repeatedly affirmed: 'your call entirely,' 'you're the one who decides,' 'including nothing at all today,' and 'no pushing from me.' The client's boundary-setting is met with graceful acceptance and no pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful questioning draws out the client's own ideas, motivations, and solutions rather than offering advice. Questions like what a 'not perfect attempt' would look like evoke self-generated plans that fit the client's life and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positions himself alongside the client throughout, using language like 'from where you sit' and explicitly attributing all insights and the plan to the client himself. The client exits remarking the plan is 'actually mine.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The standout strength: conditional language is systematically converted into evocative questions yielding preparation, ability, and commitment-adjacent talk. Change talk is reinforced with ownership attributions and processed experientially, producing a clear trajectory from resistance to concrete intent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (skeptical comparisons to his uncle, 'I'd be cranky,' 'I'm not promising anything') is met with acceptance, validation, and reframing rather than argument or confrontation. Disclosures and defenses are normalized, allowing them to naturally give way to change-oriented language.
