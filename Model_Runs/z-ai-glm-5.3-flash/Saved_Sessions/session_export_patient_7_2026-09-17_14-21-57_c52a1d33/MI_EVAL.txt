# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, non-judgmental tone and normalized Amina's retraction without alarm. However, warmth felt mechanical, delivered through repeated menu-style options, with no explicit affirmation of Amina herself.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made decent reflective attempts about music as a refuge and handled the mom disclosure gently. Reflections stayed surface-level, and the counselor pivoted to tip-offering rather than exploring the emotionally significant 'when stuff falls apart' opening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No clear target behavior was established; the session drifted from safety screening to music to a list of coping tips without a negotiated focus. The behavioral target remained undefined throughout as the counselor followed deflections into light territory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The tone was non-authoritarian and Amina's own ideas (fan use, voice note to Amara) were incorporated. However, collaboration relied on pre-packaged option lists rather than jointly developing ideas, and the counselor's agenda was repeatedly re-inserted despite deflection.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A relative strength: choices were consistently honored, boundaries respected, and options framed as preferences rather than prescriptions. Falls short of full marks because choices came as fixed menus rather than open exploration, with little explicit affirmation of Amina's ownership of her change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort was made to draw out Amina's own motivations, values, or reasons for change—the counselor supplied tip lists instead of eliciting. When Amina offered a meaningful thread ('when stuff falls apart'), it went unexplored, and spontaneous motivation was not capitalized on.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina was positioned as the decision-maker through repeated choice-offering. This conveyed respect but operated as generic menus rather than genuine power-sharing, with Amina's contributions not substantially shaping the counselor-structured session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change-adjacent language emerged indirectly—Amina acknowledged existing coping, considered a voice note to Amara, and accepted the fist-squeeze technique. However, there was no deliberate evocation of desire, ability, reasons, or commitment language, and the session stayed in coping-tip territory.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was respected and never argued with or reinforced, avoiding escalation. However, there was little strategic softening—no double-sided reflections or deliberate pivots toward change—so the accommodation left ambivalence unresolved.
