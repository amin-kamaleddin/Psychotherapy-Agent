# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor immediately normalized Anya's discomfort and affirmed her without judgment; the client's comment that this wasn't the lecture she expected shows the unconditional positive regard landed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured her ambivalence and identity ('not confrontational, but not a pushover'), though reflections occasionally paraphrased content rather than exploring deeper emotion beneath 'it's fine' responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol-related decision-making for the upcoming party, with each question building progressively toward a specific, client-generated action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative—the counselor asked rather than told, offered a gentle option (texting Priya) while letting Anya choose the person and own the step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was consistently reinforced with no persuading or pressure; ownership stayed with Anya, who independently generated and committed to the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from the client—her values, coping repertoire, and identity were elicited via open questions and affirmations, with no persuasive reasons supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was explicitly positioned as the expert on her world, and her contributions shaped the session's direction while the counselor's input followed rather than led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation moved Anya from 'it's probably fine' to commitment ('I'll text Priya... That I can actually do'), with each change statement reflected and deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well with double-sided reflections and pivots to client resources, though the counselor briefly voiced her minimization himself ('people mostly just offer once and move on, right?').
