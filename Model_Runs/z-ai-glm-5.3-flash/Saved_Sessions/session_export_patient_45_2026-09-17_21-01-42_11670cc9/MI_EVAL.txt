# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard: disarmed Erik's expected judgment ('you're your own judge'), affirmed his honesty and supervisor competence, and normalized his defensiveness. Erik explicitly felt accepted, noting the counselor was the first to ask what he thinks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, emotionally attuned reflections like 'punished for feeling good' captured Erik's frustration accurately. Some early reflections restated sustain talk rather than surfacing the deeper fear of ending up like Dave, which Erik had to raise himself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol reduction and diet tied to diabetes management throughout. The session moved coherently from exploration to a concrete client-owned plan and follow-up commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative with team-oriented language ('we'll work with all of it together') and a plan built entirely from Erik's ideas. Decisions on starting point, success criteria, and follow-up were co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: 'you're your own judge,' no prescriptive advice even when directly asked, and repeated framing of the plan as Erik's own. Erik even set the follow-up terms himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently elicited from within rather than supplied, drawing out Erik's intrinsic reasons—supervisor competence, his wife, avoiding Dave's fate. Erik moved from external justification ('the doc said') to internalized motivation ('for me, and for the missus').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located with the client ('you know your life better than any chart does'), and Erik's question was turned back to him. Erik's own contributions substantially shaped the session—exactly what high partnership looks like.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of change talk via open questions ('What would make this manageable rather than a fight with yourself?'), with strategic reflection and reinforcement of Erik's commitments. Erik progressed from 'I could probably manage that' to self-affirming 'I'll give it a proper go.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Early reflections of minimization ('I feel fine,' the wife nagging) slightly risked reinforcing sustain talk before the pivot. The counselor then consistently reframed sustain talk toward change, and Erik moved from 'fuss over blood numbers' to a concrete reduction plan.
