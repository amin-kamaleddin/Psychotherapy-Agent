# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains basic respect and avoids judgment ('That's not silly — it's sensible to notice it early'), but warmth is minimal and instrumental. Few affirmations of the client's honesty or self-awareness, and his ambivalence and attachment to coffee are never explicitly validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; the counselor responds to disclosures with facts, lists, and advice rather than reflections. Emotional statements like 'coffee genuinely helps me get things done' go unmirrored, with no exploration of the client's internal conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains consistent focus on caffeine, sleep, and blood pressure throughout, guiding toward a concrete, measurable experiment. However, direction was imposed through advice-giving rather than negotiated collaboratively.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely expert-driven, with informational monologues and pre-made plan templates. The client's own ideas emerged only because he volunteered them; genuine co-construction appeared only in the final agreement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Inconsistent: repeated premature pushes toward plans ('Want me to write this into a one-week plan?') despite ambivalence, though later improvement was notable ('you pick which path, and I'll support whatever you choose') and the morning-cup condition was honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to draw out the client's own reasons, concerns, or confidence; instead the counselor supplies reasons for change and ready-made solutions. There are no open evocative or scaling questions exploring what matters to the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, delivering dense information blocks and plan offers before the client expressed readiness. The client had to push back multiple times to slow the agenda, and his contributions shaped the plan only after resistance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerged almost entirely from the client rather than being evoked through skilled open questions. The counselor made no effort to elaborate, affirm, or reflect the client's own change statements about his BP reading and sleep.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'm not sure I need to mess with something that's not broken') dominated without strategic response. The counselor offered more facts and plan offers instead of double-sided reflections acknowledging benefits while highlighting discrepancy.
