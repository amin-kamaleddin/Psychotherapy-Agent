# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Marcus's frustration, but repeated safety-screening questions felt checklist-driven and eroded rapport; affirmations were somewhat generic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate but shallow, often followed by advice or menus rather than deepening understanding; missed opportunities to explore his father's DUI history, trust dynamics, and feelings about being distrusted.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Focus stayed on the referral topic but drifted from the drinking behavior itself to parent-management and communication tactics, substituting task-completion for examining the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus shaped the session (choosing role-play, rejecting the firm script), but numbered menus and pre-packaged advice created an advisor-client dynamic rather than true partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Marcus's choice and never felt coercive, but frequent unsolicited advice and scripts tilted toward controlling the 'how' despite respecting the 'whether'.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dominated by dispensing tips, scripts, and templates rather than eliciting Marcus's own values, concerns, or solutions; his motivation emerged spontaneously despite, not because of, counselor technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and let Marcus pick activities, but the expert-driven stance (unprompted tips, scripts, agendas) left little room to build on Marcus's own strategies and expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal intentional evocation; Marcus's own change talk ('safety-rules thing isn't the worst idea', 'I could see doing that') went unrecognized and unreinforced, with no deepening questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with 'you don't have to quit,' which validated autonomy but endorsed the status quo; no double-sided reflections paired his ambivalence with his own change talk.
