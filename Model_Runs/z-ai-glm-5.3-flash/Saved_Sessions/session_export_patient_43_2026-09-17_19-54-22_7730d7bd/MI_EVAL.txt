# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly removing blame ('You did what you knew to do') and warmly receiving Eric's defensiveness. The individualized affirmation 'Saturday—that feels like a quiet kind of courage' reflected genuine, non-formulaic regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex, emotionally attuned reflections consistently reached beneath Eric's minimization to underlying feelings, accurately naming his loneliness and missing his wife. Reflections were repeatedly confirmed by Eric's responses ('Yeah. Yeah, I guess I do.').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus while allowing it to evolve naturally, with each reflection ending in a purposeful question advancing the work. It culminated in a concrete plan (Saturday, the truck, telling his wife) without aimless exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor checked perceptions, invited Eric's input, and built the session entirely from client-generated material (the truck, the wife connection, the Saturday plan). Eric's comfort musing aloud confirms he experienced the relationship as genuinely collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly and repeatedly reinforced: 'that's completely your call, and I respect that' and 'I'll trust your pace.' Even the patch pamphlet was Eric's own idea, and no pressure was applied toward further commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation emerged from Eric himself—reconnecting with his wife, getting weekends back, feeling less on edge—with no reasons supplied by the counselor. Eric's own surprise ('it turned into something else') is strong evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Eric was explicitly positioned as the expert ('You're the expert on your own life'), and every key direction came from him. His closing remark ('I don't know if I'd have figured that out on my own') confirms the insights felt like his own discoveries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and amplified, progressing from tentative ('Wouldn't hurt to get back to that') to a specific plan (Saturday) to values-based commitment ('that'd feel like winning'). Each step was reflected and affirmed, strengthening Eric's own momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally acknowledged without reinforcement and effectively redirected, notably via a skilled double-sided reflection when Eric said he wasn't ready to commit. Scored 4 because a few early reflections briefly restated sustain talk ('nothing's actually wrong') before pivoting.
