# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations like 'That tweak sounds wise.' Praise is somewhat formulaic and tied to agreement with the plan rather than deeply individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured both content and underlying concern (e.g., 'wanting a real, workable check-in, not just another place to ruminate'). Missed chances to reflect deeper emotions like the guilt about failing or being 'physically there but my head's elsewhere.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on aligning weekday behavior with family happiness; every turn advanced toward a concrete goal with no aimless drift. Clear progression from mapping to intervention, rehearsal, and a defined success marker.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely egalitarian—Isolde shaped the plan, dropped steps, chose her script, and defined success. However, the counselor supplied pre-packaged solutions (protocol, canned lines) rather than co-creating from the client's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced choice with options at every step and no pressure or persuasion. Accepted her one-night-at-a-time preference without pushing the trial, framing success on her terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was evoked ('What would feel like a meaningful sign it's helping?'), but most content—protocol, scripts, fallback lines—was counselor-generated. Missed opportunities to explore her own ideas before proposing structures.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly conveyed that Isolde's judgment drives the session, inviting her to pick and tailor options. Falls short of 5 because the counselor remained the primary source of concrete solutions rather than drawing them from the client's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged from the client ('I want us to actually be together for a bit') and evocative questions were asked. However, the counselor rarely paused to reflect, deepen, or affirm change statements, instead moving quickly to structure and scripts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded flexibly to sustain talk ('that's a lot of steps') by simplifying the protocol rather than defending it. Lacked strategic pivoting of ambivalence via double-sided reflections—responses were accommodation more than strategic reframing.
