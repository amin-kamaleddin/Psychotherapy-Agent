# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, with warm thank-yous and affirmations like 'You're handling a lot.' However, affirmations remain fairly generic and don't convey deep unconditional regard in the face of Liora's hopelessness and sustain talk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but brief, and the psychiatrist quickly shifts to risk checklists and plan-building. Emotionally significant statements—like fear of Dani seeing her as 'something broken'—receive no empathic reflection, and many responses are instructional rather than exploratory.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on self-harm, safety, and concrete next steps. Some focus is lost to procedural details like drafting the message text rather than exploring the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration exists—Liora edits the message and chooses options—but the safety plan is delivered as a pre-formed expert list rather than co-constructed from her own ideas. Her 'whatever, fine' responses suggest clinician-direction over power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently offers choices (written plan, breathing exercise, message edits) and respects Liora's decisions without pressure. Could be strengthened by explicitly framing change as her decision rather than a list of recommended techniques.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and coping ideas are largely supplied by the counselor rather than elicited; almost no questions draw out Liora's own reasons, hopes, or past successes. Her spontaneous insights like 'sitting alone with it is worse' are left undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Input is invited and Liora's edits are respected, but the plan is largely expert-delivered. Her expertise about what does or doesn't work for her is never actively drawn upon to shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerges spontaneously ('I don't want to die,' 'I'll send it tonight') but is rarely evoked or deepened. No open questions explore her reasons for change, and her final hopeful 'maybe' goes unreinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without confrontation or amplification, which is a strength. However, no double-sided reflections or strategic pivots are used, so ambivalence is left largely unaddressed rather than resolved.
