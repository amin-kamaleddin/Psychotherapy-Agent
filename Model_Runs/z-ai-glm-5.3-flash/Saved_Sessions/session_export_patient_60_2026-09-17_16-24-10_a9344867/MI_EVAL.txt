# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental, respectful stance maintained despite defensiveness and minimization. Affirmations present ("that show responsibility"), but somewhat generic and occasionally procedural in tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ("You sound really frustrated…"), but often immediately followed by multiple questions, diluting impact. Emotional depth limited — did not explore identity around partying or fear of the "alcoholic" label.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on target behavior throughout. No topic drift; logical progression from exploring → options → commitment → confidence → concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Joint planning in places (Jason edited the statement, negotiated check-in timing), but counselor repeatedly offered solution menus and drafted materials rather than eliciting Jason's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly acknowledged Jason's choice; refusals accepted without pushback. However, multiple questions per turn created subtle pressure, and pre-packaged solutions reduced Jason's authorship of his plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence ruler used effectively; Jason's motivations (housing) elicited. But much change content originated from counselor menus/drafts; missed opportunities to explore why housing mattered or the discrepancy between self-image and the incident.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor drafted the meeting statement, roommate text, and option menus; Jason largely chose among counselor-generated solutions, tilting expertise toward the counselor rather than the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocation of concrete commitment language ("send it tonight," "we leave by 12 no matter what"). Confidence ruler well used. Limitation: options largely counselor-generated rather than client-evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, but did not skillfully roll with or reframe it ("not getting caught," "cash grab"). Some sustain talk absorbed into summaries; little active reframing of blame or minimizing.
