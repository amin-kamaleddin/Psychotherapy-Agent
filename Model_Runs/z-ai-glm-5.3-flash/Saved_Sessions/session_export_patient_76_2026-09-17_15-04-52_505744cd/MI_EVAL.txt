# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; normalized the client's reliance on cutting without disapproval. However, repeated affirmations ('You're willing to try this...') made warmth feel somewhat formulaic rather than individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of exhaustion and ambivalence were present, but they often ran together with safety questions and problem-solving, diluting the felt sense of understanding. Deeper emotional pain, hopelessness, and the meaning of the cutting were acknowledged but not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior throughout: safety assessment, concrete safety plan construction, and follow-up. No meaningful drift — exemplary goal-directed structure for a high-risk presentation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The safety plan was genuinely co-constructed, with the client's edits fully integrated and permission sought before suggestions. Some rapid-fire, checklist-style turns briefly shifted power back toward the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were explicitly and repeatedly affirmed; coping options were offered as a menu the client could freely accept or reject without pressure or moralizing. The client fully owned the plan's content and implementation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Practical, client-generated content was elicited (resistance strategies, support person, implementation ideas), but motivation itself was not evoked — her reasons for change, values, and hopes were left unexplored. Change was largely behavioral commitment rather than internally generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client was repeatedly positioned as the expert, and her contributions genuinely shaped the plan (subtle text, cover story, mirror placement). Some rapid multi-question turns still carried an assessment quality that narrowed client-led direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully capitalized on small shifts, reinforcing commitment language as the client moved from doubt to 'I'll try it tonight.' Concrete future steps were elicited, but deeper change talk about her own reasons for change was underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Doubts were acknowledged without amplification using double-sided reflections, but there was limited strategic redirection toward the client's strengths or past successes. Sustain talk was softened but not consistently resolved.
