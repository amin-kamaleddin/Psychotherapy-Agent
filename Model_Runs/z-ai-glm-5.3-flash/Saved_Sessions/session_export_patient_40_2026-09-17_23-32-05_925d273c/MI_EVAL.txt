# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and nonjudgment, normalizing ambivalence and affirming Elias's self-knowledge. However, no explicit affirmations of his courage or three months of sobriety, which would have deepened warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, repeatedly capturing both sides of Elias's ambivalence and emotional nuance (pride, shame, the double-edged quiet). He repeatedly elaborated, indicating he felt understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on maintaining sobriety through cravings, progressing from coping with urges to preparation, early warning signs, social support, and a concrete commitment. Each exchange built toward the change goal without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly framed the work as joint ('let's figure it out together') with co-exploratory questions, and each response was incorporated into the next step. The final plan was entirely co-constructed from Elias's own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Elias's ownership and imposed no advice; the middle-path disclosure idea was entirely his own formulation. His comment 'Nobody's asked me that before' reflects choice-driven exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never argued for change; questions drew out Elias's own reasons, hopes, and commitment language. This culminated in his spontaneous self-affirmation: 'Three months is something, right?'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located in the client ('You know your cravings better than anyone'), and Elias's ideas visibly shaped the entire plan. The psychiatrist amplified his insights rather than supplying solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and developed change talk through open questions, leading Elias to concrete plans (garage night, sister's text), commitment language, and self-affirmation. Consistently handed the pen to Elias rather than supplying answers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk around pride and fear of disclosure was acknowledged without reinforcement, using double-sided reflections and reframing pride toward change. Occasionally the psychiatrist lingered on sustain talk (e.g., exploring 'proving it alone') rather than pivoting sooner.
