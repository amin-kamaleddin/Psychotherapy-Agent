# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard ('I'm not here to tell you to worry,' 'you're worth it'), with no judgment of the client's alcohol minimization; the drinking ritual is respected as meaningful.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are complex and emotionally attuned, capturing both sides of ambivalence ('Both things can be true') and the meaning of the drinking ritual ('the moment your brain finally stops, the quiet').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behavior throughout, progressing logically from exploring concerns to examining the drinking ritual, generating alternatives, and concrete maintenance planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operates between equals—asking rather than telling, co-constructing the follow-up plan, and treating the client's lived experience as the primary source of solutions. The client's closing remark ('I actually figured some of it out myself') confirms experienced power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly reinforced ('on your terms,' 'no judgment either way'), the plan is entirely client-generated, and outcomes are framed as information rather than success or failure. The client directly confirms: 'Thanks for not just telling me to quit everything at once.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All reasons for change come from the client (his father's pill bottles, his wife, protecting the quiet time), and strategic questions elicit and amplify change talk, culminating in strong commitment language ('Let's do this').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within the client, whose own idea (walk-before-beer) becomes the core of the plan. The closing affirms ownership: 'a plan that's entirely yours.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation via open questions ('What would paying a bit more attention look like?') that elicit and then reflect and amplify the client's own change talk and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with acceptance and never argued with; the clinician validates the underlying need for the ritual and redirects ('What is it about that time that matters most to you?'), and the reframe 'maybe the beer doesn't have to do all the work anymore' neutralizes resistance.
