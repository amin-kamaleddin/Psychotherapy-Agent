# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with some affirmations ('That absolutely counts,' 'well done'). However, warmth is thin, and moments of shame or doubt (e.g., 'Is that bad? Using you like that?') receive only brief validation before returning to checklists.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections are adequate ('That sounds really hard — understandable to feel stuck'), but empathy stays superficial and is quickly eclipsed by advice-giving. Deep emotional content like Hana's chest-tightening overwhelm, fear of embarrassment, and self-distrust is rarely reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (exam avoidance) with logical progression from micro-task to daily plan to implementation intentions. No meaningful topic drift, though the focus is action-oriented rather than motivational.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana's input genuinely shapes specifics (task choice, 7pm timing, reward, check-in), but the session's framework is unilaterally imposed. The counselor hands down protocols without exploring her ideas and dismisses her stated overwhelm rather than adjusting collaboratively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are limited but real instances of choice ('Want me to guide the breathing now or help pick today's exact task?'). Overall, the style is highly prescriptive — advice given without permission and little reinforcement of Hana's ownership or agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor makes little effort to elicit Hana's own motivation — no open questions about her reasons, hopes, or confidence. Her motivation surfaces spontaneously ('weight lifted,' 'want that presentation feeling') but is never reflected, amplified, or built upon; instead the counselor supplies reasons and strategies for her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor occupies an expert stance, with nearly every turn consisting of prescriptive step lists and templates. Hana's expertise is barely drawn upon — her presentation success is recycled as counselor's evidence rather than honored as a resource she owns.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk is almost entirely client-generated and rarely evoked or deepened — no open questions like 'What would passing mean to you?' Responses to change talk are generic ('well done') followed by more instructions, and Hana's strong closing statement ('I want to prove I don't need luck') gets no reflective response.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Hana voices substantial sustain talk ('wants to push the exam date back,' 'I'll chicken out again,' 'nervous I'll skip it tomorrow'), which the counselor essentially ignores. There are no double-sided reflections, no affirming of ambivalence as normal, and no exploration of the part that wants to postpone — sustain talk is simply bypassed with more task instructions.
