# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth, reframing self-critical statements nonjudgmentally ('It doesn't sound silly at all'). Falls short of 5 due to lack of explicit affirmations like 'I appreciate you sharing this.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, complex reflections that go beneath the surface ('That contrast has to sting'; 'it happens *to* you rather than something you choose'). Would need deeper unpacking of the underlying shame/self-criticism for a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, disciplined focus on returning to the gym, with each turn advancing a specific agenda from past successes to defining success. No topic drift while remaining client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided: questions build directly on Rafael's answers (wife's errands → Saturday; 'I always go too big' → small workout). Nothing is imposed and the session visibly bends toward his contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong ownership language ('You're the expert on your own life,' 'You know yourself best'), with all plan elements coming from Rafael. Lacks explicit permission-giving for him to choose not to act or revise the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question is open-ended and designed to draw out Rafael's own reasons and definitions, resulting in him generating the schedule, bag strategy, and note concept. Motivation is drawn from within rather than supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in the client, and Rafael's contributions materially shape the session. Minor point: some affirming statements are declarative rather than co-explored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions elicited strong change talk ('I walk out feeling better,' 'Maybe that's doable,' 'proving I can start something and actually show up'), each consistently reflected and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never dwelled on or amplified; each instance pivoted toward change while honoring the difficulty (e.g., 'you're the one who knows what a realistic plan would look like').
