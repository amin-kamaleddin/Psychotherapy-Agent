# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental regard maintained throughout. Maria's time pressure, skepticism, and resistant tone met with patience rather than disapproval. When she said "I don't love being cornered like that," counselor responded "part of you doesn't want to feel pushed into anything. That's completely fair." Her verdict ("My verdict, remember") explicitly respected; closing reflections honored her courage.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurate and emotionally deep—both content and feeling reflected (e.g., "part of you has real questions about the safety of this medication"). Pivotal reflection "you weren't researching for fun, you were researching because part of you already knew the afternoons were getting worse" was confirmed by Maria's own response ("that's a little too perceptive for one session"). Final reflection distinguishing care from fear captured the emotional core of her ambivalence.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Session kept firmly on inhaler adherence despite client's resistance and hurry. Each response advanced toward the target behavior: exploring barriers, addressing safety concerns, offering education, defining measurable markers (rescue puffs at work, cough-free nights), and establishing a follow-up timeline. No drift; culminated in a concrete, time-bound plan.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Interview functioned between equals. Counselor asked rather than prescribed, checked understanding ("What's your reaction to hearing that?"), and co-constructed the follow-up ("Does that sound like something worth your time?"). Reciprocal "Deal on my end too" formalized the partnership; Maria's critique of prior medical encounters was answered with genuinely shared decision-making.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Choice reinforced repeatedly—menu offered (written question list vs. reviewing research now), limits honored ("I'm not promising every day"). Counselor supported independence in disclosure ("keeping the results from your husband... that's your call entirely"). No pressure or coercion; Maria's condition of being heard next time accepted as a mutual deal.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Motivation drawn almost entirely from the client. Open questions elicited her own reasons (fear of long-term effects, worsening afternoons, husband's complaints) and her own solutions (puff after brushing, three days a week). Summary—"you came in skeptical and you're leaving with an experiment you designed yourself"—captured the evocative approach; her final self-insight and commitment to self-monitoring demonstrate motivation elicited from within.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Client explicitly positioned as expert ("You're the expert on your own life"; "bring your own verdict"). Client contributions substantially shaped the session—her demand for honest information determined the educational content, and the entire plan (toothbrush, three days a week, her own markers) was her creation.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Change talk skillfully evoked: "What would make managing this feel doable for you?", "What would a difference look like to you?", "What's your gut saying they'll show?" Client's language escalated from "I'm listening" to concrete markers and "testing it my way? That I can get behind." Counselor also affirmed her honesty about rescue inhaler use.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Sustain talk (forum skepticism, fear of side effects, "don't make me regret it") acknowledged without amplification and redirected. Double-sided reflections validated while pivoting toward change ("I hear that you want to feel better AND the inhaler hasn't felt worth the hassle"). Educational reframe—"the inhaler may actually protect what you're worried about"—resolved ambivalence with information Maria requested rather than argument.
