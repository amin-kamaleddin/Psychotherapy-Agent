# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor conveys unconditional positive regard throughout, affirming Alexei repeatedly and validating both sides of his ambivalence without judgment. The closing 'the door's always open — not because something's wrong' reinforces unconditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is consistently accurate, from the opening reflection capturing both sides of Alexei's pull to later reflections on feeling ('rattled that night too'). Each summary builds on Alexei's exact words, showing deep understanding of his perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear focus on avoiding alcohol to protect soccer/scholarship goals and guides progressively from motivation to concrete strategies to a specific commitment. Each response advances the conversation toward change without wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is fully collaborative, with the counselor asking rather than telling and building each step on Alexei's responses. Alexei's own ideas determine the session's direction, exemplifying equal-partner work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supports choice, offering options while never imposing advice — every idea originates with Alexei. Ownership is explicitly credited ('That came from you, not me').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client, eliciting Alexei's connection between drinking and his college dreams and his own success criteria. Alexei's statement that the session was 'more useful than I expected' because it was about soccer, not warnings, is direct evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly conveys that expertise resides in the client ('You know your world best,' 'That's yours, not mine'). Alexei's own ideas — the excuse, practicing with his buddy — become the entire plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions like 'what would tell you it actually worked' skillfully elicit commitment language ('That I can do,' 'I've got this'). The counselor highlights Alexei's own reason for change rather than imposing it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids reinforcing minimization and redirects initial hesitancy toward goals and plans, but handles sustain talk somewhat passively through redirection rather than explicit double-sided reflections that deliberately soften ambivalence.
