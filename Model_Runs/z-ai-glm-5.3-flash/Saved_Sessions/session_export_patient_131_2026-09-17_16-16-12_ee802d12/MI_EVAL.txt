# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with consistent affirmations of Wiremu's efforts ('that shows commitment,' 'good on you'). Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, and his fear of slipping up was not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured his core tension between commitment to his kids and fear of losing independence. Deeper emotional reflections on the grief of fortnightly visits and shame around the violence were limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on target behaviors (alcohol, program engagement, living situation) with no drift. Each turn built momentum toward a concrete, time-bound plan that Wiremu fully owns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely equal partnership, with the agreement co-constructed from Wiremu's own terms ($150 board, chosen chores, Mum on check-ins). Occasionally slipped into directive mode by supplying full scripts and templates before Wiremu drafted his own version.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Wiremu's ownership ('it's your move for the kids') and offered genuine choices between practice and drafting. Embedded his autonomy directly into the agreement and respected his preference to skip further role-play.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out Wiremu's own reasons and commitment through open questions and reframes ('I'm done with that... I want to be a dad they're proud of'). Confidence evocation was thinner — his 'seven out of ten' confidence rating and past successes went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated Wiremu as the expert on his own life, with the trial idea, check-ins, and written agreement originating from or shaped by him. At times moved quickly into providing scripts and templates, shifting expertise back toward the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and the pivotal reframe ('a step toward your goals — rather than a step back') elicited strong commitment language ('this is my move, not anyone else's'). Missed opportunities to deepen confidence talk, such as exploring what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk about feeling 'like a teenager' without amplifying it, then effectively converted ambivalence into problem-solving. The redirection was sometimes quick; more double-sided reflections validating his autonomy concerns would strengthen this.
