# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with validation ('That's a completely fair point') despite the patient's initial irritability. Warmth occasionally felt formulaic and past failed attempts were not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including a skilled double-sided reflection capturing the patient's ambivalence about weight and time. Deeper emotional content, such as the fear behind 'at my age, that's... it makes you think,' went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, progressive focus on the eating behavior throughout, moving from exploring barriers to a concrete, measurable commitment. The closing questions channeled momentum into specific action steps and a review.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine partnership, adapting to the patient's efficiency-driven accountant worldview (KPIs, reviews, balancing the ledger). Plan details, metrics, and review schedule were co-constructed with the patient's contributions driving direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed choices as the patient's own, with the plan entirely client-generated ('which one feels like it could actually survive a bad Tuesday'). Autonomy could be strengthened by affirming his ownership of anticipated setbacks, not just his problem-solving skill.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited almost entirely from the client through open questions rather than lecture or advice. His closing statements ('I'm leaving with something I can actually run,' 'I'll bring the numbers') demonstrate highly successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly located expertise within the client ('You know your schedule better than anyone') and used his professional identity as a resource. Key plan elements—the lunch swap, shop rule, and KPI—were all patient-generated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and amplified the patient's own change language, highlighting 'I could walk round the block first' as his idea. Strong commitment language emerged ('That's the one that balances,' 'Five decent lunches out of seven. That's the KPI').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about time and tax-season derailment was acknowledged rather than argued against, with a well-placed double-sided reflection. Direct questions about barriers ('What does that prep actually look like... the risk is late nights') briefly amplified sustain talk before successful redirection.
