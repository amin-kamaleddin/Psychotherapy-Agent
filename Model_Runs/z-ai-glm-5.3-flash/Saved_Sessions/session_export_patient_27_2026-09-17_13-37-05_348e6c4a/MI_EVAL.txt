# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental but warmth is thin, with few genuine affirmations and checklist-style safety screens that interrupt rapport-building.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal and superficial; the counselor skips emotional content (e.g., fear of being seen as 'sick') and pivots to practical options rather than reflecting Daniela's feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on disclosure and safety planning with reasonable strategic movement, though direction sometimes tips into premature action orientation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor operates largely as an advice-dispenser, supplying scripts and rewriting Daniela's own words rather than jointly exploring; power-sharing is limited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive elements exist (offering options, honoring her pacing), but relentless prefab solutions, 'Want me to...?' funnels, and pushing send-planning after boundary statements undermine autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor rarely draws motivation from the client, preferring to supply scripts and reassurance; open-ended evocative questions are almost entirely absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A directive expert stance dominates, with heavy use of pre-written scripts and message rewrites that override the client's authorship despite occasional shaping by her ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Strong spontaneous change talk ('I'm almost excited,' 'a whole plan forming') goes unreflected and unaffirmed; no deliberate evocative questions were used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ambivalence and sustain talk are sidestepped rather than addressed; opportunities for double-sided reflections (e.g., 'I like how things are now') are ignored in favor of practical pivots.
