# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard despite the client's guarded affect and stigmatized reentry context. No labeling, moralizing, or judgment was present, with affirmations honoring the client's honesty and vulnerability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and deepened across the session, capturing both surface content and underlying meaning. Complex double-sided reflections precisely tracked the client's ambivalence and internal shifts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on target behaviors while allowing specific steps to emerge collaboratively. Each exchange advanced a coherent arc from past successes to a concrete plan, without seeming rushed or imposed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was a genuine two-way construction, with the counselor checking in and building on the client's ideas rather than directing. The client moved from monosyllables to fully co-authoring his next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and self-direction were actively reinforced throughout, culminating in explicitly handing control of the treatment focus to the client. The counselor never pressured or persuaded, and even the mandated status was reframed as a personal benefit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rather than supplying reasons to change, the counselor drew out the client's own motivations — mattering, family, stability — and reinforced them. The client's shift from obligation-based attendance to a self-generated, specific plan is the strongest evidence of effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned the client as the expert on his own life, and all session content originated from the client. The counselor explicitly avoided the expert stance, framing the client's past success as evidence of his own capabilities.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout domain: the counselor consistently evoked, highlighted, and amplified the client's own change language. The trajectory from obligation talk to self-generated, specific commitment language was directly attributable to skilled evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was effectively redirected without dismissal, using double-sided reflections and reframes toward agency. Some sustain talk was repeated back at length rather than briefly acknowledged before pivoting — minor room for improvement.
