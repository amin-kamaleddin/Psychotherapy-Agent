# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard and nonjudgment, explicitly removing judgment when Maya feared being seen as a failure. Warm and respectful throughout, even with a guarded teen mentioning underage drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured Maya's ambivalence and her 'careful rather than quit' stance. Reflections sometimes stacked with multiple questions, and deeper emotional themes (fear of bailing on friends, parental pressure) were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and its academic/weekend consequences from start to finish. Strong goal-directed momentum from exploration to a concrete plan and scheduled Monday follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session felt like joint problem-solving with co-constructed plans and negotiated check-in logistics. At times the counselor presented solution menus rather than eliciting Maya's ideas first, making some exchanges feel counselor-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Maya's ownership ('You're in charge of what you try') and accepted her harm-reduction preference over quitting. Setbacks were framed as information rather than failure, protecting her sense of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from Maya's own goals, prior effective strategies, and self-generated confidence ratings and backup plans. Points deducted because substantial strategy content was counselor-provided and change talk centered on the concrete plan rather than deeper values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Maya as the decision-maker and accepted her renegotiation of the plan ('around midnight'). However, frequent pre-packaged option lists (e.g., eight strategies at once) leaned toward an expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change language via open questions and a confidence ruler, with Maya generating her own plan and expressing growing confidence (7 to 7.5). Slightly limited by the counselor supplying many options rather than having Maya generate more herself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally and redirected toward harm-reduction goals aligned with Maya's values, avoiding confrontation about abstinence. Long lists of counselor suggestions occasionally diluted the pivot back to Maya's own reasons for change.
