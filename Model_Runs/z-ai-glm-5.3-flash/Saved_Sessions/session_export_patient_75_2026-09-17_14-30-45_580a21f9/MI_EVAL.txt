# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, and the client explicitly reported feeling heard. However, affirmations were scarce and generic, with little individualized validation of her feelings of being stuck.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal; the counselor repeatedly pivoted to information-giving instead of reflecting the client's emotional disclosures (e.g., 'it's been really difficult feeling stuck,' 'I'm still nervous'). Understanding was asserted rather than demonstrated through accurate reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on asthma management and narrowed productively to a step-down plan, diary, and appointment preparation, with a clear recap and safety-netting. Direction was imposed topically rather than negotiated with the client's own goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was partial collaboration through offering choices and asking permission for role-play, which was genuinely useful. However, the underlying content (questions, criteria, plans) was generated expert-to-layperson, inviting the client's input only at the margins.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered menus of options and did not force commitments. Still, the session was dominated by expert recommendations and pre-written materials, with little explicit reinforcement of the client's ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was assumed to reside in information and preparation rather than in the client; open questions were rare. The client's spontaneous willingness to try was never elicited, reflected, or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor largely assumed the expert role, delivering long didactic blocks and pre-made scripts without first eliciting the client's ideas. Her contributions shaped logistics but not the clinical content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client spontaneously offered change talk ('maybe I'd be willing to try'), but the counselor did not deliberately evoke, reflect, or deepen it. There were no open questions about reasons for change or importance/confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not reinforce sustain talk and offered some corrective reframing of the fear of dependence, which softened anxiety over time. However, sustain talk was often met with persuasion-by-information rather than double-sided reflections, risking reactance.
