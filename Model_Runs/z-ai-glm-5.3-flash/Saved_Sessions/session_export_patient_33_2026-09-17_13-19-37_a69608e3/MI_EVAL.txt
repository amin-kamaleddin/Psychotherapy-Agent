# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and nonjudgment, especially in affirming Diego's choice not to quit and screening appropriately for safety. Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, lacking deeper individualized acknowledgment of the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Understanding was accurate but content-level and checklist-driven, rarely reflecting Diego's emotional experience. Missed opportunities included responding to his 'desperate' needle-sharing disclosure and his ambivalence about liking the drug's effects.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on harm reduction throughout, with each turn advancing concrete behavioral goals. Direction was maintained via information delivery rather than MI-guided clarification of the client's own goals, making the session feel somewhat mechanical.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently sought Diego's preferences and tailored resources and scripts to his stated needs, letting him define the agenda. Limited by information-dump style responses and the stumble of offering then retracting live address lookups.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: explicitly honored Diego's choice to focus on safer use rather than cessation, offered options at every decision point, and used no coercive language. Diego's surprise at not receiving a lecture confirms he experienced genuine freedom of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The goal question drew out Diego's harm-reduction motivation and high readiness, but the approach was mostly supplying information rather than evoking. No elaboration questions about his reasons, values, or readiness were used to develop his own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A consultative stance with repeated requests for Diego's input, but practical content was delivered in an expert-driven, prescriptive format rather than co-constructed. Diego was not explicitly positioned as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego generated substantial change talk independently, which the clinician captured but did not cultivate. Missed chances to elaborate his 10/10 readiness, reflect or affirm his change talk, and explore confidence or what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was tolerated without argument or reinforcement, with acceptance and pivot to the harm-reduction plan. Redirections were passive rather than skillfully strategic, using no double-sided reflections to reframe ambivalence.
