# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout; Ryan's minimization and party lifestyle are normalized rather than criticized, and his efforts are affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture his ambivalence, but deeper emotional meaning — the fear behind 'maybe I have a drinking problem' and the police incident — goes unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus with a concrete weekend plan and purposeful structure, though the session moved to rehearsal quickly and the role-play turn confusion ('you did my line for me') briefly derailed flow.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructed: permission-seeking, menu of options, joint role-play, and Ryan supplying the actual words for his plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice consistently reinforced through option menus and acceptance of 'no promises'; Ryan ends feeling ownership of a self-made plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers and rehearsal elicited Ryan's own values and commitment, but the tentative drinking-problem disclosure was never explored and the counselor sometimes supplied answers rather than eliciting them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ryan's contributions decisively shaped the plan — his strategy choice, preferred phrasing, and layered-defense insight were all affirmed as his own.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of rulers, open questions, and role-play that generated commitment language and raised confidence from 6-7 to 8 across the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but quickly redirected to solutions; however, minimizing statements like 'one bad night' were reflected without double-sided reframes to strategically soften them.
