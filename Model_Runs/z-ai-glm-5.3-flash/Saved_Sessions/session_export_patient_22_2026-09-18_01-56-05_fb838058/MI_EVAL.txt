# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently shows acceptance and respect, reinforcing the patient's autonomy ('You get to set the terms here') without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections repeatedly capture the patient's meaning, e.g., linking drinking to social anxiety and post-breakup loneliness, making her feel deeply understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on behavior change around drinking, guided by open questions that lead toward concrete, patient-generated plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change strategies are elicited from the patient rather than imposed ('what do you imagine might work for you?'), with the clinician acting as a guide.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician emphasizes patient choice throughout, framing one Tuesday as 'a choice, not a promise about forever' and affirming she sets the pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient articulates her own motivations and solutions—'two drinks, then water,' the slow-sip tactic—all drawn out through skillful questioning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange feels like a joint problem-solving effort, with the clinician asking rather than telling and honoring the patient's self-knowledge about her social world.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is abundant and clinician-elicited: 'maybe Tuesday nights don't need to be drinking nights,' 'I can at least do it once,' and commitment to telling Marisol.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ambivalence is addressed non-confrontationally; sustain talk ('I don't wanna quit altogether') is validated and reframed as reasonable self-pacing rather than resistance.
