# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively sought the patient's own perspective and repeatedly affirmed her autonomy ('this is your call and your timing'), even gently redirecting after her unsolicited advice to her sister surfaced. Minor deduction because the clinician offered a specific suggestion (nicotine replacement) framed as her own idea.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently strong empathic reflections, e.g., naming the bind of the cigarette being 'both your comfort and your burden' and reflecting that the porch time is about 'ten minutes that belong to you.' Accurately captured sustain talk without arguing against it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, well-executed direction: began by distinguishing from prior lecturing, explored values (daughter, self-respect), then narrowed to a concrete experiment (porch, coffee, gum) with a follow-up plan. Transitions flowed naturally from the patient's own material.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative: 'What do you think a first step... might look like for you?' and 'I'm thinking out loud here' exchanges show the plan emerged from the patient. The clinician positioned himself as a curious partner, not an expert directing change.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong emphasis on patient choice: 'No buying, no signing up,' 'this is your call and your timing,' and positioning her as the one brainstorming and owning the plan. The follow-up appointment framing supports her agency rather than pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions consistently drew out the patient's own motivations ('what would that be like for you two?'), self-efficacy ('you've cut back before'), and solutions (the swap idea, the list). The patient explicitly noted 'nobody's asked it that way before.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was jointly constructed and patient-owned; the clinician summarized her ideas back and invited her to keep steering. The closing ('write it down... knowing you'll ask me next week') reflects shared commitment.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and deepened change talk: from values (daughter) to desired change, then commitment-flavored language ('small experiment, I'll see what happens'). Complex reflections and forward-looking questions ('what would that version of you be doing differently?') strengthened sustain talk into possibility language ('almost possible').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk expertly — reflected ambivalence without rebuttal, normalized readiness ('not asking you to be further along than that'), and let the patient resolve it herself. Never argued for change; even self-discipline doubts ('I start things and drop them') were met with curiosity.
