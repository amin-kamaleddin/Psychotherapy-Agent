# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout ('That sounds really heavy,' 'You did important work today, Mei'). Warmth is occasionally formulaic ('You've got this, Mei'), and there is no explicit normalization of setbacks or relapse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured Mei's emotional core (pregnancy fear, fear of following her father's path) and her ambivalence about the IUD. Reflections became thinner mid-session, shifting to option lists rather than exploring her deeper fear of becoming like her dad.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing drinking and improving contraceptive reliability from opening question to concrete planning. Every exchange advanced the agenda, culminating in a specific, measurable plan and a scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative elements (asking permission, offering menus, checking preferences), but the counselor supplied most content (LARC info, strategy lists, drafted text) while Mei reacted and adapted. True collaboration would begin by eliciting her ideas before offering suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice-affirming language was used and permission was asked before advising, but advice-giving was frequent and dense. Mei was often asked to pick from counselor-generated menus rather than generate her own options, subtly centering counselor expertise.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening evocative question and closing confidence scaling question were skillful, drawing out Mei's core motivations and change talk. The middle of the session relied on advice and scripts rather than eliciting and deepening Mei's own reasons, ideas, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positioned himself as the source of solutions, including long prescriptive lists and repeatedly drafting texts for Mei. Mei's own expertise drove the session, but mostly despite rather than because of the counselor's power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A confidence scaling question elicited Mei's shift from 4 to 6, and commitment talk was reinforced. However, change talk was mostly spontaneously generated by Mei, with missed opportunities to actively evoke and deepen it (e.g., exploring what raised her confidence from 4 to 6).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged but not actively softened or reframed; Mei's doubts were met with practical problem-solving lists rather than double-sided reflections. Notably, Mei generated her own reframe ('That doesn't feel like failing, right?') without counselor support.
