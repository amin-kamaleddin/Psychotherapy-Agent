# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout. Affirmed client's honesty and agency ("I really respect that you're asking directly… You've earned that kind of conversation"). Validated both sides of ambivalence ("Both of those feelings make complete sense") without judgment. Warmth was genuine and individualized to the client's family values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening. Complex double-sided reflection (calm vs. anger at the officer) was met with "Yeah… exactly that." The "your mom is your anchor" metaphor mirrored the client's own words, showing deep attunement to both frustration and underlying vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior (regaining phone privileges before release). Guided the session from venting → ambivalence exploration → concrete action planning ("ask calm, burn it off if it's a no, count down the weeks"). No topic drift; each transition followed from the client's own statements.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated between equal partners throughout. Asked permission before guiding ("Before we wrap up, I'm curious…"), solicited the client's ideas at every decision point, and co-constructed the final summary from the client's own words. Client's relaxed closing reflects a genuine alliance between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided directives entirely; framed all decisions as the client's ("you know the staff who do"). Final summary explicitly attributed control to the client ("a plan you built yourself"). Client's response ("Yeah… I did, didn't I?") demonstrates internalized autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation from within rather than supplying it — elicited the intrinsic motivator (mother), coping strategies (working out, staying busy), and the client's own plan. No advice-giving; suggestions were framed as questions. Result: client-owned motivation ("It feels different when it's my own plan").

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert ("You know yourself better than anyone in this building"). When asked about the call, appropriately noted limits and handed the problem back. Closing credit was entirely the client's ("you did the hard part yourself").

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic open questions ("What's worked for you before…?") evoked coping strategies and commitment language ("I can do three weeks," "I'll ask him tomorrow morning. Calm"). Prompts for planning elicited a concrete, self-generated plan. Final evocative question consolidated change talk effectively.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk; acknowledged the officer grievance without endorsing externalization, then pivoted toward what the client could control. Minor deduction: officer-focused content lingered for several turns before redirection; a slightly earlier pivot toward client agency would have strengthened this area.

---
**Overall Global Rating:** **4.9/5**
