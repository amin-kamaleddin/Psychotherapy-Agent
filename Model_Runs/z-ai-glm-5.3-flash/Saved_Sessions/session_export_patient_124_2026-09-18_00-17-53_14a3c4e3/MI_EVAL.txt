# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout: respected 'experiment, not commitment' framing, declined to moralize celebration smoking ('that choice stays yours'), and protected the client from judgment ('your business with your mom stays your business').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including the complex reflection that the client is 'stuck because the thing that helps has limits' and balanced reflections capturing both sides of ambivalence about weed vs. talking to Mariana.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on marijuana use as stress management and progressed steadily from exploring alternatives to a concrete plan with a one-week follow-up, with no drift off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative: brainstormed 'together,' co-constructed the check-in schedule, and the final plan (pinned contact, one text, journal backup) came entirely from the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced the client's ownership and choice, affirming the 'experiment, not a verdict' framing and explicitly promising 'no weirdness, whatever happens' rather than monitoring outcomes judgmentally.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from the client via evocative questions ('what would make this experiment feel like it worked?', 'which one feels most doable?'), eliciting her own reasons (cost, exams, self-trust) and self-generated plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert on her own life; her contributions shaped the entire session, with the counselor facilitating rather than directing the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked desire, ability, and commitment talk — from the opening question about what would need to change, to eliciting success imagery, drafting her first text, and converting vague intent into concrete steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk and reframed it effectively ('either way you get an answer'), but the closing sustain talk ('still not saying this whole weed thing is a problem') was respected rather than actively softened — a double-sided reflection tying stated downsides (cost, doubts) to her values could have deepened discrepancy.
