# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental regard throughout — validated Seamus's fatigue with repeated advice and welcomed his resistance ('you're allowed to just explore this'). Affirmed his resilience and intelligence without lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both surface content ('worn out from hearing the same message') and subtle meaning ('thirty years of mornings with that fag'). Consistently normalized his ambivalence and resistance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking while anchoring it to Seamus's teeth concern, established early and sustained throughout. Each exchange built momentum toward a concrete, self-generated step (skipping the morning cigarette).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully egalitarian — counselor disclaimed the expert role and framed work as joint inquiry. Seamus's own ideas (morning cigarette, crossword, wife's support) shaped the plan, woven in rather than replaced by a protocol.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy actively reinforced ('no promises needed,' 'that's come entirely from you'), with the plan framed as an optional experiment. Welcoming his disclaimers paradoxically enabled him to move toward change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew out Seamus's own motivations — food, fear of dentures, wife's concern, the morning ritual — through evocative questions about feelings and coping. Developed and deepened each piece of change talk rather than supplying reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Seamus as the expert ('you know your own habits better than anyone'), with all key plan elements originating from him. Problem-solving was placed squarely in the client's hands.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions ('what would it need to look like to feel doable?') produced escalating change talk — from 'cutting back would help' to 'maybe I can actually do this.' Each change statement was reflected and deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (pills 'wrecked' him, 'not committing') was acknowledged briefly, reframed as learning, and pivoted toward change without argument. Never reinforced the status quo; treated resistance as information about a workable plan.
