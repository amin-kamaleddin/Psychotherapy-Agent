# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental despite sarcasm and defensiveness, with validating responses like acknowledging feeling dismissed. Acceptance was somewhat formulaic, and the counselor sometimes rushed past Luis's skepticism about the medical system rather than fully honoring it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections and validation of feeling invalidated, but the session pivoted quickly to psychoeducation and planning. Deeper emotions — overwhelm, distrust of doctors, feeling dismissed — received only shallow, inconsistent reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on asthma management throughout, ending with a concrete 4-week trial and tracking plan with no topic drift. The focus was narrow and task-driven, occasionally at the expense of exploring motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration existed in moments — the plan was simplified based on Luis's feedback and his ideas were incorporated. However, the counselor often took an expert role with long explanations and pre-packaged plans before exploring what Luis wanted.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly framed everything as optional and accepted Luis's conditions ('four weeks, that's it') without any pressure. Fell short of 5 because the counselor supplied solutions rather than reinforcing Luis's ownership of generating them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client motivation emerged ('tired of feeling tight-chested', 'maybe it's worth a shot'), but much was elicited indirectly through psychoeducation. No open questions exploring Luis's values, hopes, or what feeling better would mean for his life.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and check-ins that respected Luis's input, but frequently assumed the expert role. Luis shaped the plan by pushing back rather than through the counselor's deliberate power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and capitalized on change talk, with Luis committing to a concrete trial ('maybe it's worth a shot') after an accessible explanation and trial frame. Affirmations of existing effort reinforced self-efficacy, though the counselor offered content rather than consistently asking Luis to articulate his own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not reinforce sustain talk but also did not actively soften or strategically redirect it. Luis's closing skepticism ('don't be surprised if it made no difference') received no reflective reframe or double-sided response — a clear missed opportunity.
