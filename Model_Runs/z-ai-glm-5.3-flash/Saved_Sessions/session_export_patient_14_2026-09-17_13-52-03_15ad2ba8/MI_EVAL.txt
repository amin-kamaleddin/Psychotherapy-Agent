# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor was respectful and nonjudgmental, apologizing for the wait and acknowledging the client's frustration. However, warmth was minimal, emotions were largely ignored, and there were no affirmations of the client's honesty, self-awareness, or willingness to try the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was nearly absent, with no complex reflections or paraphrasing of the client's perspective. The counselor skipped over meaningful disclosures (health fears, breathlessness) and repeated the same instructions verbatim despite the client's explicit feedback that he had heard it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear focus on the target behavior (weight/diet/activity) and steered toward a concrete, actionable plan — the session's main strength. Points were lost because the focus stayed on quick tactics rather than the client's underlying values, and repeated safety caveats diluted momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was largely expert-driven, with numbered options and behavioral prescriptions delivered with minimal elicitation of the client's own ideas. The client's stated knowledge of diet and exercise was never invited as a resource; the plan was essentially counselor-authored with client sign-off.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offering a menu of strategies and saying 'do whatever you can; even one check is progress' supported choice and lowered pressure. However, the overall style was prescriptive, with command-style instructions repeated despite the client twice saying he is 'not the breathing-exercise type,' and ownership was never explicitly returned to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There was virtually no evocation — no open questions about motivations, values, hopes, or confidence; all reasons and strategies came from the counselor. The client's spontaneous motivation ('The weight is something I'd like to get hold of') was never reflected or built upon, making this the weakest domain.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role for most of the session, presenting pre-packaged strategies with limited incorporation of the client's expertise. Some choice was offered, but the client's contributions did not meaningfully shape the session content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Andrei volunteered notable change talk ('The weight is something I would like to get hold of,' 'these small bits are the only way it fits around my schedule'), but the counselor never reflected, amplified, or deepened it. No open questions explored the client's own reasons for change or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing with the client's time-barrier narrative ('You're right — time is the barrier'), preventing confrontation. However, by immediately validating time as THE barrier and moving to tactics, ambivalence was never resolved; no double-sided reflections were used, making the handling neutral rather than strategically softening.
