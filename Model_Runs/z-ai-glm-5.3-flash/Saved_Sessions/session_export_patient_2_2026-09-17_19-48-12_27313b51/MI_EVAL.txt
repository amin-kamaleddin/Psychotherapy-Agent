# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard; honored the patient's alcohol boundary immediately without judgment or pressure. No lecturing, labeling, or moralizing, which the patient explicitly noticed and appreciated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and precise, capturing both practical and emotional layers of the patient's experience. The insightful reflection on whether it was the cigarette or the pause unlocked her key realization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on evening smoking reduction throughout, with each exchange advancing toward a concrete plan. Session concluded with a specific, measurable, time-bound plan and follow-up without ever becoming directive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative interaction with the plan built entirely from the patient's own suggestions. The patient set boundaries, chose the start date, and defined success criteria, confirming visible power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: control explicitly handed to the patient at every juncture with genuine choices offered. No advice was imposed and the alcohol boundary was fully respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicited motivation from within the patient through open, curious questions, with all change talk self-generated. Explicitly named the source of motivation as coming from the patient, not the clinician.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly located expertise in the client ('You know your life best'), and the plan came entirely from her own ideas. The patient's closing comment that she did the talking confirms the power-sharing was palpable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced the patient's own change language through the pause question, readiness ruler, and future-oriented success questions. Commitment-adjacent talk was elicited, reflected, and developed throughout — textbook quality.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced, argued with, or amplified, and ambivalence was held via double-sided reflections. The one area for growth is more active reframing of minimization (peer comparisons), which was simply dropped rather than gently reframed.
