# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly reframing five failed restarts as persistence rather than weakness. The patient's closing remark that he expected a lecture confirms he felt warm, nonjudgmental acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently captured both content and emotional nuance, e.g., the precise complex reflection of the guilt/shame cycle around the gym. Also validated the deeper 'what's wrong with me' feeling with care.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on returning to exercise, with every exchange building toward barriers, realistic days, and a concrete first step. Closed with a specific, measurable plan (Tuesday, bag in car, twenty minutes) and a check-in structure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The entire plan emerged from the patient's own answers, with the clinician explicitly positioning him as the expert and crediting his ideas back to him. The patient's comment that the clinician didn't 'just hand me a workout plan' confirms genuine partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was consistently reinforced with no persuasive pressure — the plan was entirely the patient's, and ownership of outcomes was returned to him ('next session is yours to share however it goes'). The patient explicitly noted this freedom felt new.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client via eliciting desired futures, the friend-advice reframe, and a values-oriented closing question. He moved from vague sustain talk to commitment language ('I can do that,' 'This Tuesday, then').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every substantive idea — days, the bag in the car, redefining the bar — came from Rafael and was consistently attributed to him. The final question actively transferred wisdom and power to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation using open questions about the desired future self and the 'advising a friend' technique, followed by strategic reflection of the patient's own change talk ('twice a week... I could actually manage that').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced and was effectively reframed (persistence reframe, capitalizing on 'bar too high'). Minor deduction: a few reflections elaborated the discouragement/guilt cycle slightly longer than necessary before pivoting toward change.
