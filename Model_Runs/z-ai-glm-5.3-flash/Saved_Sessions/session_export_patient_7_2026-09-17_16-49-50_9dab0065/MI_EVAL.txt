# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validating even hostile remarks rather than becoming defensive. Warmth occasionally felt scripted, with repeated safety caveats and reassurances diluting authenticity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Made consistent, reasonably accurate reflections that kept the client talking and drew out details about family conflict. Reflections were sometimes repetitive and wordy, and deeper feelings beneath her guardedness went unaddressed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kept a consistent focus on household conflict and how arguments are handled. However, momentum stalled in the middle stretch, with direction more about holding the topic than guiding toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered menu options and invited tweaks, but shifted into a directive stance when the client engaged minimally. The scripted responses were counselor-generated, and the client's own existing strategies weren't used as the foundation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The strongest domain: repeatedly emphasized choice, honored the request for silence, and offered a genuine menu of options. Repeated check-in offers after being told to wait slightly undermined it, and some options felt manufactured.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied by the counselor rather than drawn from the client, who merely selected and critiqued counselor-generated scripts. The client's existing coping language was a clear opportunity to evoke and refine her own strategy that was bypassed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Verbally acknowledged client expertise and let her set the pace, but the actual flow of expertise was counselor-centric. The client's contributions influenced pacing but not substantially the content of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Very little change talk occurred, and small openings (her wish for the conflict to 'just be left,' her existing coping style) were not deepened. The counselor supplied scripted lines instead of evoking the client's own motivation language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (blame-externalizing, pessimism) was not amplified or reinforced, which was appropriate. However, there was little strategic pivoting — no double-sided reflections or reframing to link complaints to the client's own goals.
