# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, normalizing ambivalence and affirming honest disclosures like the kept bottle and self-doubt. Affirmations occasionally feel formulaic and tied to plan-checking rather than deeper unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured the evening routine struggle and reframed self-questioning, but deeper emotional content — shame from the relapse and fear of returning there — was repeatedly bypassed. The session moved at the counselor's pace toward planning rather than dwelling with the client's emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on disrupting evening substance-use routines, with every exchange tied to concrete, time-bound action. The session maintained momentum from problem to a fully rehearsed plan with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client chose among options and shaped the plan (movie night, telling his wife), but the counselor supplied most expertise and solutions. Little open exploration of the client's own coping ideas occurred before the counselor offered menus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** No commands or pressure, and the client's refusal of accountability texts was genuinely respected. However, counselor-generated menus and quick suggestions subtly funneled the client toward the clinician's preferred action architecture rather than full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening question and confidence ruler drew out family-centered motivation and self-efficacy language, including the client's strongest change statements. However, values and future vision were rarely explored through open questions; the counselor suggested while the client reacted.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's expertise was acknowledged and he repeatedly selected from options, but the interaction often had an expert-driven flavor with counselor-drafted plans and phrasings. Power-sharing was present but partial.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited client change language ('I don't want to feel that way again,' 'That's where I want to stay') and moved confidence from six to seven with commitment-linked reasoning. Change talk was channeled into logistics rather than deepening the client's spontaneous motivations of family and avoiding shame.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly and redirected effectively, such as with the 'gimmicky' delay technique and accountability texts. However, 'I'm just one bad day from being right back there' was quickly softened rather than explored as material for change.
