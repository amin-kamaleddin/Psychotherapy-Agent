# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates warm, nonjudgmental regard with no labeling despite minimization ('it's not about not caring, because you clearly do'). Affirmations occasionally feel slightly effusive rather than precisely calibrated, and ambivalence or lapses could be more explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, with the double-sided reflection capturing her ambivalence well and tracking subtle emotional shifts. The reflection about her mother's complications pivoted quickly to praise rather than exploring the fear itself, and some reflections simply repeat client statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains unwavering focus on medication adherence, with each question building progressively from barriers to concrete commitment. No topic drift, and direction is achieved entirely through questions rather than directives.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative interaction where Selene's ideas shape every element of the plan — 'you've basically designed your own plan.' The tone is one of equal partners with the client's expertise consistently foregrounded.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced through questions like 'What feels like the right first step for you?' Nothing is imposed, and her request to not be let off the hook is honored without the clinician seizing control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited entirely from the client — her mother's story, dread of appointments, desire to prove it to herself — without the psychiatrist supplying reasons for change. Her self-generated plan and proactive relapse-prevention request demonstrate full ownership.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in the client ('That's your expertise showing, not mine'), with the entire plan originating from Selene. This is power-sharing at the highest level.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation and deepening of change talk ('What would make it stick?', 'which would feel like the bigger victory?'), yielding increasingly strong commitment language: 'Bottle tonight, conversation this weekend. I can do that.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without reinforcement, with effective pivots and a good double-sided reflection. However, re-asking the same question about the 'what's the point' voice gave sustain talk extra airtime; a sharper pivot would merit a 5.
