# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with validations of Evelyn's concerns and frustrations. However, affirmations are brief and formulaic, with no explicit affirmation of her strengths or courage in engaging.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures her ambivalence, frustration, and fears. Reflections sometimes gloss over emotional nuance, such as her fear of falling alone and resistance to the medical alert device.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use and fall risk, moving logically to a concrete if-then plan. Early didactic segments and a quick pivot to guidelines rather than agenda negotiation prevent a higher score.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites collaboration and checks agreement, but information is delivered in an expert-to-layperson format with Evelyn choosing from options rather than co-generating ideas. True two-way shaping emerges only later in the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks permission before sharing information, offers menus of options, and respects Evelyn's refusals. The if-then plan is largely drafted by the psychiatrist, with limited emphasis that Evelyn owns the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully uses likes/dislikes exploration and confidence rulers to elicit Evelyn's own reasons for change, with confidence rising from 6 to 7. Evocation is concentrated in the latter half, with desire and importance for change underexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids an authoritarian stance and asks for input, but much of the session features expert content delivery. Limited acknowledgment that wisdom about change resides with Evelyn.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes change talk through menu options and the confidence ruler, eliciting strong commitment language. Relies heavily on structured questions rather than open exploration of her own reasons and desire for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and occasionally reframes toward change, but responses are largely neutral acknowledgments rather than strategic double-sided reflections. Early information-giving may have amplified sustain talk.
