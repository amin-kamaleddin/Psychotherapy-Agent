# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental despite stigmatized behavior, with affirmations like 'That's honest and brave.' Praise was at times tied to plan compliance rather than unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections, but emotional themes like loneliness and fear of peer rejection were only briefly acknowledged before pivoting to problem-solving. These deeper feelings were under-explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary focus on the target behavior throughout, with every exchange building toward a concrete, dated plan. No topic drift at any point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan's choices shaped the plan, but the counselor repeatedly arrived with pre-packaged interventions delivered in rapid sequences. Jordan sometimes complied politely rather than co-creating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good use of choice-framing and respect for Jordan's decisions, including texting rather than rehearsing. However, rapid multi-item menus implicitly steered Jordan toward the counselor's framework.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and exception questions were used, and confidence rose from 5 to 8, but motivation was largely scaffolded by external structure. Deeper intrinsic values were never explored; even 'I'm choosing me tonight' was clinician-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acted more as expert coach than partner, supplying scripts, excuses, alarm cues, and even text wording. Jordan personalized some ideas, but the flow was largely clinician-generated solutions with client buy-in.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of readiness and confidence rulers, exception questions, and commitment requests; Jordan's change talk grew progressively stronger. Closing change talk was not reflected or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** No reinforcement of sustain talk and some double-sided reflecting, but doubts about feeling flimsy or losing fun were met with plan suggestions rather than reframing. The change side of ambivalence was never strategically reflected to tip the balance.
