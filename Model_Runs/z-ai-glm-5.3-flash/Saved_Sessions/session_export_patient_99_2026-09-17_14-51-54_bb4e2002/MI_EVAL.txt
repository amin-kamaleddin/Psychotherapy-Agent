# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a polite, nonjudgmental tone and affirmed Matteo's adaptations (e.g., using a chair for balance), but warmth was largely procedural and tied to rehab logistics. There was no affirmation of his honesty about struggling with exercises or acknowledgment of the referral context.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening stayed at surface-level paraphrases and quickly pivoted to planning questions. Rich emotional cues, such as Matteo feeling 'kind of useless' sitting home on Wednesdays, were never reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session's most significant failure: Matteo was referred for alcohol use flagged on screening, but the counselor never raised the topic even once. The entire session followed the client's initial deflection onto ankle rehab without ever linking back to drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative checking-in occurred ('Does that feel right?'), but the counselor designed the weekly schedule, exercises, and fallbacks in an expert role. Matteo shaped details (alarm, band placement) but not the overall structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and respected Matteo's decisions (holding off on texting Josh) without any pressure or coercion. Fell short of a 5 because the counselor heavily pre-structured the plan rather than letting Matteo generate his own options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was assumed rather than evoked — beyond a single confidence ruler, the counselor never elicited Matteo's own reasons for change or his values around soccer and social life. The approach was predominantly information-giving and planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offering choices conveyed some power sharing, and Matteo's preferences shaped plan details. However, the counselor frequently operated as the expert, prescribing the exercise list and schedule without explicitly inviting Matteo's expertise about his own routines and barriers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** One confidence ruler question elicited brief commitment language ('I could start tonight'), but the counselor did not reinforce, reflect, or deepen these statements. Missed opportunities included exploring why returning to soccer mattered to him or linking the plan to his values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Matteo's sustain talk ('haven't been doing the exercises like I should,' 'if I miss a day, whatever') was met with neutral acknowledgment rather than strategic softening. No double-sided reflections or reframing were used to pivot this ambivalence toward change.
