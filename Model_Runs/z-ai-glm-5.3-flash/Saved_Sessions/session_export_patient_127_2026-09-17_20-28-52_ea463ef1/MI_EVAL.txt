# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with no judgment about criminal history or the 'lazy' self-label. Affirmations were specific and genuine, and both sides of ambivalence were accepted, including normalizing potential relapse triggers.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that captured both sides of ambivalence and distilled the client's core insight about respect. Reflections consistently landed, with the client explicitly validating them ('that's it exactly').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful focus on desistance and legitimate employment, with each turn advancing the arc from exploration to a concrete first step, commitment consolidation, and relapse-prevention planning. No topic drift while remaining patient-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated as genuine dialogue between equals, with the counselor asking before reflecting and accepting the client's solutions without revision. Every element of the plan originated from the client, with the counselor eliciting and affirming rather than instructing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforced ownership and choice, never gave direct advice, and framed setbacks non-judgmentally. The client's statement that the decision 'feels like mine' is the strongest evidence of effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All meaningful reasons for change — respect, the porch, Fiona, self-respect — originated from the client in response to evocative questions. The final 'map I drew myself' demonstrates motivation and the plan fully reside within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located within the client throughout ('you know your situation better than anyone'). The plan emerged entirely from Timur's own contributions, exemplifying power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully targeted desire, ability, and commitment, evoking progressively stronger change talk ('One call. I could do one call this week', 'I've decided'). The counselor reinforced and amplified commitment language each time it emerged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally not reinforced, with effective double-sided reflections and reframing using the client's own counter-evidence. A few reflections briefly lingered on the frustration and rejection narrative before pivoting; quicker redirection toward change-side material would reach a 5.
