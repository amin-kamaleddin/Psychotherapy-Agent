# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, normalizing the client's worry and avoiding moralizing about underage drinking. Warmth was somewhat brief and task-focused rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moments of accurate understanding, but the style was heavily question-and-advice driven with sparse reflections. The client's rich ambivalence statement ('part of me says it's just normal stuff... part of me keeps going back to that bad practice') was never reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on alcohol use in peer contexts and progressed logically from screening to coping strategies to a committed one-month plan. Focus was more checklist-driven than values-anchored, missing chances to tie the plan to the client's identity goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided interaction where the client's input reshaped the plan repeatedly (personalized refusal line, girlfriend-as-ally). Early menu-dumping of pre-packaged lists rather than co-generating options kept it from a 5.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session's strongest domain: consistently reinforced the client's ownership, honored his objections (adapting tracking to private options), and validated his desire to decide. The final plan was entirely his own construction with non-punitive relapse framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated motivation as residing in the client, letting him set the agenda and generate his own solutions. Rarely used open questions to deliberately evoke and expand change talk; motivation emerged mostly as a byproduct of collaborative planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly handed decision-making to the client and respected his expertise about his social world, with his corrections genuinely shaping the plan. Early advice-heavy delivery of next-step lists tempered the rating.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created strong conditions for change talk and the client produced meaningful commitment language ('That's my three. I can live with that for a month'). However, the psychiatrist rarely reflected or deepened change talk when it occurred, moving instead to more suggestions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, but also did not actively soften or reframe it. The client's 'it's not a problem yet' was answered with score interpretation rather than a double-sided reflection linking his own concerns to his values.
