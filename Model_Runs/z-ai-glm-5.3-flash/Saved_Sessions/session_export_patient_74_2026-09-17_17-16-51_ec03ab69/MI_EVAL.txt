# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Linda's ambivalence as understandable and never lecturing despite her sustain talk. However, affirmations are somewhat generic and recap-heavy rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates strong reflective listening, accurately capturing Linda's core ambivalence between being a good example for her daughter and her need to unwind. Reflections tend to be summaries followed by questions, with limited exploration of deeper emotional meaning such as her stress or fear of failing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior (smoking and children's exposure) throughout, with every exchange tied back to the after-dinner cigarette or her role-model concerns. The session progresses logically from exploration to a concrete, specific plan without ever wandering off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner, co-constructing the plan from Linda's own idea (tea with her daughter) with consistent permission-seeking and choice. Some exchanges lean toward the counselor proposing multiple ideas in list form, drifting slightly toward advice-giving, though consent is always preserved.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Linda's ownership, framing everything as a low-stakes experiment rather than a forever promise and explicitly honoring her refusal to commit to quitting. There is no pressure, persuasion, or coercive language anywhere in the session, even when she reiterates she will not quit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from within using the values question, confidence ruler, and eliciting barriers and success markers from Linda herself. Evocation is sometimes procedural and logistics-focused rather than consistently deepening her values, hopes, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Linda as the decision-maker, asking permission before offering ideas and inviting her input on every plan element. The menu of options and repeated recaps slightly steer the agenda rather than letting her contributions reshape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through the values question, confidence ruler, and eliciting her own reasons, prompting meaningful responses like the tea idea and 'worth a try'. The counselor does not always deepen these moments, sometimes jumping to planning rather than amplifying her intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accepts sustain talk respectfully without arguing but does not consistently redirect toward change, and sometimes re-echoes her 'not quitting' statements in summaries, slightly amplifying sustain talk. Lacks double-sided reflections or strategic pivots using her own change talk to soften ambivalence.
