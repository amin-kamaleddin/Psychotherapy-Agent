# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and warm, with frequent affirmations ('that was brave to say it aloud') and normalized anxiety. Falls short of 5 because affirmations occasionally feel formulaic and the counselor pivoted quickly to options rather than fully accepting Daniela's autonomy statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured Daniela's core fears, including reframing Mia's hurt as caring rather than judging. Reflections were often brief and quickly paired with reassurance or option menus, limiting deeper emotional exploration of her vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout—every turn tied back to peer disclosure, progressing logically from readiness assessment to rehearsal to a concrete next step, with readiness rising from 3-4 to 6. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative, with Daniela's preferences (story style, Mia, the hurt scenario) visibly shaping the session and the plan co-constructed. The counselor still dominated content generation via scripts and menus rather than genuinely negotiated agendas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed choice and endorsed Daniela's pacing ('my call, my timing' met with 'Absolutely yes—draft it for next session'). However, choices were always counselor-constructed menus with embedded preferences, which subtly constrained the decision space.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used some genuine evocative techniques (readiness rulers, 'what would move you to a 4?'), but undercut them by supplying most of the content—scripts, replies, follow-up lines—so change language often originated from the counselor. Little exploration of Daniela's deeper motivations or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela's contributions shaped the session, but the counselor occupied the expert role substantially, supplying nearly all scripted language and structuring every turn as a choice among counselor-generated options. Partnership present but limited in genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Readiness rulers effectively evoked change talk and measurable movement (3-4 to 6), including commitment-adjacent drafting. However, the counselor frequently supplied the change-talk content themselves rather than evoking Daniela's own words, with few open questions about her reasons or values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally acknowledged sustain talk briefly without amplifying it, redirecting toward strengths and actionable steps. Missed opportunities for strategic double-sided reflections linking fears to Daniela's stated desire for disclosure to 'be no big deal.'
