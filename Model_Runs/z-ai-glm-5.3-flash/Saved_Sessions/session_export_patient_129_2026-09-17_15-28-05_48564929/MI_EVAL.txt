# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The  counselor  maintains  a  respectful,  nonjudgmental  tone  and  offers  choices  without  criticizing  Tyler's  use  or  peers.  However,  warmth  feels  somewhat  transactional,  quickly  pivoting  to  task-oriented  script  drafting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate  reflections  capture  Tyler's  frustration  and  fear  of  interrogation,  but  the  counselor  misses  deeper  emotional  content  like  loss  of  privacy  and  feeling  misunderstood.  Reflections  largely  serve  as  pivots  to  advice  rather  than  exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The  counselor  allows  the  session  to  be  redirected  entirely  to  mother-conflict  management  while  the  stated  clinical  target  —  substance  use  and  peer  influence  —  is  never  revisited.  Focus  is  maintained,  but  on  a  secondary  concern  without  linking  back  to  the  target  behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The  counselor  invites  Tyler's  choices  and  incorporates  his  script  edits,  showing  genuine  responsiveness.  However,  the  counselor  acts  more  as  a  coach  than  an  equal  partner,  supplying  scripts  wholesale  and  shaping  the  strategy;  Tyler's  input  influences  wording  but  not  direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong  autonomy  language  throughout,  including  repeated  menus  of  options  and  respect  for  Tyler's  revisions.  Falls  slightly  short  of  top  marks  because  rapid-fire  option  menus  sometimes  lead  Tyler  rather  than  letting  him  generate  ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation  and  ideas  come  almost  entirely  from  the  counselor,  not  the  client.  The  single  evocative  opener  is  abandoned,  and  Tyler's  own  reasons,  concerns,  or  ambivalence  about  substances  —  the  core  of  MI  with  a  precontemplative  adolescent  —  are  never  drawn  out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There  is  genuine  responsiveness  to  Tyler's  input,  such  as  incorporating  his  softened  script  line.  Still,  the  counselor  rapidly  assumes  an  expert  role,  drafting  and  coaching  rather  than  deeply  drawing  on  Tyler's  own  expertise  about  his  life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change  talk  is  rarely  evoked;  the  one  good  opener  ('If  you  could  change  one  thing...')  is  not  followed  up.  Dialogue  focuses  on  negotiating  with  mom  rather  than  Tyler's  own  reasons  or  motivation  regarding  substance  use,  which  is  never  revisited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The  counselor  rolls  with  resistance  well,  never  arguing  with  Tyler's  sustain  talk.  However,  sustain  talk  is  redirected  rather  than  strategically  softened  or  reframed  toward  ambivalence,  leaving  statements  about  his  peer  group  unexamined.
