# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Richard's anger and autonomy without criticism. Warmth occasionally felt formulaic, with limited explicit affirmation of his strengths beyond repeated 'that makes sense' responses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflected content accurately and used summaries, but reflections were largely intellectual restatements rather than emotional explorations. Key vulnerable moments (fear of losing his wife, guilt over dismissing her worry) were skipped over in favor of moving quickly to options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking and its relational/health context, progressively building toward a concrete plan (doctor visit, script for wife, exit cue). Slightly limited by early menu options framed around quitting-adjacent goals before Richard's own reasons were fully explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard co-authored his scripts and wording, but the interaction retained a consultant-like structure with the counselor proposing options and Richard mostly selecting or rejecting. True two-way brainstorming driven by Richard's own ideas was limited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently affirmed ('it's your choice,' 'I won't try to force anything') with repeated menus of options. A higher score would require emphasizing Richard's ownership of his capacity to change rather than only framing tasks as being 'for her.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited meaningful client-generated motivation, including Richard's own words about not wanting to lose his wife and volunteering concrete steps. However, his most vulnerable statements were met with more options rather than deeper exploration of his own reasons, hope, or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choice ('Which sounds most useful to you?'), but the session was heavily option-driven with counselor-generated strategies. Richard shaped the wording of scripts, but the underlying ideas were supplied rather than drawn from his own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Indirectly evoked meaningful change talk (doctor visit, planned conversation with wife, rehearsal commitment), but did not explicitly amplify, reflect, or consolidate these statements. Momentum largely came from the client rather than skillful evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided arguing with sustain talk, acknowledging Richard's resistance while pivoting toward what he valued. A brief double-sided reflection explicitly linking his love for his wife to possible health change would have strengthened this.
