# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains unconditional positive regard, explicitly normalizing the teen's experience ('you tried alcohol like a lot of kids your age do') while never judging the underage drinking. Repeated affirmations of the client's character and abilities with no disapproval or moralizing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections consistently and accurately capture both content and the social-emotional dynamics of being a 16-year-old. Not a 5 because some reflections summarize strategies rather than exploring the deeper emotional weight of the park fear and anticipated pride.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, progressive focus linking alcohol use to soccer and scholarship goals from the opening summary onward, with no drift off-topic. Strategically guides toward a specific target behavior with concrete next steps on the client's timeline.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functions as genuine joint exploration between equals, with the opening framing mutual inquiry and every step co-constructed through questions. The buddy plan, refusal phrases, and timeline all originate from the client and become the session's agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is woven throughout, with the plan framed as the client's choice ('what feels like the right next move for you, on your timeline?'). No pressure, persuasion, or directive advice appears anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The approach is entirely evocative rather than informative — no lecturing about alcohol risks, only eliciting the client's own reasons, values, and anticipated feelings. Actively develops change talk through future-oriented questions and articulation of what success would look like.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently locates expertise within the client, opening with 'You know your world better than anyone' and explicitly naming that the plan 'came from you, not me.' Every strategy originates from the client, with the counselor only amplifying and organizing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength — skillful open questions ('what would tell you the conversation with your buddy actually landed?') evoke increasingly strong change talk, culminating in 'do I want a beer more than soccer? Not even close.' Each instance is reinforced with affirmations that deepen the client's ownership.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well, reframing the park incident as 'a wake-up about what's actually at stake' and pivoting from peer-pressure concerns toward problem-solving. Not a 5 because a few moments of sustain talk are reflected back without an immediate pivot, allowing brief dwelling on obstacles.
