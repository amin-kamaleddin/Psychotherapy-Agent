# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, with affirmations like 'You're already trying to protect him' and validation of Elena's defensiveness without judgment. No labeling or moralizing despite her minimization, which is consistently reframed as effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures Elena's overwhelming situation, including complex reflections showing good perspective-taking. Falls short of 5 because reflections occasionally repeat the same 'torn' framing and don't always access deeper emotions like guilt or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation while appropriately acknowledging the exhaustion context, with each turn steering toward the target behavior. The session converges on a concrete behavioral plan, and Elena notes the fitting symbolism of messaging 'during my break.'

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely between equals, with Elena shaping every step and the plan co-created rather than prescribed. However, stretches of expert-driven psychoeducation (thirdhand smoke, NRT options) occur without asking permission first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced through explicit support statements, repeated choice menus, and framing the plan as 'an experiment, not a promise' in response to Elena's fear of commitment. The final plan is entirely hers, with no pressure applied.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from within using open questions, rulers, and exploration of what the break means, with motivation clearly rising from vague contemplation to a specific action plan. Not a 5 because the counselor also provides its own rationale (thirdhand-smoke education, pros of NRT) rather than relying exclusively on Elena's reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats Elena as the expert by letting her choose the agenda and asking what she wants to keep about the break; her preferences became the entire plan. Change is positioned as a co-created experiment rather than a prescription.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evokes change talk with open questions and importance/confidence rulers, and Elena's commitment grows measurably to a concrete plan. Not a 5 because the counselor doesn't always deepen change talk when it emerges, such as probing why importance spikes to a nine during infections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids amplifying sustain talk, reflecting minimization as protective effort and acknowledging barriers with neutral validation before redirecting toward options. Not a 5 because the session periodically revisits barriers without double-sided reflections to actively tilt the balance toward change.
