# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor was warm and nonjudgmental, directly addressing confidentiality fears, affirming Marcus's smart choices, and closing with 'I'm with you on whatever you choose.' Affirmations were somewhat brief and repetitive ('Good call') rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections were used early, but many were summary-style restatements rather than emotionally attuned responses. The counselor missed deeper feelings like fear of parental punishment, sensitivity about peer image, and feeling misunderstood by adults.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on weekend alcohol use throughout, from assessment to concrete harm-reduction planning. However, focus narrowed prematurely to shots, accepting Marcus's minimization ('that's not even really changing anything') without returning to his broader drinking pattern or values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor sought permission, invited brainstorming, offered to rehearse, and let Marcus select and author his own lines. Collaboration was somewhat asymmetric though, as the counselor supplied most of the content (menus of options and sample lines) with Marcus reacting rather than co-generating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear strength: the counselor repeatedly emphasized Marcus's control over sharing and choices, legitimized his rejection of formal limits, and let him build his own plan. It fell short of a 5 because rapid-fire option menus subtly steered choices toward counselor-generated ideas rather than client ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions, upsides/downsides exploration, and confidence rulers were used appropriately, and Marcus articulated his values. However, the counselor jumped to advice after key statements, never explored the discrepancy between his goals and drinking, left his insight about confidence unexplored, and relied heavily on counselor-supplied options rather than evoked motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing was present ('You choose how much to share,' 'do you want a different plan?'), and Marcus's picks shaped the plan. But the approach slid into an advice-heavy stance with long option menus delivered before asking what Marcus already knew worked for him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was evoked — skipping shots, acknowledging risk, and confidence rising from 7–8 to 8.5 with well-used confidence rulers. However, change talk centered narrowly on shots, key statements weren't deepened, and much motivation came from counselor's menus rather than desire/need/reason talk evoked from Marcus.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing and reframed toward Marcus's goals despite heavy sustain talk ('everyone does it,' 'my friends drink way more'). But reflections often repeated sustain talk verbatim, amplifying it, and no double-sided reflections were used to link sustain talk to change talk.
