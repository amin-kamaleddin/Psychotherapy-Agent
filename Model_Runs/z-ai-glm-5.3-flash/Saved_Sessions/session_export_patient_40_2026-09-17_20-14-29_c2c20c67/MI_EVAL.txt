# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with shame validated and normalized without condoning the behavior. Affirms the patient's efforts, e.g., 'You're doing more than just surviving those evenings.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, emotionally attuned reflections that go beyond surface content to deeper meaning (e.g., the image of sitting in the same spot, holding his daughter's warmth at arm's length). The patient repeatedly builds on these reflections, confirming their impact.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on recovery while building momentum: relapse reflection → values → strategies → identity change → concrete commitments → coping plan. No topic drift; the session progresses coherently toward strengthened commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided exchange with permission-style open questions and reflections woven from the patient's exact words. Every next step originates from the patient's own ideas, and summaries are co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ownership consistently reinforced through 'what feels right' framing and open questions; when the patient expresses hesitation about his two commitments, the clinician validates the cost rather than pushing. Choice and pace remain entirely with the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client; the clinician never supplies reasons for change. The pivotal question about 'maybe he's just who I was' elicited the patient's own values and commitment language, culminating in two voluntary commitments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient is explicitly positioned as the expert ('You know your patterns better than anyone'). No advice is given, and all plan elements originate from the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and deepened, with the pivotal identity question producing sustained change talk. Two concrete commitments (Saturday swimming, proactive calls to his brother) were fortified with barrier and coping planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubts are acknowledged with an effective double-sided reflection and redirected toward the evidence, without amplifying sustain talk. One point short of 5 because the 'waiting for the other shoe to drop' reflection briefly rests on the doubt before pivoting.
