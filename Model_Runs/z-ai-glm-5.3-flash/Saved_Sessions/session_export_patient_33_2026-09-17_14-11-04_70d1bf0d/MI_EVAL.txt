# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a nonjudgmental, respectful stance throughout, honoring Diego's request to avoid lectures and affirming his practical progress. Affirmations were somewhat generic and missed individualized strengths like his self-awareness about not wanting to die from his use.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed decent understanding through the housemate role-play and safety check-in, but missed several emotionally significant moments — Diego's 'I don't want to die from it' and 'the running costs more than the facing' went unreflected. Dense informational content crowded out deeper empathic listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on harm reduction, culminating in a concrete three-step plan and scheduled follow-up appropriate to Diego's preparation stage. Early safety-tips delivery was somewhat scattershot, and the counselor didn't explicitly summarize the emerging plan until Diego did so himself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functioned as genuine joint problem-solving, with Diego drafting his own scripts and deciding where to store the Narcan. The balance tilted toward counselor-as-information-provider, with long guidance blocks rather than co-constructing knowledge from Diego's experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choice, asked permission, and respected Diego's 'no lectures' boundary without imposing an abstinence agenda. Several responses defaulted to dense information delivery, and Diego's ownership of the change process could have been more explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego's motivation was largely pre-existing and emerged spontaneously; the counselor rarely used open questions to explore his values, reasons for change, or what staying safe means for his life. The deeper material about what Diego is escaping from was acknowledged but not lightly evoked before scheduling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Diego as the expert through power-sharing moves like requesting his ZIP code, offering role-play choices, and letting him draft his own message. The counselor still held most of the informational floor with long guidance blocks rather than eliciting what Diego already knew.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively supported Diego's strong commitment language through role-play practice, scripts, and a concrete three-part plan with a scheduled follow-up. Support was somewhat passive — the counselor didn't deliberately deepen his readiness by exploring what changed or what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided reinforcing sustain talk, pivoting to Diego's safety goals when he defended his use and affirming harm reduction when he stated he wasn't quitting. Limited use of double-sided reflections to help Diego work through his ambivalence between escape and safety.
