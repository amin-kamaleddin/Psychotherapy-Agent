# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with genuine affirmations and no shaming despite illegal behavior. Falls short of 5 due to brisk, task-focused warmth and lack of deeper, individualized affirmations of Jordan's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections are accurate and capture the peer-pressure dynamic. However, deeper emotional content (guilt, fear of being seen as a loser, loneliness, disappointing parents) is met with solutions rather than reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on shoplifting with an explicitly negotiated agenda and a concrete, time-limited goal. Deducted for staying purely tactical without connecting to underlying values and motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration moments include joint rehearsal and Jordan drafting his own text. But the counselor generates nearly all content while Jordan merely selects from it, resembling a consultant-client dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Consistent choice-based framing and normalizing slips as information are strengths. However, options come from counselor-authored menus, and there is no explicit affirmation that the change belongs to Jordan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Beyond the readiness ruler and one early question, evocation is minimal. Jordan's key motivations (guilt, parents, the jacket) surface incidentally and are met with tools rather than exploratory questions, conveying that motivation resides with the counselor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role, delivering pre-packaged advice, scripts, and numbered plans in nearly every turn. Check-in questions are largely rhetorical rather than genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler successfully elicited commitment language ('Maybe a six,' 'One weekend, I can do one weekend'). Missed opportunities to deepen this talk: no follow-up on why six rather than three, and no exploration of what the jacket goal means to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively neutralizes all-or-nothing sustain talk with practical reframes, e.g., 'you don't have to ditch them to change things.' Missed use of double-sided reflections, and one suggestion briefly collided with sustain talk.
