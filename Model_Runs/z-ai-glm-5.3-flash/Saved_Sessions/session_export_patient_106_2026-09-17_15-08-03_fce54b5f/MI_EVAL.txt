# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with explicit validation of the client's readiness level and honesty, evidenced by her 'thanks for not lecturing me' remark. Falls short of 5 because normalization of her ambivalence is not explicit and affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured her core dilemma (unwinding ritual vs. her son's health, protective instinct vs. needed alone time). Empathy weakens mid-session when the counselor shifts into options-listing and coaching mode, missing emotional cues about isolation, guilt, and self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear and sustained focus on smoking and the child's smoke exposure from opening to closing. Every exchange progressed toward a concrete plan with no topic drift, culminating in specific commitments and scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session was largely co-constructed — the client's input determined the final plan and the counselor explicitly sought her reactions. Power sharing was uneven at points, with the counselor supplying the option menu and behavioral hacks rather than drawing them from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent autonomy support: permission-seeking before advice, releasing pressure about quitting, and letting the client set limits and the next-visit agenda. Some advice-giving was directive in tone (bullet list of five options, suggested hacks), though the client was always free to accept or reject.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong use of open questions, values exploration, and confidence scaling that elicited genuine client-generated motivation and solutions. Falls short of 5 because counselor suggestions occasionally dominated problem-solving and deeper motivational sources (identity as a mother, loneliness, self-criticism) were not systematically developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client was consistently positioned as decision-maker, and her preferences demonstrably shaped the session (rejecting reminders, selecting the hoodie and if-then plan, choosing troubleshooting). The early expert-driven options list and occasional prescribing prevented a full score.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation via priorities questions, double confidence scaling, and small-win elicitation produced meaningful change talk ('a sane mom is better,' 'I'm doing this for him... and maybe for me too'). The most hopeful statements were not deepened or amplified — notably the unexplored 'for me too.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk never dominated; the counselor used neutral double-sided reflections and explicitly released pressure ('It's okay you're not ready to quit'). Missed opportunities to reframe the glass-of-wine comparison and 'less bad, not good' toward the client's own values.
