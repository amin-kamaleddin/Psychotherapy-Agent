# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated warmth and unconditional positive regard, affirming the client's strength and normalizing her doubts. No judgment or labeling of the co-parenting conflict.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including accurate double-sided reflections of ambivalence and defensiveness. Reflections occasionally pivoted to questions quickly rather than lingering with the emotional weight of her fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on managing reactions to co-parent texts with no drift. Each response advanced the focus from vision, to past successes, to specific strategy, implementation, and success criteria.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** True equal-partner interaction: the plan was co-built from the client's own contributions and her success criteria shaped follow-up. The client's closing comment directly confirms her sense of ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All ideas (pause, contact name, unsent drafts) originated from the client, with follow-through framed as her decision. No advice, persuasion, or pressure anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from the client rather than supplied—her values, strategy ideas, success definition, and hope all emerged through open questions and affirmations. 'That hope came from you' crystallizes the evocation-centered approach.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly located expertise in the client ('You know your family best,' 'That's your idea'). The client's closing acknowledgment that the insight was hers to find confirms the partnership was experienced as intended.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent evocation of change talk via questions about vision, past successes, and planning, drawing out commitment and activation language. Effectively amplified the client's own hope and 'proud' moment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully used double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk while pivoting to change, without dwelling on doubts. However, the 'I always slip up' fear was largely bypassed rather than explicitly reframed or softened.
