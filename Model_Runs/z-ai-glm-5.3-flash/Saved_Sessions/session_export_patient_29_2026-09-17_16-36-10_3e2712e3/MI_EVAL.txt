# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout ('You're not doing this alone') with no shaming despite past failed quit attempts. Affirmations were somewhat generic and brief given the compressed session structure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening accurately captured David's situation and validated his sleep worry, and the client explicitly felt understood. Emotional nuances like embarrassment at caving and years of struggling alone were acknowledged more than deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Crystal-clear focus on smoking cessation from readiness assessment through quit date (March 14), trigger management, and follow-up scheduling. Every exchange advanced concrete next steps toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were co-constructed with David proposing the quit date and accountability buddy, and preferences solicited at key points. Dense monologue-style turns and advice-heavy stretches limited David's room to shape the dialogue in real time.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions were consistently framed as David's with multiple options and backup plans offered without pressure. Front-loaded recommendations ('I recommend starting varenicline now') and rapid-fire options slightly constrained the decision space.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocative opening drew out David's own motivations and readiness/confidence ratings, yielding rich change talk. Evocation faded once practical planning began, shifting to information delivery rather than continued eliciting of his ideas and reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David was repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker, and his contributions visibly shaped the session (dosing timing, social plan). The clinician retained the expert role in delivering detailed advice, with some recommendations coming before exploring David's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocative questions and readiness/confidence scales drew out strong motivation and commitment language ('It's a plan now, not luck'). The gap between readiness (8) and confidence (6) was not explored to deepen confidence talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers like friends' ribbing and willpower failures were briefly validated then redirected toward concrete change strategies without amplifying sustain talk. Validation was sometimes thin ('totally reasonable'), and stress-related sustain talk was only implicitly addressed.
