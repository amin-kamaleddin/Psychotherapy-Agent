# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a neutral, nonjudgmental stance, explicitly stating they were not there to judge or force anything. However, expressions of warmth were sparse, with no direct affirmations of Erik's honesty, willingness, or strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor opened with a decent reflective statement and validated Erik's concern about his father, but reflections were limited in number. Deeper emotional content — fear of dependence, pride in self-reliance, feeling lectured — went unexplored as the session shifted into information-giving mode.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained consistent focus on the referral concerns (medication, blood sugar, alcohol) and each exchange built toward a concrete outcome. The only drift was into broad medical education that, while useful, was tangential to MI change-process work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered menus of options and asked for Erik's preferences, allowing his choices to shape the agenda. However, the stance remained predominantly expert-consultant, with plans delivered fully formed rather than co-constructed from Erik's own knowledge of his shift patterns and social life.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor regularly offered choices and deferred to Erik's preferences, with no pressure or coercion — Erik even noted he didn't expect to be asked what he wanted. Autonomy support was undermined somewhat by heavy advice-giving that prescribed specific plans and scripts rather than reinforcing Erik's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor rarely drew out Erik's own motivations, values, or reasons for change, relying instead on information-giving and external incentives. There were no open questions exploring what mattered to Erik and no confidence/importance scaling, so motivation was supplied rather than elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly asked for Erik's input, conveying some power sharing. However, the counselor frequently slipped into an expert/advisor role, delivering detailed medical information and pre-made plans without first inviting Erik's own ideas or expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik generated meaningful change talk ('a fight I can win,' 'worth hearing about'), and the counselor created conditions for this by offering choices. However, the counselor did not actively reflect, amplify, or deepen these statements, and commitment language was never explicitly elicited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not argue with or amplify Erik's sustain talk about his sacred Friday pints and fear of ridicule, which is a strength. However, the sustain talk was largely ignored rather than strategically softened — no double-sided reflections were used to acknowledge both the value of drinking and the benefits of cutting back.
