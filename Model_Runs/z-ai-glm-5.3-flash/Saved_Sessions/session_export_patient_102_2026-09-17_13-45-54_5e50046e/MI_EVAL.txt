# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Milan's irritation without moralizing despite the youth ministry context. Warmth occasionally felt clinical and formulaic, with templated safety-screening insertions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections of Milan's annoyance and pushback were offered, but responses were often question-heavy and checklist-driven. Deeper emotional themes (fear of labeling, peer mockery, feeling watched) were rarely reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use, progressing logically from minimization to assessment and a concrete plan. Focus occasionally diffused through multiple option menus and following tangents without steering back.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructed the plan and incorporated Milan's edits (private counting, face-to-face check-in, trade framing). Some content (impact checklist, harm-reduction list) was delivered in expert mode before seeking Milan's input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Milan's ownership—'pick one change,' offered a realistic 'safe max' alternative, and declined to promise outcomes. The plan was substantially Milan's own construction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some client-generated content (self-assessment, own one-liner), but motivation remained external (ending meetings, appeasing mom). Never explored intrinsic values like soccer performance or future goals as levers for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly rejected the expert role and offered genuine choices about session direction, adapting to Milan's preferences. Expertise was still occasionally asserted through checklist-style screening without inviting input first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Secured real commitments (eat first, pace drinks, accept the Luka one-liner), and assessment questions drew out self-assessment. However, motivation stayed external and opportunities to deepen personal reasons for change were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with Milan's frequent minimization and strategically let his own concessions emerge, with harm-reduction framing sidestepping the abstinence debate. Some responses counteracted sustain talk with risk lists, risking reactance.
