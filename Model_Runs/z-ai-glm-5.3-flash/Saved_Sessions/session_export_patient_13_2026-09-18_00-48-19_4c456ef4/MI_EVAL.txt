# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional acceptance, even when the patient expects judgment. Phrases like 'there's no wrong way to do this' and 'it's knowing what you need' reinforce deep respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the patient's fears and motivations, including the insight that being alone — not the news itself — was the core fear. Empathic mirroring is present throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on the patient's own goal of taking a first step toward health care. Each exchange moves gently toward a concrete plan without deviation or agenda-setting by the clinician.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is entirely self-generated by the patient, with the clinician positioning her as the expert ('You know your situation better than anyone'). Advice is never imposed; questions invite the patient's own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's autonomy is explicitly honored: 'It doesn't have to be perfect, just a start,' permission to wait for her sister, and hanging up is 'allowed too.' The clinician consistently defers choices to her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions like 'what kind of first step feels doable to you?' and 'what would help you through the freeze?' consistently draw out the patient's own change talk and self-generated solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician acts as a guide rather than director, using shared inquiry ('I wonder...') and building on the patient's ideas rather than steering. The patient explicitly notes 'the plan is completely yours.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's change talk is consistently reflected and reinforced ('nervous and doing it anyway is new for me'), strengthening her own motivation. The clinician highlights and deepens her self-affirming statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's fear and hesitation are met with acceptance and reframing rather than confrontation. Her 'freeze up' is normalized and transformed into 'letting yourself be human' rather than argued against.
