# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone; normalized Hana's ambivalence and avoided lecturing, which the client explicitly appreciated. Affirmations were somewhat generic and lacked personalized depth regarding her specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Demonstrated adequate understanding in moments, but reflections were mostly brief acknowledgments rather than emotionally attuned ones. Missed the emotional weight of Hana's shame about 'feeling pathetic' and her past experience of having 'stopped caring.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking throughout, progressing from exploration to a concrete, measurable plan with a sensible safety check. Some circling on 'what makes a reason real' and multiple parallel options slightly diluted momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was built from Hana's own values and she actively shaped the session, making her a genuine partner. However, reliance on pre-packaged templates and multi-option menus positioned the counselor as the plan-designer, and the agenda was offered before fully hearing her view.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices and affirmed Hana's self-paced commitment without pressure, leaving her feeling ownership. Some closing instructions were fairly directive, and her authority over the pace of change could have been reinforced more explicitly.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The strongest skill in the session — motivation was drawn from within as Hana discovered the financial cost and her own criterion for 'real' motivation, which the counselor built the intervention around. Could have deepened each piece of change talk further, e.g., exploring what the savings jar symbolized.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked for Hana's input and let her choices shape the intervention, showing real power sharing. However, dense bullet-pointed lists and directive instructions often created an expert/instructional register, with little explicit acknowledgment of Hana as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked client-generated motivation through values, projection, and 'real reason' questions, leading Hana to discover the financial argument herself. Missed opportunities to deepen this change talk or elicit explicit confidence/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce or amplify sustain talk, which is a strength, but the handling was passive. No double-sided reflections were used, and a prime opening when Hana asked if she was 'making excuses' was missed.
