# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and warm ("thanks for coming," "nice work," "you've earned this try"); normalizes expected excuses ("It's totally normal to expect old habits"). No judgment of nonadherence. Falls short of 5 due to somewhat formulaic warmth and few individualized affirmations beyond stock phrases.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Good reflective listening ("You're torn—knowing it matters... but busy mornings and no immediate effects make it easy to skip"); acknowledges frustration of juggling work, family, and treatment. Reflections are largely summary-based and do not deeply explore the emotional weight of her mother's kidney decline or her minimization as a protective strategy.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout — every exchange stays on medication adherence and concrete steps. Consistently guides toward a specific, measurable plan (pillbox tonight, fill Sunday, 7:15 alarm, husband's nudge, missed-dose backup plan) and secures a follow-up check-in.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing ("We can brainstorm small, practical steps that fit your routine"); final plan built almost entirely from Selene's own solutions. Slightly below 5 due to firing multiple questions in sequence (e.g., four questions in one turn), tilting the balance toward the clinician's agenda.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Choice consistently affirmed — option menus, "Which feels most helpful?", accepting her rejection of rehearsal and written excuses without pushback. Could be a 5 if the counselor more explicitly emphasized Selene's ownership of the change rather than it being partly implied by the referral context.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within — rulers elicited her own importance/confidence ratings; mother's kidney disease surfaced as her own reason for change; she generated her own proof-of-capability test. A 5 would require more sustained deepening of change talk and less reliance on structured technique.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Repeatedly asks permission ("Would it be okay if I suggest a few options?"), invites preferences ("Which feels doable?"), and lets Selene's ideas shape the plan. Falls short of 5 as the session became somewhat checklist-driven with rapid questions rather than fully client-led exploration.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Effective use of importance/confidence rulers drew out "I know I should take it more seriously" and confidence rose from 4 to 7 across the session. Change talk was often followed quickly by planning rather than deepened further; fewer open evocative questions (e.g., "What would success look like?").

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ("it's not a big deal," "I feel fine") acknowledged without reinforcement via double-sided reflections ("mostly feel okay but worry a bit"); expected excuses reframed as insight near the end. Sustain talk was occasionally echoed at length, and double-sided pivots toward her mother's story were not used strategically.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.1/5**
