# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, non-judgmental stance and normalized ambivalence throughout. Limited by brief, formulaic warmth and few explicit affirmations of Lukas beyond his practical choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections about ambivalence and identity emerged, but reflections often stayed at the logistical level. Vulnerable moments (identity fears, the lost acting fund, Sunday loneliness) were deflected into solutions rather than explored emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept consistent focus on practical harms—rent, money management, weekend spending—and moved each exchange toward a concrete plan. Meth use itself was never directly named, only addressed indirectly through spending controls, limiting depth on the core target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was genuine back-and-forth and Lukas's choices shaped the final plan. However, solutions were largely pre-packaged by the counselor (scripts, option lists), with Lukas reacting rather than truly co-creating—Lukas even noted it felt 'a bit school-like.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice—asking permission, letting Lukas select and modify options, and never pressuring or moralizing. Frequent option-lists subtly framed the counselor as the solution source, with little reinforcement of Lukas's own ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions came mostly from the counselor rather than being drawn out of the client; evocative open questions were largely absent. Rich motivational material (career stakes, appearance, identity fears) was met with logistics instead of deeper exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and allowed Lukas to shape the plan (taxi budget, auto-transfer, Sunday self-tapes). Still, the mid-session was advice-driven with instructions and menus, rarely inviting Lukas's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicited genuine change talk ('maybe it's gone a bit beyond what I noticed') and concrete commitments. But change talk arose spontaneously rather than through skillful evocation, and insightful admissions were met with option menus rather than deepening follow-ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally and not reinforced, and the 'testable experiment' framing is MI-consistent. However, no double-sided reflections paired sustain with change talk, and ambivalence about the scene and identity was deflected rather than softened and resolved.
