# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirmed Ahmed's plans while honoring his conditions (no lectures). However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and repeated, lacking deeper individualized recognition of his values beyond work/family.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening and summaries that captured Ahmed's skepticism and demand for proof. Reflections tracked content well but missed emotional depth, such as his protectiveness about autonomy and resentment toward being lectured.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on mini-cigar use and throat irritation throughout, progressively moving from eliciting feelings to a concrete plan with follow-up and symptom tracking. Gently redirected all deflections (dust, buddy comparison) back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely two-sided, with Ahmed's ideas (gum, truck loading, two-week timeline, morning scratchiness metric) substantially shaping it. Dense information-giving and repeated option menus occasionally narrowed his space to generate his own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Ahmed's ownership through permission-based information sharing, choice menus, and acceptance of his 'trial not quitting' framing and timeline. Never pressured, and Ahmed clearly perceived control over decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited Ahmed's own motivations via open questions, the confidence ruler, and future-oriented prompts, moving him from denial to a self-endorsed trial with rising confidence (6→7). Could have explored his deeper values and amplified his surprise at the risk information rather than moving quickly to logistics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Ahmed as the decision-maker, and his contributions clearly determined the plan. Fell short of a 5 due to stacked questions and predefined option menus that limited fully open exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked change talk—Ahmed agreed to a trial, developed a specific plan, raised confidence from 6 to 7, and expressed openness to extending it. Evocation was partly prompt-driven, and Ahmed's own stated reasons (family, work) were not consistently deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue with or reinforce sustain talk and used neutral summaries, but often pivoted to logistics rather than strategically softening ambivalence. Few double-sided reflections were used, leaving statements like 'not addicted' and 'going back to weekends without guilt' largely unexplored.
