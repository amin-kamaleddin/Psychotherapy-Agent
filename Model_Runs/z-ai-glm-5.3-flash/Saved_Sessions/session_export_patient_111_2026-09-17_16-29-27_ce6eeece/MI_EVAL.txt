# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone despite the client's initial hostility and validated his attendance. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic, and opportunities to affirm Richard's honesty and care for his wife were missed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening, including reflecting the tension between ritual and relationship. Deeper emotional moments, like the father-in-law's death disclosure, were bypassed in favor of practical planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on smoking and its marital impact throughout, progressing logically to a concrete, time-bound two-week plan with a scheduled check-in. Every response tied back to actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the conversation as joint problem-solving, and key plan elements came from the client himself. Reliance on pre-packaged checklists and scripts occasionally tilted the dynamic toward advisor rather than equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced client ownership ('You're in charge of any choice') and honored all of Richard's conditions, including the 'not quitting' framing and secrecy from his wife. The final plan was genuinely client-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited self-generated motivation rooted in the client's relational values. Evocation relied at times on option menus, and the powerful father-in-law disclosure was not explored or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed that expertise resided with the client, who recognized the plan as his own. Occasional pre-packaged option menus slightly reduced space for the client's own contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out progressive change talk, from 'maybe I don't smoke around her' to a two-week commitment, and reflected the client's self-generated plan back to him. Change talk was at times drawn toward a pre-scripted menu rather than emerging organically.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively responded to sustain talk with neutral, accepting reflections and adopted the client's 'not quitting' framing to prevent resistance. Offering substitutes like gum or tea briefly re-engaged sustain talk that the client had to push back on.
