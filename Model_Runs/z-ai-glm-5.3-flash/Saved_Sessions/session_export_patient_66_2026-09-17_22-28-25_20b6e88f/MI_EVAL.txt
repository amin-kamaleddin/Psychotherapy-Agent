# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard was consistent throughout, including validating his continued enjoyment of smoking and welcoming ambivalence at the close. Kareem's own feedback ('Thanks for not lecturing me') confirmed acceptance was experienced, though affirmations occasionally ran slightly formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and deep, capturing both sides of ambivalence and the emotional weight of the father disclosure ('am I going to be okay?'). The counselor also caught subtle nuances like not wanting to be 'a photo.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focus on smoking cessation was clear and consistent, with every exchange building toward a specific behavioral commitment. Even when the father story emerged, it was woven back to the change goal, with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All plan elements (morning cigarette, seeds, telling coworkers, timeline) originated from the client, with questions like 'what feels like the right pace?' reinforcing equal power-sharing. Only a slight tendency to elaborate on Kareem's statements kept this from perfect power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was exemplary, starting with the opening question addressing resistance to being forced and repeatedly framing the plan as his ('This isn't me or the company handing you a plan — it's yours'). Kareem set his own timeline, slip-recovery attitude, and disclosure plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was almost entirely elicited from the client — his own problem-solving, father's death, and children were drawn out through open questions rather than supplied. His closing comment ('Thanks for listening instead of just telling me what to do') and 'a bigger part of me kind of wants to see if I can' confirm the evocative approach worked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client, and Kareem's contributions shaped the session direction — his 'experiment, not a sentence' framing became the organizing metaphor. The counselor affirmed ownership throughout ('That's yours to carry, however it unfolds').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully selected and reflected, then amplified through targeted evocative questions, escalating to commitment language ('Two weeks starts Monday'). Motivation was deepened at the identity level ('maybe I'm not [my dad]') and connected to his children.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never suppressed or amplified — the underlying need was reflected ('You value that break, that reset') and double-sided reflections held both sides together. Sustain talk visibly diminished over the session without confrontation.
