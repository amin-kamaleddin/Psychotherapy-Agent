# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard is communicated throughout—ambivalence is normalized, slips are treated as 'information, not failure,' and there is no judgment even when strong sustain talk emerges. The session closes warmly and supportively.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and consistent, capturing ambivalence, the client's fears, and even process observations like 'something just clicked.' Reflections consistently land, as confirmed by Elise's affirmations such as 'That's exactly it, honestly.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on smoking cessation and builds progressively from exploring ambivalence to a concrete, time-bound plan with a scheduled check-in. The counselor guides without steering off-target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functions between equal partners, with plans co-constructed and agenda items offered as invitations. The client's own contributions materially determine the session's course and final plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is explicitly reinforced at every decision point, with setbacks framed as information rather than failure and no pressure or persuasion used. The client explicitly notes her appreciation: 'Thank you—for not just telling me to quit.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client through open evocative questions, with no reasons to quit supplied by the counselor. Elise generates her own realizations and consolidates them into self-made tools, demonstrating mastery-level evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positions Elise as the expert, with every strategy (toothpicks, texting sister, phone note) originating from her. Counselor contributions are limited to reflections and questions that let the client's own insights shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and developed, from the pivotal question 'What do you think it would take for you to test that?' to Elise's own one-week experiment plan. Commitment language is reinforced, and the session ends with strong mobilizing change talk ('Text tonight, then we'll see about the week').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced and is generally pivoted toward change; fears of exclusion and slipping are acknowledged without amplification and reframed. Occasionally engaged directly rather than rolled with sideways, leaving slightly less demonstration of classic rolling-with techniques.
