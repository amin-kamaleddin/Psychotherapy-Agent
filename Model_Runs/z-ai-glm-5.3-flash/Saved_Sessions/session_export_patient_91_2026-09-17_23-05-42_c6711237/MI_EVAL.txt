# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout; embarrassment about months of inaction was met with acceptance and reframed as progress, with strong affirmations like 'That's real commitment, and it came from you.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening captured both content and emotion (e.g., 'Scared, but relieved — like you'd finally be facing it instead of hiding from it'). Disclosures deepened in response, indicating the patient felt truly understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Laser focus on diabetes self-management, narrowing progressively from general overwhelm to a concrete, time-bound plan (call tomorrow; Thursday check) with no drift into unrelated territory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-way exchange where the patient built on reflections ('Maybe she's got a point') and the final plan was coalesced entirely from his own contributions, with comfortable, egalitarian rapport.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership reinforced at every turn — every strategy in the plan originated from the patient, progress was attributed to him, and imperfection was framed as acceptable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn entirely from within via questions about values, anticipated feelings, and future orientation, eliciting deeply personal motivators (his father's avoidance, family, identity) and self-attributed commitment ('I've got this').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in the patient ('You know your routines and your days better than anyone'), with all plan strategies (calling before work, wife's presence, the note) originating from him — a model of power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation through commitment and confidence questions and vision exploration, producing strong change talk ('I want to be that guy Thursday night,' 'My gut says I've got this') and a concrete plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reflected fear once for understanding and pivoted to problem-solving, but the fear/'chicken out' theme was revisited several times, giving sustain talk slightly more airtime than strictly necessary — though each return was redirected skillfully.
