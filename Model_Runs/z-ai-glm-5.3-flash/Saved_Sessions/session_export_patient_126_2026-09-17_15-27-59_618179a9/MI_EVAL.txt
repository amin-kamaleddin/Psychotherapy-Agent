# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth despite Thomas's dismissive stance, with repeated thanks for his honesty and no judgment. A 5 would require more explicit affirmation of him as a person beyond his answers.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Several accurate factual reflections were made, but emotional depth was often missed — notably the guilt and father-identity meaning behind Thomas's Sunday hangover admission. Reflections stayed at summary level rather than touching feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on drinking throughout, culminating in a concrete plan tied to a specific event with follow-up. Not a 5 due to considerable time on low-yield fact-gathering rather than building momentum toward Thomas's own reasons for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent permission-asking, menu options, and a plan co-built from Thomas's own picks. However, the advice-giving middle was expert-style and monologic, with stacked questions reducing genuine two-way exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of choice throughout, accepting refusals without pushback and building the plan entirely from Thomas's selected options. No pressuring language appeared anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good evocative questions and rulers elicited client-owned motivation (Sunday mornings with his toddler, wife as conscience), but the counselor pivoted quickly to supplying advice and solutions rather than consistently drawing ideas from Thomas. Evocation was present but superficial, especially in the second half.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Thomas was repeatedly positioned as decision-maker, with the plan built from his own chosen strategies. A 5 was missed because expertise was shared unevenly, with advice-heavy tip lists dominating the middle of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good evocative questions and rulers elicited downside talk and a tentative commitment, but the counselor failed to deepen or reflect change talk when it emerged, bypassing it in favor of delivering more tips.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and was sometimes neutrally reframed, but there was no strategic softening — no double-sided reflections, and statements like 'my dad drank his whole life and he's fine' were never explored or addressed.
