# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with individualized affirmations like 'that's a real strength' and normalizing her ambivalence. No judgment about past failure or inactivity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate complex reflections capturing both content and emotional meaning, e.g., linking exercise to self-confidence and identity. Each reflection builds on the client's previous statement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical progression from exploring options to concrete commitments (Tuesday walk, Saturday YMCA visit, weekly routine). The final identity-level question is strategic consolidation rather than drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Joint problem-solving signaled by 'plan around together,' and the session agenda is shaped by Leila's preferences. Falls short of 5 because the plan is essentially hers, leaving collaboration implicit rather than explicitly co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy actively reinforced via 'on your terms,' 'on your own timeline,' and no imposed advice—every plan element is client-generated. Her self-directed boundaries are celebrated without added pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions consistently draw out the client's own reasons, values, and vision, producing the deepest change talk of the session ('more confident—that's the biggest thing I'm chasing'). Concrete plans are connected to core values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Leila is positioned as the expert on her own life, with choice and expertise consistently hers. Could be strengthened by occasionally inviting her to guide the session agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk evoked and deepened from specific plans ('Solid ten' confidence) to values-level motivation ('I'd love for this to just be who I am again'). Commitment amplified by concrete scheduling questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (excuses, dislikes, past burnout) is acknowledged briefly and neutrally, then reframed toward agency and change, e.g., 'taking the excuses off the table'.
