# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with no labeling or disapproval despite the client's dismissiveness. However, affirmations were sparse and formulaic, with no explicit validation of the client's strengths such as his pragmatism and care about his future.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some accurate early reflections and mirrored the client's stated goals, but reflections remained shallow and quickly pivoted to checklists. Key emotional moments—including the client's admission about peer pressure and mention of family stress—went unreflected and unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session fully acceded to the client's request to keep things 'purely procedural,' drifting entirely away from the target behavior of marijuana use. No reflections ever connected the client's stated future goals (graduation, employment, record-sealing) back to his current use patterns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborated well on immediate logistics such as co-rehearsing statements and jointly scheduling, but this was limited to compliance mechanics. The counselor supplied all the content while the client's own expertise about his risks, peers, and strategies went untapped.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear strength: consistently offered choices, respected preferences, and avoided any pressure or lecturing, which the client explicitly noticed and appreciated. However, autonomy support bordered on accommodating avoidance, with no affirming of the client's capacity to make informed choices about use itself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was treated as residing in the checklist rather than the client, with questions being procedural and multiple-choice rather than open and evocative. The client's spontaneous motivation (avoiding arrest, graduating, employment, his cousin's record) surfaced on its own but was never evoked, deepened, or linked to behavior change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered the client choices and let his preferences shape session direction, supporting a sense of partnership. However, the counselor acted primarily as a service provider delivering procedural answers rather than drawing on the client's own knowledge and expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did very little to evoke or develop change talk; the client's self-generated reasons for change ('not trying to mess up graduation,' staying employed) were simply agreed with rather than amplified. No questions explored what mattered to him, his confidence, or his commitment beyond mere compliance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's minimizations ('nobody even cares,' 'sorry I got caught, not sorry I smoked') were met with simple acceptance ('That's fair') rather than strategic double-sided reflections or discrepancy-building. The counselor even aided the status quo by drafting a no-apology statement and avoiding any exploration of the underlying use.
