# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Basic respectful tone maintained ("Thanks for being honest"), but limited warmth or explicit positive regard. Few affirmations of Viktor's strengths; early barrage of assessment questions felt clinical and checklist-like rather than accepting of a defensive, externally motivated client.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5
**Comments:** Reflective listening largely absent. Viktor's irritation ("annoyed is a good word") and significant emotional statement ("I miss when things were easy between us") were not reflected. Responses tended to pivot immediately to lists of questions or advice rather than demonstrating understanding.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Reasonable focus maintained on target behavior and relational consequences; logical transition from assessment to couples communication to a four-week trial plan. However, focus shifted somewhat toward marital communication mechanics, and the substance-use component (cutting back) was never revisited or consolidated.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Some collaboration existed (asking which option Viktor preferred, asking permission before drafting the text), but rules, scripts, check-ins, and consequences were counselor-generated. Viktor's contributions shaped little beyond selecting among pre-made menus.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Some choices offered ("Which of those would you like now?") with no coercion, but the rapid-fire menu of options and pre-written scripts subtly steered Viktor toward the counselor's agenda. Autonomy-affirming statements were absent; the counselor mostly supplied the plan.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5
**Comments:** Motivation was primarily evoked by the client himself, not by counselor technique. Questions were mostly closed-ended assessment items. Key motivational material—Viktor's longing for easier times with his wife, his dislike of fighting—passed without being explored, amplified, or used to develop discrepancy.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2/5
**Comments:** Counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role: offering scripts, checklists, harm-reduction lists, and pre-drafted texts with minimal elicitation of Viktor's own ideas. Viktor's expertise was rarely tapped; the relationship plan was delivered as counselor-authored content rather than co-created.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Counselor inadvertently benefited from good client change talk ("it's worth trying, for four weeks anyway"; "maybe I keep some of it going") but did little to actively evoke it. Change talk was never reflected, deepened, or reinforced; "I miss when things were easy" was a prime evocation opportunity that was missed.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not amplified or reinforced (appropriate), but it also was not actively softened via double-sided reflections. Viktor's late denial ("I still don't think what I do is some huge problem") was left unaddressed rather than gently reframed toward his stated goal of marital peace.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** 2.8/5
