# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, honoring Ingrid's time constraints without pressure. However, affirmations were sparse and explicit validation of her juggling responsibilities was limited beyond the opening line.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Brief empathic acknowledgments early on ('juggling work, home, and a pharmacy visit') were appreciated. However, Ingrid's significant disclosure that 'things have felt a bit heavy lately' was met with scheduling logistics rather than emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed well-focused under severe time constraints, moving logically from safety information to the note to a secured follow-up appointment. The ending was slightly cluttered with repeated crisis-line disclaimers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine partnership in the scheduling phase, with Ingrid's preferences directly shaping timing, format, and focus. However, the first half was largely one-directional information delivery, and the checklist plan was prepared unilaterally rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices (in-person vs. video, 20 vs. 50 minutes, day/time, session focus) with advice framed as offers rather than directives. Slightly weakened by rapid-fire questions at close and an unsolicited grounding tip.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocative openings were posed ('what small change would feel most helpful?') but motivation was never actively developed. The clinician's own suggestions (checklist, grounding tip) substituted for eliciting Ingrid's ideas and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ingrid's input was invited at several points and clearly shaped the plan. The medication portion, however, was one-directional expert delivery, and the clinician assumed the planning role rather than co-creating it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created openings that elicited forward-looking statements ('having somewhere to talk about the heaviness might not be a bad thing'). These emerging change statements were not deliberately reinforced or deepened before moving to scheduling logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled reasonably by keeping answers brief and avoiding argument or pressure. However, the ambivalence between wanting to talk about feeling 'heavy' and having no time was never addressed with a strategic double-sided reflection.
