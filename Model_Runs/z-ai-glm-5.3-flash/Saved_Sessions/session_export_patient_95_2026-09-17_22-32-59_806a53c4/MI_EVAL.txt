# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated consistent, warm, unconditional regard throughout, including nondefensive responses when tested ('Prove me wrong, I guess') and care without alarm when suicidal ideation was disclosed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and emotionally attuned, including validating pain first when the client demanded it, which unlocked her engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on suicidal ideation, connection, and reasons for living, with each exchange building progressively toward a concrete behavioral step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned between equal partners, with the counselor soliciting Maria's perspective and the final action step (calling her sister) coming entirely from her rather than the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice was consistently reinforced, with change framed as Maria's own doing ('you're choosing to walk toward it on your own terms') and no pressure, persuasion, or directive advice present.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from within the client, surfacing her own reasons for living through her sister and the memory of being needed, resulting in authentic client-generated commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positioned Maria as the expert, and her contributions visibly shaped the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and deepened, culminating in strong client-generated commitment language ('I'll call her. Maybe. Tonight, maybe.'), with fragile change talk carefully protected at the client's request.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged without reinforcement, using double-sided reflections and reframing ('nothing lasts' becomes 'you created it'), with no arguing against or minimizing despair.
