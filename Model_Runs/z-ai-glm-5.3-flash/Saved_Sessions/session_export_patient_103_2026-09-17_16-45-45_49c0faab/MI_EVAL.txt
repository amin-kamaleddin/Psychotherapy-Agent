# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Nabil's frustration and never labeling him as having an 'anger problem.' Warmth was somewhat brief and transactional, with few explicit affirmations of Nabil as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently reflected Nabil's core perspective, accurately capturing his sense of external injustice and responsibility to work. Reflections were sometimes brief, and his deeper emotional fears (e.g., 'looking like the problem') were not explicitly named until late.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on managing anger in medical settings, with every turn advancing the goal from exploration to a concrete, rehearsed plan. Even when Nabil redirected blame to the system, responses stayed tied to concrete actions he could take.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session functioned as joint problem-solving, with the counselor offering menus, soliciting feedback, and adapting materials per Nabil's edits. Collaboration fell just short of a 5 because most content (options, scripts, letter) was counselor-generated rather than drawn from Nabil's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Nabil's ownership, framing every strategy as optional with no coercive or pressuring language. Nabil rejected elements (bringing work, the 'I appreciate that' opener) without pushback, and the final plan was entirely his selection.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong early open questions and choice questions elicited Nabil's reasoning, but the counselor then shifted heavily into advice-giving and script provision. Nabil's own motivations, values (e.g., his wife's concern, self-image as professional), and confidence were not deliberately evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Nabil as the decision-maker, with his preferences clearly shaping the plan (he chose the deadline line, documenting, and requested edits). Short of a 5 only because option menus were counselor-generated rather than co-created from Nabil's own experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used open questions and structured options that drew out commitments ('I could say that,' 'I'll try it,' 'I could send that'), with the rehearsal eliciting action-oriented language. Missed opportunity: no explicit confidence/importance scaling to deepen and consolidate commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was frequent (blaming the system, 'leaving feels risky,' fear of blowing up) but was sidestepped rather than softened with double-sided reflections. Responses often jumped to offering more strategies instead of actively pivoting back to change.
