# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'You did well' and validation that wanting his home back while staying kind is fair. However, warmth was at times formulaic and Oliver's self-criticism ('I'm kind of weak') was never directly affirmed or normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong opening complex reflection capturing the conflict between caring for the friend and wanting independence, plus validation of his kindness value. Empathy was sometimes thin — his guilt, humor, and fears of losing the friendship or becoming 'a tyrant' were not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained tight, consistent focus on the Saturday move-out goal with every exchange advancing it — readiness assessment, scripts, rehearsal, Thursday reminder, and safety planning. No topic drift and a logical progression from exploration to rehearsal to contingency planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked interactively by offering options, checking preferences, and using role-play jointly, with Oliver's requests shaping the session. Collaboration was somewhat constrained by the counselor generating most script and plan content rather than co-creating from Oliver's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly supported choice with permission-seeking before advice and offers framed as options ('Want it softer or firmer?'). Autonomy was limited by supplying ready-made scripts rather than primarily eliciting Oliver's own wording.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Oliver's own resolve through open questions, confidence scaling, and rehearsal, producing strong change talk ('My job is done... It's time'). Evocation relied heavily on repeated 0–10 confidence rulers and often jumped to solutions before fully exploring Oliver's own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited preferences and asked permission before advising, but the session became counselor-driven with most scripts and strategy supplied by the counselor. Oliver's own key insight ('my job is done') was not built upon as the foundation of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively elicited and reinforced change talk, with confidence scaling visibly raising Oliver's confidence from 4–5 to 8.5 across the session. The technique was frequent but somewhat mechanical, favoring repeated scales over richer exploration of values and hope.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged guilt and comfort of companionship without amplifying it, and redirected toward action. However, sustain talk was never worked through with double-sided reflections, and Oliver's self-criticism was left unaddressed — a missed opportunity when he said 'my job's done.'
