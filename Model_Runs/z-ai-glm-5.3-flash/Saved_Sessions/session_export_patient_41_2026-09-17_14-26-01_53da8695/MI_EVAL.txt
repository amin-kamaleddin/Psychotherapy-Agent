# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with normalization and affirmations (e.g., 'that heaviness makes sense,' 'well done for committing'). Warmth feels somewhat formulaic, with support often paired with directive instruction rather than individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections of the heaviness, overwhelm, and nervous-but-hopeful feelings. Reflections are sparse overall; most turns are instructions and recaps, missing chances to explore the self-critical pattern and emotional weight of repeated failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on a concrete target behavior—opening study notes at ~7:30 after practice—with logical progression to time, place, and contingency planning. Achieved largely through directive guidance rather than collaborative agenda-setting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative gestures like breaking things down together and offering choices. The counselor generates nearly all ideas and Elira mostly assents, making the exchange feel like a coach's game plan rather than equal co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elira's choices were respected (rejecting the timer, choosing opening notes, adopting the sticky note). The style remains heavily prescriptive, delivering detailed instruction lists each turn instead of asking what she thinks would work.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is rarely drawn from within the client; the counselor supplies reasons, techniques, and rationale instead. No open questions explore Elira's values, hopes, or her own solutions, despite spontaneous change talk worth deepening.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role with menus of options and detailed prescriptive instructions. Elira's role is essentially choosing among pre-generated options, with no acknowledgment of her expertise about her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk comes from the client herself ('maybe tiny is the trick,' confidence rising from 4 to 5-6), with little deliberate evocation by the counselor. No elaboration questions or affirmations deepen her own reasons and statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful reframes of 'I'll do it wrong' as perfectionism and failure as 'learning what works.' No double-sided reflections, and repeated 'if it doesn't happen, we'll troubleshoot' endings keep attention on possible failure rather than pivoting to change language.
