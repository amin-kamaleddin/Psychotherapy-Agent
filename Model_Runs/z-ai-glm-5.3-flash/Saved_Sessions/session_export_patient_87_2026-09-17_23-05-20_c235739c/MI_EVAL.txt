# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with individualized affirmations ('That's not a small thing') and consistent validation of Marco's honesty and nervousness. No labeling, disapproval, or pressure at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture emotional core and underlying meaning, including the sophisticated double-sided reflection 'part of you believes this could work AND part of you is protecting yourself.' Listening goes well beyond surface content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear progressive focus on the target behavior with no topic drift — each exchange builds from identifying the real challenge to a concrete, specific commitment (Saturday morning talk, cake joke opener, success signals).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely equal interaction; the counselor offers no expert nutrition content, only questions and reflections inviting Marco's ideas. Every plan element was co-constructed from Marco's own material.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames change as Marco's choice and directly addresses his self-imposed pressure ('holding it lightly — not as a test you can fail'). Never advises, directs, or pressures, and explicitly celebrates Marco's self-authorship.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every significant insight came from Marco — the all-or-nothing pattern, the one-bad-hour permission, 'being tired of him is the fuel.' Confidence rose organically from 6 to 'past a seven' as a direct result of evocative questioning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Marco as the expert ('You know your household better than anyone') and every plan element originated from him. Exemplary power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and deepens change talk: tracks confidence ratings upward (6 to 'past a seven'), asks 'What's your gut say makes that idea worth trying?', and consolidates commitment with 'What would you want to remember about how you're feeling right now?' Marco responds with strong commitment language ('This one's mine').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ambivalence is handled well with double-sided reflections, and sustain talk is acknowledged but never reinforced. Only minor weakness: a few reflections briefly restate barriers (leftovers, hard days) rather than actively pivoting toward change.
