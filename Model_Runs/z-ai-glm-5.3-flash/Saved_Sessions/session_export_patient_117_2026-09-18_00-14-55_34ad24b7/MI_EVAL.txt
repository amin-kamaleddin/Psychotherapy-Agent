# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with frequent, genuine affirmations crediting the patient's own insights. Warmth is somewhat formulaic, and there is no explicit normalization of struggle or setback.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures both surface content and underlying emotion, including the patient's ambivalence about his wife's advice. Reflections occasionally serve as springboards to the next question rather than sitting with emotional weight, e.g., the fear of relapse and shame from past cold-turkey attempts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains unwavering focus on smoking reduction with clear forward momentum, moving logically from triggers to plans to decision criteria and the larger goal. Every reflection ties back to progress with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is a genuine two-way exchange with decisions co-constructed from the patient's own material. The near-universal reflection-plus-question cadence feels formulaic; a fully collaborative session would occasionally offer information or let structure emerge more organically.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces the patient's ownership of every decision, offering genuine choice and never prescribing, pressuring, or imposing timelines. Even the journal modification is framed as the patient's idea to evaluate himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every motivation, insight, and plan is drawn from the patient, who cites his own journal data, his wife's words, and his mantra as evidence. Strategic reflections develop this change talk at a high level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient is explicitly positioned as the expert, and his contributions genuinely shape the session — the break plan, scaled-down journaling, and decision rule all come from him. The psychiatrist builds each intervention on the patient's own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open evocative questions consistently draw rich change talk ('Maybe I try it a few days this week and see,' 'that's proof I can do lunch too'). The psychiatrist reinforces and deepens commitment language through reflections at every turn.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without amplification, with an effective double-sided reflection on the fear of promising out loud. Some reflections briefly repeat sustain content, and past failed attempts are never reframed as evidence of persistence.
