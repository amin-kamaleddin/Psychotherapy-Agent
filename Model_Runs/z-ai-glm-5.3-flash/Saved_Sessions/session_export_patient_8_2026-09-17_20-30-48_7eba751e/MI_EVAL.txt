# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, including immediate reassurance when the patient apologized and playful, non-condescending closing support. Nonjudgmental tone maintained even amid the patient's doubts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, notably the double-sided reflection capturing ambivalence ('scrolling helps you unwind... and the 3am nights are scaring you'), which the patient confirmed. Emotional nuances and the patient's self-generated insights were reflected with precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Steady focus on the target behavior with a natural progression from exploring ambivalence to a concrete plan, confidence scaling, and relapse planning. Each question advanced the agenda without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully joint problem-solving: open questions before reflections, co-constructed plan from the patient's own ideas, and shared scaling/relapse-prevention work. The patient explicitly felt the partnership made the problem feel 'less impossible.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** No directives or pressure — the plan was framed as 'your experiment' and ideas came from the patient. However, autonomy support was implicit rather than explicit, with few direct statements affirming the patient's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly all substantive insights and motivation originated from the patient — the habit reframe, midnight plan, confidence rating, and self-kindness realization. Classic evocative questions systematically elicited desire, ability, reasons, and commitment talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit attribution of expertise to the patient ('you did that, not me'), with all key plan elements (midnight cutoff, roommate accountability, sticky note) originating from her. Reciprocal, playful rapport reinforced genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation of change talk through open and scaling questions, with clearly growing commitment language ('I might actually try it this week'). The counselor reinforced momentum rather than inserting an agenda.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally acknowledged and paired with change-side reflections, and the counselor built on the patient's 'keeping the fun bits' framing. Some opportunities to actively pivot sustain talk (e.g., around social-life fears) were left untouched.
