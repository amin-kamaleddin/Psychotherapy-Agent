# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates consistent respect and nonjudgment — Aizada explicitly felt heard ('first person who didn't make me feel foolish'). However, affirmations are brief and rarely individualized beyond one-line validations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections (pain easing at the wedding, illness-as-identity), but reflections are sparse and shallow. Deep disclosures like 'I don't know who I'd be without it' received brief acknowledgment followed by pivots to advice lists rather than deepened emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays consistently focused on GI concerns, testing, and symptom tracking, with concrete next steps established (diary, GP letter, referral, sewing). Score limited by a checklist-like focus on advice rather than collaborative steering toward her own goals and ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration — options offered and her requests (firm letter, diary) honored. But the counselor supplies nearly all content, leaving Aizada in a consumer role with no joint brainstorming or eliciting of her own ideas before offering solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choice ('Would you like to talk, learn coping strategies, or work on a plan?') and explicitly honors Aizada's conditional plan ('Agreed — that plan sounds sensible'). Advice-delivery format still subtly pressures toward the counselor's preferred solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely draws motivation from within the client — almost no open questions about her reasons, hopes, or confidence. Her change emerged mainly from information provision, and her poignant identity reflection was met with advice rather than exploration of her values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses permission-seeking questions well, but the session is heavily expert-driven with long prescriptive lists dominating. Aizada's role is mostly to accept or reject packages rather than being positioned as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged incidentally via psychoeducation ('I'll see it myself... I'll try it') and commitments to the diary and sewing. The counselor rarely explicitly evoked motivation with open questions or importance/confidence scaling, and commitments were not deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully avoids arguing with her biomedical certainty and validates without amplifying fear. However, no strategic techniques used — no double-sided reflections pairing her sustain talk with her own change talk (e.g., pain easing at the wedding).
