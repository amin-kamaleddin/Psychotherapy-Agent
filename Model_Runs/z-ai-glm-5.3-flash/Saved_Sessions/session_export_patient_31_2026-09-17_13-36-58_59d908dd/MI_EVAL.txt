# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout; David's boundaries (non-negotiable evening cigarette, cut-down-only) were accepted without pushback. Affirmations were somewhat generic rather than individualized, with little explicit validation of his emotional ambivalence or fear of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections captured his ambivalence well. However, emotionally significant disclosures (chest symptoms, fear of failure, partner's pressure, surprise at no lecture) were skipped over or met with information delivery instead of reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on the smoking harm-reduction goal, producing a specific, measurable plan. Direction was over-directive, however, with rapid drive toward instruction-giving rather than MI-consistent guiding.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered and David's preferences (paper tally, gum, 4 mg) were incorporated. But plans were largely pre-packaged by the counselor before David shaped them, with only client ratification rather than genuine co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently honored David's stated limits and offered choices without pressure toward full cessation. Choices were mostly within a clinician-defined framework, without explicitly reinforcing David's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain—David's own reasons for change (partner, chest symptoms, fear of failure, emerging curiosity) went unexplored. No open questions about values, importance, or confidence; change talk emerged spontaneously rather than through skillful elicitation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative language was present, but the agenda and solutions were substantially clinician-generated. David steered some details, yet power-sharing was limited to micro-choices like dose selection.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Non-coercive invitations created meaningful change talk ('maybe there's more to this than I let on,' curiosity about Thursday). The counselor did not deepen this change talk, instead pivoting to logistics and dosing facts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I like smoking,' 'non-negotiable' evening cigarette) was acknowledged neutrally and redirected to achievable harm reduction without arguing. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections leveraging David's own concerns toward broader change.
