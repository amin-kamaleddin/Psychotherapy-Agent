# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and non-judgmental. Gracefully accepted the patient's opening correction, normalized ambivalence, and reframed self-criticism compassionately.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, layered reflections that captured ambivalence and named the core fear. The reframe of 'laziness' as self-protection was explicitly validated by the patient.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful trajectory from ambivalence to a specific, measurable behavioral experiment. Guided with questions rather than pushing, staying focused on the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely egalitarian; the plan was built entirely from the patient's own ideas. The patient's closing comment ('you let me talk myself into it') is strong evidence of effective collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was repeatedly returned to the patient, and ownership of both plan and outcome was explicitly affirmed ('That's you looking out for yourself, not me pushing you'). Final commitment was fully client-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All rationale for change came from the patient's own mouth; the psychiatrist never argued for cutting back. The patient even self-generated a coping mantra, the hallmark of well-evoked motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly located expertise within the client throughout, but the closing suggestions (phone note, coping statement) were slightly more directive than the rest of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked, deepened, and consolidated change talk through strategic questions and future orientation. Patient language progressed from 'passing thoughts' to a concrete, self-generated commitment.
