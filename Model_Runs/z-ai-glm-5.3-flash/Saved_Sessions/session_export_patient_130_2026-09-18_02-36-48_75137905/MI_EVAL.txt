# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, affirming Viktor's honesty and never judging his use or defensiveness. Falls short of 5 because some affirmations feel slightly formulaic and praise-oriented rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate, well-timed, and capture both content and feeling, including two-sided ambivalence ('part of you knows... part of you is being realistic'). Every reflection lands and moves the conversation forward with no superficial or inaccurate responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, purposeful focus on substance use and its link to Viktor's value of peace at home, advancing through discrepancy, options, planning, obstacles, and success criteria. Gently redirects deflection (blaming Elena) while respecting the client's pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans are co-developed through questions and the client's expertise is treated as operative throughout. Not a 5 because the exchange is heavily question-led with little tentative idea-sharing, self-disclosure, or explicit agenda negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit and sustained: 'no pressure,' 'your choice, your terms,' and every plan element is client-generated ('My idea, my words'). Resistance is met with choice-preserving questions rather than correction or advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All reasons for change come from Viktor, evoked through a masterful sequence from vision to confidence to commitment, culminating in 'I miss being him.' His closing—'I came in ready to argue and I'm leaving with a plan'—directly evidences internally elicited motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise in the client ('you know your life and your friends better than anyone'), and the entire plan originates from Viktor, prompted but never supplied. Even on telling Elena, the counselor asks rather than prescribes.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematically evokes and develops change talk via future-oriented questions—vision, concrete plan, coping strategies, and commitment language ('I want to see if I can still be the guy she married'). Each piece of change talk is amplified through strategic reflection rather than left on the table.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoids arguing with sustain talk, pivoting instead to the client's own language ('not quitting, but being more deliberate'). Relies on a fairly uniform reframe-and-move-on strategy, with only one well-executed double-sided reflection.
