# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently respected Elena's perspective and honored her wish not to be pressured, repeatedly offering choices. Warmth was present but somewhat formulaic, with limited explicit affirmation of her courage or effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were accurate early reflections and acknowledgment of ambivalence, but much of the session was didactic. The counselor rarely reflected Elena's deeper emotional material, such as fear of judgment and parental guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on the varicella vaccine decision throughout, with every exchange tied to it. Progression toward a concrete action plan was logical with no meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative markers included co-developing the husband script from Elena's own phrasing and iterative role-play incorporating her feedback. The statistical briefing and prescriptive planning were more expert-delivered than jointly negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was repeatedly emphasized through menus and acceptance of Elena's preferences and refusals. Plan steps were counselor-prescribed rather than fully client-generated, slightly constraining ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena's motivations were partly elicited, but much of the change was catalyzed by counselor-delivered statistics rather than open questions drawing out her personal reasons and values. Evocation was secondary to information provision.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor frequently asked for Elena's input and built on it, but often assumed the expert role with dense statistics and directive planning lists. Elena's own expertise was used but not consistently foregrounded.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Preparing questions, role-play, and next steps effectively elicited strong client-generated commitment language ('I'll book the appointment this week — that's on me'). The counselor did not deeply deepen change talk with evocative value-oriented questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement and skillfully reframed ('okay to honor your experience while seeking data'). The pivot relied more on information provision than skillful double-sided reflections.
