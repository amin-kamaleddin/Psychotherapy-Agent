# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance, affirming Hana's choices and normalizing lapses without criticism. However, the warmth feels somewhat formulaic and checklist-like, with repeated scripted reminders preventing a higher score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections of Hana's situation (habit-based smoking, weekend challenges) are present, but feelings and ambivalence—like 'I'd fold' or fear of failing again—go unexplored. Responses tend to jump straight to information-giving and planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Direction is a clear strength: every response ties back to smoking reduction with tracking, delay strategies, NRT, social scripts, and a concrete 2-week plan with review points. There is no topic drift and the session progresses logically toward a specific behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is predominantly clinician-driven, with complete plans, scripts, and timetables that Hana accepts or slightly modifies rather than co-constructs. Her own insights could have been woven into a jointly designed plan but were absorbed into the clinician's pre-set framework.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-affirming elements exist, such as offering options, asking permission for plans, and nonjudgmental framing of lapses. However, rapid-fire information delivery and pre-packaged plans create a directive feel, with little reinforcement of Hana's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Limited effort to draw out Hana's own motivations: the readiness ruler (4) is never followed up, and her dental health motivator emerges incidentally without exploration. The session relies on providing information rather than eliciting intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely assumes the expert role, delivering prescriptive content while incorporating Hana's contributions only minimally. There is little acknowledgment that Hana holds expertise about her own triggers and social context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited via the readiness ruler and exploring cutting down, and Hana states she'd try the two weeks and cites dental health as a motivator. However, the psychiatrist does not actively deepen or reflect this change talk to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana's sustain talk ('I'm not promising anything yet,' 'I'd fold') is acknowledged without argument, which is good, but it is not strategically softened. The psychiatrist moves past ambivalence quickly into planning rather than using double-sided reflections to help her resolve it.
