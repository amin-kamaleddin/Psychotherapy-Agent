# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer consistently affirmed the patient's autonomy, e.g., 'I'd never tell you what to do' and honoring her self-generated plan and pace without imposing direction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer repeatedly paraphrased and reflected the patient's core concerns (social belonging, health, pride in small wins) with warmth and accuracy, including ambivalence ('both can be true').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintained a clear focus on smoking change, gently steering toward the patient's own reasons, goals, and a concrete starting point without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer worked 'with' the patient throughout, using evocative questions ('which one feels most possible to you?') and letting her construct her own plan and motivation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's choices were central — she chose the specific step, the wording for Jess, and the start day — while the interviewer explicitly validated her right to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong evocation of change talk: the interviewer drew out the patient's own reasons (teeth, pride, self-identity) and deepened them ('what would it mean for you longer term?').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer shared expertise only lightly, asked permission implicitly through open questions, and positioned the patient as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk escalated naturally from 'maybe cutting back' to 'start Monday' and 'this one's mine,' with the interviewer highlighting and slowing down on commitment language effectively.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('what's the point of one week?', fear of feeling left out) was met with non-confrontational reflection and reframed into the patient's own counterarguments rather than challenged directly.
