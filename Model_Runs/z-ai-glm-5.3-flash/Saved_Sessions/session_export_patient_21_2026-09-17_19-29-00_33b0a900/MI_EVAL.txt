# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout. Affirms the patient's expertise and self-compassion ('resting guilt-free is fine too') with no judgmental or condescending language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and goes beyond content to emotional nuance. Mirrors the patient's exact language ('letting that count,' 'on my own side') and captures key shifts like 'one more demand' becoming 'my version.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on physical activity, guiding the session from ambivalence to a concrete first step and success criteria. Each turn builds momentum while staying patient-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — the clinician never prescribes, and every plan element originates from the patient. Questions are genuinely curiosity-driven rather than leading.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy reinforced at every step ('whenever you're ready,' 'no pressure,' 'kindness either way'). The patient's closing statement about being 'given permission to do it my way' is direct evidence of success.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited from within rather than supplied, drawing out the patient's self-knowledge, coping wisdom, and own success criteria. So effective the patient independently names the change as hers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise within the patient ('You're the expert on your own life'). The entire plan is built from her own words and ideas, with visible patient shaping of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk skillfully evoked through open-ended questions and consistently amplified by naming the patient's shifts ('You're hearing it yourself'). Elicits rich commitment language like 'something I can genuinely start this week.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated and reframed constructively (e.g., tired days become information to design around) without being reinforced. Only minor gap: somewhat less active strategic pivoting, relying instead on the patient resolving ambivalence herself.
