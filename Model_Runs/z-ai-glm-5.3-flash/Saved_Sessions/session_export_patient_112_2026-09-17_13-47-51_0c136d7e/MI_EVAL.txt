# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains respect and neutral warmth without judgment, but affirmations are generic ('This is a great start') and fail to specifically affirm Rosa's strengths like her insight into past failures or her family motivation. No explicit normalization of her relapse or unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately capture Rosa's ambivalence, but empathy declines as the session becomes advice-heavy. Emotionally charged disclosures (fear of failure, smoking as identity/'thinking time') are met with facts about withdrawal rather than reflected emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation and progresses logically from ambivalence to concrete steps. However, movement to action planning outpaced Rosa's contemplation-stage ambivalence, feeling pushed rather than co-navigated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early collaboration exists (asking Rosa's preferences), but power-sharing tips toward the counselor mid-session. The pros/cons list is largely pre-filled, medication content is delivered as a lecture, and next steps are assigned rather than negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choices at several points, but also gives a prescriptive checklist (NRT, ritual restructuring, enlisting sister, avoiding smoking friends). Rosa responds by complying rather than self-directing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies on supplying information rather than drawing motivation from Rosa; most change talk emerges spontaneously, not from evocative questioning. Questions are largely closed and factual, and the readiness ruler is used mechanically without exploring her answers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership gestures (offering exercise choices, asking what feels most important), but the counselor frequently takes the expert role with prescribed steps, a medication lecture, and assigned checklists. Rosa's contributions shape little beyond minor choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rosa offers strong spontaneous change talk ('I know I've got to stop,' wanting to see her nieces grow up) but the counselor rarely elicits, deepens, or reflects it back. Instead of amplifying motivation, the counselor pivots quickly to logistics and planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced (a plus), but it is also never actively softened or reframed. Rosa's fears of failure, weight gain, and losing her identity receive factual answers and reassurance-by-information rather than double-sided reflections or strategic redirection.
