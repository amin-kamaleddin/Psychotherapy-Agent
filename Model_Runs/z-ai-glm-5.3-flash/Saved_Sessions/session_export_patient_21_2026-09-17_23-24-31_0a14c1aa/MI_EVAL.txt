# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated consistent warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard, with frequent, genuinely individualized affirmations ('You're the one who figured that out just now'). No judgment or disapproval was present, and Camila's low-energy days were normalized rather than framed as failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate throughout, including a complex reflection capturing her ambivalence cycle ('a routine with some give in it... because when it does fall apart, the discouragement makes the gap bigger'). Camila's repeated elaboration and self-discovery confirmed the accuracy of the reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on physical activity with a purposeful arc from assessing current state to a concrete plan. The clinician guided with purposeful questions while keeping the focus client-driven.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully collaborative — all key ideas (the ten-minute walk, sticky note, rain backup plan) originated with Camila and directly shaped the session's direction. Camila explicitly confirmed feeling respected rather than lectured.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership was reinforced at every step with no prescriptive advice or pressure; success criteria were defined by Camila herself ('Maybe two or three times total, and I actually let it count'). The clinician explicitly supported her right to set the bar where it felt doable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently drew out Camila's own reasons and strategies rather than supplying them, evidenced by her self-generated motto ('I've never once regretted a short walk afterward — never once') and her own coping plan. The clinician reflected these back rather than adding outside expertise.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly located expertise within the client ('You're the expert on your life') and every plan element came from Camila. She recognized this herself at the close ('one I came up with myself, mostly') — exemplary power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked through questions like 'What would need to happen this week for that ten-minute walk to feel like a success?' and explicitly named and reinforced ('That's change talk in action'). Camila responded with strong commitment and mobilizing language, culminating in a concrete, client-generated plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (all-or-nothing thinking, the 'too tired' voice) was acknowledged without reinforcement and effectively reframed by pivoting toward a low-energy version of the walk. The only minor limitation was that the final exchanges focused on eliciting obstacles, though this was done strategically for coping and planning purposes.
