# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician affirms the patient's dual priorities of motherhood and work as valid, framing the situation as 'a real bind, not a lack of effort' and consistently honoring her perspective and values without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Deep empathic attunement throughout, e.g., 'I hear the tenderness underneath that I hate asking'—the clinician reflects the emotional core of the patient's experience, not just the content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is focused on the patient's central problem (childcare blocking interviews) and stays there productively, steering toward solutions without detours.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently elicits the patient's own ideas ('what do you think might make it easier?') and lets the plan emerge from her—phone interviews, asking Ms. Tanya, backup options were all patient-generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly invokes the patient's expertise ('You know your situation better than anyone,' 'You know yourself best') and lets her choose which step to start with this week.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong change talk elicited through open questions: the patient names her own readiness (7–8), her own motivation ('my kids are watching me'), and articulates concrete steps unprompted.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuine two-way dialogue where the clinician reflects and builds on the patient's words ('rip the bandage off,' 'applying smart') rather than prescribing, resulting in a fully co-constructed plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician strategically amplifies change talk by highlighting 'I'm good at what I do,' pausing on 'maybe next interview, I just ask,' and reinforcing the shift from 'stuck' to 'not starting from zero.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician gently reframes sustain talk ('I hate asking,' 'I'm back to square one') by exploring what would make asking easier and prompting contingency planning—sister backup, aftercare programs—so one 'no' doesn't derail the plan.
