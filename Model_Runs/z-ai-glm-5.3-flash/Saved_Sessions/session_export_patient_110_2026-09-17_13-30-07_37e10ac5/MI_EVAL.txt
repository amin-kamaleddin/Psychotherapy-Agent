# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with warmth ('That's very fair'), but affirmations are somewhat procedural and deeper affirmations of Richard's strengths are infrequent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Brief acknowledgments occur but reflections remain shallow; rich emotional content (fear of 'admitting something's wrong,' retirement with Linda) is never deeply reflected before moving to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on cholesterol management and ends with concrete commitments, though direction is achieved through counselor-scripted information delivery rather than MI-guided exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative moments exist (drafted message, chosen check-in day), but the counselor supplies nearly all content, functioning more as expert consultation than equal-partner problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Option menus and validation of Richard's chosen lifestyle-trial path show solid autonomy support, though choices are pre-packaged and ownership language is implicit rather than explicit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Few deliberate efforts to elicit motivation; Richard's retirement goals and his father's experience are left unexplored, with no open or scaling questions to draw out his own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power sharing (asking which option sounds helpful, letting Richard pick the day), but the counselor frequently slips into an advice-dispensing expert role without soliciting Richard's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong change talk emerges spontaneously ('I want to be there for all of it') but passes without reflection, affirmation, or evocative follow-up; commitment language is never amplified or consolidated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (minimization, fear of medication, father's side effects) is not amplified, but there are few double-sided reflections or values-based reframes; the lifestyle-trial 'plan B' works as information rather than MI-consistent reworking of ambivalence.
