# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, warm, nonjudgmental tone, validating Isabella's ambivalence as normal rather than pathological. Warmth was somewhat formulaic and did not affirm her strengths beyond the plan itself.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections like 'staying home feels safer and showing up feels hard, so a 3 makes sense' captured surface content accurately. However, emotional depth around her history of failed attempts and self-doubt ('I still don't fully believe I'll go') was never explored, with reflections quickly pivoting to options and checklists.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on community engagement, progressing logically from exploration to a specific commitment (Thursday 10 AM walk with Dana) with contingency planning. No drift off-topic; each turn advanced the behavioral goal while respecting her pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Checked in before each step, adjusted the text tone per Isabella's feedback, and co-constructed a plan she endorsed. Full collaboration was limited because core tools (if-then plan, grounding script, checklist) originated from the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized client choice ('you can leave anytime'), offered menu options rather than directives, and tailored materials on request. Withheld from 5 due to a directive solution phase and occasional leading questions like 'Would that move you toward a 4?'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler and 'smallest, least scary first step' questions successfully elicited motivation and commitment language. Much of the session relied on supplying answers rather than drawing out her own reasons for wanting connection, which were never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Included Isabella in decisions, but mostly at the margins of choosing among counselor-generated options. The expert role dominated content creation (scripts, checklists, text drafts), and her own knowledge of what helps her anxiety was rarely consulted.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used the readiness ruler and small-step questions to generate commitment ('Yes, I guess. Book the Thursday walk') and mobilizing talk ('maybe I'd do the library thing after'). Fell short of 5 by sometimes supplying solutions and not deepening change talk by reflecting it back or exploring her reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Pragmatically redirected sustain talk, e.g., meeting her at the park to remove the escape route and the 'just 10 minutes first' if-then for anticipated cancellation. Some sustain talk, like 'I still don't fully believe I'll go,' was left unreflected rather than reframed toward her capability.
