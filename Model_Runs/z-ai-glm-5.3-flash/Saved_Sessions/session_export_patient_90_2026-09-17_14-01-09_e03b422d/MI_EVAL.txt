# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, especially around the sensitive dog issue, which Marcus explicitly noted he expected to be lectured about. Affirmations were present but somewhat formulaic, and warmth was brief and task-focused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected Marcus's overwhelm, fear of nighttime wheezing, and attachment to his dog without judgment. However, reflections remained relatively shallow, with several turns jumping quickly from reflection to advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept clear, consistent focus on asthma management, tying each step to the referral concern, with appropriate safety information. Direction was delivered via rapid multi-item agendas rather than collaboratively narrowing focus, risking overwhelm.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine back-and-forth with solicited preferences, budget adjustments, role-play, and a plan Marcus co-shaped and ultimately owned. Collaboration was uneven early on, with the counselor proposing most solutions before eliciting Marcus's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and honored Marcus's dog boundary and self-selected pace ('one step at a time'). Prescriptive checklist-style turns occasionally constrained Marcus's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong change talk emerged ('that's what scares me straight', 'I'll give it a real shot'), but largely from Marcus spontaneously rather than through skillful evocation. Key evocative questions about his values, confidence, and vision of success were never asked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some input was invited and Marcus's choices shaped the plan, but the overall stance leaned expert-driven with long prescriptive lists dominating multiple turns. Solutions were frequently supplied before exploring Marcus's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited commitment and ability language through doable-option questions and role-playing the doctor script. Missed opportunities to deepen the change talk, such as exploring what success would look like after the one-week trial.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled the dog-related sustain talk well by immediately reframing toward workable solutions, pivoting Marcus from hopelessness to possibility. Advice-giving sometimes pre-empted Marcus from voicing and resolving his own ambivalence.
