# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with validating statements like 'I appreciate you showing up' and 'I don't think that means you're bad.' No judgment of her drinking or secrecy, and her fear of being lectured was acknowledged without defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured her social motivations and ambivalence about costs. However, the emotional layer beneath (stress of secrecy from her mum, fear of being 'the weird one') was rarely reflected, missing chances to deepen emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use from the first exchange through a concrete experiment at Rachel's party and a follow-up plan. Every response built toward the target behavior with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided with permission-seeking, check-ins ('Sound right?'), and a co-constructed final plan. Deduction because several bulleted strategy lists were counselor-generated, tilting the expert balance slightly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy strongly reinforced: 'I'll follow your lead and support your choices,' options offered at every juncture, and the client's rejection of refusal lines accepted without pressure. Her comment that it 'felt like talking to a normal person' confirms the non-controlling stance landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good open questions and a well-placed confidence ruler drew out the client's own reasons and plan. Deduction because the counselor often supplied content (strategy lists, suggested numbers, pre-made lines) rather than consistently evoking solutions from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing: asked permission before advising, offered choices, and the client's ideas (sister backup, helping the host) shaped the plan. Deduction because the strategy menus were somewhat counselor-generated rather than emerging primarily from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked substantial change talk, including a self-generated plan, desire ('if mornings feel better'), and commitment to follow up. Could have deepened further (e.g., exploring why mornings matter) rather than moving quickly to strategy menus.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('some people do way more,' 'cringe lines') was acknowledged without arguing and reframed as costs she notices; the early double-sided reflection balanced both sides well. Missed exploring her ambivalence when she said 'two feels low,' and occasionally responded to resistance by listing more strategies.
