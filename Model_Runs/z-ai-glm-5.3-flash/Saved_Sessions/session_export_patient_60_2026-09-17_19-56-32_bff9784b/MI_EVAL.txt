# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, even when Jason admits to blackouts or blames others. Affirmations like 'That takes some honesty' feel genuine and normalizing without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including complex double-sided reflections and catching the significance of Jason's word 'yet'. Consistently mirrors his values and emerging ambivalence without distortion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior, with each exchange advancing toward change—from incident account to goals to concrete strategies. The closing summary ties change directly to Jason's stated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session reads as a genuine exchange between equals, with Jason's own ideas (buddy's water trick, roommate accountability) forming the intervention plan. No expert stance or pre-set agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ownership repeatedly reinforced ('that's your call and your life'), with no imposed advice and all strategies self-generated. Even the confidence ruler is framed collaboratively.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within Jason, who shifts from 'this isn't even my fault' to 'that'd actually be my fault this time' through questions and reflections rather than persuasion. Change linked strategically to his identity and goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jason's expertise is central, and the plan comes entirely from him. Falls slightly short only because some structured moves (confidence ruler, 'one small thing') were counselor-initiated rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation of desire, reasons, need, and commitment—from 'solid as is' to plan talk (water between drinks, stated limit) and a self-raised confidence rating from six to seven. Commitment language was self-generated, not persuaded.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged empathically, rolled with, and reframed toward Jason's own goals—never argued with or amplified. Double-sided reflections keep change concerns active while validating his perspective.
