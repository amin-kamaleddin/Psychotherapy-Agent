# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing past failed attempts and reassuring Linda that slips will be met without judgment. Affirmations were somewhat generic ('Nice work practicing', 'you've got this') and lacked individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Brief acknowledgments of feelings were present, but reflective listening was largely absent. The psychiatrist pivoted quickly to advice and logistics, leaving emotional disclosures like the fear after the lung doctor visit and shame over past failures unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintained a clear, undeviating focus on smoking cessation covering triggers, reduction goals, refusal scripts, NRT, and follow-up. The weakness is that focus was achieved through prescriptive advice-giving rather than MI-consistent guiding.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Surface-level collaboration existed, but the interaction was structurally unequal: the counselor delivered expert plans and scripts while Linda's role was mostly to accept or adjust them. Linda's own ideas were never invited into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices about steps, scripts, and meeting format, and honored Linda's preferences (every-other-week, 10 AM, in person). Fell short due to occasional pressure toward action and framing the plan largely on the clinician's own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely used evocative open questions, importance/confidence rulers, or exploration of Linda's hopes; her reasons for change emerged despite, not because of, the counselor's technique. The dominant mode was information delivery, assuming expertise lay with the clinician rather than the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda was allowed to pick which cigarette to cut and the meeting format, showing some power sharing. However, the overall stance was expert-driven, with the intervention content supplied almost entirely by the clinician's checklist.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda spontaneously produced meaningful change talk and the counselor supported agency by eliciting her choice of step. But change talk was never deliberately deepened or reinforced — no evocative questions, reflections of commitment language, or confidence rulers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced, which is a strength, but it was bypassed rather than acknowledged and strategically reframed. The forty-year coffee ritual, Doris's social pressure, and fear of public failure were left unexplored, so ambivalence remained unresolved.
