# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opens with non-judgment and consistently affirms Marcus's efforts ('Nice work naming a 6 honestly,' 'You did a strong thing booking and sending the text'). However, warmth is somewhat formulaic and needle anxiety is never explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Early reflections are accurate and insightful, including capturing Marcus's pattern of bracing despite things going easier. Emotional reflections taper off in the second half as the session becomes logistics-focused.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains exceptionally clear focus on blood sugar monitoring throughout, ending with a fully operationalized plan (Friday 10am nurse demo, reminder, note of questions). No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Joint problem-solving is evident in asking permission, incorporating Marcus's preferences, and the valued role-play intervention. Falls short because counselor's option menus and drafted scripts dominate agenda-setting rather than ideas coming from Marcus first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly frames choices as Marcus's ('Which feels most helpful?', 'as-is, tweak it, or role-play?'), and Marcus exercises meaningful choice. However, pre-built options subtly steer rather than inviting Marcus to generate his own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The confidence ruler effectively moved Marcus from 6 to 8 and drew out his own reasons and self-efficacy. Evocation is uneven, shifting later toward counselor-supplied tools rather than continuing to draw out his internal resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The tone is friendly and Marcus's input shapes the plan, but the counselor frequently leads by offering menus, scripts, and techniques. Power sharing is only partial as solutions are pre-packaged rather than drawn primarily from Marcus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk via a doable-step question and confidence scaling, with Marcus producing strong commitment language ('I'm not gonna let that be the thing that stops me'). A 5 would require deepening each change statement through reflection rather than moving quickly to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced; doubt is met with acceptance and redirection to manageable steps. However, softening is mostly implicit, lacking deliberate double-sided reflections, and his 'half-expecting something to go sideways' worry is acknowledged but not deeply explored and reframed.
