# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental, acknowledging stress and affirming client efforts. However, warmth is brief and formulaic, with few explicit affirmations of Daniela as a person or her efforts as a single mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; brief acknowledgments are immediately followed by advice, and the client's emotional content (complicated feelings about her mother, distrust of her sister, exhaustion) is never explored in depth. Empathy is stated rather than demonstrated through complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains strong, clear focus on the target concerns with each turn advancing a concrete step. Slightly over-structured, with the counselor driving the agenda via task lists rather than jointly steering toward client priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes some collaborative elements like check-ins, options, and role-play at the client's request. But most substantive content originates with the counselor, with genuine co-construction only around softening the text to her mother.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for preferences early and offers to adjust tone, acknowledging some choice. Still, the overall style is prescriptive—exact wording, task order, and coping tools are counselor-supplied rather than client-chosen.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely draws motivation from within the client; questions are closed and logistical, and Daniela's spontaneous motivation is not explored or amplified. Missed opportunities for evocative questions about her values as a mother and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Opens collaboratively but quickly assumes the expert role—drafting texts, writing scripts, and sequencing the client's week. The counselor's contributions dominate, leaving little evidence that Daniela's expertise shapes the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerges spontaneously from the client rather than being evoked; the counselor never uses open questions or confidence rulers designed to elicit it. Some reinforcement of client momentum occurs, but evocation is minimal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Client-expressed barriers are absorbed into problem-solving task lists rather than met with reflections or double-sided reframes. Sustain talk is not reinforced, but ambivalence is left unaddressed.
