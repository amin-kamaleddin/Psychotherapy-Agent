# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with affirmations that normalize Linda's fears rather than judge them. However, warmth feels somewhat procedural, with frequent menu-style offers that come across as scripted rather than personally warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections consistently and accurately capture Linda's perspective, and she repeatedly signals feeling understood ('That makes sense,' 'this is perfect'). Reflections occasionally stay at surface content, missing deeper emotional themes like her fear rooted in watching others struggle and her shame about past 'flaking out.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on consistent asthma management and inhaler use from start to finish, progressing logically from fears to psychoeducation, question list, script rehearsal, and follow-up plan. Every turn ends with a forward-moving, purposeful question without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely two-way, with Linda's ideas (pre-walk albuterol, spacer, calming line, note) materially shaping the co-created question list, script, and handout. The plan was built jointly rather than delivered by an expert.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Linda's ownership is consistently reinforced — information is offered with permission and choices are left entirely to her. She drives the plan herself, booking the appointment and deciding to try the daily inhaler on her own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited from within via choice of focus, her own questions, and personal goals ('feel better out with my dog'). However, evocation relies on practical planning rather than deeper techniques like confidence rulers or exploring what made past attempts falter.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda is clearly positioned as the decision-maker and her contributions directly shaped the session. Still, the frequent a/b/c menu format, while collaborative in form, sometimes feels pre-structured rather than fully open-ended power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully elicited and developed, anchored to Linda's own values (walking the dog, time with her husband), and grows visibly from willingness to commitment ('I'll book the appointment this week') plus behavioral rehearsal of her script.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged briefly then pivoted toward accurate information and choice, without being amplified. Some early reflections restated the barrier without double-sided reflections explicitly linking it back to her desire for control.
