# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive tone with no judgment, though affirmations are somewhat generic and formulaic rather than deeply personalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected the client's perspective on multiple occasions, but reflections were often immediately followed by question barrages or advice menus, diluting empathic depth and leaving the emotional layer beneath his reactance unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear and consistent focus on the target behavior, folding tangents back into the plan and progressing coherently from problem to plan to contingency planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used a collaborative stance with shared decision language and the client's input shaped the final plan, but the counselor generated nearly all options while the client merely selected or rejected them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and accepted the client's edits and rejections without pressure, but frequent advice-giving and pre-written scripts constrained the sense that solutions originated from the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain: the counselor's default was providing solutions (bedtimes, alarms, scripts, phrasing) rather than drawing out the client's own motivations, values, and ideas, leaving his intrinsic versus compliance-based motivation unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices from menus, but expertise flowed predominantly from counselor to client; when the client rejected a script as 'corny,' the counselor offered two more counselor-authored options instead of asking what he would say.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was present and readiness/confidence scales evoked some commitment language, but few open questions elicited intrinsic reasons for change, and the client's 'For me' versus external pressure distinction was never explored or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk and occasionally redirected, but spent significant session time on sustain talk themes (nagging, 'getting off my back') without strategic double-sided reframing, even scripting the conversation with Mom rather than pivoting to intrinsic reasons.
