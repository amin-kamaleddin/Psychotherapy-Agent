# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental throughout, normalizing ambivalence and affirming small steps without any labeling or disapproval, even when Kenji described drinking as his 'off switch'.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, emotionally attuned reflections throughout, capturing Kenji's ambivalence, the emotional weight of the doctor's numbers, and his emerging identity-level motivation about 'steering instead of drifting'.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear progressive focus from awareness to values, specific behavior, strategy, measurement, and success criteria. The session never wandered and landed on a time-bound plan with follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuine partnership of equals: the psychiatrist asked rather than prescribed and co-constructed the entire experiment from Kenji's ideas. Kenji explicitly noted no one had ever asked what he thought before.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions were repeatedly handed back to the client ('You'd know better than me whether that's realistic'), and Kenji's self-imposed limits (two nights, not seven) were honored without challenge. He left with a plan he built himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited from within rather than supplied—no arguing for change or listing risks. The counselor's reflections amplified Kenji's own meaning-making, deepening his commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('You're the expert on your own life'), and Kenji's contributions—the route change, Monday/Tuesday target, phone check-ins—genuinely shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open-ended questions evoked desire talk (not wanting to be like his father), self-generated solutions (not buying weeknights), and strong commitment language ('I'd rather the experiment work than find that out'). Kenji left with specific, self-generated commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged but never reinforced, using double-sided reflections and pivots toward Kenji's own identified 'crack' in the routine. Minimization ('everyone I know drinks') was reframed rather than dwelt on.
