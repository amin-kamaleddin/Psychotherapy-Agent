# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with thoughtful validation and affirmation of the patient's plan. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic (repeated 'I hear you' openers) and few individualized affirmations of strengths (e.g., quitting smoking).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections consistently and accurately captured the patient's perspective, including his ambivalence and family history. Reflections sometimes served as transitions to questions rather than deepening emotional understanding of his fear and family history.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained unwavering focus on cholesterol management, with every turn tied back to the medication decision or lifestyle plan. The session progressed logically from ambivalence to a concrete commitment with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor behaved as an equal partner, seeking input at each decision point and incorporating the patient's preferences into the plan. Some risk-disclosure turns were information-heavy and counselor-led, and multiple questions were occasionally stacked in one turn.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforced the patient's ownership, offering choices and asking permission before advising. The final plan was entirely the patient's construction, with no pressure or coercion anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful questions elicited the patient's own motivations, including past successes and confidence scaling. Evocation was somewhat inconsistent, with some turns defaulting to information-giving and key change talk left unexplored as motivational material.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly conveyed that expertise resides with the patient, asking permission before sharing and letting his preferences shape the session. When asked directly for advice, the counselor delivered fairly directive guidance with less back-and-forth negotiation than ideal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evoked change talk through questions about past successes, what would move confidence, and confidence scaling, and the patient responded with meaningful commitments. Emerging change talk was not consistently deepened or reinforced (e.g., 'It's not me just blowing it off anymore' went unaddressed).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of muscle weakness, cynicism about doctors) was acknowledged without reinforcement, and reframes avoided arguing. Some responses, like the detailed statin risk explanation, engaged objections on content grounds rather than strategically redirecting toward the patient's own motivations.
