# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor conveys unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly disarming shame ('This isn't about judgment at all') and affirming the client regardless of outcome. No labeling, moralizing, or disapproval is present even when the client minimizes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing ambivalence and deeper meaning ('That beer at the end of the day — it's just... mine'). The client's elaborations confirm the counselor understood beneath the surface content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains steady focus on alcohol reduction tied to blood pressure, advancing each turn toward a concrete plan without pushing. Direction is achieved through questions rather than steering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is fully co-constructed, with nearly every element (three nights, tea, balcony, spousal disclosure, follow-up) originating from the client. The counselor offers ideas tentatively while positioning the client as decision-maker.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is reinforced at every turn, with outcomes framed as learning rather than pass/fail and client ownership explicitly celebrated. The client directly acknowledges it: 'Thanks for not... pushing.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently draw out the client's own reasons, ability, and commitment, and the client arrives at his own conclusions and plans. The shift from skepticism to self-authored change talk is the hallmark of effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is actively transferred to the client, who substantially shapes the session content and plan. The counselor explicitly names this power shift ('You took the wheel: your experiment, your terms').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully notices and amplifies nascent change talk and uses strategic questions to elicit commitment language. The client's arc from 'I don't know if that does anything' to 'I'd keep going' demonstrates successful evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (minimizing, attributing outcomes to pills) is consistently acknowledged without reinforcement and handled with effective double-sided reflections. A minor gap: the 'pills do the heavy lifting' belief is never explicitly reframed, though the client largely resolves it himself.
