# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with unconditional positive regard ('a slip isn't failure, it's information'). Patient's self-criticism was met with normalization rather than judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflections captured the core ambivalence between social connection and drinking consequences. However, the loneliness driving solo drinking was acknowledged but not explored in emotional depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on reducing weekday drinking, with every exchange tied to triggers, a concrete experiment, and Thursday tactics. Even the separate solo-drinking issue was kept connected to the overall goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plan genuinely co-constructed — patient selected scripts, added her own wording, and chose tactics. The psychiatrist's options were refined and embraced by the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced patient ownership with open questions and 'your experiment' framing. No pressure, warnings, or directives; patient's closing 'ask me Thursday' reflects full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers effectively elicited importance (80) and rising confidence (40 to 65–70), with reasons and hope drawn from the patient. Some evocative moments shifted too quickly to supplying options and scripts rather than deeper values exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Patient treated as expert on her own life, with permission-seeking and choice among options; her contributions shaped the plan. Falls short of 5 due to fairly structured menus rather than fully open co-creation at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled, consistent evocation of change talk through open questions and rulers, generating concrete commitment language. Patient's confidence rose from 40 to 65–70 and ended with spontaneous hope.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'll probably cave', 'no social life') was acknowledged and the slip-reframe effectively softened all-or-nothing thinking. However, double-sided reflections weren't consistently used to pivot sustain talk toward change.
