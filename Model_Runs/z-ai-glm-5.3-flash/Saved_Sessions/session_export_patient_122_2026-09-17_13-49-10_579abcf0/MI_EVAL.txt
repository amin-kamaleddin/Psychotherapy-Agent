# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect, safety screening, and brief normalization are present ('your doubt is totally normal'), but warmth is thin and affirmations are formulaic. Sofia's self-criticism is not deeply validated beyond the scripted 'missed days are data' line.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections ('That sounds tiring and isolating') show some empathic attunement. However, reflections are minimal and quickly followed by advice, leaving Sofia's fear of failing, self-criticism, and emotional content largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a tight, clear focus on light activity and a concrete, realistic plan from start to finish. The focus is arguably too narrow, never revisiting the emotional eating and body-image concerns Sofia offered as agenda options.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite a superficially cooperative tone, the underlying structure is expert-prescribes, client-complies, with repeated step-by-step instructions. Sofia's useful insights about her own patterns are not drawn upon to co-construct the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offering structured options and asking whether Sofia wants to plan now or talk more honors choice to a degree. However, the session is heavily prescriptive (specific times, scripts, video titles, reminder labels), and Sofia's autonomy emerges from her own modifications rather than counselor invitations.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor relies almost entirely on providing solutions rather than eliciting Sofia's own motivations, values, or ideas. Sofia's motivation emerges spontaneously but is met with menus of options instead of exploratory questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely assumes the expert role, prescribing exercises and reciting clinical checklists while directing the session's content. Sofia's demonstrated expertise about her own habits is rarely acknowledged as a source of knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The option-based question ('Which feels easiest to try, and when would you schedule it?') effectively evokes commitment, and Sofia generates meaningful change talk herself. However, the counselor never deepens it—no exploration of why it matters or confidence/importance rulers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia's doubt ('every plan I make tends to fall apart') is acknowledged neutrally and the heat barrier is handled with practical indoor options. There are no double-sided reflections or deliberate pivots of her language toward change, making the response problem-solving rather than MI-consistent.
