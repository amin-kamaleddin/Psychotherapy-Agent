# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with thanks for honesty, but warmth feels formulaic. Affirmations are mechanical and safety disclaimers crowd out personal connection.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal, with only one real reflection ('that sounds confusing and a bit uncomfortable'). Emotionally significant content — drinking to shut his brain off, fear of what the log will show — is repeatedly skipped over in favor of question batteries and advice lists.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Stays focused on alcohol use and produces a concrete next step (two weeks of tracking plus follow-up). Direction is achieved through prescriptive checklists rather than guiding exploration, with premature action-oriented tools for a contemplative client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is markedly counselor-led, consisting of question batteries, bulleted instructions, and scripted safety reminders. Kenji's choice of tracking is accepted but not built upon; repetitive closers suggest a protocol-driven rather than joint process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine acceptance of Kenji's tracking-only choice ('totally okay to start there') and advice to consult his doctor first. However, directive advice menus dominate and explicit reinforcement of his ownership of the decision is absent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no evocative open questions beyond initial assessment; the counselor never asks why cutting back might matter or how confident Kenji feels. His motivations (sleep, liver numbers, wanting real answers) surface spontaneously but are never elicited or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor positions itself as the authority via bulleted instructions and directives; Kenji's 'is that okay, or do you think I need to do more?' reveals he seeks the counselor's approval. His expertise about his own life is not acknowledged or leveraged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerges spontaneously from Kenji rather than being evoked; the counselor never explores what matters to him or what success would look like. Change talk is met with generic praise ('solid plan') instead of being reflected, deepened, or consolidated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue with or reinforce sustain talk, avoiding direct resistance. But sustain talk is simply left unaddressed, with no double-sided reflections or strategic pivots linking drinking's function to Kenji's own concerns.
