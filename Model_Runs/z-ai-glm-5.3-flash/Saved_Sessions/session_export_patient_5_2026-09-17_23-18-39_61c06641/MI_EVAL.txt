# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, explicitly respecting Alessia's cautious, information-first approach. Her ambivalence about medication was normalized rather than judged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured both content and emotion, e.g., 'the tug-of-war' and 'it's not the medication, it's the combination.' The patient confirmed feeling understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus maintained on medication education and safe initiation of citalopram, with each exchange moving from concerns to a concrete next step. No drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions invited Alessia's input and her answers shaped each response. Collaboration was mostly question-driven rather than fully co-constructed agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice consistently reinforced—'what do you think your next step should be?' The psychiatrist never prescribed a plan; Alessia owned every decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out motivations (safety, feeling like herself, partnership in care) from Alessia's own words. The closing statement about being a partner in her care demonstrates successfully evoked intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing, positioning Alessia as the expert who built her own path to readiness. Not a 5 because an explicit statement of her expertise was missing and some reflections did interpretive work for her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Effective questions evoked strong change talk ('I'm tired of pushing through,' 'I think I'll be ready to start') and a self-generated action plan. The 'you built your own path to readiness' reflection amplified commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Self-stigma sustain talk was acknowledged via a double-sided reflection and pivoted to the client's own blood-pressure reframe. The self-stigma moment could have been explored more before pivoting.
