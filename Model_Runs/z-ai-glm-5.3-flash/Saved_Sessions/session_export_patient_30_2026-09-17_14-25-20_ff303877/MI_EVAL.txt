# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and respect throughout, with validating statements and no frustration when dismissed. However, acceptance stayed somewhat surface-level — the clinician missed opportunities to affirm David's honesty and courage in admitting he freezes up with doctors.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections early on captured David's core fear about medication side effects, but reflective listening faded once the session shifted to advice and checklists. David's emotional experiences — fear of dismissal, self-deprecating humor, genuine surprise — went largely unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, appropriate focus on cholesterol management and medication decision-making with no meaningful drift. Direction was achieved largely through clinician structuring and advice rather than guiding questions, keeping it from a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently asked permission before offering material and co-created a plan that David actively shaped. It falls short of a 5 because the substantive content (question lists, scripts, lifestyle steps) was largely pre-packaged rather than jointly generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's strongest domain — everything was framed as David's choice, and the clinician actively supported his right to defer medication rather than pushing adherence. David explicitly noted the absence of a lecture, and the final plan was entirely self-authored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some of David's own reasons surfaced, but mostly through practical structuring rather than deliberate evocative technique. There were no open questions exploring values, no confidence or importance rulers, and no requests for elaboration when David voiced commitment — motivation was accommodated more than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician offered choices, but much of the session became clinician-driven content delivery of question lists, scripts, and checklists. David's own ideas shaped the diet-first plan, yet the overall dynamic leaned toward consultant rather than equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Substantial commitment language emerged ('I'll call tomorrow, I swear'), but it was channeled by the clinician rather than evoked. The psychiatrist rarely deepened change talk with evocative questions or rulers — David's motivation was captured more than cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David's heavy sustain talk was handled well — the clinician validated concerns and pivoted to a concrete alternative pathway rather than arguing for medication. A 5 would require deliberate double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence rather than routing around it.
