# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout ('safe, nonjudgmental space'; normalizing minimization). Falls short of 5 due to formulaic warmth and few affirmations of Kevin's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections but generally shallow reflective listening. Kevin's embarrassment, work stress, and ambivalence were only superficially acknowledged, with complex reflections largely absent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on weekend drinking and closed with a specific, measurable plan and check-in. Deducted for premature shift to tips and logistics rather than exploring the behavior's meaning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some menu options and plan adjustments early, but the session became counselor-delivered tip lists with Kevin in a compliant 'sounds good' role. Kevin's own ideas were rarely sought or built upon.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choice and accepted Kevin's scaled-down goal and his declining role-play. Slightly limited by the advice-heavy structure that positioned the counselor as directing change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal effort to draw motivation from the client; solutions were supplied quickly instead of exploring Kevin's values, reasons, or confidence. Progress owed more to an amenable client than skillful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power sharing via menu choices and plan flexibility, but the middle became advice-heavy with detailed lists and scripts. Kevin adapted the counselor's plan rather than co-creating one.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged (e.g., not wanting another doctor's visit, feeling better Sunday), but mostly spontaneously. Missed chances to deepen, explore importance/confidence, or summarize his reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I don't really want to give that up') was met neutrally and redirected to harm reduction, avoiding amplification. But no double-sided reflections or use of sustain talk to develop discrepancy.
