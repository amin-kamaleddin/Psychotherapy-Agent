# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations ('That sounds really strong') and validated her rationalizations without disapproval. Falls short of 5 because warmth was sometimes formulaic ('totally understandable' repeated) and affirmations of her strengths as a single mother were not deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured her ambivalence well (e.g., balancing concern for her son with her need to decompress). However, reflections sometimes missed emotional depth—her poignant 'if it's easy, then I have no excuse left' admission received only brief acknowledgment rather than exploration of the shame beneath it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed well focused on smoking cessation in the parenting context and guided toward a concrete outcome (one-week patch trial, after-dinner craving strategies, day-8 check-in at Tuesday 5pm). Minor drift occurred when offering option menus, but each exchange returned to the target behavior with clear momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently sought permission before sharing information and co-constructed the plan from Natalia's own suggestions, reinforced by 'we'll troubleshoot together.' Some exchanges leaned informational (patch vs. gum pharmacology) with counselor-supplied option menus rather than eliciting her ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength—options were repeatedly framed as choices, slips were normalized, and her 'not all-or-nothing' condition was accepted without pushback. The counselor supported ownership rather than imposing control, reflected in her genuine self-direction at closing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions evoked motivation at key moments, but much of the middle session involved supplying options and psychoeducation rather than drawing out her own reasons and solutions. Deep motivations (what quitting would mean for her relationship with her son) were never explicitly explored, and the emerging commitment ('I can do a week') was only partially developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly invited her input ('Which of those feels worth exploring first?'), and her ideas (gum in the drawer, Monday start, written check-in) directly shaped the final plan. Falls short of 5 due to occasional slips into expert-informative mode with structured numbered option lists.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully used importance/confidence rulers and targeted questions that elicited her concerns and ended with clear change language ('Monday it is... I can do this'). Score limited because the counselor did not consistently deepen change talk when it emerged, e.g., not exploring why importance was only a 5 or what would raise it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk but sometimes reflected it extensively without pivoting toward change (e.g., repeated 'this is your one-thing' reflections without double-sided links to her values). Her minimization ('He's healthy') was acknowledged neutrally rather than gently reframed against her own worry about unseen harm—a missed discrepancy opportunity.
