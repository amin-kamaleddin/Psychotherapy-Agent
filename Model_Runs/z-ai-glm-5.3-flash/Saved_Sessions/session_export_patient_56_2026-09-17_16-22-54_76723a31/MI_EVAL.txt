# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard ('That's meaningful work,' 'Nice work, Isolde'), with Isolde's values treated as worthwhile throughout. No labeling or judgment was evident.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured her core dilemma (dinners and Sunday walks as anchors, weekday evenings slipping away). However, reflections were often followed by 2-3 stacked questions per turn, limiting depth of exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus from start to finish: mapping a weekday, selecting one specific change, specifying details, and scheduling a concrete Thursday 7pm follow-up. Every exchange moved the plan forward without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A genuinely joint process — mapping the day and co-constructing nudge language with Isolde's ideas shaping the plan. Slightly deducted because the psychiatrist sometimes supplied more options than a fully equal exchange would suggest.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent support of choice through offering options rather than directives and asking which drafts fit her best. Isolde owned every decision and the day/method/success criteria were entirely hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Isolde (success markers, confidence scaling) eliciting her own reasons and strong commitment language. Deducted because emphasis leaned toward tactical planning over deepening the emotional meaning of her core value.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Isolde as the expert, building the plan directly from her suggestions. Deducted because repeatedly offering pre-formatted message and note drafts took over generative work she could have done herself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked commitment and planning language ('if it goes well, I could build from there'; 'I'd say an eight'), culminating in strong commitment ('it's time my Wednesday nights showed it'). Relied heavily on practical planning questions rather than deeper values exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Addressed obstacles briefly and pragmatically with neutral, solution-focused reflections, without reinforcing reasons against change. Slight deduction for several turns focused on potential failure points, which could have amplified worry.
