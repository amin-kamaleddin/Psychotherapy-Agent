# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained unconditional positive regard throughout with a guarded, defensive client, never judging or arguing. Affirmations were genuine and specific, accepting both change talk and sustain talk alike.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, picking up subtle cues like 'uncomfortable as hell' and the tension between valued weekends and the cost of hiding. Reflections were complex rather than parroting, and shifts in the client's emotional position were named without judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor kept a clear thread on meth use and its health consequences, advancing toward a concrete two-week plan with each response. A 5 would show slightly tighter tethering to the target behavior itself, as the focus stayed on Adrian's health-and-honesty framing rather than directly naming reducing use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction read as two equal partners, with the session outcome (harm reduction and honest follow-up) entirely client-generated. The counselor offered no unilateral direction and co-constructed meaning through double-sided reflections.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforced client ownership ('That's your own idea,' 'the weekends are yours') and imposed no advice. The client left owning every element of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within the client via questions tied to his own values, eliciting reasons like fear of another infection and exhaustion of hiding. Change talk was developed into commitment-level language and a self-generated plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located within the client, and permission-style, client-centered questions shaped the session. Adrian's own ideas became the session's outcome.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions tied to the client's values evoked progressively stronger change talk, from 'I'd rather know what I'm putting in my body than guess' to a concrete plan. Each instance of change talk was reflected and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor rarely argued with sustain talk and effectively pivoted toward change, such as reframing weekends as not the target. Some responses restated sustain talk longer than necessary, and a 5 would require more consistent pivoting away from the minimizing framing.
