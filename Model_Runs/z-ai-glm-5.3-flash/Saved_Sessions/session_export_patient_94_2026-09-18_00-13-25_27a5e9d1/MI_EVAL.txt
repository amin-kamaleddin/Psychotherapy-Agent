# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard throughout, even under pushback and time pressure. Reframed her self-protective skepticism as care ('careful, not difficult') and honored her request not to make a big deal of it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, emotionally attuned reflections that reached the core of her exhaustion ('tired of 3 a.m. gasping getting old') and her fear of being judged. Reflections were concise, matching her hurried style.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained steady focus on daily inhaler use despite resistance, moving from refusal to a concrete one-month trial with specific tracking measures and a same-day action step. Nothing wandered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine peer-level exchange: honest side-effect information offered as an equal, choices about conversation direction offered explicitly, and her conditions fully incorporated into the plan. Playful humor reinforced the level playing field.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy reinforced at every step — 'your call,' 'your study,' 'your verdict' — including the explicit right to abandon the inhaler if data don't improve. No persuasive pressure or covert steering evident.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never argued for the inhaler; strategic questions like 'what would you count as proof?' turned her skepticism into investment. Accepting the null outcome evoked stronger commitment than persuasion would have.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise consistently located in the client ('you're the researcher here'); she set the trial length, dose conditions, and communication method, and the plan changed accordingly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked self-generated commitment by having her design the trial — she defined outcomes, set the duration, and volunteered 'part of me wants to be wrong' and a commitment to message her doctor. Each change talk statement was reinforced while preserving autonomy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (side-effect fears, distrust, no-commitment hedges) was validated but never dwelt upon, with consistent pivots back to her own criteria. Final hedges were reflected once and released rather than chased.
