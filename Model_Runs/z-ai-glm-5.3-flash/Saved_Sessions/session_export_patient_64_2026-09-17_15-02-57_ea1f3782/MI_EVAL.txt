# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with consistent validation ('That makes sense,' 'You make sense') and affirmations of prosocial motivation. Only slightly formulaic phrasing kept it from being flawless.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Jordan's autonomy concerns and motivations, including his worry about talking himself out of it. However, the emotional layer beneath his rationalizations and the significance of his grandparents were explored only lightly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on flu vaccination in every response, progressing logically from reluctance to motivation, concern-solving, concrete planning, and accountability. Each turn moved toward the change goal while remaining patient-paced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every response invited Jordan's input, and the plan was co-constructed (his pharmacy, his reminder label, his timing). Closing responses were slightly checklist-like, but power-sharing was genuine.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit choice menus, permission sought before offering information, and no pressuring language. The plan was entirely Jordan's own, exemplifying strong respect for his autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan's motivation was self-generated through open questions, particularly the grandparents connection. Minor limitation: later turns offered ready-made option lists rather than continuing open evocation of his own solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise clearly resided with Jordan, who shaped the logistics and outcome of the plan. Option menus, while collaborative, occasionally prescribed the framework of choices rather than purely open exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and developed change talk, from the grandparents motivation to commitment language ('I'll do it,' 'I'll book it tomorrow on my lunch break'). Reinforced with affirmations and an accepted accountability check-in.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was reflected neutrally without amplification and consistently redirected toward change; vaccine concerns were acknowledged as reasonable and answered with permission. Slightly less than perfect because sustain talk was sometimes restated fully before redirecting.
