# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental, and warm stance. Opening with "I'm here to listen without judgment" sets a supportive tone; multiple affirmations provided ("that was solid," "you held the boundary and kept the vibe"). FOMO and peer pressure normalized without labeling. Affirmations somewhat formulaic/brief and could be more individualized and depthful.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening ("it sounds like you're proud of the progress") and validation of weekend struggles. "How did it feel in your body saying that?" invited meaningful emotional exploration. Some reflections thin or skipped in favor of option lists; vulnerable admissions (going quiet after drinking, fear of "a fight I can't take back") received tips rather than deep attuned reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on managing alcohol use in high-risk weekend situations. Each turn advanced a concrete agenda: trigger identification → strategy selection → refusal role-play → partner text drafting → implementation cue (alarm). No topic drift; session closed with a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely egalitarian: Luka selected, edited, and rejected options; role-play conducted on his terms ("you be Dave... he'll push back at least twice"). Counselor accepted his refusal to role-play partner's reply without resistance. Contributions leaned toward pre-packaged option lists; agenda-setting more counselor-initiated than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced client ownership: "Which of those feels most doable," "Which one fits your vibe," etc. Adapted instantly to client modifications and explicitly supported his judgment. No persuasive pressure or directive language; client clearly experienced himself as choosing his own path, evidenced by the self-authored text message.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Role-plays drew out strong self-directed statements ("I'm not being soft, I'm being smart for once"). However, much input consisted of supplying solutions and lines to choose from, risking imported motivation. Deepest motivational material (violence history, fear of "a fight I can't take back," long-term vision) never explored via open evocative questioning; rarely asked about reasons, hopes, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned client as the expert: "Which one fits your vibe," respecting his rejection of option B without pushback. Luka's contributions genuinely shaped the session (support-request text, wording tweaks, alarm strategy, second role-play). Rapid-fire option menus at times felt slightly prescriptive, reducing full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked change language via open questions and rehearsal generating strong commitment/ability statements ("smart for once," "couple-max means couple-max," "sober me can handle an alarm"). Reliance on multiple-choice menus rather than open evocative questions; deeper motivation tied to core values (violence history, long-term goals) never explicitly drawn out.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk; pivoted to strategies and rehearsal rather than dwelling on resistance. Role-play converted sustain talk ("I'd probably just cave") into practiced change behavior. Occasionally listed options tacitly accommodating continued drinking without exploring alignment with longer-term goals; crucial admission about going quiet after drinks received tips rather than reflective reframe.
