# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard from start to finish: no judgment or disapproval, warm validation of her pub nights and honesty, and explicit affirmation of her self-generated plan ('You've just done something quite skillful'). Margaret's minimization is met with respect rather than correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening throughout—the ambivalence is captured precisely ('I hear two things at once'), and reflections repeatedly land, with Margaret affirming them ('you're right to press on that'). The counselor anticipates and mirrors her own insights back to her.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on reducing drinking with a deliberate arc from exploration to concrete plan and success criteria. Scored slightly below top because alternative-activity tangents (quiz night, jigsaw, sister) aren't always explicitly tethered back to the drinking-reduction goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is fully co-constructed: choice-offering questions at every turn, and the entire plan (lemonade, bottle off the counter, counting, tea) originates from Margaret. She sets the success criteria and follow-up format; summaries serve her goals rather than a preset agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently reinforced—her boundary ('I'm not quitting') is accepted without pushback, motivation is framed as hers, and the closing hands control to her ('jot notes, or trust yourself?'). No pressure toward abstinence; setbacks framed non-judgmentally.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key motivations—staying in her home, avoiding a second fall, independence—come from Margaret herself, drawn out by skillful open questions. The counselor never lectures or persuades, resulting in authentic self-authored commitment and plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located with Margaret ('you know your life better than anyone—your doctor, even your son'), and her contributions genuinely shape the session. Every element of the plan is hers, which the counselor explicitly names.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions ('what might feel manageable?', 'what would make this plan stick?') reliably elicit client-generated change language ('maybe just weekends', 'staying in my own home', 'it's worth a go'). The counselor strategically reinforces it ('that's your idea, not mine') and develops it into concrete commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally handled well with double-sided reflections that acknowledge without amplifying, and her 'not quitting' boundary is honored rather than argued. Scored slightly lower because the opening question invited extended early sustain talk before the pivot to her own concerns.
