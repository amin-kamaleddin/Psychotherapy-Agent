# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard shown throughout, including reframing 'soppy' as strength and normalizing a potential slip without judgment. No labeling or conditional regard anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex, accurate reflections captured the emotional core (shame, the partner's fear, self-trust), going beyond content restatement. The patient repeatedly confirmed accuracy ('you've nailed it, actually').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, purposeful focus on the target behavior from opening reflection through trial design to values clarification, with each exchange building progressively. No drift off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine equal partnership — the plan's elements (trial, note, partner involvement) all originated from Luka and were woven into summaries. The patient explicitly acknowledged the collaboration: 'Thanks for making me say it.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Luka designed his own trial, setback plan, and note, with the psychiatrist explicitly crediting his ownership ('notice who did the saying: you did'). No persuasive or controlling language appears.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully elicited intrinsic motivation, culminating in Luka's first spoken articulation of his core reason ('She's the reason, mostly'). The psychiatrist highlighted that motivation came from Luka, not the program or court.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Luka was consistently positioned as the expert ('You know those Saturdays better than anyone'), and his contributions substantively shaped the plan. The psychiatrist explicitly credited him for each element.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions ('what would give that trial its best shot?') elicited rich commitment language ('I want to be able to trust myself again'), which was then affirmed and deepened ('what was it like to hear yourself say that?').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective double-sided reflections paired doubt with capability, and resistance to lifelong abstinence was not argued against. One question ('what kind of person do you imagine that is?') briefly invited elaboration of sustain-talk imagery (the AA stereotype) before pivoting to change.
