# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Polite and nonjudgmental with a clear confidentiality framing, but warmth is generic and there is no affirmation of Isabelle's strengths (saxophone, IB workload management). Her legitimate question about the screening was somewhat dismissively deflected rather than validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal—one modest reflection early on, then the session becomes question-and-advice driven. The counselor misses Isabelle's curiosity, anxiety about why she was referred, and implicit defensiveness about substance use.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lacks focus on the referral concern (alcohol risk from the screening), wandering among safety checks, stress, sleep, and study tips. The counselor actively deflects when Isabelle raises substance use, so the target behavior prompting the referral is never explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is largely counselor-led with rapid-fire checklists and pre-packaged advice. Isabelle's attempts to shape the conversation are superficially accommodated but do not co-create content, making it feel like an information transaction between unequal parties.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers menu options and self-referral information, which is a strength. However, unsolicited advice bundles are repeatedly pushed even after Isabelle opts for brevity or declines plans, undercutting genuine choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation is treated as residing in the counselor's tips rather than the client. There are no open questions about Isabelle's values, goals, or feelings, and the counselor never asks what the screening or meeting meant to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor positions herself as the provider of tips rather than recognizing Isabelle's expertise, repeatedly offering prescriptive plans despite Isabelle stating she already time-blocks and knows her weak topics. Client contributions do not shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No effort is made to evoke change talk; the counselor never explores Isabelle's ambivalence about alcohol ('some people at parties drink, whatever') or asks about her own reasons, desires, or confidence regarding any behavior. No client language favoring change is generated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Isabelle expresses clear sustain talk ('I don't really need a whole plan,' 'it's not really a thing for me'), but the counselor simply acquiesces or supplies unasked advice. No double-sided reflections or strategic pivots are used to address it.
