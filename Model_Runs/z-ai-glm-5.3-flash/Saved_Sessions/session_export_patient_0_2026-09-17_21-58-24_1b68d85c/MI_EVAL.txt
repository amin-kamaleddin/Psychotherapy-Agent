# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, even when the client was minimizing ('my doctor's overreacting'). Phrases like 'Both of those parts are welcome here' conveyed full acceptance without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, emotionally attuned reflections captured the client's experience, e.g., naming the loneliness and shame of hiding the infection before he articulated it. Reflections like 'the scared part is still doing the steering' precisely summarized his ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed well focused on methamphetamine use and health consequences and progressed logically to a concrete, client-owned plan. Minor deduction: some responses circled on repeated themes rather than advancing, relying on long cumulative reflections.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently asked before directing and invited the client to shape each next step. All plan elements (fentanyl strips, truck weekends, telling Dane) originated from the client and shaped the session's outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit and consistent autonomy support: 'I'm not here to tell you what to do' and framing change as 'something you're choosing.' The clinician honored the client's request to keep the plan loose without pushing for commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within — the clinician never argued for change. Questions like 'Which of them has been louder?' elicited the client's own reasoning, culminating in his self-generated commitments ('that's non-negotiable now').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly located expertise within the client ('You're the expert on your life,' 'those are your ideas, on your terms'). Every plan element was credited to the client's own discoveries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions ('What would it take to keep weekends from costing you like that again?') reliably drew out client-generated change talk and concrete commitments. The psychiatrist deepened this talk with affirmations and follow-up exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections generally acknowledged minimization without amplifying it. However, some responses restated sustain talk at length before pivoting, and occasional pivots were slightly leading.
