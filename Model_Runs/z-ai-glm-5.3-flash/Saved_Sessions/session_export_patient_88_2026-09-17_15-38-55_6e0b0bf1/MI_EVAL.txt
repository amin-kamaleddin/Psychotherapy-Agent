# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Marcus's frustration at the referral without shaming. Warmth is somewhat formulaic and affirmations of his honesty and self-awareness are underused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections throughout, capturing his annoyance, sense that drinking is normal, and the close call with his mom. Reflections stay at content level and miss emotional undercurrents like feeling misunderstood by adults and peer belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use, converging on a concrete behavioral step (pacing, full cup, refusal line). Slight drift into counselor-directed risk education rather than progressive movement toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks permission before advising and co-constructs the plan by having Marcus pick tactics and adapt refusal lines to his peer culture. Still often counselor-offers-menu/client-picks rather than joint brainstorming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary: repeatedly frames every step as Marcus's choice ('You're in charge,' 'No pressure — your call') and accepts his non-commitment without pushback, strengthening his plan even when he hedges.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine evocation (likes/bugs question, 0-10 confidence, 'what would raise it to an 8'), but much content is counselor-provided and deeper motivations are never explored. Change talk is not developed after it emerges.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus clearly positioned as decision-maker; his ideas (full-cup tactic, Diego's line) shaped the plan. Most tactics and refusal lines were counselor-generated, only partially leveraging his peer-culture expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited useful ambivalence and a self-generated goal (not being 'the guy puking'), but Marcus's change talk ('pacing sounds easiest') was never deepened; the plan came more from counselor menus than expanded client motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing and reflects sustain talk neutrally, preventing defensiveness. Lacks double-sided reflections or reframing, and the risk-education list briefly amplified concerns Marcus dismissed, reinforcing sustain talk.
