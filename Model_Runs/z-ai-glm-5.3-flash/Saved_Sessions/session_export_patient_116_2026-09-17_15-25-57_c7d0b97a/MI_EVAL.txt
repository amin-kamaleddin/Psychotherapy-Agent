# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, never lecturing about medication nonadherence, which the patient explicitly appreciates. Falls short of 5 due to formulaic affirmations ('That makes total sense') lacking deep, individualized recognition of Samir's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture surface content (valuing control, feeling 'off') but are repetitive and formulaic. Misses the emotional weight of key experiences — feeling dismissed by his doctor, nagged by his mother, and fearing 'stumbling around in a fog in front of everybody.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session begins aimlessly, fully deferring the agenda to a deflecting client, with medication focus emerging only when the patient raises it. Once focused, it moves productively toward a concrete step (role-play), but early drift and frequent broad menus dilute direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission, offers choices, and follows the client's lead, but relies heavily on pre-packaged option lists. The script was handed over rather than co-created from Samir's own words, making power-sharing uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirms the client's choice ('I won't push anything on you,' 'on your terms,' 'I'll follow your lead'), and the patient responds well. Falls short of 5 because autonomy is expressed mainly through menu-offering rather than reinforcing the client's ownership of his own insights.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively elicits core values with 'What does being sharp let you do or feel that matters most?' drawing out work and family motivations. However, evocation is inconsistent — many turns offer options instead of drawing out reasons, confidence, or commitment from within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses 'I'll follow your lead' and defers to Samir's choices, respecting his expertise. Yet counselor-generated three-option menus dominate nearly every turn, and the pre-written script was provided rather than built from Samir's own language.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerges ('talking to him differently couldn't hurt,' 'that wasn't terrible') but is not strategically deepened with confidence/commitment questions. The counselor misses chances to consolidate Samir's movement with affirmations or summaries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoids arguing against or dwelling on the client's dislike of meds, acknowledging his stance neutrally and pivoting to values. However, prompts like 'talk through specific side effects you've had' risk amplifying sustain talk, and ambivalence is not actively reframed toward change.
