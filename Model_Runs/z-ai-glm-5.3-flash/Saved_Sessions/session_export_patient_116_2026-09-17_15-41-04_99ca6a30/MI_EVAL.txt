# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone even when the patient deflected with humor and requested an early exit; affirmations and honoring his 'no promises' conveyed unconditional regard. Fell short of 5 due to somewhat formulaic warmth and limited normalization of his ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections including capturing his underlying value of 'less nagging and more control.' Reflections occasionally stayed at surface-level content without exploring emotional meaning beneath his bravado.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept steady focus on medication adherence and safety, landing on a concrete behavioral step (photo of bottle, message to prescriber). Direction shifted partway from motivation exploration toward informational content, limiting progressive movement on ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered options and sought agreement before proceeding, presenting the plan as suggestions rather than directives. Some informational blocks (rundown, numbered plan) were delivered didactically with limited integration of the patient's own knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized the patient's choice with conditional advice ('If you want, I can...'), affirmed his 'just looking' stance, and honored his pace and 'no promises' at the close. Strong, continuous reinforcement of client ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened with good evocative questions, but the patient's minimal engagement led to a shift toward clinician-delivered psychoeducation as the main source of motivation. Little effort to elicit his own values or self-generated reasons for adherence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and asked the patient's preferences, letting his input shape the session. The informational rundown used an expert/lecture format and the plan was largely counselor-drafted rather than co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some nascent change talk emerged ('maybe I'll take a photo,' 'maybe I'll come back'), but largely from the patient's reaction to withdrawal information rather than deliberate evocative technique. Missed opportunities to deepen these commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Met resistance with acceptance and reframing rather than argument, pivoting from 'I feel fine' to exploring his actual withdrawal experience. Some early questions inviting elaboration of reasons against meds briefly amplified sustain talk before redirecting.
