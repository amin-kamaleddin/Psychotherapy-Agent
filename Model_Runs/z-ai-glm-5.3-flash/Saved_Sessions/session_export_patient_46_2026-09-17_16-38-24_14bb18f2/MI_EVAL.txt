# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with explicit respect for Ethan's choices and no moralizing. Affirmations were somewhat generic ('that's smart,' 'that's a real win') and could be more specific to his strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Ethan's ambivalence and minimization well, with good normalization of his hesitations. Deeper emotional reflections were missed, e.g., his frustration with parents 'riding me' and being tired of trouble.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Crystal-clear focus on reducing risky alcohol use from start to close, with purposeful progression through assessment, options, rehearsal, commitment, and safety planning. No drift into tangents, and a follow-up plan was established.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided session with permission-seeking, idea menus, and co-constructed plans like the check-in text. Ethan's comment that he expected a lecture confirms the successful egalitarian tone.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced through permission-seeking and options Ethan found acceptable, with his self-defined goals accepted without correction. The final plan was entirely his selection and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Ethan's own reasons for change ('tired of throwing up,' 'getting busted') rather than lecturing. Some moments supplied pre-packaged tactics and scripted lines rather than drawing out Ethan's own ideas, and confidence language wasn't fully evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing with permission-seeking and a plan built from Ethan's own selections. Some exchanges drifted toward prescriptive coaching with multiple tactic menus rather than purely drawing out his input.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and escalated from 'I could probably slow down' to a concrete self-selected plan with commitment. Role-play rehearsal and commitment questions deepened the change talk effectively.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement and met with neutral or double-sided reflections before pivoting to change. Moments like 'not quitting forever' and 'parties are more fun with a buzz' could have been more actively addressed with double-sided reflections or confidence-evoking questions.
