# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental despite resistance; acknowledged "It's okay to feel forced and not want to talk" and offered an exit ("say I don't want to and I'll stop"). No labeling or disapproval even when suggestions were dismissed as "babyish." Positive regard was somewhat procedural rather than warm; few individualized affirmations of client strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Adequate reflective efforts ("that sounds really frustrating, and it makes sense to feel blamed when fights with your mom feel mutual"; "leaving to your room can feel like it just makes things worse"). However, reflections were sparse; her key disclosure received only brief acknowledgment before pivoting to a tool. Deeper meaning of her defensiveness (feeling unfairly blamed, distrust of adults) only partially explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Maintained focus on family conflict and coping despite extreme resistance; clear agenda at start, focused closing question, and consistent return to concrete strategies (break scripts, safe place, cooling-off plan). Direction was partly achieved by accepting a very narrow scope ("one question, then I'm done"); final lengthy recap after client said goodbye showed poor attunement to the session's endpoint.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Operated as an equal partner — offered menus rather than directives and incorporated her own knowledge (bathroom as quiet place, locking door escalating things). Her contributions visibly shaped the plan. Less collaborative moments: final multi-step plan delivered as an instruction list; rehearsal suggestion pitched in a way she rejected ("that's weird"), suggesting imperfect attunement to an adolescent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Standout strength. Explicit choice from the outset (quick disclosure, grounding exercise, or stop); repeated permission to refuse; honored her decision to leave; plan framed as optional ("If you want, I can help tweak the wording later"). Her increasing voluntary engagement ("Maybe I'll come back") reflects the success of this approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Modest evocative skill — elicited her central concern ("it's not all me... my mom and me just fight") and drew on existing coping ("I already do the leaving thing") rather than lecturing. Limited by tendency to supply solutions rather than draw out her reasons, hopes, or confidence; no exploration of what made her willing to consider returning. Appropriate given precontemplation-stage constraints.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Conveyed that expertise resided with the client via repeated choices and deference to her preferences. Incorporated her detail about the bathroom into the plan; the plan was built largely from her own contributions. Falls short of 5 due to a fairly prescriptive step-by-step plan she did not help shape, and a closing monologue she had already disengaged from.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Some successful evocation despite minimal engagement — "Maybe I'll come back. Maybe not," "I guess saying something before I leave might stop her from following me," her script preference, and "this wasn't as dumb as I thought it'd be." The question "what's one thing you want me to know before you go?" elicited her key disclosure. However, emergent change statements were not deliberately reinforced or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk and resistance. Accepted the "I don't want to" boundary without argument and pivoted to choice; did not debate her dismissal of breathing exercises. Minor weakness: the end-of-session recap restated details after she said "I got it. Bye," mildly inviting disengagement rather than consolidating motivation.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **3.7/5**

**Summary:** A skilled engagement-focused session with a highly resistant, precontemplation-stage adolescent. Major strengths: exceptional autonomy support (consistent choice menus, permission to refuse, no pressure), respect for boundaries, elicitation of one meaningful disclosure ("it's not all me"), and incorporation of the client's own knowledge (bathroom as safe place) into a concrete, client-shaped plan. Session ended with client-generated openness ("Maybe I'll come back," "this wasn't as dumb as I thought"). Areas for improvement: deepen empathy and reflections beyond surface acknowledgment, deliberately reinforce and explore emergent change talk before moving to advice, avoid prescriptive step-lists delivered after client disengagement, and affirm client strengths more explicitly and warmly. Overall a solid MI performance adapted well to a very difficult engagement context.
