# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, unconditional positive regard throughout; Mai's fears of being 'difficult' or 'reckless' are consistently normalized and her skepticism is treated as legitimate rather than problematic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, culturally attuned reflective listening including double-sided reflections; however, reflections occasionally repeat the same themes without deepening into the emotional core of her frustration with her doctor's poor communication.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful focus maintained throughout, steering every exchange toward resolving Mai's engagement with the statin and anchoring a concrete three-month recheck plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative: Mai's ideas are adopted wholesale as the session's direction, the printed plan is offered as a suggestion, and the clinician explicitly frames her approach as 'partnership, not recklessness.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding autonomy support with explicit choices offered early and repeatedly, and Mai's self-authored plan is honored rather than corrected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within Mai, eliciting intrinsic reasons; some deduction because substantial direct education (statin mechanism, side effects) was necessary in this preparation-stage encounter.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located within Mai and her contributions genuinely shape the session; the educational content is briefly delivered in a somewhat didactic monologue, momentarily shifting the expert role back to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation of commitment language through forward-looking questions that generate Mai's own specific commitments—three-month trial, dietary changes, adherence plans—all in her own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled well with double-sided reflections and reframing hesitation as diligence; slightly more consistent pivoting toward her reasons for change would improve this.
