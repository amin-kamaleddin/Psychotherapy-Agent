# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, with affirmations of Richard's efforts and acceptance of his minimization and desire to keep beer and decent food. However, praise is somewhat formulaic ('Nice,' 'Nicely done') and lacks deeper individualized affirmations of his honesty and self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Content is reflected accurately and ambivalence is named, but reflections stay surface-level. Rich emotional material—fear of a heart event derailing retirement plans, guilt about his wife, relief at feeling less scared—goes unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on cholesterol management and builds toward concrete, actionable steps each turn. Points lost for drifting into card-formatting logistics instead of consolidating progress or addressing the core medication decision.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Richard's own ideas (walking, the beer question, phone card) directly shaped the plan, and his input was solicited before finalizing. However, bulked questions and options sometimes made the counselor feel like it was steering the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicit choice was offered on treatment options, walking plans, and doctor questions, with no pressure on the medication decision. Falls short due to option menus crowding Richard's authorship and no explicit reinforcement of his ownership of the plan at closure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions, open-ended prompts, and barrier exploration successfully drew out Richard's own reasoning and solutions. However, his core value—active retirement with his wife—was never explored or reflected as a sustained values anchor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration occurred, notably the wallet card built from Richard's beer-question suggestion. Yet many turns read as expert-driven checklists with stacked questions and pre-packaged scripts, underleveraging Richard's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of scaling (confidence 5→7), open questions, and concrete planning evoked strong commitment language. Missed opportunities to reinforce emerging change talk, such as reflecting his 'heart thing stopping me' statement as a value affirmation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally without amplification, which is adequate. But minimization and medication fears received no double-sided reflections or strategic reframing, and reassurance-heavy responses inadvertently reinforced anxiety.
