# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with frequent specific affirmations tied to the client's values and no labeling or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on accurately captured emotions and values, but reflections became less frequent as the counselor shifted to action planning. Emotionally rich moments received practical responses rather than deeper empathic reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on the target behavior (calmer handovers) from the first utterance through a concrete, time-bound plan, with logical progression and no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were largely built from Amina's own ideas with check-ins throughout, but the counselor sometimes presented complete solutions for ratification rather than co-construction, making some exchanges feel like expert delivery.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regular menu-offering and experiment framing supported Amina's ownership ('That part's on me'), though advice density (scripts, Do Not Disturb, police instructions) at times tilted toward directive problem-solving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, confidence rulers, and role-play effectively drew out strong intrinsic motivation and self-efficacy, but the counselor also supplied substantial content rather than consistently evoking the client's own solutions, and desire/need talk could have been deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client input was invited at key junctures and her ideas were incorporated, but the counselor frequently assumed the expert role with scripts, checklists, and protocols that shaped the plan more than the client's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation and confidence rulers produced strong change talk with confidence rising from 5-6 to 9, though some change talk stemmed from counselor-generated proposals and desire/need talk was underexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubts were briefly acknowledged then redirected to strengths and solutions without amplifying sustain talk, but problem-solving was used instead of strategic MI techniques, and 'what he might do' scenarios kept some focus on obstacles.
