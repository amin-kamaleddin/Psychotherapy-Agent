# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with affirmations like 'That's great — you handled it calmly' conveying warmth. Regard remains somewhat formulaic and performance-tied, without explicitly normalizing Oliver's guilt and ambivalence as valid parts of him.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers accurate reflections early ('torn between care and wanting your space') but quickly shifts to problem-solving mode, with reflections becoming rare afterward. Oliver's core emotional barriers — his guilt and fear of being a 'terrible person' — are managed with scripts rather than deeply reflected or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on the target behavior — setting a boundary and communicating a move-out date — with each element (scripts, role-play, checklist) building toward action. The session ends with a specific plan and timeline, though the direction is counselor-driven rather than collaboratively stepped.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is heavily counselor-led, with advice, scripts, and checklists flowing one-way and almost no joint exploration before solutions are offered. The role-play is the one genuinely collaborative element, but even there the counselor supplies and edits the words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Repeatedly prescribes solutions ('pick a firm move-out date,' 'send the text tonight') and offers choices only narrowly. Oliver had to assert his own authority himself ('I need to do that bit on my own'), highlighting that the counselor did not protect his ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies on an information-giving, expert stance rather than drawing out Oliver's own motivations, values, and confidence — questions are almost entirely procedural. His strong intrinsic motivation ('my job is done') emerges despite, not because of, the counselor's approach.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Operates largely from an expert stance, supplying scripts, checklists, and ready-made texts rather than building on Oliver's contributions. Oliver's good ideas emerge from him, not from collaborative exchange, and he had to claim power-sharing for himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Oliver produces strong change talk ('I'll send it tonight'), and the role-play format serendipitously gave him opportunities to voice commitment aloud. However, the counselor rarely deliberately evoked change talk with targeted questions about reasons, confidence, or meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not amplify or dwell on sustain talk (guilt, comfort in companionship), but also never strategically softens it. Ambivalence is bypassed rather than resolved, with scripts offered instead of double-sided reflections acknowledging both his care for the flatmate and his need for independence.
