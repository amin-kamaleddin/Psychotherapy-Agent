# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout — validating Jamal's resistance to adult pressure and framing slips as 'info not failure.' Falls short of 5 because affirmations are present but somewhat formulaic and lack explicit affirmations of Jamal as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections were made, but they were sparse relative to the volume of advice. Vulnerable material — loss of nighttime freedom and embarrassment about being stared at — was never deeply explored or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on punctuality and the driving sleep/phone behavior, with a coherent arc from barriers to a concrete experiment and follow-up plan. Not a 5 because direction came through rapid solution-finding rather than MI-appropriate guiding.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration was strong in the second half (Jamal picked his cutoff, reward, and alarm label) but started weak with a lecture-like menu of fixes. The early stance was expert-driven despite Jamal's signal that he'd 'heard it all.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear strength — the counselor framed the plan as Jamal's choice, invited him to pick behaviors, and affirmed his autonomy-sensitive alarm label. Falls short of 5 because early prescriptive advice subtly undermined the ownership message.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal's intrinsic motivations (avoiding embarrassment, reclaiming autonomy, self-ownership) emerged from his own readiness rather than skillful evocation. No open questions explored what being on time meant to him or his confidence, and the reward suggestion risked extrinsic framing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mixed message about where expertise resides — Jamal chose his reward and cutoff, but the counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role with menus of fixes. Jamal's pushback ('I know what to do — I just don't do it') signaled the advice-heavy stance was misattuned, only partially corrected later.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor reinforced Jamal's choices and let his ownership statements stand, but missed opportunities to deepen change talk. No follow-up on why punctuality mattered personally; change talk arose from client readiness rather than evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of failure, attachment to his nights) was bypassed rather than worked through — met with neutral acknowledgment and pivots to practical fixes. The 'info not failure' reframe was a good move, but no double-sided reflections were used to pair ambivalence with his stated reasons for change.
