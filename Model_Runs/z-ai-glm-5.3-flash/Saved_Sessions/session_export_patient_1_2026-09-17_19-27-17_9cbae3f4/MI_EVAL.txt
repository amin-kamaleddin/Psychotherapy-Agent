# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with genuine, individualized affirmations and full respect for the client's boundary ('Deal — fair is fair'). No labeling, disapproval, or pressure despite the client's initial defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including effective double-sided reflections ('unwind ritual AND want to know the truth') and reflections that landed visibly, as the client opened up progressively. Repeatedly checked understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on the target behavior with a coherent arc from minimization to a concrete trial plan. Minor tangents (wife's thanks, social logistics) and a missed opportunity for an explicit values-linking summary kept this from a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element originated from the client (month timeline, gum checks, Friday coping, Saturday ritual), with the clinician acting as consultant offering balanced options rather than directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: no pressure, choice space explicitly opened ('all or nothing, or a middle ground?'), and the client's condition (no nagging) honored. The client confirms ownership: 'a plan I actually chose.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out the client's own reasons, confidence, and values through open, gut-level questions. The client's motivation visibly expanded in-session ('almost makes cutting back more appealing').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the client as the expert, deferring to his judgment on scheduling and next steps. Power was equally distributed, with the client's imposed condition accepted fully.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk evoked consistently, from 'I'd want to know' through anticipatory coping and a self-negotiated concrete plan. The session was structured to produce the client's strong closing commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well — reflected neutrally, never argued with, and visibly declined across the session. Occasionally restated the pack-a-day comparison at moderate length before pivoting, though likely for rapport.
