# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms the patient's autonomy and expertise on his own life, treating him as the expert on his schedule and circumstances. There is no judgment even when the patient describes brushing off his wife or couch habits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly reflects the emotional core of the patient's statements, including his fear of overpromising and discouragement about past failures. Reflections like naming the worry underneath his hesitation demonstrate deep attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stays clearly focused on rebuilding an exercise routine while following the patient's own ideas for how to do it. Each response moves toward concrete planning without steering away from what matters to the patient.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently frames the patient as the expert and elicits his own solutions ('you found that idea yourself,' 'you're the expert on your own schedule'). The tone is side-by-side problem solving rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element originates from the patient, and the clinician explicitly highlights that he found the ideas himself. There is no prescribing or telling him what to do.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions throughout draw out the patient's motivations, risks, and deeper meaning, such as why walks with his wife matter and what made tonight feel different. The patient generates his own contingency plans and self-identified traps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue flows as genuine back-and-forth, with the clinician following the patient's leads and checking his perspective at each step. The patient does most of the talking and idea generation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician strategically amplifies the patient's desire, ability, and commitment talk, reflecting strengths and hope ('that hope hasn't gone anywhere,' 'that's you choosing something realistic'). This culminates in concrete commitment talk: specific days, times, and a plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** When the patient expresses worry about overpromising and being talked out of walks, the clinician acknowledges the ambivalence while highlighting his self-efficacy and past success. The patient's sustain talk ('couch wins,' 'that's my pattern') is met with acceptance and reframed toward action.
