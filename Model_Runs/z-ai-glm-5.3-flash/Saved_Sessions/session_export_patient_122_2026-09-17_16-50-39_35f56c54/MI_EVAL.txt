# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations and the 'data, not failure' framing that communicates unconditional regard. Falls short of 5 because affirmations are somewhat formulaic and Sofia's self-critical inner voice ('why do you even bother') goes unexplored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates accurate complex reflections capturing both content and emotional nuance, such as reflecting relief that the plan feels 'tiny and doable.' Empathy is inconsistent in depth—the emotional drivers of stress-eating, Sofia's self-distrust, and homesickness undertones are never reflected back emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains exceptionally clear, consistent focus on a single concrete target behavior: the post-work transition walk to interrupt evening snacking. Every exchange advances this focus, progressing logically from exploration to a fully specified, measurable action plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely co-constructed, with the final plan composed almost entirely of Sofia's own contributions and ideas offered as 'experiments.' Shared accountability is established ('I'll hold that plan with you') and confirmed by Sofia's own experience of the partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Sofia's ownership of every decision—she selects the behavior, days, trigger, backup, and check-in format, while the counselor never pressures or prescribes. Explicit choice points and asking permission before adding structure powerfully support her self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from within, most notably by eliciting Sofia's own expected benefits and self-generated success metric. Evocation is occasionally shallow—the opening menu presents counselor-generated solutions rather than eliciting Sofia's ideas first, and deeper motivations are left unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Sofia as the expert with questions like 'Which of these feels most doable?' and her contributions visibly shape the plan. Falls short because the counselor initially presents a pre-made menu of options and Sofia's 'Don't let me overthink it' hints at partially outsourced authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and builds on change talk through specific micro-goals, triggers, and a textbook confidence ruler with follow-up ('what would nudge that to a 7 or 8?'). Stops short of 5 because spontaneous change talk like 'maybe I can actually do it' goes unexplored and commitment language isn't explicitly elicited in her own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well without amplifying it, using the 'data, not failure' reframe and a self-compassion phrase to neutralize fears of failure. Doesn't reach 5 because double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence are absent, and the counselor pivots to solutions rather than dwelling with the doubt.
