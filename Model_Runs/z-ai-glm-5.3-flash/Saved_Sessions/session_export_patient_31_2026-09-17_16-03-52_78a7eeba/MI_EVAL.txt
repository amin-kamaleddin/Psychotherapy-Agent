# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard — normalizes slips, includes the client's requested 'don't feel guilty' wording, and promises 'no judgment.' No labeling or moralizing despite David's initial resistance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing David's core motivations and need for control. Reflections thin out in the second half as the session shifts to logistics, missing chances to deepen understanding of his stress, rituals, and guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction with no topic drift; every turn ties back to the target behavior. Ends with a fully specified plan, materials, and scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction — David's ideas (half-pack goal, doorstep coffee, fridge card wording, Tuesday 6 pm) are integrated verbatim, with approval actively solicited. David's willingness to give 'straight' feedback reflects an equal, safe partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is actively reinforced: David sets his own goal, format, schedule, and level of partner involvement, with choices offered throughout. Slips framed as data, not failure, and no pressure toward full cessation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocation through letting David set goals, pick the hardest cigarette, and articulate his own commitments. Deeper personal motivation (beyond partner pressure and health numbers) and confidence are under-explored; importance/confidence rulers weren't used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David's expertise is central — his goal, card format, timing, and partner-involvement choices are all honored and shape the plan. Detailed dosing/technique instruction, though appropriate, occupies considerable space and slightly tips the expert balance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Draws out commitment language by having David set his own reduction goal, identify the hardest cigarette, and design his routine. Would be strengthened by explicit exploration of David's own reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argues with sustain talk; validates 'I like smoking' and reframes the evening cigarette as compatible with change. Relies on reassurance and practical reframing rather than strategic double-sided reflections to pivot ambivalence.
