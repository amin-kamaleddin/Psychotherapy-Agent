# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates consistent respect and a nonjudgmental stance ('no judgment — just facts,' 'not a confession'), and the client explicitly felt unpushed. Falls short of 5 because warmth is somewhat formulaic, with little individualized affirmation of the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate early reflections (mixed feelings about smoking) but quickly shifts to problem-solving and logistics. The client's core concern about social belonging and isolation is never reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains consistent focus on smoking throughout, returning to concrete, target-relevant steps without drifting. Focus is arguably too narrow and action-oriented too early for a contemplation-stage client, but direction within the target behavior is clear.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers menus of options and offers to review tracking data together, showing some collaborative elements. However, ideas flow predominantly one way—the psychiatrist supplies templates, tactics, and plans while the client remains largely an agreeable recipient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirms autonomy ('no promise to change,' 'just information') and accepts the client's minimal commitment without pressure, which the client explicitly appreciated. Edges toward directive problem-solving with pre-formed solutions and closing advice rather than fully reinforcing client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies on providing reasons, options, and techniques rather than drawing out the client's own motivations. Beyond the rulers, almost no open questions elicit her reasons, values, or ideas; the client leaves still saying 'I don't know if I want to quit' with intrinsic motivation untapped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes permission-adjacent questions and lets the client choose her first step, showing some power sharing. But the agenda, template, options, and plan are all counselor-supplied, with no acknowledgment that the client is the expert on her own social world.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Uses importance/confidence rulers but misses the classic follow-up ('why a 5 and not a 2?'). No exploration of the client's reasons for quitting or hopes; most change direction comes from the counselor's suggestions rather than the client's own language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue against the client's attachment to smoking, which is a strength. However, sustain talk about 'instant friends' and social bonding is glossed over rather than rolled with using double-sided reflections or strategic reframing.
