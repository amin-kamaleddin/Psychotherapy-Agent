# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, explicitly validating Erik's privacy preference and normalizing struggles. Affirmations are somewhat sparse and formulaic, missing deeper individualized affirmation of his courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captures both change and sustain sides, including the deflating effect of gym comparisons. Reflections often pivot quickly to option menus, and the emotional core of Erik's shame isn't deeply explored at the level of feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on consistent gym attendance and self-kindness throughout. Every exchange moves the plan forward concretely with no meaningful drift off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans are largely co-constructed and Erik's own ideas shape the final plan. However, the counselor frequently generates option menus unilaterally, keeping some power unevenly distributed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently reinforced through permission-seeking, honoring Erik's refusal to text a friend, and framing all choices as his. Every commitment originates from Erik with no pressure or imposition.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from within via the confidence ruler and questions eliciting Erik's own reasons, strategies, and success criteria. Could explore deeper values beneath the behavior change rather than staying at logistics and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik is repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker and his habits substantially shape the plan. The counselor typically supplies the option menu rather than first eliciting Erik's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evokes change language with scaling questions and success-criteria prompts; Erik produces meaningful change talk and confidence rises from 7 to ~8. Misses chances to reflect and deepen change talk when it emerges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged briefly and empathically without reinforcement, then redirected toward doable actions. Redirections sometimes come quickly, cutting short exploration of ambivalence rather than resolving it through double-sided reflection.
