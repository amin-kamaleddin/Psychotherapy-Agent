# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with genuine congratulations and normalization of slips. Falls short of 5 due to formulaic, checklist-like delivery of bulleted advice lists that can feel impersonal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some validation present ('huge relief,' 'that makes sense'), but reflections are sparse and shallow. Missed a clear emotional cue when the patient said his wife's comment 'stung a bit,' pivoting quickly to advice instead.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus, moving purposefully from sleep to smoking success to alcohol use with concrete goals and follow-up planning. Progress feels somewhat prescriptive and clinician-driven rather than patient-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration—the patient's 3-drink counter-proposal was adopted and he shaped the refusal line. However, the clinician supplies nearly all strategies, and the patient's role is mostly to accept or modify clinician-generated plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers menus of options and accepted the patient's revised limit rather than imposing two. Undermined by heavy directives and 'quick tips' with little explicit reinforcement that the patient controls the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies heavily on providing advice, scripts, and structured plans rather than drawing out the patient's own motivations. His strongest self-efficacy statement ('quitting smoking taught me I can do this') was passed over without reflection.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for the patient's input ('Which feels most useful?'), but consistently positions himself as the advice-giver with dense prescriptive lists. Little explicit recognition of the patient's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient generates substantial change talk himself, which the clinician supports. However, the clinician rarely evokes or deepens it with open questions about confidence or what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('not that I think it's a problem,' 'two feels a bit tight') is largely met with advice lists rather than double-sided reflections. When the patient pushed back on the two-drink limit, the clinician immediately accepted the counter without exploring the ambivalence.
