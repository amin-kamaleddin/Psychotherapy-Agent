# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm,  respectful,  and  nonjudgmental  throughout,  including  normalizing  Lars's  literacy  struggles.  However,  warmth  is  brief  and  functional,  with  missed  chances  to  affirm  his  courage  in  disclosing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good  accurate  reflections  early  on,  but  empathic  reflection  drops  off  once  problem-solving  begins.  Emotional  content  like  fear  of  sounding  stupid,  peer  judgment,  and  identity  hope  was  repeatedly  glossed  over.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear,  consistent  focus  on  course  enrollment  as  the  target  behavior  with  no  topic  drift.  Session  culminated  in  a  rehearsed  script,  scheduled  call,  reminders,  and  checklist.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative  framing  exists  via  menu  choices  and  rehearsal,  but  the  counselor  generated  most  substance  (scripts,  tips,  checklists)  while  Lars  mainly  selected  among  options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices  offered  and  disclosure  respected,  but  style  was  heavily  prescriptive  with  rapid  advice-giving  and  pre-drafted  deliverables.  Lars  had  to  actively  push  back  to  slow  the  pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely  drew  motivation  from  Lars;  his  deepest  motivators  (avoiding  prison,  cars,  Donna,  identity  change)  surfaced  despite,  not  because  of,  the  interviewing.  No  rulers  or  explorations  of  his  own  reasons  were  used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid  power-sharing  —  Lars  steered  away  from  the  Donna  message,  chose  phone  over  email,  and  set  the  day/time,  all  incorporated  by  the  counselor.  Expert-heavy  deliverables  limited  his  own  input  somewhat.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Lars  produced  meaningful  change  talk  spontaneously,  and  commitment  devices  built  some  momentum.  But  strong  statements  were  never  affirmed,  reflected,  or  deepened  —  significant  missed  opportunities.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain  talk  (boredom,  peer  pressure,  fear  of  embarrassment,  self-doubt)  was  neither  reinforced  nor  actively  softened.  Peer-pressure  and  failure  worries  got  no  reflection  or  reframe.
