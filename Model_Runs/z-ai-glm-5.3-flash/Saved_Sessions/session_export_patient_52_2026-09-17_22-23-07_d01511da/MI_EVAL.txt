# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, unconditional positive regard throughout—no judgment or disapproval. Embarrassing disclosures are reframed as capability, and ambivalence is normalized as 'weighing, not weakness.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, layered reflective listening that captures subtle emotional nuances, such as fear of becoming like her mother and of being 'treated like a case file.' Reflections consistently go beyond surface content to meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the drinking behavior and underlying pressure, moving progressively from exploration to a concrete, specific plan. Every exchange ties back to the target with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session functions between equal partners, with Irina's ideas (bath, sister, social worker) driving every decision. The counselor invites her input on process decisions, including how to present herself at the meeting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor never prescribes, consistently reinforcing Irina's ownership ('That's you calling the shots'). Even the social worker referral emerges from her own decision-making rather than a directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is built entirely from the client's own material via questions and a confidence ruler, culminating in her self-authored summary and plan. Textbook evocation rather than persuading.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within Irina ('You're doing your own math here'), and the plan originates entirely from her ideas. The counselor elevates her contributions rather than substituting his own.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions and a confidence ruler elicit escalating change talk ('one drink instead of two' → 'I'll talk to the social worker' → 'I actually feel like I can do this'). The counselor even secures client-initiated accountability ('Ask me next time').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully with double-sided reflections rather than argument, effectively pivoting toward change. Minor deduction: early exploration of drinking's rewards slightly extended time on the status quo side.
