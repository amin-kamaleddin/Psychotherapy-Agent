# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, affirming Lars without judgment ('That takes guts, real guts') despite his criminal history and drinking. Restlessness and literacy struggles are treated without disapproval, and the closing is genuinely warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both content and emotion, such as naming Friday nights as the point where 'good judgment goes quiet.' The counselor repeatedly builds on Lars's own words (restlessness, boredom, the car dream), showing a nuanced grasp of his internal conflict and hope.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, progressive focus on avoiding reoffending through structured, sober Friday evenings and re-entry goals. Each exchange advances momentum toward a concrete plan, with every summary tying back to Lars's stated values and goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Lars's ideas drive every decision (go-karting, garage work, notes, Friday dinner), and the counselor frames help as shared ('Between your eyes and Donna's brain, we'll crack it'). Self-disclosure of being 'dead slow at writing' further equalizes the relationship.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced ('Your call, your pace,' 'in your own way, on your own terms'), and the counselor never prescribes. Lars generates all plans himself, and offers of help are framed as optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from the client through questions like 'what would genuinely feel worth staying in for?' Lars ends with strong self-generated commitment language: 'I actually reckon I can do this.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positions Lars as the expert ('You're the expert on your own life'), and the session's plan originates from his own contributions. Offering to help with forms 'together... your call, your pace' exemplifies power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoke desire, ability, and commitment language, which the counselor then highlights and reinforces ('the boredom doesn't even show up'). Change talk is tied to Lars's larger dreams and moves naturally into commitment about timing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (boredom, restlessness, embarrassment about forms, fear of seeming 'a wuss') is consistently reframed rather than amplified. Each piece becomes material for problem-solving, e.g., 'the forms felt like a wall; now they're just a step.'
