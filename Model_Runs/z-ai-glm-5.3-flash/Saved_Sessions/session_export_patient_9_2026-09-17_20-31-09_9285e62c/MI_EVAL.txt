# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, opening with 'this is your space.' Affirmations are specific and genuine ('That's not luck, Amina. That's you'), with no judgment anywhere, including toward the ex-partner or Amina's guilt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate, emotionally attuned reflections, such as capturing the 'deep-down tired' of loving her son while worrying conflict lands on his shoulders. Complex reflections mirror her ambivalence and extend her metaphors ('pouring from an empty cup... refilling isn't a luxury').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear arc from presenting distress to vision, barriers, concrete plan, and relapse safeguards, culminating in a specific self-generated action step. No aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every step is co-constructed through open questions, and the client's input demonstrably changed the session's direction ('I hadn't connected those two things'). Summaries weave her words back to her, reinforcing equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice is repeatedly affirmed ('this is your space,' 'That's yours, not mine,' 'you're deciding, not anyone pushing you'). Questions rather than prescriptions let Amina set the plan, pace, and safeguards.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from the client through open questions eliciting her values, strategies, commitment, and relapse plan—never argued for by the counselor. The session ends with her self-directed reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in Amina ('That's a strategy, and it's yours, not mine'), and her own ideas shaped the entire plan. The psychiatrist offered no advice of her own.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of change talk, eliciting strong commitment language ('I'm going to do it. This Sunday.') and reinforcing it with reflections anchored to her values, producing escalating commitment and planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced and is consistently met with reflective acknowledgment and gentle pivots toward change (e.g., the double-sided 'I hear both sides' reflection). Loses a point only because fear-of-relapse statements were recycled as evocation prompts rather than being pivoted away more decisively.
