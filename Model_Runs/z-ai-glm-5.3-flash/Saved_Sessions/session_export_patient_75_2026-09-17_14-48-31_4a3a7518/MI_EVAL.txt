# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental affirmations throughout, and the patient explicitly thanked the counselor for listening rather than lecturing. No labeling or disapproval; her fears were validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening capturing her ambivalence and 'skip when I feel fine' pattern. Reflections were occasionally formulaic ('that's understandable') and didn't fully explore her fear of dependence and shame about past lapses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on inhaler adherence from opening question through concrete next steps with no aimless drift. Ended with a specific, sequenced action plan for that same night.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative — co-created scripts, joint role-play, and preferences sought at decision points. The ICS information and habit-strategy list were somewhat delivered before Linda could shape the content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Permission sought before sharing information, options offered rather than directives, and the plan framed as her choices. Linda expressed clear ownership ('I can start that tonight') and the counselor never pressured.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within via past-success question and the confidence ruler; values (hiking, family, dog) were elicited. Some solutions were counselor-supplied rather than Linda-generated, and the ruler response wasn't explored further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda positioned as the expert — permission-based sharing, values questions, and her choices visibly shaped the session. The ICS block was somewhat clinician-delivered, keeping it from a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited values-linked change talk ('protects my hikes') and strong client statements ('I want to give it a real shot'), with confidence rising to 6. Change talk wasn't always deepened — e.g., no exploration of why she chose a 6 rather than lower.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and redirected toward values without arguing or amplifying. Reflections sometimes paraphrased sustain talk verbatim rather than using strategic double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
