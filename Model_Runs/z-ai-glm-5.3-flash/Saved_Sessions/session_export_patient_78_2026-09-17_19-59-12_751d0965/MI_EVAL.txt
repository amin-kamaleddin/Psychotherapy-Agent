# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, honoring the patient's boundaries ('don't push your luck') and explicitly promising 'no lectures.' Even resistance was met with respect rather than correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections captured both surface content and underlying meaning, e.g., smoking as tied to identity ('losing a piece of yourself') and her daughter's influence. The patient repeatedly confirmed feeling understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, progressive focus on smoking throughout, moving from exploring motivations to a concrete plan (10 a.m., one break, no cigarette) with no topic drift. The close consolidated her specific commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely between equals—the plan was entirely co-constructed, with the patient's agenda ('no lectures') governing the session's style and pace. Success was explicitly framed as her own discovery.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's ownership was consistently reinforced: she set the terms, timeline, and disclosure plan, and imperfection was normalized as useful data. No advice was imposed; every idea originated from her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from within—the patient generated her own reasons (her daughter, her coping strengths) and even formulated the experiment herself. The counselor supplied no reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient was positioned as the expert ('You know your rhythms best'), and her contributions demonstrably shaped the session. She chose the specific time, tool, and framing, all of which the counselor followed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions and reflections skillfully evoked change talk, moving the patient from 'nothing would help' to a self-generated experiment. The counselor deepened and reinforced her spontaneous commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was validated briefly and redirected without argument—resistance was rolled with grace ('not signing any pledges' reframed as 'gathering information'). No lectures about risks were given.
