### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, framing the referral neutrally and validating ambivalence. The reframing of Richard's self-criticism ('not a guy who doesn't worry as much...') is a standout moment of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures both sides of the patient's ambivalence, including the Tuesday-night burger versus the trail at 65. Consistently demonstrates deep understanding of the patient's emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully steers the conversation toward the patient's own reasons for change—retirement dreams, his wife, his dad's story—without imposing the doctor's agenda. Every pivot builds toward commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Constantly invites the patient's input ('what are your thoughts,' 'which feels most doable') and lets him generate his own solutions. The plan is entirely patient-authored, with the counselor serving as guide.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Emphasizes 'that's your reason, not the doctor's' and supports the patient's choice of starting point and commitment strategies. Reinforces his agency in designing around his own patterns.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Draws out powerful change talk ('eventually is now,' 'I don't want to be the guy huffing') through evocative questions connecting daily choices to core values. The patient articulates his own motivations rather than receiving advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works alongside the patient as an equal, highlighting his insights back to him and asking rather than telling. The tone is that of a collaborative exploration, not expert-led persuasion.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits and reinforces progressively stronger commitment language, from 'maybe starting with diet' to concrete weekend plans and specific walking days. Skillfully uses summaries and questions to deepen resolve.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Meets sustain talk ('steaks aren't going anywhere,' 'doesn't worry me as much') with acceptance and reflection rather than confrontation. Reframes doubts as ambivalence to be explored, never argued against.
