# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opens non-judgmentally ('listen without judgment and support whatever you want to work on') and consistently affirms Mateo's efforts and self-control without labeling or disapproval, even when he expresses skepticism about others' expectations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflects content accurately and validates strengths, but sometimes skips emotional nuance—when Mateo shares fear of rejection ('guys like me don't get first pick') and past freezing up, the counselor pivots to problem-solving rather than reflecting the underlying shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a relentless, clear focus on a single target behavior (asking Dave for more hours), with every turn advancing the plan—rehearsal, timing, accountability, and contingency scripts—without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo's ideas genuinely shape the plan (adjusting the script, proposing Marco, choosing Thursday 8:30), but the counselor frequently supplies the next step and pre-packaged scripts first, making Mateo more reactive than co-authoring.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames everything as Mateo's choice ('support whatever you want to work on,' 'want to role-play now or make a reminder plan?') and his ownership grows visibly, but the session is dense with counselor-supplied scripts and directives that reduce client-generated solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently draws motivation from Mateo rather than supplying it—eliciting his own strengths, step, accountability mechanism, and reasons, with scaling questions producing genuine self-generated momentum ('actually making a plan makes it feel more possible').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly asks Mateo's opinion and treats him as the expert on timing and accountability, but some exchanges slide into advice-giving mode with ready-made scripts offered before Mateo generates his own wording.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through strengths questions, repeated confidence scaling, 'what would increase it' probes, and planning questions—Mateo's change talk grows steadily from 'might as well' to 'I'll actually do it. Thursday' and 'I actually feel kinda ready.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Does not reinforce obligation-based talk and counters fatalism by affirming strengths, but fear of failure and rejection ('I'd probably just take the no and leave') is managed through task focus rather than deliberate double-sided reflections resolving ambivalence.
