# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with good affirmations and a helpful reframe of the patient's relapse as learning rather than failure. Warmth is somewhat brief and validation of her deadline anxiety could be deeper.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening that captures her ambivalence between connection and sleep, and validates fears about emergencies and friends. Reflections are often short and the emotional weight of her procrastination-panic cycle gets limited exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary focus on a single target behavior throughout, progressing from goal identification to obstacles to a crisply defined micro-plan. The session ends with a concrete, specific commitment with no aimless wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative with plans co-constructed and shaped by the patient's preferences. The tendency to offer pre-packaged solutions and stacked questions slightly dilutes equal-paced partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirms patient choice ('You decide what we focus on') and frames setbacks as information. Would be stronger if the patient were given more room to generate her own solutions rather than choosing among counselor-drafted ones.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently draws motivation from the patient via rulers, open choices, and amplification of her self-generated insight about the phone as an 'escape hatch.' Her climbing confidence ratings and self-directed commitment show motivation truly residing within her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient's expertise genuinely shapes the plan, and she chooses or tweaks each element. However, the counselor often provides fully drafted solutions, with the patient's 'lazy, but I'll take it' suggesting somewhat expert-driven problem-solving at times.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance and confidence rulers with repeated check-ins produced a visible climb in confidence from 4 to 8. The patient's organic commitment language ('saying it out loud now so it's real') demonstrates excellent evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about emergencies, friends, and self-criticism is acknowledged briefly and redirected to solutions without amplification. At times the counselor generates technical countermeasures rather than using double-sided reflections, and rapid-fire questions can spotlight barriers.
