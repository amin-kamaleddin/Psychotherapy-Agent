# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly affirmed the patient's autonomy ('that's completely yours to decide') and conveyed unconditional positive regard, including validating that he still likes smoking without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, accurate reflective listening throughout, e.g., mirroring the hassle of smoking outside in winter and the value of breaks with coworkers. The reflections captured both content and underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear focus on the workplace smoking issue and skillfully guided the conversation toward the patient's own solutions without forcing an agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently returned decisions to the patient ('if the decision were entirely yours') and framed the final plan as self-generated. Advice about gum was offered only after the patient asked.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong emphasis on choice and ownership, e.g., 'this stays yours' and 'no pressure here.' The patient's resistance softened as autonomy was honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions drew out the patient's own motivations—the cold, hiding at work, his daughter's reaction—and change talk like 'maybe I like my breaks more' emerged organically.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange felt like joint problem-solving, with the clinician inviting the patient's expertise on his own routine and planning ('you know your routine best').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully reflected and reinforced change talk, from 'I could just power through' to concrete commitment ('One Monday, one workday'), plus sustain talk was honored without confrontation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'm not gonna quit') was met with acceptance and reflection rather than argument, which allowed the patient to move toward change on his own terms. No confrontation or premature persuasion was used.
