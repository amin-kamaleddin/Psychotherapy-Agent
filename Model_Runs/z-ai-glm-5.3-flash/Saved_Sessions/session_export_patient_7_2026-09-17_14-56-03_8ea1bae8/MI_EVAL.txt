# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains consistent respect and warmth with a guarded adolescent and does not react defensively to criticism. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic; deeper, individualized affirmation of Amina's coping skills would elevate this.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate complex reflections capturing her frustration and distrust about confidentiality and problems at home. Empathy is occasionally diluted by long option menus, and the emotional toll of months of silence goes unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Prioritizing engagement over a target behavior is clinically defensible for this precontemplative client. However, repeatedly re-offering the same menu of options created circularity and felt like pestering; tolerating the requested silence would have given better direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The tone is collaborative and the counselor repeatedly asks for Amina's preference. But counselor-authored option menus put all the possibilities in the counselor's hands, and Amina's rejection ('I don't need you to teach me phrases') revealed a counselor-driven rather than co-created agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's strongest domain: choice is explicitly and consistently supported ('You choose the focus,' 'use this space however feels best'), and the request for silence is honored immediately without defensiveness. Confidentiality limits are disclosed transparently, respecting rather than overriding autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is almost never drawn from the client; important openings (relief about confidentiality, distrust of adults, fights at home) are met with reflections and menus rather than open evocative questions. The counselor's suggestions substitute for evocation of Amina's intrinsic reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine effort at power sharing ('You choose the focus'), but the pre-packaged menus suggest steering rather than following, and Amina explicitly rejected them. Asking what would be useful from her perspective would better reflect true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Essentially no change talk is elicited; small openings (her relief that things stay between us, why she stopped talking) are not deepened. Offered menus gesture toward this but are counselor-generated rather than open evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced and is accurately reflected, but never strategically pivoted away from. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with moments of openness (e.g., the 'mostly' in 'It works... mostly').
