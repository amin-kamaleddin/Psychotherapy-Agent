# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, validating both sides of Farid's ambivalence and repeatedly affirming his own thinking ('That's you doing the thinking, not me'). No judgment or labels appear despite Farid raising 'lazy' himself.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, deep reflections capture both surface content and emotional undertone, using Farid's own words ('the mountain in your head') and double-sided structures that precisely track ambivalence. The counselor even catches subtle shifts in his language from searching for time to claiming a specific slot.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange advances the target behavior of increasing physical activity, moving from first step to feasibility to a concrete trial (Tuesday, twenty minutes) with success criteria and relapse-proofing. Each turn ends with a forward-oriented question producing clear momentum to a specific, client-defined plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor behaves as an equal partner, framing questions as shared curiosity and positioning Farid as 'the expert on your own schedule.' The plan is co-constructed entirely from Farid's material, and he visibly internalizes the collaborative frame ('I'm basically building a trap for my own excuses').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces Farid's ownership ('you defined success on your own terms') and explicitly offers choice about pacing in the closing exchange. No advice is imposed; all strategies originate from the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The method is almost entirely evocative — Farid generates his own insights, from the all-or-nothing pattern to his identity shift and reason for change (energy). The counselor never lectures, educates, or argues for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Farid is explicitly positioned as the expert, and his contributions (calendar strategy, shoes, one-Tuesday-at-a-time pacing) genuinely shape the session and become the plan. The final question hands the pacing decision entirely to him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change questions consistently elicit commitment language, with Farid moving from 'maybe that counts for something' to 'I'll put it in my calendar before I leave today.' The counselor skillfully amplifies Farid's own change language without inserting his own agenda.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently validates sustain talk then redirects it toward change without debate — pivoting from 'feels silly' to what would make it legitimate, and from laziness doubts to what makes a walk doable. Doubt is converted into problem-solving rather than amplified.
