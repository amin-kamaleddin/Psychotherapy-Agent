# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and honors Aisha's request to skip the alcohol topic, but warmth is thin and transactional. No affirmations of her strengths (juggling work, classes, motherhood), and the session ends on PDF logistics rather than affirming her commitment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal — one brief early reflection ('You're juggling a lot') before pivoting to advice. Emotionally meaningful disclosures (cigarettes as her only 'me time,' fear for her health as a mother, 5/10 readiness) go unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently focused on evening smoking reduction with a concrete, time-limited plan (3-to-2 limit, tracking, follow-up) and respects the alcohol boundary. Loses a point for never clarifying the longer-term goal (quitting vs. reduction) and an ending that drifts into formatting details.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Advice-heavy and expert-driven, with three consecutive blocks of prescriptive tips and minimal dialogue. The workable plan that emerges is mostly Aisha's own common sense, not jointly generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respects the client's boundary on alcohol and avoids pressuring toward quitting or NRT, supporting choice. However, autonomy is supported by withdrawal rather than active affirmation — menu options are fired off checklist-style with little language reinforcing her capacity for self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is conveyed as residing in the expert's information rather than drawn from the client. Aisha's powerful reasons for change (cancer fear, motherhood) are volunteered, not evoked — no open exploration of her 'why' or developing of discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role with prescriptive lists and little client input. There is no explicit acknowledgment that Aisha is the expert on her own life, and the session closes on PDF logistics rather than collaborative consolidation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Aisha spontaneously offers strong change talk ('three instead of six,' 'my son deserves a healthy mother,' 'I think I can do that'), but the counselor never elicits, deepens, or amplifies it. No evocative questions, no exploring the 5/10 readiness rating — change talk is passively received and followed by prescriptive tips.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with sustain talk and doesn't push on alcohol after the deflection, preventing reinforcement of resistance. However, sustain talk is simply skipped over rather than addressed with double-sided reflections or reframing — neutral non-reinforcement rather than active softening.
