# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalizing every expression of resistance ('Both are welcome here,' 'No promises, no homework, no feeling bad'). Even disclosures like not touching the glucose machine in weeks were met with validation rather than judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate at both content and meaning levels, with a standout reflection catching the client's half-hidden change language ('if something had to fit somewhere'). The client's progressively deeper self-disclosure confirms the empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained unwavering focus on diabetes self-management while progressing organically from overwhelm to a specific, committed behavior and success indicators. Every question tied back to the target behavior without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely egalitarian—work was framed as joint ('Would it be okay if we looked together'), and the entire plan was co-constructed from the client's own stated constraints. No advice was imposed at any point.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively reinforced at every step ('I'm not going to add to that list,' 'no agenda attached'), with the week framed as an experiment rather than an obligation. The client's own ownership language ('Keep it dumb and small, it might actually stick') confirms he experienced genuine choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question drew out the client's own reasons and solutions, transforming a compliance-driven task into intrinsically motivated information-gathering. The client moved from 'nothing comes to mind' to self-generated commitment ('I could try that this week') without the counselor supplying any motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positioned the client as the expert ('You're the expert on your own routine,' 'that was your thinking, not mine'), and every substantive plan element originated from him. Permission was asked before offering direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and deepened through selective reflection and questions eliciting client-generated solutions and personal meaning. The client's trajectory from sustain talk to commitment language ('Doable... I could try that this week,' 'not avoiding it anymore') is directly attributable to these skills.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly, validated, and consistently pivoted toward possibility without ever arguing against resistance ('That's not lowering the bar—that's knowing exactly where the bar needs to be'). Repeated reframing turned concerns into conditions for workable change.
