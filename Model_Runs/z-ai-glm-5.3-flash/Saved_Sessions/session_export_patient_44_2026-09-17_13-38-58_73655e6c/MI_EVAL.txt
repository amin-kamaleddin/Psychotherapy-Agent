# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirmations of progress and normalization of slips, and the client felt accepted. Acceptance sometimes took a backseat to quick problem-solving, and affirmation of personal strengths beyond behaviors was limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections (back pain relief, better function), but empathy was inconsistent. When the client disclosed discouragement about comparing himself to younger men and frustration about scheduling, the counselor pivoted to advice rather than reflecting the emotional weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on exercise consistency and dietary change with concrete goals and a check-in plan. Direction was achieved largely through advisor-style tasking rather than MI-consistent guiding.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration—client input shaped specifics (off-peak times, voice memos), but the counselor offered pre-packaged strategy menus and checklists. The client mostly selected from counselor-generated options rather than co-creating ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client was given real choices and shaped the plan details, but the counselor's default was advice-heavy and checklist-driven. Autonomy was respected in form, yet prescriptive micro-instructions subtly pressed the counselor's agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's motivations emerged spontaneously (treadmill math, back pain relief), but the counselor rarely evoked them with open questions about values or confidence. Instead, reasons and strategies were supplied, conveying that solutions come from the counselor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role with prescriptive advice blocks and detailed checklists. Session direction was largely counselor-driven, with little acknowledgment that the client holds expertise about his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generated strong change talk (treadmill math, back pain as motivation, 'progress, not perfection'), but the counselor missed chances to deepen it. Key insights received generic advice or a single reflection rather than elaboration into values-based change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and was sometimes reframed, but doubts like 'I'm not sure I'd stick to that' and 'easier said than done' were bypassed rather than explored or reframed. The counselor moved to prescribing experiments instead of resolving ambivalence.
