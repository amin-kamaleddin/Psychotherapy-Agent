# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and respect, with nonjudgmental affirmations ('you're not stubborn; you're protecting a social ritual'). Affirmations were somewhat generic and the repeated scripted recaps felt mechanical.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections about the social ritual and loneliness, but reflections were brief and quickly pivoted to advice. Core ambivalence (belonging vs. health) and her fear of irreversibility were never deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on a concrete target behavior—a smoke-free Thursday break with a specific line, gum, and backup replies. Focus wavered in the middle during a barrage of general tips.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative framing ('we'll problem-solve together') and patient input shaped details (she chose the line and the day). However, the counselor delivered bulleted expert advice and the overall structure was pre-determined rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong choice framing ('Which idea do you want to try first?', 'Which line feels easier?') and no lecturing, which Elise explicitly appreciated. The option of not changing or slower pacing was never genuinely held open.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The importance ruler and open questions drew out Elise's own ambivalence and change talk ('maybe I try one night this week'). The counselor relied heavily on providing solutions rather than evoking her own reasons and strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early menu-based choices invited her expertise, but the counselor shifted into expert mode with rapid-fire tips rather than exploring her ideas first. When she asked 'What did you have in mind?', answers came from a preset toolkit rather than joint problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The importance scale elicited change talk and emerging hope ('I might actually do this'), but the counselor never deepened it through elaboration questions, confidence scaling, or strategic reflections. Evocation opportunities were present but underused.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not confronted, and the social bond was briefly validated. However, no double-sided reflections paired her social attachment with her health concerns, and reframe opportunities were missed.
