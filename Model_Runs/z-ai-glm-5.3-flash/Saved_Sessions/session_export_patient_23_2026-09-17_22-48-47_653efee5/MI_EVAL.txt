# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates consistent unconditional positive regard with genuine, specific affirmations ('You did two brave things there—you counted honestly') and zero judgment about the patient's drinking. Slips are framed as 'information, not failure' within a collaborative review rather than met with disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate reflective listening, mirroring the patient's exact words ('far away, not impossible') and capturing ambivalence precisely from the opening reflection. Complex reflections go beyond content to emotional meaning, e.g., 'you're standing up for how you want to feel.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear progressive focus moving from ambivalence exploration to a specific goal, concrete strategies, a monitoring plan, success criteria, and a follow-up, with no topic drift. Each turn builds on the last and closes with a concrete next step (bringing the phone in a week).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works fully between equal partners, framing the work as joint ('explore together', 'we'll see what the mornings looked like together') and never prescribing advice. Every substantive idea in the plan comes from the patient, with the psychiatrist contributing only reflections and evocative questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforces client choice throughout, fully accepting her self-selected two-drink goal when she rejects the seven-drink guideline ('There's no promise needed here'). The non-negotiable bath and movie rituals are honored, and the follow-up is offered as a question she genuinely opts into.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Draws motivation from within the client rather than supplying it, using open questions like 'What made that number start feeling possible to you?' Her strongest change statements—'far away, not impossible', 'a slip is information, not failure'—are all self-generated and then amplified through reflection and follow-up questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively fosters power sharing with repeated attribution of expertise to the patient ('Since you're the one who knows your life best', 'That's real self-expertise at work'). The entire plan originates from the patient, and the review-the-week check-in was co-created in response to her own worry about fudging notes.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes escalating commitment language ('I could actually start this week', 'far away, not impossible') through open questions about success on her terms and what shifted. Change talk is deepened by asking what made the number feel possible rather than letting it pass.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently met without argument or amplification—her rejection of the seven-a-week target is validated as 'your own middle path' and the non-negotiable rituals are accepted rather than challenged. The session's momentum stays with her own reasons for change while acknowledging her hesitations as legitimate.
