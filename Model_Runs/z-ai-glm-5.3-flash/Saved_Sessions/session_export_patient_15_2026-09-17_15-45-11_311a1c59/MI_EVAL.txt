# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with brief affirmations. Positive regard is somewhat formulaic and not deeply individualized to the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflective statements captured the client's situation well. Responses stayed logistical rather than emotional, with deeper meaning behind ambivalence and minimizing left unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on hypertension with a smooth transition to a concrete behavioral plan. The clinically relevant alcohol topic was allowed to fall out of the agenda entirely when the client deflected.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed with the client's ideas incorporated and affirmed. Collaboration was somewhat menu-driven, with the counselor framing the choices rather than fully shaping the session together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent permission-seeking and choice-offering with no pressure around the alcohol issue. Slightly limited by supplying concrete solutions rather than letting the client generate his own, subtly narrowing ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Importance and confidence rulers effectively drew out motivation and commitment. Evocation stayed at the surface of logistics, with the client's own values and reasons for change never fully developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine power sharing through permission and choices, but the counselor's expertise dominated the option list and solutions. The client's ideas were not actively solicited, so expertise was not located within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and doability questions successfully elicited commitment to walking with solid confidence ratings. Change talk was channeled into one behavior, and the client's tentative broader change talk at closing was not deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not argue with sustain talk and gently pivoted to a client-chosen behavior. However, alcohol-related sustain talk was avoided rather than worked with, using no double-sided reflections to address the ambivalence.
