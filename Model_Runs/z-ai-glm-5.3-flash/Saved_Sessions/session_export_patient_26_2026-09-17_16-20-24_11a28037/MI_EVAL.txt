# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with normalizing support for Daniela's disclosures about smoking and drinking. Warmth is somewhat formulaic and affirmation-heavy rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and captures emotional content, such as the frustration over unreliable childcare and fear of last-minute cancellations. Reflections are sometimes brief, shifting quickly to advice-giving before fully exploring the emotional weight beneath her concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays well-focused on childcare instability and cutting back smoking/drinking, moving consistently toward concrete next steps. Direction is occasionally over-strong, jumping to plans and options when deeper exploration might have served better.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Daniela's input on deliverables, and the role-play is genuinely collaborative with her edits shaping the final plan. Collaboration is limited by presenting fully-formed solutions (scripts, 3-step plans, A/B/C menus) first rather than co-creating from the start.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly frames decisions as Daniela's choice and does not push when she declines the quitline. Autonomy is somewhat undermined by heavy option menus and pre-drafted scripts that steer her toward the counselor's solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Questions do draw out Daniela's own resourcefulness (meal exchange, classmate swap, signal word), but much of the session relies on counselor-supplied content. Deeper exploration of her own motivations, such as her son and identity as a mother, is left on the table.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sometimes positions Daniela as expert through choice questions, but frequently assumes the expert role with scripts, plans, and structured options delivered without first inviting her ideas. Her meaningful contributions emerge mostly despite, not because of, the counselor's elicitation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions successfully evoke commitments ('the neighbor ask first,' 'I'm sending it tonight'), and affirmations deepen her mobilizing language. Evocation is often delivered via option menus rather than deeper open-ended exploration of her own reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoids amplifying sustain talk and offers a nice reframe of the quitline as 'an extra tool,' but relies on reassurance rather than double-sided reflections. Several barriers (daycare cost, distrust of family) receive only brief acknowledgment before pivoting to solutions, leaving ambivalence unresolved.
