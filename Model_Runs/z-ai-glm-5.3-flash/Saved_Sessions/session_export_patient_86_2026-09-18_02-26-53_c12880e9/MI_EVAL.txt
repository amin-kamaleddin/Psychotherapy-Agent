# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and respect throughout; explicitly honors Marco's boundaries ('you're not signing anything today, and that's completely your call') and receives his humor graciously. Affirmations are genuine though occasionally lean on generic refrains rather than deeply individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and often complex, capturing meaning beneath the content, and Marco's responses confirm he feels understood. Depth is capped slightly by staying light-touch on the underlying grief/fear around his cardiac and cancer history.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear trajectory from 'what's the point' to a concrete first step and a plan to sustain it. New micro-goals arise organically from Marco's own statements, and pacing feels patient-led rather than pushed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-way interaction with permission-seeking before information sharing and Marco's own metaphors shaping the plan; he notes 'nobody's asked me that before.' A brief shift into an informational expert role when presenting success rates slightly tempers the balance, though appropriately so.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support is arguably the centerpiece, repeatedly reinforcing Marco's ownership ('your pace, your call', 'this was your decision, yours alone'). Information is permission-based and even the final question strengthens his internal attribution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from Marco rather than supplied, with open questions like 'What do you think it would take?' and 'What do you think changed for you today?' His change talk—'maybe I'm not ready to let the cigarettes win the last round'—is elicited, not implanted.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is clearly located in the client ('You know yourself better than anyone'), and the entire plan originates from Marco with the clinician as a resource, not an authority. The clinician positions himself as available regardless of outcome ('I'll be here either way').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout domain: the clinician systematically evokes, reflects, and deepens Marco's change talk, moving him from 'I can't see myself changing' to a concrete step ('an hour, tomorrow night') and willingness to look at patches. The final autonomy question converts change talk into a self-reinforcing commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently pivoted away without dismissal, opening with a double-sided reflection that reframes resistance as ambivalence with a change lean; Marco's pessimism progressively disappears across the session. A couple of early reflections briefly restate the 'all-or-nothing has failed' history before pivoting.
