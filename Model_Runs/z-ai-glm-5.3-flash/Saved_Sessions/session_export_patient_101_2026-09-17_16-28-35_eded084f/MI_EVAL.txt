# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with specific genuine affirmations and no judgment about Mei's drinking or contraceptive lapses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Mei's situation and ambivalence, but reflections were often summary-style and the counselor rarely explored the anxiety or shame underlying her 'I don't really care' moments while drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use and improving contraceptive reliability, progressing logically from exploration to planning to commitment with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was co-constructed from Mei's suggestions at every decision point, though the list-heavy menus and drafted scripts occasionally felt more provider-driven than truly mutual.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: Mei chose the focus, set her own limits, picked the session format, and the counselor respected her preference against a check-in text.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within, with Mei articulating her own reasons and solutions and ending with self-generated commitment; confidence rose from 4 to 8.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mei was consistently positioned as the expert, with her ideas shaping the plan, but the counselor supplied many ready-made options and scripts that slightly tipped toward an advice-giving stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation with open questions, confidence ratings tracking growth from 4 to 8, and specific reinforcement of Mei's own change talk and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and pivoted to practical solutions like texting Jess and exit scripts, though no explicit double-sided reflections were used to resolve ambivalence, and the conversation moved to tools before naming the ambivalence directly.
