# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, with appropriate safety screening and affirmations of the client's choices. However, affirmations were brief and formulaic, missing chances to honor deeper individualized strengths like her weekly values-review habit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, but responses became increasingly checklist-like and advice-heavy as the session progressed. Emotionally meaningful statements — such as her worry about 'showing it, not just believing it' — passed without empathic exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on aligning daily behavior with the client's core value of family happiness, narrowing to a concrete 10-minute presence goal. Direction was achieved through repeated task recaps rather than purposeful movement, with final turns becoming repetitive reminders.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Friendly tone and invitations to agree, but the plan was largely delivered as pre-packaged bullet points rather than co-constructed. Collaboration appeared in word choice ('we'll troubleshoot') more than in substance, which was prescriptive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered choices, framed strategies as optional, and reinforced client ownership with phrases like 'we'll tweak as needed.' Points lost because options were counselor-designed rather than genuinely open exploration of the client's own preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Very little effort to draw out the client's own motivations, meanings, or confidence — no evocative questions about why family happiness matters or what the ritual would mean to her. The counselor responded to the client's rich self-generated material with tips and reminders instead of eliciting and expanding.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing, such as letting the client choose between two actions, but the session quickly became expert-driven with prescriptive tip lists. The client's own ideas were mostly unsolicited additions, and the counselor rarely asked what she thought would work best.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generated strong commitment language spontaneously, and the counselor occasionally anchored it to her values. But change talk was not actively deepened — no confidence ratings, no exploration of why family happiness matters, no consolidating of the client's own stated meanings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk was handled well by not dwelling on obstacles and proactively offering problem-solving options, preventing amplification of resistance. A point lost because solutions came from the counselor rather than being elicited from the client's own ideas.
