# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, framing Linda's questions as smart and responsible rather than non-compliant. No labeling, disapproval, or subtle judgment appears anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurately captures Linda's ambivalence and momentum. However, reflections focus mostly on content and progress rather than deeper emotional layers, such as the grief and fear tied to her mother's ER visit and years of second-guessing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains exceptionally clear focus on inhaler adherence, with every reflection and question advancing a coherent arc toward the doctor-appointment plan. There is no topic drift, and the session ends with actionable momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely collaborative, with the question list co-constructed line by line in Linda's own words. The psychiatrist acts as a practice partner rather than an authority, and Linda's ideas drive the plan's content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly reinforces Linda's ownership of choices ('You get to choose what works') and offers genuine options with no imposed advice. Linda's growing sense of agency directly reflects this autonomy-supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client, eliciting her own reasons for change: freedom, her husband, and avoiding her mother's ER outcome. No lecturing or persuasion appears; even factual questions are routed back to Linda's own plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located within the client ('You're the expert on your own life,' 'it's your list, your lungs, your life'). Linda's contributions visibly shape the session, making this active rather than superficial power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked, reflected, and amplified, moving Linda from 'it's been really difficult' to 'let's do it' in one session. The strategic sequencing of evocative questions is exemplary.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without reinforcement and often reframed toward change, as in the double-sided reflection holding both fears and risks of skipping. Some early reflections repeat the difficulty language without actively pivoting, and the sustain side receives substantial airtime mid-session.
