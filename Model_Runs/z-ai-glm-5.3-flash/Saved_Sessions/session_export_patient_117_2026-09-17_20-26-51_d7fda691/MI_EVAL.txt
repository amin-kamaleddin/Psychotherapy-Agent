# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, unconditional positive regard, and non-judgment throughout. Samuel's self-criticism ('weak-willed') was reframed into self-knowledge and his nervousness was normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, deep reflections that advanced the conversation, e.g., 'nothing to do but sit, and the cigarette just fits with it' and the tension reflection about not wanting to feel like failing his wife. Excellent perspective-taking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful focus on smoking reduction with strategic sequencing: journal review → work successes → lunch plan → wife's support → Monday's urges → reframing setbacks. Ended with a concrete, self-generated action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session operated as a genuine partnership, with each question built on Samuel's answers and plan elements originating from him. Client confirmed it: 'talking it through made the plan feel real.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice-giving or pressure; Samuel's pacing was honored and setbacks framed flexibly ('a slip is just a line in the book'). Closing reinforced self-direction and decoupled worth from outcomes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key insights and commitments came from Samuel via open, evocative questions like 'what would it mean for you if it worked?' The counselor never argued for change or supplied motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Samuel was consistently positioned as the expert ('What might help you in that exact moment?'), and every plan element came from his own reasoning. His closing summary showed the plan was genuinely his.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and amplified change talk, deliberately flagging Samuel's commitment language ('maybe telling her is worth a shot,' 'someone in my corner, no pressure').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubt and sustain talk were generally not amplified and were redirected to past successes. A few reflections briefly dwelled on worry before pivoting; more double-sided reflections emphasizing the change side would be even more efficient.
