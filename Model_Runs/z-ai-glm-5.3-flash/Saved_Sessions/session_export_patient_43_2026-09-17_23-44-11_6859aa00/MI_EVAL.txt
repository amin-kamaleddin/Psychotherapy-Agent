# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and nonjudgment maintained even against pushback, sarcasm, and sustain talk. Warmth is present but some affirmations feel slightly scripted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, often complex reflections that capture ambivalence well and track emotional shifts precisely. Some emotional content is skipped, particularly the grief/fear around his father's decline and the defensiveness about his son.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays tightly anchored to smoking with clear momentum toward concrete steps and self-defined success markers. Missing a strategic summary to consolidate the emotional themes circling the son and father.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong joint problem-solving with the plan assembled from Eric's and Sarah's ideas, but the counselor's question-reflection rhythm is somewhat unilateral and the honest-take moment is one-way information flow.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly and repeatedly protected with no pressure or control language anywhere in the session. Textbook autonomy support: no promises extracted, Eric sets his own terms and success markers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Evocation at a high level for a pre-contemplative client: Eric spontaneously discovers his own reasons to change through the counselor's questions. His closing self-motivational statements are direct products of this masterful elicitation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located within Eric and client-generated solutions substantially shape the session. The single advice moment is transparent and autonomy-preserving, though the counselor dominates closing summaries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout domain: Eric's language shifts progressively from external motivation to personal commitment ('I'll try the truck thing this week') through skillful values-based questions. Commitment language is amplified and client ownership repeatedly highlighted.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is rolled with skillfully via double-sided reflections rather than argument, with consistent pivots toward change. A few sustain statements pass without a pivot and the 'what am I supposed to replace that with?' moment is underdeveloped.
