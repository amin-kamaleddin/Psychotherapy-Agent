# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveyed warm, nonjudgmental regard, defusing Erik's expectation of a lecture and validating his frustration. He affirmed honesty about slip-ups and closed with unconditional positive regard ('small steps count').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening captured both content and feeling, including shift-work context and check-in preferences. Reflections were sometimes brief and quickly followed by option lists, and the emotional layer around 'pills for the rest of my days' was left unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on diet after alcohol was deferred, translating the agenda into concrete, time-bound steps with a Saturday follow-up. The alcohol concern was never explicitly renegotiated, and early turns drifted into generic option-listing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-authored, built on Erik's preferences (bread swap, tuna, packed lunch, keys cue). A few turns were counselor-led with rapid instruction lists that presumed the plan rather than negotiating it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was repeatedly affirmed, information offered as options, and Erik's boundary about beer was respectfully honored. Some framing steered toward the counselor's preferred actions, and ownership could have been more explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor drew out Erik's own reasons and solutions, shifting him from 'Not sure what you want from me' to 'I'll give it a go and tell you straight.' Motivation was channeled mainly into logistics, with few 'why does this matter to you' questions connecting to deeper values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik's judgment drove the plan, with his own ideas affirmed and substantially shaping the outcome. Two dense expert information lists temporarily took the expert role rather than first eliciting Erik's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of doable-choice questions and two confidence rulers elicited commitments and rising self-efficacy ('bread first,' 'tuna sandwich tonight'). Change talk wasn't deepened with elaboration on personal meaning, nor explicitly summarized as Erik's own.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoided amplifying sustain talk and respectfully did not press on the beer boundary. However, barrier-inviting questions echoed sustain framing, and statements like 'I feel fine' were moved past rather than strategically reframed toward change.
