# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opens with validating, nonjudgmental framing of Mateo's struggle, though warmth is somewhat formulaic and explicit affirmations of strengths are sparse early on. Later turns add more affirmation (e.g., 'that shows responsibility,' 'being able to say I'll be there is big').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections accurately capture Mateo's ambivalence, re-entry pressures, and fear of failure. Reflections could occasionally go deeper into the emotional weight beneath his worries.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (avoiding reoffending, employment, follow-through) across the session with no drift. Every element ties to concrete re-entry goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Turns are often dense and counselor-led, with scripts, checklists, and plans presented in packaged form before Mateo contributes. The later invitation 'Which would you like help with now?' improves power-sharing somewhat.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers help conditionally ('if you want') and lets Mateo choose options (text vs. call, neutral script, role-play timing), reinforcing agency. Falls short of 5 because the pace and content are largely counselor-directed rather than client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled use of importance/confidence scaling questions and open questions about what would move Mateo higher, eliciting his own motivations. Some turns supply substantial counselor content that dilutes purely client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites Mateo's input and expertise, but the agenda and solutions (disclosure line, checklist) are largely counselor-supplied. Mateo does increasingly shape the plan (choosing the neutral text, checklist first), showing modest power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits strong change talk via scaling questions and consistently reinforces it—affirming Mateo's honesty, follow-through, and 'I'll be there' commitment. Mateo's motivation clearly builds (from a 6–7 to an 8 on confidence).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk ('doesn't feel in my control,' 'mornings I start negotiating with myself,' dread of calling Ray) with nonconfrontational reflections and practical reframes rather than arguing. Strategic double-sided reflections to deepen softening are limited.
