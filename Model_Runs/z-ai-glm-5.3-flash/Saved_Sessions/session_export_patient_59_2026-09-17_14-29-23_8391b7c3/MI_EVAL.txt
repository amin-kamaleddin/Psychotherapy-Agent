# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained basic respect and a nonjudgmental tone, avoiding labeling or scolding despite Jamal's minimization. However, offered almost no affirmations or warmth beyond procedural politeness, making the interaction feel largely transactional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal, with factual recaps of intake data rather than reflections of Jamal's feelings. When Jamal expressed worry about the judge or his mother's fears, the counselor responded with information instead of reflecting his anxiety or frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the cannabis citation and court requirements, which is a strength. However, the direction was administrative rather than therapeutic, never moving toward exploring use, risks, or motivation for behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was largely expert-driven, with the counselor setting the agenda, supplying the court statement, and determining next steps while Jamal responded 'yeah, whatever, do it.' Jamal's contributions never shaped the session's direction or content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked permission before proceeding and offered options like online vs. in-person classes, showing some respect for choice. However, autonomy support was superficial — the counselor scripted Jamal's court statement for him and framed options around court compliance rather than Jamal's own goals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There was virtually no evocation — the counselor never asked open questions about Jamal's own reasons, values, or hopes, and instead supplied information, recommendations, and even a statement for him to sign. No discrepancy between Jamal's goals (summer freedom, college, ball) and his use was ever developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor positioned himself as a paperwork processor and court liaison rather than drawing on Jamal's expertise, using a rapid-fire intake checklist with minimal power sharing. Jamal's own ideas never influenced the session content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal's nascent change talk ('I'll knock 'em out,' wanting this behind him by summer) was never reflected, affirmed, or deepened. Opportunities to explore what getting this behind him would mean were missed in favor of returning to logistics and checklists.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal produced substantial sustain talk ('I don't have a problem,' 'it's California, no one cares') that went largely unaddressed. There were no double-sided reflections or rolling with resistance, leaving the ambivalence unexplored.
