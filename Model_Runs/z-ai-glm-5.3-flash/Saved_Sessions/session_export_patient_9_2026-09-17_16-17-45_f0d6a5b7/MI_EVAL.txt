# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout ("I'm glad you came," "That worry is totally reasonable" when the client asked "am I making excuses?"). Warm, judgment-free affirmations ("That's a strong, child-centered plan," "it absolutely counts").

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including skillful double-sided reflections ("You're torn — you know pausing can protect your son... yet the breathing feels awkward"). Reflections sometimes pivot quickly to action-planning rather than deepening emotional exploration (fear, guilt, feeling "wound up").

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the target behavior (reducing conflict exposure in front of her son) from the first question onward. Every exchange advanced toward a concrete plan; no topic drift; ended with actionable steps set up in-session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Plans were largely co-constructed; the client's instincts shaped the strategy. Some turns packed multiple suggestions/directives into a single response, momentarily shifting from joint exploration to a task checklist.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5
**Comments:** Choice consistently reinforced; explicitly honored the client's decision not to announce the plan. Final plan assembled entirely from client selections and acted on autonomously in-session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from within ("What would peace look like day-to-day?", scaling questions, affirmations of existing strengths). Client articulated her own reasons repeatedly ("He shouldn't have to hear that"); counselor never argued for change on the client's behalf.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as the expert ("Which of those feels most doable?"); ideas offered as options, not prescriptions. At times supplied considerable solution content (scripts, DND specifics), slightly tilting the expertise balance toward the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of future-oriented, scaling, and "what would move you up by one?" questions. Client's change talk grew measurably — confidence rose from 5 to 8 with specific in-session commitments; counselor reinforced commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ 4 ☐ 5
**Comments:** Generally reframed toward what the client can control rather than dwelling on the father's faults. However, some engagement with the problem narrative persisted, and opportunities for double-sided reflections tied to client values (e.g., "that's usually when I give up on these plans") were missed.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.6/5**

**Summary:** A strong MI session demonstrating near-exemplary technique. Key strengths: exceptional direction and focus, skillful evocation of change talk with confidence scaling from 5 to 8, consistent autonomy support, and warm, judgment-free acceptance. Areas for growth: deepen emotional reflections before pivoting to problem-solving, soften sustain talk more strategically with values-linked double-sided reflections, and reduce advice density in some turns to strengthen full partnership and collaboration.
