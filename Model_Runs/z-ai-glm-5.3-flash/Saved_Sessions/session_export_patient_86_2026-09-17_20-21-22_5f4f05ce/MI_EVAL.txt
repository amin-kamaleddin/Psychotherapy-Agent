# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently shows unconditional positive regard, immediately respecting Marco's 'no lectures' boundary without defensiveness. No judgment about his 40-year smoking history, even after serious health events, and potential slips are normalized without disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and consistently complex, capturing both the habitual and emotional weight of Marco's smoking. Reflections skillfully reframe ambivalence ('the path has to fit you') and pick up on subtle emotional content like pride and his partner's concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction and dental health with no topic drift. The session builds logically from exploration to a specific plan, support, and a Friday check-in, all without being pushy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly states and consistently behaves as an equal partner, with every plan element originating from Marco. The counselor asks rather than tells at every juncture, co-constructing the check-in rather than accepting a subordinate role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is exemplary, with the plan framed as 'yours, not anyone's prescription' and questions consistently hedged with 'if anything.' The counselor accepts Marco's pace, never pushing for grander commitment, and Marco confirms it felt like it was 'coming from me this time.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice or persuasion is given; instead, open questions and hypotheticals draw out Marco's own reasons and solutions. Change talk escalates naturally from 'maybe that I could manage' to concrete plans, with confidence amplified by recalling his past successful cutback.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Marco is repeatedly positioned as the expert ('You're the one who knows what "filthy" feels like'), and the plan is entirely client-generated. The counselor acts as a curious guide rather than an advice-giver throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions and vivid hypotheticals produce progressively stronger change talk, from 'can't see it' to 'I could give that a go this week.' The counselor amplifies change talk by highlighting Marco's movement within a single conversation, on his own terms.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently met with double-sided reflections rather than argument, honoring resistance while redirecting toward possibility. Fatalism about failing is reframed as a low-stakes, client-owned experiment, yielding client-generated change talk by session's end.
