# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, explicitly de-stigmatized the referral ('you're not in trouble here', 'nothing bad happens if you stop coming'), and validated Sofia's irritation without judgment. Warmth felt genuine rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, mostly accurate reflections, including a perceptive reflection of the client's implicit frustration. Reflections sometimes stayed at surface content and did not probe the emotional layer beneath her dismissiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on the alcohol-use target, linking questions and reflections back to party situations. Some drift occurred mid-session and the session ended without a concrete behavioral focus or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the conversation as mutual inquiry and built reflections from the client's input. Collaboration was strong in stance but somewhat one-directional in practice, with no explicitly negotiated agenda or co-created plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support—the counselor repeatedly stated the decision was the client's ('that's really up to you', 'whatever you decide is okay') and explicitly removed pressure. No advice, persuasion, or coercion was used at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used well-designed open questions showing evocative intent, including a skillful 'friend' question, but did not deepen minimal answers or connect the client's values to personal reasons for caution. Motivation never clearly emerged from within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly and explicitly positioned Sofia as the expert on her own life, and her contributions visibly shaped the counselor's subsequent reflections. Open questions invited her perspective rather than imposing an agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk was almost entirely absent, and evocative questions that yielded minimal responses ('sort of?', 'mostly just habit') were not followed up or deepened. A good closing elicitation also produced no change talk and was not pursued.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk, using double-sided reflections that paired it with client strengths. At times sustain talk was echoed back somewhat verbatim, and one exchange slightly reinforced the status quo rather than pivoting toward change.
