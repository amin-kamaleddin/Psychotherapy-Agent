# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with no criticism of the patient's habits and specific, genuine affirmations ('real problem-solving, Richard'). Ambivalence is normalized and the patient's strengths are reframed as wisdom.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is consistently accurate and deepens over the session, repeatedly capturing the feeling beneath the content (e.g., fear of the unknown outweighing medication worries, his wife's fear rather than nagging). Nearly every response contains a complex, accurate reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays firmly focused on cholesterol management and advances purposefully from exploration to a fully specified plan (fill prescription, question list, walk-and-talks, one-month follow-up). No topic drift; even relationship talk is folded into the change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every decision point is posed as a question and options are floated lightly for the patient to choose; the plan is co-constructed entirely from his own ideas. The counselor offers no expert directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor never prescribes or pressures, returning even medical questions to the patient and affirming ownership language repeatedly ('a change you chose yourself goes further than a perfect plan you resent'). Future self-management is also supported through self-selected accountability and reminders.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn systematically from the patient's own reasons (retirement, hiking, his wife), solutions, and coping imagery, then amplified through reflections. By session's end the patient expresses hope, commitment, and anticipated success — all client-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located with the patient ('you know your life best'), and all key plan elements — monthly steak, walk-and-talk check-ins, question list, follow-up — originate from him. Textbook power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions elicit escalating commitment language ('I'll do it. Both things,' 'I feel ready,' 'I kind of want to be the guy with the good news'), which is caught, reflected, and anchored to the patient's values. The session ends with detailed self-generated commitment and coping plans.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged briefly and skillfully pivoted to change, notably converting medication fear into a doctor question list and reframing willpower doubts as 'knowing your playbook.' Some reflections give fairly full voice to sustain talk before pivoting, slightly airing the status quo, though the pivots are always effective.
