# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard despite initial resistance; validated the client's attachment to smoking and affirmed his honesty throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections deepened the client's meaning, e.g., capturing both his valued autonomy and self-discovery about habitual smoking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking behavior while guiding steadily from resistance to a concrete, client-authored experiment and follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered options rather than prescriptions; the session plan emerged from the client's own experiment idea and was co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced client ownership: 'I'm not going to tell you to quit,' 'no grade, no pass or fail,' and framing his self-set terms as the ideal mode of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation from within rather than providing external reasons; change talk was entirely self-generated, including realizing the break matters more than the cigarette.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert who defines success; his contributions substantially shaped the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions evoked preparatory and commitment language ('maybe those don't need to stay,' 'I could do that'), and reinforced the client's self-authored framing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally acknowledged sustain talk without amplification and sustain talk diminished across the session, but early reflections gave generous airtime to reasons for maintaining smoking; more double-sided reflections could have resolved ambivalence faster.
