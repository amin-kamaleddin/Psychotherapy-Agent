# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance with clear validation of Tariq's frustration and affirmations of his progress. Warmth was slightly formulaic and did not explicitly normalize his anger or the unfairness of the disciplinary situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captured the emotional core — the loss of the call, fear of backlash, and relief a note would bring. Missed opportunities to reflect the deeper vulnerability beneath the anger, such as anxiety about release and his father's expectations.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, purposeful focus on reconnecting with family despite the phone restriction, moving progressively from venting to a concrete plan. Tariq left with a note written, a script rehearsed, and a refusal fallback ready, with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative — options were offered as a menu, the birthday note was co-authored with Tariq's own wording, and the role-play was iterative with feedback he requested. Tariq explicitly expressed co-ownership of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was consistently reinforced with questions like 'Which option feels most doable right now?' and Tariq selected the role-play scenario and next steps himself. Transparency about limits and lack of pressure led Tariq to own the outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Tariq's own reasons, strategies, and goals, surfacing family connection and his desire to finish clean. Evocation was somewhat task-oriented, with less exploration of broader values and confidence beyond the immediate goal.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Tariq as the decision-maker and immediately incorporated his contributions to the note and script. Partnership was practical and solution-focused rather than explicitly honoring his expertise about his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was elicited and amplified effectively, with 'staying on track' reflections leading to commitment ('Three weeks left. I can do three weeks') and role-play producing self-efficacy statements. Could have further developed commitment language and explicit confidence exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk about the officer without amplifying it, but often pivoted quickly to solution menus rather than using double-sided reflections. The blame narrative ('he has it out for me') was bypassed rather than reframed toward Tariq's agency.
