# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance, affirming Amira's courage and honoring her requests without judgment. Warmth was somewhat formulaic and repetitive, lacking deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening reflection and acknowledgment of isolation were appropriate, but reflective listening was sparse afterward as the psychiatrist quickly pivoted to advice-giving. Core emotional themes—loneliness, fear of the night stretch, and discouragement about past failed attempts—went largely unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing drug use, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete, achievable plan (7:30 disposal, coping strategies, crisis resources, follow-up check-in). Every turn tied back to the target behavior with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amira's input meaningfully shaped the plan (disposal choice, show strategy, timing, check-in arrangement), but the psychiatrist dominated with pre-packaged advice and the patient remained largely passive. The exchange was more counselor-led than genuinely joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist affirmed choice and asked permission for several elements, but the overall style was notably directive, prescribing multi-step plans and techniques rapidly. He did honor her refusals (walking, chatline), which kept the score from being lower.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening question successfully drew out Amira's desire for change and her willingness to act, and she generated meaningful commitments. However, motivation was treated as largely logistical thereafter, with little exploration of her internal reasons, hopes, or what might make this attempt different.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative elements appeared (offering choices about check-in, asking which step to map out), but the psychiatrist repeatedly assumed the expert role with prescriptive step packages and detailed protocols. He did adapt to her pushback, which kept this above a 2.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist actively elicited change-oriented language, and Amira generated meaningful commitments (disposal plan, specific time, check-in) that he reinforced well. A 5 would require more systematic deepening of her own motivations, values, and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amira offered substantial sustain talk (helplessness, isolation, fear of the night, past failed attempts), but the psychiatrist rarely reflected or softened it, instead skipping to logistics. Her final relapse doubt received no response at all—a significant missed moment.
