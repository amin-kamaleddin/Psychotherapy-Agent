# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with specific client-tailored affirmations, though praise occasionally feels stacked or formulaic, slightly diluting genuineness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate double-sided reflections captured both sides of ambivalence, but reflections were often embedded in praise-laden summaries and deeper emotional layers (fear of aging, vulnerability) went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus from ambivalence to concrete commitments (talking to wife, filling prescription), with each exchange moving the client forward without dragging.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions outnumber statements and plans evolved from Richard's responses, but summaries sometimes arrived pre-packaged without his confirmation, and agenda negotiation was not explicit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support — ownership repeatedly returned to the client and options emphasized over mandates, with the client confirming the shift became about 'what I want.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All meaningful motivations came from Richard's own words — the retirement vision, team framing, and commitments — with the clinician asking rather than lecturing about why change matters.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Richard was consistently treated as the expert on his own life, with his contributions genuinely shaping the plan (portion approach, dinner conversation, follow-up schedule) — the highest level of power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength — the pivotal 'what made the difference' question evoked rich spontaneous change talk, and follow-ups deepened commitment through explicit statements like 'I'm ready.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged via double-sided reflection and pivoted toward values rather than reinforced, though the direct reassurance 'most people do fine' missed a chance to evoke the client's own coping confidence.
