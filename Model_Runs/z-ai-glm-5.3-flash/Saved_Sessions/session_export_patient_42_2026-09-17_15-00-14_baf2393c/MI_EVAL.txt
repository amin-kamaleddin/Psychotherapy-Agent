# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental opening and consistent validation of Elise's skepticism and the legitimacy of smoking as social bonding. Loses a point for occasional formulaic affirmations ('Nice move') and few explicit affirmations of her personal strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate simple reflections early on, but emotional nuances like Elise's fear of exclusion and her tentative hope are acknowledged only superficially or skipped. Complex reflections of feeling are rare, and reflections often blend into multi-question bundles.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on smoking cessation, funneling from ambivalence to a concrete plan without the session wandering. The focus is sometimes diluted by heavy emphasis on social logistics, and deeper health-related motivations go unexplored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed from Elise's own ideas, and the role-play of Marie is an excellent collaborative device. Points lost because the counselor drives agenda transitions (exploration to menu to ruler to role-play) rather than co-negotiating session structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: options framed as choices, veto power repeatedly offered, and tentative 'maybe' respected rather than pushed. Slightly prescriptive confidence-ruler framing ('If you're under a 7, we can brainstorm') subtly steers the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Practical change language is elicited well, but deeper values-based motivation—belonging, her self-image as manager, what cutting back would mean for her life—is never explored. Motivation elicited is largely logistical rather than internal.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elise is treated as the expert and her choices visibly shape the plan (evening cigarette, Marie, Thursday timing). Points lost due to an extensive strategy menu that shifts toward advice-giving, with perfunctory permission-seeking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk grows effectively from 'maybe' to concrete commitments via the doable-change question, confidence ruler, and self-generated planning. However, change talk is not deepened with follow-up questions about why the change matters or what success would mean.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about bonding and fear of exclusion is consistently acknowledged and reframed toward workable change, rolling with resistance well. Sustain talk is sometimes echoed in ways that could reinforce it, and double-sided reflections toward change are underused.
