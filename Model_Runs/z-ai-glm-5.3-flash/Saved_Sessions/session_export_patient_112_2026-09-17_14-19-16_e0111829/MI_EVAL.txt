# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with affirmations and normalization of Rosa's fears, but warmth is brief and formulaic rather than tied to her specific strengths and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections appeared early on, but reflections were sparse relative to questions and advice. Rosa's fears of failure, friend disapproval, and identity loss received little reflective acknowledgment before pivoting to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, unwavering focus on smoking cessation throughout, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete plan with relapse normalization and a scheduled check-in. A clear strength of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa chose her starting count, the text to Marisol, and agreed to the plan, but power-sharing was uneven. The session's architecture was counselor-designed with Rosa consenting rather than co-creating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered menu choices and framed the plan as something Rosa 'could try,' but heavy prescriptive instructions (exact percentages, drafted scripts, log templates) created an expert-driven, somewhat controlling style.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely drew out Rosa's own motivations and resources; no evocative questions, importance/confidence rulers, or exploration of past quit attempts. Motivation emerged from Rosa despite, not because of, counselor technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-style questions partially conveyed that Rosa's input mattered, but the interaction was heavily advice-driven with detailed menus, plans, and scripts. Expertise remained implicitly with the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rosa's spontaneous change talk was never strategically evoked, reflected, or deepened. No open questions designed to elicit or strengthen commitment language were used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk (weight gain, withdrawal, friends, identity) was not reinforced, but also not actively softened or reframed. The counselor pivoted to options rather than using double-sided reflections to address ambivalence.
