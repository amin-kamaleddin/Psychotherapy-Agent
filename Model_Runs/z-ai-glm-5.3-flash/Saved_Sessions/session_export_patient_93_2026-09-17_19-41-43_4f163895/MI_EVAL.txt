# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard with genuine affirmations ('wanting to know the truth of it takes some courage'). The patient explicitly reports feeling un-lectured and lighter, showing unconditional positive regard landed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate double-sided reflections capturing emotional nuances. Some opportunities to deepen emotional content were missed — e.g., fear of the next fall ('I'd been worrying about that fall more than I let on') was not reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on alcohol reduction with purposeful sequencing from the fall–alcohol link through to a concrete behavioral plan. Tangents were only pursued insofar as they connected to the target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully co-constructed session with an egalitarian tone from the opening. Every plan element (walking group, measuring jug, weekends-only) originated from the client, with the clinician following her lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy actively reinforced throughout — 'you're the one living your life,' options rather than prescriptions, and honoring her pacing. The patient's closing comment ('a plan that's mine, not something being forced on me') directly evidences successful support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from the client's own reasons, solutions, and confidence, culminating in a fully self-generated action plan. The clinician supplied no reasons for change beyond normalized background information.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise repeatedly located within the client ('That's you doing that, not me'), with Margaret positioned as the authority on her routines and choices. Her contributions visibly shaped the session's direction and plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful open questions reliably produced DARN and commitment language, from planning ('weekends only') to self-initiated action ('I've talked myself into ringing her this week'). Change talk was reinforced with affirmations at every turn.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced, with effective double-sided reflections redirecting toward change. However, some sustain statements ('I don't need to quit,' 'I'm not giving that up for anyone') were bypassed rather than explicitly validated and reframed.
