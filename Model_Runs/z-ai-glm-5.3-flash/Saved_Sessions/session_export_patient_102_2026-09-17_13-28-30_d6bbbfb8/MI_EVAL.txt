# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a neutral, respectful tone and validates Milan once, but delivery is checklist-like with no affirmations of his strengths or warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are superficial and repetitive ('I hear you — frustrated'), missing deeper meanings like his fear of labels and desire for trust, and he misidentifies the youth minister as his dad.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No clear focus is established; the session drifts from damage control to safety screening to communication coaching without ever addressing the alcohol use itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is counselor-led with scripts, plans, and coaching; Milan's contributions are incorporated only reactively and he notes going in circles.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices, but they are narrow menus of counselor-generated options paired with prescriptive advice, leaving Milan feeling managed rather than in charge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Ideas, scripts, and plans all come from the counselor; he never asks what Milan wants, what matters to him, or his view of the referral — the weakest domain.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor positions himself as advice-giver supplying scripts and menus rather than seeking Milan's expertise or input on the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No effort to evoke Milan's own motivations or values; his one genuine self-reflection (admitting he lied) emerged spontaneously, not from counselor evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Milan's minimization is met with reassurance and deflection to communication scripts rather than used to develop discrepancy; rapid agreement may reinforce minimization.
