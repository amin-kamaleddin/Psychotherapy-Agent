# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, including at moments of shame and vulnerability. Never moralized about the violence or drinking, and used affirmations like 'There's real wisdom in what you just said' and 'You're the expert on your own life here.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including a precise complex reflection of ambivalence in the opening. Repeatedly mirrored Wiremu's own language (korero, man-to-man, fronting up) with attunement to content and cultural context.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on target behaviors (alcohol reduction, violence program, family reunification) throughout. Each response advanced the work purposefully toward commitment and action without drift, while remaining client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully equal partnership, with plans co-constructed entirely from Wiremu's stated preferences and ideas. The clinician reflected and organized the client's contributions rather than replacing them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced through open questions, explicit permission for pacing ('at your speed,' 'that can wait till I'm ready'), and no prescribing or pressure. Even safety planning was framed as open questions answered entirely by the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every reason for change, plan element, and confidence statement came from Wiremu's own words. Skillful evocative questions led to his self-reframing from 'the screw-up' to someone who fronts up — the session's most therapeutic moment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit power-sharing, repeatedly positioning Wiremu as the expert on his own life. Every plan element originated from the client, with no clinician-supplied advice or expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength — future visioning and preconditions questions elicited rich change talk ('I'm not that guy from that night anymore,' 'maybe I've been believing the wrong story'). The clinician then deepened it by reflecting it back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided amplifying sustain talk, reflecting ambivalence once and pivoting to future visioning. Pivots could have been slightly more decisive at points, keeping sustain talk present a touch longer than necessary.
