# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with validating responses throughout, though affirmations like 'Nice work' became repetitive and formulaic. Ambivalence was accepted ('We can hold both aims') but unconditional regard and normalization were only partially explicit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured core tensions (gym as care, not punishment), and the client confirmed them. However, reflections stayed brief and surface-level, not exploring deeper emotional content like feeling like a failure or years of punitive thoughts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus with no topic drift; every exchange moved the plan forward concretely from goals to specific moves, times, barriers, backups, and implementation details. Questions were purposeful and built sequentially toward a fully specified plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely between equals — the counselor asked permission, incorporated client ideas, and co-constructed the plan. But the counselor often supplied solution menus (habit options, reframe scripts, workout structure) rather than eliciting the client's own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and framed options rather than prescriptions, with the client exercising real choice throughout. At times the tone presumed compliance (e.g., 'What might get in the way Tuesday'), and repetitive praise could subtly pressure adherence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited via confidence rulers and the client's own reasons (enjoying the gym, feeling strong), but many solutions were counselor-supplied rather than client-generated. Deeper wellsprings of motivation — values, self-worth, the meaning of 'giving up on herself' — went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client was framed as the expert ('I'll follow your lead'), and her contributions genuinely shaped the session, including the core habit and her phrase 'I'm building, not shrinking.' Some planning-phase exchanges became slightly expert-driven with pre-packaged suggestions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked client change talk through goal-framing questions and repeated confidence rulers that moved from 6 to 8, yielding strong client-generated statements ('I'm building, not shrinking'; 'I actually feel kind of hopeful'). Evocation was concentrated in action planning, with desire and need talk less developed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('still want to lose weight,' fear of 'giving up on myself') was met with a directive reframe rather than a double-sided reflection, and was never revisited. The counselor pivoted quickly to planning, leaving the ambivalence unresolved ('Maybe. We'll see.').
