# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and never scolded Dylan for the violation. However, warmth remained largely instrumental, moving quickly into task mode without affirming Dylan's strengths (e.g., the instinct to call his roommate).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were reasonably accurate, capturing Dylan's sense of bad luck and fear of 'handing them a win.' Later reflections became perfunctory, and understanding stayed at the content level without exploring the emotional layer beneath his defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifted almost immediately from the target behavior (underage drinking and responsibility) into hearing-prep logistics like timelines and exact wording. The core MI target was never revisited after the opening question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Functioned as a genuine working partnership on the concrete task — Dylan's objections were incorporated and submission was deferred until his approval. However, collaboration was confined to document drafting rather than shared exploration of the underlying behavior.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor's strongest domain — client choices were repeatedly honored and Dylan was never pressured to admit drinking despite several openings. The autonomy offered was narrow, though, limited to hearing strategy rather than the change itself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was never elicited regarding the drinking itself; no values exploration, discrepancy development, or confidence/importance rulers were used. The counselor supplied answers and scripts rather than drawing anything out from Dylan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used some inclusive, choice-offering language reflecting partial power sharing. However, the counselor quickly assumed the expert role, treating Dylan as a client to be coached through a hearing rather than the expert on his own situation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Efforts to evoke change talk were rare and weak, with no open questions exploring Dylan's own reasons for changing his drinking. The counselor even reinforced non-change goals — a quick, confession-free resolution — rather than language favoring behavioral change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor largely mirrored and operationalized Dylan's sustain talk, entrenching the denial narrative through repeated 'neutral, no confession' framing. There were no double-sided reflections or gentle links between Dylan's stated values and the drinking behavior itself.
