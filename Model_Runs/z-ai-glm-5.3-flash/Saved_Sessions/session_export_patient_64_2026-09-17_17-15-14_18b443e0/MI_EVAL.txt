# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Jordan's hesitancy and affirming his final values-based decision without labeling or disapproval. However, warmth became somewhat transactional and checklist-oriented in the latter half, with less explicit affirmation of Jordan as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening that captured Jordan's ambivalence and priorities, such as weighing his own low risk against harm to his grandparents. Reflections were sometimes brief and quickly paired with questions or information, limiting deeper exploration of emotional content like his guilt about infecting his grandfather.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on flu vaccination from start to finish, with each turn advancing toward resolution and a concrete action plan. There was no topic drift, and the session moved progressively toward commitment and specific logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan's answers directly shaped the agenda, including his question about asymptomatic transmission, his convenience concern, and his choice of Walgreens and Thursday. The counselor occasionally slipped into an expert/teacher role, delivering fact blocks and pre-drafted scripts rather than fully co-creating content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Jordan's choice, framed help as optional, and accepted his 'if I chicken out, that's on me' statement without pressure. Rapid information-giving and step-by-step instructions in the mid-to-late session leaned slightly toward directing rather than consistently reinforcing Jordan's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongest MI work involved eliciting Jordan's own motivation—his family-protection rationale and closing insight show the change was genuinely self-generated rather than imposed. The counselor supplied substantial information and practical solutions rather than consistently eliciting Jordan's own ideas about barriers and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated Jordan's contributions as central, with his convenience concern driving the action plan and questions like 'Which option would feel easiest for you' honoring his expertise. At times the counselor assumed an advisory role, offering drafted texts and checklists rather than consistently drawing plans from Jordan himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk by asking what would sway Jordan, drawing out key statements about protecting his grandpa and committing to Thursday. Evocation was somewhat front-loaded; once logistics began, fewer opportunities were taken to deepen commitment language such as confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged Jordan's sustain talk about low personal risk and side-effect worries without reinforcing it, using neutral, normalized reflections and pivoting toward his protective motivations. Some early reflections restated both sides of ambivalence without strategically tilting toward change.
