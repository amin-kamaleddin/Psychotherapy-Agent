# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated unconditional positive regard and warmth throughout, meeting the patient's shame and self-criticism with reframing and affirmation rather than judgment. No labeling, disapproval, or moralizing appeared at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate mirroring of ambivalence and emotional core, including a well-attuned reflection on shame. However, the patient's expressed grief and identity loss ('I don't even know who I am without it') was never explicitly revisited, a missed empathic opportunity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained unwavering focus on smoking cessation while progressing logically through ambivalence, past attempts, barriers, future vision, concrete planning, and slip management. Each turn built steadily toward preparation and action without forcing or wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully collaborative and egalitarian, with genuine questions whose answers visibly redirected the session. Decisions and the final plan were entirely client-generated, with summaries co-constructed from her own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively reinforced throughout ('no pressure,' 'a plan that fits your life, not someone else's idea'), and every choice point was handed back to the patient. The clinician never directed, prescribed, or pressured, and the patient repeatedly took ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within the client via evocative questions, scaling questions, and affirmations amplifying her self-generated insights. The patient's motivation visibly strengthened across the session as a direct result.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly located expertise in the client ('you're the expert here'), which the patient confirmed as novel and validating. Her contributions substantially shaped the session, with the entire plan drawn from her words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of change talk using open questions, commitment-adjacent planning, and a confidence ruler, with the patient producing rich spontaneous change talk ('this feels different,' 'that's the plan'). The clinician consistently reflected and amplified this language rather than replacing it with advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification and skillfully redirected via reframing slips as 'information rather than failure' and double-sided reflections. Some sustain talk was met with reassurance rather than strategic redirection, and the 'old friend' framing was briefly validated without a pivot.
