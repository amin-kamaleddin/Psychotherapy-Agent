# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, affirming even small acts and reframing past struggles as learning. No judgment or disapproval anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that builds on the client's exact words and captures emotional meaning, e.g., the jar-of-salsa moment and 'once you're out the door, you usually keep going.' Reflections consistently reveal client strengths.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains unwavering focus on increasing physical activity, progressing from vision to specificity to concrete action and follow-up commitment. No topic drift; each response steers toward the next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative between equals, with invitational language ('what do you think,' 'we could plan around together') and questions shaped from the client's contributions. No advice is imposed; the client's ideas drive the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces choice and ownership, offering genuine options and reflecting 'something I'm choosing, not dreading.' The closing 'You built this' powerfully reinforces self-authorship.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation content — strength, energy, confidence — is elicited from the client through open questions rather than supplied by the counselor. The counselor never lectures, only draws out and reflects the client's own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise within the client ('it came entirely from you,' 'You built this'), and the plan is drawn exclusively from the client's own ideas. Genuinely open questions throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes strong desire and commitment language ('I want to feel capable in my own body again') and deliberately amplifies it ('You just said something important — I want to do it'). Closes with a question cementing commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk briefly and pivots to strengths, with an excellent reframe of past failure into evidence. Point withheld only because explicit double-sided reflections were not used when doubt was voiced.
