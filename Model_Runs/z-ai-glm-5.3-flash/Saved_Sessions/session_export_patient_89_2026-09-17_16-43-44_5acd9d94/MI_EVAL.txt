# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with no criticism of past failures. However, affirmations became formulaic and repetitive ('I'm proud of you' appeared multiple times in nearly identical form), diluting their impact.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured Marcus's frustration and hope, including a skillful double-sided reflection. Inconsistent pacing with rapid-fire questions (3-4 per turn) left Marcus's fear of failure ('I just don't want to fail again') never reflected or deepened.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on physical activity with no topic drift, culminating in a fully concrete plan with cue, route, duration, backup plan, and accountability mechanism. Every exchange moved the plan forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was built entirely from Marcus's own ideas (walking, dancing, family involvement). However, rapid multi-question turns made the exchange feel at times like a structured checklist rather than an evenly paced dialogue between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions framed as Marcus's own throughout; he explicitly noted the clinician didn't 'hand him a gym brochure like everybody else.' Confidence ratings were elicited rather than asserted, and the offer to 'tweak the plan' reinforced his control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Marcus's own reasons and commitment, demonstrated by his shift from 'why bother' to being 'kind of pumped' with confidence of 8-9. Some evocative questions were bundled with psychoeducation instead of trusting Marcus to generate his own reasons, and the emotional 'why' beneath his desire to change was never explicitly drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently treated Marcus as the expert, with choices offered as options and the plan drawn entirely from his preferences. The relentless stream of questions kept the clinician steering, with no moment where Marcus's contribution visibly redirected the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilful readiness ruler use elicited Marcus's own reasoning, and confidence rose from 4 to 8-9 over the session. Missed opportunities to reflect back and savor his most powerful change statements, with excitement met by new planning questions rather than deepening momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and a good early double-sided reflection pivoted toward hope, but sustain talk was not strategically softened or reframed. The fear of failure was never revisited, and counters sometimes relied on psychoeducation or question changes rather than skilled reframing.
