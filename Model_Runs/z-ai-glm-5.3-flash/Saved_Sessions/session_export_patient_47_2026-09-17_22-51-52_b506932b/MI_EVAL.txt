# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates unconditional positive regard with no judgment, even when the patient minimizes her drinking. Authentic affirmations ('That's real wisdom,' 'it's your own idea, not mine') and respect for both sides of her ambivalence are present throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately capture emotional nuance, including the son's 'look,' the 'nagging voice,' and the pub as 'a treat again instead of a habit.' The closing reflection demonstrates deep tracking of what success means to her.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with a progressive arc from exploring the son's concern to a concrete commitment. There is no topic drift, and each exchange advances toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operates between equal partners, with every question genuinely handing direction to the patient. Her answers visibly shape the next move, and the counselor builds on her cupboard idea, fridge tally, and weekend structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy-affirming language permeates the session ('that's your choice,' 'on your terms,' 'if you wanted to'), with no persuasive pressure or control. Even potential setbacks are framed with self-compassion and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently elicited from the patient herself, drawing out identity-level commitment ('I'd rather be that woman'). The counselor develops her own reasons—her son, her footing, her agency—rather than imposing clinical rationale.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element is the patient's own, with the counselor explicitly positioning her as the expert ('You know your drinking better than anyone'). No plan is prescribed; direction consistently comes from her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoke strong change talk ('I'd rather not have to wonder next time,' 'That would please me'), which is then reinforced through strategic reflections. Her commitment deepens progressively across the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without amplification, using double-sided reflections and pivots to the patient's expertise. On one or two occasions (e.g., 'I'm not signing my life away'), the pivot toward change talk could be slightly more active.
