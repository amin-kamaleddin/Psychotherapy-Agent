# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental and respectful throughout, with affirmations of autonomy and a genuine apology when the client noted the repetition. Warmth felt somewhat formulaic, and repetitive closings eroded the sense of regard until the final apology.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were mostly mechanical summaries restating surface content rather than exploring what the piercing meant to her or the feeling of being judged. Bundled multi-point reflections felt like summaries rather than empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The dental-health target was quickly abandoned in favor of social-script coaching the client explicitly dismissed as 'beating a dead horse.' The counselor followed the drift rather than redirecting toward the actual referral concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative in tone with optional framing, but the counselor persisted in offering scripts and checklists after the client repeatedly declined them. Client contributions did not substantially shape the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session's strongest element—no pressure to remove the piercing and repeated 'it's your call' framing, well-suited to a precontemplative client. However, autonomy was expressed as passive tolerance rather than active support of her agency in gathering information.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was supplied rather than drawn out—the counselor provided question lists and scripted replies instead of eliciting the client's own ideas. Her one generated idea (asking the dentist) was taken over and scripted rather than explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-style questions conveyed some partnership, but expertise clearly resided with the counselor, who supplied questions and interpretations rather than drawing on the client's knowledge. Offers of wording help continued despite her explicit rejection.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No meaningful evocation of change talk; the seed of engagement ('ask the dentist what to watch for') was met with supplied content rather than exploration of why it mattered. No questions explored values, goals, or discrepancy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing, which is a plus, but repeatedly echoed the client's strong sustain talk back as affirmations, amplifying status quo language. No double-sided reflections or strategic pivots toward the health concern.
