# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, normalizing Mateo's discouragement and treating his stoic upbringing respectfully rather than as a defect. The closing 'you did that yourself, not me' affirms his dignity and ownership.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and progressively deepens from surface content (aching nights, stiff mornings) to underlying meaning and values. The reflection on being 'a partner out there in the garden... not watching from the sidelines' shows exceptional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on rehab engagement while directly addressing the pain barrier tied to it. Each exchange builds momentum from pain to sleep to doctor visit to plan, guided purposefully without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operates between equals — Mateo supplies all content and solutions while the counselor provides MI structure. Questions invite joint problem-solving and there is no expert-driven agenda imposition.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Mateo's choice is honored at every juncture, with questions framed as 'what do you think' and 'what fits where you are right now.' The counselor offers no directives and explicitly reinforces ownership ('you did that yourself, not me').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited entirely from within the client through questions like 'What would getting a decent night's sleep let you do that matters most?' The shift from 'slow going nowhere' to 'the pain doesn't get the final say' is entirely client-voiced.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All solutions — telling the doctor, writing it down, involving his wife, porch watering — originate from Mateo and are elicited rather than suggested. The final line explicitly transfers credit and ownership to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked with open questions drawing out desire, ability, reasons, and commitment. The counselor strategically reflects and amplifies each increment as Mateo's language evolves from 'I could bring it up, maybe' to concrete planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged briefly and empathically, then consistently pivoted toward change using double-sided reflections like 'You're not used to asking for yourself, and yet here you are considering it.' Barriers are never dwelled upon or reinforced.
