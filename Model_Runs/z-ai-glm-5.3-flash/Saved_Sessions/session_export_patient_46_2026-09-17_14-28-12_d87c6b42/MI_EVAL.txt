# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains basic respect, a nonjudgmental tone, and a brief safety screening, but warmth is thin and largely transactional, dominated by checklists and advice. Misses opportunities to affirm Ethan's honesty, his willingness to practice, and his self-generated compromise ideas.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal — a few early reflections ('I hear skepticism') but most emotional content, such as fear of being grounded and peer pressure, is skipped or ignored. Complex disclosures are repeatedly met with 'try this script' responses rather than understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The referral target of risky drinking is eclipsed by parent-management strategy, with roughly 70% of the session devoted to scripts, check-ins, and curfews. The counselor accepts 'not getting caught' as the goal and never returns to the alcohol-related risks Ethan identified.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission before some suggestions and offers role-play, but the power balance is uneven — the counselor supplies all the content while Ethan's role is to accept or perform it. During role-play, the counselor even delivered Ethan's scripted line himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor's strongest area — explicitly rejects an all-or-nothing demand, offers option menus, and lets Ethan choose between approaches. Capped because support is expressed through options rather than affirming Ethan's ownership of and responsibility for his own change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Operates from the assumption that answers come from the clinician, delivering information and scripts nearly every turn. Ethan's best ideas (calling mom 'no questions,' skipping shots, acknowledging parents' worry) originate from him but are met with more pre-packaged advice instead of further evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Assumes the expert role for most of the session, structuring responses as prescriptive packages and handing Ethan pre-written scripts rather than co-creating them. When Ethan asks whether the plan would work with his strict parents, the counselor responds with two more scripts instead of exploring his knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ethan spontaneously offers meaningful change statements — alternating drinks, sticking to beer after getting sick on shots, and his full spoken commitment script — but the counselor never reflects, affirms, or deepens any of them. There are no open questions, confidence rulers, or summaries designed to evoke change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Neither amplifies nor redirects sustain talk — Ethan's minimizations are consistently ignored, which avoids harm but misses chances to develop discrepancy with his stated values. The counselor even explicitly supports continuation of drinking ('it can keep you social') without linking back to the negative consequences Ethan himself identified.
