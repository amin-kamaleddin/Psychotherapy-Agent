# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance with regular affirmations ('showing real strength keeping it together,' 'nice job staying composed') and no criticism of the client's struggles. However, warmth is somewhat task-focused—limited normalization of her fear or the emotional weight of single parenting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captures Daniela's stress and social anxiety ('overwhelmed, stretched thin, and anxious that one cancellation could topple everything'). Reflections are occasionally brief and pivot to questions or scripts, missing deeper emotional content such as her reduced hopelessness or frustration with her mother.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on reliable childcare with every turn advancing the plan—prioritizing, role-playing, rehearsing rejection, planning the Head Start call, and building a backup plan. The session closes with specific, timed commitments; the smoking/drinking mention was appropriately deferred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely an exchange between equals—Daniela generated the meal swap, playdate framing, and backup plan, with the counselor building on her ideas. Occasionally slips into directive consulting (e.g., providing the full Head Start checklist and word-for-word scripts before exploring what she already knows).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces client ownership through repeated choice-offering ('Which feels doable this week?', 'say it aloud or type it here?'), with advice framed as options rather than directives. Reframing the neighbor's partial refusal as informational further preserved agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently draws motivation from within via open questions and confidence rulers, letting Daniela articulate her own reasons ('free childcare... would change everything'). Frequent provision of scripts and checklists sometimes substitutes external content for fully evoking the client's own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Daniela as the expert ('which would you prefer,' 'which one feels doable?'), and her contributions shaped the core of the session. Slightly undercut by somewhat advice-heavy delivery of ready-made scripts and checklists.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully and consistently evokes change talk—confidence rulers moved Daniela from 5 to 7 to 8, and open questions elicited client-generated plans and desire language ('I'll do that tonight before I lose my nerve'). Change talk deepened naturally from vague wishes to specific commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not dwell on or amplify barrier talk; instead redirected ('making the call gives you information and options') and reframed rejection as partial success. A 5 would involve more deliberate double-sided reflections to actively resolve ambivalence rather than moving past it.
