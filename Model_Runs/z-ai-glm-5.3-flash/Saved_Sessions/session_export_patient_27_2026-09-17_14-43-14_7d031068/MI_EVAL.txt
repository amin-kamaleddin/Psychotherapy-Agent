# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated warmth and unconditional positive regard, opening with 'I'm here to listen with warmth and without judgment' and repeatedly validating Daniela's bravery while normalizing her anxiety. No judgment or labeling appeared at any point, and even her hesitancy was met with acceptance ('stumbling or changing your mind is totally fine').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on accurately captured her ambivalence and core stigma fear ('the diabetes girl'). However, reflections became sparser in the middle and later portions, where emotional experiences like guilt and nighttime worries were affirmed but not deeply explored before shifting to options and scripts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, consistent focus on disclosing diabetes to peers with no topic drift. Each exchange progressed logically from exploring fears to identifying a first person, crafting a disclosure line, role-playing, and planning responses and privacy boundaries.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was genuinely collaborative with co-created agendas, negotiated role-play, and Daniela's ideas visibly shaping the intervention. A 5 was withheld because the counselor sometimes presented pre-formatted option lists and scripts that leaned toward expert provision rather than fully joint generation of solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strongly reinforced Daniela's ownership with 'I'll follow your pace,' repeated option menus, and never any pressure or insistence on disclosure. The client's own comment 'thanks for asking me instead of just doing whatever' is direct evidence that autonomy support was felt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew motivation from within, eliciting strong client-generated change talk ('I'm tired of keeping it secret') and her self-crafted script. Evocation was less prominent in the second half, where the counselor shifted to a more didactic, skills-coaching mode of providing response scripts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Daniela as the expert, following her pace, asking permission before each activity, and letting her phrase 'same me, extra tech' shape the practice scripts. It fell short of 5 because supplying menus of options and pre-written lines slightly limited how much her own contributions drove the session's content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and reinforced through questions like 'What would you want or need from them if they knew?' and built upon with affirmations and graduated rehearsal. The score is not 5 because some momentum came from counselor-supplied scripts rather than consistently eliciting Daniela's own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged with neutral, non-reinforcing reflections and gently pivoted toward small, safe steps, with catastrophizing met supportively with practical tools. However, explicit double-sided reflections were rarely used, and some sustain talk (stomach hurting, 'I don't know if I'm ready') was passed over relatively quickly in favor of moving to action.
