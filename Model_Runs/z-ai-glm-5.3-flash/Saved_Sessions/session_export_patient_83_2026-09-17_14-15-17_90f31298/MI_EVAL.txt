# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect and nonjudgmental tone were maintained, but warmth was sparse and formulaic, with no affirmations of Mai's honesty or resourcefulness. Validation of her frustration was brief and mechanical ('Totally reasonable to be upset/annoyed' repeated verbatim).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was nearly absent; the counselor rarely reflected Mai's feelings or statements back. Emotional expressions (frustration about her doctor, anxiety about muscle pain) were met with scripts or factual information rather than empathic reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus stayed firmly on statin adherence and its practical barriers (purpose, side effects, grapefruit interaction, doctor communication) with no topic drift. Direction was maintained through advice and checklists rather than collaborative goal clarification, preventing a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration existed through practice invitations and accepting Mai's rewording of questions, but power-sharing was uneven. The counselor assumed the expert role with monologue-style information blocks and did not explicitly integrate Mai's ideas into a shared plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choice was offered (options, permission before drafting scripts), but the style was prescriptive with pre-written questions and reminders rather than eliciting Mai's own solutions. The counselor pushed additional scripting even after Mai indicated she had it down, and never affirmed the decision was hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was little intentional eliciting of Mai's own motivations, values, or confidence — no open questions like 'What matters most to you about your health here?' Change promotion relied on persuasion through information rather than evoking internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor adopted an expert stance dominated by bulleted medical information, pre-drafted scripts, and checklists. Mai's contributions did not substantially shape content, and her demonstrated good judgment went largely unacknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged largely from the patient, strengthened by rehearsal practice ('That feels doable'). However, the counselor rarely reflected, reinforced, or deepened it — responding to Mai's 'I can do this' with more reminders instead of affirming her confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not amplify sustain talk and moved past initial resistance, but through advice-giving education rather than double-sided reflections. Opportunities to redirect Mai's frustration with her doctor into motivational framing were missed.
