# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician affirms the patient's autonomy throughout, emphasizing that he chooses his own quit date and approach. There is no judgment or criticism, even when discussing relapse and ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture both sides of the patient's ambivalence (e.g., work stress vs. readiness to quit, hope vs. doubt about his motivation). The clinician sensitively acknowledges the emotional weight of the wife's father's death.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation while honoring the patient's pacing. Each exchange moves purposefully toward a concrete, patient-generated plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly invites the patient's input ('which feels most manageable from where you sit?') and positions expertise as shared ('options we can look at together'). The plan is entirely designed by the patient with the clinician facilitating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are consistently offered, and the clinician explicitly relieves pressure ('that dilemma isn't one you have to solve today'). The patient's own schedule, values, and preferences drive every decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully draws out the patient's own motivations, including the deeply personal reason involving his wife. Questions like 'what would it take to make that reason something you can reach for?' elicit powerful change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The tone is egalitarian throughout, with the clinician deferring to the patient's knowledge of his own patterns and calendar. Decisions about medication, timing, and strategies are jointly explored rather than prescribed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently invites and reinforces change talk ('I don't want to be that guy anymore,' 'I've stalled long enough') and reflects it back with affirmation. Doubt is met with gentle exploration rather than confrontation, deepening commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of stress, doubt that his reason is 'enough') is met with reflective listening and double-sided reflections rather than argument. The clinician neutralizes the 'hard stop' dilemma by reframing it as solvable on the patient's timeline.
