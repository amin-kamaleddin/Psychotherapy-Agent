# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates consistent unconditional positive regard and nonjudgment ('Both are real, and I'm not judging either'), even when the client expresses doubt. Client explicitly responds with appreciation for not being lectured.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate reflections of ambivalence and hope. Falls short of 5 because reflections sometimes restate rather than deepen—e.g., the emotional pain around the client's absent father was volunteered but not emotionally explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains consistent focus on the target behavior from opening discrepancy framing to a concrete closing plan. Every response ties back to the change goal or the client's stated values, with no topical drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the work as joint ('we figure out the next step together') and the plan is entirely client-generated. However, the counselor dominates the closing exchanges with affirmations, reducing back-and-forth texture; an invitation for the client to shape summaries would help.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support—the counselor deliberately contrasts intrinsic choice with external mandate, and the client articulates 'It's because I'd be choosing it.' No pressuring language; outcomes normalized as imperfect.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Core strategy is eliciting motivation from within; every motivation (father, cousin's business, legit money) comes from the client and is reflected back as his own. The final self-generated anchor is a textbook outcome of skilled evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly positions the client as the expert, and session direction is clearly shaped by his contributions (Thursday nights, landscaping, his father). Loses a point because reflections occasionally restate rather than build and agenda negotiation is implicit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk as Mateo progresses from desire to commitment ('It's a plan,' 'that's what I'm gonna be'). Selectively highlights client-generated motivational moments with reflective affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively pivots away from sustain talk using double-sided reflections and reframing ('not for you, not for them, for me'). Could improve by more explicitly acknowledging ambivalence before redirecting and normalizing it more actively.
