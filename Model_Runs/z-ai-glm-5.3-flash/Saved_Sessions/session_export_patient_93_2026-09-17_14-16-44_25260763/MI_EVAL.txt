# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout with affirmations conveying positive regard. However, affirmations were formulaic and repeated verbatim, lacking individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured Margaret's perspective, but reflections faded as the session became logistical. Rich emotional disclosures (fear of falling, wanting independence from her son) were skipped rather than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on alcohol reduction and fall risk, culminating in a concrete plan and scheduled follow-up. Direction came partly through information-delivery rather than guiding, without exploring the emotional roots of ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The closing plan was co-shaped (log format, follow-up frequency, appointment time). However, the mid-session risk education block and pre-designed log template were expert-driven rather than built together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated choice-offering and respect for Margaret's refusal to quit or be checked daily affirmed her autonomy. Falls short of 5 due to a mildly pressuring undertone in risk education and no explicit affirmation of her ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied through didactic education rather than drawn from the client. Her self-generated motivations (hip fracture fear, proving independence, curiosity about wine's mood effects) emerged unprompted and went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret's preferences were incorporated, but the session was largely counselor-driven. Little acknowledgment that Margaret herself was the expert on what would fit her life—she supplied ideas without the counselor drawing them out.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and menu options successfully elicited commitments (water between drinks, shaving a glass, honest logging). These were not deepened by reflecting her own reasons or explicitly eliciting confidence and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Double-sided acknowledgment of her minimization was skillful early on. But sustain talk ('not giving up the pub') was accepted without strategic pivoting, and the heavy educational focus may have amplified the problem focus.
