# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor communicates consistent warmth and respect, and normalizes lapses without judgment. However, repeated use of phrases like 'I love this' feels slightly formulaic, keeping it from a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflects Laura's content and validates her fears, but reflections remain brief restatements tied to the plan. There is limited exploration of the emotional meaning of her self-criticism and the gap between her stated values and actions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains an exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior throughout, with every turn moving the plan forward concretely (time, sequence, reminders, backup rules). All responses are tied back to Laura's values of health and self-love.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely between equals, and Laura's contributions genuinely shape the final plan (five-minute rule, Wednesday backup, arriving-home anchor). However, the counselor does most of the drafting, with Laura often selecting among counselor-provided options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently frames steps as Laura's choice and immediately embraces her tweak to anchor the cue to arriving home. Offering pre-packaged option menus rather than open-endedly eliciting her own solutions slightly limits full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor taps Laura's values and motivations but relies heavily on supplying strategies, scripts, and checklists. Questions elicit logistics rather than motivation, and Laura volunteers most of her own reasons and vision unprompted.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offers choice and invites Laura to adjust the plan, which she meaningfully does. The score is held back because the counselor still supplies most of the ideas, occasionally edging toward an expert stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura offers substantial change talk, but it arises largely from her own motivation rather than skillful evocation. The counselor affirms it briefly but misses opportunities to deepen commitment language through rulers or elaboration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoids amplifying Laura's doubts and responds supportively. However, responses tend toward reassurance and problem-solving rather than double-sided reflections that strategically pivot her ambivalence toward resolution.
