# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validated Sofia's perspective, and accepted her boundary-setting (e.g., 'I'm not promising to skip smoking') without challenge. However, warmth was somewhat brief and task-focused, with limited explicit affirmations of Sofia as a person (e.g., her first-gen resilience).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were generally accurate and concise, capturing her first-gen pressure, ambivalence ('quick relief but often just delays the stress'), and her win ('noticeable and confusing all at once'). Some reflections stayed surface-level, missing the deeper emotional weight of being 'the example' for her family, and he sometimes pivoted quickly to menu options rather than deepening emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a general focus on her stress and moved productively toward concrete next steps (top-3 list, wind-down experiment). However, when Sofia declined discussing weed, the target behavior was dropped almost entirely, and the session drifted into homework management for a stretch without linking coping work back to marijuana use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offered options rather than directives, let Sofia shape the agenda, and invited joint work ('Want help drafting the text or the 10-min outline?'). Collaboration was uneven, though—he supplied the strategies, template, and filled-in lab paragraph largely himself, so key session products originated from the counselor rather than being co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength: he repeatedly offered genuine menus of choice, framed the plan as 'a tiny experiment, not a fix,' and fully accepted Sofia's conditions ('walk first, then decide') without pressure, persuasion, or moralizing about her marijuana use. This consistently reinforced her ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Though Sofia's own insight ('I can't fully tell myself I need it to get things done') was the session's most powerful element, the psychiatrist relied mostly on advice-giving and structured options rather than deliberately eliciting her motivations—never asking key evocative questions (e.g., 'What would cutting back do for you?' or scaling/confidence questions). Much of the productive change talk was a byproduct of the homework exercise rather than intentional evocation of her reasons for change regarding marijuana.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** He consistently conveyed that expertise resides within Sofia through check-ins ('How would you like me to support you?', 'Your call') and treated her observations as meaningful data. Several interactions drifted toward teaching/consulting mode, however—a detailed lab outline, four-item strategy menu, and step-by-step instructions let his expertise dominate rather than drawing solutions out of her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia volunteered notable change statements ('I did all this without smoking first,' 'I proved I can just... do stuff without it'), which the psychiatrist deepened with follow-up questions like 'How did that feel?'. Much of this emerged indirectly through the homework exercise rather than strategic evocative questioning about her values, and the change talk was never consolidated with a summary.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** He avoided arguing with or reinforcing Sofia's sustain talk ('It's not a big deal,' 'weed isn't running my life') and acknowledged it lightly ('It makes total sense you'd want a chill ritual after school'). He did not strategically soften it, however—no double-sided reflections, and when she deflected the weed discussion entirely ('can we skip the whole weed helps or hurts thing'), he complied rather than gently keeping the door open.
