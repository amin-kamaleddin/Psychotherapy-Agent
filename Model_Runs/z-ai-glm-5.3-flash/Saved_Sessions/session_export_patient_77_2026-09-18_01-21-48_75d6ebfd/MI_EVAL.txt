# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms the patient's autonomy and perspective, treating ambivalence as understandable rather than problematic. The patient explicitly states the session did not feel like 'another lecture' and that the plan felt like her own.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician reflects both sides of the patient's ambivalence ('part of you wants change, part feels discouraged') and accurately mirrors her emotional experience, including guilt, fear of quitting, and emerging self-trust. Reflections are precise and emotionally attuned throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a clear focus on self-compassionate, intrinsically motivated movement rather than results-driven exercise, and skillfully channels the patient's own ideas into a concrete plan. The conversation stays purposeful without imposing the clinician's agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly every turn ends with an open question inviting the patient's expertise ('What do you think might work for you?'), and the plan is entirely generated by the patient. The clinician positions herself as a guide, not an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly reinforces patient ownership ('it wasn't mine or anyone else's plan,' 'this is fully on your terms') and supports the patient's decision to keep the walk private from her mother. Change talk about self-directed choice is elicited and strengthened.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician draws out the patient's own reasons and self-discoveries — walking for feeling rather than results, trusting her own judgment over the scale. The patient articulates novel insights ('it's the first thing that's actually mine') in response to evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue is a genuine back-and-forth, with the clinician building on each patient statement through summaries and open questions. Decision-making is shared, and the patient's ideas are treated as the foundation of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong change talk is elicited across all phases: desire ('that actually sounds nice'), ability ('I think I could actually try that'), reasons ('it still did its job'), and commitment ('I'll try it tomorrow... actually try, not just say it'). The clinician consistently reflects and deepens these statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (guilt, fear of quitting, mother's expectations, past failures) is met with reflective, non-confrontational responses that validate both sides ('Both of those can be true') rather than arguing for change. This allows the patient to work through doubt and arrive at her own resolve.
