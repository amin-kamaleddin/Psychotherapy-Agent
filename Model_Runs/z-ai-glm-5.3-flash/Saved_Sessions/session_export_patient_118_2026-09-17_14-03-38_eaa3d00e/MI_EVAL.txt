# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, honoring Seamus's request to focus on teeth and avoiding lectures—the client explicitly appreciated this. Warmth was somewhat formulaic and opportunities for individualized affirmations (e.g., of his honesty about the failed medication attempt) were missed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections early on (frustration with the expected lecture, side-effect deterrents), but responses were predominantly informational. The counselor jumped to lists of options instead of reflecting the emotional meaning of disclosures like the 'nasty dreams' and needing to 'really want it.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on target behaviors (dental treatment, smoking) and followed the client's lead appropriately. Direction was passive—the counselor followed rather than guided, never linked dental concerns to smoking to build momentum, and the closing was repetitive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and followed the client's agenda, but the interaction was largely one-way information delivery (treatment lists, scripts) rather than joint problem-solving. Decisions were not truly co-constructed; the counselor supplied content and Seamus accepted or rejected it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided pressure, framed options as choices, and let Seamus set the pace—options like patches were presented without pushing. Short of a 5 because the counselor never affirmed Seamus's ownership of the decision, and repeated unsolicited script offers subtly imposed the counselor's agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied almost entirely on providing information rather than eliciting the client's own motivations, with essentially no evocative open questions. The valuable change talk that appeared was self-generated and never developed—motivation was left mostly untapped, the core MI failure of this session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Intermittently invited input but repeatedly took an expert stance, delivering dense lists and pre-written scripts even after Seamus twice declined them. The client's own expertise (why the pills failed) was only tapped because he volunteered it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerged spontaneously ('maybe I'll mention the patch thing... just to ask') but the counselor did little to evoke or deepen it. No open questions about his reasons for quitting, no elaboration, no affirmations of change talk, and no exploration of importance or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or lecturing and deftly accepted Seamus's boundary-setting, but made no strategic efforts to pivot sustain talk toward change. No double-sided reflections acknowledging ambivalence, and the past failed quit attempt was never reframed as useful information.
