# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental throughout; Hana directly affirmed feeling accepted ('Thank you for not making me feel stupid'). No judgmental language appeared.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening capturing both anxiety and ambivalence. Reflections were sometimes brief and didn't deeply explore the shame of past cancellations or fear of friends' judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained crystal-clear focus on the target behavior with a fully specified plan (times, materials, check-in). No topic drift; each exchange advanced toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan was co-constructed with Hana's choices (time, place, reward, check-in framing), but the initial strategy menu came from the counselor rather than being jointly generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent choice points (check-in type, reward, reminder wording) with explicit permission framing ('you can stop after 30 minutes, that's still a win'). No pressure or coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from Hana's own values and past success ('tired of being stuck', 'show up for myself'), but supplied several solutions proactively and used no confidence/importance rulers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly solicited Hana's input and positioned her expertise as central (e.g., shaping reminder wording). Occasionally a directive strategy menu tempered full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked and reinforced change talk, producing strong client commitment language ('nervous but doing it anyway'). Could deepen further with elaborative questions or confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk briefly then pivoted to concrete change-oriented solutions. Balance clearly favored change language, though some reflections slightly dwelled on worry.
