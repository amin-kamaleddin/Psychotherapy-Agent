# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warmth and nonjudgment were consistent throughout, from the opening line to validating David's skepticism ('That's a reasonable concern'). Even when the patient dismissed medication, the counselor normalized his frustration without any disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and captured both content and emotion (e.g., balancing heart disease worry with fear of losing hand function). Held at 4 because reflections followed a repeated formula and didn't deeply explore his father's cardiac history or underlying vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor kept clear focus on medication adherence and cholesterol management with steady momentum from ambivalence exploration to a concrete plan. Offered multiple parallel options mid-session, which briefly diffused focus rather than converging on one strategic path.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely collaborative — menu-based options, David's selections shaped the direction, and rehearsal was interactive role-play. Fell short of 5 because the counselor structured most content (question wording, tips) in expert format rather than inviting co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: explicit menu choices, permission-seeking, and acceptance of 'no promises today' without pressure. The final plan (low-dose trial plus 3-month diet) was entirely David's construction, with tools framed as supports for his own decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor drew motivation from David himself via questions like 'What would need to be true for you to feel okay trying a statin?'. Occasionally supplied ready-made content (question lists, scripts) and missed a chance to consolidate his 'doable' commitment with a confidence/commitment scaling question.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David's contributions drove the session ('Which would help most?', 'Want to tailor these?'), and his choice directly shaped the intervention. Some moments were didactic — bulleted question lists and communication tips were delivered as expert guidance rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk, especially with the pivotal question that produced conditional commitment ('maybe I'd give the low dose a shot'). Rehearsal exercises moved him from passive agreement to strong commitment language ('that actually feels doable'), with each change statement reflected and built upon.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged briefly and redirected toward change, converting it into problem-solving. A few early reflections dwelled on the anti-change position without an immediate pivot, and double-sided reflections weren't always used to tie sustain talk to change talk.
