# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, but warmth was thin and transactional, with few genuine affirmations of the patient's courage or insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections were brief and formulaic, and significant emotional disclosures (shame, chest tightness, fear of judgment) were met with reassurance and checklists rather than accurate or complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Focus was clear and persistent with concrete actionable steps and a role-play rehearsal, but the direction emphasized financial/social logistics over the methamphetamine use itself, repeatedly bypassing the patient's core ambivalence about the scene.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Collaboration was largely one-directional, with the counselor generating ideas, drafting messages, and setting plans while the patient picked from a menu; the patient's insider expertise was never solicited as a resource.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options and choices were repeatedly offered and the patient's selections were genuinely respected, but the framing was strongly directive with pre-selected menus and unaffirmed patient ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation came almost entirely from counselor-provided solutions, with virtually no evocative questions; spontaneous change talk emerged but was never harvested or deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed an expert/problem-solver role, dispensing rapid advice and pre-packaged scripts; incorporating the patient's corrections prevented a lower score, but the overall stance was advisory.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Notable change talk emerged spontaneously ('sounds worse than I thought', 'that stung a bit', the role-play commitment), but the counselor almost never reflected, deepened, or amplified it, and never explored the patient's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was neither reinforced nor argued against, which is a strength, but there was no active softening — no double-sided reflections or strategic pivots; ambivalence was largely ignored rather than redirected.
