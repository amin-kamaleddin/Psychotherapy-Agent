# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with early affirmations and neutral framing of the criminal record. Affirmations are sparse and generic, missing chances to name Mateo's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some accurate brief reflections ('that barrier is real'), but reflective listening stays shallow. Emotional content — shame, self-doubt, skepticism about follow-through — is repeatedly met with logistics rather than reflection of feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on employment with logical task sequencing (resume → certs → applications). Misses the key opening linking idle time to past trouble and never ties job work back to the deeper recidivism/stability goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo genuinely shapes the session (choosing resume first, picking ProLogistix), but the counselor shifts into expert mode with pre-built plans, templates, and menus rather than co-creating solutions from Mateo's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frequent choices and respect for Mateo's pacing ('one thing at a time'). Undermined by the counselor doing most of the work ('I'll draft it'), positioning Mateo as a recipient rather than the agent of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ideas and motivation come almost entirely from the counselor's lists and scripts; Mateo mostly picks from menus. Virtually no open questions about his values, reasons for change, or vision for the future.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Functional but hierarchical — the counselor assumes the expert role, presenting plans and scripts without first asking what Mateo knows or has tried. Mateo's input steers the session but expertise is never located within him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Genuine change talk emerges ('worth trying,' 'this time might be different') and is occasionally affirmed, but never deepened or reflected back. Missed evocative follow-ups like 'What makes this time different?'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids amplifying sustain talk and acknowledges it briefly, but never actively softens it. Skips self-doubt without affirming real capabilities (2 years forklift experience, GED); no double-sided reflections used.
