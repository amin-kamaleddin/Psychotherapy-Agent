# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with genuine affirmations ('You've connected the dots yourself,' 'You did all of that') and no hint of disapproval. Elena's fear of 'sounding difficult' is met with normalization, and the closing 'I believe in you' reinforces unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and often complex, including the deeper observation that Elena's accomplishments 'go unnoticed, even by you.' A 5 is withheld because some reflections lean toward praise/summary rather than exploring the emotional meaning beneath Elena's fear of seeming difficult at work.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on a concrete target behavior (saying no to the colleague's grading requests), with each exchange building progressively toward a plan. A 5 is withheld because the tightness owes much to Elena's already-articulated readiness and narrow, pre-given target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element (the sentence, Monday, the café ritual) originated from Elena, and the psychiatrist frames each turn as curiosity rather than direction. The session reads as joint exploration in which the client leads and the counselor facilitates, with no expert-driven steering.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decision-making is explicitly handed to Elena ('What timing feels right for you, since you know your schedule and your readiness better than anyone?'), with no imposed advice or pressure. The psychiatrist reinforces her ownership throughout and leaves follow-up open rather than mandated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every meaningful element—boundaries, the target situation, prepared words, timing, and the final insight 'I already have what I need'—was elicited through open questions rather than provided. Questions like 'What would help you feel most prepared?' consistently draw out Elena's own reasons, confidence, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located within Elena ('You're the expert on what feels realistic for you'), and the psychiatrist consistently credits her authorship of the plan. This is active power-sharing that materially shapes the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is the session's standout strength—the psychiatrist skillfully evokes and deepens change talk at nearly every turn, e.g., 'What would it mean to you if this first no went even a little bit well?' Elena's commitment language builds steadily from intention to a concrete, energized plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** When sustain talk appears (fear of seeming difficult, the 'sure, no problem' reflex), the psychiatrist handles it well with double-sided framing ('scary and important') and pivots to Elena's own coping insight. A 5 is withheld because sustain talk is so minimal that softening wasn't heavily tested.
