# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental — validates the patient's pain and never disapproves of her suicidality. Held back from a 5 by occasionally formulaic affirmations ('I hear you') and limited explicit normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects the patient's exhaustion, her 'hum' metaphor, fear of her sister's 'sad voice,' and resistance to promises. Missed chances to explore deeper emotional material such as her loneliness and the 'exit sign' meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Immediately and directly assessed plan, means, and intent (0–10 scale), then methodically built a concrete safety plan with warning signs, solo coping, a contact person, and an ER agreement. Every exchange stayed tied to safety with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Highly collaborative — drafted and tailored the text to her voice, offered coping steps as a menu, and pivoted when she noted outside time helped. Slightly clinician-driven menus (5-4-3-2-1 grounding) rather than drawing from her own past successes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Patient retained choice at every step, with the clinician explicitly honoring her 'no promises' stance and refusing to label her actions as 'progress.' Autonomy was affirmed even when she resisted being praised.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited the patient's own reasons for living (her sister, the 'small part,' going outside) and let her generate the plan content. Would be stronger with more open evocative questions about what makes that part want to stay alive.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing — 'You're the expert on your life' — and the patient's own warning signs and choices shaped the plan. Slightly below 5 because the safety plan structure was somewhat clinician-framed rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully amplified the patient's rare change talk ('small part keeps holding on,' 'huge awareness'), which culminated in concrete commitments. A 5 would need more explicit evocative questions about what keeps that part hanging on.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk wasn't reinforced, but it was rarely strategically softened with double-sided reflections pairing hopelessness with her surviving part. Notable sustain talk ('Nothing fixes anything') near the end went unreframed.
