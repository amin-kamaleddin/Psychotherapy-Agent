# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental tone with brief affirmations, but warmth feels formulaic. The abrupt pivot to a safety check after Mateo's discouragement felt disconnected, and positive regard lacked individualization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections early on, but reflective listening was largely skipped in favor of checklists and instructions. Mateo's deeper feelings of discouragement and prior dismissal went unexplored, missing a key empathic opening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong focus on the target behavior with a concrete plan, specific times, and a Friday check-in. However, direction occasionally tipped into over-directedness, with repetitive verbatim recaps adding little.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration improved over time as Mateo's modifications were accepted, but the opening was expert-driven and collaboration operated only within a plan the counselor had already designed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive moves (time flexibility, quieter alternative, simplified log), but the overall prescriptive structure left little room for Mateo to author his own approach, yielding passive compliance rather than genuine choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation came predominantly from the counselor; Mateo's own reasons for change were never elicited or built upon. The session relied on persuasion and structure rather than the client's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist rapidly assumed the expert role with a mini-lecture and pre-formed plan. Client input was solicited only narrowly, so the plan's architecture was counselor-imposed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mateo offered change talk, but it was rarely elicited or deepened. Commitments emerged from the counselor's plan rather than being evoked from the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced, which is positive, but it was never actively softened or reframed with double-sided reflections or affirmations. Ambivalence was bypassed rather than resolved.
