# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with some normalization and affirmations, but the warmth is formulaic and repetitive, diluting sincerity. Little individualized positive regard tied to Claudia's specific experiences.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; emotionally meaningful disclosures about depression, shame, and her wine ritual were bypassed in favor of prescriptive advice lists. The interaction reads as information delivery rather than understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on reducing alcohol use with a concrete goal, check-in, and no drift. Lost a point because the focus was counselor-imposed and checklist-driven rather than progressively negotiated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is predominantly expert-to-patient, with plans and techniques prescribed rather than jointly developed. Claudia's ideas shaped the plan somewhat, but despite—not because of—the counselor's collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choice-offering questions were present and Claudia selected her own strategies, but the overall style was directive with rapid-fire advice lists. Repeated, unindicated crisis disclaimers further undermined autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was supplied via psychoeducation rather than elicited; the confidence ruler was the only genuine evocative move and was never followed up. Claudia's reasons for change were volunteered by her, not drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Expertise clearly resided with the counselor, who delivered pre-packaged strategies before asking Claudia's own views. Even when Claudia shared her expertise (e.g., the bath as her wine moment), the response was another strategy list.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence question was useful, and Claudia produced substantial change talk, but mostly on her own initiative. Opportunities to deepen and amplify her desire and commitment language were repeatedly missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not argue with Claudia's sustain talk, which is a strength, but also did not actively soften it—no double-sided reflections or gentle pivots toward her stated goals. Ambivalence was bypassed rather than resolved.
