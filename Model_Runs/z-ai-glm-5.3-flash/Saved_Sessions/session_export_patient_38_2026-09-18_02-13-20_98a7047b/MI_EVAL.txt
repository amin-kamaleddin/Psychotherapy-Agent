# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms the patient's autonomy ('no pressure, no decisions today') and validates her ambivalence without judgment. Acceptance is strong throughout, though a couple of affirmation turns were left as missing response placeholders.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections capture both content and feeling ('That scare sounds like it shook you,' 'no look from me, I promise'). Empathy is warm and accurate, notably addressing the patient's fear of being judged by doctors.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear focus on exploring contraception readiness at the patient's pace, without drifting. The direction is gentle but consistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician asks permission implicitly ('Would it help to write these down... or would you rather sit with them?') and lets the patient set the agenda and pace. A highly collaborative stance with the patient directing next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasizes the patient's ownership: 'Those answers belong to you, on your timeline' and 'your list, your friend, no promises.' Autonomy is actively reinforced rather than passively tolerated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions like 'What would make next week feel just as safe as today did?' and 'When you imagine next week going well, what are you doing?' effectively evoke the patient's own vision of change. Slightly limited by the patient's early reluctance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positions the patient as the expert ('you're the one setting the agenda') and offers options rather than directives. Partnership is evident in every exchange.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully draws out the patient's own motivations and questions, culminating in her generating a concrete list of questions and agreeing to return. The shift from avoidance to 'I guess I do have questions' is well facilitated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician does not argue against sustain talk ('I'm not ready to pick anything'); instead she reflects and reframes it as self-protection, which the patient rewards by engaging more. Well handled.
