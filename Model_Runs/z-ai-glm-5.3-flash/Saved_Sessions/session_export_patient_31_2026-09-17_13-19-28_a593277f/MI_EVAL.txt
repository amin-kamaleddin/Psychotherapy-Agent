# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations of honesty and normalization of slips as part of learning. Fell short of a 5 due to a formulaic crisis-screener opening and affirmation that felt procedural rather than individualized to David's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some accurate reflections, but quickly pivoted to information-giving at emotionally significant moments, such as David's fifteen-year coffee-and-cigarette ritual and his fear of failure. Emotional weight was acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on smoking reduction with a concrete weekly target ('half a pack') and a clear actionable summary. Direction was achieved through content structure rather than MI-guided clarification of goals tied to David's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David's input shaped practical details (shift times, technique selection, phone tracker), showing partial collaboration. However, the session was largely structured as advice delivery with long instruction blocks, positioning David in a receptive role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered genuine choices, invited David to select techniques, and respected his limits ('no promises beyond that') without pushback. Framing slips as data supported self-direction, though much of the interaction involved expert recommendations for David to accept rather than reinforcing his ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied primarily on providing reasons for change rather than drawing out David's own motivations; the importance ruler answer (4/10) was never deepened. David's motivations emerged spontaneously rather than through skillful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Despite David offering the 'expert' role, the counselor responded with a directive, information-heavy posture. Some collaborative elements existed (letting David pick techniques, offering to tailor the schedule), but the overall structure positioned the counselor as the advice source.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used an importance ruler and invited David to choose his own techniques, eliciting commitment-adjacent language ('half a pack by next time we talk'). Key missed opportunity: the 4/10 ruler response was never explored with 'Why a 4 and not a 2?', and change talk was not developed systematically.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and used a harm-reduction reframe that softened resistance to the quit/not-quit binary. However, sustain talk was mostly bypassed through advice-giving rather than resolved with double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to David's own reasons for change.
