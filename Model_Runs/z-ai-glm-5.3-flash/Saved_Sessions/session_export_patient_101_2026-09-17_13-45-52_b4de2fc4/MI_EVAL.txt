# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental tone with several affirmations ('Baby steps are smart,' 'No judgment on the numbers'). However, warmth is brief and formulaic, missing the emotional weight of Mei's fears about family alcoholism, pregnancy, and shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is sparse; vulnerable disclosures ('I've been carrying this around alone for a while,' 'That feels really exposed') are met with plan bullet points or tips rather than reflections. The counselor skips chances to explore the fear underlying her family history concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on alcohol reduction and contraceptive reliability, progressing logically to a defined plan with a Sunday 6 PM check-in. Focus is occasionally narrow, pushing contraception solutions before exploring readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The final plan reflects Mei's negotiated preferences, but the counselor presents solutions first and asks for assent afterward rather than eliciting her own ideas. Mei's contributions shape the plan only reactively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are repeatedly framed as Mei's own ('Which of these feels doable to start?') and her modifications are accommodated. Still, the bullet-point menu format pressures toward pre-selected options, and 'skip the risky friend group' was offered despite her stated discomfort.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Open-ended evocative questions are nearly absent, and the profile's importance/confidence ratings (8 vs. 4) were never explored to build discrepancy or self-efficacy. Mei's motivation emerged despite, not because of, the counselor's evocative efforts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role with prescriptive bullet lists rather than asking 'What do you think would work?' Mei's preferences shaped the plan only because she pushed back. Her comment about expecting to be told to quit everything implicitly confirms the directive dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is reinforced and 'Which of these feels doable?' elicited commitment, but the counselor never deliberately evokes it with open questions about motivations, hopes, or confidence. Mei's change talk was largely self-generated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does not argue and adjusts the plan when Mei pushes back, which is good. However, sustain talk about losing her social life and IUD fears is never explored or resolved with double-sided reflections—only worked around.
