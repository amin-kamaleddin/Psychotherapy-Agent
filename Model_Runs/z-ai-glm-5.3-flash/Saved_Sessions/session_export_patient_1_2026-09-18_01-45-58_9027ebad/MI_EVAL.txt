# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently honors the patient's autonomy, explicitly affirming his right to decide and negotiating terms ('that's your right,' 'you're the one holding the criteria'). No judgment or pressure is evident throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections demonstrate deep understanding, including the conflict between enjoying his one break and health concerns ('you're protecting your break AND thinking about your health'). The clinician also validates reluctance and identifies the workplace dust explanation without dismissing it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All questions and reflections steer toward the patient's own motivations and a concrete change plan (Saturdays-only trial). The conversation progresses steadily from ambivalence to a self-generated experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly positions the patient as the decision-maker and partner ('help me think this through like partners,' 'let's do the check together'). The patient sets his own criteria and terms, which the clinician adopts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's autonomy is explicitly protected: no lecturing, no scare tactics, and the clinician frames the plan as 'you taking charge of your own decision.' The patient chooses the conditions and duration himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions draw out the patient's own reasons and language ('maybe I'd think about cutting back' is highlighted back to him). Change talk is elicited rather than supplied, culminating in his self-designed one-month trial.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The check-up is framed as a shared process with shared criteria ('that way we're both looking for the same things'). The patient's demand to see the screen himself is warmly accommodated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully notices and reinforces emerging change talk ('you said maybe I'd think about cutting back... What makes cutting back feel possible?'). This leads to strong, sustained commitment language ('Saturdays only, four weeks, then you check again').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (dad smoking forty years, dust at work, not wanting to quit) is reflected non-judgmentally and gently reframed rather than confronted. The clinician avoids arguing, allowing the patient to move himself toward change.
