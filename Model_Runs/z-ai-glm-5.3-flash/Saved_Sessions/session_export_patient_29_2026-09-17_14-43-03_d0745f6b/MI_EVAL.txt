# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opens warmly with nonjudgmental affirmations ('You're clearly ready,' 'Nice plan'). Affirmations remain brief and formulaic, with little normalization of past failed quit attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections early on, but reflections become sparse as the session shifts to information-giving. David's anxiety about work stress goes unreflected, with option lists offered instead of complex emotional reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tight, consistent focus on smoking cessation throughout—covering triggers, medications, quit date, coping scripts, and next steps with no drift. Every exchange advances the change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan is largely co-constructed—David chose medications, quit date, coping strategies, and script wording. However, much content (dosing, scripts, option lists) was pre-packaged in expert format rather than jointly brainstormed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent permission-seeking before offering information and choices at every decision point, including letting David set the quit date. Never imposes; David's choices determined the plan's content.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions elicited some change talk ('Feels different this time'). However, the counselor mostly supplied reasons, options, and scripts rather than drawing motivation from David, and never explored why this time feels different.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent permission-asking and choice invitations, with David's input visibly shaping the plan. Slightly expert-led flavor from the pre-packaged scripts and step-by-step medication instructions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Importance/confidence rulers elicited strong change talk, which was affirmed. Missed a classic opportunity to develop the confidence-5 answer ('What would move you from a 5 to a 7?').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Barriers (work stress, restaurant triggers) were acknowledged and pivoted to problem-solving, but via advice-giving rather than MI-consistent softening. No double-sided reflections or reframing of past relapses as learning.
