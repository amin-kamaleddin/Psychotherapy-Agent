# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with consistent affirmations and belief in the patient. However, affirmations were occasionally formulaic and repetitive, lacking deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections, but these quickly gave way to problem-solving and question-stacking. The emotional core—feeling 'gross' and fear of failing again—was never explicitly reflected or validated in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior with each turn progressing logically toward a concrete plan. No topic drift, culminating in a clear, specific first step with backups and accountability.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was largely co-constructed, with the patient choosing options, schedule, rewards, and accountability. Several pre-packaged idea lists tilted slightly toward counselor-generated solutions rather than fully drawing out the patient's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of the patient's ownership—offering options, letting her design her own plan, reward, and accountability structure, and respecting her wish not to have materials drafted. Support was offered without pressure or control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Importance/confidence rulers effectively drew out intrinsic motivators, and confidence rose from 6 to 8 through the patient's own problem-solving. Relied partly on structured questions rather than deeper open evocation of her core reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient was consistently treated as the expert, choosing her own times, solutions, reward, and accountability person. Some suggestion lists leaned toward advice-giving rather than fully eliciting her ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of rulers and commitment questions drew out strong desire statements (importance 9) and robust end-of-session change talk ('I can do ten minutes'). Her core reasons and change talk were not deeply elaborated or reflected back to strengthen them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk was appropriately not dwelt upon, and the 'starting and stopping' pattern was reframed constructively. However, the fear of failure ('I don't want to fail again') was never directly addressed or softened—a missed opportunity.
