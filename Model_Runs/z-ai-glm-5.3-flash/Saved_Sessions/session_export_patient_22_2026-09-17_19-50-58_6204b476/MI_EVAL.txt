# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with strong affirmations and skillful avoidance of labeling ("You're not calling yourself an alcoholic, and you don't have to"). Some affirmations risk feeling formulaic, and ambivalence/fears are not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflective listening that captures both explicit content and emotional undercurrents, e.g., recognizing the weekday drinking is about loneliness rather than the wine. Deep complex reflections surface guilt, self-criticism, and fear of failure with minimal distortion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Crystal-clear focus on reducing weekday alcohol use from opening to close, with each question building progressively toward a concrete plan. No topic drift; even the gym discussion stays tethered to the drinking behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician behaves as a genuine equal partner ("let's think it through together"), offering joint problem-solving frames rather than solutions. The final plan is a negotiated, shared product.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice is consistently reinforced — her weekend boundaries are honored without pushing, and no advice-giving or persuading occurs anywhere. The entire plan is client-generated, with the clinician supporting her right to define success.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions are systematically drawn from the client herself; every element of the plan (weekday cutoff, gym, Dani, Friday makeup rule) is voiced by Camila. Open questions evoke her own history, insights, and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Camila is consistently positioned as the expert on her own life, and her contributions genuinely shape the session. The entire plan originates from her, with the psychiatrist reflecting, amplifying, and organizing her ideas — active power-sharing, not token collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked via a future-projection question and amplified by reflecting commitment language back ("listen to what you just did"). Camila responds with strong, deepening commitment statements throughout.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections effectively reframe sustain talk toward change, and labeling concerns are handled without arguing. However, some sustain talk receives fairly full airtime without an active pivot each time, keeping this from a top rating.
