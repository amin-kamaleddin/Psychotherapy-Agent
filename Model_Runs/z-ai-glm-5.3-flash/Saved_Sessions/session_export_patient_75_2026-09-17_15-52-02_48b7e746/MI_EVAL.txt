# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating Linda's fears and normalizing her rehearsed lines. However, affirmations were brief and task-focused, with little individualized positive regard for her strengths beyond performance feedback.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Linda's ambivalence, naming both sides of her conflict. Reflections were sometimes formulaic and deeper emotions—her fear of 'failing,' embarrassment, and feeling 'chained'—were acknowledged only briefly rather than fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed well-focused on asthma management and inhaler adherence, with structured next steps (tracking, question list, role-play) creating clear momentum. Direction was maintained through counselor-supplied structure rather than collaboratively guided focus, with early drift toward information-giving.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda was asked for her preferences on logistics (which option to practice), but the pros/cons list, question list, scripts, and safety plan were largely counselor-generated. True bidirectional problem-solving—building on what Linda already knows works—was absent, resulting in uneven power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently offered choices ('What would feel most helpful?') and framed advice as information to weigh, respecting that the prescriber owns the decision. Strengthening explicit ownership statements ('this is your decision') and linking choices to Linda's values would elevate this.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions early in the session drew out Linda's perspective, but the counselor relied heavily on supplying expert information and scripts rather than systematically evoking her own reasons and confidence. Tools like confidence rulers or 'What makes staying on track feel possible for you?' were never used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Inclusive language and menu-based options reflected some power sharing, but the mid-session shift to content delivery (pros/cons, scripts, safety plan) moved the counselor into an expert-driven role. Linda shaped logistics but not the clinical content itself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged largely from the client herself, but the counselor did not reinforce or deepen key commitment statements like 'I think I can commit to that.' Responses pivoted quickly to offering materials, missing chances to amplify commitment language through the client's own voice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement, but redirection was implicit rather than strategic. No double-sided reflections paired sustain talk with change talk, and Linda's 'it's been really difficult' was not actively reframed; sustain talk resolved mostly via supplied information.
