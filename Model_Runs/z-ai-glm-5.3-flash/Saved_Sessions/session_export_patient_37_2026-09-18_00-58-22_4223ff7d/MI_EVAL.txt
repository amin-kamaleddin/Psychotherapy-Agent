# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed the patient's worth, especially reframing her doubts as thoughtful planning rather than being difficult. Absolute support was conveyed through respectful, judgment-free engagement with her concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both surface content and deeper emotions, such as the aunt's shingles suffering and the weight lifting as fears resolved. The psychiatrist repeatedly mirrored the patient's feelings with precision and warmth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear, purposeful focus, structuring the conversation around the patient's three specific concerns one at a time. Each transition was collaborative and kept the dialogue moving toward informed decision-making.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange was highly collaborative, with frequent check-ins ('Does that land okay?') and shared agenda-setting. The patient was actively involved in shaping the direction and pace of the discussion.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist emphasized wanting the patient to leave 'feeling informed, not pushed' and framed decisions as hers to make. Information was offered in service of her choice rather than persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation through open questions about what would need to be true for her to feel confident and by drawing out her own reasoning. Scores slightly lower because some change talk emerged naturally rather than being deliberately deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The tone was consistently 'walking through it together,' with the clinician positioning herself as an ally rather than an authority. The patient explicitly noted that no one had sat down with her like this before.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective questioning drew out change talk, including the patient's shift from 'eighty percent sure I'd say no' to 'less scary' and wanting to decide. The clinician reflected and reinforced these statements well, though opportunities to deepen commitment language were not fully explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled with skilled double-sided reflections that paired doubts with the patient's motivation for her child's health. Counter-claims about vaccine safety were addressed directly without dismissing her concerns, though some sustain talk (e.g., mistrust of articles) was minimally explored.
