# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout despite Eric's defensiveness and minimization, framing slips as 'a learning chance, not failure.' However, explicit affirmations of Eric's honesty and strengths were scarce.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections throughout, including capturing the social dimension of smoking ('the break is about connection, not just nicotine'). Reflections were sometimes list-like and summary-heavy rather than deeply exploring the emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session focused on smoking with no drift, moving efficiently to a concrete, measurable one-week experiment with a specific Wednesday check-in. Lost a point for jumping to action planning early with a client at importance 3/10 without first consolidating personal motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed—Eric chose the coffee replacement, sitting with the crew, the backup strategy, the check-in time, and added his own cough question. Collaboration was slightly limited by the counselor supplying option menus rather than open-ended joint brainstorming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice ('only what you choose') and accepted Eric's conditions ('one week... if I hate it, I'm done') without pressure. Autonomy support stayed at the level of menu-offering, missing chances to explicitly reinforce Eric's ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The importance ruler and 'what would move 3 to 6' question drew out Eric's perspective, and he voiced personal insight ('It's not just the nicotine'). But the counselor met these motivational openings with logistics, and motivation remained largely external (Donna).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked Eric's preferences and let him shape the plan, but offered pre-packaged option lists each turn without explicitly acknowledging his expertise about his own work rhythm and triggers. Power-sharing was present but uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evoked change-relevant language ('Cutting down a bit maybe', 'I'll give it a fair shot') and helped Eric commit to a concrete experiment. Missed opportunities to deepen this change talk, such as exploring why Eric asked about the cough.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without amplifying it and used partial reflections that gently pivoted toward change. However, some reflections repeated the sustain side ('the cough is minor'), risking reinforcement of minimization, and no double-sided reflections linked Eric's values to change.
