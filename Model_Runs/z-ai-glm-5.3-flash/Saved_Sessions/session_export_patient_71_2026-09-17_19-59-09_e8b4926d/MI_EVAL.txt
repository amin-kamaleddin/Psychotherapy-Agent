# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard communicated clearly, including the explicit statement 'You're the expert on your own life.' No judgment about missed sessions or takeout nights; warm, respectful acceptance of Laura's pace and autonomy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The opening reflection accurately captures Laura's core ambivalence between values and behavior. Slightly limited by not always deepening into the emotional tone Laura expressed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus remains clearly on aligning daily actions (yoga, health) with Laura's stated values, channeling discussion toward actionable change. Stays on target throughout, though consistency was best demonstrated in the earlier exchanges.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction framed as equal partners—the psychiatrist reflects, affirms, and asks rather than tells. Laura's own ideas (Sunday planning, identity-based self-talk, the yoga metaphor) fully shape the plan, demonstrating genuine power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** 'What do you think might help' explicitly hands choice and ownership to Laura, with no advice-giving or pressure. Laura's closing comment ('Thank you for not giving me the answers') validates the autonomy support was experienced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited entirely from within the client; her values, identity ('someone who shows up'), and strategies emerge from her, not from counselor suggestions. Laura explicitly confirms this: 'Thank you for not giving me the answers and making me find my own.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit partnership statement ('You're the expert on your own life') locates expertise within the client. Laura's contributions genuinely drive the session's direction and the resulting plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions directly invite change-oriented reasoning and planning, generating strong change talk (scheduling yoga, treating health like volunteering). More explicit reinforcement of her change talk would elevate this further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor does not dwell on Laura's reasons for maintaining the status quo ('one missed class,' busy days), instead gently pivoting toward change without arguing against her ambivalence. The 'week's ruined' spiral was met with a curious, non-confrontational question.
