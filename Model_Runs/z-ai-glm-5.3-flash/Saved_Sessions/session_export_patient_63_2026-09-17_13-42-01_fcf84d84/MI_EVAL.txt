# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout with no shaming or moralizing, offering warmth and normalization even around potential relapse. Score limited by generic affirmations that overlooked Jordan's specific strengths (e.g., his creativity with the 'glow-up budget' line, his insight in choosing Marcus).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured Jordan's ambivalence, but empathic reflection was almost entirely replaced by advice lists as the session progressed. His most vulnerable moments — 'worried about looking weak' and 'I just... do it' — were met with tactics rather than reflective engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on weekend shoplifting and moved steadily toward a concrete, actionable plan with a specific follow-up commitment. Not a 5 because direction came via pre-packaged advice, and the core driver ('looking weak') was never made the explicit focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative moments existed — Jordan's 'saving for X' reframe, his recruitment of Marcus, and his self-designed line became core plan elements. However, power-sharing was uneven, with ideas flowing predominantly from clinician to client rather than being drawn out first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asked permission, never coerced, and adopted Jordan's own choices rather than overriding them. Autonomy was undercut, though, by the volume of directives handing him ready-made answers and the absence of explicit credit for his authorship of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Not a single question explored why Jordan wanted to stop, what he valued, or what change would mean for him; questions were closed or procedural. The session read as CBT-style problem-solving delivered to the client rather than elicitation of his own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician operated predominantly in an expert/advice-giving role, with bulleted strategy lists in nearly every turn. Jordan's meaningful contributions to the plan were client-initiated rather than clinician-fostered, leaving the prescriptive pattern intact.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan offered meaningful change talk ('I'm serious, I'm done with it'), but the clinician rarely used open questions to evoke or deepen it, and commitment language was affirmed only generically. Change talk grew more from the client's own momentum than from interviewing technique.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician wisely never reinforced or dwelled on sustain talk ('I want it,' 'we'll see, I guess') and occasionally normalized the ambivalence. However, pivots away were accomplished by jumping to new advice rather than strategic techniques like double-sided reflections or developing discrepancy — sustain talk was skirted, not resolved.
