# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard is maintained with explicit statements against lecturing and frequent, specific affirmations. Some affirmations feel slightly effusive or scripted for a guarded adolescent, risking a patronizing tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate, consistently capturing both surface content and emotional undertone (e.g., the 'Catch-22,' the 'heavy weight' of one identity). Complex reflections dominate over questions, demonstrating strong MI empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session coherently maintains an internal focus but drifts entirely to parent-teen trust and never re-anchors to the referral problem of substance use. Tyler's 'my friends do way more than me' was a prime directional opening that went unused.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely two-sided, with tentative advice and plans co-constructed from Tyler's own ideas. Falls short of a 5 only because the clinician occasionally leads slightly, such as introducing the 'trade' framing before Tyler fully owned it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is exemplary throughout, from 'whether anything changes is genuinely your call' to reinforcing Tyler's ownership of the plan. No directive pressure is present, and Tyler's conditions are reflected rather than negotiated away.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nearly every substantive move elicits Tyler's own reasoning, successfully drawing out motivation absent at intake. Evocation is limited, however, as it is channeled exclusively into the relationship domain and never evokes Tyler's concerns about substance use itself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly positions Tyler as the expert on his life, and the texting plan, trade framing, and mediated-conversation idea all originate from Tyler. Session turns are built directly on Tyler's contributions, meeting the standard for a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked, moving Tyler from 'nothing is worth my time' to a self-generated, concrete plan and commitment-adjacent language ('I'd try it'). A 5 is not given because change talk around the substance use itself is never explicitly evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently met with reflective listening and pivots toward Tyler's goals, including a textbook double-sided reflection. However, key sustain talk such as 'I'm like the responsible one in my group' is left unexplored, and the framing sometimes drifts toward the mom-conflict frame without rebalancing.
