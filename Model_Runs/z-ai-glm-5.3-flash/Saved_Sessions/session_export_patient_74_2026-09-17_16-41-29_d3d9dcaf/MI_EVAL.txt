# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth without judgment ("That tension makes sense," "we can check in next time nonjudgmentally, however it goes"). Affirmations, while present, became somewhat formulaic and repetitive; limited explicit normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening — accurate complex reflection of ambivalence ("you enjoy smoking and don't want to quit, yet you worry about the example you're setting"). Reflections occasionally shifted too quickly into questions/suggestions, limiting deeper exploration of guilt and fear about her daughter.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior; session progressed to a fully specific plan — Wednesday, 6:30, back porch, sister's mug, pre-made tea, podcast backup, 3 breaths, anticipated stress barrier, and follow-up check-in at next session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Joint framing ("we can explore," "I can help brainstorm options"); plan genuinely co-constructed. Clinician initiated most agenda items (option lists, scaling questions), with Linda largely in a responsive role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Excellent — "without pressing you to quit," acceptance of "don't hold me to quitting," nonjudgmental check-in offered. Validated by Linda's own statement: "Thanks for not pushing me to quit everything at once." Change framed as a self-directed experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within — rulers elicited importance (10) and Linda's own reasons ("for the kids, mostly... maybe a little for me too"); confidence rose via her own problem-solving. Occasionally supplied options/rationale before eliciting Linda's own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Linda's expertise treated as central; her contributions (mug, podcast, timing, location, tea prep) shaped the plan. Mild expert flavor at times, as the clinician generated the option menu, though Linda freely selected and modified.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance/confidence rulers; confidence rose measurably from 4 to 6. Commitment language evoked ("It's worth a shot," "I'll give it an honest shot," "maybe I'll surprise myself"). Specificity requests solidified commitment; podcast question elicited her insight: "Maybe that's the real point of all this anyway."

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Neutral, validating double-sided reflections rather than amplifying barriers; consistent redirect toward small experiments. At times dwelled briefly on obstacles (stress/urge troubleshooting) and affirmed "one experiment, not a quit plan" without a gentle reframe to develop discrepancy.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.3/5**
