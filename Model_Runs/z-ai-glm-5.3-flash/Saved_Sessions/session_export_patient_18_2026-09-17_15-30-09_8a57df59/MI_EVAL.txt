# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance ('You're not in trouble here') with affirmations of the patient's existing coping skills. Warmth is somewhat brief and transactional, moving quickly through content rather than lingering with her ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captures her surprise, embarrassment, and sense of unfairness at being singled out. Reflections stay mostly at content level, with little exploration of deeper feelings like fear of judgment or belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and party safety once the patient chooses the topic. Transitions smoothly from exploration to risk assessment to concrete planning with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative with permission-seeking, menu-based options, and a plan co-created around the patient's 🐸 signal. The initial strategy list is counselor-generated rather than jointly brainstormed, placing her in a passive respondent role at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces choice ('You get to decide') with permission-based advice and options. The patient freely rejects components (phone timer, two-drink rule) without pushback — exemplary autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation via confidence/readiness rulers and reflective questions, with the patient generating her own insights. Much of the session relies on counselor-supplied strategies rather than drawing out her own reasons, values, and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Incorporates the patient's input (frog emoji, dropping the two-drink rule) and asks permission before advising. Advice-giving is heavy and top-down, with her contributions shaping the plan only modestly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler elicited useful change language ('only accepting drinks I pour myself... maybe that gets me to an 8') and she self-generated the 🐸 strategy. Change talk is not systematically deepened or reflected back, and commitment language is underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient voices notable sustain talk ('it's not a big deal,' 'two-drink thing feels babyish') which the counselor neither reinforces nor actively softens. No double-sided reflections or strategic reframing; redirection is passive rather than skillful.
