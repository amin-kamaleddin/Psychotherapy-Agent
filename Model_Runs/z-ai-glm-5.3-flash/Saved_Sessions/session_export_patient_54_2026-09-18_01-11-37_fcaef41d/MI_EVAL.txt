# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, with specific, individualized affirmations. Even Isabella's autonomy assertions and skepticism are met with respect rather than correction; the client explicitly notes she braced for a lecture and never got one.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening frequently operates at a complex level, capturing meaning and affect (e.g., 'real pride in your voice'). Slightly limited by pivots to problem-solving before fully exploring emotional nuance, such as the family-approval anxiety behind hiding the grape juice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains unwavering focus on the target behavior (alcohol use in pregnancy) and its immediate behavioral steps while still feeling client-led. Each exchange builds sequentially toward concrete, client-generated plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely co-constructed — the client's input determines the direction and content, from the plan for Marco to the Friday-night rehearsal. Informational contributions are framed as choices rather than prescriptions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Locates expertise firmly in the client and ensures all strategies (grape juice ritual, mocktail, rehearsal, Lucia backup) originate from Isabella herself. Information is offered as a menu of options, and the client reports 'I found the answer myself.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently draws out the client's own motivations, values, and solutions rather than supplying them. Elicited change talk is deepened through questions like 'What makes that feel doable?' and obstacle-anticipation prompts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power-sharing demonstrably landed — Isabella states 'Nobody handed it to me' — and her contributions shape every plan made. The counselor's stance as a collaborator rather than an authority is consistent across the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout domain: evocative questions target values and meaning, and reflections selectively amplify change talk. Isabella's language escalates from desire to reason to concrete commitment and implementation planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections honor sustain talk while pivoting toward change, and autonomy statements are reframed as compatible with change rather than resistance. Held at 4 only because the transcript contains only mild sustain talk; the counselor was never stress-tested against heavier resistance.
