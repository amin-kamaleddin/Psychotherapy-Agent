# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming throughout ('That's real change talk, and it's yours'). Falls short of a 5 as some affirmations feel slightly formulaic and there is no explicit normalization of past struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including a strong double-sided reflection on ambivalence and precise use of the client's own words ('quit on quitting', 'harder to pretend I didn't decide'). Reflections track emotional nuances at increasing depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus on smoking cessation throughout, moving from ambivalence to a specific commitment (Thursday dentist call) and a concrete behavioral plan. Each question builds momentum without rushing or drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions are co-constructed ('let's figure out that pub plan together'), and the client genuinely shapes the session's direction, order of steps, and next session's focus. The counselor operates as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy strongly reinforced: 'explore this at your own pace', 'a fair test, not a perfection test', and crediting that the plan 'came entirely from you'. No persuasive pressure; her harm-reduction preference is honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from within via questions targeting her values and judgments ('What does your own gut say?'). The client's language shows deepening desire, ability, reasons, and commitment, all self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client positioned as the expert ('What would you need from me or anyone else?'), with her contributions genuinely shaping the plan. The counselor explicitly credits her ownership of each decision.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk skillfully evoked and deepened ('What would make that easier?'), explicitly named and developed into a concrete commitment. The plan emerges almost entirely from the client's own language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk acknowledged without amplification and redirected with change-oriented questions ('What does your own gut say?'). Could be more active — repeated the routine/friends theme slightly more than necessary and missed chances to reframe fear of failure with normalization or self-compassion.
