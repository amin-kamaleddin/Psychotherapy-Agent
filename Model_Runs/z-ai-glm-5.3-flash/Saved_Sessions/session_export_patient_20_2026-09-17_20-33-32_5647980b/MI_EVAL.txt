# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently offered unconditional positive regard, affirming Brandon's honesty and normalizing his ambivalence without judgment. Explicitly addressed his fear of 'a lecture' and promised slips would not trigger a 'disappointment lecture.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and deep, consistently capturing both sides of Brandon's ambivalence (e.g., feeling judged while wondering if family has a point). The counselor reflected emotional nuance including the sting of family criticism, fear of being counted, and past pattern of bailing after slips.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained an unwavering focus on alcohol reduction with no topic drift, advancing strategically from exploring family concerns to a concrete two-week plan with defined success markers. Each response built toward securing commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was fully co-constructed, with the counselor treating Brandon's lived experience (the marathon, family dynamics) as valid expertise and offering options as questions rather than directives. Brandon co-designed the two-week experiment and volunteered to report back even after slips.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor reinforced client ownership at every turn ('at your pace,' 'you designed a plan that fits your life') and validated Brandon's choice to leave weekends off-limits. The closing support menu handed decisions directly to the client, who reported the plan felt self-chosen rather than forced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor never supplied reasons for change, instead evoking Brandon's own motivators (presence with his nephew, his mom's glass-watching, sharp mornings) and affirming his self-generated insights. This linking of change to the client's own values is the hallmark of expert evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positioned Brandon as the expert ('nobody handed you that answer,' 'you've designed your own experiment'), and Brandon's own contributions shaped the session's direction and final plan. The closing question about desired support further distributed power to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked through targeted questions ('What would look right to you?', 'What's your finish line?'), moving Brandon from desire to ability and commitment. Each change talk moment was reflected and amplified, including evoking confidence from his half-marathon experience.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally handled well by pivoting to planning around obstacles rather than dwelling on doubt (e.g., reframing 'Friday nights run long' into troubleshooting). However, some sustain talk ('no promises it'll be perfect,' 'nobody really cares anyway') was absorbed into neutral reflections rather than explicitly softened or reframed.
