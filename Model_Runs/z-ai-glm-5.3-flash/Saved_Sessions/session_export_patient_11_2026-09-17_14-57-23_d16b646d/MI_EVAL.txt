# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, supportive tone with affirmations like "That's solid" and "I like the 'no zero days' approach." Warmth was somewhat task-focused, with little explicit normalization of struggles or affirmation of courage in seeking change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate, e.g., capturing how the move disrupted her routine and her preference for small steps. Reflections tended to summarize logistics rather than explore the emotional layer, such as the salsa moment and what feeling weak means to her self-image.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained exceptionally clear focus on increasing physical activity, with every exchange moving toward a concrete plan of specific days, times, cues, backup plans, and tracking. When the patient raised nutrition, the psychiatrist did not chase it.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely between equals, with the plan co-shaped through menus of options and the patient's answers (e.g., co-writing the if-then plan, tweaking the fridge note). Some structured menus steered the agenda rather than letting the patient's own ideas alone shape direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforced the patient's ownership throughout — she chose days, times, tracker format, note wording, and check-in questions, with options offered rather than directives. Her rejection of external accountability was accepted without pushback.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out motivation from the patient — her reasons (energy, strength, jar-opening, body confidence) and her core value of proving self-reliance after the move. Much of the session focused on plan mechanics rather than continued development of deeper values and emotional motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the patient as the expert on her own life, with questions like "Which of those supports feels most helpful to you?" genuinely handing over decision power. Counselor contributions were offered as options, never directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked change talk through confidence scaling, meaningful win questions, and inviting the patient to articulate her 'why' in the fridge note, yielding strong change talk ("I feel hopeful"). Did not deliberately deepen or elaborate the change talk, often moving straight to planning logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well — consistency worries and postponing nutrition were acknowledged without amplification, and the 10-minute backup plan softened her fear of failure. Repeatedly asking "What might get in the way?" invited barrier talk without double-sided reflections to pivot back toward motivation.
