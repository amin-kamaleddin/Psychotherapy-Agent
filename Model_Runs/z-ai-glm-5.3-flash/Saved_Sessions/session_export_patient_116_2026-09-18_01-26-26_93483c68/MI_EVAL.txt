# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with no judgment despite the patient's guarded, deflecting style. Warmly received his humor and honesty, and rolled with minimization without disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening captured both content and emotion, notably the pivotal reflection reframing the problem as 'being managed' rather than the pills. Each reflection deepened the conversation and was explicitly confirmed by the patient.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on medication nonadherence, progressing logically from ambivalence to a concrete experiment and follow-up agenda. Deflections were gently redirected without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan and next session's agenda were fully co-constructed from the patient's own ideas. The counselor genuinely shared power and framed future work as something to 'figure out together.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support ('it's your call, and I'll be here either way'; 'no verdict here'), with the plan framed as the patient's own experiment. The patient directly validated this: 'Thanks for treating me like an adult for once.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation was drawn from the client—no advice or persuasion. Strategic open questions moved him from precontemplation to a self-initiated commitment, with the insight credited entirely to him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient was explicitly positioned as the expert ('you know your life and body best'), and every intervention built on his words. The entire plan originated from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked preparation-level change talk ('maybe I'd take them, if it's my call') from a precontemplative client via open evocative questions. Amplified it with a change-talk summary and consolidated the insight.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged and redirected toward change without argument—e.g., reframing 'I've managed this long' as evidence of self-management experience. Dislike of pills was converted into autonomy-relevant material.
