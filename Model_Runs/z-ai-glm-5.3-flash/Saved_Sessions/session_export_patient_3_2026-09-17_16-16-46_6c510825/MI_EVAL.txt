# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveyed warmth and unconditional positive regard ('I'm glad you reached out,' 'Ten minutes absolutely counts') and honored Aisha's request to avoid weight/scale talk without judgment. Potential setbacks were framed non-judgmentally as opportunities to build on what worked.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate, acknowledging the strain of balancing family, work, and weight worries alongside her hopefulness. However, they were somewhat brief and formulaic, with limited deeper exploration of the emotional core of her body-image distress ('I feel gross').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on the target behavior of resuming exercise, moving efficiently from values to a specific behavior (15-minute post-dinner walks) to a concrete plan with supports and a scheduled follow-up. No topic drift occurred; every exchange built toward the behavioral goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Aisha choosing the days, times, reminder method, and accountability partner, and the counselor asking permission for follow-up timing and co-designing check-in questions. The counselor acted as an equal partner facilitating Aisha's own ideas rather than prescribing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly affirmed Aisha's choice ('I'll support whatever you choose,' 'work at a pace that fits your life') and offered a menu of supports for her to select from. When Aisha declined to modify the plan, the counselor respected that without pushing additional advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently elicited from Aisha herself through open questions, drawing on past success, and using a confidence ruler to identify a small change that would boost confidence. Evocation was strong but somewhat process-focused, with missed opportunities to explore the deeper personal meaning of 'feeling comfortable in her own skin.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Aisha as the expert on her own life, asking what felt most important and doable to her and inviting her input on the check-in format. Aisha's suggestions (morning check-in, no scale talk) were adopted verbatim, showing her contributions genuinely shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor actively drew on Aisha's existing motivation by asking evocative questions about past confidence and consolidating her 'feels doable' statement into a concrete commitment. Since Aisha was already highly motivated, opportunities to deepen her reasons for change were limited but could have been explored further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha's minimal sustain talk (time barriers, self-consciousness about gyms, 'It's been Monday for months') was acknowledged neutrally without amplification. The counselor then immediately pivoted toward solutions, ensuring sustain talk was never reinforced or dwelt upon.
