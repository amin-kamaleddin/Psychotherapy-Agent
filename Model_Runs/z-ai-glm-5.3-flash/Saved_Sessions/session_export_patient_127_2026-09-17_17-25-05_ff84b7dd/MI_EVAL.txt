# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with affirming statements like acknowledging 22 days clean as real progress. However, affirmations were brief and formulaic, with little explicit unconditional positive regard when the client voiced self-criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made accurate reflective statements capturing surface content well (e.g., fear of becoming 'a drone'). Reflections were often followed immediately by advice, cutting short deeper exploration of shame and rejection sensitivity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained very clear focus on securing lawful employment as a step toward a pro-social, crime-free life. Every exchange tied back to concrete steps protecting the 22 days clean—a clear strength of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed partial collaboration by asking preferences, adjusting the agenda to the client's request, and using permission-based language. Power balance remained uneven—most ideas and scripts originated with the counselor rather than being co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and asked permission before suggesting options, but was quite prescriptive with exact scripts, times, and sequences. Little explicit affirmation of the client's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied primarily on providing solutions rather than eliciting motivation from within. The client's rich motivations (Fiona, identity, small pro-social pleasures) emerged spontaneously but were rarely probed or developed with open, evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Expertise resided heavily with the counselor, who repeatedly supplied scripts, lines, and strategies rather than drawing on the client's own problem-solving. Some permission-seeking occurred, but the session remained largely expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client generated genuine change talk and the counselor's future-oriented question and concrete planning helped elicit commitment. Change talk was not consistently deepened with evocative questions, and the counselor pivoted quickly to providing scripts instead of amplifying the client's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk (positive), but also did not actively soften or strategically redirect it—no double-sided reflections or reframing were used. A major missed opportunity to flip the client's own insight ('you don't survive inside being unreliable') into counter-evidence against 'I'm lazy.'
