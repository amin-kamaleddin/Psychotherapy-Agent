# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental with explicit assurances ('I won't judge or lecture') and respect for Marcus's dismissiveness and request to pause. Fell short of 5 because warmth felt somewhat formulaic and strengths/ambivalence were not explicitly affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured both the social benefits of drinking and the hassles (stressful exit, hangover). Reflections were occasionally long summaries rather than emotionally attuned, and feelings about adult control and mistrust went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on alcohol use and built effectively from exploration to importance ruler to a concrete plan. The opening was somewhat open-ended given Marcus's precontemplative stance, and the session closed without a summary or follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A genuinely collaborative tone with co-construction of the plan and direct incorporation of Marcus's own ideas (the homework/game excuse). Power balance tipped slightly toward the counselor during the strategy-listing turns, where options were presented before Marcus generated his own.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support through 'it's your call,' menu-based choices, harm-reduction framing, and honoring the pause request. The long unsolicited strategy list edged toward directing the session's content.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Marcus via the pros/cons question and importance ruler, producing clear client-generated movement from 'I don't see the problem' to 'limits might not be the worst idea.' Evocation was uneven in the middle when the counselor supplied strategies himself, and values or longer-term goals were never probed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Marcus as the expert, and his choices genuinely shaped the session, with the counselor adopting Marcus's 'pacing tonight' line. A brief shift into advice-giving with a prescriptive strategy list occurred before Marcus's own ideas were fully explored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The importance ruler and pros/cons questions skillfully elicited Marcus's own reasons and a self-generated plan with readiness language ('maybe I'll come back and tell you'). Confidence was never explicitly assessed, and change talk was not deepened with follow-ups like 'What would success look like?'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was reflected neutrally and redirected toward problem-solving without lecturing or dwelling on risks. Reflections sometimes repeated sustain talk verbatim (e.g., 'nothing catastrophic') without double-sided reflections to pivot toward change.
