# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental — Camila's 'I'm not an alcoholic' is met with neutral reflection rather than correction, and slips are framed as 'information, not failure.' Warmth is somewhat brief and functional rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and timely, capturing her ambivalence and fear about weeknight drinking. The emotional layer beneath the drinking (breakup, loneliness, social anxiety) is mentioned but never explored in depth, as the counselor pivots quickly to planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains an unwavering focus on reducing weeknight drinking from the first question through the scheduled Thursday check-in. Every exchange ties back to the concrete Wednesday plan, with no drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor works from Camila's priorities and adjusts the plan based on her feedback (one night instead of two, sparkling water instead of tea, cupboard instead of pouring out wine). Collaboration is uneven, however, as the counselor tends to present structured option sets rather than co-creating from scratch.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Permission is repeatedly asked, choices are offered, and Camila feels free to decline and renegotiate, noting the plan 'fits my life.' Choices are largely counselor-constructed menus, and the pace mildly nudges toward commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful evocation occurs via the opening question and confidence scaling, which elicits Camila's own solutions and insights. However, a substantial portion of strategies are counselor-supplied ideas to pick from, and the deeper 'why' of her change is not fully developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission is sought, client modifications shape the plan, and confidence is checked. Still, the agenda and options are largely counselor-defined, with little explicit acknowledgment that expertise about her life resides with her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked through open questions and the confidence ruler, producing commitment language ('I'll try the delay thing,' 'I can set the alarm right now'). Some change talk is prompted by counselor-supplied options rather than freely generated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I don't wanna stop altogether,' 'I'm not an alcoholic') is met with neutral acceptance and productively reframed, and Camila generates her own counter to all-or-nothing thinking. Some offered options keep focus on negotiating around drinking rather than consistently pivoting toward change.
