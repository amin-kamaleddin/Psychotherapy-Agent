# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated unconditional positive regard with repeated no-judgment statements, appreciation for Marek's honesty, and validation of his skepticism throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Marek's view of his drinking as controlled and social. Reflections were occasionally mechanical/list-like, with limited exploration of the emotional meaning of drinking as part of his social identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear focus on alcohol use throughout, gently redirecting tangents back to concrete next steps. The session culminated in a specific, agreed-upon plan: one tracked night, voice memos, Monday morning check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor sought permission before sharing information, co-constructed the tracking plan around Marek's preferences, and agreed to his terms. Marek genuinely shaped the plan's specifics, including voice memos and message-based check-in.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with explicit 'no pressure' framing and respect for Marek's choice to observe rather than set a limit. The counselor adapted when Marek said imposed limits felt like a trap.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from Marek rather than lectured, using open questions, his own predictions, and confidence scaling with self-generated data (tracking). Deeper exploration of his personal values and reasons for change beyond hypothetical illness was not pursued.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveyed that expertise resides within Marek, and his contributions shaped the plan (voice memos, Monday check-in were his ideas). Somewhat limited by the counselor proposing most option menus rather than asking Marek to generate ideas himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk with open questions and confidence ratings (eliciting a 6-7), reinforcing follow-through via a phone reminder. There was limited deepening of Marek's 'got something to think about' and 'if I got sick' statements into more personal commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement, then pivoted toward neutral data and harm-reduction options rather than argument. More double-sided reflection could have resolved ambivalence faster, but sustain talk was never amplified.
