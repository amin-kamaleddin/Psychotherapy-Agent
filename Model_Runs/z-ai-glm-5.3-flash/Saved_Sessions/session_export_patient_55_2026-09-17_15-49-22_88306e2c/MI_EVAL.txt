# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a polite, nonjudgmental tone throughout, but acceptance was formulaic rather than deeply unconditional. Isabelle's dismissal of the screening was never normalized or accepted before the session pivoted to study tips.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections stayed superficial, paraphrasing surface content without exploring the meaning behind Isabelle's dismissiveness or discomfort. Emotional nuance was consistently missed, including her repeated attempts to leave.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The session lost focus on the alcohol-use risk within the first two exchanges, shifting to school stress and study tips. A high-risk screening flag was dismissed with no focused exploration, a serious direction failure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor twice delivered multi-part advice blocks that Isabelle met with lukewarm compliance or rejection. Decisions were not jointly explored, and Isabelle's own expertise was never incorporated into a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The strongest domain: the counselor consistently offered choices and honored Isabelle's decisions without pressure. However, options centered on whether to end the session, and quick compliance with 'no follow-up' may have meant premature abandonment of a high-risk teen.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No open questions were asked about Isabelle's drinking, values, or motivations — motivation was outsourced to generic advice. The counselor functioned as an advice-dispenser, the opposite of evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor positioned herself as an advice-giver rather than recognizing Isabelle's expertise, and Isabelle's input shaped the session mainly by ending it. The choice menu was a superficial power-sharing gesture.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There was virtually no effort to evoke change talk about alcohol use; the only mention of parties was buried in an advice block. The change talk that emerged concerned physics test prep, not the target behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Isabelle's clear sustain talk ('I'm not an alcoholic,' 'everyone does it') was met with no double-sided reflections or strategic response. The counselor passively allowed the dismissals to end the conversation rather than pivoting toward change language.
