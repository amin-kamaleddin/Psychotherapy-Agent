# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard, notably with 'neither makes you a bad mom' when the client disclosed guilt over smoking and resting. Never judged her smoking, rationalizations, or past failed quit attempt, and affirmations felt genuine and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing her core experience: 'those five minutes are the only moments where nobody needs anything from you,' which the client confirmed verbatim. Also named the unspoken layer of guilt about resting, prompting client insight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking cessation throughout, with each response steering toward the target behavior and related themes (coping, ritual, guilt, slips). Session progressed logically to a client-defined starting plan with no meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative—'we could map out the evening piece together'—with every plan element coming from the client's own ideas. Her hesitations and conditions (slowly, no announcements) directly shaped the session's outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced choice: 'whether they'd fit for you is really your call to make' and 'you don't have to promise a quit date.' Honored her request for privacy and pacing without pushback; no pressure or persuasion used.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from within ('what do you think made those feel peaceful?', 'what was missing last time?'), eliciting the client's own insights about guilt, slips as data, and evening rituals. Motivation and answers clearly resided with the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert ('let me ask you as the expert on you') and credited her insights ('you noticed it first'). The plan that emerged was built entirely from her ideas and shaped the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk with open questions and elicitations, producing rich client-generated language ('I'd want him to not have a mom who smells like smoke,' 'that feels doable'). Reinforced change talk by highlighting the shift from 'whether' to 'how.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it, using double-sided reflections and skillful reframes (slips as 'data,' flipping the 'game over' trap). Minor deduction: several extended exchanges exploring fears gave some air to sustain talk before redirecting.
