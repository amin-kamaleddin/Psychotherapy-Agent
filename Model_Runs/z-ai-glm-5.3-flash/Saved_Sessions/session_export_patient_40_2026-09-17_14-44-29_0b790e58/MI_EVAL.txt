# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental tone with effective normalization of shame and a caring safety check. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and lacked deeper individualized validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections synthesized Elias's core struggles and self-doubt well. Reflections sometimes stayed surface-level without deeper exploration of the shame and relapse meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus from processing the relapse through to a concrete evening plan with no drift. Each response built momentum toward Elias's goal of doable relapse prevention.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely equal partnership—Elias selected plan elements, named his sister, and refined the cue wording himself. Exemplary power-sharing where client contributions shaped session outcomes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered menus of options rather than directives, and Elias's preferences directly shaped every artifact. Never pressured or prescribed, reinforcing Elias's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out Elias's own coping evidence and values, leading to his self-generated conclusions about strength. Could have deepened exploration of ambivalence about his motives for stopping and developed desire/need language further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Elias as the expert and adapted plans to his stated constraints, with his contributions substantially shaping the plan. Counselor still proposed the plan structure rather than fully co-constructing from Elias's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and amplified change talk through strategic questions and the confidence scale, moving Elias from shame to hope ('Now it feels possible'). Missed opportunities to explore importance/need language more deeply.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully reframed Elias's avoidance doubt as strength and adapted when he mentioned past failed plans. Limited use of double-sided reflections to explicitly work through his ambivalence about why he stopped.
