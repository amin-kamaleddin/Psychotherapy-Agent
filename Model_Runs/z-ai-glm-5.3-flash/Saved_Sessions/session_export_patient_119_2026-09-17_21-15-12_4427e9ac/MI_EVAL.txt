# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, reframing self-deprecation ('that's not falling apart') and explicitly offering non-judgment. The patient confirmed it landed ('helps more than you know') with no labeling or conditional acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Delivered highly accurate, deep reflections including capturing the 'chaos' of mornings and elevating 'sugar levels matter more than your pride.' The patient repeatedly confirmed feeling truly heard.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on medication adherence, ending with a concrete plan (pills by the coffee maker, bottle out tonight). Some emotional explorations (sister's criticism, guilt) drifted without immediate bridging back to the adherence target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative with equal partners, using 'let's figure this out together' and letting the patient's ideas shape the plan and follow-up agenda. The entire plan originated from her coffee maker idea.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced patient ownership and choice ('You get to decide what pace makes sense'), with the plan entirely her own. The patient explicitly preferred this to 'being told what to do.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew out intrinsic motivations — being there for her daughter in twenty years, lifting guilt, being 'in charge of my health' — rather than inserting expert arguments. The patient moved from ambivalence to explicit commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the patient as the expert ('You know your life best'), and her contributions substantially shaped the session. She noticed the difference ('at least this is my idea, not another lecture').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk ('that's what I actually want' reframed as 'a reason, not a chore') and credited her self-generated plan. The patient reached explicit commitment language by the end.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well with reframes rather than dwelling, turning the habituation worry into an invitation to problem-solve. Occasionally restated sustain talk content (e.g., the fridge calendar worry) before redirecting.
