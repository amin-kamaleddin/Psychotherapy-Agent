# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, acknowledging resistance without judgment and repeatedly offering choice. However, warmth was somewhat task-focused, with limited explicit affirmation of Mateo's worth beyond his usefulness in planning steps.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections (routine as structure, record barrier), but deeper emotional content — fear of failure, record shame, and distance from his family dreams — was met with brief acknowledgments rather than sustained exploration. The counselor repeatedly pivoted to logistics instead of reflecting emotional weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on employment tied to stability goals and steered toward concrete steps. Direction occasionally became rigid, continuing to drive tasks when Mateo signaled feeling pushed, and left the deeper discrepancy work (family, recidivism risk) underexplored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively co-constructed materials with Mateo's input (text with his name/number, scaled-down plan). Collaboration faltered when the counselor steered back to the job agenda despite Mateo's stated preference to keep things light and asked for details he'd already declined.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: accepted 'no promises' without pushback, framed options as menus, and affirmed Mateo's choice to search on his own. Consistently reinforced ownership without pressure or coercion while avoiding enabling avoidance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo's motivations were elicited (real money, something going somewhere, steady life) but developed superficially. The powerful 'family, kids maybe' moment was met with a logistics pivot rather than exploration of what that future means to him; the session leaned on counselor-provided solutions over intrinsic reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo's preferences genuinely shaped the plan (warehouse/construction, simplicity, own pace), and refusals were adapted to. Partnership wobbled when the counselor kept pushing the job search despite 'keeping it light' and re-asked the ZIP code after refusal.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out desire, commitment, and vision ('something solid eventually,' 'maybe I'll have done it by next time'). Missed a key chance to deepen and amplify Mateo's family/stability vision — a golden discrepancy opportunity left unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('no promises,' plans falling through before) was acknowledged without reinforcement and redirected to doable steps. However, doubts weren't strategically softened with double-sided reflections linking them to Mateo's own goals — mostly accepted and moved past.
