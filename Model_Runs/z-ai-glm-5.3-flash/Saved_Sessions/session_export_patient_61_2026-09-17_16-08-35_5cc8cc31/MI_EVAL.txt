# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance and validated Jonathan's resistance ('A 2 makes good sense'). Affirmations were occasionally brief and formulaic, and missed directly affirming his honesty about fear of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, accurately reflecting overwhelm and the burden of eating changes. Missed a deeper empathic exploration of his self-identified relapse pattern ('gung-ho for a week, then it falls apart and I feel like a failure').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus — every exchange advanced a concrete plan from vague overwhelm to a specific commitment (four mornings, 7am, meter by coffee maker, photos, relapse plan, two-week review). No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plan was genuinely co-constructed: Jonathan generated the step idea, timing, reminder system, and data method himself. Counselor offered technical information only after asking consent and positioned Jonathan's input as shaping the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent autonomy support: 'You're in charge of what you try,' permission-seeking before advising, and reinforcement of a self-compassionate stance ('progress, not perfection'). No coercive or pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out intrinsic motivation via the readiness ruler and linking to Jonathan's own value ('fewer surprises at work'), culminating in genuine curiosity about his numbers. Relied mainly on questions early on; deeper personal reasons for change were never elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Jonathan as the expert, letting him shape nearly all plan details. Some numeric/glucose guidance was delivered didactically before checking his existing knowledge, though permission was requested beforehand.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled gradual cultivation of change talk in a resistant client, moving from 'a huge amount of stuff' to 'I could give it a shot' and 'I'm a little curious.' Commitment and confidence language was never explicitly deepened (e.g., confidence ruler).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently acknowledged sustain talk ('I like what I like,' 'I'm not calling the doctor') without arguing, and reframed non-defensively. Could have used more double-sided reflections and affirmation around his fear-of-failure pattern.
