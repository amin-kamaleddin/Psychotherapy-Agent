# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opens without judgment, affirms Margaret's values and efforts, and normalizes slips. However, warmth is somewhat formulaic and the closing feels checklist-like rather than deeply person-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate double-sided reflections capture Margaret's ambivalence between her social routine and her independence well. Reflections often serve as transitions to advice rather than deepening emotional understanding of loneliness or fear of losing her social world.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol intake and fall/medication risk throughout, with no topic drift. Every exchange ties back to building a concrete change plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language and genuinely incorporates Margaret's edits into the plan and son's text. Several turns are counselor-driven information dumps offering pre-formed options rather than inviting her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports autonomy with choices at every step and the 'experiment, not forever' framing; never pressures abstinence. Margaret's closing gratitude ('Thank you for not doing that') reflects this.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation via confidence rulers and 'which feels doable' prompts, but motivation remains anchored to external factors (son, screening data, counselor-supplied options). Missed opportunities to elicit intrinsic reasons, e.g., why independence matters to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks permission before advising and co-develops the plan and son's text with Margaret's edits. Some exchanges tip toward the expert role with advice-heavy lists; crediting Margaret's own expertise would strengthen this.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes change talk with open questions and confidence rulers, drawing out specific commitments and raising confidence from six to seven. Missed chances to deepen change talk by exploring what success would look like or why cutting back matters beyond her son.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections pair sustain talk with change-relevant values, and the 'experiment' framing softens resistance effectively. Repeated option lists occasionally invite pushback rather than actively pivoting toward Margaret's own change reasons.
