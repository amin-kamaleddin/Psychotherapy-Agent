# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout with no labeling or criticism of Amina's phone use. Affirmations like 'nice choice,' 'solid, realistic plan,' and normalizing slips ('one night won't erase progress') reinforce an accepting tone even when the client is ambivalent or playfully resistant.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections early on (e.g., the 'stress-scroll loop') demonstrate good perspective-taking on Amina's ambivalence and shame. However, deeper emotional content—fear of disappointing her parents and the all-or-nothing failure pattern—is acknowledged but not fully explored before pivoting to planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, consistent focus on late-night phone use and progresses logically from exploring ambivalence through scaling, trigger identification, a concrete experiment, if-then plans, and follow-up arrangements. Each counselor turn advances the change process without topic drift, ending with a time-bound plan and morning check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely two-way: options are offered as experiments, Amina's reactions are invited, and her modifications (moving the charger, telling the roommate, keeping it simple) are integrated. Her comment that she 'needed someone to actually make me pick a plan' reflects a plan she genuinely owns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly reinforces choice and ownership ('What would you like to be different, if anything?', 'Which of those feels most doable?') and accepts Amina's refusal to commit to a boring audiobook without pressure. Slips are framed as normal, and her self-set commitment level of 6/10 is honored rather than pushed higher.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from the client—her own reasons, solutions, self-motivator (roommate teasing), and success imagery—supported by effective scaling and confidence-raising prompts. A point is lost because the counselor supplies several solution options (airplane mode tricks, breathing exercises, app limits) before exhausting Amina's own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amina's contributions are central—she selects the audiobook, chapter choice, 9am check-in, and roommate disclosure, all client-generated. However, the counselor generates most of the menu of options, leaning slightly on expert content rather than consistently drawing solutions from her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked through open questions, importance/confidence scaling, and commitment questions, producing strong client statements ('I'd actually sleep before 3am,' 'Maybe the audiobook one? I could try that tonight') and a commitment rating of 6. Asking what Amina will notice tomorrow generates vivid positive projections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoids dwelling on sustain talk, using double-sided reflections and re-anchoring on the small experiment and self-compassion rather than arguing. A point is deducted because sustain talk is sometimes acknowledged neutrally rather than actively redirected with strategic reframing.
