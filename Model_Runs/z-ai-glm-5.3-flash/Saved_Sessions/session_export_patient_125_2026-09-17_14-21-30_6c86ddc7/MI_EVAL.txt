# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental, respectful. Some felt formulaic or mechanical, especially the repetitive safety checks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Decent early reflections but sparse. Missed opportunities to explore deeper feelings like fear of failure or family pain.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on concrete, actionable next steps. Smooth transitions and maintained momentum throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient helped refine, but counselor originated most content. More eliciting of patient's own ideas would strengthen true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice-offering, no pressure. However, options were counselor-generated rather than open exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient volunteered good motivators (family, release, trigger), but this emerged spontaneously rather than through skillful evocative questioning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor leaned expert-driven with scripts and plans. Genuine partnership moments existed but overall felt like coaching rather than power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Capitalized well on patient's own motivation. Would deepen by reflecting and exploring change talk further rather than moving to structure.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing blame and pivoted to action. Lacked double-sided reflections acknowledging and redirecting sustain talk strategically.
