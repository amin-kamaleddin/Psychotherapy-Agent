# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, with validating statements and honoring the patient's request to defer the substance discussion. Affirmations were somewhat generic, and the identity-level struggle (fear of losing his community) was not warmly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, but the clinician shifted into checklist-driven problem-solving mode. Vulnerable disclosures about shame and fear of loneliness were not met with empathic reflection of their emotional weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on the mutually agreed target of rent and financial stability, with concrete steps and a structured recap. Slight deduction because deferring the substance discussion left the core referral concern almost unaddressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options were offered, but the psychiatrist supplied most of the ideas while the patient's own problem-solving was rarely elicited. The exchange resembled guided selection rather than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions were repeatedly framed as the patient's, and his preference to defer the substance topic was explicitly agreed to. Deduction because the plan itself was largely clinician-generated advice, somewhat undercutting full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician relied on providing solutions and menus rather than drawing out the patient's motivations, values, and reasons for change. No evocative questions, discrepancy exploration, or confidence/importance discussion occurred.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician invited the patient's choices, signaling some power sharing, but frequently slipped into an expert role by prescribing the full plan. The patient mostly selected from the psychiatrist's menu rather than shaping direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient spontaneously offered change talk, but the psychiatrist did little to actively evoke or deepen it. No open questions about reasons for change, scaling, or exploration of a changed life; change work was almost entirely logistical.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with neutral acceptance rather than reinforcement, which was appropriate, but no strategic softening occurred. No double-sided reflections or reframing of statements like 'if I stop, I'm basically choosing to be alone.'
