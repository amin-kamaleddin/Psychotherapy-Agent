# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and nonjudgment: honored the no-lectures request, affirmed her efforts, and framed slips compassionately as data. Slightly formulaic warmth and repetitive summaries diluted the individualized quality.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections showed good perspective-taking, but reflections were brief and quickly pivoted to advice. Emotionally significant content (son's questions, fear of failure, avoidance of secondhand smoke info) received only surface acknowledgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed anchored to smoking and stress, moving to a concrete plan with a defined one-week follow-up and no drift. Direction was occasionally overly rapid, jumping to prescriptive steps before exploring motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative elements existed (choice of focus, option menus, negotiated check-in), but the counselor repeatedly offered pre-packaged solutions for Elena to pick from. Elena shaped specifics while the plan's architecture remained counselor-driven, leaving power-sharing partial.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently honored through permission-seeking, acceptance of her limits, option menus, and respect for informal tracking. Deduction for directive, prescriptive advice lists delivered before Elena articulated her own ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation came mostly from counselor-provided options rather than elicited from Elena, with few open questions about her own reasons or confidence. Her spontaneous change talk about her son was met with prescribed tools instead of deepening evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor periodically sought Elena's input and respected her decisions, but much of the session followed a pattern of counselor-generated solution lists. The plan was primarily counselor-authored, with Elena's expertise acknowledged only implicitly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena generated meaningful change talk and the counselor supported it with low-pressure option menus and choice questions. However, the counselor did not deepen it with open questions about her reasons or use her son-focused statements as a motivation springboard.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing or dwelling on Elena's minimizations and resistance, reflecting ambivalence neutrally and redirecting toward her own willingness. Double-sided reflections linking sustain talk to her motivations were absent, and redirections were often behavioral prescriptions.
