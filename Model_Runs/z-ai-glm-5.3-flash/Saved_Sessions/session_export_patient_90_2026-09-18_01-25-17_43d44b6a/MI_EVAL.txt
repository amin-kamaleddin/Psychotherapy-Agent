# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist fully accepted the patient's autonomy, explicitly positioning him as the expert and never pushing an agenda, even reframing the dog as something to protect rather than a problem. All sustain talk was received without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently reflected the patient's feelings, including overwhelm, financial stress, and love for his wife and dog. Reflections were accurate and affirming, though occasionally slightly more celebratory than purely reflective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintained a clear, purposeful focus on asthma management while remaining flexible to the patient's concerns. Each exchange moved meaningfully toward a concrete, patient-generated plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly framed the work as joint ('let's figure this out together, your way') and elicited the patient's own solutions rather than offering advice. The final plan was entirely shaped by the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's ownership was continually emphasized ('that's yours,' 'you're taking charge, not me'), and choices were offered about how to structure the check-in. The clinician reinforced that the plan belonged to the patient, not the doctor.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions drew out the patient's own motivations — breathing easily, his wife's sleep, walks with his aging dog — and his own solutions like the travel inhaler and coffee-morning routine. Change talk emerged naturally and abundantly.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The tone throughout was egalitarian and supportive, with the clinician following the patient's lead and affirming his competence. The patient explicitly noted the plan was his own and nobody handed it to him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicited and reinforced change talk through evocative questions about the desired future ('what will that be like when this works?') and reflective summaries that amplified the patient's own reasons for change. Commitment language strengthened progressively toward a concrete plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk about cost, disrupted routines, and fear of overwhelming lifestyle changes was met with acceptance and gentle reframing rather than argument. The clinician used double-sided reflections to pair ambivalence with the patient's own change reasons.
