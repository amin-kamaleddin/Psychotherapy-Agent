# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect maintained and never judgmental, but warmth is sparse and formulaic. Missed opportunities to affirm Mateo's persistence despite pain and validate his discouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal — one empathic opening followed by questionnaires and planning. Mateo's emotional disclosures (hating his wife hauling mail, feeling nothing helps, being overloaded) are never reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tightly focused on the target behavior with a concrete, measurable goal (5-minute mailbox walk) and clear follow-up planning. Weakness: focus maintained through repetitive instruction recaps rather than collaborative refinement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Largely expert-driven — counselor sets dose, progression, and logging format with minimal negotiation. Mateo's suggestions (fridge calendar, keep it simple) are absorbed but not truly co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive elements: initial three-option menu, offering alternatives to notebooks, flexible cane use. However, the bulk of the session is prescriptive instruction without emphasizing Mateo's ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation comes almost entirely from the counselor; no questions about what matters to Mateo or his confidence. His own change talk (independence from wife, 'beats sitting around') emerges despite — not because of — the interviewer's technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor positions himself as the expert designing the regimen. After Mateo chose pacing, the session became counselor-directed prescriptions delivered with little input from the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Mateo spontaneously offers meaningful change talk ('It'd be nice to do that one small thing myself again,' 'This I can actually do'), but the counselor never reflects, reinforces, or deepens any of it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('not sure anything helps much,' 'not expecting miracles') is not amplified — a strength. However, it is also ignored rather than met with double-sided reflections connecting it to his desire for independence.
