# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates unconditional positive regard ('That takes real courage,' 'you won't be carrying it alone'), with no judgment even when discussing failed attempts. Warmth feels genuine and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both ambivalence and underlying feelings (shame, fear of disappointing her daughter), notably the 'five minutes of peace' reflection. The patient explicitly confirms feeling understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation from start to finish, with each exchange building momentum toward a concrete plan and relapse-prevention. There is no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely co-constructed, with the counselor actively seeking consent ('Does that feel right to you?') and shaping the plan around the patient's own ideas. Decisions are negotiated rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces client ownership ('What feels like the right first step for you?') and reframes failure as learning. The patient ends with self-directed language: 'I can do that. I think I can.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions consistently draw motivation from within the client, eliciting her own reasons for change (her daughter, the tea ritual). The patient notes the contrast: 'Nobody's asked me that before.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located within the client ('You know your rhythms better than I do'), and every plan element originated from her. This is exemplary power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked, reflected, and deepened throughout, culminating in rich commitment language ('I'll tell her. Saturday,' 'I'm committed'). The counselor reinforces this effectively.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally handled well through double-sided reflections that honor ambivalence without amplifying it. The question exploring past barriers ('what got in the way') briefly invited sustain talk, though it was framed constructively — keeping this from a 5.
