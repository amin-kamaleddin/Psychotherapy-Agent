# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently warm, non-judgmental tone with affirmations and normalization of setbacks ('treat it as information, not failure'). Affirmations become formulaic and repetitive, with little individualized acknowledgment of her deeper body-image pain.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate early reflections but largely jumps from emotional disclosures ('I feel gross,' 'uncomfortable in my own skin') straight to goal-setting and logistics. Her core emotional concerns—body image distress and fear of quitting—are never deeply reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior of initiating exercise from the first turn through the end. The session moves efficiently from goal to concrete plan, scheduled days, prep steps, and a structured check-in with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The plan is fundamentally clinician-designed, with the counselor supplying the routine, structure, tracking system, and metrics. Aisha is consulted on preferences, but her own ideas and hints about past failed attempts go largely unexplored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers a genuine choice point (short daily habit vs. longer sessions) that Aisha uses, but the bulk of the plan is handed to her rather than co-created. Advice is repeatedly given without permission ('Would it be okay if I shared some ideas?').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation resides mainly in the counselor's encouragement ('You've got this') rather than being drawn from Aisha, with almost no open questions eliciting her reasons, values, or confidence. Rich client-generated hopefulness is met with logistics instead of deepening questions—a significant missed opportunity with a preparation-stage client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Assumes a strongly expert-driven stance, delivering prescriptions for the program structure, scheduling, and self-talk scripts in nearly every turn. Aisha's contributions have minimal impact on the plan's design, with no acknowledgment that she is the expert on her own life and exercise history.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha arrives with abundant change talk, but the counselor does little to deliberately evoke or deepen it, relying mostly on information-giving. The non-scale wins reframe lands well, but opportunities to explore her motivations are missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers helpful reframes like the 'just 5 minutes' rule and 'information, not failure' that soften her fear of quitting again. However, her fear of failure and relapse history are never explicitly explored, addressed only with generic coping strategies.
