# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and supportive with affirmations like 'brave step' and 'proud of you,' but affirmations are somewhat generic and formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes some accurate reflections (home feels triggering, a six is a solid start), but the counselor rushes to problem-solving and checklists, missing chances to deeply reflect Marco's discouragement and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, logical focus on diet/weight loss with barriers, a plan, start date, contingency planning, and a check-in. Deducted for narrowing early to logistics while sidelining the core issue of self-doubt.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative elements (permission-seeking, options, co-created check-in questions), but the counselor often shifts into expert-delivery mode with prescriptive advice bundles rather than joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices and obtains permission before advising, keeping Marco in control. Falls short of a 5 due to repetitive prescriptive instructions and no explicit affirmation of Marco's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation largely comes from Marco himself (kids, feeling better); the counselor supplies most reasons and strategies and relies on one readiness scaling question without evoking reasons, ability, or commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks Marco's preferences and incorporates his input into the plan, but the session is structured largely as instruction delivery with bulleted to-do lists, positioning the counselor as the plan architect.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicits a confidence rating and mild change talk ('the walk might work,' 'I'll give it a shot') but never deepens it — missing chances to explore why a 6, what gives hope, or what success means for him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk about relapse and lost hope is labeled 'normal' but never explored, amplified, or resolved; the counselor pivots to contingency logistics instead of working through ambivalence with double-sided reflections.
