### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard with individualized, genuine affirmations ('There's real wisdom in what you just said'). Sustain talk is received with equal warmth, with no judgment anywhere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including a double-sided reflection mirroring the client's ambivalence and reflections capturing unspoken emotion and identity-level meaning. Reflections repeatedly land—the client builds on each one.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, progressive focus on seeking medical evaluation throughout, with each exchange moving the arc toward a specific, time-bound commitment. Tangents are quickly connected back to the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Functions between equal partners; the plan is fully co-constructed from the client's own material with no prescriptions. The client's answers visibly alter the session's course.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions the client as expert ('You're the expert on your life') and offers options as choices rather than imposing advice. The client's insights reflect internalized ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited entirely from within the client, drawing out values-based reasons like modeling courage for her daughter. The session visibly builds momentum from overwhelm to agency.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All substantive ideas originate from the client, with the psychiatrist reflecting and asking questions that let the client's expertise shape the plan. Client contributions demonstrably drive the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes commitment language through open questions and an expertly used confidence ruler anchored to past progress. The client's own words ('it doesn't feel as impossible as it did an hour ago') evidence effective evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk masterfully—acknowledging avoidance within double-sided reflections and reframing practical fears as solvable problems. The excuses-voice is effectively pivoted, which the client adopts ('maybe I just go scared').
