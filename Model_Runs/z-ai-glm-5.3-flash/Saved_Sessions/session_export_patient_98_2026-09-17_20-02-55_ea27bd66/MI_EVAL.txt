# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Consistent warm, unconditional positive regard throughout (e.g., "That tiredness deserves real acknowledgment," "no judgment either way"). Normalizes discouragement, honors both sides of ambivalence, closes with genuine affirmation. No judgment, labeling, or disapproval anywhere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, deep reflective listening. Captures both emotional and physical pain ("real pain in that gap between what you want back and what your body lets you do"). Mirrors client's own language ("mailbox moment," "going backwards") and validates his spontaneous insight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on increasing activity/rehab engagement despite pain. Every exchange ties back to the target behavior. Elicits a concrete action step with a built-in evaluation plan ("let's check in next week... adjust from there") without being pushy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Genuine equal partnership. Explicit team framing ("thinking as a team here," "we'll figure it out together"). Client's own ideas (fridge calendar, wife's reactions as evaluation criteria) drive the intervention; counselor input never overrides client judgment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces ownership and choice ("You're the one who'll be living with it"). Plan is fully client-generated; stopping is endorsed as a legitimate option. Client explicitly notes the contrast with expected directive advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within the client via evocative questions (hopes for rehab, past coping, "mailbox moment"). Highlights client's own wisdom and self-efficacy. Motivations—wife, grandkids, resilience—all elicited, never supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Actively positions client as the expert ("You know yourself better than anyone here"). Calendar plan originates entirely from the client; counselor affirms that ownership repeatedly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Change language grows from "I show up" to "I don't want to quit" to "I'm willing to give it the week." Counselor explicitly reinforces change talk and evokes self-generated solutions; commitment language deepens through session end ("socks and all").

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk acknowledged without reinforcement. Doubts met with double-sided reflections that pivot toward strengths and reasons for change. Validating client's self-knowledge ("You know yourself better than anyone") redirects toward problem-solving rather than dwelling on barriers.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **5.0/5**
