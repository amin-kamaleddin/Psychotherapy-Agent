# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, thanked the client for honesty, and normalized slips as information rather than failure. Affirmations were brief and formulaic, and safety disclaimers occasionally interrupted rapport-building moments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some accurate reflections but rarely explored the client's emotional world, missing rich material like his Friday-night loneliness and surprise at his automatic drinking habit. Reflections were mostly one-sided restatements rather than complex reflections of feeling or meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on alcohol reduction throughout, moving efficiently from assessment to a concrete goal, Friday plan, and scheduled follow-up with no topic drift. This strong focus came somewhat at the expense of deeper motivation exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing occurred at key decision points, but the session often resembled delivery of a treatment package with an expert-led agenda. The counselor could have asked for the client's own ideas before supplying strategies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly offered choices multiple times and supported client ownership over the goal, plan, and log format without pressure. Advice lists were delivered without explicit permission, and the counselor didn't affirm that the decision about whether drinking is a 'problem' rests with the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence scaling question was a good evocative technique and the client generated his own reasons (sleep, curiosity), but the counselor never deepened this motivation with open questions about meaning. Motivation was emerging but not systematically developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked some collaborative questions, but the session followed an expert-driven pattern with extensive prescriptive content and limited exploration of the client's own ideas. The client shaped some details, yet the overall frame positioned the counselor as protocol provider.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evoked change talk including a confidence rating, commitment to the week, and intrinsic motivation, and the choice between goals effectively produced a client-selected goal. Missed classic MI strategies like asking 'why a 5 and not a 3?' and never asked the client to elaborate on why the goal mattered.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it, redirecting toward exploration and data, and reframing slips as information softened fear of failure. A double-sided reflection honoring both sides of the client's ambivalence would have been ideal.
