# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor was consistently polite and supportive, but positive regard was formulaic and information-focused. No specific affirmations of Ingrid's initiative, proactive planning, or honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no reflective listening; the counselor never reflected the client's surprise, time pressure, or her admission she wouldn't have thought twice about starting the new medication. Responses defaulted to safety instructions rather than understanding her perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on the medication interaction matching the client's presenting concern. However, direction was purely informational, and repetitive closing exchanges stalled rather than advanced the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction was one-way information delivery with no shared agenda-setting or invitation for the client's perspective. The counselor's repetitive final messages ignored the client's clear cues to end the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately deferred decisions to the client's prescriber and allowed her to control next steps. Tone was nonetheless directive and precaution-heavy, with no explicit affirmation of her ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No open questions were asked at any point; every counselor turn was declarative advice or safety information. The client's motivation and plans were entirely self-generated, never elicited or explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor assumed an expert role, delivering safety rules with directive phrasing while the client's contributions never substantively shaped the session. Each turn repeated the same closing script rather than building on what she offered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Client volunteered change talk (showing the leaflet to her doctor, writing a medication list), but the counselor only gave generic approval without deepening it. No open questions evoked her reasons, confidence, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor did not reinforce reasons against change, but also failed to skillfully redirect barriers. Repeated time-constraint signals were ignored as the counselor kept adding information instead of converting them into brief, focused engagement.
