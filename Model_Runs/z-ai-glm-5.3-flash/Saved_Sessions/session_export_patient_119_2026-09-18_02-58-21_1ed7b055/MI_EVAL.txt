# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly offers a nonjudgmental stance ('there's no judgment here') and normalizes ambivalence as 'a really human place to be.' Patient concerns, including reluctance about her daughter's role, are accepted without judgment or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflections run throughout, including complex reflections that capture both sides ('her asking stung, and part of you wonders if she could help'). The clinician consistently demonstrates understanding of the patient's feelings of being stretched thin, guilt, and realism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear focus on medication adherence is maintained throughout, with every exchange tied to the patient's values (her kids, her mother's course of illness). Off-focus topics like diet changes are deliberately deferred to preserve a manageable agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly defers to the patient's expertise ('You know your rhythms better than anyone') and invites her to generate her own solutions, which she does (wash bag placement, photo by the coffee maker). The plan is explicitly framed as the patient's own.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient controls pacing ('one step at a time'), and the clinician offers choices rather than directives ('would you rather just take this one step at a time?'). Affirmations emphasize her agency ('That's your own discovery,' 'a plan... it's mine').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions elicit the patient's own motivations and ideas rather than supplying them, drawing out her reasons for change (being there for her kids, avoiding her mother's fate) and her self-generated solutions. The resulting change talk is substantial and patient-sourced.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue works as a joint inquiry, with the clinician exploring alongside rather than advising, and the patient actively shaping the plan and the follow-up agenda. The tone positions both parties as contributors to the solution.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation produces abundant preparatory and mobilizing change talk ('I'd rather she sees me trying,' 'maybe the certainty comes after I start,' 'I'll dig it out tonight'). Questions are carefully sequenced to build commitment talk from the patient's own discoveries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('novelty wears off,' 'Monday chaos wins,' 'back here in a month having done nothing') is met with double-sided reflections and honest acknowledgment rather than argument or reassurance. The clinician rolls with ambivalence, allowing the patient to resolve it herself ('a bigger part wants to walk in here... and say it worked').
