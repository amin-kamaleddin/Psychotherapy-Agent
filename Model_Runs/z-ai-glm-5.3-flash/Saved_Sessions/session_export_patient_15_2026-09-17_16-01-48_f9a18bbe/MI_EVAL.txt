# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing Andrei's cautious stance ('You're doing this to see the facts, and that's smart'). However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic and lack explicit recognition of Andrei as a person beyond his behaviors.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections capture Andrei's ambivalence well (e.g., 'You're torn — worried about your father's stroke risk but feeling fine now'). Reflections are sometimes brief and quickly followed by multiple questions, limiting emotional depth, particularly around his father's stroke.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on hypertension and linked alcohol use from opening question to concrete plan. Excellent progressive momentum toward a specific, measurable 7-day monitoring protocol with timing, chart, follow-up, and safety contingency.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative elements: Andrei chose BP monitoring over cutting drinks, paper over phone, email delivery, and requested a notes column which was incorporated. Scored down because the counselor tends to pre-package options rather than genuinely co-constructing the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Steps are consistently framed as optional ('I'm here to listen and support you in whatever steps you choose'), and Andrei's 'no promises' on drinks was accepted without pressure. Rapid-fire option menus occasionally feel scripted, and ownership of decisions isn't explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is effectively drawn from within the client — the readiness ruler elicited 'I don't want a stroke like my father had,' and past success quitting smoking surfaced his own reasons for change. Deeper, more varied evocation (e.g., exploring his 'maybe the readings surprise me' statement) would raise this score.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Andrei's input is invited and options are offered rather than imposed, but substantial psychoeducational content and multi-question turns leave him in a passive 'sure, okay' role. Little acknowledgment that Andrei holds the expertise on his own life beyond extracting logistical preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of the readiness ruler and confidence scaling elicited meaningful change talk ('maybe that's my reason. Like before with the smoking') and connecting past smoking cessation to present capacity was well done. More consistent deepening of change talk and explicit commitment language would be needed for a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Andrei's minimizations ('That's normal, isn't it?', 'The first one stays') were mostly side-stepped rather than addressed with double-sided reflections. The pragmatic focus on BP monitoring avoided amplifying sustain talk, but strategic reframing opportunities were missed.
