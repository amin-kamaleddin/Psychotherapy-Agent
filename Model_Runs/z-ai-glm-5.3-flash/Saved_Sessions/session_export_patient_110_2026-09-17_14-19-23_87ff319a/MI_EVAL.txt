# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental tone with affirmations of Richard's efforts. However, affirmations were somewhat generic and lacked deeper, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed accurate understanding of Richard's retirement values and normalized his ambivalence, but most responses quickly pivoted to advice-giving and structured plans. The emotional layer of his minimization went unexplored, and reflective listening was sparse relative to the advice dispensed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on cholesterol management throughout, with every exchange advancing the plan toward concrete next steps. No topic drift; a genuine strength of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was unequal, with the counselor positioned as the expert source of answers and scripts while Richard was cast as the implementer. Menu choices replaced genuine joint problem-solving, and Richard's own ideas drove the plan without being built upon.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally framed elements as optional and respected Richard's decisions without pushback, but the overall approach was prescriptive. Offered only superficial yes/no choice within a counselor-directed agenda, with little reinforcement of Richard's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made little effort to draw motivation from Richard; his strongest motivational material was self-generated rather than elicited. Never asked about his own ideas for change, confidence, or the importance of change, treating motivation instead as something to be supplied through education and scripts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Adopted a predominantly expert-driven stance, delivering pre-packaged plans, SMART goals, and fully written scripts. Did not convey that expertise about how change happens resides primarily with Richard.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard generated meaningful change talk largely on his own, which the counselor affirmed but did not actively deepen or evoke. No open questions or scaling were used to deliberately elicit commitment, and the counselor failed to reflect commitment statements back to amplify them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce Richard's minimization and appropriately normalized his ambivalence, but the approach was neutral rather than skillful. No double-sided reflections pairing his doubts with his motivations, and the minimization was largely passed over rather than redirected toward his values.
