# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, affirming the client's skepticism and plan. Client explicitly noted feeling treated like an adult rather than a problem, reflecting genuine acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Made good efforts to understand the client's conflicted feelings and values, including family norms and fairness concerns. Reflections were often brief and quickly paired with advice, limiting deeper exploration of the emotional weight of cultural identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in pregnancy from opening question to concrete behavioral plan. The session progressed logically from exploration to a rehearsed, specific commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission before educating, offered menus of options, and co-constructed the plan with the client's ideas. At times provided fairly complete solution packages before the client could generate her own ideas, making some exchanges feel advisor-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced choice with low-pressure options and decision-framing well-matched to this autonomy-driven client. The client's shift to 'this is my call' demonstrates successfully client-owned change, and slips were normalized without blame.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used rulers, open questions, and elicitation of the client's own solutions to draw out client-owned motivations and commitment language. Often followed evocation with immediate advice rather than letting the client's reasons and ideas fully develop.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned the client's expertise as central, incorporating her ideas (husband involvement, brochure, slip plan) into the outcome. Occasionally moved quickly to solutions and materials, mildly taking the driver's seat.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of importance/confidence rulers elicited strong change talk ('eight months is nothing compared to my kid's whole life'). Missed opportunities to deepen change talk, e.g., asking 'Why a 5 and not lower?' after her confidence rating instead of jumping to options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged skepticism without arguing, then provided brief factual information and pivoted back to exploration. The information-giving was somewhat lecture-adjacent; a double-sided reflection on her ambivalence would have strengthened the pivot toward change.
