# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations, avoiding labeling when Alexei minimized. However, acceptance felt somewhat transactional, with premature reassurance rather than unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was limited, with responses defaulting to information and advice instead of reflecting feelings. Emotions like fear of his mother and frustration were met with checklists rather than empathy, and complex reflections were absent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session focused on alcohol use and its connection to goals, moving coherently from risks to concrete skills. Slight drift early on when the counselor offered generic advice menus before soccer gave the session clear direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor often took an expert stance, generating lists and polishing the client's ideas rather than building on them. Power tilted toward the counselor as instructor despite a genuinely collaborative role-play.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some choices (role-play vs. drafting a text, letting Alexei pick his message), but the session was dominated by prescriptive advice that subtly controlled. Never explicitly affirmed Alexei's authority over his own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was mostly self-generated by Alexei rather than drawn out through evocative questions or skillful reflection. Key change talk moments (scholarships, addiction fears) were missed opportunities to deepen commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Frequent slips into expert advice-giving replaced power-sharing, with Alexei's own contributions carrying the session while the counselor responded with more options rather than crediting his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong change talk emerged but was largely self-generated; the college policy point was a good evocative move, yet commitment language wasn't deepened after 'that kind of locked it in for me.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk neutrally without amplifying it, briefly validating minimization. However, no double-sided reflections were used to pivot back to goals or resolve ambivalence.
