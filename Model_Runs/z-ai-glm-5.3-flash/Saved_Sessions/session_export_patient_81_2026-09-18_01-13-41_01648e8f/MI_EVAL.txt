# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard throughout — no judgment of Lukas's use or social scene, and explicit validation of his insights. The closing affirms acceptance even in anticipation of possible failure ('being straight about it is exactly the kind of honest testing').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are consistently accurate and go beneath the surface, e.g., distinguishing weeknight 'numbing out' from weekend 'belonging' and reflecting the untested belief that sober-you might not be enough. The clinician tracks and mirrors Lukas's own language throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on meth use and its consequences and progresses logically from consequences to pattern to smallest change to barriers to a concrete plan and check-in. No aimless drift; even exploratory questions about belonging serve the change process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructed — the clinician asks what Lukas wants to focus on at intake and every plan step emerges from his own ideas. The client's closing remark ('I walked in thinking you'd lecture me... instead we just sorted out a climbing trip') is direct evidence of experienced collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is explicitly affirmed from the outset ('which is yours to decide, not theirs or mine'), and the clinician accepts Lukas's pacing ('weekends untouched for now') with no directives or pressure. The closing emphasizes the plan was built on his terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within rather than supplied; the pivotal question about using vs. belonging elicits the client's core insight. Follow-ups keep Lukas generating his own reasons, strategies, and commitment language through to a self-initiated plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located in the client ('You know that world better than I do'), and every plan element — texting Theo, money aside, one-thing-at-a-time — originates from Lukas. The clinician frames this as 'change shaped on your terms.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked ('What would look different?', 'What would make it a fair test?'), producing substantial client commitment language ('I'd hate letting Theo down,' 'Thursday I can do'). The clinician reinforces it with affirmations rather than supplying it herself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without amplification and typically pivoted toward exploration or change. However, 'weekends untouched for now' is accepted without strategic reframing, and a few openings (e.g., the 'standing outside the party looking in' fear) were met with a question rather than a double-sided reflection.
