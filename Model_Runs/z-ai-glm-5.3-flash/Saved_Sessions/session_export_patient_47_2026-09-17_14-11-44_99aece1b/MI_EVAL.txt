# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing Evelyn's enjoyment of wine and closing without judgment. However, affirmations are generic rather than personalized, and the rapid-fire risk checklist feels clinical.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections, such as acknowledging the loneliness and validating Evelyn's defensiveness about the fall. Reflections are sparse overall; the counselor moves to advice rather than exploring her emotional experience of loneliness or family concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use from the outset, tying the fall and family concern to drinking. Steered toward a concrete one-week tracking and reduction plan with follow-up, with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and an invitation to make a plan together, but the substance of the plan was unilaterally determined. Evelyn's input was limited to choosing her alcohol-free days.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy support present, such as asking permission and inviting Evelyn to choose her alcohol-free days. The overall stance remains prescriptive, with the plan's components dictated rather than co-developed and little affirmation of her ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied primarily on education, risk information, and direct advice rather than drawing out Evelyn's own motivations. Her motivation emerged despite, not because of, the counselor's technique, with no open questions about values, hopes, or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role with an interrogation-style checklist and unilateral prescriptive instructions. Evelyn is rarely asked what she thinks would work, and her contributions had limited influence on the session structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evelyn produced genuine change talk about easing off, partly elicited by the fall-risk question. However, the counselor never deepened or reinforced these statements, passing them over in favor of prescribing the plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with Evelyn's minimization and did not reinforce sustain talk directly. But no strategic double-sided reflections were used, and repeated listing of risks kept attention on problems rather than her reasons for change.
