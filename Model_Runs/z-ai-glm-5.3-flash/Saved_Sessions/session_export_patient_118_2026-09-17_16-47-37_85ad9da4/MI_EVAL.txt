# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicated consistent respect and unconditionally honored Seamus's boundaries (no smoking talk, no lectures, comfort preferences). Warmth was expressed mainly through accommodation rather than explicit affirmations of his strengths, such as his honesty and decades of effort.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early summaries accurately captured his situation, but reflections were repetitive ('I hear you') and focused on facts rather than feelings. Emotional core issues—embarrassment about failing, wife's pressure, weariness—went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained dual focus on dental care and smoking cessation with directional intent via the readiness ruler. However, the smoking thread was dropped once Seamus redirected, and multiple turns drifted into appointment logistics without a closing summary tying his values to future change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scheduling, comfort preferences, and treatment decisions were genuinely co-negotiated. The session agenda itself was set unilaterally, however, without checking whether Seamus was open to smoking questions before proceeding.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear strength: permission was requested before offering ideas, choices were framed as Seamus's, and his refusal was fully respected without pressure. Mild deduction for repeating the pre-visit smoking instruction after he had already agreed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort was made to draw motivation from the client; the session's best change talk emerged spontaneously and was never capitalized on. The counselor supplied option lists instead of eliciting Seamus's own ideas, values, and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Seamus's preferences were treated as decisive for scheduling and comfort, but the content remained clinician-driven, with the option menu delivered as a list. His own expertise and theories about what might work were not explicitly invited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler and open questions successfully elicited change talk, including client-generated links between whiter teeth and quitting. These moments were never deepened or amplified (e.g., 'What would make that a 5 instead of a 3?').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** No persuasion battles occurred; sustain talk was accepted without argument, and the righting reflex was effectively avoided with a resistant client. Deduction because redirection was passive acceptance rather than strategically pivoting 'teeth first' into a motivational foothold.
