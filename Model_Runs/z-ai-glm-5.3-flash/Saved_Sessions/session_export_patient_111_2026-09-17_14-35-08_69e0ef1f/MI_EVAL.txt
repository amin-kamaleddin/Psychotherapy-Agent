# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, normalizing Richard's anger and never pushing or labeling. Fell short of a 5 because affirmations of Richard himself were sparse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening, capturing both the meaning of the smoking routine and the pain of his wife's worry. Missed opportunities to deepen reflection on the grief and fear beneath his 'maybe she's got a point' moment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept clear focus on smoking and its relational context with no topic drift, progressing logically to a concrete trial and rehearsal. Some momentum channeled into marital scripts rather than sustained toward the smoking behavior itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission, checked in, and incorporated Richard's edits into the plan, showing genuine two-way exchange. The initial 'sacred 15' proposal originated from the counselor, making the power balance slightly uneven at a key moment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced choice from start to finish, honoring Richard's 'not quitting today' boundary and framing the plan as a trial he controls. Richard's closing remark about leaving with a plan confirms ownership stayed with him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions about what cigarettes meant successfully evoked Richard's values and his own motivation. However, the counselor relied heavily on supplying scripts and solutions rather than drawing reasons and plans from Richard himself, with no importance/confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly signaled Richard's expertise and choice, allowing his contributions to shape the session. Later advice-giving leaned expert-driven rather than co-authoring the plan with Richard.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged substantially but was evoked largely by the patient rather than deliberately deepened. Missed chances to affirm and amplify key statements like 'maybe she's got a point' and 'I'd think about it,' and used no scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rolled with resistance skillfully — never arguing or reinforcing sustain talk, instead reflecting smoking's function and respecting Richard's stated boundaries. Kept focus partly on the wife's worry rather than consistently pivoting toward Richard's own reasons for change.
