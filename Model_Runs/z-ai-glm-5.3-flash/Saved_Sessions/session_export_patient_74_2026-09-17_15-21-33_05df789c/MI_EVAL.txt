# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with frequent affirmations ('well done for considering it'), but affirmations were somewhat formulaic and generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Linda's ambivalence (pleasure vs. worry about her kids), but the 'You're torn' reflection was repeated verbatim and the emotional core (daughter's disappointed look) wasn't explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking throughout, moving from exploring ambivalence to a concrete, client-generated plan without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative with menus and co-constructed decisions, though rapid-fire checklists of steps occasionally shifted toward a directive, coach-like delivery.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support — repeatedly reinforced Linda's choice ('I'm here to explore options only if and when you want to') and honored her ambivalence ('no promises').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Linda's own motivations (her daughter) via open questions and a confidence ruler, but supplying lists of substitutes and coping strategies reduced reliance on client-generated content.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda's ideas shaped the plan (tea, pack in the car, telling her daughter), but the counselor supplied most of the concrete option menu rather than drawing more from her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and the confidence ruler produced genuine change talk ('maybe I could try it for a few days'), but emerging change talk wasn't always deepened or reflected back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and used some double-sided reflections, but lacked consistent pivoting to motivations, reframing of doubts, and one question ('What might get in the way?') arguably amplified barriers.
