# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, affirming Rafael's choices without judgment ('There's real wisdom in what you said'). Even his admission of choosing two days over three is met with affirmation rather than pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate and progressively deeper, capturing both content and emotional meaning (e.g., his wife noticing his energy touching 'something deeper than fitness'). Key reflections distill Rafael's rambling into crisp insights.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, purposeful focus on returning to exercise, strategically building from insight to a complete plan (two days, bag strategy, success markers) without topic drift. Direction feels client-paced rather than forced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is fully collaborative, framed as joint work ('help us figure out what might actually work'). Rafael's ideas formed the entire plan, and he explicitly confirmed the power-sharing ('figuring out my own stuff instead of being told what to do').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Rafael's ownership ('which one feels like something you could actually see yourself doing') and frames non-adherence as data rather than failure. Never pushes for more or prescribes solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are almost entirely evocative, drawing out Rafael's own reasons (wife's feedback, self-respect from follow-through, gym identity) rather than supplying them. His closing insight shows motivation genuinely emerged from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within the client ('You know your life best,' 'You did the connecting today, not me'). Rafael's contributions substantially shaped the plan and he thanked the counselor for not dictating.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and amplified, with commitment language growing steadily ('I'll show up,' 'the bag's going out Monday night') culminating in concrete commitment. Specific evocations targeted obstacles and success markers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers are generally reframed as solvable logistics and pivoted toward change. However, the opening reflection slightly amplified sustain talk by restating barriers ('demands of work and home life keep pulling you away').
