# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard — "There's no right answer — your perspective matters most in this room." Validated Sofia's sense that the referral was unfair without judgment or disapproval of her use; remained warm and non-reactive even when she minimized ("It's just weed").

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, consistent reflective listening throughout. Tracked emotional nuances — ambivalence, family pressure, missing her sister — e.g., "You didn't pick this, your mom's worry did"; "You're torn, and that makes total sense." Accurately captured the anxiety loop ("the calm never quite lasts") and checked understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior from the opening question to the final exchange. Every reflection and question tied back to the weed/stress loop; session progressed logically from ambivalence to a concrete behavioral experiment with no aimless wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — "I'd like us to figure out together what, if anything, would be useful." Agenda co-created from the first turn; counselor incorporated Sofia's ideas (sister, money-saving framing) and shared power through open questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced: "No promises past that" accepted without pressure; plan framed as "this experiment of yours." No pushing abstinence or imposing advice; permission-style questions kept Sofia in control of the pace of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within — "is there anything that bugs you?", "which one would feel like the bigger win to you?" Sofia articulated her own reasons (money, legal risk, family expectations, anxiety relief) and her own discrepancy with virtually no counselor-supplied arguments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sofia positioned as the expert: "You know yourself best" used repeatedly. The run-with-sister plan, "tell her tonight" commitment strategy, and success markers all came from Sofia, with the counselor amplifying rather than directing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation eliciting Sofia's own doubts, then deepening change talk through strategic questions ("what would help you follow through?"). Sofia generated her own plan, markers, and commitment language ("I guess I'll try it. One run.") — client-authored motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk acknowledged then pivoted toward change without confrontation. Sofia's "not a big deal" framing was reflected without reinforcement; the counselor elicited the half-working comparison leading Sofia to conclude "smoking doesn't fully quiet it either." Sustain talk never derailed the session.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **5.0/5**

*Summary (for supervisor review):* Exemplary MI session — trust established immediately by validating Sofia's sense of unfairness; concerns (money, anxiety loop, family/legal risk) evoked rather than supplied; sustain talk pivoted skillfully; session ended with a self-generated, concrete plan and Sofia's own success criteria. Minor growth area: occasional double-barreled questions (e.g., "what would help you follow through? You know yourself best — what's worked before?") — allowing more space for each part could add breathing room.
