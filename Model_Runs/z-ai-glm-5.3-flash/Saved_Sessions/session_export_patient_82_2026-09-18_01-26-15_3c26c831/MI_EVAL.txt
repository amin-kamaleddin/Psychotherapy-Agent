# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, treating the patient's ambivalence and peer concerns as valid rather than dismissing them. The response to the patient's fear of being seen as preachy is met with warmth and zero judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both surface content and deeper feelings, such as the tension between 'relief' and 'hiding' after using the designated driver strategy. Statements like 'caring what your friends think at seventeen... is about as real as it gets' demonstrate strong emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is skillfully steered toward exploring the patient's own motivations and concrete steps, without pushing an agenda. Each question builds progressively toward the patient's self-generated plan and timeline.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions the patient as the expert on his own life, repeatedly asking 'what do you think' rather than dispensing advice. The patient explicitly recognizes this, noting 'I talked myself into it.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist emphasizes the patient's ownership throughout, reinforcing that the change 'is yours' and contrasting this with external lecturing. All ideas, including the designated driver plan and scrimmage approach, originate from the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open-ended questions skillfully draw out the patient's own reasons for change, such as his dislike of feeling 'off-balance' and not being 'sharp.' The memorable self-articulation of wanting to be 'someone who decides, not someone who manages' was evoked rather than supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acts as a thinking partner, reflecting and building on the patient's words without redirecting or taking over. The dialogue feels like a joint exploration, with the psychiatrist following the patient's lead at every turn.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is abundant and clearly elicited by the psychiatrist's questions, moving from vague ambivalence to specific commitment language ('not a trick, not a strategy. Mine'). The psychiatrist then reinforces this language by reflecting it back and asking what made the shift possible.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk, such as worrying the plan is 'tough talk' or fear of the 'reflex moment,' is acknowledged honestly rather than argued against. The psychiatrist could marginally deepen exploration of these doubts, but they are largely transformed into contingency planning by the patient.
