# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Derrick's frustration. However, warmth is minimal and key affirmations of his honesty, past sobriety, and openness with Marcus were missed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ('one bad night makes sense,' peer pressure), but reflections are brief and shallow, missing key emotional openings around isolation and the breakup.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on preventing another DUI and regaining the license, with concrete steps and a scheduled follow-up. Slightly narrow—underlying drinking and isolation go unaddressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite menu-style questions, the counselor generates nearly all content (if/then plans, refusal lines, texts), creating a teacher-student dynamic rather than true partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers genuine choices (plan options, check-in decision) and doesn't pressure quitting, but pervasive prescriptive checklists leave only minor logistical decisions to the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies on providing information and scripts rather than eliciting Derrick's own motivations; no open questions about importance, confidence, or values, so his reasons for change go undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role with prescriptive advice, bulleted checklists, and scripted texts, treating Derrick more as a plan recipient than an active partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Derrick spontaneously offered rich change talk (license, 'done with driving drunk'), but the counselor rarely reflected, reinforced, or deepened it, pivoting instead to checklists.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('just beer,' 'bad luck') passes largely unaddressed—no double-sided reflections, reframing, or effort to resolve ambivalence about drinking versus friendships.
