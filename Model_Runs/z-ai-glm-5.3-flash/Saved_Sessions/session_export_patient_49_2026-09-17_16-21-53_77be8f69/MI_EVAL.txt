# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and non-judgmental, normalizing Hana's fears (e.g., 'not failure; it's useful data'). Warmth feels somewhat procedural/checklist-driven, limiting depth of positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections early on captured her ambivalence well, but reflective listening faded as the session became logistical. Emotional content like smoking being her break routine and her 'weirdly okay' feeling went largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary focus throughout — every exchange tied to the smoking goal with a concrete, sequenced plan and no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Agenda-setting, check-in format, and next steps were genuinely co-decided, and Hana's edits shaped the plan. However, pre-packaged option lists and templates sometimes shifted content from her ideas to counselor-generated ones.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, explicit reinforcement of Hana's choice ('I can support whichever you choose'), with preferences on format, timing, and wording all honored. Hana's surprise at not being pushed confirms the non-controlling tone.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Most content was counselor-supplied (strategy lists, templates, scripts), with few evocative questions about Hana's own values, hopes, or confidence. Her motivations surfaced but were never explored or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana's input genuinely shaped the session, including her editing of the check-in text. Partnership fell short of a 5 because option lists were pre-packaged rather than drawn primarily from her own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged ('more doable than I expected,' 'I'll try it for a week') but wasn't actively evoked or deepened. Missed chances to reflect her 'weirdly okay' comment or ask what makes the plan feel doable.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about failure and break-routine attachment was normalized and reframed as data rather than reinforced. Responses were neutral reassurance rather than strategic double-sided reflections pivoting toward change.
