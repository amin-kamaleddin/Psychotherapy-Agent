# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and respect with no judgment, using affirmations like 'It says a lot that you're taking your medications seriously' and normalizing her concern. A 5 would require deeper, more individualized expressions of unconditional regard beyond the repeated affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate simple reflections captured Ingrid's stated needs, but deeper emotional reflections were limited. When she expressed fear about the undisclosed two-week gap, the counselor moved to information-giving rather than reflecting her distress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout — after Ingrid clarified her need, the counselor delivered a tightly targeted consultation on the interaction, washout period, warning signs, and a concrete written plan. Every exchange stayed on the target behavior of safe medication use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Collaborative language ('so we figure out the safest next step together') and genuine choices about format and follow-up, crediting Ingrid for building her own plan. However, the session was largely counselor-delivered information, limiting how much her own ideas shaped the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed Ingrid's choice with options like 'a way to reach your doctor today, or going over what to watch for?' and the reminder plan reinforced ownership. It fell short of a 5 because the safety-critical content was necessarily directive with limited explicit emphasis on her ultimate control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and expertise were rarely drawn from within the client; most content came from the counselor. The only successful evocation was the reminder-planning question, while the opening change question fell flat and no questions explored her own reasons or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ingrid's contributions were incorporated and her self-built plan was credited, but much of the session consisted of the counselor delivering expert information. True power-sharing was limited and the collaborative framing felt somewhat formulaic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk efforts were rare and weak; the opening evocative question felt mismatched and was deflected. The best moment was the reminder-planning question eliciting a concrete plan, but there was little systematic elicitation of her own reasons or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoided amplifying time-barrier sustain talk, pivoting efficiently to essential information and concrete next steps. However, redirects were practical accommodations rather than strategic MI softening, and ambivalence about time versus safety wasn't resolved with double-sided reflections.
