# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveyed warmth and unconditional positive regard, even when Nabil was sharp or resistant. Notable moves include welcoming his unedited expression, validating his desire to be taken seriously, and explicitly disavolding labels.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were consistently accurate and deeply attuned, including the double-sided reflection on blowing up vs. swallowing it that Nabil confirmed as 'exactly it.' The counselor named the core dynamic of respect and fairness underlying his anger in a way Nabil said he'd never heard before.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear focus on the target behavior throughout, with every turn building momentum — from validating anger to exploring alternatives, identifying the trigger, and scripting a specific response. The session culminated in a concrete, testable plan with obstacle planning and no aimless drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session proceeded between equal partners, with the counselor explicitly rejecting the expert stance and joining Nabil's plan as a team member. Nabil's ideas shaped every step, and his spontaneous check of understanding ('planning for the wall') reflects genuine two-way dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor reinforced Nabil's ownership throughout, never prescribing solutions and simply amplifying his own 'rough instinct.' They accepted his limited commitment ('that's as far as I'll commit right now') without pushing, respecting his pace and framing change as his decision to test.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open-ended evocative questions ('what would actually work for you') drew out Nabil's own desires, reasons, and plans rather than supplying them. By the end, Nabil had generated his own plan, coping script, and key insight — classic evidence of evocation over persuasion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently located expertise within the client ('you know yourself better than anyone') and credited Nabil when he generated his own insight about the ambush. Nabil's contributions substantially shaped the session direction, and the counselor joined the plan as an explicit partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk, drawing out Nabil's desires ('walk out still feeling like myself,' 'getting too old for feeling like this') and highlighting his shift from 'no plan' to a concrete strategy. Nabil generated nearly all the content himself, including his own phrasing ('firm but not nuclear').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never amplified — the counselor validated the legitimate cause of frustration while pivoting toward what Nabil wanted instead, and reframed his skepticism about 'soft' approaches as useful information. The score falls short of 5 because some responses (e.g., 'Delays happen, vague answers happen') briefly dwelt in the external-blame frame before redirecting.
