# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, including when directly tested about slipping—explicitly promising 'no lecture' and reframing slips as 'information, not failure.' No judgment or disapproval anywhere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, deep reflective listening that captured ambivalence, the control paradox, and underlying emotions like pride and fear of failure. The patient confirmed the empathy landed, feeling like 'a person talking, not a checklist.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on asthma self-management from opening to closing, progressing logically from ambivalence to a concrete two-week plan. Every question and reflection advanced the target behavior without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely equal partnership—the plan, metaphors, and conditions for return all came from the patient, and his direct challenge was welcomed. The interaction reads as two equals negotiating a deal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: the plan was entirely the patient's own, permission was asked for follow-up, and the patient's conditions ('no lectures, no lying') were honored. The patient clearly articulated ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from the patient through evocative questions, eliciting his own solutions, values, and commitment language. Textbook evocation, confirmed by the patient's surprise at being asked what he thinks would work.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the patient as the expert on his own schedule and life, with the plan coming entirely from him. The patient directly confirmed the partnership: 'Nobody does that.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoked strong change talk, including recall of past success, a client-generated plan, and commitment language ('boots, inhaler, and the truth'). Closed by amplifying change talk about what made the conversation different.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally redirected well, with fear of failure reframed into a plan. Minor growth area: some pessimism and 'homework' talk was met with neutral reflections rather than more active double-sided reflections pivoting toward change.
