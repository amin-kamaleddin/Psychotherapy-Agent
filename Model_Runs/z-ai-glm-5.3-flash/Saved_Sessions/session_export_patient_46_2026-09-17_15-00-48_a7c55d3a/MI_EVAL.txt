# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout and Ethan feels safe enough to engage. However, affirmations are generic ('good move practicing,' 'Nice plan') rather than specific and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections capture Ethan's values and ambivalence ('You're torn — you like the looseness and bonding...'). But emotional depth is limited; reflections pivot quickly to advice rather than lingering on the meaning of peer belonging and freedom.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol-related consequences with no topic drift. The session progresses logically from ambivalence to a concrete, testable behavioral experiment with contingency planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative — options are co-selected, the plan reflects Ethan's preferences, and role-play was his idea. But the counselor defaults to advice-menu structure, giving parts of the session a consultant-like feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language throughout ('Whatever you choose is okay'), and the plan is entirely Ethan's own, including his refusal of the drafted text. Mild over-directiveness appears in unsolicited menus and scripted comebacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good evocation via open questions and confidence scaling drew strong change talk, but the counselor frequently supplies reasons and solutions rather than having Ethan elaborate them. Deeper exploration of why freedom matters and what success looks like is missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ethan is treated as the expert, with his contributions genuinely shaping the plan (NA beer, one-liner, Marcus as ally). However, the counselor supplies option menus rather than evoking Ethan's ideas first, slightly undercutting client wisdom.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Targeted questions effectively evoked strong change talk ('tired of waking up feeling like garbage,' 'less drama at home means more freedom'). But moments of change talk are not deepened with elaborative follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with values-based reframes rather than argument, and fears about being 'the guy who can't loosen up' are acknowledged. Residual ambivalence ('not promising anything crazy, like quitting') goes unaddressed, and some reassurance is premature.
