# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental opening and repeated affirmations ("Nice—good job," "You handled that calmly"); risky behavior never moralized. Scored 4 because warmth is somewhat brief/task-focused, with little normalization of peer pressure or acknowledgment of the courage to disclose.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening throughout, showing real understanding of Jamal's social dynamics and reputation concerns. Reflections are occasionally brief and quickly paired with advice or menus, limiting depth, especially around the emotional weight of the "scared" jab.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on refusing alcohol at team parties from start to finish, with clear momentum: assessment → rulers → strategies → rehearsal → backup plans. No topic drift and repeated guidance back to the concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices and asks permission ("Which of those feels doable?" "Want me to play De-shawn?"), and the role-play format is interactive. Held at 4 because collaboration is mostly selecting from counselor-generated options rather than jointly generating ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jamal's choice is repeatedly reinforced—opening with "what would you most like to focus on," menus of strategies, selecting lines and backup plans, no coercive language despite the compliance referral. Falls short of 5 because counselor-supplied scripts constrain his own solutions and his ownership of change is never explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers draw out importance/confidence and Jamal articulates his own reasons, but much of the session is psychoeducational with the counselor supplying the refusal content. Jamal's questions are answered directly rather than turned back ("What do you think you'd do?").

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal's input does shape the session (choosing the humor line, identifying Marcus, requesting harder role-play), but the stance leans expert-driven with prescriptive menus of lines. Strategies originate almost entirely from the counselor rather than inviting Jamal's ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective rulers elicited clear change talk (stamina, regionals, coach would bench me), and role-play rehearsal has Jamal voicing self-generated refusal language; his plan to recruit Marcus is client-initiated. Missed exploring his low confidence rating of 3 (e.g., "what would move you to a 4?").

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (team belonging, "everyone does it," fear of looking soft) is acknowledged without argument and redirected toward refusal strategies. Scored 4 because team loyalty and reputation concerns are never reframed as resources for change, and advice-heavy responses occasionally crowd out the ambivalence.
