# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed the patient's worth as separate from outcomes ('the win is that you asked') and showed unconditional regard even when discussing her past avoidance and embarrassment. Respected her as the expert on her own life and children.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered frequent, accurate reflections of both content and feeling ('I hear real relief in that') and acknowledged the lingering sting of past shame without rushing past it. The patient repeatedly felt heard and built on the reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kept the conversation focused on the concrete goal of making job interviews workable, guiding it step-by-step from vague hopes to a specific, dated plan. Each response moved the agenda forward without imposing the clinician's own goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned herself as a partner throughout ('you leading, me just listening for what sounds like you') and invited joint problem-solving on the text message draft. Solutions were developed with the patient, not delivered to her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized the patient's authority ('You know your schedule and your children better than anyone here') and let her choose the backup person, the wording, the day, and her own definition of success. Choices and pacing remained entirely hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew out the patient's own ideas with open, evocative questions ('What would a solid enough backup plan look like to you?'; 'What would move it up, even by one?'). Every key element of the plan — the neighbor, the text, Tuesday at four — came from the patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a balanced, active partnership with well-timed open questions, reflections, and affirmations. The patient did most of the talking and owned every step of the final plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Used a confidence scaling question and forward-looking prompts to elicit strong change talk: a committed date ('Tuesday'), a self-designed accountability step (the text), and self-affirming resolve ('No more missing rooms I earned'). Change talk was reflected and strengthened each time it emerged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Met avoidance, fear of rejection, and overthinking with reframes rather than confrontation — shrinking the ask to a low-threshold text and separating self-worth from the neighbor's answer ('Even if she says no — at least I wasn't the one who froze'). Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged, then gently transformed.
