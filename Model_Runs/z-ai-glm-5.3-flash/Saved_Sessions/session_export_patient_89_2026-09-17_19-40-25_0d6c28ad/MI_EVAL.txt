# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout with explicit affirmations of Marcus's expertise and strengths. Falls short of 5 because affirmations focus more on the change process than the person, with no affirmation of his persistence through repeated setbacks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is a clear strength, including a complex early reflection capturing both emotion and meaning ('every ounce of you is already spoken for'). Empathy is occasionally formulaic, and the emotional core — the sting of failure and longing for pre-children connection — is reflected but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on physical activity with a logical arc from barriers to ambivalence to values to commitment and a concrete closing plan. Loses a point for circling the same barrier-versus-possibility theme mid-session and leaving behavioral details (frequency, timing) loose.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely equal partnership — questions framed as joint inquiry and the walking/dancing plan originates entirely from Marcus. His description of the plan as 'mine' is direct evidence of successful collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support — choice and ownership repeatedly located with Marcus, with no advice-giving or pressure. The patient's own thanks for 'listening instead of just telling me what to do' confirms the autonomy-supportive climate.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from within Marcus rather than supplied, using meaning-focused questions like 'what would it mean to you to have that feeling back?' This elicited authentic, self-generated motivation rooted in his marriage, kids, and identity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine power sharing, with Marcus positioned as the expert and the plan built entirely from his stated interests. Falls short of 5 because power sharing is occasionally asserted rhetorically ('you know your life better than anyone' repeated) and the psychiatrist somewhat steers toward walking/dancing once surfaced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook change-talk cultivation — open evocative questions reliably produce desire, ability, and commitment talk, and every burst of change talk is reflected and amplified. Culminates in strong mobilizing language ('worst week of my life, I still walk') and concrete commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through double-sided reflections and pivots toward strengths, never arguing with resistance. One reflection risks amplifying catastrophizing ('doing nothing is what's really been killing me') without an immediate strategic redirect.
