# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations throughout, and Daniel's fear of being told to quit entirely was implicitly disproven. However, warmth was at times formulaic, and his slip past his intended limit was never explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic accurate reflections were present, but the session was dominated by advice and checklists rather than reflective listening. Emotionally salient material—the wife's reaction, peer-pressure anxiety, shame—was consistently redirected to risk criteria and next steps instead of reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on alcohol use and progressed logically from exploration to a concrete, actionable plan with no topic drift. Direction was largely counselor-led rather than anchored in Daniel's own stated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaboration existed—preference questions and format adjustments to Daniel's requests—but power-sharing was uneven, with the counselor proposing and Daniel reacting. Key plan elements came from Daniel, yet collaboration remained reactive rather than proactive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine autonomy moments appeared (choice menus, accepting his refusal of abstinence), but prescriptive framing like 'Next steps I recommend' undercut them. The substance of the plan was counselor-authored, and ownership was never explicitly reaffirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew out Daniel's self-efficacy transfer from smoking cessation and his own support ideas, but the dominant mode was providing content. No confidence/importance rulers or open exploration of his deeper motivations were used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role, prescribing limits, six refusal scripts, template fields, and directive checklists. Daniel's contributions were incorporated only after counselor-created content, leaving expertise clearly weighted toward the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was successfully evoked, including the smoking-success transfer and goal commitment. However, the plan was largely counselor-generated, and commitment language was never deepened through confidence/importance scaling or having Daniel summarize his reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided amplifying sustain talk or coercing abstinence, preventing backlash. But there was little active softening—no double-sided reflections, and Daniel's 'Does that count as a problem?' was answered didactically rather than reflecting the ambivalence beneath it.
