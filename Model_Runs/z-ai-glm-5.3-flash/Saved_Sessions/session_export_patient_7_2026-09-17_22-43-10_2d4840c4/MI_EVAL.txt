# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard from the first exchange, with non-defensive responses to testing ('Fair enough - you didn't choose this') and reframing guardedness as adaptive. No disapproval, labeling, or pressure appears anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that captures both surface content and underlying emotion (unfairness, hopelessness, wary openness). The client's responses ('Nobody's asked me that before,' 'Better than what I expected') confirm she feels understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear engagement-phase focus, steering minimal answers back toward understanding the home conflict and building alliance. Direction serves rapport-building more than change momentum, which is developmentally appropriate but not fully directional toward a change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue between equals — confidentiality, pacing, and topic boundaries all come from the client, and the counselor asks 'what would you need to see from me?' Commitments are offered only in response to the client's stated conditions rather than a imposed agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support with explicit statements ('whatever you choose to share, and when, is completely up to you') and a concrete commitment ('Time and no lectures - I can absolutely commit to that'). The counselor never pressures or persuades, even when tested.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out the client's own reasons and conditions for trust rather than lecturing. Stops short of 5 because language about any specific behavioral change (e.g., what she might want different at home) is not yet elicited or deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions the client as the expert ('You're the expert on your own life') and invites her to shape the terms of the relationship. The client's contributions demonstrably steer the session's direction, pacing, and boundaries.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes nascent change talk from a resistant client ('people leaving me alone sometimes,' 'maybe I'd say more'). Not a 5 because deeper development of desire/ability language around a specific change goal was not pursued, though largely appropriate given precontemplation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids amplifying sustain talk, using textbook pivots from hopelessness ('What keeps you showing up?') and brief, strategic reflections. Slightly less than 5 because a couple of sustain statements (e.g., 'I'm not gonna spill everything right away') were left without any gentle redirect.
