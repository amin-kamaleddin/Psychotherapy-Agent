# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains basic respect and acknowledges safety upfront, but warmth is limited with no affirmations of Thomas's strengths or values (fatherhood, work ethic, social connection). Safety disclaimers feel scripted rather than personal, and unconditional positive regard is not clearly communicated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate early reflections (e.g., acknowledging the frustration with a previous doctor nodding off), but empathy is superficial and crowded out by question lists. Misses emotionally meaningful material, including Thomas's subtle admission 'I'm not bouncing back like I used to,' which deserved deeper reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Keeps a general focus on smoking and alcohol, but direction comes through fact-gathering checklists rather than purposeful movement toward change. The smoking concern is dropped without resolution, and the client's closing ambivalence ('probably nothing will be different') is not addressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choices at several points and scales down the plan when Thomas pushes back, but the underlying stance is expert-driven. Thomas merely selects from counselor-generated options with no joint brainstorming or invitation of his own ideas — a 'counselor prescribes, client complies' pattern.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine autonomy statements appear (offering to focus on what feels most useful, offering choice between tips or talking), but the counselor repeatedly gives unsolicited advice after the client signaled it was premature. Autonomy affirmations emphasizing Thomas's ownership of decisions are largely absent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Makes almost no effort to draw out Thomas's own motivations or values; motivation is addressed by supplying risk statistics and ready-made tips. The one client-generated concern (worse mornings) came spontaneously, not from evocation, and was not developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Positions himself as the expert, delivering tip lists and writing the plan largely unilaterally. Thomas's explicit feedback that tips were 'jumping the gun' and plans felt 'over the top' was overridden, and the counselor never treats Thomas as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Thomas spontaneously offers change talk ('the mornings after aren't great anymore — I'm not bouncing back'), but the counselor fails to reinforce, reflect, or deepen it. Questions are fact-gathering rather than evocative, with no exploration of importance, confidence, or what change would mean to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with Thomas's sustain talk (minimization, paternal comparison) and sometimes reframes toward change, preventing reinforcement of it. However, responses are counter-argumentative informational corrections rather than skillful softening — no double-sided reflections or strategic use of Thomas's own concerns to resolve ambivalence.
