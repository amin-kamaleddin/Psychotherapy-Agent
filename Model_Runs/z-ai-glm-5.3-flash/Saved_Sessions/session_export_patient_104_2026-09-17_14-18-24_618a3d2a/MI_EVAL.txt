# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental tone with frequent reassurance ('You're not failing,' 'You deserve rest') and normalized safety screening. Affirmations are somewhat generic and formulaic rather than individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections and reassurance about infant sleep norms addressed her core worry. Emotional disclosures ('I hate feeling like I can't cope,' 'feels like survival') were met with advice lists rather than deep reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on sleep deprivation and the infant sleep/feeding routine throughout, with a concrete plan and structured follow-up metrics in 3-5 nights. Every turn ties back to the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nadine co-developed plan elements and messages were offered collaboratively, but most turns were unilateral advice delivery (pump how-tos, storage guidelines) rather than joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some choices and invites Nadine to personalize the messages. The session is heavily directive, with scripts and protocols prescribed rather than affirming Nadine's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is largely presumed rather than elicited—the counselor supplies reasons and solutions herself. Aside from a couple of questions, there's almost no exploration of Nadine's own values, hopes, or confidence; her change talk emerged despite, not because of, counselor technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for input and incorporates Nadine's ideas into the plan, but the overall stance is expert-driven with prescriptive bullet lists dominating. The counselor assumes the expert role rather than treating Nadine as the primary source of wisdom.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions prompted useful commitment language ('I could try that this weekend,' 'I'm willing to try tonight') and Nadine generated her own plan. Change talk was never reflected or deepened, and no confidence/importance questions were used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced and occasionally reframed ('needing help doesn't mean I'm failing'). However, ambivalence is sidestepped with task lists rather than actively worked with through double-sided reflections or strategic affirmations.
