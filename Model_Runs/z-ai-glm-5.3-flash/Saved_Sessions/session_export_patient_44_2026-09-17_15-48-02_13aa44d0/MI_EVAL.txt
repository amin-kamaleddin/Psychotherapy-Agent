# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with consistent affirmations, but praise is somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper individualized regard (e.g., normalizing setbacks, affirming courage in discussing discouragement).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture Erik's ambivalence and validate feelings (pride/frustration, self-comparison), but reflections are brief and the counselor rarely slows to explore deeper emotional layers before pivoting to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus—every exchange ties back to gym consistency, diet, and sleep, progressively building a concrete plan (Tue/Thu mornings, wind-down routine, wife accountability) with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely works as a partner, checking fit after suggestions and letting Erik shape decisions, but collaboration is uneven when lengthy prescriptive blocks (gym routine, wind-down schedule) are delivered rather than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly frames choices as Erik's own and accepts his preferences without pushback, but pre-packaged routines were presented in a take-it-or-leave-it manner that mildly constrained options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective scaling questions and evocative prompts drew out strong client change talk, but questions were often bundled and answers under-explored, with some solutions counselor-supplied rather than client-evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mixed evidence—Erik's input shaped the plan, but the counselor frequently slipped into an expert-prescriptive stance, delivering detailed routines before exploring Erik's own ideas (e.g., 'What did you have in mind?').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of scaling questions and leverage of spontaneous change talk (back pain improvement, accountability plan), but rapid-fire question bundling limited room for Erik to elaborate his own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well without amplifying it, validating briefly then pivoting effectively (self-comparison, morning worries), but validation is sometimes perfunctory and double-sided reflections are not used to hold ambivalence.
