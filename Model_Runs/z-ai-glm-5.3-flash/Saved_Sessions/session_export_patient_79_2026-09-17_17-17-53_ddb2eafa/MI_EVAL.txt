# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validating Luis's pushback without criticism and affirming his small efforts. Fell short of 5 due to occasional formulaic warmth and limited explicit normalization of his skepticism or relapse risk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening, accurately capturing Luis's core concerns about work functioning and feeling overwhelmed. Reflections leaned toward practical reframing rather than deeper emotional exploration, missing the fear and frustration implied in his statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on asthma self-management with each turn advancing concrete steps (cue, backup, refill logistics, follow-up). Skillfully redirected even when sarcasm derailed the conversation, exemplifying directional steering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner, building the plan almost entirely from Luis's own ideas (cupholder, locker storage, payday Friday). Occasionally slipped into advising mode by supplying scripts and if-then formulations before Luis requested them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly and consistently honored Luis's choices, from skipping reminders to selecting the pharmacist route and Friday timing. Options were offered as menus rather than directives, and his 'no promises' limit was fully accepted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Luis himself using open questions, the confidence ruler, and meaning-focused prompts, eliciting his own solutions and commitment language. A 5 would require deeper exploration of his values and stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Luis as the expert ('I'll follow your lead') and his contributions substantially shaped the plan. The counselor still supplied most structural scaffolding (if-then phrasing, scripts, backup plans) with limited invitation for Luis to generate these himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked change talk through opening questions and a well-executed confidence ruler that produced Luis's own solution (spare inhaler at locker). Desire and need statements were sometimes merely summarized rather than deepened, with no exploration of why change matters beyond work functioning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Luis's dismissals ('Oscar hasn't made me die yet,' 'it's too much') were acknowledged neutrally but not strategically softened—no double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk. Missed opportunities to gently reframe his skepticism toward his own stated goals, though the sustain talk was at least not amplified.
