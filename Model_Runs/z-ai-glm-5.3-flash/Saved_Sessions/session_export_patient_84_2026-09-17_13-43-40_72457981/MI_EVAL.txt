# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offers choices without pressure, but warmth feels somewhat formulaic with limited explicit affirmation of Malik's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ('being made to come feels unfair,' 'constant fighting is draining'), but reflections are sparse and shallow, missing deeper ambivalence and self-awareness while much of the session is advice-delivery.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on a concrete target — reducing home conflict — and moves efficiently to a rehearsed plan, though the cannabis use itself is never re-established as the clinical focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Malik picks options, requests role-play, and co-edits wording, but the counselor repeatedly hands down structured plans and scripts with limited joint problem-solving or curiosity about Malik's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong early statements of choice and repeated menu options with no pressure to quit, though heavy provision of pre-written scripts tips toward advice-giving and ownership of change is not consistently reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies heavily on providing plans, scripts, and tips rather than drawing out Malik's own motivations; almost no open-ended evocative questions, and Malik's change talk emerges spontaneously without being deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client contributions shape some content (choosing the casual script, adapting the role-play), but the expertise clearly sits with the counselor for much of the session, as Malik's comment 'she'd know I didn't write it' highlights.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Capitalizes somewhat on emerging change talk by pivoting to the mother-relationship goal, and the role-play elicits commitment language, but evocation is largely incidental rather than skillfully elicited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue with or reinforce Malik's minimization ('It's just weed'), a strength, but never uses double-sided reflections or develops discrepancy around his sustain talk.
