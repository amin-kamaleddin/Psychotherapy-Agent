# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with compassionate reframes of self-criticism and reassurance about setbacks. Falls short of a 5 because affirmations feel somewhat generic ('You've got this') rather than individualized to Elira's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections acknowledged the tightness, the 'you'll mess up' voice, and avoidance's short-term relief. However, reflections were brief and quickly pivoted to advice, bypassing deeper emotions like shame and fear of failure in favor of problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, coherent focus progressing from exploration to a specific dated commitment with a check-in structure. Direction was achieved through prescriptive steering rather than guided discovery, and the counselor slightly pushed the in-session experiment after Elira declined.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative elements existed (choosing the buffer, adopting her music and DND ideas), but the dominant pattern was counselor-generated solutions with Elira reacting. Genuine co-construction occurred mainly in the later planning phase, making collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice offers and respect for Elira's declines, with adaptive flexibility when she rejected the timer. Autonomy was honored implicitly but not actively reinforced ('this is your plan, your pace'), and the rapid technique menu felt somewhat pressure-laden.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest dimension — motivation was largely supplied by the counselor rather than elicited from Elira. Her volunteered motivation ('I want to do better,' good-day examples) went unexplored, with no evocative questions, rulers exploration, or values queries.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally positioned Elira's expertise as central, but the overall interaction was expert-driven with numbered prescriptive plans delivered rapidly. Elira's own question ('why do I do this?') was answered with another intervention rather than joint exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerged ('I want to do better,' 'first plan that hasn't felt completely impossible,' confidence 4–5), aided by choice offers. But it was never deliberately deepened — no elaboration questions, no exploration of why her confidence was 4–5 and not lower, and little reflection of her motivational statements back to her.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and acceptance was reframed as compatible with change, which was good. However, responses were reframes followed by new techniques — no double-sided reflections or strategic pivots, so ambivalence was largely bypassed rather than resolved.
