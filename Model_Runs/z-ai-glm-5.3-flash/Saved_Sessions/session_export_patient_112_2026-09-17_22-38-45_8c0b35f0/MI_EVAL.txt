# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard with a nonjudgmental tone ('I'm not here to judge or push you'). Reframes 'someone who can't quit' without judgment and affirms specifically ('that's someone who knows herself').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including double-sided reflections validating ambivalence ('both of those are true'). Captures emotional nuance, e.g., past attempts ended in feeling like failure, not lacking willpower.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking cessation throughout, guiding each exchange from importance to confidence, worries, planning, and commitment. Every response advances the change process without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates between equal partners with open, invitational questions and tentative summaries. The plan is fully co-constructed from Rosa's own material.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong emphasis on Rosa's ownership ('What would feel like a good first step, in your book?'), with no directives or pressure. Rosa sets the date, chooses her support person, and notes 'I think I just talked myself into it.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within Rosa — her reasons, confidence, and solutions (busy hands, fridge notes). Her self-generated insight ('maybe I gave up too easy on myself') shows elicited rather than instilled motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise in Rosa ('you designed your own plan'), and her ideas drive the plan. Even when she asks for information, the counselor turns it back to her experience rather than imposing advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully names and reinforces change talk and uses evocative questions ('what would need to be true for quitting to feel more possible?'). Rosa's language progresses from 'need to' to a self-initiated commitment ('Sunday. I think I just talked myself into it.').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated but never amplified; 'someone who can't quit' is reframed as six days of practice. Fears are acknowledged in double-sided reflections and redirected toward problem-solving, moving the arc steadily toward change language.
