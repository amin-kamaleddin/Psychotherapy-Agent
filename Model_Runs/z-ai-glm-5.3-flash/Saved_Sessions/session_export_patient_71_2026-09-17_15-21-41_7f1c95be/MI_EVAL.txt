# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with affirmations of Laura's efforts, but affirmations are somewhat generic and lack individualized warmth or normalization of her struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture Laura's values, pride, frustration, and fear of burnout. Reflections are sometimes brief content summaries rather than deep explorations of the emotional weight of her repeated failed attempts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains an exceptionally clear focus on making health a lived priority, with every exchange advancing from values discrepancy to a concrete, supported plan with accountability and a scheduled follow-up. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely an exchange between equals, with Laura's ideas driving the final plan. Occasionally shifts into an advisor role by supplying drafted text and invite content rather than fully eliciting Laura's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers menu-based choices and lets Laura retain ownership of the plan. Falls short of 5 because some supports (templates, wording) feel prescriptive and Laura's authority is never explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Draws motivation from within via open questions, a confidence ruler, and success framing. Questions skew practical/planning-focused, and some motivational content comes from counselor templates rather than the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Laura's expertise is central, with plans emerging from her own ideas and self-designed check-ins. The counselor at times slips into a consultant role, offering ready-made templates and options before fully inviting Laura's solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evokes change talk via open questions and a confidence ruler, and Laura produces substantial commitment language. A 5 would require deeper exploration of why health matters rather than moving quickly to planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it's just one night,' past plans that slid) is not amplified, but it is largely passed over rather than actively softened — no double-sided reflections or reframing of her self-awareness as a strength.
