# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with explicit validation of autonomy and genuine, individualized affirmations. No disapproval shown despite the patient's initial minimization and defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, emotionally attuned reflections (e.g., naming the social fear and the goals-vs-friends trade-off) that the patient repeatedly confirms and builds upon.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear progression from ambivalence to concrete plan and relapse prevention, with each response moving forward strategically. Minor deduction: the closing exchange about the cousin and mom drifts slightly without tying back to an explicit commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is genuinely co-constructed — the patient generates all plan content while the psychiatrist acts as a thinking partner. Power-sharing is palpable, with decisions entirely the patient's own.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently emphasized, with options offered rather than prescriptions and no controlling language. Successes are framed as the patient's own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivations for change are elicited from the patient, never supplied by the psychiatrist. Questions develop and consolidate existing motivation, building language from skepticism to commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client throughout, and the patient's contributions demonstrably shape the session. Every plan element originates from him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful open questions draw out abundant change talk ('I don't wanna become addicted,' 'lock screen's getting changed tonight'). Change talk is reinforced by slowing down and highlighting it rather than adding the psychiatrist's own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged briefly and pivoted toward change without argument. The psychiatrist never debates whether use is 'serious enough,' letting the patient connect behavior to his own stakes.
