# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and never shames Jamal about the marijuana. However, warmth is thin, the opening question barrage feels like a checklist, and there is little affirmation of Jamal's strengths (practical thinking, athletics, college concerns).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is limited and formulaic, with 'I hear you' used repeatedly rather than genuine reflections. The counselor rarely explores the emotional core — fear of roasting by friends, irritation with the system, family conflict, or college anxiety — and answers important questions with information instead of reflecting the worry beneath them.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on marijuana use, testing, and PO conditions, with effective redirections and a concrete ending plan (Wednesday 4:30, paperwork, role-plays). Not a 5 because momentum drifts into administrative logistics and the target change is never defined beyond test-passing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal does influence the agenda and chooses among options, but the counselor's stance is predominantly informational and advisory. Repeated question barrages and 'Want me to: 1)... 2)... 3)...' menus create a service-provider dynamic rather than true two-way collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice menus ('Which sounds doable for you?') superficially support choice, and Jamal's stated limits are respected. But directive advice and rapid-fire option lists railroad rather than empower, and the counselor never proactively reinforces Jamal's ownership of decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Evocative questioning is limited to a scripted opening barrage, after which the counselor lapses into information delivery. Motivation stays external (test-passing, compliance); even when Jamal volunteers college and basketball concerns, the counselor responds with administrative facts rather than eliciting what a clean record would mean to him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positions himself as the expert, delivering test timelines, refusal scripts, and quick tips without first eliciting Jamal's own ideas. Jamal's suggestions did shape the plan, keeping this from a 1, but the overall pattern is expert-driven rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Skillfully capitalizes on Jamal's self-generated change talk about taking a break, linking it to ball/college goals. However, the counselor rarely explicitly elicits or deepens change talk and misses chances to amplify it (e.g., 'What would it be like to finish with a clean record?').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk but also fails to strategically soften it — minimizations like 'It's not a lot' are met with neutral acknowledgment that repeats sustain language. No double-sided reflections or development of discrepancy between 'it's no big deal' and Jamal's fears for his record and college prospects.
