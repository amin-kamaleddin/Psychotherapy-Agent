# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations and validation of Elias's shame without blame. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic ('That shows strength' used twice) and lacked deep individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured Elias's ambivalence and shame, including complex reflections of his 'not today' moment. Fell short of top marks because reflections sometimes pivoted quickly to questions or advice rather than fully exploring the emotional depth of his shame about his children.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus with no topic drift, deliberately pivoting from shame exploration to evidence of agency and converging on a specific, time-bound plan (Wednesday back-way drive with accountability texts). Every turn built momentum toward the concrete action plan, ending with a clear thread toward generalization.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative — the counselor co-constructed the text message, rehearsed together, and let decisions like Wednesday timing emerge from dialogue. But the counselor still controlled the option sets (scaling questions, coping strategies, draft texts), so full equality in shaping the session wasn't achieved.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choice ('Which feels more useful right now?') and accepted Elias's confidence rating of six without pressure. It stopped short of a 5 because menus were counselor-generated with occasional mild leading, though Elias's decisions were consistently honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently drew motivation from within Elias, eliciting his own reasons (tiredness of shame, daughter's school event) and his own accountability solution. Some moments shifted toward the counselor supplying strategies (routes, reminders, grounding) rather than consistently evoking his resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Incorporated some of Elias's contributions (the back-way plan, text choice), but the interaction leaned toward counselor-as-expert, notably when Elias said 'You tell me what would actually work' and the counselor supplied all three draft texts. Power-sharing was present but lukewarm.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and amplified change talk, drawing out the powerful liquor-store anecdote and Elias's own reasons for change, then reinforcing it with confidence scaling and concrete planning. Elias left with escalating change talk, including his own suggestion to extend beyond Wednesdays.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to Elias's doubt ('I've made promises before and broken them') with effective reframes and redirection to evidence of agency rather than amplifying doubt. The reframe was somewhat brief; a deeper double-sided reflection acknowledging the doubt's legitimacy before pivoting would have softened it more fully.
