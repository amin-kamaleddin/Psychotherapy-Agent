# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated unconditional positive regard through specific, genuine affirmations and normalized future lapses ('However the week goes, it's all welcome here'). No judgment or disapproval appeared anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was consistently accurate and often complex, including double-sided reflections and emotionally attuned observations. The one growth edge was exploring the emotional undercurrent of 'one more thing to fail at' more deeply before pivoting.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, unwavering focus on aligning Laura's health value with a concrete home yoga practice, moving logically from gap identification to a specific plan. Every psychiatrist turn advanced the target with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely two-way, with Laura's ideas visibly shaping every next step — from the yoga/service comparison to the negotiated check-in arrangement. The tone reflected walking alongside rather than guiding from above.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decision-making authority was explicitly returned to the client ('you're the one who gets to decide what counts, not me'), and options were offered without pressure. Laura's choice to carry the plan herself with a collaborative check-in was honored as a 'deal.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key motivations — groundedness, self-love, the ten-minute version, the definition of success — originated from Laura in response to open, evocative questions. The clinician reflected and amplified this self-generated motivation throughout.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was repeatedly located within the client, and the final plan (ten minutes, notes, morning schedule) was entirely Laura's creation. The clinician consistently reinforced that ownership, modeling the client-as-expert stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked through questions drawing on Laura's prior success and inviting commitment on specifics (days, times). Laura responded with strong commitment language, ending with anticipation ('I'm kind of looking forward to it').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly and pivoted toward change using double-sided reflections that paired it with change talk. Laura's reframing ('slipping doesn't mean I've failed') was amplified so lapses became part of the change process.
