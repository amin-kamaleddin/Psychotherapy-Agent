# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician affirmed the patient's choices without judgment, including his decision not to involve his sister yet. Potential shame about laziness was actively defused and reframed as self-protection.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflected ambivalence ('part of you... part of you...') and validated fears of failure and embarrassment. The patient explicitly thanked the clinician for 'not making me feel lazy about it.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintained a clear focus on developing a concrete, self-generated plan for exercise. Each exchange moved incrementally toward a specific first step scheduled for tonight.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The stance was fully collaborative—no advice was imposed, and the clinician deferred to the patient's judgment about privacy, timing, and pacing ('that's your call'). The plan was explicitly framed as the patient's own design.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician reinforced autonomy throughout ('no promises, no pressure—just your own idea, at your pace'). The patient's closing reflection ('it feels like mine somehow') confirms strong autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open-ended questions evoked the patient's own solutions ('what do you think would help?') and his own motivation (sleep, feeling less foggy). Change talk about noticing benefits came from the patient, not the clinician.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized the patient's authorship ('you came up with it yourself,' 'you made it happen, not me'). The entire plan (shoes, timing, phone note) emerged from the patient's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively cultivated and consolidated change talk, inviting commitments ('what feels most useful to do first') and a measurable marker ('two walks in a week'). The patient articulated commitment language repeatedly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently softened sustain talk ('both can be true,' 'protecting the plan, not avoiding it'), reframing self-doubt as ambivalence rather than failure. Sustain talk was met with reflection and hope rather than agreement.
