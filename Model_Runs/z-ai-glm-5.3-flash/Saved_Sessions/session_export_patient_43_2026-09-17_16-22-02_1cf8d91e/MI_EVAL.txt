# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing setbacks ('S days aren't failures, just data') and honoring Eric's conditions like 'no lectures' and confidentiality from his wife. Warmth was somewhat formulaic and repetitive, with the plan recapped four times — a point Eric explicitly noted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflective listening was good, but reflections largely disappeared after the opening turns as the session shifted to planning and tips. Emotionally meaningful material — Eric's 'proving myself right' motive and his mild concern about the stairs — went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor kept clear focus on smoking throughout and advanced steadily toward a concrete, measurable experiment with specific steps and a scheduled check-in. Early exploration of Eric's values and his wife's concerns was cut short in favor of rapid movement to planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Eric's preferences genuinely shaped the plan (walk over gum, timer and truck tips, 5:30 garage call), with check-ins at each step. However, the menus, tracking system, and rulers were counselor-initiated structures Eric adopted rather than co-created, and he grew somewhat passive toward the end.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength: the counselor repeatedly emphasized choice — 'no commitment,' 'you can still smoke after the walk,' 'pick 1-2' — and respected Eric's request to keep things short and nonjudgmental. Eric's confidence rising from 5 to 6 reflects the effectiveness of this approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor drew motivation from Eric rather than lecturing, using rulers and open questions that elicited his own motivator (stairs improving) and genuine change talk ('kind of curious what the numbers show'). Opportunities to elaborate on 'proving myself right' and his admitted concern were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Eric's expertise was consistently positioned as central: he chose the experiment, selected his own tips, self-designed plan details, and set the check-in time. Some options and structures were counselor-generated rather than drawn from Eric, limiting full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Readiness rulers visibly moved Eric from 2/10 importance to confidence rising from 5 to 6, and the plan was built around his own stated motivator (stairs/breathing). Deeper elaboration of his personal reasons for change beyond the stair symptom would be needed for a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and cleverly softened resistance with the 'experiment, not quitting' framing and 'S days aren't failures' normalization. Some minimization was simply restated rather than strategically pivoted or explored.
