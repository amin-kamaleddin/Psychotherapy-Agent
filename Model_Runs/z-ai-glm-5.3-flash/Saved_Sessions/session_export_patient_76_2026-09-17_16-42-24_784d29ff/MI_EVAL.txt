# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout; safety checks framed as care and behavior validated without condoning. Short of 5 due to formulaic warmth and limited explicit affirmation of Liora's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening that accurately captured her triggers, ambivalence, and nighttime fears. Reflections were brief and quickly pivoted to problem-solving, leaving emotional pain and somatic distress under-explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on self-harm safety, urge management, and means restriction with no topic drift. Steady progression from risk assessment to a concrete, personalized action plan with regular urge check-ins.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Liora edited the text, co-built the night kit, and shaped backup plans, with input actively solicited. At times counselor-proposes/client-reacts; eliciting her own coping ideas first would more fully equalize idea generation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly every turn offered genuine choices with permission-seeking and no directive or coercive language. Liora's experiments (breathing before cutting 'just once') were left fully as her own decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice-based engagement drew out Liora's own hope and commitment ('less stuck,' 'I'll send that text tonight, I mean it') from a resigned, precontemplative client. Rarely asked open evocative questions about her values or hopes for a life without self-harm.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Liora was positioned as the expert and her contributions materially shaped the session. Some offerings (scripts, kit list, grounding note) arrived as complete counselor-generated products rather than co-created ones.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conditions fostered clear commitment and ability statements, which were reinforced ('strong, practical step'). Change talk arose opportunistically; targeted evocative questions and confidence exploration were missing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was validated rather than reinforced, but double-sided reflections or pivots toward change were rarely used. Some responses risked reinforcing status-quo framing without counterbalancing her desire for alternatives.
