# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental tone throughout with supportive statements like 'I'm here to listen and support you' and 'You can do this.' However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic and rarely highlight Anisa's personal strengths, such as her honesty and courage in trying again.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections, but the session is dominated by advice-giving rather than deep reflective listening. Key emotional disclosures — shame ('like being a leper') and fear of letting her daughter down — are not met with genuine empathic exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus never wavers from smoking cessation and converges on a concrete, achievable first step with a slip plan and follow-up structure. The pace toward planning is somewhat rapid, moving ahead before ambivalence is fully resolved.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine co-creation occurred (Anisa chose the 30-minute delay, living room coffee, and gum), but three separate bulleted instruction lists positioned the counselor as the expert. Power sharing largely happened only after Anisa pushed back that the plan 'feels like a lot at once.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks permission, offers options, and respects Anisa's preference to start small and avoid nicotine products. Still, repeated prescriptive lists convey a controlling, expert-directed style with little affirmation of her capacity to chart her own path.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The importance/confidence rulers elicited useful change talk, but answers were never explored for meaning, and much of the content (reasons, strategies, even the daughter's message) was supplied by the counselor. Anisa generated most change talk largely unassisted.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor occasionally invites Anisa's input and adapted the plan when asked, but the dominant mode is expert-driven instruction. Anisa's own ideas were explored only after she explicitly requested something smaller.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The rulers effectively elicited strong change talk (importance 8-9), but the counselor pivoted immediately to planning instead of deepening it. Motivations around her daughter and hope were never amplified or strategically reflected back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced, and reassurance about slips helped neutralize fear of failure. However, concerns about past failures and shame were met with advice rather than reframing or double-sided reflections linking them to her motivations.
