# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect and positive regard with no judgment despite patient minimization, but affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic, with less explicit warmth about the patient as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening tracking the patient's ambivalence between social belonging and academic/legal stakes. Reflections tend to be more cognitive than emotional, rarely naming feelings like fear of suspension or peer pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on alcohol use with clear progressive structure: importance ruler → confidence ruler → commitment → implementation plan → rehearsal → peer-response planning. Every exchange advances toward a concrete, fully specified action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructed session — the counselor solicits feedback, adopts the patient's script revision verbatim, and operates as a consultant rather than a director. The patient's contributions visibly shape session outcomes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, explicit support of choice: options offered as choices, the patient designs his own consequence, and chooses his preferred text draft. No pressuring or coercive language anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited almost entirely from the patient — his own reasons, solutions, and consequences — culminating in spontaneous self-affirmation ('I can actually do that'). Exemplary use of open questions and elaboration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is clearly located in the client — every decision originates from Tyler, including his improvements to the counselor's script. The patient's closing comment ('Thanks for not just lecturing me') confirms the non-expert stance was felt.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook change talk cultivation using importance and confidence rulers, commitment questions, rehearsal, and reinforcement. The patient's change talk visibly escalates from 'maybe a 4-5' to confidence 8 with concrete commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally honored rather than argued with, and the harm-reduction framing skillfully integrates it. Occasionally leaves sustain talk unaddressed rather than actively using double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
