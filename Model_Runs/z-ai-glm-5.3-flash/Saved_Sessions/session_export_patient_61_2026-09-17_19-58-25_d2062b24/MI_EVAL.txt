# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, never judging or correcting even when the client sets limits or expresses frustration. The client explicitly notes 'You don't look at me like I'm failing,' showing he experiences this acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The reflections are highly accurate and nuanced, capturing both surface content and underlying emotional meaning (e.g., 'the list feels like a second job you never applied for'). The client repeatedly responds with elaboration ('Yeah, exactly that'), demonstrating he feels deeply understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, progressive focus on diabetes self-management from the opening question to a concrete, collaboratively chosen target situation. Each exchange advances toward identifying one manageable behavior, ending in a specific trial run without aimless wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a genuine joint enterprise, with the counselor asking rather than prescribing and co-constructing the plan ('What if we picked just one situation together'). Decisions about which behavior and situation to target are made by the client with counselor support, exemplifying fully collaborative interaction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly and repeatedly affirmed ('that's your call and it's yours to make'), and the counselor does not push back when the client threatens to quit. The client's shift from external obligation ('supposed to') to internal ownership demonstrates effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor draws motivation entirely from the client rather than supplying it, using open questions that elicit his own reasons and solutions. The client identifies his own learning goal and target situation, with motivation visibly growing from within across the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently locates expertise in the client ('You'd know better than me which situation comes up most') and actively shares power. The client's own contributions directly shaped the session direction, with his pick of the lunch situation becoming the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evokes and deepens change talk, progressively moving the client from 'I came here because I'm supposed to' to proposing a concrete trial run. Change talk is reinforced throughout, culminating in the client choosing a target situation himself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoids amplifying sustain talk and uses double-sided reflections effectively. However, a few questions ('Which of those feelings wins out on most days?') briefly invited sustain talk before pivoting, giving some airtime to reasons against change.
