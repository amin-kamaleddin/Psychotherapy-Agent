# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout ('You're welcome here whether you see a problem or not'), never arguing with Derrick's minimization. However, affirmations are formulaic ('Nice work') rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections, but significant emotional content (isolation during sobriety, the breakup, fear of exclusion) is repeatedly glossed over in favor of practical tips. The counselor also missed that the girlfriend was an ex, suggesting superficial listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays well focused on preventing another DUI, anchoring on concrete steps (ride cash, refusal lines, sober hangouts) and closing with a role-play plan. Only minor drift into generic safety questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration — Derrick picks starter steps and tailors refusal lines to his style. But the counselor repeatedly dumps pre-packaged solution lists, making Derrick more a consumer of advice than an equal co-designer.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers surface-level choices ('which do you want to start with?'), but relentless prescriptive advice pressures the client toward the counselor's agenda early. No explicit statements reinforcing Derrick's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation comes from Derrick himself rather than being elicited; the counselor rarely asks evocative open questions like 'How did you manage six months sober?'. Default mode is providing information, with the role-play suggestion as the lone late evocative move.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing via 'which sounds most helpful?' questions, but the dominant style is expert-driven with bulleted advice lists nearly every turn. Derrick's own ideas are incorporated only lightly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Affirms Derrick's spontaneous change talk (ride cash, six months sober) but does so sparsely and generically. Never deliberately evokes or deepens change talk with targeted questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and once cleverly reframes it ('focus on the specific problem'), but statements like 'I still don't think I'm some alcoholic' pass largely unaddressed. Double-sided reflections pairing minimization with Derrick's stated stakes are absent.
