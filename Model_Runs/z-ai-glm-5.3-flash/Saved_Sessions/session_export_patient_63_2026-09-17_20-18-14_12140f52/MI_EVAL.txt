# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly accepting Jordan's residual desire to shoplift ('you don't have to pretend otherwise for me') and normalizing his fear of exclusion without any judgment or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate, with complex and double-sided reflections (e.g., 'Two real fears, pulling in opposite directions') that capture Jordan's ambivalence so precisely he confirms 'that's it exactly,' marking the session's pivotal moment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on reducing shoplifting, progressing logically from exploring ambivalence to a concrete, time-bound first step with built-in accountability, with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Problem-solving is explicitly framed as joint ('we can figure this out together'), and even decisions about the therapy process itself—like the next-session check-in—are offered as choices, demonstrating genuine shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly reinforces Jordan's ownership ('it's your pace,' offering options rather than directives) and frames outcomes as information rather than failure, leaving execution of the plan entirely with Jordan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from Jordan himself via open questions about his values and priorities, and his language traces a clear arc from 'I haven't worked out how to want stopping' to 'I'll do it'—the counselor never lectures or persuades.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client ('You know yourself and those friendships better than anyone'), and the plan originates entirely from Jordan's own ideas, with the counselor affirming 'it was yours' at the close.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and developed—open questions elicit Jordan's own reasons (sleep, earned money, freedom from dread)—culminating in strong commitment language ('I'll text him... that I can actually do this week').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally redirects sustain talk effectively by balancing ambivalence ('two real fears, pulling in opposite directions'), though Jordan's fears about Marcus and group exclusion receive substantial airtime, and the normalizing of residual desire near the end slightly gives air to sustain talk.
