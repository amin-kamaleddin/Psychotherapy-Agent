### Acceptance

**Comments:** Respectful, non-judgmental, and embracing of Matteo's framing and humor. Acceptance occasionally slips into accommodation, e.g., dropping the drinking topic when he calls it 'beside the point' without affirming unconditional regard.

### Empathy

**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early, accurately capturing his dual priorities and practical barriers. Reflections thin out later and the social anxiety behind 'the injured guy' comment goes unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear target and moves to concrete, measurable action with a defined follow-up plan. Focus on drinking was temporarily lost when Matteo deflected it as 'beside the point'.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans are genuinely co-constructed with Matteo editing scripts to fit his voice. Counselor sometimes slips into advice-dispensing with dense option lists before drawing out the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers choice and reinforces Matteo's ownership of every decision, which he explicitly claims. Matteo's closing shows he fully perceives change as his own choice.

### Evocation

**Comments:** Effective use of the confidence ruler and a self-generated insight about sleep. However, much motivation is supplied externally via information and ready-made scripts, and deeper values around drinking remain unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise is often placed with the client, whose contributions shape the plan. Falls short due to frequent pre-packaged option menus rather than eliciting the client's ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence ruler successfully elicited genuine change talk and reasons for change. Key moments were not deepened or reflected back, and the counselor rushed to logistics instead of consolidating commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
