# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, non-judgmental stance with permission-seeking before feedback, but affirmations are sparse and warmth feels transactional rather than genuinely individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is limited; emotionally significant moments (the fall realization, fear of falling alone, wanting independence from her son) are met with checklists rather than complex reflections or exploration of feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays consistently focused and converges on a concrete, measurable plan with follow-up; loses a point because focus is maintained through directive instruction and a counselor-set target rather than negotiated goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing (choosing alcohol-free days, joint review framing), but the counselor unilaterally sets limits, log format, and safety rules; Margaret's input shapes the plan only at the margins.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respects her refusal to give up pub nights and frames steps as options, but prescriptive, rule-like language repeated multiple times (patient notes being told 'three times now') undermines genuine autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies heavily on expert information rather than drawing out motivations; the importance rating of 3–4 is never explored, and Margaret's own motivations (fear of falling, independence) surface spontaneously but go unexploited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Overall expert-driven stance: counselor supplies the target, techniques, and rules largely without eliciting Margaret's ideas, and she must push back to retain agency; her expertise is minimally incorporated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was evoked (tracking, alcohol-free days, 'a bit more hopeful'), but it is never deepened—the importance ruler is unexplored, and change talk is often met with logistics rather than affirmation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor avoids arguing and respects her pub-night boundary, but uses no active softening strategies—no double-sided reflections, reframing, or pivoting to her reasons for change; sustain talk is mostly sidestepped.
