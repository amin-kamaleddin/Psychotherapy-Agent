# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and unconditional positive regard throughout, with frequent substantive affirmations like 'That's knowing yourself.' To reach a 5, warmth could be more varied and emotionally attuned during harder moments of self-disclosure, such as when Daniel admits waving his wife off.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening capturing both content and ambivalence, e.g., 'the signal with your wife feels doable, and you're realistic about how the limit gets shaky.' Empathy occasionally stays at the strategic/cognitive level and could go deeper into emotional layers, such as the social belonging function of drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, progressive focus on alcohol use at social gatherings from the first turn, building sequentially toward a concrete action step. Every question advances planning and commitment without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The tone is one of equals, with the session direction fully co-constructed through implicit permission-seeking. Collaboration is practiced behaviorally but not always named explicitly as a joint 'we' enterprise or negotiated agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions are consistently reinforced as Daniel's own ('which of these feels most solid to YOU'), with no persuasive or directive language anywhere. Daniel's closing observation that he arrived expecting a lung talk but built his own plan is clear evidence of honored autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from the client—'what's worked for you before?' activated his smoking-cessation self-efficacy, and 'what would make you feel like it went well' surfaced intrinsic motivation. By the end, all change motivation is client-sourced and client-articulated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positions Daniel as the expert ('You know these nights best') and offers no advice at all—every strategy is client-generated. This exemplifies the belief that expertise about change resides within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's greatest strength: every turn ends with an evocative question that elicits rich desire, ability, and commitment language, culminating in 'I'm walking in with a plan and a partner instead of just going with the flow. That's new for me. Feels good.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I might still slip,' 'the limit gets shaky') is acknowledged honestly as realistic planning and consistently pivoted back toward solutions. A 5 would require sharper pivots, such as exploring how a slip would be handled.
