# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms the patient's autonomy ('I'm not here to take your tools away') and unconditionally accepts ambivalence without judgment, honoring the patient's view that mornings are non-negotiable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and nuanced, capturing dualities ('part of you wants to slow down... another feels pushing through') and both validating and reframe-understanding ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Each exchange moves the conversation meaningfully forward—from ambivalence toward a concrete, patient-generated experiment—without rushing or steering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue is exploratory and question-driven, with the clinician following the patient's lead rather than directing. The plan emerges from the patient's own ideas about which change 'fits actual days.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient repeatedly owns the plan ('Small, but it's mine'), reflecting strong emphasis on self-directed choice. Every decision point returns responsibility to the patient rather than prescribing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently draw out the patient's own motivations, values, and solutions ('what would feeling okay look like?'), rather than the clinician supplying reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positions the patient as the expert ('it sounds like you're the one weighing what's a small thing'), and the final plan is explicitly designed and owned by the patient.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicits and reflects self-motivational statements ('I'd rather know than keep guessing') and reinforces the patient's own articulation of reasons, ability, and commitment for the afternoon swap.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (mornings sacred, not convinced it's a problem) is consistently reflected rather than challenged, allowing the patient to resolve ambivalence naturally while still committing to the experiment.
