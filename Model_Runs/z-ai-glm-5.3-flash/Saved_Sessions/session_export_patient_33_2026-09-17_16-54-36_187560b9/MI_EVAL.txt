# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validating Diego's fears and pragmatism without shaming or labeling. Falls short of 5 because warmth is somewhat procedural, with few personalized affirmations of Diego's strengths (e.g., his courage in practicing the disclosure).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but often summary-level, and deeper emotional content is repeatedly missed — pivoting to strategies when Diego says 'even saying it out loud feels heavy,' bypassing his fear of homelessness, and skipping past 'my instinct was to snap back.' Empathy is present but superficial at the moments that mattered most.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on harm reduction, progressing logically from exploration to role-play to a concrete action plan with a timeline (Saturday morning). The opening turn stacks four questions in one utterance, diffusing focus, and some turns drift into information delivery.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is a genuine joint enterprise — scripts are co-developed and edited by Diego, and the role-play shapes subsequent coaching. Falls short because the counselor tends to pre-package solutions rather than drawing content out of Diego first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: asks permission before offering options, provides choices at every step, and gracefully accepts Diego's 'no' responses (declining methadone, local lookups, further rehearsal). Diego explicitly confirms the non-lecturing approach, direct evidence his sense of choice was honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The role-play does elicit Diego's own problem-solving, but motivation is more often supplied by the counselor (scripts, strategy lists) than evoked from him. Prime evocation opportunities — his 10/10 readiness and emergent 'maybe down the road we revisit the quitting conversation' — are left unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego genuinely shapes the session — his rejections, script edits, and timeline choices are all incorporated without resistance. Stops short of 5 because the counselor still controls the menu of options and uses permission-style questions rather than fully co-creating material.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego generates substantial change talk (10/10 readiness, 'I want to do this for real soon,' 'maybe... revisit quitting'), but the counselor does not deepen or reinforce it. Missed opportunities to amplify commitment language and explore what success would look like keep this at an average level.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('not looking to quit right now') is met with acceptance rather than argument or persuasion, appropriately redirecting energy toward harm reduction. Misses chances to use double-sided reflections that would strategically leverage sustain talk (e.g., linking 'using alone at night scares me' to the safety plan).
