# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance with explicit honoring of Marco's autonomy and 'no lectures' boundary. Missed deeper affirmations of his resilience surviving heart surgery and cancer.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective summaries, including a well-done reflection on the 'best mate' metaphor. Reflections were often long stacked summaries, and the emotional meaning behind his fatalism and past quit failures went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking tied to the dental context and guided the session to a concrete, measurable one-week swap experiment with implementation details, fallbacks, and a check-in agreement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely co-constructed—Marco chose the cigarette to swap, the walking cue, and his own coping phrases. Several pre-packaged idea lists shifted the problem-solving balance somewhat toward the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly and explicitly supported Marco's choice through permission-seeking, 'no commitment' framing, honoring the 'no lectures' boundary, and the slip-without-self-blame framing. The experiment framing matched his autonomy needs well.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ruler questions drew out some motivation and confidence, but exploration of Marco's own reasons and values remained surface-level. Much of the change content came from counselor-generated menus rather than sustained elicitation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Marco as the expert and returned ownership of decisions, with his contributions visibly shaping the plan. Some options were delivered as counselor-generated menus rather than co-discovered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of open questions and rulers moved Marco to concrete commitments, with confidence rising from 6 to 7. Change talk was concentrated in action planning, with missed chances to deepen why change mattered.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to heavy sustain talk with neutral, non-reinforcing reflections and pivoted toward what smoking provides, avoiding argument. Could have used double-sided reflections more actively rather than moving past sustain talk.
