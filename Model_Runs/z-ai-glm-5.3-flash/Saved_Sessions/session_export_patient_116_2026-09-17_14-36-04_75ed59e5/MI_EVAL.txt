# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Polite, nonjudgmental, and no shaming about nonadherence, but warmth is procedural and no affirmations of Samir's honesty, humor, or willingness to attend.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Early reflections are adequate, but the key emotional disclosure about feeling like 'a sick person' was not reflected or explored, and repeated safety screening despite his objections showed inattention.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifted from the referred target (medication adherence) to family boundary scripts and never returned or linked the two.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor authored nearly all content while Samir merely selected and reacted; no true co-creation built on his own ideas occurred.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice-offering was present but shallow — limited to scripts and wording, with no reinforcement of Samir's ownership of the medication decision itself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions elicited compliance rather than motivation, and the client's one glimmer of change talk ('maybe I could try') was never explored or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** An advice-dispensing expert stance dominated, leaving Samir as a consumer of scripts rather than the source of expertise about his own life and mother.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Samir's soft 'maybe I could try' was not reflected, amplified, or consolidated; no questions about importance, confidence, or benefits of change were asked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I don't like taking them,' 'not promising anything') went unaddressed, and the focus on reducing nagging arguably reinforced the status quo.
