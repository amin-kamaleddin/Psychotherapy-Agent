# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental toward a guarded, mandated client, including normalizing his loyalty to friends and skepticism. Affirmations were somewhat generic, preventing a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured surface dynamics (loyalty pull, wanting something 'real') but stayed shallow, briefly acknowledging before pivoting to problem-solving. Emotional weight of his loneliness, prior failures, and shame was glossed over.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the concrete target of protecting his job from peer pressure before shifts, tying each turn back to this goal. Tactical logistics somewhat dominated over deeper motivational work, but the target never drifted.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo's input shaped the direction, but the interaction was often asymmetrical — the counselor generated ideas while Mateo merely selected among them. His own past experiences and expertise were never drawn upon to co-create the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly supported Mateo's choice throughout ('I'll follow your lead'), respecting his declines of role-play and written help. Stacked unsolicited suggestions occasionally edged toward steering rather than following.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The one-year vision question successfully drew out Mateo's intrinsic motivation, and he voiced his own reason for change. Much of the session relied on counselor-supplied scripts and menus rather than eliciting his own solutions and deeper meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice menus gave surface-level input, and Mateo's selections shaped the plan, but the session had an advice-dispensing, expert-like quality. Mateo even had to reclaim ownership ('I can write it myself'), revealing partial power-sharing only.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evoked desire and commitment language (e.g., 'it has to start somewhere,' 'I'll try it this week'). However, key change talk was passed over rather than amplified or deepened, and the counselor shifted to advice-giving.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally and met with adequate reassurance ('treat slips as information'). No strategic double-sided reflections or deliberate pivots toward change statements were used to soften it skillfully.
