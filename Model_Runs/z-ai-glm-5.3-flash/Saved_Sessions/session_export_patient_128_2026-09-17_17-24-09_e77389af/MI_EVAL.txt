# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with genuine affirmations throughout. Warmth is somewhat task-focused, lacking deeper normalization of ambivalence and unconditional regard independent of session productivity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Tyler's core ambivalence and practical barriers. Reflections stay at the content level, missing emotional material like shame about the police incident and his vulnerable 'ask me Saturday night' doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains an exceptionally clear, consistent focus on the target behavior from start to finish. Each response advances the agenda, building progressively toward a concrete, rehearsed plan without wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructs the plan, with the role-play and roommate text shaped by Tyler's input and feedback. Falls short because the counselor supplies dense pre-packaged option menus that Tyler reacts to rather than co-creates.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently places decisions with Tyler (strategy, script, timing, backup plan) with no coercive language. Advice is offered conditionally and Tyler's self-generated choices demonstrably shape the final plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Tyler's own motivations through scaling questions and the opening values-based inquiry, leading to real movement in readiness and confidence. Sometimes answers her own questions with solution lists rather than letting Tyler generate ideas, and avoids deeper personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for Tyler's opinion and incorporates his choices into the plan. However, the counselor frequently functions as the expert solution-generator, offering menus of 3-5 options each turn, which tips power toward the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through strategic scaling questions and open prompts, producing measurable movement from readiness 5 to confidence 7.5 with commitment language. Limits the counselor's own option-provision and failure to amplify Tyler's own phrasing ('degree > one night') back as his motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk but largely passes over it rather than meeting it with double-sided reflections. Misses opportunities to use Tyler's minimization ('everyone does it,' 'being the guy with a limit') to develop discrepancy against his stated values.
