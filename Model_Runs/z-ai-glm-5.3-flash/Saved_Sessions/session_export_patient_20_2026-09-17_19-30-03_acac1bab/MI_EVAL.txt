# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with affirmations like 'That takes honesty to say out loud.' Affirmations were sparse (2-3) and there was no explicit normalization of ambivalence or defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including an emotionally resonant reflection contrasting hangovers with lost trust. Limited by two significant misattunements (the Dad/Dr. Chen reflection and repeated role-confusion echoes) that disrupted the alliance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, progressive focus on drinking behavior once past the opening, building from problem to consequences to actionable change. The chaotic first three turns cost session momentum, keeping this below a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan was genuinely co-constructed, building on Brandon's own ideas. Collaboration was one-directional at times, and there were no explicit check-ins like 'does that fit for you?' after summaries.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never pressured or lectured; every plan element originated from the patient, with ownership reinforced ('That's your expertise talking'). Lacked explicit affirmations of the patient's right to decide or to own setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicited the patient's own motivations—identity as 'the guy people count on,' trust concerns—and the entire practical plan came from his mouth. Reflections deepened his motivation rather than importing a clinical agenda.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly located expertise in the client and asked what he thought would work, yielding a client-generated plan. The opening role confusion and absence of explicit agenda negotiation muddied the partnership frame.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation throughout; open questions systematically drew out change talk, from 'maybe walking the dog' to a concrete self-generated plan. Brandon's language visibly shifted from 'it's not a crisis' to 'weekday nights are the loose thread.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided debate, using effective double-sided reflections to hold sustain talk while tilting toward change. Occasionally restated sustain talk content without pivoting back to the patient's change language.
