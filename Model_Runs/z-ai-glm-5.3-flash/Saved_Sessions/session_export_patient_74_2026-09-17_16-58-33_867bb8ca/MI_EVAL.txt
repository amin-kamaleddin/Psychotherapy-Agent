# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and nonjudgment ("it's okay that you're not ready to quit"; affirmations like "That's a solid, realistic plan"). No labeling or disapproval present. Acceptance was at times somewhat formulaic and bundled with the counselor's own agenda rather than purely unconditional regard for the client's pace.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate double-sided reflections ("You're torn — you enjoy smoking... yet you worry about being a role model") and accurate reflections of values. Reflections often pivoted quickly to question batteries; the emotional weight of the client's regret about smoking young and fear of her daughter asking for a smoke was not deeply explored.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on reducing children's exposure to smoking. Each turn advanced the target: importance scale → likes/worries → small-step options → concrete plan (9pm alarm, hidden lighters, husband conversation) → barrier planning → recovery line → rehearsal. No topic drift; systematic guidance anchored in the client's stated values.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly co-constructed — options offered as suggestions, selected and modified by the client; plans built from her daily realities. Permission sought before planning steps. Deduction: counselor led the agenda structure (scales, rehearsals, choice menus), making the flow counselor-initiated even when content was client-generated.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Everything framed as the client's choice ("Would you be open to...?", "Which feels most doable?", "You've chosen this"); refusals of options (e.g., the tea suggestion) honored without pushing. Rapid-fire menus and follow-up questions sometimes created subtle pressure; the client twice reasserted boundaries ("I'm still not quitting").

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation effectively drawn from the client: her own reasons, solutions, scripts, and barrier plans. Ruler and confidence scaling elicited self-motivational statements (confidence rose 7 → 8). Deduction: counselor occasionally supplied content herself (suggested scripts, "wait 10" backup) rather than consistently evoking it.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client's contributions were the engine of the plan — she chose the delay strategy, wrote her own scripts, and set her own confidence ratings. Explicit choice questions ("Which of these feels most doable?") demonstrated partnership. Deduction: frequent pre-packaged option menus slightly shifted expertise away from the client.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful importance ruler with the "what would move you from 5 to 7" question eliciting the client's own condition for change. Confidence scaling (7 → 8), elicited rationale for the delay plan, rehearsed scripts, and closing commitment ("my kids won't be watching me light up anymore") reinforced change talk. Change talk centered on protecting kids rather than quitting itself; not deepened into desire/ability language about her own smoking.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ("I just enjoy it," "I'm still not quitting") consistently neutralized with accepting reflections rather than argument; no reinforcing of reasons against change; pivoted toward protective goals. Deduction: sustain talk sometimes fully accommodated ("the cigarettes are staying" accepted without gentle discrepancy-building), limiting ambivalence resolution.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** 4.1/5

**Summary:** A strong, well-structured MI session with a pre-contemplative client. Strengths: excellent direction (a concrete, client-owned plan emerged from a hesitant referral), skillful ruler-based evocation, accurate double-sided reflections of ambivalence, and consistent warmth without judgment. The client's confidence rose from 5 to 8 through her own elaborated plan, and she left with rehearsed scripts and a recovery plan. Areas for growth: reduce reliance on pre-packaged menus and rapid question batteries; deepen emotional reflections before action steps; gently build discrepancy around the client's own smoking; and slow the pace, particularly with less agreeable pre-contemplative clients.
