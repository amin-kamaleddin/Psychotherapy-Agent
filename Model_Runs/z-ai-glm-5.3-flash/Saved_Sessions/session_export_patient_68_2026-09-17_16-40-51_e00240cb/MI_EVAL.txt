# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations ('Nice work, Kevin') and no shaming of Kevin's drinking. However, warmth is somewhat brief and checklist-style rather than deeply person-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections (e.g., 'you're torn — nights out are how you unwind and connect') show empathy, but reflections are often thin or bundled with multiple questions. Emotional nuances like embarrassment, fear of being the last one standing, and his sister's disapproval were rarely reflected back with depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on safer drinking from opening to close, with every turn tied to concrete steps (rulers, buddy plan, alarms, coping line). The session progressed logically from exploration to a specific, testable plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was largely co-constructed from Kevin's own ideas (buddy Dave, water alternation, cheeky text tone). At times the clinician dominated with lists of suggestions and stacked options, tipping the balance toward the counselor's agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Kevin's ownership through choices, accepting his limit of no big promises, and framing the plan as 'not quitting, just protecting the fun.' Kevin explicitly noted he expected a lecture and instead got a plan he chose himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from Kevin himself via rulers, eliciting his own reasons (work, mates, hitting his head, his sister) and self-generated insights. The counselor supplied more practical solutions (alarms, keys, scripts) than ideal in MI, shifting slightly from evoking to advising.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kevin was positioned as the expert, and his contributions genuinely shaped the session (buddy plan, code word, coping line choice). Expert-voice crept in with rapid-fire advice menus offered before fully exploring Kevin's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of importance/confidence rulers and doability questions, with Kevin's change talk clearly growing (importance 4 → confidence 7, 'this weekend, slowing down... I can do that'). Missed opportunities to deepen commitment language, such as exploring why this matters personally.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was mostly avoided being amplified and ambivalence was sometimes reflected neutrally, but Kevin's 'I'm not quitting, that's not happening' received no gentle reframe or double-sided reflection. Little strategic use of double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
