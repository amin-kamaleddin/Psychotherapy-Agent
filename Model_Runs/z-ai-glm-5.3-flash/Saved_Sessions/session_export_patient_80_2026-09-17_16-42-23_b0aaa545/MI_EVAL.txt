# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental stance with genuine affirmations; no disapproval even around past violence or resistance to abstinence. Some affirmations feel formulaic and repetitive, with little explicit normalization of his ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately tracks Luka's triggers, social fears, and insight. Reflections are often brief and paired with questions/advice, and deeper emotional content (shame, fear of failure) is not explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the target behavior—alcohol and aggression on Friday nights—throughout the session. Every turn advances a concrete agenda from trigger identification through to a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided: Luka's ideas (cash limit, refusal lines, partner involvement) drive the plan. The counselor's rapid suggestions and follow-ups occasionally set the pace, making power-sharing strong but not fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions are consistently framed as Luka's own, and his choice of harm reduction over abstinence is respected without pressure. Pre-generated option menus and some telling (e.g., quick tips) somewhat structure his choices in advance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is skillfully drawn from Luka himself, whose commitment statements are strong and self-authored ('Plans I can do. Willpower on its own, that's what's failed me before'). Confidence rulers and open questions elicit client-generated reasons and strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Luka is repeatedly positioned as the expert, and his contributions genuinely shape the session. Reliance on menus of pre-generated options partially assumes the expert role rather than drawing strategies primarily from him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation via open questions, confidence rulers (7→7.5→8), and rehearsal prompts yields strong client-generated commitment language. Luka's move from sustain talk to concrete self-authored commitment is highly effective.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with acceptance and a pivot to harm reduction rather than argument. However, softening relies more on ignoring sustain talk than active techniques like double-sided reflections, and risk language keeps the negative in focus.
