# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, even with defensive and minimizing statements. Viktor noted he came in ready to waste an hour and left with something real — evidence the acceptance landed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and often deeper than surface content, precisely capturing ambivalence (e.g., the two-sided reflection about relaxing vs. tension at home). Viktor repeatedly confirmed understanding ('You got it,' 'that makes sense').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session progressed logically from identifying what matters, to benefits, to a first win, to barriers, and finally to commitment and a concrete next step. The focus stayed on drug use as it relates to the marriage without wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated between equals, with the plan built from Viktor's own ideas rather than counselor prescriptions. Viktor became an active partner, proposing the return appointment himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit autonomy support from the first utterance through the end, with no pressure or imposed goals. Viktor's shift from 'my wife is making me be here' to 'this is actually mine' demonstrates its effectiveness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client via open questions that elicited his own reasons (sleep, guilt, money, the trip). Viktor articulated personal motivations never suggested by the counselor and left with 'an actual plan' instead of 'a guilty feeling.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned Viktor as the expert on his own life, and every key plan element originated with him. His closing comment that 'most of the work is still mine to do' confirms this landed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation producing increasingly strong change talk, from 'I'm willing to give it a month' to 'I'd rather have her than the Fridays.' Each piece of change talk was strategically deepened with follow-up questions rather than left standing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally handled well through acceptance-and-pivot techniques, but early sustain talk ('maybe she just needs to trust me more') was not explicitly softened. Double-sided reflections naming sustain talk directly against change talk were a missed refinement opportunity.
