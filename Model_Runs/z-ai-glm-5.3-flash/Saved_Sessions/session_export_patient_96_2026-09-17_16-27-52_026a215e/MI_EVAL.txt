# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard for Marisol's choice throughout. Validated her irritation with the dentist's tone rather than defending the referral source. Client explicitly affirmed: "you didn't try to talk me out of it" and "thanks for not making it weird."

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured the autonomy theme and irritation (e.g., "you value your choice... the dentist's concern felt overblown"). Reflections stayed at content level, rarely touching deeper emotional meaning; sometimes doubled as summaries followed immediately by menus, limiting space for deeper understanding.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Implicit focus maintained and concrete commitments secured (brushing, twisting reduction, tongue-pause, dentist script, red flags). However, focus drifted from the oral-health target into extended communication coaching; target behavior was never explicitly or collaboratively named.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Co-edited the dentist line until Marisol said "that sounds like me"; affirmed her plan. But interaction was structurally asymmetric—counselor delivered pre-packaged lists while client mostly selected; client's own ideas and knowledge were not invited.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Standout strength. Reinforced ownership consistently ("you've decided to keep it"), offered advice as optional, adapted without pressure when Marisol rejected ideas (fidget toy, rehearsal). Client directly credited this approach.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Good dual-sided evocative opener ("what do you like most... what would you want to protect or change?"). Afterward, motivation was largely counselor-supplied; no values exploration, scaling questions, or open-ended elicitations despite the client's limited intrinsic motivation.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked "Which would be most useful?" and positioned Marisol as decision-maker. Yet session flow was largely counselor-generates-options, client-picks; little explicit acknowledgment that Marisol held expertise about what would work for her.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Evoked some commitment-adjacent language ("I could maybe catch myself more"; "I'll do the brushing thing, maybe ease up on twisting"). Change talk was accepted and affirmed but not deepened—no evocative questions about reasons, no importance/confidence scaling.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk; responded to "That's already decided" with an acceptance-based reframe pivoting to achievable protective behaviors. Never argued with or reinforced sustain talk; initial question inviting sustain talk was a reasonable strategic acknowledgment in pre-contemplation.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 3.7/5**

A competent, warmly non-judgmental session with a pre-contemplative client. Strengths: exemplary acceptance and autonomy support; skillful pivoting from a non-negotiable decision to achievable protective behaviors, yielding small real commitments. Areas for growth: elicit the client's own ideas before offering information; use evocative questions and importance/confidence rulers; keep focus anchored on the target behavior; reflect deeper emotional undercurrents to strengthen empathy.
