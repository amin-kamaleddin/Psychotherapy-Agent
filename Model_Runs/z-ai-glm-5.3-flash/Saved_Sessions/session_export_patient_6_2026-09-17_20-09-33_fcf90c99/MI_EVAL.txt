# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with specific affirmations ('That's real commitment,' 'That's you problem-solving in real time'). No judgment of alcohol use or the near-legal incident; response to initial defensiveness was acceptance, and the closing 'Deal?' reinforced care beyond the problem behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including complex reflections capturing the emotional core (friend pressure heavier than alcohol) and the shift in client language. Fell short of top marks because reflections occasionally restated content verbatim and missed deepening the emotional layer when Alexei admitted 'That night scared me more than I let on.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on preventing alcohol use in social situations tied to soccer goals, with each exchange advancing toward a concrete plan (refusal phrase, memory cue, self-monitoring, follow-up timed to tryout pressure). No aimless drift despite rapport-building moments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership between equals — counselor co-constructed every step and explicitly deferred to client knowledge of his social world and season. The plan was entirely composed of the client's own ideas, and he confirmed authorship ('I kind of figured out my own plan. That felt way better.').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced client ownership and choice ('Your call,' 'What feels like it could work for you?') with no directive language anywhere. Even the follow-up schedule was framed as the client's decision, and his closing reflection confirmed he felt autonomy rather than a lecture.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully drew out the client's own motivations rather than supplying them — connecting alcohol to soccer, eliciting anticipated consequences and self-reflection. Client articulated his own reasons unprompted and attributed the change to his own insight, reflecting textbook evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert ('You're the expert on your friends and your season, so you tell me') and credited him with authorship of insights and the plan. Client contributions substantially shaped session direction, including check-in timing, the refusal script, and the memory cue.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk throughout — asking about past successful pushback elicited the client's own solution, and highlighting his language shift amplified commitment. Change talk was abundant, progressive, and clearly a product of counselor evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely avoided reinforcing sustain talk, using double-sided reflections and letting the client draw his own conclusions from the park incident. Slight deduction for early reflections briefly mirroring minimization without an immediate strategic pivot, and friend-pressure sustain talk explored at length when it could have been redirected more efficiently.
