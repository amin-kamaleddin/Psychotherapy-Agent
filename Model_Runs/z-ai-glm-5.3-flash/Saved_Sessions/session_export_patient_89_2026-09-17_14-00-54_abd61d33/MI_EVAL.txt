# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating frustration and normalizing weight regain as common rather than a personal failure. Warmth is genuine but slightly formulaic and checklist-like at times, preventing a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures emotional content, e.g., 'That sounds exhausting — it's understandable you feel frustrated.' Reflections are brief, quickly pivot to advice-giving, and miss deeper emotional exploration (e.g., family dynamics, the wife's comment).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains consistent focus on increasing physical activity from start to finish, with every exchange advancing the target behavior. Culminates in concrete commitments, a check-in structure, and a follow-up plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly invites Marcus's input and co-shapes the plan around his preferences (walking, dancing, family nights). Collaboration is uneven, however, as fully-formed plans and menus of tips are often presented rather than brainstormed together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames activities as choices ('no pressure, just fun') and lets Marcus select his own activities, nights, and music, reinforcing ownership. Dense advice-giving and offers to do research slightly tip toward directing rather than consistently reinforcing self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation is largely assumed rather than drawn out — Marcus's own reasons (family joy, hope) emerge spontaneously and are celebrated but never systematically explored. No evocative questions, importance/confidence scaling, or elaboration of stated values, with frequent advice crowding out opportunities for Marcus to articulate his motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for Marcus's input on choosing activities, showing some power-sharing. Overall stance is noticeably expert-driven, with prescriptive plans supplied before inquiry into Marcus's own ideas, and his expertise rarely acknowledged explicitly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively capitalizes on Marcus's spontaneous change talk by concretizing and amplifying his commitments. However, change talk is mostly client-generated; few open questions, rulers, or deepening of rich moments (e.g., wife's happiness being 'worth it') are used to evoke it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk, meeting past-failure statements with normalization and gently preempting future doubt. Handling is largely passive rather than strategically skillful, lacking double-sided reflections or deliberate pivoting of doubt language toward change reasons.
