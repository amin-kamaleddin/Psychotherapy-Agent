# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, explicitly stating there was no judgment in the room, and responded warmly even to the patient's warnings and defensiveness. No judgment, labeling, or disapproval appeared at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured her ambivalence well, including the double-sided reflection about being the mother versus not wanting her son to worry. However, some emotional layers (loneliness, the sting of her son's concern, mild shame about the fall) were touched once but could have been deepened further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, steady focus on alcohol reduction, with every exchange advancing toward the target behavior and ending with a written plan and follow-up in a fortnight. Even the brief detour about the fall and rugs tied back to health risks related to drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was consistently collaborative ('let's figure this out together'), with the plan fully co-constructed from Evelyn's own ideas like the tonic ritual and ringing her sister. Safety advice was offered gently alongside her commitments rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly affirmed Evelyn's ownership of the plan ('your own gauge,' 'your own terms') and offered genuine choices, such as whether to write down the plan or keep it in her head. No pressure or persuasion was used, and Evelyn noted she expected a lecture but didn't get one.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from Evelyn herself, with no lecturing about guidelines or risks; questions led her to her own realizations ('I never really put those two things together before'). This was textbook evocation, producing her own plan rather than an imposed one.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Evelyn as the expert, crediting her with authorship of the plan ('nobody talked you into anything today — you weighed it yourself'). Her own ideas (tea/tonic ritual, phone-a-friend strategy) became part of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was systematically evoked through questions like 'how would you like things to look for yourself?' and 'what would make it feel doable?', leading Evelyn to commit to concrete steps ('two evenings without wine this week'). The abundant client-generated change talk and plan were clearly the result of skillful prompting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections effectively acknowledged sustain talk without amplifying it, and the counselor reframed rather than argued when Evelyn defended her drinking ('seven drinks isn't a problem'). Points were lost because sustain talk was sometimes restated verbatim ('it's just a glass of wine') rather than quickly pivoting toward change.
