# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful tone throughout; values work and practice performance affirmed ("Those values are strong anchors—nice work"; "You've practiced well—trust that steady voice"). No judgment or labeling. However, warmth is brief and formulaic, with rapid pivots to prescribing solutions rather than savoring the client's progress in depth. Deeper unconditional positive regard around guilt-proneness and people-pleasing history was never explored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections ("aligning choices with your values and protecting your self-respect"), but much of the session skips reflection in favor of scripted instruction. Emotionally rich disclosures — "guilt is my weak spot," "that last one made my heart race" — were not reflected or explored; missed opportunities to mirror her pride and fear limited empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on the target behavior — boundary-setting aligned with stated values of self-respect and honesty. Every turn advanced the agenda: values naming, scenario selection, script choice, live role-play with pushbacks, real-world message template, and a concrete follow-up plan. Logical, efficient movement from exploration to rehearsal to action planning without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (asking which focus feels most helpful; offering role-play vs. drafting options), but overall flow was counselor-as-instructor: menus of scripts, decision rules, and checklists delivered in bulk. Elena's suggestions were accepted but not integrated into a co-created plan; the power balance tilted toward the counselor's expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices offered at several points ("Name core values now, or pick a recent misaligned choice?"), supporting some perception of choice. However, choice architecture was narrow and content heavily prescribed — all scripts, decision rules, tone instructions, and somatic techniques were supplied by the counselor without asking what the client thought would work for her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation resided almost entirely in the client and was largely untapped. The counselor did not ask why the values matter, what boundary-keeping would mean for her life, or her confidence level. Rich self-knowledge volunteered by the client ("guilt is my weak spot," "not shrinking myself to keep the peace") was never drawn out or expanded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role, launching into pre-packaged plans and dense lists of scripts and tips delivered prescriptively. Although the client offered good material (her own scripts, her over-explaining habit, her template preference), her contributions did not shape the session structure; direction was counselor-driven throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client spontaneously offered abundant change talk ("I'd like to keep building on what I've started," "This actually feels doable"), but the counselor rarely amplified or deepened it, responding with more scripts and task lists instead of reflections or evocative questions. Momentum was largely self-generated; the counselor's contribution was procedural rather than evocative.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk in the session, and none was reinforced. Client doubts ("saying it out loud feels awkward," "made my heart race") were redirected to practice and small next steps — appropriate but somewhat passive. Missed chances to actively reframe the awkwardness as growth or normalize discomfort, rather than simply bypass it.
