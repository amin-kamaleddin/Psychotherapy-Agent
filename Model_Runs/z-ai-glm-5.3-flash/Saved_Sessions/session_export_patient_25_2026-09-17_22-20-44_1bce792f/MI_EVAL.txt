# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard throughout, including nonjudgmental handling of the alcohol disclosure. Notable affirmations and normalization of imperfection (e.g., 'one night that doesn't go perfectly isn't failure, it's just Friday').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both sides of ambivalence and core values (e.g., the son copying the good parts). Reflections build on the client's exact words and advance understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use at social/work dinners with no topic drift. Every exchange advances the arc toward a concrete self-generated plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functions between equals, with the client generating all content and decisions. The client explicitly notes the difference from a expected lecture: 'a plan I actually made myself.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces choice and ownership ('That's your plan,' 'on your own terms') and offers no directives or prescriptive advice. Setbacks are reframed without judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within the client—his reasons, strategies, and visualizations—never supplied by the counselor. Evocative questions build progressively deeper change talk culminating in 'He just needed a plan.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor positions the client as the expert from the first turn and every plan element originates from him. The client's closing thanks for 'not handing me answers' confirms genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and developed via open questions targeting strengths and possibilities. This elicits sustained commitment language ('I'll tell her tonight, actually,' 'I'm playing the long game').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally met with double-sided reflections and pivots toward change rather than elaboration. Minor refinement needed: occasionally the counselor restates the client's caution ('you're not promising perfection') without actively reframing it toward confidence.
