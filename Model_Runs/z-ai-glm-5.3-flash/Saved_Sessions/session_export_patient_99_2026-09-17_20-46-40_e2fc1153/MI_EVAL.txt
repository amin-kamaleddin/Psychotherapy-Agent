# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard with no judgment, labels, or corrections about the client's drinking, even when minimization appeared.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections throughout, with the counselor catching subtle cues like 'mostly' and the client's 'huh,' and each reflection eliciting client elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong focus maintained on alcohol with clear progression to a concrete plan, slightly reduced by one values question that briefly confused the client and required clarification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated between equals with explicit permission-seeking, agenda choice, and co-constructed solutions built on the client's own ideas (telling Jake, capping Wednesdays).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership repeatedly reinforced through genuine choices and explicit statements that the client is the only one who can decide what feels worth it; no pressure or moralizing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice or external persuasion was offered; motivation grew from the client's own reasons (ankle injury, study group, soccer) with visible deepening from denial to a self-initiated plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located with the client ('I'd rather hear it from you'), and the plan that emerged (Wednesday cap, telling Jake) was entirely client-authored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of change talk through open questions about how drinking fits with goals, followed by active reinforcement of commitment language, deepening to a specific plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (e.g., 'social suicide') was acknowledged briefly then redirected to collaborative problem-solving, successfully transforming it into change-supporting insight via the Jake example.
