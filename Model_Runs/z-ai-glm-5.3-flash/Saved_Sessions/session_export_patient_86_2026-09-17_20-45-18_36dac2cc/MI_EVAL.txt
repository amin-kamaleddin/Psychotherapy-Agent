# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly stating 'There's no judgment from me in this space' and never criticizing Marco's smoking, guardedness, or refusal to commit. Affirmations like 'That's real self-knowledge, not failure' reinforced acceptance even of sustain talk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, capturing nuanced shifts like 'those cigarettes went from being your middle finger to life, to being just another thing breaking down.' One notable misstep occurred when Marco noted 'You asked me something like that already,' and his vulnerable 'I'm scared of what I'll see' could have been explored more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician maintained clear focus on smoking cessation, guiding the conversation from deflection through values, barriers, and concrete next steps. Repetition in the final exchanges—Marco explicitly noting a repeated question—slightly diluted momentum and circled back rather than advancing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated between equals with explicit joint framing ('Let's put our heads together'), and every next step originated from Marco: the partner conversation, self-monitoring, and interest in hand aids. Marco's shift from 'I don't like lectures' to engaged planning demonstrates effective power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly affirmed Marco's ownership ('decisions about your health are yours to make, full stop') and honored his refusal to commit without pressure. No advice was imposed, and the single offer of information was client-requested.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was entirely client-sourced through open, evocative questions rather than persuasion. Marco generated his first articulated link between smoking and his values, designed his own plan, and volunteered commitment-like language ('I don't want to turn up here with nothing').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly transferred expertise to Marco ('you just designed a plan smarter than anything the doctors handed you'), which Marco acknowledged directly ('Nobody's ever really asked me what I want... What you're asking though—that's different'). All next steps were built from Marco's own language and ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic future-oriented and evocative questions drew out strong change talk, including Marco's spontaneous connection of smoking to losing workshop time. The session produced concrete outcomes: agreeing to talk with his partner, self-monitoring triggers, and requesting information about aids.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently met with neutral acceptance and effective reframes ('a problem you haven't solved, not one you've given up on'). At times sustain talk was simply accepted and sat with rather than strategically reframed, and the repetitive ending slightly diluted momentum.
