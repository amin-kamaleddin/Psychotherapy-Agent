# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance even when the patient is defensive or dismissive. Falls short of 5 due to sparse, somewhat formulaic affirmations with no explicit acknowledgment of the patient's honesty or willingness to engage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections validate frustration and the value of beer with friends, but the session is dominated by checklists and information delivery. The poignant disclosure about his father's drinking ('that part hits a little') receives no empathic reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use anchored by AUDIT-C screening, risk feedback, and a concrete tracking plan with defined start date and Monday check-in. Not a 5 because direction is rigidly procedural, focused on logging mechanics rather than the patient's own goals and readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers menu options and incorporates the patient's ideas (track first, phone app, weekend start), but the plan, checklists, and review cadence are counselor-supplied rather than co-constructed. Interaction has an expert-driven flavor with limited joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently presents choices, asks permission, and accepts the patient's conditions without a lecture — the patient explicitly notes he expected one. Falls short of 5 because harm-reduction tips and the protocol are delivered prescriptively without reinforcing the patient's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely draws motivation from within; responds to the patient's 'something to think about' with a risk lecture and prescribed plan instead of evocative questions. His own reasons for change (father's drinking, health concerns) surface but are never explored or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for the patient's preference and honors his input on app choice and start day, but the session is largely expert-driven with prescribed protocols and checklists. His stated conditions are accepted but not built upon collaboratively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicits some change talk through offering choices, yielding commitment to tracking and a Monday check-in. However, change talk is never deepened with evocative questions about what would make tracking or cutting back feel worthwhile.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and gently reframes minimization with factual feedback. Misses opportunities for double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk, and the patient's repeated minimization and conditions go largely unaddressed.
