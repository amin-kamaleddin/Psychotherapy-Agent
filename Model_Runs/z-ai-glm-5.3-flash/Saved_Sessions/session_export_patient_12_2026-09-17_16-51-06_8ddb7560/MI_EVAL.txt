# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, honoring Amira's preferences without judgment (e.g., revising the reminder text, accepting any outcome as 'dumb' or 'helped a little'). Amira explicitly confirms she felt neither lectured nor given up on.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture her regret, isolation, and helplessness, but occasionally remain brief — e.g., 'no one really wants to hear from me' was met with logistics rather than deeper reflection of the loneliness and shame beneath it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays anchored and builds concrete momentum to a specific, time-limited experiment with accountability check-in. However, the plan's link back to her drug-use goals is never explicitly reconnected in the second half.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every decision (time, reminder wording, check-in format) is negotiated with Amira, whose preferences shape the final plan. She explicitly recognizes the power-sharing: 'People don't usually do that.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with no-pressure framing, explicit permission to decline, and immediate honoring of her refusal of motivational language. Score limited by somewhat directive delivery of plan details (protocol, fallbacks) rather than letting Amira generate them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions about what matters most and eliciting drawing as a past coping resource evoked desire and commitment talk. Falls short because the counselor sometimes supplies solutions and misses evocative techniques like scaling or values exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently positioned within Amira — she shaped the reminder wording, timing, and check-in structure. She explicitly names this as a difference from past experiences, marking this as the session's strongest domain.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked meaningful change talk ('I don't want to feel like this anymore,' 'I'll actually do it') and reinforced it. Misses a 5 due to lack of commitment amplification (no scaling or exploring what follow-through would mean) and occasionally supplying plan content herself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged without amplification and skillfully reframed (double-sided reflection: 'still trying is meaningful'). Not a 5 because some responses restate negative expectations without strategic reframe and don't link sustain talk back to her own desires.
