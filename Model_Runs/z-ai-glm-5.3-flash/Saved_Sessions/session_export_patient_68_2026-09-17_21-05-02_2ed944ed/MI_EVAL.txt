# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard throughout — 'you're the expert on your own life' and 'you did the hard part, not me.' Patient explicitly noted it didn't feel like a lecture, confirming the nonjudgmental warmth landed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, deep reflections tracking his ambivalence precisely (drinking's 'double duty,' fear of being 'the boring one'). Mirrored his own language consistently.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on drinking from the opening double-sided reflection, converging on concrete next steps (the call, Wednesday football) without drift or premature advice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely equal-partner work — 'let's figure it out together,' with the entire plan built from the patient's own ideas and joint problem-solving of obstacles.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice repeatedly affirmed ('what, if anything...?', 'you get to set your own pace'), accepting his refusal to promise abstinence without pushback. Patient's closing that the plan was 'mine' confirms effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from the patient via evocative questions ('what has ever helped you unwind?') rather than persuasion. He ends self-motivated: 'I can manage that much.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in the client; the plan was entirely his material (football, Daz, public commitment). Closing 'you did the hard part, not me' reinforced it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful open questions progressively strengthened change talk from 'maybe knowing when to slow down' to concrete commitments. Counselor explicitly highlighted the shift as patient-driven.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (social fun, stress relief, fear of being boring) acknowledged without reinforcement and reframed toward change. Sustain talk visibly diminished across the session.
