# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, accepting Daniel's skepticism without pushback. Affirmations were sparse and generic; more specific affirmations of his honesty and self-awareness would strengthen this.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflections of ambivalence and his surprise about drinking totals. Occasionally stayed at surface level, missing deeper exploration of the frustration around his marathon plateau and its connection to drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, sustained focus on the target behavior from exploration through psychoeducation to concrete planning. Each turn moved the session forward, closing with a specific, structured plan and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner, offering options and accepting the client's rejections without pressure. The guidelines summary was largely one-way information delivery rather than interactive exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with repeated choice-offering ('whatever you choose', 'what would you like to try first?'). The plan was entirely assembled from the client's own preferences with no coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the client's own motivations via open questions, future visioning, and the importance ruler, producing genuine change talk. Missed opportunities to amplify these statements and probe why importance was a 4 rather than lower.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Daniel as the expert, with menus rather than prescriptions, and his choices visibly shaped the plan. The guidelines rundown slipped into a lengthy expert-style lecture instead of negotiating how much information he wanted.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked meaningful change talk ('maybe I don't need it to be alcohol every single night', 'tracking wouldn't hurt'). Did not deepen the plateau insight or explicitly elicit commitment language before moving to options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Never argued with or reinforced sustain talk, acknowledging it neutrally, but softening was passive. Rarely used double-sided reflections or reframes linking the client's wind-down needs to his health and fitness values.
