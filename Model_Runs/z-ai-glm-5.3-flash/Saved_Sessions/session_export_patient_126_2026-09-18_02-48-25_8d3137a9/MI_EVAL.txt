# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, including during the patient's most dismissive moments; resistance and boundaries were validated without judgment. The patient's escalating openness suggests acceptance was genuinely received.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every response contained an accurate reflection, most capturing meaning beneath surface content (e.g., naming the true function of the smoking routine and the identity theme behind 'sticking to something'). No misreads or course corrections needed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear strategic arc from minimization to a specific testable behavior and values-level motivation, with focus maintained throughout. Held at 4 because the alcohol component—one of two referral concerns—was named only once and then abandoned, never revisited or seeded even in the closing summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine meeting of equals—patient contributions visibly redirected the session and became the entire action architecture. Minor deductions for a closed either/or question (Turn 3) and double-barreled questions (Turns 5-6) that lightly structured responses.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Zero direct advice or persuasion across the session; every idea and plan originated with the patient, whose explicit limits were actively ratified. Ownership was reinforced at every critical juncture, keeping the change process self-authored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor supplied no reasons for change at any point—every motivation (son copying him, fitness for footy, self-image as a follow-through person) was drawn out and developed. The patient's closing 'it matters more than I gave it credit for' is direct evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was repeatedly and explicitly located within the patient, and the session visibly ran on his contributions—nothing was prescribed. Closing questions were genuinely open with autonomy-preserving hedges.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful, well-sequenced evocative questions moved a precontemplative patient through every change talk category (DARN-C), from desire/need to identity-level commitment. Change talk was consistently reflected and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk (father's longevity, social life refusal, 'not there yet') was handled flawlessly—never argued with or reinforced. Double-sided reflections acknowledged the sustain side first before joining it to the change side, with skillful pivoting toward the controllable element.
