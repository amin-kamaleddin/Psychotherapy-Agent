# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, normalizing Diego's situation and responding to his heroin disclosure with harm-reduction support rather than disapproval. Warmth was present but affirmations felt somewhat formulaic and could be more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and captured Diego's ambivalence well (e.g., 'using to cope, not ready to quit, but worried'). However, reflections sometimes served as transitions to advice rather than deepening emotional understanding, leaving Diego's fear and weeks of avoidance unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on the harm-reduction target with each turn advancing the agenda — from exploration to Narcan rehearsal to safer injecting planning. The session progressed logically with clear momentum, ending in specific committed actions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The exchange was largely between equals, with the psychiatrist seeking Diego's input at each decision point and role-playing the housemate at his request. Bulk delivery of a seven-item harm-reduction list slightly tilted the exchange toward clinician-as-expert rather than co-creating knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Diego consistently owned every decision — choosing priorities, scripts, and next steps — while the psychiatrist offered options and returned choices to him ('Which feels safer for you right now?'). His autonomous decisions clearly drove the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Diego's own reasons and priorities ('What would feel most helpful to add?'), with the rehearsal drawing out his own words and solutions. Missed opportunities to probe deeper into why survival and change matter to him personally or to affirm his stated readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Diego as the decision-maker and asked permission before sharing information. Advice-giving was occasionally dense (a seven-item list), edging toward expert-driven delivery, though Diego's choices consistently shaped the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked and built on Diego's commitment language ('I'm serious about doing both') through questions that invited his own priorities. Lacked deliberate deepening — no evocative questions about personal meaning, confidence rulers, or affirmation of his 10/10 readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, but also did not actively soften it — no double-sided reflections acknowledging heroin's escape function while pivoting toward change. Sustain talk was reflected neutrally and passed over rather than strategically integrated.
