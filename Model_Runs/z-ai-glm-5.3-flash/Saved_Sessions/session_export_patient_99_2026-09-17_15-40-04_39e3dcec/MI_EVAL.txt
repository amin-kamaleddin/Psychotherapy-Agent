# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with consistent affirmations of the patient's honesty and insight, despite his minimizing stance. Warmth was somewhat brief and formulaic, and ambivalence was not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate, capturing both content and ambivalence in good summaries. However, reflections sometimes repeated earlier material verbatim rather than deepening, and the patient's mild surprise at his drinking totals was not explored emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus maintenance throughout, with every exchange tied back to drinking and ankle recovery. The session ended with a concrete, specific behavioral plan and follow-up without ever drifting aimlessly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed — the patient chose the water alternation, four-drink cap, decline script, and check-in himself. The psychiatrist's ideas were offered as options rather than prescriptions, and patient input shaped the session at every step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with repeated permission-seeking, choice-offering, and acceptance of the patient's ankle-focused framing and flexible commitment. More explicit reinforcement of his ownership of the change process and long-term direction would have strengthened this further.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist effectively drew out the patient's own motivations, confidence rating, barriers, and solutions, yielding meaningful change talk. Emerging hope ('maybe I'll sleep better') was not elaborated, and option lists replaced open evocation at times.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient substantially shaped the plan (ankle excuse, water trick, four-drink target), and the psychiatrist repeatedly asked what felt most doable. The reliance on prescriptive option menus occasionally shifted toward an advice-giving expert role rather than fully eliciting his ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong change talk evocation through open questions, confidence scaling, and barrier exploration, with the patient producing meaningful change talk and generating his own coping plan. Option menus sometimes replaced open evocation, and tentative change talk was not deepened into elaborated motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk and minimization were met with neutral, double-sided reflections rather than argument, and the psychiatrist consistently pivoted toward what was doable. However, repeatedly re-presenting the four-drink cap and skipping nights after the patient pushed back briefly re-invoked sustain talk instead of fully pivoting to his self-generated solutions.
