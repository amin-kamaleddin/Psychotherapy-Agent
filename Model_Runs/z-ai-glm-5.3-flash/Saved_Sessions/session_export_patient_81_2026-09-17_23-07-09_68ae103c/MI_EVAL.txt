### Acceptance

**Comments:** Non-defensive when corrected about meth vs. drinking, warmly thanked the client, and explicitly rejected a fixing stance. Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, even when the client minimized or was defensive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections repeatedly captured the emotional core (e.g., 'flat' as 'almost empty,' Friday fear as fear of being left out). The client responded with self-discovery, confirming the reflections landed and deepened insight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear progressive focus on meth use and related social/financial patterns, building from callback memory to concrete action steps. Addressed the alcohol confusion immediately and kept the session on target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered hypotheses tentatively and let the client's own material shape the session's direction. The client's ideas visibly determined what happened next throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively reinforced the client's ownership, noting he came up with every plan element himself. Asked rather than told, and left all decisions with the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never supplied reasons for change; open questions drew out the client's own values, desires, and confidence. The closing self-generated commitment language was the hallmark of strong evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated the client as the expert on his own life, with the money plan, Dean text, and Thursday dinner all originating from the client. Genuinely conveyed that wisdom about change resides within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions like 'what's one evening that felt good?' and 'what does Dean say back?' elicited escalating client-generated change talk. Each exchange ended with the client producing a next step himself.

#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('not out of control,' fear of being left out) was acknowledged accurately then gently pivoted toward change, never argued with. Redirecting from 'can't picture saying no' led directly to the Dean dinner idea.
