# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental toward a defensive client, acknowledging pressure and honoring non-negotiables. Warmth occasionally felt formulaic, with affirmations somewhat generic and limited individualized praise of Richard's candor.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, accurately capturing ambivalence (choice vs. love for his wife) and his emotional vulnerability about his wife's tears. Reflections sometimes summarized content rather than deepening emotional meaning, leaving moments of vulnerability and pride unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking throughout, converting an angry, deflecting opening into a concrete behavioral plan by session's end. Progress relied substantially on counselor-provided menus and checklists rather than momentum built from Richard's own values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acted as an equal partner, offering choices about direction and co-constructing the plan around Richard's self-set limits and wording. Occasionally slipped into a checklist/advice structure (practical tips, ashtray suggestions) that was delivered rather than negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly and consistently reinforced Richard's ownership with choice-based questions, non-pressuring language, and acceptance of his 'no promises' stance. Richard's closing behavior confirmed his autonomy was genuinely preserved and experienced as such.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clearly elicited motivation from Richard's own values — his wife, the marriage, keeping the peace — with his surprise at leaving with a plan evidencing successful evocation. Occasionally supplied coping ideas (patch, walk, craving tips) rather than drawing out Richard's own, and did not explicitly elicit his confidence or hope.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Richard as the expert; his self-named plan (porch-only, minus one, his own script wording) became the intervention. At times front-loaded menus and checklists and offered expert-driven practical suggestions before Richard had chosen.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked Richard's own change language by linking change to his relational motivation and reframing 'quitting' into his tolerable step ('easing her worry'). Generated concrete, self-generated commitments (porch-only, minus one, a rehearsed script, a tentative follow-up goal) and reinforced them through choice and ease-based questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently met sustain talk with acceptance and redirection rather than debate, and reframing quitting into smaller steps effectively dissolved resistance. Some reflections briefly restated sustain talk without an immediate pivot, and a few practical tips shifted slightly from evocation to advice-giving.
