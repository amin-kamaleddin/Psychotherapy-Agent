# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard despite Marcus's guarded, defensive presentation; owned his own error ('that was a jump, my bad') and offered genuine, well-calibrated affirmations without condescension.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening captured both content and emotion accurately, including complex reflections like 'that line you drew — that's you already knowing how to protect yourself.' Repaired empathy ruptures immediately when Marcus pushed back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear progressive arc (upsides/downsides → driving incident → coping plans → future vision), but the premature jump from Danny's driving to Marcus's own drinking and a triple-barreled closing question slightly disrupted focus and momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative interaction with a co-constructed agenda; Marcus's ideas visibly shaped session content, and the psychiatrist adapted in real time to his objections. Genuine equality of power rather than adult-over-adolescent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy explicitly reinforced throughout: 'Not for your dad, not for me — just for you?' framed change as Marcus's own choice. Questions were invitations, with no advice, warnings, or pressure anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** All change rationale was drawn out via open questions rather than supplied externally, producing three self-generated goals. The direct attempt to bridge peer-risk thinking to Marcus's own drinking triggered defensive pushback, showing evocation wasn't fully attuned to his readiness level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly located expertise in Marcus ('You're the expert on your own life') and repeatedly elicited his own definitions and solutions, which became the session's content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Future-oriented questions produced Marcus's strongest change talk and three self-generated reasons for change, reinforced with affirmations. Evocation worked mostly through the driving/safety door rather than directly engaging his own drinking, and the one direct bridge backfired.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argued with sustain talk; reflected minimization neutrally and pivoted to future/values questions. However, sustain talk dominated much of Marcus's speech, and some reflections briefly amplified the minimization before redirecting — an earlier double-sided pivot could have shifted the balance more decisively.
