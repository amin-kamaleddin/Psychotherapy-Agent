# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental regard throughout. Affirmations were specific and genuine ("That takes guts to admit"). Client noted he expected "some lecture" and didn't get one—strong evidence acceptance was communicated effectively. No judgment, labeling, or disapproval present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally attuned, moving beyond content to meaning. Captured both sides of ambivalence and reflected deeper material ("It sounds like respect for yourself"). Reflections consistently landed—client built on them rather than correcting them.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Clear focus on target behavior (alcohol use/pacing at parties) from the first utterance. Each exchange moved progressively: ambivalence → past successes → concrete plan → confidence → values → maintenance. Guided purposefully without being directive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Fully collaborative—every plan element (three-drink limit, buddy accountability, tracking, check-ins) originated from the client, with the clinician asking rather than prescribing. Tone was that of two equals exploring the problem together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced client ownership ("What's your take on this?", "a plan that's yours"). Accepted the client's boundary that quitting entirely was off the table without pushback. No advice, pressure, or control language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Motivation drawn entirely from within the client—fear of arrest, valuing education, self-respect—all elicited via open questions and strategic reflection, then amplified. The econ analogy was the client's own discovery prompted by a skillfully evocative question.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in the client ("it came from you, not from anyone telling you it should matter"). Client's closing comment—leaving with "a plan I actually made myself"—directly confirms power-sharing occurred.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Session's greatest strength. Skillful evocative questions elicited rich commitment language: "I actually wanna try it. Starting this weekend," "For me," "I wanna give it a real shot." Change talk reinforced with reflections and affirmations each time it emerged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **4**
**Comments:** Sustain talk acknowledged briefly and neutrally without amplification, then redirected toward change. The double-sided opening reflection was a textbook handling of ambivalence. Room to grow: more active strategic softening (e.g., reframing the "boring one" fear) rather than relying mostly on acceptance.

---

**Overall Score: 4.9/5**

**Summary:** Excellent MI session with near-textbook execution across all domains. Opening double-sided reflection framed ambivalence without judgment; change talk escalated naturally from tentative possibility to firm commitment ("starting this weekend... for me"). Minor room for growth in actively softening sustain talk, but otherwise an outstanding performance.
