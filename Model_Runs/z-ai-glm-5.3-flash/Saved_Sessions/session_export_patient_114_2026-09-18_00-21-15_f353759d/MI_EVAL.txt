# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician unconditionally accepts Ryan, including his ambivalence and reluctance to commit to failed strategies like hard stops, affirming his worth throughout. Ryan's increasing openness ('weird', 'that landed') reflects deep felt acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture both sides of Ryan's ambivalence (e.g., 'sober Ryan has the plan, drunk Ryan doesn't care'). The clinician accurately mirrors content and emotion, helping Ryan feel understood and heard.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is purposefully steered toward change via open questions ('what's your take...'), each answer building toward a concrete, self-generated plan. Direction is achieved collaboratively rather than through instruction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly defers to Ryan's expertise ('you know yourself better than I ever could') and frames every question as 'your take.' Ryan generates all ideas himself, with the clinician as a guide rather than director.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ryan's autonomy is emphasized throughout; plans are framed as his own ('that came entirely from you'), and his right to choose is implicit and respected. The clinician never prescribes a solution, only invites Ryan's judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully evokes Ryan's own motivations—protecting friends, texting his mom, education—and connects change talk to values he already holds. Ryan's insight about 'treating future Ryan like one of my boys' emerges entirely from elicitation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Each exchange builds on Ryan's previous statements, with questions flowing naturally from his words (e.g., 'never blown off' → friends/mom → self-protection). The partnership deepens visibly as Ryan moves from 'I don't know' to 'I can actually do that.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently invites and amplifies change talk, then reflects and reinforces it ('listen to what you just said: I can actually do that'). Sustain talk is met with acceptance, never confrontation, making room for shift.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (hard stops fail, drunk Ryan resists rules, plans feel like homework) is acknowledged non-judgmentally and used as material for exploration. The clinician's acceptance of these concerns allowed Ryan to work through rather than defend them.
