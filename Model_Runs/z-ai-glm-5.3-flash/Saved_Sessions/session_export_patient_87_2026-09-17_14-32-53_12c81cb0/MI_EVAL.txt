# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; normalized the birthday indulgence ('neither is failure') and affirmed commitments. However, affirmations were brief and missed key moments like Marco's honesty, his 'choosing vs. forcing' insight, and his confidence rising to six and a half.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered accurate content summaries but largely skipped emotional content in favor of practical problem-solving. Marco's expressions of shame and fear of judgment ('I keep waiting for someone to tell me all the ways I'm going to screw up') were met with scripts rather than reflections of vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on evening eating and post-dinner activity throughout, with no topic drift. The session progressed logically to a concrete, time-bound plan with specific nights, scripts, and coping strategies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Surface collaboration existed—options were offered and preferences accommodated—but the counselor set the agenda each turn and generated all strategies. Valuable client material (his workplace countdown trick, the 'experiment vs. permanent diet' framing) was not integrated into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and gave explicit permission regarding the birthday, reinforcing Marco's autonomy ('then I decide'). However, choices were frequently between counselor-generated options, and the volume of prescribed tips tilted toward advice-giving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was supplied through menus of tips rather than drawn out from Marco; almost no evocative questions were asked. His own motivations (doing it 'for my kids,' pride in cutting soda, first-ever disclosure) surfaced spontaneously but were never elicited, reflected, or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and invited input, but the interaction was predominantly counselor-driven, with nearly every turn ending in supplied tips and scripts. Marco's self-generated idea (the countdown trick from work) was noted but not built upon, positioning expertise with the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged and some was affirmed ('That's a solid commitment'), but no deliberate evocative questions deepened it. Marco's confidence increase from six to six and a half went completely unremarked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (the couch, 'start Monday,' fear his wife would see it as 'just another phase') was acknowledged but not strategically softened. No double-sided reflections paired sustain talk with his motivations; only the birthday worry was well handled.
