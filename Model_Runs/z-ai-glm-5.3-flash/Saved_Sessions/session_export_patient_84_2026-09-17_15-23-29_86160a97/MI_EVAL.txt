# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with consistent acceptance statements ('That makes sense,' 'Sounds fair,' 'I hear you'). However, acceptance feels somewhat formulaic, with little explicit warmth, affirmation of strengths, or normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening accurately tracks Malik's concerns about unfairness and conflict with his mother. Reflections stay largely at the content level, missing deeper emotional themes such as fear of breaking promises and desire for trust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, coherent focus evolving from defensiveness to a concrete negotiation plan and rehearsal, anchored to Malik's chosen goal of reducing mother conflict. Some drift occurred as the cannabis use itself was almost entirely set aside in favor of the agreement work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Inclusive language and incorporation of Malik's edits show some power-sharing, but the pattern is counselor-generated solutions that Malik reacts to and tweaks. Malik's contributions were reactive edits more than co-creative inputs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly affirmed ('It's your choice') with permission sought before sharing information and no pressure to commit to abstinence. Autonomy support is diluted by heavy advice-giving that subtly undercuts Malik's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplies the motivation machinery—reasons, plans, scripts, and compromises—rather than drawing motivation from Malik. Tentative change talk ('worth a shot I guess') was met with logistics instead of being developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Malik's input is invited and his ideas shaped the final agreement, but the counselor frequently assumes the expert role with pre-packaged solutions. The overall pattern is counselor-generated options that Malik reacts to and edits.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited, including Malik saying 'if this stops the fighting, worth a shot I guess,' but it was never deliberately reinforced, deepened, or summarized. No exploration of importance, confidence, or commitment language occurred.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Malik's frequent sustain talk was neither reinforced nor actively softened—no double-sided reflections, reframing, or pivots back to change. The counselor accommodated sustain talk by structuring the agreement around it, leaving the ambivalence about use itself unexplored.
