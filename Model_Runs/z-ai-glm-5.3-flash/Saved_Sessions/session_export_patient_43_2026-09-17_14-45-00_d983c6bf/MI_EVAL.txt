# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout and offered affirmations without arguing against the client's skepticism. Some affirmations felt formulaic, and ambivalence was not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections captured Eric's torn feelings, pride in his work, and preference for personal evidence. Reflections tapered in the middle-late session, missing deeper emotional content like resentment toward his wife and fear beneath his minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on the smoking target behavior with every response tied to concrete next steps. The session progressed logically to a specific, measurable plan without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Built the plan from Eric's stated interests, and his own ideas substantially shaped the outcome (he chose the self-experiment and designed his template). The collaborative stance weakened when the counselor delivered information blocks and tip lists without checking first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed decisions as Eric's choice and honored his framing of change as a limited experiment, including his request not to tell his wife. Frequent option menus and prescriptive tips slightly constrained rather than opened choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Eric through open questions and confidence scaling, with his own reasons driving the plan. Evocation tapered into practical planning, and the personal meaning beneath his minimization and 'prove everyone wrong' stance went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited Eric's input, but repeatedly adopted an expert role with detailed information, prescriptive coping tips, and a supplied tracking template. The flow was often counselor-driven option menus rather than genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through open questions and confidence scaling, with Eric progressing from confidence 5 to 7 and readiness 8 while generating his own plan elements. Change talk was partly elicited via directive option-offering, and his limited commitment ('no promises past the week') was left unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing, but several reflections actively amplified it (e.g., echoing 'enjoy smoking to unwind') without strategic double-sided reflections or pivots toward change. Key minimizations like 'I'll probably feel exactly the same' went unaddressed.
