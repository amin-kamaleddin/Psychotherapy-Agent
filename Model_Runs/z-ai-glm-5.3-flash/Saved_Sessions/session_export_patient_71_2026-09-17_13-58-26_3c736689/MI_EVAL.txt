# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, non-judgmental tone with occasional affirmations, but warmth is limited and interactions feel transactional. Repeated safety screening interjections at awkward moments disrupt rapport.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; Laura's rich material (fear of the snowball pattern, feeling grounded from yoga, self-love values) is rarely reflected back. The counselor responds to emotional disclosures with advice lists instead of reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, consistent focus on a concrete target behavior (one-minute post-yoga breathing habit with fallback and tracking). However, direction comes from steering toward a pre-selected solution rather than guiding Laura through her own change process.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is uneven, with the counselor delivering numbered lists, scripts, and checklists in an expert-to-client format while Laura acts as a compliant recipient. Laura's ideas are accommodated but never elicited, and the agenda is not negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy support exists through choice questions and affirming Laura's decision to start small. However, choices are offered within a counselor-defined menu, advice is given without permission, and Laura's own options are not explored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Laura's motivations (yoga's grounding effect, health as top value, self-love) are present but never deliberately explored or deepened. The counselor assumes motivation and moves immediately to solution delivery rather than evoking Laura's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes a clear expert role with bulleted instruction lists dominating nearly every turn. Laura's genuine insights come from her despite, not because of, counselor facilitation, and her own ideas about what would work are never solicited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Laura spontaneously offers substantial change talk, but the counselor neither evokes nor deepens it — no open questions about importance, confidence, or reasons for change. Change talk is passively received rather than cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor briefly names the all-or-nothing voice but pivots to more instructions rather than strategically softening ambivalence. No double-sided reflections or exploration of sustain talk's meaning; it is overridden with prescriptive content rather than used to build discrepancy.
