# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed warmth and unconditional positive regard, never arguing or expressing disapproval even during patient pushback. Daniel's defensiveness visibly softened over the session, indicating he felt fully accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, capturing both sides of ambivalence (e.g., the valued nightly ritual vs. the 'what if I ran even faster?' question). Complex reflections connected material the patient hadn't linked, deepening his insight each time.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, progressive focus on alcohol reduction from start to finish, with each exchange building deliberate momentum toward a concrete change commitment. There was no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned as a true partnership, with the plan co-constructed from Daniel's fitness-oriented worldview rather than imposed clinical advice. Daniel increasingly led while the counselor shaped process, not content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently reinforced ('It's yours - weeknights off, weekends intact'), with the experiment framing preserving the patient's ownership. The counselor never pressured, and affirmed Daniel's right to return to his routine without pushback.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from within the client via strategic open questions, with no advice-giving or persuasion. Daniel articulated his own reasons, resources, and self-efficacy, including his own philosophy for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned Daniel as the expert on his own life, and the patient substantially shaped the session by designing the experiment, identifying metrics, and setting the start date from his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked across desire, ability, reasons, and commitment through open questions, then reflected back and deepened. Daniel progressively owned the plan himself ('Monday. I can do that').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without argument and never reinforced, using double-sided reflections to hold both the value of the ritual and curiosity about potential. The counselor consistently pivoted toward the patient's curiosity and goals without letting sustain talk dominate.
