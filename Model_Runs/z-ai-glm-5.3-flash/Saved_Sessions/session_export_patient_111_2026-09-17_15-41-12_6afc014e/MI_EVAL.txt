# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Richard's hostility and defensiveness without criticism. Fell short of 5 due to limited explicit affirmations of him as a person beyond his immediate statements.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflections that captured both sustain talk and the meaning of his 40-year ritual. Missed opportunities to deepen emotionally, particularly around the wife's brother's death and Richard's fear of loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on smoking throughout, guiding the session from resistance to a concrete, actionable plan with specific counts, locations, coping tools, and a script for his wife. Every exchange advanced the session toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission, offered choices, and co-constructed the plan using Richard's own ideas (reading pack, gum placement). The 'no-nonsense plan' and wife script were somewhat counselor-delivered rather than fully co-developed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Richard's control with choice offers, respected his boundaries ('no lectures,' 'don't call the house'), and honored his self-imposed limits ('I'm not promising that'). His decision framing was fully preserved.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out Richard's own motivation ('I don't want to lose her') via values and confidence questions. Some motivation was channeled through counselor-supplied options, and elaborative change talk wasn't systematically evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Richard as the expert ('You're in charge of what we talk about') and his ideas substantially shaped the final plan. A shift into advice-giving with a pre-packaged five-option list slightly reduced client-driven solution generation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questions (what would need to be different, 0–10 confidence) elicited substantial change talk that was well-reflected and consolidated. Lacked deepening through elaboration questions and exploration of importance alongside confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, but handled it neutrally or bypassed it rather than using double-sided reflections to pivot toward change. The approach worked in practice, but the pivot-and-reframe repertoire was limited.
