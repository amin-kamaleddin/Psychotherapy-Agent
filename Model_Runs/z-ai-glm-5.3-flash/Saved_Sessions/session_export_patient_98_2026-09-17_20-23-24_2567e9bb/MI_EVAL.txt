# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard with frequent genuine affirmations ('Those are beautiful goals, Mateo'). Ambivalence is normalized and honored without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening capturing both emotion and meaning, e.g., 'what you're really after isn't just less pain, it's getting your life back.' Skillfully reframes 'slow' into 'still moving.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus on increasing physical activity despite pain. Every exchange advances deliberately from barriers to a concrete plan with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative interaction between equals, with the plan co-constructed from the client's own material (pacing, the block loop, success criteria). The counselor builds on and explicitly acknowledges Mateo's contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice imposed; ownership consistently reinforced with questions like 'What's your sense of that balance?' The session closes by having Mateo notice his own motivational shift ('my plan, really').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every reason for change, solution, and coping strategy originates from the client, who concludes 'I sort of talked myself into it.' Motivation is systematically elicited rather than supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Mateo is actively positioned as the expert and explicitly recognizes it: 'You didn't hand it to me.' The counselor's contributions amplify rather than direct the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation and deepening of change talk through open questions that produced strong client commitment language ('I could try one loop this week'). Each instance is reflected and amplified, ending with the shift from 'supposed to' to 'want to.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is briefly acknowledged then pivoted toward change via double-sided reflections and reframes (doing less 'wouldn't be slacking'; the worry becomes 'part of the plan itself'). Reasons against change are never reinforced.
