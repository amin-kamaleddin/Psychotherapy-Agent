# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with multiple affirmations and no lecturing despite the drinking context. Some affirmations are exaggerated or slightly patronizing ('That's a kid who knows where she's headed'), risking a performative tone with an adolescent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and complex, accurately capturing ambivalence and emotional shifts. The counselor sometimes moves too quickly from reflection to the next question, leaving emotionally weightier material (fear of being 'boring,' the 'gross feeling' of regret) underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on Anya's approach to alcohol at parties, with each exchange building purposefully toward concrete implementation (the line, success criteria, contingency planning). No topical drift, and direction is maintained through the closing check for anything 'unfinished or wobbly.'

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative with every plan element originating from Anya and the closing agenda handed to her. The counselor never introduces content Anya didn't generate, and the power balance is genuinely equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is actively reinforced as the plan is framed throughout as Anya's creation fitting her values, with no prescribing or pressure. The client explicitly confirms the intended effect: 'That made it feel like my plan, not something I was told to do.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is masterfully drawn from the client via techniques like the advice-to-a-friend reversal and the 'what changed between then and now' question, eliciting intrinsic reasons rather than imposing external ones. Anya explicitly credits the approach: 'You just asked questions and I ended up answering them myself.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly and explicitly steps out of the expert role ('You know yourself best here,' 'You did the work; I just held the space'), with every plan element originating from Anya. The client recognizes and values this: 'You just asked questions and I ended up answering them myself.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding evocation and development of change talk — asking which idea 'feels most like you,' eliciting predictions, defining success, and the advanced advice-to-a-friend technique. Anya's language shows a clear arc from 'I guess I could...' to 'I actually feel ready.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled effectively by redirecting toward Anya's agency and reframing anxiety as bigger than reality, without amplifying status quo reasons. At times doubt is bypassed rather than transformed — e.g., 'it's just easier to say that than believe it' met with a forward-moving question rather than a reflective processing of the doubt itself.
