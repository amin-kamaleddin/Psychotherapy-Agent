# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout with no judgment despite the patient's defensive blaming. Validated his frustration, affirmed his identity, and normalized his emotional vulnerability around his dad's birthday.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured the double-sided nature of the patient's experience, pairing his sense of unfairness with his own goals. Consistently reflected his insights on coping, family motivation, and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on resolving the phone privilege conflict and planning for the final three weeks. Session arc ended with concrete coping plans and a specific follow-up for next session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction read as two equal partners, with tentative suggestions and check-ins. The patient's responses visibly drove the agenda, and the counselor built on them rather than steering.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice and ownership, offering options without prescribing. All decisions—Ms. Rivers, timing, coping strategy—came from the patient, and the final summary credited him entirely.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from the client through open questions about what matters and what success looks like. The patient's core family motivator and self-generated coping strategies were elicited and deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the patient as the expert, with the plan built entirely around his own definition of success. Active power sharing at its best.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced the patient's change language with open questions. Strategically reflected commitment language back, amplifying it ('I love that — "I handled it"').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk by using double-sided reflections pairing the grievance with his goals. Point withheld because the unfair-treatment theme was acknowledged several times without always being actively redirected.
