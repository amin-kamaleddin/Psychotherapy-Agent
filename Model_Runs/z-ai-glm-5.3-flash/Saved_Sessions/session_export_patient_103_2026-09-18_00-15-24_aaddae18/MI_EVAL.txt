# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed Nabil's autonomy and persistence ('you haven't stopped trying'), never criticized or argued, and explicitly validated his frustration as legitimate rather than framing the problem as internal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reflected the deeper meaning of the patient's words, including naming the core tension ('not soft, not exploding') and the emotional cost of carrying anger all day. Reflections like 'it's not the hour, it's carrying it around all day' demonstrated strong accurate empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focused steadily on what Nabil wanted (protecting his afternoons, being back in charge of his day) rather than pushing an agenda, and allowed the patient to determine the direction of change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained an equal footing throughout, asking permission-style questions and crediting the plan to the patient ('That's you, not me'), with the plan generated entirely in the patient's own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforced Nabil's choice and control ('puts me back in charge of my day'), offered the calm response as a low-pressure experiment rather than a commitment, and invited rather than directed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out the patient's own motivation, values, and solutions—the 'switch' moment, his desire to make his afternoon meetings, and his self-authored script—all elicited through open questions rather than supplied by the clinician.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Engaged the patient as the expert on his own life, consistently soliciting his input ('what do you think would help most?') and adjusting pace in response to his skepticism.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Successfully evoked sustained change talk, from the desired future picture to the self-generated plan and commitment ('I'll put it in my phone'), while also honoring the patient's stated doubts ('doesn't mean it'll work').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('the polite version doesn't land,' 'talking here vs the real thing') was met with reflections that acknowledged and dignified the ambivalence rather than arguing against it, allowing the patient to move toward change himself.
