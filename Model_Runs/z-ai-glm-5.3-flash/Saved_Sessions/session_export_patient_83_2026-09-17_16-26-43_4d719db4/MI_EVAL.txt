# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Mai's skepticism ('You're reasonable to want to understand'). However, warmth is brief and formulaic, quickly pivoting to information rather than deepening connection.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections, but the session is dominated by information provision over reflective listening. Emotional content (mother's heart problems, living alone, phone anxiety) is acknowledged once but rarely explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on starting atorvastatin safely; every exchange ties to purpose, side-effect monitoring, labs, or the action plan. Recaps and concrete next steps keep the session on track with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration via menu choices and preferences, but knowledge flow is largely one-directional counselor lecture. Mai's ideas are accepted but not built upon as equal expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices and asks permission, with Mai independently deciding on juice, log, and calls. However, the change is pre-determined by the prescription, and the counselor never affirms her freedom to decide whether to take it at all.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Aside from the opening question, there is little effort to elicit Mai's own motivations, values, or confidence. Her readiness emerges from counselor education rather than guided self-persuasion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menu choices convey some power-sharing, but the counselor assumes the expert role for most of the session, delivering dense medical content in lecture format. Mai shapes logistics but not substance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk arose spontaneously from Mai rather than through evocation; no importance/confidence questions or open questions about her reasons for change. Affirmations were generic rather than strategically deepening.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial sustain talk was validated and reframed appropriately without amplification. However, ambivalence was resolved mainly through education and persuasion rather than double-sided reflections or eliciting Mai's own resolutions.
