# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Warm affirmations ('That was brave — well done', 'You're not pathetic') are present but brief and formulaic, quickly giving way to prescriptive advice. Limited normalization of Anya's fear of losing her social life beyond one reassurance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; responses mostly consist of scripted advice lists and multiple-choice options. Key emotional content (regret about Egypt, fear that the bank call means admitting a real problem) goes unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on gambling reduction with a logical progression from debt overview to concrete actions to relapse-risk rehearsal. Slightly mechanical — focus stays on tasks and scripts rather than connecting to Anya's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (offering options, asking 'Want me to draft...?', joint rehearsal), but the counselor dominates with scripted content and lists. Anya is often reacting to the counselor's agenda rather than co-creating it, and the five-step plan was delivered before she expressed readiness.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong emphasis on choice (menu options, reversible temporary block), and Anya explicitly noted reversibility 'makes it easier to say yes' — clear evidence autonomy support worked. Some loss of credit for pushing quickly toward action and framing steps as counselor-directed next steps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely draws out Anya's own reasons for change; her powerful, values-rich motivation (Egypt, the mother she wants to become) emerged spontaneously and was left unexplored and unamplified. Change talk occurred mostly through role-play structure rather than skilled evocation — a significant area for growth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers menus of options and asks 'Want to...?', conveying some choice, but the stance remains largely expert-driven. Long prescriptive lists arrive before Anya's own ideas are explored, making partnership superficial rather than genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Role-play of the bank call and pushy-friend scenario effectively elicits strong client change talk ('I'm skipping the pokies', 'Fifty bucks, that's my limit'). However, change statements are never reflected back to strengthen them, and confidence or commitment is not explicitly explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not amplify sustain talk and uses a good harm-reduction reframe so Anya doesn't have to cut out her friends. However, her doubt ('I haven't managed any of this so far, so why would a plan suddenly fix it?') is met with reassurance and a task menu instead of a double-sided reflection or exploration.
