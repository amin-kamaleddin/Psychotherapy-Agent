# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, even during client pushback. Boundaries like 'no lectures, no I'm proud of you' were accepted without defensiveness and honored verbatim.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, notably the accurate reflection of smoking as 'permission to stop' rather than nicotine. Some turns clustered multiple questions together, briefly overwhelming the client ('Whoa — that's a lot of questions').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus on smoking, moving progressively from ambivalence to a concrete plan (Tuesday lunch, car, stress ball). Focus briefly scattered when several substitute options were introduced rapidly mid-session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed: Lucía chose the car ritual, timing, fallback, and check-in style. Counselor asked permission before structuring and repeatedly solicited her preferences, confirmed by her 'Thanks for actually listening.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly and consistently protected ('you're still in charge,' 'whatever you decide'). The client's experience — 'You didn't lecture me once' — directly confirms genuine choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from the client; the pivotal question about what the five minutes provide elicited her own articulation of the underlying need. All commitments were self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client was consistently positioned as the expert and shaped the session's direction and outcome. Occasional front-loading of counselor-generated option menus briefly reasserted an expert stance before her pushback re-centered collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked spontaneous change talk ('the thing that's slowly killing me') and developed it into a self-generated commitment and even future-oriented motivation about her daughter. Evocation was client-driven rather than imposed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally met with empathic reframes and effective pivots rather than argument. A few reflections voiced ambivalence back ('aren't ready to quit'), briefly amplifying reasons against change.
