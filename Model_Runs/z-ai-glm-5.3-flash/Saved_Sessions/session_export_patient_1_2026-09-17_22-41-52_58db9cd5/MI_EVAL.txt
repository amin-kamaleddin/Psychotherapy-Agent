# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, validating Ahmed's self-care ('You're clearly not careless') and accepting his limits ('No promises needed') without any judgment about cigar use. Warmth felt genuine and individualized throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both content and underlying meaning (e.g., the trickle-vs-flood reflection, noting Ahmed's connection to his daughter unprompted). Caught Ahmed's pride and fear beneath the minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on the cigar behavior and health outcomes, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete, follow-up-anchored plan. Every exchange connected to the target without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative: the weekends-only plan, limited purchasing strategy, and follow-up at the cleaning appointment all came from Ahmed, with the psychiatrist building each response on his words. Equal-partner power sharing was exemplified throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly and repeatedly reinforced with 'no promises attached' and 'that's your call to make,' ending with an open question about what Ahmed wanted to set up for himself. No persuasive or coercive language appeared.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within the client — eliciting the baby realization, personal reasons, father-identity vision, and Ramadan as self-efficacy evidence. The psychiatrist never lectured or supplied his own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Ahmed as the expert ('You know your triggers better than anyone,' 'that's your call to make'). The session's plan was entirely Ahmed's construction, with the psychiatrist honoring his contributions as self-discovered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and deepened change talk through questions like 'What makes that worth something to you?' and the father-identity prompt, drawing out Ahmed's daughter, Ramadan self-efficacy, and growing curiosity about not needing cigars at all. Change talk strengthened progressively across the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk, pivoting with 'It doesn't have to be all or nothing' and redirecting to Ahmed's own middle-path idea rather than arguing. Minor growth area: occasional explicit double-sided reflections acknowledging sustain talk while tilting toward change would elevate this further.
