# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, including genuine, specific affirmations and respectful handling of David's self-deprecation. Validated his skepticism as legitimate rather than treating it as resistance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate complex reflections that captured meaning beneath David's words, including his ambivalence, fear of wasted effort, and hesitation about self-advocacy. Reflections deepened the dialogue and were confirmed accurate by David's elaborations.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session moved purposefully from skepticism to a concrete, self-generated plan. Lost points only for lingering slightly long on phone-call mechanics when the core behavior change was already established.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely bilateral interaction with repeated menus of options and David's answers visibly steering the session. The final four-part plan was co-created, demonstrating empowerment rather than direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No pressure to accept the statin; David's diet-first alternative was fully honored as legitimate. Every plan element came from David, and the closing plan explicitly preserved contingency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key question drew out David's own reasons, ideas, and accountability strategies rather than supplying external motivation. David's arc from 'Okay, sure. Fine' to a self-authored plan is direct evidence of masterful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located within David, whose contributions (wife's role, burger routine, communication patterns) became the architecture of the plan. Textbook power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** David progressed from surface compliance to concrete commitment language ('I can do that this week'). Change talk was highlighted when it emerged and developed further with forecasting questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was largely handled with neutral or double-sided reflections rather than reinforcement. A few early responses dwelt briefly in sustain-talk territory, preventing a perfect score.
