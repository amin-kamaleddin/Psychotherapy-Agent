# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and non-judgmental, validating Sofia's exhaustion and reframing walking away as strength. Affirmations were brief and formulaic ('That was great'), missing deeper individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate but short, often transitioning quickly to menu options rather than exploring deeper emotions like humiliation, shame, and years of unfair blame beneath Sofia's dismissive 'whatever' tone.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus throughout: moved skillfully from exploration to goal selection, drafting a text, boundary lines, rehearsal, and troubleshooting. Every counselor turn advanced the plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely joint—Sofia's edits, preferences, and requests genuinely shaped the plan. Slightly constrained by the counselor presenting ready-made lists rather than opening with Sofia's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and respected Sofia's rejections (e.g., the teacher option) without pushing. Fell short of a 5 because options were counselor-designed menus with little explicit emphasis on Sofia owning the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some motivation and plans, and role-play evoked intrinsic realization ('felt weirdly good'). However, the counselor supplied most scripts and rationales, missing rich openings like 'I want it to feel automatic' and 'Not doing that again' to develop Sofia's own why.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia's contributions shaped the session (casual text, blunt line, practice request), but the counselor frequently handed over pre-packaged menus and scripts, shifting the expert role toward the clinician rather than drawing out Sofia's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia produced meaningful change talk which the counselor built into concrete plans and rehearsal. However, momentum largely came from the client; the counselor rarely deepened or explicitly reflected change talk back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently acknowledged sustain talk briefly and pivoted to action—reframing screenshot fears, normalizing ambivalence, and moving to peer-based options. Reflections were single-sided rather than strategic double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with her desires.
