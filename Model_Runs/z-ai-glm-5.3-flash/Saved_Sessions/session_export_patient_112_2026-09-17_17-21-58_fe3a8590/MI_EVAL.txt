# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone with effective affirmations ('that's a realistic, strong first step'), and Rosa explicitly noted feeling unpressured. Affirmations are somewhat sparse and formulaic, and there's little explicit normalization of her fear of relapse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, including an accurate complex reflection capturing Rosa's ambivalence between identity, health fears, and motherhood. Reflections taper in the second half as the counselor shifts to advice-giving, and deeper emotional layers (identity loss, 'one slip and I'm done' pattern) are acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary, consistent focus on smoking cessation with no topic drift—every exchange ties back to the goal and builds progressively from exploration to a concrete, specific plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructs the plan from Rosa's own preferences (walking, phone reminders, texting Marisol) and invites her input at key decision points. The mid-session advice block was delivered as a counselor-generated list rather than jointly brainstormed or introduced with permission.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently frames actions as Rosa's choice ('It's completely up to you'), never pressures commitment, and reinforces self-compassion around slips. Rosa's closing comment—'thanks for not pushing me too hard'—is direct evidence that autonomy was experienced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Textbook use of the confidence ruler with 'what would raise it by one point' follow-ups reliably elicited Rosa's own solutions and change talk grew noticeably across the session. Evocation is uneven—the counselor sometimes supplies ready-made answers where eliciting Rosa's ideas first would have been stronger, and importance/need dimensions are never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites Rosa's input at multiple decision points, giving her genuine influence over the plan. However, the counselor frequently assumes the expert role, especially in the advice-dense mid-session passage on nicotine replacement and weight strategies, without acknowledging Rosa's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful open questions and twice-used confidence scaling reliably elicited change talk ('planning it out makes it feel a little less impossible') and self-generated commitments. Missed opportunities to deepen or reinforce change talk when it emerged, such as 'maybe one of my big excuses would be gone' passing without response.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Skillfully lowered stakes by framing the walking plan as 'a test, not a promise to quit' and validating fears rather than arguing against them. However, reflections sometimes repeat sustain talk content without pivoting toward change, double-sided reflections are never used, and Rosa's 'can't imagine life without cigarettes' receives no strategic redirection.
