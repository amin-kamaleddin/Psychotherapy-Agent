# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental respect throughout, including validation of Mateo's self-blame and no judgment about his record. However, affirmations felt sparse and warmth was somewhat formulaic ('That makes sense' repeated often).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured pointless days, record-related shame, and the stability/stuck discrepancy. Reflections occasionally stayed at surface content rather than deepening the emotional meaning of his fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus from vague purposelessness to a specific, time-bound commitment (text Uncle Ray Thursday 3 PM with reminder and follow-up). Every turn advanced the agenda toward the landscaping goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong co-construction: jointly drafted and edited the text, chose timing and follow-up together. Slightly weakened by multi-question barrages that put the counselor in an agenda-setting position.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent explicit support of choice: 'Whatever you choose is fine,' 'pick or tweak,' and respecting Mateo's refusal of role-play. Offered options rather than directives and reinforced Mateo's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited Mateo's own reasons (independence, family, kids) via the dream question and readiness ruler. Counselor sometimes supplied solutions and templates himself rather than letting Mateo generate more.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo's input drove the session—his edits shaped the final plan and text. Early option menus were somewhat prescriptive and Mateo could have been framed more explicitly as the expert on his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk ('being my own boss... family too') and used the readiness ruler and scaling follow-up to elicit concrete commitment. Missed chances to deepen with confidence questions or affirm his change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally but not actively softened; when hopelessness and self-defeat emerged ('a guy like me with a record'), the counselor pivoted quickly to solutions rather than using double-sided reflections.
