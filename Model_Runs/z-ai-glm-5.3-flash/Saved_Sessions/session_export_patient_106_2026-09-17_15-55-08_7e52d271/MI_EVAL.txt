# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental responses throughout, including validation of her boundary-setting. Falls short of top marks as affirmations are somewhat formulaic and miss deeper strengths like her resolve not to lie to her son.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures her core meaning (smoking as rare quiet time) and her ambivalence. Deeper emotional undercurrents—her mother's death, fear of her son's question, the pregnancy relapse—pass without reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on smoking within her son's wellbeing and progressively narrows to a concrete plan. Momentum occasionally stalls in logistics/script-tweaking, and no summaries tie the plan back to her core values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed with Natalia's amendments (tea instead of gum, her own phrasing). Collaboration is uneven, though, as the counselor often presents pre-packaged menus and expert information before eliciting her ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength: choice is consistently framed as Natalia's, all boundaries are accepted without pressure, and setbacks are reframed as learning rather than failure. No coercive or controlling language anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation is drawn from Natalia, but the counselor relies heavily on supplying options, scripts, and psychoeducation. Rich motivational material (motherhood, honesty with her son) is never explicitly evoked or explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Natalia's input genuinely shapes the plan, showing real power sharing. The balance tips toward expert mode, however, with option lists, unsolicited psychoeducation on thirdhand smoke, and proposed scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good evocation of future-oriented change language ('maybe I do it on Sundays too'). However, the counselor doesn't reflect or deepen this change talk to build momentum, never assesses importance/confidence, and misses value-based connections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids reinforcing sustain talk and redirects toward change, but minimizations like 'my mom died at 68, so it can't be that bad' go unaddressed. 'I'm not superwoman' is absorbed but never woven into a double-sided reflection.
