# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and briefly validates Dylan's frustration, but the regard feels procedural and formulaic. There are no affirmations of Dylan as a person and no exploration of his feelings about being singled out.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are minimal and content-level only, restating facts without touching emotion or meaning. Dylan's frustration, fear about his record, and feelings of unfairness are acknowledged superficially and quickly bypassed in favor of drafting paperwork.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Focus stays on the conduct referral but on the wrong target—administrative logistics (statement, email, witnesses, record classification) rather than the clinical target of underage drinking. There is no movement toward exploring the behavior or its risks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor dominates the agenda from the start, offering framings and templates while Dylan mostly approves or edits documents. There is no joint agenda-setting beyond the conduct paperwork, making this a consultant-client dynamic rather than a partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers genuine procedural choices (contesting vs. minimizing, who sends the email, declining check-ins) and respects Dylan's decisions. However, support is limited to menu-offering and subtly steers toward the cooperative option, with no affirmation of Dylan's capacity regarding drinking itself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation is never elicited from the client; the counselor asks almost no open questions about Dylan's values or reasons to reconsider drinking. His concern for his record and academics—natural leverage for discrepancy—is used only to shield him from consequences.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor acts as fixer/scribe, producing templates and drafts rather than drawing out Dylan's own ideas. Dylan's input is incorporated only as material for the counselor's documents, with no recognition of his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is virtually no effort to evoke change talk about drinking; Dylan's admission 'I guess I was there' is met with a statement template instead of exploration. All session energy goes toward minimizing conduct consequences, not the behavior itself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor mirrors and operationalizes Dylan's sustain talk, drafting statements asserting he wasn't responsible and reinforcing denial of accountability. No double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancy work are used, so sustain talk dominates unchallenged.
