# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains respectful, nonjudgmental regard with a sarcastic patient, affirming his self-directed reading and reframing his father's 'just deal' ethos as a value. Falls short of 5 only because some affirmations border on flattery, feeling slightly formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, accurately capturing emotional core ('it matters to you to be sharp and strong for your crew') and the client's own metaphors. Some emotional material, like the shame of hiding his condition from his boss, is moved past quickly before pivoting to strategy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains laser focus on asthma self-management despite deflection, with every exchange advancing the target. The final question sharply targets the exact moment of behavior choice at the coffee maker with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** 'Maybe we build the plan from there, together' sets a collaborative frame that is sustained throughout, with genuinely exploratory questions and no advice imposed. The interaction reads as two equal partners problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is woven into nearly every turn, framing the plan as the client's own design and honoring his stated limits without pressure. The client spontaneously moves toward commitment rather than being pushed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out the client's own reasons for change — work performance, his father's model, and his own discovered evidence that 'the mornings are better.' The final exploration of his internal decision dialogue is textbook evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions the client as the expert ('I'm not gonna pretend I know your life better than you'), and the entire plan comes from the client's own words. Co-construction is concrete and genuine.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength — open questions consistently evoke the client's own change language, from 'taking the thing before work when it's cold' to 'actually doing the one thing I already set up.' Each change statement is reflected and reinforced rather than ignored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk exceptionally well, briefly mirroring minimizations ('Oscar's no big deal') before non-arguing pivots toward consequences and change. Constraints like 'it's not a project' are honored as design constraints rather than resistance.
