### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with frequent affirmations ('Good call,' 'That's solid'). However, warmth occasionally felt scripted, and Kieran's disclaimers ('Not promising it'll stick') were only briefly acknowledged rather than deeply honored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections ('feels soul-sapping, like you're just ticking boxes'), but the counselor quickly pivoted to solutions. Deeper emotional content — resignation, going blank in meetings, 'I don't really think about what I want anymore' — was not explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on one concrete behavior: asserting himself in compliance meetings. Every exchange advanced the plan — script selection, rehearsal, date (Thursday the 14th), backup email, and reminder — with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some options and check-ins, but the counselor increasingly executed the work himself, drafting the email, deadline, and reminder. Collaboration shrank to yes/no confirmations by the end.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly affirmed choice ('You get to decide what we do here') and framed tasks as optional. A 5 is withheld because menus steered toward preselected solutions and the counselor supplied several plan elements rather than eliciting them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited Kieran's own words and decisions (script, chosen item, 'more of one than I had last week'), but stepped past a critical open door — 'I don't really think about what I want anymore' — resulting in a plan about external compliance rather than Kieran's own values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional permission-asking and choices signaled collaboration, but power-sharing was inconsistent — the counselor supplied scripts, drafted the email, and drove the agenda. Kieran's 'you'd know it better than me anyway' revealed the shift in expertise to the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited concrete commitments: Kieran chose the redirect script, rehearsed it, set Thursday the 14th, and ended with change language ('It's a plan... more of one than I had last week'). Not a 5 because evocation relied on menu options rather than intrinsic reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing ('It's fair to doubt it'll change much') and pivoted to action, preventing reinforcement of resignation. However, ambivalence received only brief acknowledgment rather than strategic double-sided reflections.
