# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with affirmations and respectful handling of risky disclosures and confidentiality requests.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of Maya's ambivalence, though question batteries sometimes crowded out reflective depth, and the emotional function of drinking was not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on alcohol use tied to grades and college goals, ending with a concrete measurable plan and scheduled check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving: options offered as menus, plans built from Maya's preferences, and scripts drafted 'for her voice.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy explicitly reinforced throughout; counselor accepted Maya's rejections of options, honored confidentiality with a safety caveat, and never pressured commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from Maya's own values and goals; scaling questions elicited her self-assessments and produced measurable change talk (confidence rising from 3 to 6).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maya was positioned as the expert and her contributions substantively shaped the session — she rejected, modified, and selected plan elements that the counselor incorporated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of change talk via scaling, values exploration, and choice; Maya generated substantial commitment language, and the counselor built on counter-change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled well with double-sided reflections and redirection to solutions, though an early question briefly invited it and the safety disclosure could have gotten one more reflective loop.
