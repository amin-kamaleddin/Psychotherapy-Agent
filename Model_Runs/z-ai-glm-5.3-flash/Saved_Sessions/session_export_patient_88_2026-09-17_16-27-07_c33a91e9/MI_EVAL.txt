# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and nonjudgment with a defensive teen, affirming honesty and reflecting rather than arguing with 'I'm not some alcoholic.' Deducted for somewhat formulaic warmth and not addressing Marcus's fear of parental notification or judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured both the functional value of drinking and the minimization, plus peer pressure realities. Reflections were brief and formulaic at times, with limited exploration of emotional undercurrents like fear of seeming lame and social belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Steady focus on alcohol use and related risks, progressing logically to a concrete, committed safety plan. Some drift in the extended closing, and the counselor accepted no reduction in drinking without revisiting the core target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were genuinely co-constructed, with Marcus's own strategy ('crew thing, not a me thing') shaping the intervention. The early expert-driven health-facts block and repeated script offers reduced true mutuality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary permission-seeking, option menus, and graceful acceptance of Marcus's rejections; he explicitly noted the session was 'not as preachy' as expected. Confidentiality limits about parents were respected but not explicitly confirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and the confidence ruler elicited Marcus's own reasons, solutions, and commitment language. Evocation stayed at harm-reduction micro-steps without exploring deeper values or the 'I don't have a problem' discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus was repeatedly positioned as the expert, choosing and rewriting plans in his own words. Deducted for the initial prescriptive health-information block delivered before exploring his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocation of ambivalence and measurable commitment (confidence 8 and 6) ending in genuine commitment language. Missed opportunity to deepen the discrepancy between 'no problem' framing and his own risk admission.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without arguing, but reflections sometimes reinforced minimization ('hasn't felt harmful') without strategic pivoting. Double-sided reflections and reframing linking 'it could get me in trouble' to the rides-with-drinking risk were underused, and the health-facts lecture risked reactiveness.
