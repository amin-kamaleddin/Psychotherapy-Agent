# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains basic respect and a nonjudgmental stance, but warmth is minimal and largely transactional. No affirmations of Tyler's honesty, vulnerability, or valued goals (e.g., education) were offered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is nearly absent after one brief early acknowledgment; emotionally significant disclosures like 'I maybe have a drinking problem' were never reflected back. The counselor shifted almost entirely to factual questions and advice lists.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on the conduct citation and alcohol use, progressing logically to a plan Tyler summarized at the end. Direction is task-focused rather than change-focused, with drinking addressed only after Tyler raised it.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The stance is that of a consultant dispensing solutions, with unsolicited step-by-step plans and yes/no questions dominating. Tyler's ideas (drink limits, roommate as buddy) were folded in incidentally rather than through joint agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive elements exist ('I can't decide for you', offering choices about drafting statements), but these are token formatting choices. The bulk of the interaction is prescriptive advice without affirming Tyler's ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little sense that motivation resides within Tyler; most turns deliver information rather than eliciting his reasons or values. Tyler's direct request for the counselor's opinion was met with assessment criteria instead of evocative exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor consistently assumes the expert role, with nearly every turn a list of directives. Tyler meaningfully shaped the harm-reduction plan despite, not because of, the counselor's approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged ('School's worth it', 'I'll try it') but from Tyler's own reflections, not deliberate evocation. Key opportunities, like Tyler asking 'do you think I actually need to change?', were met with information rather than being turned back to explore motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('everyone drinks at these things', 'I'm not promising to quit') was repeatedly met with silence or problem-solving rather than double-sided reflections or reframing. Ambivalence was never pivoted into discrepancy with Tyler's valued education goals.
