# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth; never judged Elena's hesitancy or mom-group sources ("keeping your child safe matters a lot to you," "That makes sense"). Non-lecturing tone despite educational content. A 5 would require deeper, more explicit affirmations of Elena's character and values rather than somewhat formulaic acknowledgments.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening, e.g., "because your own chickenpox was mild, it's hard to see why your daughter would need a shot" and "I hear the push–pull — wanting protection but not to rush." However, counselor often moved quickly from reflection to information-giving, leaving less room for exploring the emotional layer (fear of seizures, family pressure).

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on the vaccination decision from opening menu through plan and phone script. Well-sequenced momentum: concerns, efficacy, side effects, risk mitigation, plan, rehearsal. Slight drift into logistics (printing, reminders, breathing), and the mother-influence issue was raised but not explored.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed as joint work ("explore the risks and benefits together"); plan co-developed at Elena's request ("Can we write that down together?"). However, educational segments tilted toward the counselor as expert, and the plan/script was drafted by the counselor with Elena approving rather than actively co-authoring.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary. Consistent menus ("Would you like to: 1)... 2)... 3)..."), permission-seeking before offering materials, and closing with "I'm here to support whatever you choose." Recommendation to book soon was framed as encouragement with stated exceptions. Elena's "my daughter's my responsibility, not hers" reflects internalized autonomy.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good opening evocative question, but motivation was later supplied through education rather than elicited. Pivotal change moments followed counselor-provided statistics rather than Elena's explored values. No probing of deeper reasons, no confidence/readiness rulers; her protective-mother motivation was affirmed but never actively drawn out.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Stated collaborative intent and offered menu-based choices, but informational segments were notably expert-driven — dense data summaries instead of first eliciting Elena's own reasoning or co-examining her sources. The counselor's agenda (facts, then plan) drove the session rather than Elena's contributions shaping direction.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Substantial change talk emerged ("I want to understand it," "I feel a lot better than when I walked in," "I'll commit to calling," "more like... ready"). Counselor created conditions via choices and targeted questions. However, change talk was rarely amplified or reflected back explicitly (e.g., "I'm not a hundred percent comfortable yet, but..." went unmet with targeted reflection); momentum came largely from Elena herself.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk engaged respectfully without reinforcement (minimizing "I was fine," seizure worry acknowledged). However, no double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk; some responses (immediately presenting complication statistics after minimizing) functioned more as persuasion via education than MI-consistent softening.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** 3.8/5
