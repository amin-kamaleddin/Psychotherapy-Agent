# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with specific, genuine affirmations and respectful use of te reo mirroring the client's cultural identity. His history of violence and drinking was met with unconditional positive regard and normalization of his nervousness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and reached the level of meaning, advancing Wiremu's self-understanding (e.g., 'it stopped being punishment and started being your choice'). The client explicitly noted reflections made things 'feel real.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on alcohol reduction, violence program engagement, and living arrangement, with a discernible arc from exploring ambivalence to a time-bound commitment ('I'll ring my uncle this week'). No topic drift; consistently purposeful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely two-way — the clinician checked in before the rehearsal and Wiremu co-created the script, the ring-vs-text decision, and the coping strategies. All key decisions were made by the client with the clinician facilitating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently reinforced Wiremu's ownership, framed the rehearsal as optional, and never dictated content. His parting statement — 'it's mine' — is direct evidence that autonomy support landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every reason for change came from the client, not the clinician, via strategic evocative questions about his kids, shame, and identity as a dad. Elicited material was developed and consolidated into a self-authored commitment plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('You know your uncle better than anyone'), and his contributions became the actual plan. Power-sharing was at the highest level, with the client's own words forming the treatment plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout domain — evocation deepened Wiremu's change language from ambivalence to concrete commitment ('maybe that bit of freedom is worth giving up'; 'I'll ring my uncle this week'), with change talk strategically reinforced by ownership-focused affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with reflective, non-reinforcing responses and effective double-sided reflections and reframes ('a step towards your tamariki'). The minor deduction reflects occasional re-voicing of ambivalence framing without an immediate pivot.
