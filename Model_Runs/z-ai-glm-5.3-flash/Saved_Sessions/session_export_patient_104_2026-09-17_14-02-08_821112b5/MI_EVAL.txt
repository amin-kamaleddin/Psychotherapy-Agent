# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with reassuring and encouraging statements throughout. However, warmth is generic rather than personalized, with no affirmations of her strengths as a mother or validation of her guilt and self-comparisons.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; the psychiatrist responds to disclosures of exhaustion, guilt, and self-doubt with information and advice rather than reflections. The only empathy attempt ('that sounds exhausting') is immediately followed by advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps a clear, consistent focus on infant sleep and Nadine's exhaustion, ending with a concrete follow-up appointment. Falls short of a 5 because direction is achieved through directive information delivery rather than collaborative goal clarification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is strongly counselor-directed, with solutions and schedules delivered rather than jointly developed. Nadine's own ideas are absorbed without credit, and the counselor never asks what she has already tried or what might work for her family.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor provides detailed prescriptive instructions and a fully specified hourly schedule with minimal acknowledgment of Nadine's choice. The few choice points offered are minor within an otherwise expert-driven framework.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation is assumed rather than elicited; the counselor never asks about Nadine's reasons, confidence, or hopes. All of her motivation and change talk emerge spontaneously without being cultivated by counselor technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor clearly assumes the expert role, delivering prescriptive information and a fully constructed schedule each turn. Nadine's expertise about her own baby and family is not meaningfully drawn upon or credited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nadine generates substantial spontaneous change talk, and the counselor capitalizes on some of it by proposing a concrete plan and inviting commitment. However, there is no active evocation, deepening, or deliberate reinforcement of her change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nadine's doubts ('I don't know how much longer I can do this,' 'I'm not promising miracles') are essentially ignored rather than acknowledged. The counselor responds to ambivalence with more information instead of double-sided reflections or affirmations.
