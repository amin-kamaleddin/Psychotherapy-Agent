# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently demonstrated unconditional positive regard, pushing back on self-critical statements and normalizing avoidance as anxiety-driven protection. Every self-critical statement was met with warmth and reassurance with no judgment anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both content and deeper meaning throughout. The patient repeatedly responded with insight and confirmation, indicating she felt deeply understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus and logical progression from pattern exploration to a concrete, time-specified plan with confidence re-rating. One point lost because movement was largely therapist-driven rather than co-anchoring the agenda with the patient.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were co-constructed and the patient's ideas directly shaped the intervention, with her self-knowledge explicitly validated. One point lost because the agenda was clinician-set at the outset and some closing reflections echoed the patient near-verbatim rather than building jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was reinforced at nearly every turn, honoring the patient's chosen commitment level as wisdom rather than pushing for more. The patient explicitly thanked the psychiatrist for not making it 'a huge thing,' direct evidence the non-pressuring style registered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly every change idea originated with the patient, with no prescribed solutions; scaling questions twice measured motivation growing from within. Confidence measurably rose from 4-5 to 5.5, with spontaneous commitment statements demonstrating motivation was elicited rather than installed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated the patient's expertise as central and consistently framed the work as a shared effort. The patient's contributions substantially influenced the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and amplified, including scaling questions, reinforcing commitment language, and highlighting the patient's self-generated coping idea. Confidence rising from 4-5 to 5.5 provided concrete evidence the evocation worked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently softened by reframing avoidance as anxiety-driven protection and exploring the short-lived relief, letting the patient discover the cost herself. The patient's language shifted from 'I just avoid it' to 'I think I can do that,' evidence of successful redirection.
