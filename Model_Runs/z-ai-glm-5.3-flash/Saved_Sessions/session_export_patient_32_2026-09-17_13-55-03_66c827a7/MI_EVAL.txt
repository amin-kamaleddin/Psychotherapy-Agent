# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains respect and a nonjudgmental tone but offers no genuine affirmations of Derrick's strengths (prior sobriety, insight, willingness to plan) and never normalizes his ambivalence or fear of social exclusion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; Derrick's vulnerable disclosures about loneliness, his breakup, and fear of being 'the outcast guy' were met with bullet-point advice rather than reflections. No complex reflections capture his internal conflict between belonging and safety.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus with every exchange advancing a concrete plan, but direction is skewed toward legal logistics and hearing preparation rather than the drinking behavior and relational pain Derrick explicitly asked for help with.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration (permission-seeking, inviting selection among options, revising drafts per Derrick's edits), but the counselor generates nearly all content while Derrick reacts, edits, and approves, and his expertise about his friendships isn't integrated into the strategy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks permission and offers menu-style options without coercion, but repeatedly ties choices to legal compliance and takes over authorship of statements, scripts, and plans, with no explicit 'it's your choice' ownership language and unexplored ambivalence about long-term change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is treated as residing in the counselor's plan rather than within Derrick; the counselor never asks about his own reasons, values, confidence, or hopes. Even Derrick's strongest ideas (cab money, 'I'm driving' line) came from him, not evocation, and his isolation disclosure was answered with generic advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and 'which of these feels doable?' show some partnership, but the session is predominantly expert-driven with pre-made plans, drafts, and scripts, and Derrick's relational pain is treated as a problem to be solved with scripts rather than explored through his own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Derrick produces meaningful change talk organically ('cab money,' 'worth playing it smart,' 'maybe there's something to this plan') which the counselor briefly acknowledges, but the counselor never evokes change talk with open questions, nor deepens, amplifies, or reflects it—missing the pivotal 'maybe there's something to this plan' opening.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor actively incorporates Derrick's minimization ('a serious lapse, not an ongoing pattern') into the court statement and leaves 'it's just beer' unaddressed, while framing sobriety supports purely as legal strategy, which reinforces external motivation and leaves sustain talk intact.
