# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently honors the patient's autonomy, explicitly avoiding the lecture the patient expected and allowing him to sort risks into his own readiness piles. His ambivalence and decision not to commit to quitting are accepted without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both sides of the patient's ambivalence (relief vs. the 'running list' of risks) and track his emotional state, including his fear of the deeper issues and his fear of his housemate becoming a 'babysitter.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays anchored to the patient's stated priority — the Narcan/housemate conversation — and moves it toward a concrete, same-night plan. Slightly less focus is placed on the 'running from things' theme the patient raised.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly positions the patient as the expert ('You're the one who knows how he'll hear it,' 'you know your situation better than anyone') and builds each next question on the patient's own answers.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element originates from the patient, with the clinician only reflecting and asking which step feels most doable. Change talk like '10 out of 10 ready' and 'tonight, actually' is self-generated, not extracted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions ('What's your own sense of where things stand?', 'What would make that conversation go well?') reliably draw out the patient's own reasons, resources, and detailed change talk about safety steps and timing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue flows as genuine back-and-forth, with the clinician weaving the patient's words ('two parts,' 'the running list') into each response and explicitly framing decisions as shared but patient-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is strongly evoked and reinforced — the patient articulates willingness ('willing to act on'), ability ('10 out of 10 ready'), and a full plan ('tonight... straight talk, show him the drawer, done').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician doesn't argue against sustain talk ('future Diego's problem,' not quitting cold) and instead reflects it neutrally while keeping focus on the readiness the patient does express. This is handled well but could occasionally invite the patient to explore the sustain talk a bit more deeply.
