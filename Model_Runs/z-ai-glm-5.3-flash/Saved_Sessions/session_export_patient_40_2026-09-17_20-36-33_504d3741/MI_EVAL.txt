# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, reframing the patient's self-criticism ('pathetic' answer) and affirming his efforts without judgment about relapse or shame. Warmth feels genuine and individualized rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening captures both emotional nuance and ambivalence, with the patient repeatedly confirming reflections ('Yeah, that's it exactly'). Reflections consistently mirror shame, fear, and hope with precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains clear focus on recovery and concrete next steps, building progressively from values to a specific action plan. The counselor steers toward target behavior while summarizing and closing loops without imposing an agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction occurs between equal partners, with the patient's ideas driving every decision and the counselor offering only tentative suggestions he freely modifies. Explicit deference to the patient's expertise ('You know yourself and your patterns better than anyone') indicates genuine power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient makes all decisions himself, including pacing ('too big for this week'), which the counselor supports. Options are offered rather than directives, with no pressure or coercion present.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key insight and element of the plan comes from the patient, elicited through open questions that draw out deeper meaning. The counselor never lectures or supplies reasons for change; the patient articulates his own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within the patient, whose contributions substantially shape the session—the dinner table, Marcus, written list, and sister conversation are all his own ideas. The counselor offers only gentle scaffolding.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk through open questions, reflecting it back and affirming progress. The patient's change talk grows stronger across the session, culminating in 'I want to do it right' and concrete commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally acknowledges sustain talk with double-sided reflections and redirects toward coping resources without reinforcing reasons against change. Minor deduction: a few reflections restated the patient's fear ('you've blown it before') without an immediate pivot.
