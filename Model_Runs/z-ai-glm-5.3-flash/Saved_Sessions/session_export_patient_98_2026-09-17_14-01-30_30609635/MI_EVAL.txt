# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect and no judgment, with supportive openers and acknowledgment of discouragement, but warmth feels thin and formulaic. Missed affirmations of Mateo's persistence despite clear opportunities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal — only one brief reflection ('pain can feel in charge'). Key emotional content like discouragement, fear of setbacks, and skepticism was never reflected back or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on increasing physical activity, converging on a specific, measurable goal (5-minute flat-loop walk at 7:30 AM for one week with pain tracking and follow-up). Direction achieved through prescriptive checklists rather than guiding, and the emotional dimension was neglected.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partially collaborative: menu of options and 'Which feels doable?' invited participation, and Mateo's input shaped the plan. However, the counselor dominated with checklist-style content and restated the plan at rather than with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offering options and allowing choice shows some autonomy support, but the counselor quickly took over planning with rules and reminders. Flare concerns were controlled with preemptive rules rather than explored as the client's own choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to draw out Mateo's own motivations beyond one good question ('What would working look like?'). No confidence or importance rulers, no exploration of his wife's encouragement or frustration with doing nothing — most content was counselor-supplied advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The menu of options and 'Which feels doable?' were collaborative gestures, and Mateo shaped details like timing and route. The overall stance remained expert-driven, revealed when the client asked 'What did you have in mind?'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicited change talk about what 'working' would look like and secured a commitment ('Start tomorrow,' 'one honest try'). However, the change talk was never reinforced, deepened, or summarized back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue against doubts, which is a strength, but sustain talk like 'pain just decides what happens' and 'I'll skip days' was ignored rather than softened. No double-sided reflections tilting toward change were used.
