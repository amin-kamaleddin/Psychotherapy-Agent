# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and affirmed Marisol's ownership of her decision ('I respect that,' 'kept it your call') without judgment despite her dismissiveness. No shaming or pressure to remove the piercing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately tracked her skepticism and conditional stance. Reflections stayed at content level, missing deeper emotional nuance and the meaning behind her attachment to self-expression.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on the target behavior with every turn advancing the concrete plan (options → steps → barriers → scheduling → backup → follow-up). Session ended with a fully specified behavioral plan with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine equal-partner dynamic: menus rather than prescriptions, follow-up time and check-in questions set by the client, and pushback welcomed and incorporated. The plan was co-constructed step by step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary framing of everything as optional ('reduce risk while you keep it'), explicit support for her decision to keep the piercing, and full adaptation to her preferences (Sunday 2pm check-in). Marisol retained ownership throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Weakest domain: the counselor supplied most content (lists, drafts, tactics) and never explored her values around self-expression or developed intrinsic motivation for oral health. Missed opportunities for deeper evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Marisol as the decision-maker and her contributions genuinely shaped the session (she picked options, requested the script, set the time). Slightly limited by the counselor supplying option lists and drafts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited commitment language ('I'll text my piercer this week, probably go Saturday') and moved her toward planning with barrier questions. However, intrinsic reasons for caring about dental health were not explored; motivation remained external ('so everyone chills out').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong sustain talk (10/10 piercing) was met with acceptance rather than argument, and the harm-reduction pivot avoided a power struggle. But sustain talk was never strategically reframed using her own conditional criteria ('if a tooth actually chipped').
