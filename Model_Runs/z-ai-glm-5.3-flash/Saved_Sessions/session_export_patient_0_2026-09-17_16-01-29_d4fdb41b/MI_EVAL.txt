# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently respects Adrian without judgment, never lecturing or labeling even when he minimizes his use. However, explicit affirmation of his strengths is limited and warmth occasionally feels formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured Adrian's ambivalence well, such as balancing control with worry about the infection. Missed opportunities remain, particularly reflecting the humiliation he disclosed about seeking care.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on methamphetamine-related health risk and harm reduction despite Adrian's deflections. However, the core behavior of meth use itself is never revisited as a longer-term focus, which slightly limits direction toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Menus of options, choices at each step, and Adrian's genuine input (discreet product list, Thursday visit) shaped a co-constructed plan. The options were largely counselor-generated rather than co-created with Adrian's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces Adrian's ownership of decisions, accepting his refusals without pressure and emphasizing 'you're in control.' Adrian's explicit statement that he wasn't lectured and actually heard some of it confirms the autonomy-supportive stance landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** 'What matters most to you right now?' was a genuine evocative opening and some motivation emerged. However, the psychiatrist supplies most content herself through long option lists, never asking about Adrian's own reasons for change, confidence, or vision of a good outcome.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adrian is repeatedly positioned as the expert, with his preferences (discreet product list, Thursday pickup) directly shaping the plan. It falls short of a 5 because his expertise about his own life and social context is not explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was evoked ('rinsing my nose, fine. I can do that'; 'I don't want to deal with that again'). These statements are not deepened with strategic reflections, confidence/importance ratings, or amplification into commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with Adrian's frequent sustain talk and sometimes reframes toward autonomy, a softening strategy. However, double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk are rare, and ambivalence about the meth use itself is sidestepped rather than resolved.
