# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently showed respect and non-judgment, immediately agreeing to the client's 'no smoking talk' request and acknowledging his frustration. However, affirmations were brief and formulaic, lacking explicit affirmation of Seamus's honesty and self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections captured the client's frustration and lecture fatigue, but later responses shifted to practical checklists. Emotional themes like past failed quit attempts and ambivalence ('not saying never') were never reflected back or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained tight focus, but on dental logistics rather than the therapeutic target of smoking cessation, which was abandoned after the client's first pushback. No motivational link between dental outcomes and smoking was developed beyond one checklist item.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was counselor-driven, with pre-packaged plans, checklists, and reminders delivered unilaterally rather than co-created. The client's useful ideas (bringing his wife, plain text) emerged on his own, not through collaborative elicitation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor respected all client choices, dropping the medication topic and smoking talk without pressure or persuasion. However, autonomy support was largely passive compliance rather than active reinforcement of the client's ownership of change decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions were logistical ('Which one?', 'plain text or PDF?') rather than motivational, and no exploration of values, hopes, or confidence occurred. The client's spontaneous motivation ('I'm not saying never') was met with a generic closing instead of being evoked or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role, delivering a directive 5-step plan and constrained choice questions rather than sharing power. The client shaped the agenda only through pushback, with no acknowledgment of his expertise about his own health and motivation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client spontaneously offered change talk at least three times ('I got to really want to,' 'smoking isn't doing me any favours,' 'I'm not saying never'), but the counselor ignored or deflected all of it. No open questions about the client's own reasons for quitting or affirmations of this language occurred.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well and not amplified — the counselor pivoted gracefully when the client rejected medication and demanded no smoking talk. However, pivots were abrupt accommodations rather than strategic use of sustain talk as a springboard for change dialogue.
