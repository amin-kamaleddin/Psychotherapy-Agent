# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, normalizing the meter still in the box and affirming help-seeking. However, affirmations feel occasionally formulaic and miss individualized strengths like Marcus's honesty about avoidance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture ambivalence well (e.g., overwhelm about checking vs. pills feeling easier). Reflections are sometimes brief and quickly merged with problem-solving, missing deeper emotional themes like fear of burdening his wife and history of abandoning efforts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on blood glucose monitoring with a logical progression from exploration to a concrete one-week plan and follow-up. Session momentum is confirmed by Marcus's shift from 'drawer full of stuff' to committed action steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The action plan is genuinely co-constructed, integrating Marcus's choices (coffee-time checking, wife as reminder, clinic number strategy). Some segments (meter how-to, BG protocols) are delivered as unidirectional instruction rather than jointly explored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently offers choices and asks permission, reinforcing Marcus's ownership of decisions. Occasionally presents prescriptive solution lists before Marcus has generated his own ideas, slightly constraining self-directed problem-solving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The confidence ruler and open questions successfully drew out Marcus's own solutions and commitment language ('I want to actually try this time'). Evocation is uneven—solutions are sometimes supplied before exhausting his ideas, and personal values underlying diabetes management are never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus is positioned as the decision-maker, and his contributions genuinely shaped the session agenda. The counselor occasionally slips into directive expert mode with long instructional blocks delivered without first checking what Marcus already knows.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation through targeted questions and the confidence ruler, generating self-efficacy exploration and commitment language from Marcus. Missed opportunities to deepen statements like 'this one matters more' with follow-up evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubts are briefly validated and redirected toward change-oriented planning, with an effective double-sided reflection holding ambivalence. Sustain talk is sometimes glossed over with reassurance rather than strategically reflected and reframed toward Marcus's own reasons for change.
