# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations of Samir's right to choose, but warmth is generic and lacks individualized affirmation. Repeated safety re-asking despite clear answers undermines unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal, relying on brief acknowledgments followed by question batteries and advice. Key emotional content — disliking 'needing' pills, dig-in resistance to family pressure, tentative change talk — is never deeply reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays broadly on medication adherence, but drifts into repeated safety screening and logistics rather than resolving ambivalence. Ends with no summary or collaboratively identified next step tied to Samir's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaboration exists (offering options, asking permission), but the counselor frequently dumps pre-formed lists and question batteries without negotiating the agenda. True co-creation of a plan never occurs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A relative strength: choice is repeatedly affirmed ('It's your body and your choice') and resistance is accepted without coercion. However, autonomy is never leveraged explicitly as a resource, and repeated safety re-asking subtly signals distrust.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is supplied via psychoeducation and practical tools rather than elicited from the client. No evocative questions explore Samir's own reasons, hopes, or what matters to him about his health.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician assumes the expert role, pushing structured deliverables Samir repeatedly declines. Client input has limited impact on session direction, which feels checklist-driven rather than client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Samir spontaneously offers change talk ('maybe I'll think about the monitoring thing', 'I'm not saying no') but the counselor fails to reflect, amplify, or build on it, pivoting instead to checklists and plans. Change talk occurs despite, not because of, counselor technique.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does not reinforce sustain talk and occasionally redirects, but questions about benefits and side effects invite more sustain talk. Double-sided reflections are never used to pair sustain talk with Samir's expressed ambivalence.
