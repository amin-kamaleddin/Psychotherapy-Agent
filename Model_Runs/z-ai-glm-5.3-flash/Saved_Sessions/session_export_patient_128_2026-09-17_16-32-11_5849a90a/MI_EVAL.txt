# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms Tyler's values and honesty. However, affirmations are sparse and somewhat formulaic, and Tyler's fear of looking 'lame' is acknowledged but not warmly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening accurately tracks Tyler's ambivalence, fear, and desire to cut back rather than quit. Reflections sometimes remain brief summaries rather than deep emotional exploration (e.g., his Sunday-morning shame).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains clear, consistent focus on reducing risky drinking, with every turn advancing the concrete plan. There is no topic drift, and momentum builds steadily toward commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is fully collaborative, with a menu of options, a co-constructed plan, and the counselor role-playing as Tyler's friend at his request. Tyler's own ideas directly determine the final plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: the counselor asks permission before offering options, lets Tyler choose and reject strategies, and confirms his preferences on check-in method. Tyler frames commitments in his own words without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is effectively drawn from Tyler through values exploration, eliciting his own past strategies, and rehearsal that boosted his confidence. Some exchanges lean on counselor-supplied tips and scripts rather than consistently evoking his solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor conveys that expertise resides in Tyler, asking which options feel doable and building the plan from his past successes. Pre-packaged option lists and rapid tips slightly dilute Tyler's sense of generating solutions himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent change-talk cultivation via open questions about goals, eliciting past successes, confidence scaling, and concrete planning. Tyler progresses from tentative ('maybe I could cut back') to active commitment ('I kind of want to see if I pull it off').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor uses double-sided reflections pairing social motives with goals and pivots quickly toward solutions. However, frequently echoing sustain talk ('cut back, not quit,' 'avoid looking lame') somewhat amplifies ambivalence language.
