# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental throughout ('Thanks for being direct — that helps'), with no labeling or disapproval. However, warmth feels somewhat formulaic, and repeated crisis-line disclaimers read as scripted rather than personalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections appear ('It makes sense you'd feel surprised and defensive'), but reflections are sparse and shallow, often quickly followed by information-giving. Emotional nuances like Marek's social identity tied to drinking and his fear after learning the risks go unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use with a clear two-week follow-up and specific monitoring plan. The focus sometimes drifts into information dumps and tactical lists rather than progressing toward resolving ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration (asking permission, offering option menus), but options are counselor-constructed and much of the session is delivered from an expert stance. Marek's own ideas rarely shape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent autonomy support: 'it's absolutely your call,' 'You don't have to do anything dramatic right away,' and framing tracking as gathering facts, not commitment. The counselor explicitly validates Marek's right to decide even if he concludes his drinking is fine.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor rarely draws out Marek's own motivations, relying instead on prescriptive education and tactic menus. No open questions explore his values or reasons for change, and his spontaneous change talk goes undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some inclusive language ('we can figure out what's going on') and deference to his choices, but the counselor frequently shifts into expert/educator mode, delivering guidelines and health-harm lists with limited elicitation. Client contributions do not substantially steer the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marek offers change talk ('I have something I gotta think about,' 'I could write down what I drink'), but the counselor neither evokes nor reinforces it. Instead of exploring his motivation, the counselor pivots quickly to tactics and trackers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it's mostly fine,' 'maybe if I got sick') is passed over without double-sided reflections or gentle redirection. The counselor avoids reinforcing it but responds with neutral avoidance rather than skillful softening.
