# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a neutral, respectful stance with brief affirmations like 'Thanks for sharing' and 'well done.' However, warmth is minimal and somewhat formulaic, and the closing safety-script language feels boilerplate rather than individualized care.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is limited, with most turns consisting of questions, pros/cons lists, and instructions. The psychiatrist does not reflect Hana's meaningful statements (e.g., 'less overwhelming than quit forever') or explore emotional nuances like guilt, social belonging, and appearance concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, coherent focus on smoking reduction, moving logically from exploration to readiness to a concrete tracking plan and practice target. Direction was efficient, though the push toward a structured plan came quickly and somewhat preempted deeper exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration exists through permission-seeking and inviting Hana to pick steps, but power-sharing is uneven. The counselor structures the plan, provides all materials, and recaps unilaterally, leaning toward counselor-directed problem solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is moderate autonomy support through permission-seeking and letting the client choose steps, with 'no pressure, just observe' messaging reinforcing choice. However, rapid-fire option menus and instructions are fairly directive, and affirmations subtly steer toward the counselor's preferred choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor relies heavily on providing information and options rather than drawing out Hana's own motivations. There is little exploration of her personal reasons for change, and her self-generated ideas (like 'holding a drink') are missed opportunities to build on.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist assumes the expert role substantially, supplying the pros/cons, tracking protocol, checklist, reminder message, and Friday script almost entirely on their own. Hana's contributions are acknowledged but do not meaningfully shape the session content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler successfully elicits change talk, and Hana volunteers that the plan sounds 'doable' with a commitment to tracking. However, the psychiatrist does not deepen these statements with elaboration questions, confidence rulers, or reflections amplifying her own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about social benefits and stress relief is acknowledged but not strategically softened. The counselor lists it under 'Pros' and moves on, avoiding reinforcement but making little active use of double-sided reflections to pivot toward change.
