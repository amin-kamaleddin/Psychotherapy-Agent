# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout; the patient explicitly thanked the counselor for not lecturing her. However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic rather than individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** One good early reflection about cigarettes being 'her ten minutes,' but the counselor's responses are dominated by advice and recaps. Key emotional themes — overload, fear of being snappy, minimization of her son's risk — go unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking and progresses coherently to a concrete one-week experiment with follow-up. Direction is achieved through directive advice rather than guiding, and the deeper discrepancy around her son is never pursued.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor drives the agenda with option lists, step-by-step instructions, and unilateral recaps, with no joint agenda-setting. The patient contributes details but her own ideas only fill in minor parts of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive elements like offering a choice between exploring coping or planning, and framing the plan as an experiment. But prescriptive imperatives dominate, and her ambivalence and right to decide are never explicitly honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerges from the patient spontaneously rather than being evoked; there are no open questions about her reasons for change or confidence. The counselor's contributions are almost entirely informational and instructional.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role with prescriptive instructions, menus, and tracking methods delivered without asking what she thinks would work. Her expertise (smoking outside, patches failing) is acknowledged but never shapes the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Commitment language ('I'll do it,' 'my son's worth one week') comes from the patient, not from evocation. Opportunities to deepen motivation ('sixty percent sure') and explore her parental values are missed in favor of logistics-focused questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Repeated sustain talk ('a walk isn't a cigarette,' 'he's fine, he's healthy,' 'patches made me crazy') goes largely unaddressed. No double-sided reflections or reframing; the counselor simply moves past ambivalence to offer more options.
