# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental, acknowledging the stress of the referral and affirming Maya's efforts without lecturing. Held at 4 because warmth is brief and functional, with little deep validation of her fears (blacking out, her mother's reaction) or normalization of her ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses accurate summaries and reflects Maya's concerns, but reflections often read as formulaic restatements with quick pivots to option menus. Emotionally weighty disclosures (blackouts, 'it would destroy her') were acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and harm reduction from opening framing through a concrete, actionable plan with follow-up. No drift, with every exchange tied to the target behavior — exemplary directional control.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates as an equal partner throughout, with Maya's wording, preferences, and requests substantively shaping the plan. Her closing comment ('someone actually asked me what I think') confirms she experienced the session as collaborative rather than directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly asks permission, offers choices, and reinforces that change is Maya's decision ('Quitting isn't required right now'). Pre-packaged option menus slightly constrain the space for Maya to define her own goals, and her 'no promises' is accepted but not explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits intrinsic motivation tied to Maya's design aspirations and graduation, with role-play building her confidence. Evocation is sometimes outsourced to option menus rather than open questions, and deeper values-based exploration (what a design future means to her) is left unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Maya as the expert, evident in her remark that 'nobody's ever actually asked me what I think,' and embraces her edits to the script. Full power-sharing is slightly diluted by frequent menus of options and drafted scripts rather than drawing solutions primarily from Maya.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evokes change language through targeted questions, producing Maya's own reasons ('I don't want to screw up graduation') and volunteered accountability. Not a 5 because the counselor sometimes pre-packages change options rather than letting Maya generate her own solutions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully softens sustain talk with practical reframes ('Quitting isn't required right now') without dwelling on reasons against change. Occasionally restates sustain talk in double-sided summaries without consistently pivoting Maya's language back toward her own reasons for change.
