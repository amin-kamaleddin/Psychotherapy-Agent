# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist validated Aizada's anger and fear, but affirmations felt formulaic and advice-giving frequently displaced warm, individualized regard for her strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections were offered, but they were sparse and quickly followed by advice; key emotional moments (fear of cancer, humiliation, loss of her former life) were underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on GI symptoms, workup, and reducing isolation with concrete next steps, though direction was achieved through task-oriented advising rather than MI-consistent guiding.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Power-sharing was uneven: the clinician supplied expert knowledge and drafts while the patient largely received services, with minimal joint agenda-setting or shared decision-making.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some choices offered and respect for her preferences, but the interaction was largely directive with little explicit emphasis that decisions belonged to Aizada.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist rarely used open questions to elicit the client's own reasons, hopes, or confidence; nearly all change talk emerged spontaneously from the patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly assumed the expert role with test lists, treatments, and directives, offering little acknowledgment of Aizada as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist captured and acted on Aizada's spontaneous change talk (wedding observation, sister visit, walking plan), but rarely evoked or deepened it with open questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not amplified and fears were acknowledged, but extensive biomedical/testing discussion reinforced the sustain position, and no double-sided reflections were used to resolve ambivalence.
