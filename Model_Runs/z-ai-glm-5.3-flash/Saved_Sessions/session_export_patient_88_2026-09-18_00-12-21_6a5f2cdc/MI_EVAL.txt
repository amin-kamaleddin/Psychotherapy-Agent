# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, never lectures or labels the drinking as problematic, and affirms Marcus's expertise throughout. Even potentially judgmental moments (e.g., 'keeping the trust by not getting caught') are met with genuine curiosity rather than disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Responses consistently contain complex, double-sided reflections that capture both content and ambivalence (e.g., weekend normalcy vs. loss of trust). Reflections accurately mirror Marcus's emotional nuances from a peer-oriented, precontemplative perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on alcohol use while progressing naturally from current perspective through trust, honesty, and concrete proof behaviors to a specific first step. Steering follows Marcus's own concerns, so it advances without feeling forced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor fully embodies an equal-partner stance, positioning Marcus as the expert on his own family and co-constructing the plan from his ideas. Even the 'sneaky' trust question is acknowledged with humor and humility rather than defensiveness.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is actively reinforced with legitimate no-change options ('or does it feel like things are fine just the way they are?') and explicit ownership statements ('that choice is entirely yours'). There is no advice-giving or pressure, making Marcus's movement toward change genuinely self-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key insight originates from Marcus — the trust-vs-not-getting-caught distinction, the bike story lesson, the stepped plan, and the selected first step. His arc from 'things feel fine' to articulating a concrete step demonstrates motivation evoked rather than instilled.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located within Marcus ('you know your dad better than anyone here'), and his contributions shape the session's direction at every turn. Marcus himself notices the shift ('there's options I hadn't really considered'), confirming a genuinely client-driven process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is systematically evoked and developed, from the opening 'anything you'd want to be different?' to strong client-generated commitments ('build up to it,' 'it's like a first step'). The counselor strategically highlights Marcus's own insights and closes with an invitation to select a specific step.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence while leaning toward change. However, sustain talk is occasionally mirrored verbatim (e.g., repeating 'just a normal part of the weekend scene, a few beers...'), briefly amplifying status quo language before pivoting.
