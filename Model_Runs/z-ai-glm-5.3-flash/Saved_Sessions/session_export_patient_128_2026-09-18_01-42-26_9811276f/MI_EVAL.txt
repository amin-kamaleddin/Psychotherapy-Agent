# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with strong validation and competence-affirming praise; however, affirmations are so frequent and formulaic that they risk feeling like trained technique rather than spontaneous regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures Tyler's words and ambivalence, including a precise reflection on lost tracking; but reflections stay cognitive and change-side selective, leaving emotional material (shame, fear, identity anxiety) unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, purposeful trajectory toward change planning—from identifying strategies to a concrete Saturday plan and accountability setup—without slipping into agenda-driving or persuasion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently positions Tyler as the expert ('You know these parties better than I do,' 'in your words, not mine') and draws the plan entirely from Tyler's own ideas, with strong ownership of the change effort.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** No advice-giving or telling; the opening explicitly removes pressure ('I'm not here to tell you to give up your social life') and decisions, framing, and commitment language are consistently attributed to Tyler. Testing the plan as an experiment further reinforces autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength: nearly every turn evokes or reinforces change talk, with a clear escalation arc from tentative possibility to firm commitment. Techniques like selective reinforcement, self-attribution, and the creative 'Saturday-Success Tyler' device are textbook-quality.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The back-and-forth feels genuinely collaborative, with questions inviting Tyler's own analysis and the psychiatrist building on his answers; only mildly constrained by the repetitive affirmation structure that keeps turns somewhat scripted.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, skillful change-talk evocation throughout, producing Tyler's strongest commitment language ('Saturday happens, and then we talk,' 'I've got this') and specific, self-generated strategies consolidated into a concrete plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced or argued against, and doubt is reflected accurately then pivoted to solution-building. Limitations: three separate obstacle-focused questions allocate substantial airtime to the sustain side, and no double-sided reflections are used to hold sustain talk alongside change talk.
