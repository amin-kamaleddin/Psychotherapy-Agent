# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, with an appropriate safety check at the opening and a supportive close. Affirmations were sparse and somewhat generic, often immediately followed by advice rather than individualized recognition of the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were present but largely content-level and infrequent, with the session quickly pivoting to advice-giving. A poignant client insight about protecting others' time but not her own was missed as an opportunity for deeper emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on the target behavior of protecting weekly family time, with each exchange building toward concrete implementation. Points deducted for pivoting to a detailed plan prematurely, before fully exploring the client's values and vision.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly handed over fully formed plans and scripts that the client then edited, rather than co-constructing ideas from her expertise. Despite using collaborative language, the default posture was expert-prescriber.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive elements were present, such as offering choices and framing the plan as a low-commitment trial. However, substantial unsolicited advice and detailed prescriptions were given without asking permission.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Aside from a few early exploratory prompts, solutions and motivation came largely from the counselor's pre-built toolkit rather than being drawn out from the client. The client's strong, spontaneous motivation was never deepened with evocative questions about her reasons, hopes, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session was dominated by pre-packaged bullet-point plans, scripts, and checklists, assuming expertise resided with the counselor. The client repeatedly modified the counselor's suggestions, effectively correcting an expert-driven agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client spontaneously generated meaningful change talk, but the counselor rarely evoked or deepened it, offering no open questions about her reasons, confidence, or commitment. Change talk was incidental to the counselor's structured advice rather than actively cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor never reinforced reasons against change and responded practically to mild client hesitations. Little sustain talk occurred, so no active ambivalence work was needed; a higher score would require skillful management of ambivalence that was never tested.
