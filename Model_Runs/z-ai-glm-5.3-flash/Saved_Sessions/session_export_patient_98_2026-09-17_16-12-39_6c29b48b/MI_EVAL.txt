# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with affirmations like 'that takes real persistence' and 'win or learn.' No judgment appears even when Mateo expresses doubt or past patterns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections early on, including Mateo's grandkids goal and fear of flare-ups. However, reflections are somewhat formulaic and deeper themes like distrust of his body and initial hopelessness go unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tight focus on the target behavior with every exchange advancing the plan: specific behavior, concrete time/place, contingency planning, and supporting actions. No topic drift anywhere.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructed plan: Mateo edited the PT message, requested a more casual text to Rosa, and set his own timer, reward, and schedule. The final plan is entirely in Mateo's own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Permission-seeking before advising, offering choices, supporting Mateo's decision to consult his PT first, and letting him set his own confidence ratings. Mateo explicitly recognized his own control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from Mateo via open questions, confidence rulers, and his own goals, with confidence rising from 5 to 8. Some strategies (graded play, breathing, flare plan) were supplied by the clinician rather than evoked, though permission was sought first.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated permission-asking and deferring to Mateo's input shaped the plan substantially. Slightly below a 5 because the psychiatrist drove the structure of options rather than having Mateo generate them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation with confidence rulers used twice and 'What might raise that number?' Mateo's change talk progressed steadily from confidence 5 to 8 with strong commitment language like 'I could start this week' and 'win or learn.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced, and stopping was reframed protectively: 'choosing to rest is protecting your goal.' Limited use of double-sided reflections to acknowledge the legitimate side of his reservations.
