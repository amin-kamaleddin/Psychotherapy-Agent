# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, non-judgmental tone throughout, but acceptance is expressed formulaically, and the early contradiction of Eric's 'I feel fine' risks subtle disapproval. Affirmations of Eric's strengths and honesty are largely absent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; the counselor favors questions, advice, and scripts over reflections. Eric's deeper feelings — the doctor's 'for now' comment, the worsening cough, fear of failure — go unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on smoking with a logical progression toward a concrete plan Eric articulated himself. However, focus centers on logistics and scaffolding rather than resolving ambivalence about the behavior itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative elements exist, such as asking permission and praising Eric's rewrites of scripts. But the counselor unilaterally writes scripts, sets negotiation terms, and supplies the medical plan, making it feel like coach-and-client rather than equal partners.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers choice, accepts refusals, and honors Eric's 'not promising to quit' stance without argument. However, this is undermined by heavy prescriptiveness — dictating exact wording, rules, and terms — without returning ownership of the plan to Eric.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor relies almost entirely on providing content (facts, scripts, tips) rather than drawing out Eric's own motivations. No open questions about his reasons, values, or what change would mean to him; his opening about the cough was met with another script.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor positions himself as advice-giver, supplying word-for-word scripts and prescribing steps, casting Eric as a trainee rehearsing lines. Eric's contributions were accepted, but power-sharing is thin and his expertise is never explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Eric generated change-relevant commitments (house/car rules, check-ins, doctor visit, patch interest), but these emerged indirectly through problem-solving rather than deliberate evocation. No evocative questions, deepening reflections, or consolidation of change talk were used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor doesn't argue with or amplify Eric's sustain talk, which is a strength, but early mild counter-argument ('many effects are invisible') risks reactance. Role-play scripts actually cemented sustain talk ('I won't promise to quit') rather than softening it.
