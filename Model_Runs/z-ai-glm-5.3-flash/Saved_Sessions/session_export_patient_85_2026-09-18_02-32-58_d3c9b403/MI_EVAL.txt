# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed warmth and unconditional positive regard, with well-placed affirmations and a reframe protecting the client's self-worth. Kept from a 5 by somewhat formulaic affirmations and an unanswered direct request for drink-alternative tips.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and insightful, tracking meaning rather than just content. Kept from a 5 by pivoting quickly to problem-solving at clear emotional openings ('gut punch,' 'feeling like a failure') rather than deepening the affective layer.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear strategic focus on the target behavior from start to finish, with every turn advancing a deliberate progression: identify obstacle, generate options, select one, anticipate barriers, assess confidence, commit. Zero topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing with inclusive language and a plan overwhelmingly client-designed. However, Marco's repeated direct requests for information about zero-calorie drinks went unanswered, a notable responsiveness gap.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership was consistently reinforced: Marco chose among his own options, and the counselor never pressured, lectured, or prescribed. The only directive moment merely echoed Marco's own stated commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every reason, plan, and piece of confidence came from Marco himself; the counselor never supplied arguments for change. The session trajectory—from 'feeling like a failure' to leaving motivated—directly demonstrates motivation elicited from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently positioned within the client ('You know your routine better than anyone'), and every substantive decision originated from Marco. The counselor's role was purely facilitative, with no imposed expert views.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook-level skill: systematically moved Marco through the full DARN-to-CAT arc with evocative questions, a scaling question, and a commitment question. Every piece of change talk was immediately reinforced and used as a springboard, yielding specific, time-bound commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced or argued with; the ritual was skillfully converted into a design constraint for the plan. Kept from a 5 by a narrow acknowledge-and-pivot repertoire—no double-sided reflections, and the 'cave by Wednesday' worry went unaddressed due to missing counselor responses.
