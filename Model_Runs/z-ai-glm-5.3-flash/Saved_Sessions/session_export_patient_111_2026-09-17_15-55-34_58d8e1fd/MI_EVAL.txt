# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental despite the patient's hostile, defensive opening, explicitly stating 'I'm here to listen without judgment.' Affirmations were relatively sparse and somewhat generic; stronger, individualized affirmations would have elevated this.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured ambivalence well (e.g., reflecting the tension between smoking as identity and not wanting to lose his wife). Reflections sometimes stayed at summary level rather than deepening into the emotional core, such as the fear behind his wife's word 'kill'.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained steady focus on smoking throughout and progressed from venting to a concrete plan (three evening smokes, pack out of sight, dishes as replacement). Early off-target options like 'planning a calm way to leave' briefly diverted from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission before suggesting, offered choices rather than prescriptions, and let the patient's own ideas (dishes, telling his wife himself) shape the plan. Occasionally supplied solution menus before the patient generated his own options, making some exchanges slightly counselor-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support was central to the session's success, repeatedly affirming choice ('it's your choice how to use this time'). This directly matched the patient's stated need for self-direction ('keep it mine'), fostering genuine ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from within the patient via open questions, confidence scaling, and reflective emphasis on his relational motivation rather than lecturing. Some motivation emerged spontaneously; deeper exploration of his values could have further expanded intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the patient as the expert ('It's your time and your choice'), and his own contributions directly shaped the final plan. Menu-style suggestions were offered fairly often rather than consistently drawing options from the patient first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and reinforced change talk, moving the patient from resistance to 'three instead of five,' a confidence rating of 6-8, and a concrete commitment. Early change talk emerged largely spontaneously rather than from consistent strategic evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never reinforced sustain talk, instead reflecting it neutrally and pivoting effectively toward the patient's relational motivation. Some sustain talk ('I'm not saying I'm quitting') was left unaddressed rather than actively softened with double-sided reflections.
