# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently validates Aizada's pain and self-advocacy without judgment, affirming her diary-keeping and legitimizing her wish for proper examination. Warmth is somewhat transactional, with affirmations brief and formulaic amid heavy task focus.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early empathic reflections accurately capture her fear of cancer and dismissal. However, pivotal vulnerable disclosures — tearfulness at feeling believed, nighttime loneliness — were met with document drafting and option lists rather than emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifted into administrative case management — letter drafting, logistics, and food lists — rather than maintaining focus on the stress-symptom connection and readiness for gut-directed therapy. Direction returned mainly when the client volunteered insights herself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor co-edited documents, respected her 'one step at a time' pacing, and let her decide message content. Collaboration often took the form of counselor-generated menus rather than genuine joint problem-solving built on her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were consistently offered ('Which would feel most helpful?'), and her edits were fully accommodated, with her leaving owning her decisions. Heavy information delivery sometimes replaced exploration of her own reasons.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerged (connecting symptoms to her son's wedding, loneliness, conditional openness to therapy), but largely despite rather than because of counselor technique. Contributions were mostly didactic, with few evocative questions and no reinforcement of change talk when it appeared.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered choices and let the client direct content, but often slipped into an expert/service-provider role — generating scripts and lists, at times acting as her assistant. Little acknowledgment that she holds expertise about her own condition beyond one affirmation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Seeds were planted effectively via the brain-gut explanation and non-pejorative framing, and the client softened. But when change talk emerged, it was rarely deepened with open questions exploring her motivations or readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was skillfully reframed — 'not all in your head' — and biomedical bridges (motility testing, SIBO) honored her framing while opening doors to psychological approaches. Pushback was avoided when she requested deletions, though some sustain talk was accommodated rather than actively softened.
