# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with a defensive mandated teen, validating rather than challenging minimization ('That makes sense'). Fell short of 5 because warmth occasionally felt functional rather than individualized, with few direct affirmations of Malik himself.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured both sides of Malik's ambivalence, including his worry about sarcasm coming across wrong. Reflections stayed mostly at content level, rarely exploring deeper feelings like his hurt over his mother's distrust or fear of social exclusion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained steady focus on cannabis use and its tie to mother conflict, with each exchange ending in a forward-moving question toward a concrete action. The session narrowed into communication coaching, leaving discrepancy between peer-driven use and Malik's own goals underdeveloped.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint with option menus, check-ins before advancing, and Malik actively shaping choices (scenario, technique, timing). The script content was largely counselor-authored, leaving Malik in a rehearser rather than co-author role during that segment.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength: repeatedly affirmed Malik's choice ('it's your call'), honored his boundary of not promising to quit without pushback, and never pressured toward abstinence. Malik set the pace and plan entirely on his own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from within Malik rather than lecturing, eliciting his own reasons (less conflict, regaining trust) and self-generated plan, culminating in 'Tonight's the night.' Confidence/ability talk was never explicitly elicited, and most problem-solving content in the script was supplied rather than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made genuine power-sharing efforts early (asking permission, offering choices, inviting preferences). The middle portion shifted toward advice-giving, with the counselor supplying the entire script and delivery tips while Malik's expertise about his mother was tapped only lightly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk from a precontemplative teen, moving Malik from 'my mom made me come' to his own plan and 'Tonight's the night.' Missed opportunities to deepen commitment, such as asking what makes him hopeful or how confident he feels about sticking to the agreement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged Malik's substantial sustain talk but did not consistently pivot away from it, rarely using double-sided reflections linking sustain talk to change talk. Incorporating 'I'm not ready to quit' directly into the script honored autonomy but arguably reinforced the status quo rather than softening it.
