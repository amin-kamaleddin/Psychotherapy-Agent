# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, validating Daniel's minimizing perspective without judgment and closing with a warm, individualized affirmation. Skepticism and defensiveness were met with respect rather than correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate throughout, capturing both sides of ambivalence and landing precisely on Daniel's core concerns like the 'work is done' ritual. Reflections were consistently met with elaboration, a strong sign of understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on alcohol reduction from open to close, with each turn connecting drinking to Daniel's training goals. The conversation progressed logically from ambivalence exploration to a concrete, time-limited plan with a follow-up date.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was fully co-constructed, with Daniel's own 'dry two weeks' idea and pace-data suggestion incorporated rather than replaced. The closing team-like dynamic ('I'll bring the curiosity, you bring the data and the complaints') crystallized genuine partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Daniel's ownership was explicitly reinforced, including reframing 'nobody's making me do it' as self-driven choice. The counselor affirmed his partial change (weeknights only) rather than pushing abstinence, and accepted his conditionality without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key motivation emerged from Daniel himself via strategic evocative questions, with the patient explicitly noting 'thanks for not just lecturing me.' Change talk like the sabotage discrepancy and anticipated extension to four weeks was voiced by Daniel and developed skillfully.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Daniel was repeatedly positioned as the expert ('you know yourself better than any questionnaire does'), and the entire experiment plan originated from his own ideas. This was active power-sharing where client expertise drove the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was evoked strategically through questions like 'what would that mean to you?' and then reinforced, drawing out increasingly strong commitments ('maybe two weeks becomes four'). This was sustained, skillful evocation culminating in Daniel's own acknowledgment he may need to take it more seriously.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally redirected toward values and change without argument, and minimization was met with affirmation of Daniel's planning. Weekend drinking reluctance was acknowledged but not explicitly explored with double-sided reflections to fully resolve that ambivalence.
