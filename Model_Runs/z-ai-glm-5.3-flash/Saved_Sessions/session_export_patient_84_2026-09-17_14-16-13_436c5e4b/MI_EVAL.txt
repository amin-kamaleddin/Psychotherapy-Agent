# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validating Malik's anger and never moralizing about cannabis use. However, affirmations of Malik himself were sparse and the safety screening felt checklist-like.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections ('it makes sense you're angry'), but emotionally significant statements like 'Hiding it is half the stress' went unreflected. Most turns ended with options or next-step questions rather than understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed well-focused on reducing parent-child conflict, with an early choice of focus and a coherent build from venting to role-play to a concrete two-week plan. Only minor drift into generic psychoeducation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Malik chose the focus, scripts, and role-play content, but the counselor's expert-driven lists of options left him reacting rather than co-constructing. The strategic frame (deals, trials, trade-offs) was imposed rather than built jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Explicit choice and no pressure to quit were strengths, but pre-packaged scripts and directive tips undercut genuine autonomy. Malik's 'I'm not promising to quit entirely' went unaffirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain — rarely asked open questions to draw out Malik's own motivations or values, relying almost entirely on advice and tactics. Spontaneous change talk was never deepened or explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine partnership gestures like offering to vent or ask questions, and collaborative role-play. But overall stance leaned expert-driven, casting Malik as a consumer of the counselor's strategies rather than the expert on his own situation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerged ('maybe I could cut back,' 'I actually want to try this'), but largely by accident through problem-solving rather than deliberate MI strategy. The counselor missed key moments to reflect and amplify commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with Malik's minimization, which was a strength, but sustain talk ('she's overreacting,' 'everyone does it') passed without double-sided reflections or pivots toward discrepancy. Supplying counter-arguments for the mom arguably introduced reasons for concern without resolving ambivalence.
