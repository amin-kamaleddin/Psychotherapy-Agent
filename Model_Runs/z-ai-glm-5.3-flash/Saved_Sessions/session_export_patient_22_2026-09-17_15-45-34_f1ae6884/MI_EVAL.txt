# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirmations ('I'm glad you noticed') and validation of her fears as understandable. Falls short of a 5 because warmth is somewhat procedural and affirmations tie mainly to readiness to change rather than her worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing Camila's situation and reluctance. Later reflections become thinner, and deeper emotional exploration of her anxiety and shame ('I always cave') is not fully pursued.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintains clear focus on reducing weekday drinking and building refusal skills, tied directly to the Friday scenario she identified. Minor drift occurs when offering tactic lists, but the overall trajectory is purposeful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration is present at the start and during the role-play, but the plan tilts toward counselor-supplied scripts and tactic menus rather than co-created solutions. Camila's own ideas emerge despite, not because of, the counselor's approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices, seeks permission, and respects Camila's rejection of abstinence without pressure. A 5 would require more explicit reinforcement of her ownership and capacity to succeed, especially given her unaddressed concern about 'life getting messy.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ruler questions and 'What would you like to be different?' successfully elicit motivation and goals early on. The psychiatrist then shifts to a didactic mode, and Camila's final question about coping when life gets messy—a direct invitation for evocative exploration—goes unaddressed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and offering doable options support client expertise, but the middle of the session becomes noticeably advice-heavy with refusal lines and step lists. This positions the counselor as the source of expertise, inconsistent with MI principles.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes change language via rulers and open questions, and reinforces successes ('That was a big win'), generating substantial commitment language from Camila. However, change talk is not consistently deepened or amplified once it emerges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I don't think I'm an alcoholic,' fear of losing her social life) is acknowledged neutrally without arguing, but is never actively softened or reframed. No double-sided reflections are used to work through the ambivalence.
