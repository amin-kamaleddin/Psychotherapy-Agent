# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental throughout, with explicit unconditional positive regard ('You're the expert on your own life'). The psychiatrist honored Marcus's attachment to his dog rather than pathologizing it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that captured both content and feeling, e.g., 'you're not saying no—you're saying you need it to be small and manageable,' which the client confirmed. Reflections also surfaced deeper emotional meaning, like the 'nagging' concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on asthma management from start to finish, with no topic drift. The clinician guided the conversation from global stressors toward a concrete plan (inhalers, one appointment, weekend talk with wife) with a follow-up check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuine partnership between equals: ideas were offered tentatively, input was sought on every decision, and the plan was co-constructed. Marcus's own ideas (truck inhaler, one appointment, weekend timing) were adopted wholesale into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently reinforced ('This is your pace, your plan'), with no directives or pressure, even on the dog issue. The client explicitly noted it felt 'less like I'm just getting told what to do for once.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited entirely from the client, drawing out his own values, resources, and rationale (crew-leader experience, protecting his wife, dog walks). The session ended with client-owned commitment: 'That's what I can do right now, and that's enough.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly positioned Marcus as the expert on his own life, and questions consistently invited his own solutions. The session's direction was visibly driven by the client's ideas and shaped the final plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked ('What would make this feel doable?', 'What would need to happen for that habit to stick?') and deepened by strategic reflection. Marcus responded with strong commitment language: 'that I could probably do,' 'it's mine,' and 'I want to get it right.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (financial strain, overwhelm, dog resistance, past failure) was acknowledged without reinforcement and often reframed effectively, notably converting the dog from barrier to motivation. Minor deduction because finances and overwhelm were at times fully reflected rather than actively pivoted toward change.
