# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicated nonjudgment and respect consistently, especially the 'no lectures' promise and affirmations of her honesty. Warmth occasionally came packaged in directive tip lists, prompting Lucía to push back twice about sounding like her doctor.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured content (smoking as her break, daughter's reaction, failed patch attempt). Largely missed emotional depth—did not reflect the grief or personal meaning behind 'that stings' and 'it's mine,' pivoting instead to questions and options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking and converted ambivalence into a concrete Thursday walk with her daughter plus structured follow-up. Direction was achieved through checklists rather than collaborative goal-shaping and wasn't connected to her deeper values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative moments (she chose the walk, the day, the check-in), but the style was often transactional with numbered lists and templates. Lucía had to repeatedly reclaim equal footing ('I don't need a script,' 'enough reminders').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support—'low-pressure and on your terms' and 'you can still smoke, no judgment' were explicitly noticed and appreciated by the client. Falls short of 5 because repeated tip lists and reporting requirements subtly imposed the counselor's process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions and rulers surfaced her own motivations (daughter, honesty, autonomy), and she volunteered a possible Friday follow-up. The counselor did not build on these—her reasons and strengths were rarely reflected back to sustain client-generated momentum.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and avoided imposing a plan, but frequently took an expert stance with rapid-fire lists, prefabricated tips, and scripting a line for her daughter. Lucía's repeated corrections indicate her expertise wasn't consistently treated as primary.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively elicited genuine change talk via rulers and open questions, and 'you can still smoke' paradoxically strengthened commitment. After her importance 5 / confidence 3 ratings, the counselor did not explore what would move the numbers or amplify her daughter-centered motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Never argued against sustain talk and skillfully defused defensiveness with the 'no judgment' framing. However, sustain talk was often met with pivots to logistics rather than double-sided reflections pairing ambivalence with her own change statements.
