# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with normalization, confidentiality clarification, and affirmations. However, praise is generic rather than specific to Anya's values and strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections show perspective-taking, but reflections are sparse and formulaic ('That makes total sense' repeated). The counselor repeatedly misses the emotional core — fear of social exclusion and the sting of teasing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related risk moving logically toward a concrete plan with a next step. Focus is slightly scattered early by multiple stacked questions and driven more by a pre-set agenda than client priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative moments exist (role-play negotiation, code word, Maddie plan). But the session often shifts into asymmetric, expert-led teaching with pre-packaged lists and little exploration of what Anya already knows.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent menu-style options with no pressure, and Anya drives key decisions. Falls short because heavy advice-giving implies the counselor holds the answers rather than reinforcing Anya's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some questions draw out Anya's concerns, but most content is counselor-provided strategies rather than elicited reasons, values, or confidence. Importance/confidence are never directly explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices are offered at decision points, but the overall stance is expert-driven with lecture-style lists and one-way knowledge flow. Anya even signals overload ('Whoa, that's a lot of questions at once!').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective questions and skill practice elicit strong client engagement and commitment-like behavior ('that feels doable,' requesting follow-up). Misses opportunities to reflect back and amplify her change talk when it emerges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never amplified and pivots toward skills are effective. However, softening is largely passive — the 'saying no makes you boring' concern goes unvalidated and unreframed.
