# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with respect for Anika's perspective throughout. Affirmations, while frequent, occasionally felt slightly formulaic or adult-voiced for a teenager.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captured ambivalence using the client's own words. Occasionally leaned toward positive reframing rather than fully dwelling in emotional experiences like fear of ridicule or stress.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on vaping connected to Anika's values (basketball, eligibility). Session progressed deliberately from ambivalence exploration through values clarification, confidence testing, obstacle planning, and commitment without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor asked rather than told, incorporating every client contribution into the next question. All strategies came from Anika, with the counselor as curious facilitator, culminating in a fully self-authored plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly stated 'I'm not here to tell you what to do' and framed every decision as Anika's own. Her growing ownership ('on my terms,' 'this week, it's a start') demonstrates effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice, education, or persuasion about vaping harms was offered; all motivation, coping strategies, and commitment language came from the client. Confidence was explored through her own rating and obstacle identification.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Anika as the expert, with every plan element originating from her. Some reflective statements carried a slightly evaluative, approving adult tone, and occasional leading hypotheticals shaped direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked strong change statements ('I play better when I'm not high anyway,' 'this week. It's a start') through open questions and hypotheticals. Each piece of change talk was reinforced with reflections and affirmations, then deepened via confidence and obstacle exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely avoided amplifying sustain talk, using double-sided reflections that paired it with change talk. The social enjoyment theme was revisited several times before being reframed rather than consistently pivoting toward change.
