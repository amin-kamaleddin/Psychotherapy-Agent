# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently positioned the patient as the expert on her own life, repeatedly eliciting her perspective on what would work. There was clear support for her autonomy in designing her own plan, including her decision about the note, schedule, and check-ins.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflected the patient's core experiences, including feeling 'lighter' both physically and emotionally and the weight of 'I need to stop putting myself last.' Reflections captured both her ambivalence and her strengths.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Each response used open questions to focus on change and practical planning, culminating in a concrete plan for specific days, barriers, and check-ins. The conversation moved steadily toward sustaining motivation without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician worked 'with' the patient throughout, inviting her to generate solutions and asking what she wanted from him rather than prescribing. The final plan was explicitly framed as her own creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient authored every element of the plan, from the note's wording to where it would be kept, with the clinician only eliciting and affirming. Language like 'you're the expert' and 'as Monday-morning you feels set up to win' reinforced self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully evoked change talk, drawing out the patient's own reasons ('the ready part gets to drive') and strategies (writing a note in her own words). Patient-generated solutions like 'that voice is lying' were highlighted rather than clinician-imposed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue was highly reciprocal, with the clinician building on each patient statement and asking for her input at every step. The patient repeatedly expressed ownership and even thanked the clinician for making 'it a plan instead' of wishing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong change talk was elicited consistently, including commitment language ('I want to actually stick with something'), ability statements, and visualizations of success. The clinician then deepened this talk by asking her to elaborate on readiness and memorable feelings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'm too tired,' 'I'll start tomorrow') was met with acceptance and reframed as something the patient could problem-solve, rather than argued against. The patient herself transformed ambivalence into a concrete coping strategy.
