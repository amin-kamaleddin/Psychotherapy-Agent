# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'An 8 is really strong — nice work.' However, some praise feels formulaic, and the client's fear that something's wrong with her was not explicitly normalized or validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured multiple layers of the client's experience, including discouragement. Loneliness and chronic self-criticism were reflected only once and not explored with full emotional depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Crystal-clear focus on a specific target behavior, with every exchange advancing toward a concrete, client-shaped plan. Related themes were woven back in without drift, with summaries each turn.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed from the client's own ideas (PB toast, texting mom, self-note). The psychiatrist occasionally front-loaded detailed suggestions before fully eliciting the client's input, creating some uneven power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent permission-seeking and option-giving ('Which option would feel safest...'). Autonomy could be reinforced more by explicitly emphasizing that the client owns the decision and any setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence rulers effectively elicited authentic change talk, moving the client from 5 to 8. Some turns shifted to education and information rather than continuing to evoke the client's own reasoning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked permission and offered options, letting the client's ideas shape the plan. Some prescriptive suggestions (text scripts, prep steps) slightly tilted the expert balance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled evocative questions and confidence rulers generated commitment language ('I think I want to try it'). Some multi-part questions diluted the evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about the scale and fear of gaining was met with collaborative reframing and normalization. Sustain talk was sometimes pivoted to problem-solving quickly rather than fully reflected first.
