# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** The clinician conveyed unconditional positive regard and affirmed the patient's worth as a parent ("That commitment matters"; "You're already doing something powerful"). The patient's intrinsic value was never questioned, even while exploring her role in the conflict. Affirmations were specific and genuine, tied directly to the patient's own observations and courage.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Consistent, accurate reflective listening throughout. The clinician reflected both content and emotion ("There's tenderness underneath it too: you're doing this for him"; "There's a steadiness in your voice now that wasn't there an hour ago"). Reflections deepened the patient's insight and she repeatedly responded with further self-disclosure, indicating she felt deeply understood.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** The interview maintained a clear focus on the desired change (calmer Sunday handoffs/co-parenting) without drifting. Each response guided the patient toward envisioning change, identifying exceptions, and building a concrete plan, while remaining rooted in the patient's own material. The session closed with a strong sense of purpose and forward momentum ("I'm not walking in braced for war anymore—I'm walking in for my son").

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly positioned the patient as the expert: "You know this rhythm, and yourself, far better than I ever could—I'd rather build on your ideas than hand you mine." The plan was entirely co-constructed from the patient's suggestions, and the clinician reinforced this repeatedly ("Every piece of that plan came from you"). No advice was imposed.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Choice and personal agency were emphasized at every step: "The one thing you can't control is what his dad says... walking in lighter is yours to choose"; "That's mine to keep." The confidence ruler was framed without judgment ("There's no right number. Wherever you land tells us something worth hearing"), honoring the patient's honest self-assessment of a 5–6.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Masterful elicitation of the patient's own motivations and solutions. Open questions drew out her vision of change ("what would that look like?"), exceptions ("are there moments when things already feel that way?"), and self-generated strategies. The patient explicitly recognized this: "You didn't tell me what to do—you made me see I already knew most of it."

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** The conversational rhythm was balanced, with the clinician building each exchange on the patient's prior words. Power-sharing was explicit and consistent; the patient experienced herself as an active collaborator ("I'm actually the expert on my own family"), which directly strengthened her confidence from a 2 to a 6.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Abundant, high-quality change talk was elicited across all categories: desire ("I want to try. For him"), ability ("That part is mine to choose"), reasons ("My son would see his mum calm instead of braced"), and commitment ("Six. Let's say six"). The confidence ruler skillfully deepened commitment, and the patient closed by asking to return and report progress—a strong indicator of mobilized change talk.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Sustain talk ("He shouldn't have to carry that"; "I can't promise his dad won't say something that sets me off"; "I can't say eight or nine") was met with acceptance and reframed rather than confronted. The clinician used the patient's own exceptions (calm Sunday moments) to counter hopelessness, and gently separated responsibility from blame ("it doesn't mean you're to blame... it means you have some influence"). Doubt was transformed into realistic confidence.
