# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, especially in honoring the client's boundary on drinking ('I won't push that'), with warm affirmations. Falls short of 5 because warmth is brief and transactional, with little explicit normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections early on, but reflective listening is thin, with quick pivots to options and plans. The client's core maternal motivation was misread as suicidal ideation and never revisited, and her minimization was not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus from broad 'stress' to a specific measurable target behavior (swapping one evening cigarette for box breathing), anchored by checklist, reminder, and follow-up. The extended risk-screening moment briefly diverted direction, though recovery was quick.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership elements: incorporated client revisions, checked in before offerings, and scheduled around her needs, leaving her with genuine ownership. However, the counselor often presented complete packages for approval rather than building plans jointly from the client's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently honored through menus, permission-seeking, and acceptance of the client's pace — directly confirmed by her closing comment ('you didn't lecture me'). Falls short of 5 because suggestions were dense and prescriptive, with the client adapting to a pre-formatted framework.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation emerged organically (maternal concern, plan parameters, readiness rating), but the counselor rarely asked direct evocative questions and never explored the 5/10 readiness score. Much of the session supplied content rather than drawing out the client's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeated permission-asking and choice-offering convey that client input matters, and her contributions shaped the plan. Still, an expert-driven undertone persists: the counselor supplied most content while the client's role was mostly to accept or adjust.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective choice architecture generated authentic client change talk ('I could try three instead,' readiness statement, maternal motivation). Missed opportunities to deepen it — not reflecting commitment language or asking what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with neutral acceptance rather than debate, which is a strength. But no double-sided reflections were used, and the peer-comparison minimization was ignored rather than gently reframed — ambivalence was sidestepped, not resolved.
