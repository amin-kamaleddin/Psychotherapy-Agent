# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates warm, unconditional regard throughout, validating Tariq's pain without judgment and explicitly countering his shame. No disapproval, labeling, or correction of his frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and progressively deepens, from surface content to precise emotional reflections with accuracy checks. Double-sided reflections and mirroring of his core value ('not for the officer, for her') show genuine understanding of meaning, not just content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session follows a clear forward trajectory from venting to values to concrete plan with consistent momentum. However, early grievance exploration lacks a summarizing checkpoint, and no closing summary consolidates the final plan or next step for the review meeting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tariq's ideas determine the session agenda — the letter, lawyer, review meeting, and check-in request all come from him. The clinician asks rather than tells and meets the highest standard of power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership are consistently reinforced through open 'what feels right' framing and explicitly returning responsibility to Tariq. The clinician proposes no solutions, and Tariq's response shows genuine self-authored ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently elicited from within rather than supplied, with questions drawing out Tariq's own reasons — his mother's sacrifices, his identity, his father's words. The strongest change talk of the session is entirely self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positions Tariq as the expert ('You know this place better than anyone'), and every strategy originates from Tariq. The clinician checks accuracy and the plan is entirely the client's own.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and deepens change talk at every turn through open questions eliciting Tariq's own reasons and knowledge. This yields rich spontaneous change talk ('maybe I write mom a letter tonight... For her'), which the clinician reinforces by reflecting it back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced or amplified, with the psychiatrist acknowledging it briefly before pivoting to Tariq's agency and values. However, some reflections briefly dwell on the grievance before redirecting, with limited use of intentional reframing to resolve ambivalence.
