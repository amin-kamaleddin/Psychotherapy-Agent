# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone, affirmed the patient's ideas, and respected her humor despite the gambling and debt content. Affirmations were somewhat generic, and ambivalence and past failed attempts were not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good reflective statements early in the session gave way to question barrages and advice lists, reducing reflective listening. Emotional undercurrents — shame about past failures, self-critical 'bad mom'-style statements, fear about friendships — went largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on gambling and card spending and progressed to a concrete action plan with timing, support, and a Sunday self-check-in. Focus was sometimes maintained through rapid-fire task questions rather than strategic summaries tying actions back to the patient's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with the patient modifying wording, choosing the support text, and redefining her approach to friends. The counselor nonetheless dominated the conversational structure with multiple bundled questions per turn, making pacing uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offered choice, framed advice as optional, and let the patient select, edit, and reject options. The volume of prescriptive lists and repeated scaling questions had a subtle pressuring quality, and ownership of setbacks was not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicited client-generated motivation, including the Egypt dream as a values anchor and the patient's own strategies, moving readiness from 6 to confidence 7. Evocation relied heavily on formulaic scaling questions rather than varied exploration of deeper values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently sought the patient's input and let her edits shape the plan. A drift toward expert mode with long advice lists and rapid multiple questions slightly undercut the sense that expertise resides with the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was effectively elicited and reinforced, including concrete steps and the 'Egypt, not pokies' mantra, with confidence rising to 7. Commitment language was not systematically deepened (e.g., exploring why this time is different).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubtful statements were not amplified, and the sustain-ish 'cutting out the girls' remark was gently reframed by eliciting the patient's own solution. Double-sided reflections to honor ambivalence were underused.
