# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, never criticizing Elena's guardedness or reliance on mom-group forums, and offers brief affirmations ('You're doing great planning this'). However, warmth is functional and terse, with little explicit validation of her emotional experience as a parent navigating conflicting information.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections adequately capture Elena's perspective, but as the session progresses the counselor shifts into a heavily informational stance. Notable emotional statements ('I was kind of gambling,' 'a weight's lifted') receive no reflective response at all.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains exceptionally clear focus on the vaccine decision from opening question to concrete plan, with no topic drift. Every exchange advances the goal, culminating in a specific commitment to the first dose at next month's checkup.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frames the work as joint ('We can explore risks, benefits, and options together') and consistently checks Elena's preferences. The score falls short of 5 because collaboration often takes the form of the counselor producing deliverables (summaries, scripts, checklists) rather than genuine two-way problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is a clear strength: the counselor repeatedly offers menus of choice, affirms Elena's 'ease into it' approach without pushing the full schedule, and never pressures. Elena's closing thanks for 'not just lecturing me' directly validates this respect for her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain—almost no evocative questions are asked, and Elena's own motivation ('I was kind of gambling') emerges largely from her rather than being drawn out and deepened. The motivation shift occurs primarily through supplied facts, contradicting the MI principle that motivation should be elicited, not instilled.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena's input is genuinely solicited and visibly shapes the session's direction. However, the counselor frequently shifts into an expert-educator role with pre-formed materials, and despite Elena's cue ('I don't want to just be told what to do'), the overall structure remains counselor-provided content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena generates meaningful change talk ('I'm not saying no anymore,' 'we'll book her in'), but the counselor neither reflects nor deepens it, instead asking mostly logistical questions. These key MI opportunities are missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is briefly acknowledged and not reinforced, but the pivot relies on didactic persuasion (complication statistics) rather than MI-consistent techniques like double-sided reflections or eliciting Elena's own counter-arguments. The shift from hesitancy to openness resulted largely from information delivery.
