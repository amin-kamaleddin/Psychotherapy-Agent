# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, validating Marcus even when he was defensive ('I don't need a lecture, I'm not five') with responses like 'no judgment, just options' and 'that makes sense.' Marcus was never judged, labeled, or shamed despite sustained resistance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and frequent, consistently hitting the emotional undercurrent — the fear beneath the blackout bravado, peer-pressure concerns, and embarrassment about practicing lines. Reflections captured minimization without challenging it and built the plan around Marcus's social reality.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on alcohol use and party safety, with each exchange moving purposefully from quantifying use through rulers to a concrete plan and refusal skills. The shift from reducing drinking to harm reduction was clinically appropriate but could have been named and negotiated explicitly with Marcus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned as genuine teamwork between equals — Marcus's ideas determined which strategies survived, and the counselor built on rather than corrected his contributions. Marcus himself synthesized the plan ('that's actually a whole system'), and practice happened at his pace with no pushback.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was reinforced at every turn: permission was sought before offering information, all choices were returned to Marcus ('Your call,' 'pick one that sounds like you'), and his limits ('Two ideas, then we're done') were honored without pressure. Marcus's ownership of the final plan was complete.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from Marcus himself through rulers, open questions, and permission-based menu options rather than persuasion. When he resisted, the counselor kept eliciting rather than arguing, keeping the locus of motivation squarely within Marcus — the strongest dimension of this session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned Marcus as the expert, with every plan element originating from his own social world — the beer-holding trick, the Danny nod signal, and refusal lines selected by what 'sounds most like me.' Counselor suggestions were offered only after asking permission, demonstrating textbook power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evoked change talk from a precontemplative client using open questions, importance/confidence rulers, and commitment attempts, progressively deepening Marcus's own concerns ('the blackout was kinda freaky'). By session's end, Marcus had verbalized a fully self-generated plan — pacing, deflecting, a backup line, and recruiting Danny — in his own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoided amplifying sustain talk, meeting statements like 'everyone does it' with neutral reflections and forward-moving questions rather than debate. Some responses restated sustain talk before reframing (double-sided reflections), which was competent but slightly repetitive — a leaner approach would earn a 5.
