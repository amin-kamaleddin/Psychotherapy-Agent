# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, e.g., 'you're not failing at anything here; you're carrying a lot.' The 'stubborn teenager' part is validated rather than pathologized, and the final affirmation is individualized and warm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate and emotionally attuned, using the client's own metaphors (the fire, 'staying in the game'). The psychiatrist's question about whether breakdown is forgetting or avoidance unlocked the client's key insight about pill-taking making diabetes 'real.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on medication adherence throughout, with each turn advancing toward a concrete behavioral plan (note by the coffee maker, telling husband, evening dose later). No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership: 'worth exploring together,' 'these are your solutions,' and the entire plan emerges from Selene's ideas. Her own metaphors become the session's working language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client chooses the note, location, wording, timing, and whether to involve her husband; the psychiatrist never prescribes. Her pacing ('one thing at a time') is explicitly honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within: her 'why' (kids, mother's story), an 8/10 confidence rating, self-generated accountability ('Prove it'), and newly expressed curiosity. The 'staying in the game' frame was claimed and personalized by the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client ('You're the expert on your own life and schedule'), and all solutions are elicited rather than prescribed. Client contributions substantially shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and developed via the 'staying in the game' reframe, a confidence scaling question, and prompts for commitment strategies, culminating in 'you said eight out of ten. Prove it.' Preparatory talk about evening doses and bloodwork also emerges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally handled well through double-sided reflections and compassionate reframing ('now you can see it coming'). Minor limitation: the 'stubborn teenager'/'rebellion' material is explored at some length before pivoting, though productively.
