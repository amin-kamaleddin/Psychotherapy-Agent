# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with consistent affirmations throughout. However, praise is somewhat generic, with limited validation of the client's emotional vulnerability (e.g., her fear-of-failure pattern received only a practical tip).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal, with responses quickly pivoting to advice and checklists rather than reflecting the client's statements. Occasional empathic moments exist, but emotional content (self-criticism after missed days, frustration with sales pressure) goes unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior from start to finish, with logical progression from motivation to concrete plans and a clear closing recap. No topic drift; direction is the standout strength of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine integration of client input (schedule preferences, injuries, dislikes). However, the default mode is dispensing expert content in bulk, with the client's role largely to approve rather than co-create ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** No pressure or coercion, with some choice offered at the margins. But the overall structure is counselor-prescribed, with no explicit emphasis that the choices and process belong to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor relies on structure and practical tools rather than drawing motivation from the client. Rich spontaneous motivational statements ('I want to feel stronger,' 'ten out of ten confident') are never explored, elaborated, or reflected back.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role, generating nearly all content (plans, circuits, scripts, trackers) with minimal client input. Client contributions mostly take the form of agreement rather than shaping the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no deliberate effort to evoke change talk despite the client offering abundant spontaneous change talk. No open questions, rulers, or elaboration prompts (e.g., asking what makes her 10/10 confident).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is limited and handled neutrally without amplification, which is appropriate. However, there are no strategic redirects, double-sided reflections, or reframes to consolidate resolve around the barriers mentioned.
