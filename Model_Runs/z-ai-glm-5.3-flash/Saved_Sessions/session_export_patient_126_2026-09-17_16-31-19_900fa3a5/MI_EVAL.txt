# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, respecting Thomas's reluctance and avoiding scare tactics or labels. Affirmations were somewhat sparse and formulaic, and there was limited explicit normalization of his ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent brief reflections accurately captured Thomas's perspective, including his dislike of scare tactics and fear of a lecture from his wife. Reflections tended to stay brief and pair with questions rather than deepening the emotional undercurrents, such as the meaning of his wife's eye-rolls.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session kept clear direction through a logical progression: values, risk information, behavior change steps, confidence scaling, and commitment. The smoking thread was dropped quickly rather than explicitly negotiating the agenda, and some redundant questions diluted momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative, with permission-seeking before sharing information and letting Thomas select and modify strategies. However, the batched advice lists felt expert-driven, and the final summary was clinician-generated rather than co-constructed in Thomas's words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Thomas's ownership through permission-seeking, choice-offering, and accepting his refusals of rehearsing and timers without pressure. Honored Thomas's self-determined pace and choices throughout the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivation from within via values questions and confidence scaling, with Thomas generating his own solutions and reasons for change. The psychiatrist frequently supplied ideas himself through option menus rather than consistently asking for Thomas's own ideas or deepening his stated motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed decisions around Thomas's expertise and let his contributions substantially shape the final plan. Early 'you're the expert' framing and rapid-fire option menus sometimes positioned the psychiatrist as the primary idea source.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through values questions and confidence scaling, moving Thomas from 'not much to focus on' to specific self-generated commitments and raising his confidence from 5 to 7. Some opportunities to deepen change talk were traded for quickly offering advice menus.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it, responding with brief reflections and pivots toward change while avoiding argument or confrontation. Occasionally spent time restating sustain talk content rather than consistently pivoting toward change language.
