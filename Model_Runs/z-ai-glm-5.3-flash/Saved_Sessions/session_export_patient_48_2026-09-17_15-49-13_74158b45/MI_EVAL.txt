# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicated consistent unconditional positive regard with a warm, non-evaluative tone throughout. No judgment or disapproval appeared, even when the patient disclosed the failed gym membership and self-skepticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections repeatedly captured both content and feeling, including discouragement about past failures. Reflections were sometimes immediately followed by multiple questions, diluting the depth of empathic exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity and progressed steadily toward a concrete plan with days, times, cues, backup plans, self-monitoring, and a follow-up. There was no topic drift; every exchange advanced the change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with the patient's preferences shaping every decision point, including the check-in format and counselor's response style. The counselor explicitly honored the patient's request for accountability rather than imposing one.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient's ownership was repeatedly affirmed, with no pressure or coercion, and his 'no guarantees' caveat was accepted. The counselor sometimes steered with prescriptive menus (specific times, phrases) rather than letting the patient fully generate his own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently drew out the patient's own motivations, confidence ratings, and success markers. Peak change talk like 'kind of okay... weird' and 'more up for trying than an hour ago' could have been explored or amplified more deeply.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient directly shaped the plan, choosing lunchtime walks, the alarm cue, backup options, and check-in format. At times the counselor offered pre-packaged menus of options rather than openly eliciting the patient's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong change talk was successfully evoked, including 'I want to feel better' and 'I'm more up for trying than I was an hour ago,' and was affirmed. The counselor did not always deepen the most promising change talk before moving to the next question.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about past failures and self-doubt was acknowledged neutrally and effectively reframed with 'one missed walk isn't failure' and small doable steps. Occasionally listing options briefly kept attention on obstacles rather than pivoting to the patient's own solutions.
