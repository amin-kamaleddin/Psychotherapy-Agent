# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance even when Erik opened defensively, and never pushed abstinence after his refusal. Warmth was somewhat checklist-like, with closing reminders feeling practical rather than genuinely individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections like naming Erik's anger and normalizing drinking to unwind visibly softened him. Deeper emotional content, especially his fear about the diagnosis and lifelong medication, was left largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on alcohol and diet, progressing logically to a specific, measurable plan. Direction was sometimes achieved by instruction rather than guiding Erik toward self-set goals, and the emotional agenda was dropped without negotiation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik's selections did shape the final plan, but the pattern was largely counselor-provides-list, client-picks-one. The counselor rarely asked for Erik's own ideas or built on his knowledge (e.g., low-alcohol beer at the barbecue, his wife's cooking role).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered genuine menu choices and accepted Erik's refusal of grounding exercises and total abstinence without pressure. Plans were counselor-generated rather than client-authored, and ownership framing was weak.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and content were largely supplied by the counselor, with almost no open questions about Erik's own reasons, importance, or confidence for change. His self-generated motivations (his wife, being easier to live with, proving the doctor wrong) were never surfaced or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for Erik's preferences and let his choices shape the plan, but the middle of the session was heavily advice-driven in an expert stance. Little acknowledgment that expertise about his life resided with him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menu options elicited useful client language ('this I could probably do', 'let's lock it in'), but mostly as a byproduct rather than skillful evocation. Missed opportunities to deepen commitment language, including Erik's spontaneous request for accountability after slips.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue with sustain talk and effectively rolled with resistance when Erik refused abstinence. Little active softening—no double-sided reflections or deliberate redirecting to his own change statements.
