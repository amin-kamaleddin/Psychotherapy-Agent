# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, including validating protective avoidance ('It's not stupid at all') and honoring his worth without judgment despite his criminal history. No labeling or conditionality appears anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that reaches emotional meaning, e.g., reframing 'a job I hate' as 'meaning behind the paycheck' and capturing the fear of being told no by someone he looks up to. Reflections repeatedly go beneath surface content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear progressive focus on transitioning toward stable pro-social employment across all turns. Each exchange builds strategically toward a concrete, client-designed action step without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operates between equal partners, with the counselor positioning himself as curious rather than expert and Timur's contributions shaping every subsequent step. The action plan was co-discovered with Timur as primary author.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of ownership and choice ('that's your call and your pace', 'in your own time') with no advice or pressure. Even self-imposed accountability is credited as Timur's own design.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question draws out Timur's own reasons and capacities, and his motivation visibly escalates from 'not yet' to 'closer than I've ever been.' The counselor never supplies reasons for change; all change arguments come from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located within Timur ('You know yourself best here'), and the entire action plan (texting the chippy, telling Fiona, reporting back) originated from him. The counselor offers no advice or prescriptions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoke escalating commitment language, from 'maybe I text him this week' to 'this week. The text gets sent.' The counselor strategically reflects this back, amplifying desire, ability, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated and effectively pivoted toward possibility via double-sided reflections, e.g., reframing 'I'm lazy' as an effort/meaning issue. Some questions briefly re-evoke the pull toward crime, which was handled well but could be more economical.
