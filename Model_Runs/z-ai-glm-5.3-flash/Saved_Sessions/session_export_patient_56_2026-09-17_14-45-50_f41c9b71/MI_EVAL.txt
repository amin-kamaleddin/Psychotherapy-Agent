# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with consistent affirmations throughout the session. Points lost for affirmations that are somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized to the client's values or character.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capturing both content and emotion, such as the client's commitment to family happiness and frustration about slipping time. Reflections are occasionally brief, with quick transitions to questions that miss deeper emotional layers like fatigue, guilt, and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus maintained on the target behavior of evening presence with family. Session progresses logically from values exploration to a fully specified, concrete plan without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely egalitarian interaction with the counselor asking permission and seeking the client's input on which options fit her style. Collaboration is briefly uneven when the counselor supplies multiple scripted replies and text versions rather than eliciting the client's own wording first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of client ownership, with every suggestion framed as an option she can accept or decline. All elements of the final plan originated from the client's own words and choices, with no pressure or coercion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from the client through open questions, confidence rulers, and implementation prompts that surfaced her own reasons, solutions, and success criteria. This culminated in strong self-generated commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positions the client as the expert, and her ideas substantially shaped the plan. Occasional slips into a slightly expert role when offering scripts and revised text versions go beyond pure elicitation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation of change talk through open questions, confidence rulers used twice, and preparation-focused prompts. These effectively deepened commitment talk, culminating in the client's strong statement of a real start.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal and handled well when it appeared, reframing fear of failure as a desire for something realistic. Slight deduction because these moments were met with practical problem-solving rather than reflective softening of the underlying anxiety.
