# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout with sustain talk received respectfully rather than corrected. Affirmations tended to be global ('real hope in your voice,' 'something remarkable') rather than tied to specific, individualized strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were consistently accurate and often captured underlying meaning, with the client repeatedly confirming feeling understood ('That hits something'). Reflections tended to restate and build on the client's words rather than capturing deeper, not-yet-spoken emotion like shame and grief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on moving from rigid dieting toward sustainable behavior change, with every question advancing the arc. Direction was achieved almost entirely through questioning rather than summarizing or explicit agenda-setting, and the body dissatisfaction theme was less directly explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent joint inquiry with shared power ('let me turn it back to you,' 'what feels right'), and all plan content originated from the client. The clinician subtly steered toward a fixed agenda (away from dieting/weight focus) rather than fully negotiating it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Near-exemplary autonomy support: the client generated the exercise plan, weekly structure, and journaling ideas herself, with no prescriptions—only open questions and reflections. The sequence of questions somewhat pre-shaped the choice toward a non-diet framework, with no explicit statement that weight loss remained a valid option.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clinician consistently positioned the client as the expert and elicited her own motivations, visibly building her motivation in real time from ambivalence to excitement. A few moments (e.g., the 'kinder approach' framing) inserted the clinician's preferred narrative rather than purely evoking the client's.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client repeatedly positioned as the expert ('You know your history better than anyone'), and her contributions genuinely shaped the session's plan. Done through deft questioning rather than fully shared formulation, with the clinician's hypotheses subtly leading.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's core strength: nearly every turn deliberately evoked the client's change language, yielding rich change talk ('I could commit to that,' 'I want that') that was then reflected back and amplified. Approaching mastery; slightly more varied techniques (scaling questions, elaborative follow-up on her excitement) would round it out.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and de-emphasized with strong double-sided reflections, and the clinician wisely never argued with the weight-loss desire. Sustain talk was neutralized rather than actively worked through—the persistent desire to lose weight and fear of scale stalls were reflected but never revisited or resolved.
