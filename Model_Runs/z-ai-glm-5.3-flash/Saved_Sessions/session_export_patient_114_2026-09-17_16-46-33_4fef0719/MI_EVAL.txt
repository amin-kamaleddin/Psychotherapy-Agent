# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Ryan without judgment, treating anxiety about the incident and parental disappointment with respect. Affirmations were somewhat tied to performance and tasks rather than deeper, individualized affirmations of Ryan's worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections were present but most responses moved quickly from reflection to advice and task lists. Deeper emotional content (scholarship fear, feeling like 'the weird one', questioning friendships) was acknowledged but not explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained unwavering focus on risky drinking and moved purposefully from rulers to options, rehearsal, and a concrete written plan. Every turn tied back to the Friday party, scholarship, or practical safeguards.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative moments existed (co-creating the Marcus text, choosing options, role-play), but the session often followed a give-advice/get-rubber-stamp pattern with counselor-directed content. Inviting Ryan's ideas first before offering menus was limited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choice and accepted Ryan declining further rehearsal, preserving ownership of the plan. Advice was frequently given in lists before asking for Ryan's own ideas, subtly positioning the counselor as the source of solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ruler questions and role-play drew out strong client insights ('maybe that says something about who I'm around', 'I don't owe anyone a reason'). However, motivation was often supplied through option menus and counselor-provided reasons rather than consistently evoking Ryan's own values and motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ryan shaped specifics (choosing Marcus, the emoji, the refusal line), but the session was advice-heavy with the counselor supplying nearly all solution content. Little explicit positioning of Ryan as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was consistently elicited via importance/confidence rulers and developed through role-play practice. Ryan moved from ambivalence to concrete commitment ('I didn't cave. Huh.', 'that's proof I don't have to choose between my friends and my future').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with normalization and reframing rather than amplification, and the pushy-friend role-play creatively confronted it. Some sustain talk (e.g., 'cash-only seems a bit much') was met with a list of new options rather than rolling with it or a double-sided reflection.
