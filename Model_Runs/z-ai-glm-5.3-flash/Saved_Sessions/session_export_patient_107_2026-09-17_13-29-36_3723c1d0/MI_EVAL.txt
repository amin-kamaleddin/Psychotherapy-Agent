# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, honoring the client's hesitancy and framing tracking as a low-stakes step. Warmth was somewhat procedural, with missed opportunities to affirm his honesty about coffee's personal meaning.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered basic accurate reflections but they were sparse and shallow given the emotionally meaningful material the client shared. Repeatedly pivoted to logistics instead of exploring moments like 'Nobody's ever put it that way to me before.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on the target behavior and moved toward a concrete behavioral experiment tied to the presenting concern. Direction drifted toward logistics management rather than clarifying the client's motivation and goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Solicited the client's preferences and co-scheduled check-ins, but the session's structure was largely counselor-designed with the client consenting rather than co-creating. The client's own insights weren't visibly built into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language was present, including explicit affirmation of 'doesn't mean I'm committing to anything yet' and 'only change things you agree to.' At times steered toward a pre-selected menu of strategies rather than opening the field to the client's own ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Beyond one early readiness question, made little effort to elicit the client's own motivations, values, or reasons for change. The client's spontaneous motivation was consistently redirected toward tools and logistics rather than drawn out and developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for client preferences and affirmed his agreement, which the client explicitly valued. The interaction remained expert-led, positioning the counselor as planner and the client as implementer, with little effort to draw out the client's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created conditions for change talk, and the client generated meaningful statements like 'maybe the half-caf swap' and 'I brought this up for a reason.' These were rarely highlighted, reflected, or deepened, with no evocative questions about importance or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Responded reasonably well to frequent sustain talk by validating ambivalence and reframing change as ritual-preserving. Spent considerable time on logistics rather than strategically resolving ambivalence, and gentle framing sometimes sidestepped it rather than working with it.
