# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirmed Kieran's perspectives without labeling his apathy as resistance. However, affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, with limited individualized expressions of genuine regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and captured Kieran's resignation, frustration, and the meaning of the bike project. Reflections sometimes stayed at the summary level, missing deeper emotional content (the loss of the bike, the pain behind 'what's even the point of trying').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear focus on disengagement and purposelessness and steered consistently toward a concrete behavioral step anchored to Kieran's values. Direction occasionally felt pressured during the rapid planning barrage, but focus never drifted and the session ended with a specific commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kieran co-constructed the plan and his preferences shaped the intervention (dropped the chart, adopted his chair-tip reminder). However, collaboration was undermined by pre-packaged menus, the 'I'll steer' handoff, and rapid-fire planning questions that prompted Kieran to say 'slow down, one thing at a time.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly affirmed Kieran's choice and ownership ('You chose it and you're in charge') and respected his refusal of the if-then chart. Autonomy was slightly diminished by repeated multi-option menus Kieran experienced as imposed choices and pressing planning questions after he signaled he was done.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evocative questions (the bike memory, one small change, readiness/confidence rulers) successfully drew out Kieran's own reasons — progress, ownership — and genuine commitment language. Evocation fell short of maximal because the meaning of Kieran's pointlessness and his final vulnerable disclosure weren't deeply explored, and some questions were bundled into multi-part prompts.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kieran's expertise and contributions clearly shaped the plan, and the counselor honored his rejections. This was undercut early by menu-driven questions, the 'I'll steer' statement, and a rapid-fire list of planning questions that overtook the client's pace.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively evoked change talk from a resistant precontemplative client, eliciting 'Have a reason to get up, maybe,' a readiness of 'a three, maybe,' and commitment language ('I said I'd do it, I'll do it'). Missed an opportunity to deepen and amplify Kieran's closing statement ('the first thing in a while that's actually mine').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it doesn't matter,' 'it won't fix anything') was consistently acknowledged briefly and redirected toward change-oriented exploration without reinforcement. The counselor could occasionally have more explicitly validated the legitimacy of Kieran's doubts before pivoting.
