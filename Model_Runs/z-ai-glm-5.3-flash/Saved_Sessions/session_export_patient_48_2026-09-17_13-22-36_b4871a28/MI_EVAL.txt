# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor was warm and nonjudgmental, reframing Farid's self-criticism about laziness and encouraging self-kindness. However, affirmations were brief and formulaic, with no individualized statements of Farid's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was notably scarce; the counselor pivoted quickly to advice and questions instead of reflecting Farid's statements. Emotionally significant disclosures like 'I don't want to set myself up to fail again' received no reflective acknowledgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear focus on exercise and progressively narrowed the conversation into a concrete plan (Wednesday walk, cues, reminders, follow-up). However, direction was achieved through premature problem-solving rather than collaborative goal clarification, and broader values were never explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor used collaborative framing ('we'll troubleshoot together') and sought input on details like reminder tone. However, the counselor generated nearly all ideas and delivered them as instructions, with Farid's role limited to approving or tweaking.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy was supported through offering choices and accepting Farid's refusal of the backup script, but the counselor supplied the entire solution plan as directives. Ownership was never explicitly returned to the client, who had to request accountability himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor relied on providing reasons and solutions rather than eliciting Farid's own motivations, with only one evocative question whose answer went unexplored. Farid's motivations surfaced through his own initiative, not counselor skill.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite occasional invitational questions, the counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role with prescriptive solution lists before asking what Farid thought or had tried. The dynamic resembled a coach issuing a training plan rather than a partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Farid's change talk emerged mostly spontaneously rather than through skillful evocation, and the counselor's single confidence question was not explored further. Change talk was rarely reflected back or reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not amplify sustain talk and effectively reframed the 'laziness' narrative, but key doubts like 'nothing stuck' and 'I wonder if I'll actually do it Wednesday' went unaddressed. Sustain talk was ignored rather than strategically softened.
