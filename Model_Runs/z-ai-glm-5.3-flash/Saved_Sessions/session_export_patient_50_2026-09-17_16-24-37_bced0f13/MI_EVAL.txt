# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with multiple affirmations and normalization of Hana's discomfort (e.g., self-compassion feeling 'fake at first'). Warmth is somewhat formulaic and brief, lacking the deeper individualized positive regard needed for a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate double-sided reflections capture Hana's ambivalence well (e.g., 'tired of waiting but the worry still hijacks you'), though reflections occasionally become repetitive. Deeper emotional content — shame from years of avoidance and fear of being seen failing — is acknowledged but not explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (taking a first concrete step toward the exam) with no topic drift. Every turn advances the plan logically through readiness scaling, action selection, contingency planning, and a scheduled check-in that Hana explicitly endorses.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The final plan is genuinely co-constructed, incorporating Hana's ideas (two-minute fallback, phone-away strategy, reminder wording) and handing her the honesty-monitoring role. The counselor still drives the process structure (timing, scaling, check-in format), making power-sharing strong but uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actions are regularly framed as Hana's choice ('Which feels most doable') and she makes the key decisions, including her refusal to rehearse aloud. Autonomy support is inconsistent, however, as the counselor supplies most specific techniques and uses rapid-fire 'Would you like...' closers that narrow options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from within via the meaning question, the 4-vs-2/3 probe, and the confidence ruler, with Hana arriving at her own self-compassion line and connecting her presentation success to present capacity. Falls short of 5 because the counselor sometimes supplies coping phrases and techniques rather than eliciting Hana's own strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hana's expertise is respected in places and her contributions genuinely shape the plan, but the counselor frequently takes the lead with pre-packaged option menus, scripted exact phrases, dictated logistics, and homework assignments. The repeated 'Would you like...' question appears in a formulaic way rather than as genuine negotiation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocative questions (the 4-vs-2/3 readiness probe and confidence ruler) elicit genuine desire, need, and commitment language ('There's a part of me that's done waiting'; 'I'm actually doing this. Finally.'). Strong change talk is not consistently deepened or reflected back, and turns close with option menus rather than consolidating commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced and practical problem-solving (two-minute fallback, phone in another room) effectively reduces avoidance. However, there is limited active softening or reframing — fears of failure, embarrassment, and self-criticism are acknowledged but not worked with directly, as the counselor pivots quickly to planning.
