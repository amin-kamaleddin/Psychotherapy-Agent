# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, with affirmations like 'You did something brave by going' and 'I'm proud of you.' However, affirmations occasionally felt formulaic and were bundled with checklists of advice, diluting their depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflections capturing loneliness, dread of pity, and mixed pride/disappointment, showing nuanced perspective-taking. However, reflections were sometimes brief, with the counselor moving quickly to solutions rather than deepening understanding of his discouragement about his body.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on a concrete, achievable target—attending the group for 15 minutes—and moved steadily toward a specific plan. Minor digressions into anxiety techniques added some clutter.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative rehearsal exchanges and invitations like 'Which feels doable to try?', but the counselor frequently dominated with rapid-fire option lists and checklists that shifted toward a prescriptive stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly reinforced Elias's ownership: he chose his arrival/exit lines, added his own tweak, declined a script for Marta, and set his own timeline. The exit timer and 'you can leave anytime' emphasis directly addressed his fear of feeling stuck.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Drew out some of Elias's motivations (connection, avoiding feeling stuck), and his own words shaped the plan, but much of the session consisted of counselor-generated solutions. Change talk largely emerged because the patient was already in contemplation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated Elias's contributions as valuable—his option choice and exit-line tweak shaped the plan, and his rejection of a script for Marta was accepted. Partnership was limited by counselor checklists and pre-drafted scripts taking up conversational space.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged ('Maybe I could go once more... fifteen minutes') and was reinforced, but the counselor relied on pre-packaged options rather than eliciting Elias's own reasons and confidence. Missed opportunities to explore what made the experience 'beautiful' or use confidence rulers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify or reinforce sustain talk and offered helpful reframes ('asking for adaptations is practical, not shameful'). However, sustain talk ('I'm too far gone,' 'admitting defeat') was mostly ignored or briefly acknowledged rather than worked through with double-sided reflections.
