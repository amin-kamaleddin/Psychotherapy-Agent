# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, meeting Dante's ambivalence with patience. Warmth occasionally feels formulaic, and the emotional weight of his discouragement receives little explicit validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects Dante's core experiences, including his lost rhythm, sluggishness, and the hurdle of getting out the door. The session moves quickly to planning, leaving deeper emotional reflection (regret, fear of failing again) under-explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains laser focus on the target behavior, with every turn advancing toward a specific, measurable plan (Sunday 9am, 20 minutes, concrete cues, backup, check-in). Guides progressively from exploration to commitment without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans are largely co-constructed, and Dante's input redirects the plan (family walk over solo walks, kids picking the route). The counselor's repeated menus of options and scaling questions lead the agenda more than true equal partnership would.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Dante's ownership by offering choices, asking rather than prescribing, and honoring his preferences (no Sunday call, not making the walk 'a bigger deal than it is'). Dante arrives guarded ('another exercise talk') and leaves owning the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within — Dante's values, past success, self-chosen accountability (kids), and own rewards. Culminates in his self-generated insight: 'I just forgot I could still start small.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Dante's contributions substantially shape the plan (family walk, telling the kids, kids picking the route), and his expertise is clearly honored. The counselor frequently supplies menus of options rather than consistently drawing solutions from Dante first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through the values question, confidence scaling, and 'what would raise your confidence from 6 to 8' prompts, eliciting self-motivating statements ('proving to myself I can start'). Dante's sustained change talk and final commitment reflect successful evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges barriers without amplifying them and pivots toward planning around obstacles with an if-then backup plan. Repeatedly asking Dante to enumerate barriers briefly amplifies focus on reasons against change.
