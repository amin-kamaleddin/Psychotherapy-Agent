# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard, never judging the two years of secrecy. Affirmations like 'That's a powerful thing to know about yourself' are specific and genuine, honoring Daniela's identity and self-generated plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate, repeatedly capturing the emotional core ('the real fear is that silence afterward - not the words themselves'). Reflections demonstrate deep understanding of both surface content and underlying meaning of fear of stigma and desire for normalcy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains an exceptionally clear focus on disclosing diabetes to peers, with each exchange advancing from imagining the outcome to a concrete plan and relapse preparation. There is no topic drift; every question moves the change process forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ideas are offered only as questions for Daniela to evaluate, and she freely rejects, modifies, or builds on them. The session's direction is co-constructed, a model example of MI's 'two experts' stance adapted for an adolescent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice is consistently reinforced ('you get to define what a win looks like here') and the clinician never pressures or directs disclosure. Daniela retains full ownership of the plan and its pacing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn out from Daniela's own reasons - relief, freedom, reclaimed energy, and self-efficacy - rather than supplied by the clinician. Evocation is developmentally attuned, leveraging her identity as a storyteller so the coping strategy comes entirely from her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located within the client, and the entire disclosure strategy originates from her, prompted by open questions. The final plan is her own construction, which the clinician reinforces.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk escalates across the session, from 'I'll make it short and simple' to 'I think I'm actually gonna try it this week.' The clinician amplifies commitment by asking her to elaborate on success and what makes the approach doable.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but never reinforced, with effective double-sided reflections and pivots toward change. A 5 is missed only because some sustain talk (loss of control, 'freaks me out a little') is passed over with a light touch rather than explicitly softened.
