# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and nonjudgment, with no criticism of past lapses. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and could be more individualized, especially around her body-image shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture both frustration and hope, as well as her all-or-nothing pattern. However, deeper emotional content (gym shame, fear of self-betrayal) is glossed over in the move toward problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains relentless, appropriate focus on resuming exercise, progressing logically from barriers to a fully operationalized plan with zero topic drift. Every turn advances the change agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving: the counselor proposes, Aisha modifies, and revisions are adopted without defensiveness. Technical support complements rather than overrides her expertise about her own habits.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is reinforced from start to finish with menus, invitational offers, and deference to Aisha's judgment on days, times, wording, and tracking. Her internal locus of control is explicitly supported.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of open questions and confidence scaling drew rich client-generated motivation and moved her confidence from 6 to 8. Evocation stays at the level of logistics and confidence rather than exploring deeper values and meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha is clearly positioned as the expert, and her preferences materially shaped every decision. Some elements (reminder wording, options presented) feel pre-packaged rather than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, confidence rulers, and conditional follow-ups elicited strong commitments and optimistic change talk, which was reflected back. Moments like 'felt lighter' aren't deepened into values exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently pivoted toward problem-solving, and the 'hiccup, not a quit' reframe was internalized by the client. No responses amplify reasons against change.
