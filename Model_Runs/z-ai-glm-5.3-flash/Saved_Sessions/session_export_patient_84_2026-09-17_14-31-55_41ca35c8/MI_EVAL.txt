# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Malik's frustration without disapproval of his cannabis use, respecting his choice to keep things as-is. Warmth was somewhat brief and formulaic, with limited affirming of his strengths or honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections (e.g., 'feeling watched sucks'), but reflective listening was sparse and often bypassed in favor of question batteries and advice. The deeper feelings around privacy violations, his mother's fear, and peer-vs-family tension were minimally explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear, consistent focus on the cannabis use and parent-teen conflict, ending with a concrete, time-limited plan (four-week trial). Focus occasionally drifted toward parent-management advice rather than Malik's own use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Malik substantially shaped the final agreement and chose the role-play, but the session was often expert-driven with rapid option lists, pre-written scripts, and checklists resembling information delivery more than joint exploration. Two-way idea generation was limited early on.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly offered options including 'keep things as-is,' respected Malik's rejection of the first plan, and accepted his framing that the issue was family conflict, not the weed. Frequent advice-giving and scripts delivered before eliciting his own ideas subtly eroded perceived choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The mother role-play skillfully elicited Malik's own arguments and commitment language. However, much content was counselor-generated, and open questions rarely evoked Malik's own reasons for change regarding the cannabis itself or his broader values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice menus and invitations for input supported partnership, but the counselor frequently slipped into an expert stance with templates and scripts before Malik asked. Malik's pushback ('that'd sound fake coming out of my mouth') suggested the counselor moved faster than his pace, though power-sharing improved later.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively created conditions for change talk, with Malik progressing from 'I don't really want anything to change' to 'trying for a month isn't the worst thing.' The role-play was a skillful move, but the counselor missed chances to deliberately reflect and consolidate the change talk when it emerged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rolled with Malik's rejection of the first plan and backed off appropriately, but early problem-checklist questions invited sustain talk, and minimizations ('not affecting my grades or my life') were passed over without double-sided reflections. No strategic development of discrepancy between his minimization and his irritation with home conflict.
