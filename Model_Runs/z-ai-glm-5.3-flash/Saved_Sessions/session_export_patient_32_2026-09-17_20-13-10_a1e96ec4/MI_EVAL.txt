# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout — no arguing with minimization ('just beer,' 'bad luck'). Patient explicitly noted 'thanks for not preaching at me the whole time,' reflecting strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured both sides of ambivalence, including a sophisticated double-sided opening reflection. The counselor tracked emotional cores like loneliness and resentment of control, and the patient repeatedly confirmed feeling understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange stayed anchored to the target behavior with a clear arc from ambivalence to risk identification, coping strategies, and a concrete plan with a follow-up check. No topic drift; each question built toward the next.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session read as dialogue between equals, with open questions and no prescribed solutions. The plan emerged from the patient's own answers, and the counselor closed by inviting him to report back on his own terms.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively reinforced throughout ('your call, your plan') and skillfully connected to the patient's past failed sobriety feeling controlled. The patient's own language — 'nobody's making me do anything this time' — confirms the message landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation was elicited from the patient rather than supplied — key insights about autonomy, high-risk moments, and self-generated strategies came from him. The counselor's closing crystallized his internal motivation without imposing external goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly conveyed expertise resided with the patient ('you know yourself better than I do'), and the plan content was entirely Derrick's. The patient's closing comment showed he did the discovery work himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful open-ended questions consistently evoked strong change talk ('cab money from day one, no exceptions,' 'it'd be my call, my plan') plus concrete commitments. The counselor then reflected and affirmed these commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally acknowledged without reinforcement and reframed toward the patient's own reasons, with the closing 'we'll see' doubt validated rather than challenged. Minor limitation: the opening double-sided reflection briefly restated the DUIs and drinking-centered social world before pivoting.
