# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental with appropriate safety concern and encouragement, but warmth is generic and formulaic. Tone risks feeling impersonal for a client presenting with flat affect and hopelessness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; Liora's emotional disclosures (exhaustion, secrecy, fear of losing her only coping mechanism) are met with task instructions rather than accurate empathy or complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, unwavering focus on the target behavior and safety throughout, from risk screening to follow-up planning. Direction is achieved through directive task assignment rather than collaborative goal-setting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplies the safety plan, coping techniques, and text messages with limited input from Liora; offers of 'tweaking' are superficial. The session resembles guided instruction more than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Choices are framed as tasks and instructions ('send Dana the text now,' 'keep the ice on'), with pressure over genuine choice. Liora's passive compliance ('whatever,' 'I guess') suggests she is following rather than choosing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor never asks about Liora's own reasons for change, hopes, or what worked for her; all strategies and rationale are supplied. Her self-generated insights ('It actually went down') were not evoked or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, dictating techniques and safety steps, with little explicit acknowledgment of Liora's expertise on her own experience. Her statements are incorporated but never explored as sources of insight.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged and was acknowledged (urge dropping from 4 to 2, 'today I didn't cut,' sending Dana's text), but this was largely incidental. Key moments to deepen change talk were met with more to-do lists instead of exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it's the only thing that helps,' 'part of me doesn't want to,' 'I don't know if I can keep doing this') was largely ignored rather than strategically addressed. No double-sided reflections or ambivalence exploration; pushing tasks forward leaves the client's ambivalence unresolved.
