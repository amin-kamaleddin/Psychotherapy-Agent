# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent affirmations throughout (e.g., 'That's wisdom, not weakness,' 'You figured that out yourself'). The clinician unconditionally regards the patient's ambivalence and self-protective caution without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates deep empathic understanding by reflecting both sides of ambivalence and mirroring the patient's exact words ('my legs, my pace, my Thursdays'). Reflections consistently capture surface content and deeper meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on increasing physical activity via the Thursday walks throughout. Every exchange builds toward the patient's own actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — all ideas come from the patient, with the clinician asking 'What's your sense of which morning might work best?' rather than prescribing. Patient explicitly recognizes 'This one's mine.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces patient agency and self-generated solutions ('That's real motivation coming from inside you,' 'your legs as the judge'). Explicitly contrasts with externally imposed plans the patient expects to fail.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertly evokes change talk, eliciting 'I'd like to,' reasons tied to his father and wife, and the patient's own commitment ('Thursday I'll try'). Uses questions like 'What would count as enough for you?' to draw out meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine expert-as-partner stance, with the clinician explicitly deferring to the patient's expertise ('You know your body better than any doctor'). Guided by curiosity rather than authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rich in sustaining and commitment language ('my yes,' 'Thursday I'll try,' 'I'd like to'), with the clinician skillfully reflecting and deepening it each time. Progression from tentative 'maybe' to concrete commitment is evident.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of failing, 'not really exercise,' all-or-nothing thinking) is met with acceptance and reframed as protective wisdom rather than argued against. The patient himself softens it into graded, realistic steps (mailbox before bench).
