# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout — validated Irina's boundaries, normalized slips as 'data, not failure,' and never argued with her defensiveness. Affirmations were somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized to her strengths as a mother and student.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered accurate reflective listening that captured her ambivalence and helped shift her from defensiveness to engagement. Several reflections restated content rather than exploring deeper emotion — the guilt about watching the clock was acknowledged but deferred to Friday instead of addressed in the moment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior (alcohol as stress coping) with no topic drift — even childcare was tied back to the evening drinking pattern. The session progressed logically from exploring concerns to eliciting change talk to a concrete experiment and follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invited Irina's agenda at the start, asked permission before offering ideas, and co-constructed a plan she genuinely owned. Rapid option-listing after each of her statements tilted the power balance somewhat toward the counselor's agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed changes as Irina's choice — the experiment was explicitly reversible, slips were reframed as data, and her refusals (ZIP code, tea/breathing options) were respected without pushback. Repeated suggestions and pre-formed option menus carried a directive flavor, and autonomy could have been more explicitly reinforced at key moments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within — the worry question elicited her own reasons (foggy mornings, guilt, snappiness), and open questions produced her commitment and self-generated plan additions. Missed evocative tools like importance/confidence rulers and deeper values exploration, and sometimes supplied reasons herself rather than letting Irina articulate them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated Irina as the expert on her own life — her choices (the experiment, shower strategy, Thursday night, Friday check-in) demonstrably shaped the session. Frequent pre-formed option menus constrained her choices rather than openly asking 'What ideas do you have?'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk with targeted questions, directly eliciting her own change language ('One glass instead of two could still be my break') and reinforcing it via her smoking quit. Option menus were offered fairly quickly rather than letting her generate more solutions, and no importance/confidence scaling was used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with her substantial sustain talk, validated the underlying concern, and reframed slips as data — directly softening her fear of failure rooted in her mother's harshness. Sometimes pivoted to option lists quickly rather than using double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence about alcohol as her only coping tool.
