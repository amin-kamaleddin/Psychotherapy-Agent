# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor remained nonjudgmental throughout, but warmth was minimal and mostly transactional, with only one brief affirmation near the end. No explicit normalization of Julian's anxiety about phone calls or fear of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections were nearly absent; the counselor mostly asked factual questions and gave instructions. When Julian expressed fear of messing up or embarrassment, the counselor moved straight to scripts rather than reflecting his emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, consistent focus on completing and documenting the 40 community service hours without drift. However, direction leaned toward practical problem-solving rather than motivational progression toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was largely counselor-driven with rapid-fire questions, unsolicited scripts, and dense to-do lists, leaving Julian mostly a passive recipient. His real-world constraints (weekday limitations, low email use) were accommodated only after pushback.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered concrete options and respected Julian's stated preferences (email over talking, starting with weekends). But the tone remained directive, delivering dense checklists without checking readiness or reinforcing Julian's ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was treated as purely external, mirroring the court mandate. The counselor never elicited Julian's own reasons, values, or confidence, and his self-generated flickers of motivation went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role throughout, dominating with checklists, scripts, and deadlines. Julian's contributions were incorporated only superficially and he was never positioned as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Julian spontaneously offered change talk ('better to know sooner than later,' 'maybe I start with just weekends'), but the counselor rarely reinforced or deepened it. No open questions explored what completing the hours would mean to him personally.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not reinforce Julian's sustain talk ('that's kinda insane... I've got stuff going on') and avoided dwelling on barriers. However, no active softening occurred — no double-sided reflections or affirmations of legitimate concerns while pivoting back to goals.
