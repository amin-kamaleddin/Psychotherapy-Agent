# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect and non-judgment are maintained ('You're allowed to feel resistant'), but warmth is formulaic and genuine affirmations are scarce. The twice-inserted safety screening feels abrupt and impersonal rather than caring.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections capture Jonathan's overwhelm and fear of failing, but they stay superficial and quickly pivot to advice menus. Deep emotional content (resentment, secrecy about soda) is acknowledged briefly rather than explored, and repeated 'that makes sense' becomes rote.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on diabetes self-management with effective narrowing to one concrete behavior (soda swap, three days). A strength, though the narrowing was counselor-driven and broader concerns were deferred without explicit agenda-setting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite inclusive language, the session is largely counselor-directed: the counselor diagnoses, generates solutions, and sets parameters while Jonathan merely selects from pre-built menus. Jonathan's contributions are quickly overridden with the counselor's own prescriptions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is consistently emphasized ('pick one or none,' experiment framing, explicit permission to slip). However, when Jonathan pushed back ('you're really pushing this'), the counselor kept offering alternatives rather than stepping back.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicits Jonathan's own motivations; questions are mostly multiple-choice menus of counselor-generated options rather than open evocative questions. His motivations emerged despite, not because of, the counselor's technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positions itself as the advice-generator with option lists, and Jonathan's expertise about his own routines is drawn out only minimally and late. Little recognition that Jonathan knows what will work in his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The experiment framing elicited meaningful change talk: Jonathan named soda as his worst habit, chose the swap himself, and committed to three days. However, these gains came late and were never deepened by exploring why change mattered or his past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally tolerated and ambivalence reframed ('just info'), but repeated doubts ('never stuck,' 'drop in the bucket') were met with reassurance and new menus rather than strategic double-sided reflections linking his change talk against his doubts.
