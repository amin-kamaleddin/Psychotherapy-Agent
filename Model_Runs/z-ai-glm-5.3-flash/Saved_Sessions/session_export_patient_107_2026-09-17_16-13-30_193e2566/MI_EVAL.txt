# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, explicitly validating Nathaniel's right to his own view ('No promises is totally fine') and normalizing slips without judgment. Affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic ('That sounds solid') rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures ambivalence well ('a real push–pull') and accurately names the ritual-as-break nuance. Some reflections track content rather than deeper emotion, especially regarding his self-criticism about broken streaks and fear of the 3pm slump.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains an exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior throughout, with every turn advancing a concrete, fully specified plan: timing, replacement ritual, slump hierarchy, start date, and accountability. Even digressesions are redirected without losing momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving: options are offered rather than prescribed, tools are co-drafted and edited by the client, and the final plan was substantially shaped by his own ideas. Nathaniel explicitly noted the plan was 'way less painful' than expected lecturing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: the plan is framed as optional and self-chosen, 'no promises' is accepted without pressure, and the safety-net design deliberately lowers perceived coercion. The slip reframing removes the all-or-nothing trap Nathaniel identified as his failure pattern.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently elicited from the client rather than supplied—curiosity, health awareness, past success, and social accountability all came from Nathaniel. Iterative confidence scaling with 'what would raise it by a point' is classic evocative technique, culminating in his own 'I've kind of talked myself into actually doing this.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise clearly resides with the client: choices are repeatedly offered, and his contributions (safety net, sister check-in, outdoor walk) genuinely shaped the plan. The hallmark of high partnership is evident throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful and consistent evocation of change talk: scaling questions drew out self-generated solutions (5→6→7), past success was affirmed, and the session culminated in spontaneous commitment language ('I've kind of talked myself into actually doing this'). Exemplary MI practice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well by working around it rather than arguing—e.g., transforming the rejected half-caf into a safety-net and accepting 'just an experiment' disclaimers. However, late-session sustain language ('not signing anything permanent,' 'assuming I don't cave by Tuesday') is largely left unaddressed rather than actively pivoted.
