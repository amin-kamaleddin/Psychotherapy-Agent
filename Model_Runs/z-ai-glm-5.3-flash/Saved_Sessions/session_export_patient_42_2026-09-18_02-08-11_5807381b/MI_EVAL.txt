# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently honors Elise's autonomy and perspective, affirming her self-generated insights ('I'm struck that you caught that distinction yourself'). There is no judgment, correction, or pressure regarding her ambivalence about quitting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections repeatedly capture both sides of her ambivalence — connection versus health fears — and accurately mirror subtle statements like needing cigarettes 'to feel like I belong.' The clinician demonstrates deep understanding of the emotional meaning of smoking in her life.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician uses open questions to move the conversation from exploring belonging toward identifying a concrete, client-generated step (lunch with Dana). The direction follows Elise's own momentum rather than a predetermined agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently evokes Elise's own ideas rather than offering solutions, evidenced by her comment, 'You're just... letting me decide. That's kind of the point.' Every plan element originates from the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient explicitly notes she is not being talked out of her own choices, and the clinician supports her decision to separate the lunch goal from any quit-date commitment. The framing throughout emphasizes Elise's agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully draws out Elise's own reasons, strengths, and change talk — from her realization about 'doors into people' to her shift from 'probably' to 'that's a yes.' Change talk is elicited and amplified rather than supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation flows as a genuine collaborative exchange, with questions inviting Elise's expertise about herself ('What do you think belonging could look like?'). The patient actively problem-solves and self-corrects throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician strategically evokes and reinforces change talk — 'What would help you give that lunch idea its best shot?' and 'What would it take for the probably to feel more like a yes?' — culminating in strong commitment language ('that's a yes').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of retreat, 'I keep thinking I'll try lunch, but I'm not quitting smoking') is met with acceptance and reflection rather than confrontation, which allows Elise to soften it herself. Slightly stronger direct reframing of the fear of rejection could have deepened this process.
