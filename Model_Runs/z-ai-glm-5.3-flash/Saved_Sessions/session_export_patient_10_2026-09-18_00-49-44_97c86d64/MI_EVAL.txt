# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirmed the patient's autonomy and expertise, reflecting her values and strengths without judgment or pressure. Language like 'that's real expertise about your family' reinforced acceptance throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both her childcare struggles and her sense of being torn. The clinician repeatedly voiced her underlying feelings, such as the frustration of knowing she can do the job but being stuck.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on solving the childcare barrier and building a concrete call plan. Each exchange moved the conversation toward a specific, timed next step without distraction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All decisions and next steps came from the patient, with the clinician offering gentle prompts and an optional follow-up rather than directives. The plan ('Tuesday it is') was fully shaped by the patient's own circumstances.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician emphasized that 'the next move stays yours' and framed uncertainty as questions only she could answer. Advice was minimal, with responsibility for the plan and follow-up left with the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions drew out her reasons for change, including wanting her kids to see her 'trying, not stuck.' Confidence rulers and future-vision questions elicited strong change talk and her own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The offer to 'sit with the answers together' established a shared follow-up process. The clinician consistently positioned the patient as the expert while offering companionship in problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician selectively reinforced change talk ('that would change everything,' naming a specific step) through reflections and confidence rulers. The patient ended with strong self-affirming statements: 'The confidence I have in myself? Still a 10.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Doubts about hours, waitlist, and cost were met with reflections reframing them as answerable questions rather than obstacles. Even the contingency discussion (long waitlist) was channeled into proactive planning.
