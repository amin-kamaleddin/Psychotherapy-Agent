# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with validation of Tariq's frustration and anxiety; however, missed affirming his months of good compliance, and warmth feels somewhat procedural with rapid menu offerings.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ('That call matters — not stupid'), but the counselor repeatedly pivots to scripts and solutions rather than reflecting Tariq's deeper vulnerabilities about his father and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on restoring phone privileges, compliance, and family connection, with every response tied to concrete steps; not a 5 because it stays narrowly on compliance skills rather than broader motivation and release preparation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative structure (roleplay, menus, checking agreement), but the counselor supplies all scripts and strategies while Tariq mostly executes them; his own ideas aren't elicited or built upon.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices and frames compliance as Tariq's own strategic decision ('You can comply now and still protect yourself'); falls short because heavy scripting does the thinking for him and his self-direction capacity isn't affirmed in moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no open questions exploring Tariq's own reasons, values, or hopes; his motivation around family and release emerges spontaneously but is left unexplored in favor of an advisory script-provision style.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites Tariq's choices at times, but the interaction is heavily counselor-driven with scripts handed over rather than drawn from him, and his own contributions (e.g., his mother's wisdom) are never reflected back or leveraged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tariq generates meaningful change talk largely on his own ('I want to be home more than I want to win this'), which the counselor affirms but never actively evokes or deepens into commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Grievance and blame talk is acknowledged but not strategically softened; validating 'feeling anxious and wronged' somewhat reinforces the blame focus, and the 'smart, not surrender' reframe isn't supported by double-sided reflections.
