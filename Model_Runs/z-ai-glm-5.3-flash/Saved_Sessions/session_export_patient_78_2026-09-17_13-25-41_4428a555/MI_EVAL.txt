# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout; explicitly avoided the lecture the client expected and accepted her limits. Deducted because the repetitive plan recaps felt formulaic rather than individually attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured the ambivalence (smoking as a protected break vs. guilt about her daughter) and validated her frustration with lectures. Missed deeper exploration of the emotional weight of her daughter's reactions and past discouragement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed well-focused and narrowed to a concrete, dated plan (smoke-free car rides starting tomorrow). Deducted for redundant recaps and failure to connect the plan to her deeper values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client meaningfully shaped the plan (car goal, mints, no announcement) with regular offers of choice. The final third's one-sided action-item monologues weakened the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong choice language throughout and explicit disclaiming of the sermon role; the client even noted she was being asked what she wanted. Slightly undercut by the prescriptive, repetitive coaching tone near the end.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good open questions drew out her own motivations (daughter's reaction emerged as her internal motivator). However, this motivation was never developed — no exploration of what quitting would mean for her relationship with her daughter, no confidence exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Incorporated her contributions and offered choice points, but the structure remained counselor-driven with pre-packaged numbered plans. Her expertise was only partially drawn upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited a concrete commitment (no smoking in the car) and tentative openness to future change. But change talk was rarely reinforced or deepened with affirmations, evocative questions, or confidence exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Never argued with sustain talk and generally accepted her boundaries, but the response was largely passive rather than strategic. Repeated restating of the slip protocol across multiple turns kept attention on failure rather than pivoting toward change.
