# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, affirming Tyler's realizations and using neutral double-sided reflections like 'Both of those things can be true.' Affirmations were somewhat generic, and there was little explicit normalization of his setbacks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate complex reflections capturing both sides of Tyler's ambivalence and invited his perspective on the incident. Deeper emotional undercurrents—fear of repeated failure and shame about the arrest—were touched on but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and the conduct incident, with each exchange purposefully advancing from ambivalence to a concrete, self-authored plan. No topic drift; questions built progressively on each other.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided interaction—invited Tyler's perspective at every turn, built his contributions into subsequent questions, and offered summaries for confirmation. Tyler's own framing shaped the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed choices as Tyler's own, gave no advice or pressure, and every plan element was self-generated. Reinforced ownership by noting the steps 'came from you' and validating his self-monitoring idea as 'a reminder to yourself, from yourself.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilfully drew motivation from within Tyler, eliciting his reasons, values about friendship, and own plan. The worry 'it didn't exactly work last time' was not developed into a confidence-building discussion, and scaling/elaborating questions were underused.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary partnership—explicitly positioned Tyler as the expert on his own life and credited him with his own insights ('You figured that out just now, on your own'). Every plan element originated from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk with forward-looking questions that produced strong commitment language ('I want to try it', 'I'd rather graduate than be a legend at parties'), which was highlighted and reflected back. Confidence and ability talk were only lightly addressed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided reinforcing sustain talk, responding to minimization with neutral double-sided reflections and pivoting toward Tyler's own reasons for change; the 'boring one' worry was handled especially well. Some sustain talk was met with simple open questions rather than strategic reframing.
