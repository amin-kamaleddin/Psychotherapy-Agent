# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates consistent respect and non-judgment; Diego explicitly credits the absence of a 'just quit' lecture for his openness. However, warmth is somewhat formulaic with few individualized affirmations of Diego's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; the counselor rarely paraphrases or explores the emotional content Diego offers (shame about use, fear of Marcus's judgment, pain he is escaping). Instead of reflecting, the counselor jumps to question batteries and logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on heroin use and harm reduction with concrete targets and a defined one-week follow-up. Direction is achieved largely through structured advice-giving rather than collaborative agenda-setting, and the opening scatters focus across many questions at once.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Planning phase is genuinely collaborative — safety plan labeled 'editable,' goals and check-in timing negotiated, Diego leads role-play. Earlier phases are prescriptive, with advice and assessment delivered without eliciting Diego's input or existing knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support in planning: offers menus, negotiates timing, accepts declines without pressure, and honors the harm-reduction framing. Held at 4 because autonomy is supported via counselor-generated options rather than affirming Diego's own capacity and ownership of his plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to draw motivation from within Diego — no open evocative questions, importance/confidence rulers, or values exploration. Diego's strong change talk emerged from his own readiness rather than skillful elicitation, a significant missed opportunity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing in the planning phase, letting Diego's priorities shape the session. Earlier, the counselor adopts an expert stance, delivering an eight-point advice list and assessment barrage that duplicated what Diego already knew.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits strong commitment ('10 out of 10') and consolidates it through practical planning and role-play. Misses chances to deepen change talk (confidence, values, what success looks like), so most commitment language came from Diego himself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately avoids dwelling on or reinforcing Diego's sustain talk ('I still like how it makes me feel'). But there is no active softening — no double-sided reflections or deliberate pivots toward change talk; the approach is avoidance rather than skillful management.
