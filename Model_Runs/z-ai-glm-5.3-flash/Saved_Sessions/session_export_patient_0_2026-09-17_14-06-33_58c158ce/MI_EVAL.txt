# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with a defensive client who explicitly noted he wasn't treated 'like some junkie.' However, affirmations were thin ('Good plan,' 'Good call') rather than deeply individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections, but the session was dominated by questions and information delivery rather than reflective listening. Key emotional disclosures — being high during the infection, his mother's reaction, fear of clinic stigma — were under-processed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on methamphetamine use and risks despite deflection, ending with a specific behaviorally anchored plan. Lost points because direction was pursued via rapid checklists rather than progressively deepening focus, with some drift into logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Included menu-based options and asked permission, but the session was largely counselor-as-advisor with long instructional monologues. Plans like the safety checklist were presented rather than co-constructed, and Adrian's own expertise was minimally acknowledged.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and framed the skipped weekend as a 'low-risk experiment' rather than a demand, accepting Adrian's framing without pressure. Falls short because harm-reduction advice was delivered in dense directive blocks without explicitly reinforcing his ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The non-confrontational style allowed intrinsic motivation (infection fear, contamination worries) to surface spontaneously. Questions were mostly yes/no screening and logistical prompts, and the pivotal 'I haven't admitted that to anybody' moment was met with a brief reflection before pivoting to next steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and respected preferences, but most of the session involved expert content delivery with Adrian in a receptive role. His own expertise was rarely acknowledged and he was never positioned as the authority on his own life or invited to shape the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created conditions for meaningful spontaneous change talk ('maybe not every weekend') and used a clever low-pressure experiment to evoke commitment. However, change talk was never deepened — no amplification, scaling, or exploration of why avoiding infection matters to Adrian.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing minimization and moved past sustain talk ('I'm not saying I'm cutting back') without arguing. Occasionally used double-edged framing ('you can be careful without quitting') that implicitly endorsed continued use.
