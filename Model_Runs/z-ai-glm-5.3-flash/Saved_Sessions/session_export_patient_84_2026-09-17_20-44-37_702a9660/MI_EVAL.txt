# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, with warm affirmations and no judgment about the cannabis use or Malik's deflections. Even resistant remarks ('That's kind of on her') met with acceptance rather than correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, consistently deployed reflective listening capturing both surface content and deeper meaning, including skilled double-sided reflections tracking Malik's ambivalence. Core values (trust, being treated as capable) were accurately named.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on cannabis use in peer contexts and its consequences, advancing purposefully from venting to a specific behavioral plan without imposing an agenda. Excellent directional skill for precontemplation/early contemplation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership of equals: the counselor asks rather than tells and Malik's own ideas (Deon's model, holding the controller) became the working plan. No authoritarian or advice-giving slips despite the adult-adolescent dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Malik's ownership consistently reinforced, including explicitly conditional language ('if you decided you wanted one'). Resistance to commitment was accepted without pushback, and the plan is entirely Malik-generated and Malik-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within Malik rather than supplied, eliciting his own reasons (trust, quiet at home, post-practice peace). He spontaneously generated change talk and a self-authored plan despite presenting as defensive and externally motivated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Malik explicitly positioned as the expert ('You know your crew better than I ever will'), and his contributions genuinely shaped the session. Textbook power-sharing given the adult-adolescent power differential.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions tied to Malik's values (trust, 'less drama,' peace at home) elicited progressively stronger change talk, culminating in a self-generated reduction plan and concrete first test at J'Sean's. Each piece of change talk was reinforced with reflections and affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk generally reflected briefly and redirected toward change, with skilled double-sided reflections pairing it with Malik's own concerns. A few reflections briefly echoed the peer-normalization/blame frame without immediate counterbalancing.
