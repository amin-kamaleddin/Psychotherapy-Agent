# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; Erik's expectation of a telling-off was met with normalization and affirmations like 'that's a solid, realistic plan.' Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic rather than individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections captured Erik's ambivalence well, but empathic responses were inconsistent. Meaningful emotional content — beers to unwind, workmate teasing, spousal dynamics — was mostly bypassed in favor of logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on diet, alcohol, and self-monitoring, culminating in a specific, dated action plan. It occasionally felt checklist-like and transactional at the expense of exploring underlying motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Erik's choices steering decisions and the two-week experiment framing him as the tester of his own hypotheses. Repeated detailed advice lists (snack portions, glucose timing) introduced some expert-driven content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently affirmed through choice-structured options, 'I'll follow your lead,' and Erik's rejection of options being accepted without pushback. A few suggestions were directive in tone, though Erik always remained free to decline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ruler questions and open prompts effectively elicited Erik's own reasons, moving him from importance 4 to confidence 6 and ownership of the plan. Evocation was somewhat concentrated in the rulers, with less exploration of his deeper values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik's judgment clearly drove the session; his rejections (yogurt, checklist) were accepted and his phrasing shaped the plan. The counselor did provide a good deal of expert content, tilting the exchange somewhat toward professional knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Importance and confidence rulers were well-timed, and Erik's 4-to-6 shift was explicitly highlighted, producing strong commitment language ('I'll give it an honest go'). Less time was spent deepening the change talk, such as exploring what the 6 meant.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I feel fine') was handled via double-sided reflection, and worries about slipping were met with normalization and concrete coping strategies. Some sustain themes were repeated in reflections but never reinforced and were effectively redirected.
