# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful opening ('it makes sense, and I'm glad you're here') visibly softened Erik's defensiveness, with affirmations of his own insights. Some praise felt excessive, as Erik noted with 'no need to lay it on thick.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, accurately capturing Erik's ambivalence and the deeper autonomy theme. Missed opportunities to explore the emotional weight of the 'sentence for life' fear and the identity-laden meaning of the beers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus progressing from ambivalence to a concrete first step (meal planning with wife) and a follow-up plan. The alcohol component—half the referral concern—was tabled without a bridge for revisiting it, and some exchanges circled over the same theme.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently joint work between equals, with all key decisions (food first, wife's involvement, deferring alcohol, check-in) co-constructed from Erik's own suggestions. Erik's ideas visibly shaped the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy messaging throughout ('where to start—that's really up to you'), honoring Erik's choice to defer the alcohol talk. Repeated emphatic affirmations of his autonomy occasionally had a slightly leading, cheerleading quality.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reliably elicited Erik's own motivations and content, including the meal-planning vision and approach to his wife. Evocation was one-dimensional, focusing almost exclusively on food without gently exploring alcohol motivations or confidence/importance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly conveyed that expertise resides with Erik ('you know your life better than I do'), and his contributions substantially shaped the session. The clinician occasionally over-orchestrated moments by feeding interpretive framings rather than letting Erik arrive at meanings himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully highlighted and reinforced Erik's change talk ('I could' instead of 'I've got to') and evoked concrete commitments. Some evocation was repetitive and confidence was not directly explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used double-sided reflections effectively and avoided arguing with sustain talk. However, significant alcohol-related sustain talk (beers as identity, feeling fine) was left unexplored rather than strategically softened—more avoidance than skillful redirection.
