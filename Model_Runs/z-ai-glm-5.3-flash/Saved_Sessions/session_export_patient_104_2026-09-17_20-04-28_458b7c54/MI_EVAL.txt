# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated warm, unconditional positive regard throughout, validating Nadine's worry and exhaustion, affirming her instincts, and honoring her self-generated decisions without judgment. Nonjudgmental space was held even when Nadine self-criticized ('That probably sounds pathetic'), allowing her to move past it herself.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including accurate dual reflections capturing her simultaneous worry and exhaustion, and meaning-making reflections of the reassurance. Emotional depths—such as her 3am loneliness and fear of failing—could have been explored more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained steady focus on infant sleep, maternal self-blame, and coping strategies with no topic drift, moving smoothly from exploration to prioritization, information-giving, and a concrete plan. The session closed with consolidated next steps and a follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated as a genuine partnership: the agenda was chosen jointly and the entire action plan was co-constructed from Nadine's own words. Her comment about leaving with 'your own plan instead of someone else's rules' confirms she experienced the collaboration authentically.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was exemplary, with options framed as possibilities rather than prescriptions and Nadine selecting her own choices throughout. Even the self-talk prompt was something Nadine created herself, reinforcing her ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within Nadine—her goals, self-talk, plan, and desire for follow-up—rather than through persuasion. A 5 would require more systematic elicitation of confidence and ability language, which was never directly explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positioned Nadine as the expert and let her contributions shape the entire plan. Some expert content (normal sleep patterns, common strategies) was supplied without first checking whether she wanted information, though it was appropriate and requested.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was actively and skillfully evoked through desire statements, choice questions, and commitment prompts, producing strong client change language ('I do want to figure this out,' 'maybe I try it this week'). More deliberate confidence-scaling or elaboration questions would have elevated this further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently redirected toward change via reflections that pivoted to her capability, and the mess acceptance was reframed as 'the smart move.' Occasionally the pivots were performed for the client rather than eliciting her own counterarguments, slightly limiting the score.
