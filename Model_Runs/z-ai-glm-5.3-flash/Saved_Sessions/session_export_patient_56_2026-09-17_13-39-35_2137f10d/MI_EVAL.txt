# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful tone with affirming statements like 'be gentle with yourself.' However, warmth feels somewhat formulaic and lacks individualized positive regard beyond generic encouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections are reasonable, but the session quickly shifts to checklists and tasks. Deeper emotional content — the values-behavior gap and self-criticism — was not explored with complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on reducing evening email intrusion to protect family time; plan, obstacles, and check-in all tie to this goal. A 5 would require more ongoing connection to the client's deeper values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client picked a night, message option, and check-in time, but the core plan, coping strategies, and checklists were pre-packaged rather than co-created. Client input shaped details but not substance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent genuine choice points with no pressuring. However, many choices are narrow logistical options within a counselor-constructed framework, and client ownership of change isn't explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor rarely drew out the client's own motivations, instead supplying plans and techniques. The most evocative moment — why family presence matters — came from the client, not the counselor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some input is invited, but the overall stance is expert-driven, with the counselor supplying plans and templates. The client's expertise (e.g., choosing Tuesday) shaped the plan only marginally.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Commitment was elicited and strong change talk emerged ('I can commit to that'), but the counselor responded with logistics rather than deepening it or exploring the client's reasons for sustaining change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** When worry about falling behind at work emerged, the counselor jumped to problem-solving rather than reflecting or reframing the doubt. Effective but mechanical, without softening the ambivalence.
