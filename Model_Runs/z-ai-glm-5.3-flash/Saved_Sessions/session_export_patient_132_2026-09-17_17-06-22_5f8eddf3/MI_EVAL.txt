# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, normalizing slips and validating Yasmin's desire for weight loss. Question stacks occasionally feel pressured rather than accepting of her pace.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections, but rich emotional material (failure, fear of spiraling, cultural food connections, social pressure) is met with practical tips rather than deeper emotional reflection. Empathy remains surface-level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, clear focus on a specific target behavior, with each turn narrowing toward a concrete, actionable weekly plan (veg at dinner, same-night timing, fridge cue, restaurant swaps). No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan is genuinely co-constructed from Yasmin's own preferences and ideas, with frequent check-ins. Multi-item option lists and advice blocks create uneven power-sharing at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language ('You're in charge') with choices driving the plan and her rejection of texting friends accepted without pushback. Reliance on pre-packaged option menus keeps it from full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits meaningful change talk through confidence ratings and preference questions, but much content originates from counselor-generated menus. Deeper motivations (self-kindness, breaking the failure cycle) are never explored with open evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Yasmin is consistently positioned as the decision-maker and her contributions shape the plan. Sizable advice blocks (slip-up plan, restaurant list, prep instructions) occasionally tip toward expert-driven territory.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of confidence scaling (0-10) and open invitations that elicited strong change talk ('I want to prove I can handle a slip without quitting'). Change talk is rarely reflected back or deepened to consolidate it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and validated, but reassurance about weight shifting 'over time' partially reinforces the weight-loss focus rather than using double-sided reflections. Fears of slow results are met with tips rather than strategic redirection.
