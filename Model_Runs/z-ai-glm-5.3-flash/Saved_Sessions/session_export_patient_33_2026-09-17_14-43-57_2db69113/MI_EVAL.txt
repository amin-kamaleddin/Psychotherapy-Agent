# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, honoring Diego's harm-reduction framing and offering frequent affirmations. However, affirmations were somewhat repetitive and generic, lacking individualized depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Initial reflections were accurate, but reflections became sparse as the session shifted toward information delivery. Missed opportunities to validate emotional undercurrents, including Diego's remark about 'things I don't want to think about' and his financial stress about lacking insurance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus maintained on harm reduction as the agreed target, with every exchange tied to concrete risk reduction (Narcan, test strips, sterile supplies, wound care). The session built progressively toward a specific, actionable plan with no meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Fostered collaboration through asking permission, offering option menus, and co-developing the action list, including a genuinely collaborative role-play of the 2-1-1 script. Heavy reliance on counselor-delivered checklists, however, tilted the exchange toward the counselor as the primary advice source.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently strong autonomy support: repeated choice offers, explicit honoring of Diego's right to decline MOUD, and no pressure toward abstinence. Diego's decisions genuinely shaped the session direction, reinforced by the closing practice invitation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Relied more on providing information than drawing out Diego's own motivations and reasons, with few open questions probing deeper values (e.g., why safety matters, the meaning behind his 10/10 readiness). Motivation was affirmed more than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Periodic permission-asking and choice invitations supported partnership, but the mid-session pattern of counselor offering tip lists and Diego picking from them positioned the counselor as the information expert. Diego's own expertise was only implicitly affirmed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reinforced Diego's substantial change talk with affirmations and concrete commitment questions like 'Which of those feels most doable right now?' Missed chances to deepen change talk by exploring the reasons behind his 10/10 readiness or what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argued with or reinforced Diego's sustain talk, avoided pushing abstinence, and handled his rejection of MOUD well by offering it as one option among many. Deduction because sustain talk was mostly met with neutral redirection rather than skillful double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
