# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently treats Maya with respect and warmth, avoiding any lecture or judgment. Affirmations tend to reward her cooperative engagement rather than convey unconditional regard independent of her choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is frequent and accurate, including strong double-sided and emotionally attuned reflections. Reflections occasionally serve as springboards to the next question rather than fully dwelling in Maya's emotional experience, such as her fear and loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus is consistently maintained on the target concern via Maya's own values, advancing from ambivalence to a concrete plan. The drinking behavior itself is never explicitly named or linked to school risk, leaving the target behavior somewhat implicit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operates as genuine dialogue between equals, with each counselor response built directly on Maya's previous words. The middle ground proposal emerged from Maya and was refined through joint dialogue, exemplifying full collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maya is repeatedly framed as the decision-maker and expert on her own life, with all ideas (proposal, Sunday timing, honesty about drinking) originating from her. No advice, pressure, or directives were given; only process guidance was offered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question is evocative and open, assuming motivation resides within Maya, and no reasons for change are supplied by the counselor. The only limitation is the absence of explicit confidence/ability exploration to round out self-efficacy evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maya is explicitly positioned as the expert, and the entire plan came from her rather than the counselor. The counselor offers no content expertise, only process guidance, reflecting true power-sharing rather than token partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is the strongest domain, with skillful evocation of change talk at every turn through strategic questions (e.g., 'What would show your parents they can trust you?', 'What's different afterward?'). Maya's motivation—trust, family normalcy, autonomy—was entirely client-generated and progressively deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (minimization, frustration) is handled skillfully with double-sided reflections and consistent pivots to change-oriented material, without arguing or amplifying. One reflection briefly echoes the minimization of the drinking concern, though it serves rapport.
