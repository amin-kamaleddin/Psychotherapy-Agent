# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with consistent affirmation of the patient's efforts and plan. However, praise is somewhat formulaic and affirmation centers on the plan rather than on the patient as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections were present, but they were often quickly followed by advice or questions. Emotional nuances like embarrassment about inactivity and longing for her active self were acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior (re-establishing exercise) from opening question to close. Every exchange moved toward concrete action with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine back-and-forth existed with the patient's ideas incorporated, but the power balance was uneven. The counselor supplied most solutions, as reflected in the patient saying 'you basically organized my brain for me.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered and no pressure applied, but heavy prescriptive content (detailed checklist, scripts, journal formats) reduced the patient's ownership. The counselor answered questions the patient hadn't asked.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong evocative opening, scaling questions, and success markers were used effectively. However, the counselor frequently pre-empted evocation by supplying options, checklists, and scripts rather than drawing them out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked for input but often assumed the expert role by proposing options, designing the checklist, and prescribing journal structure. The patient's own ideas shaped the plan only partially.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked substantial change talk, including stated desires, 10/10 confidence twice, and concrete commitments. Missed opportunities to strategically deepen the change talk when it emerged, such as elaborating on 'I'm doing something for me again.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (dislike of swimming, contract worry, weather) was acknowledged but not skillfully redirected. The counselor relied on logistical reassurance rather than double-sided reflections or affirming autonomy around sustain talk.
