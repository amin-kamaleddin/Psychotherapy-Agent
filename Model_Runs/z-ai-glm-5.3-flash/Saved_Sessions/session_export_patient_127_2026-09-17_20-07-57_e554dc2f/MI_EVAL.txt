# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout. No judgment regarding criminal history, ambivalence, or temptation to return to crime. Affirmations such as "That's not a small thing, Timur" and "it doesn't sound lame at all" communicate full acceptance even when the client voices shame and self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including complex and double-sided reflections. The opening reflection captured both sides of ambivalence ("tempted by quick money... at the same time you don't want to lose Fiona"). Later reflections captured subtle emotional nuance ("a hint of nervousness underneath"; "carrying that label of ex-con, lazy, all of it").

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus on reducing recidivism and building a pro-social life. Each exchange built momentum: from exploring what would make staying legit possible, to defining a small win, to a concrete plan with dates ("tell her Sunday, job centre Monday"), to relapse-prevention planning. No aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership between equals. Inclusive framing ("building yourself a team"), implicit permission-seeking before directing, and client ideas driving the agenda. The counselor built on Timur's suggestions (involving Fiona, weekly check-ins) rather than overriding them. The closing plan was entirely co-constructed from the client's own preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of client ownership and choice: "that's a plan, and it's yours"; "that's on you"; "what works for you." Options were offered without prescribing, and the decision about involving Fiona was explicitly left to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Motivation elicited from within the client rather than provided. Every key intervention was a question drawing out Timur's own motivations (Fiona, porch mornings, identity as a decisive person, desire to stop writing himself off). Client ended the session articulating intrinsic hope: "I kind of want to find out which one it is."

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Client explicitly positioned as expert: "You know your situation better than anyone here." Progress repeatedly attributed to the client ("notice you're the one who built all of this"). The entire plan originated from Timur, and follow-up format was requested rather than prescribed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5/5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation via open questions ("What would count as a small win?", "What would be different about how you see yourself?"). Client's change talk escalated from desire → ability → commitment → full plan, and the counselor reinforced this progression ("You weighed both sides... you landed on something concrete").

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4/5
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk and redirected effectively using double-sided reflections pairing negatives with strengths ("That's the same guy who could stack shelves for a month"). Score of 4 rather than 5 because sustain talk around not belonging and quick money occupied several opening exchanges before the pivot toward change was fully established.

---
**Overall Global Rating:** **4.9/5**

**Summary:** Exemplary MI session demonstrating mastery of relational foundations and technical skills. Particular strengths include double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with client strengths, progressively deeper evocative questions moving from contemplation to a concrete client-generated plan with dates, explicit attribution of progress to the client, and attention to emotional nuance. Minor growth areas: earlier pivot away from opening sustain talk, and a brief structured exploration of confidence/readiness rulers could further strengthen the intervention.
