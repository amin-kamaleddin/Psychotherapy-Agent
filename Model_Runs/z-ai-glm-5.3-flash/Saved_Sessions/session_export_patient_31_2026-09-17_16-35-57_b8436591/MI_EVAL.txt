# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, explicitly validating David's evening ritual and choice to keep it. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic ('Nice plan') and lack deeper individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening that captures David's ambivalence and pressure sensitivities well. Reflections stay at the content level rather than validating deeper emotions like his fear of failure or worry about being asked about slips.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on a specific, negotiated target (no cigarettes at work this week) with every turn tying back to the plan. Session concludes with a concrete, time-bound goal and follow-up structure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration in goal-setting with David shaping the plan and rejecting options. Balance tips clinician-led through unsolicited advice lists and four consecutive closing recaps of the same plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change consistently framed as David's choice, with failures framed as learning and his evening smoke explicitly endorsed. Autonomy diluted somewhat by prescriptive instruction-giving and repeated recaps.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The experiment framing successfully elicited commitment language and David's own success criteria. Missed opportunities to explore his deeper values, the meaning of his ritual, and fear of failure; much content is clinician-supplied advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David's input shaped the goal, check-in timing, and rejected options, showing real power sharing. Heavy advice-dispensing and multiple recaps shift the dynamic toward a directive expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully moved David from 'I don't really wanna quit' to commitment ('no cigarettes at work this week, I can wrap my head around that'). Some change talk elicited via yes/no questions, and deeper 'why' exploration was missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged and reframed respectfully without arguing. Little active softening—doubts about gum and beer-and-delay worries were met with advice rather than double-sided reflections linking to his change reasons.
