# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard ('You're not broken, Liyana') with no judgment about snacking, family food environment, or fear of tracking. Respect for confidentiality ('I won't tell your mom') and warm affirmations reinforced the client's worth throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured frustration, fear, and loneliness (e.g., 'I hear how lonely home can feel'). However, deeper emotional experiences like the sting of friends' weight jokes and the mother's critical comments were only acknowledged at surface level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the weight/health behavior and guided toward a concrete plan (3-day tracking at ~8pm, review next session) while keeping the agenda client-driven. Minor drift into loneliness and peer comments occurred without integration into the plan or follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly collaborative session with joint framing ('Is it okay if we look at this together?', 'we'll pick one tiny change together'). The client's contributions genuinely shaped the plan around her constraints, and the counselor immediately honored her request to review notes together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support through permission-seeking, menu-based options, and explicit choice about tracking format and reminders ('Would you like a quick reminder, or would you prefer to set an alarm yourself?'). Normalized imperfection and consistently reinforced client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Liyana's preferences, coping strategies, and commitment ('I'll do the notes'), including her insight that 'just knowing I'm not doing this alone helps.' Deeper intrinsic motivation was less explored—solutions were offered early, and importance/confidence rulers were never used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as expert on her own life, honoring her choices on format, timing, and privacy. The balance tipped toward counselor-generated solutions at times (e.g., providing the 3-line template) without first eliciting her own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evoked commitment language effectively by asking 'What would success look like for you?' and offering menu options, resulting in clear commitments ('I'll do the notes,' 'I'll have the three days done'). Evocation was somewhat directive, and hope-related ambivalence ('what's the point') was redirected rather than explored as motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully reframed sustain talk like 'what if I'm just broken' and pivoted from hopelessness to strengths with a one-small-change framing. The scale-focused definition of success was gently challenged but appropriately not over-pursued, leaving that sustain talk somewhat unresolved.
