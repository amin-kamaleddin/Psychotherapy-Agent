# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations throughout; however, affirmations are somewhat generic and ambivalence is not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong early reflections accurately captured Richard's ambivalence and values (active retirement with his wife); later reflections thinned out in favor of practical checklists, missing deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on medication adherence and lifestyle change with no topic drift; momentum progressed logically from exploration to commitment to contingency planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was genuinely co-constructed: Richard modified the plan, set his own timeline, and requested supports; the counselor accommodated each adjustment without friction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were consistently framed as Richard's with no pressure or persuasion; frequent pre-packaged option menus slightly constrained his generative role.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler was skillfully used, but motivation largely arrived unbidden; no classic evocative questions were asked, and spontaneously emerging change talk was never deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Richard was repeatedly positioned as the expert and his choices genuinely shaped the plan; option menus rather than open inquiry slightly limited his role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was elicited (confidence ratings, commitment statements) but never deliberately deepened or consolidated with targeted affirmations; most change language arose from Richard's own style rather than skillful evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (minimization, side-effect worries) was acknowledged without amplification and pivoted toward solutions; a 5 would require more consistent strategic reframing and double-sided reflections.
