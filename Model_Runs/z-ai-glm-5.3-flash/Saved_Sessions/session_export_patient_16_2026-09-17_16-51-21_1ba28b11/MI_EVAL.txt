# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, normalizing the referral and accepting Anika's 'not forever' boundary without pushback. Lacks explicit affirmations of Anika as a person beyond her plan, keeping it short of a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Anika's perspective, including vaping's social value and her basketball worry. Reflections are often compressed into advice transitions, and deeper emotional layers (fear of Sarah's judgment, minimization) receive only surface acknowledgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every turn ties back to vaping and basketball eligibility, with no meaningful drift. The session progresses logically from assessment to rulers to planning, rehearsal, and a concrete first step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent inclusive language and a genuinely joint role-play with debriefing. The agenda and option lists originate mostly from the counselor, so the closing plan is more counselor-framed than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes Anika's choice and explicitly respects her limits, offering advice as a menu rather than directives. Misses chances to reinforce her ownership of the change (e.g., 'these are your ideas and your plan').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions effectively evoke Anika's own motivation ('I play better clean,' 'I can do this'). However, substantial content like refusal lines and delay strategies is supplied rather than evoked, and deeper values beyond basketball go unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anika's input is sought at key points, and she chooses strategies and scripts. Most options are counselor-supplied even though Anika repeatedly generates the best ideas herself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Structured rulers and role-play elicit strong change and commitment statements, with confidence rising from 7 to 8. Emerging change talk is not always deepened — e.g., 'I play better when I'm not high' prompts advice rather than elaboration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced and the counselor pivots back to the season goal. However, no double-sided reflections or reframes are used, so minimization ('no one would catch me') is tolerated rather than strategically resolved.
