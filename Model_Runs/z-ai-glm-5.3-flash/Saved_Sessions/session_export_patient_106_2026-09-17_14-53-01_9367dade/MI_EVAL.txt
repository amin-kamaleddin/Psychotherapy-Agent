# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, supportive tone ("Thanks for being here — it takes courage"; "I respect you keeping it just the car"). No judgment when the patient rationalizes smoking. Directly addressed her fear of judgment by inviting a follow-up "good or bad." Warmth somewhat formulaic/repetitive; little explicit normalization of slips.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Good reflective listening ("You're juggling a lot — work, parenting, and the worry..."); validated the car as decompression time through tailored suggestions. Reflections often followed by multiple questions, diluting depth. Emotional nuances (fear of being "snappy," self-criticism pattern, "He's watching") noted but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Clear focus on a specific target behavior — car smoking after pickup — progressed to a concrete plan (supplies Sunday, lighters out, dash note, if-then coping, reward, backup, follow-up). Stacked questions sometimes scattered focus; broader ambivalence (house/rest of day) bracketed without acknowledgment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Largely co-constructed: patient selected dash note wording, chose toothpicks, invented coffee reward and drafted her own if-then. Check-ins invited, not imposed. Power-sharing uneven at moments — multi-part question bundles and suggestion lists preceded adequate elicitation of her own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Autonomy strongly affirmed ("I'm here to listen and support your choices"; "I respect you keeping it just the car"); permission asked before offering ideas; patient's self-set scope honored without pushing. Advice sometimes delivered as long prescriptive menus; slip reframe generated by the client rather than actively reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from within ("What would breathing easier look like?", confidence rulers, "What one small thing would move that number up?"). Patient's closing change talk ("For him, mostly. And yeah, a little for me too") was personally meaningful. Advice menus sometimes crowded out evocation; deeper values discrepancy left implicit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Patient positioned as expert ("Which one or two of these feel most doable?", "pick one or tweak it"); her choices substantially shaped the plan. Suggestion lists long and rapid-fire at times, tilting toward an advice-giving stance rather than fully eliciting her ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4 ☐ 3 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk: "What would breathing easier look like?", "one small, doable change", repeated confidence rulers moving patient from 4 → 7. Patient generated her own plan, if-then, and commitment language ("I'll actually do this one"). Falls short of 5 due to rapid-fire question bundles; desire/need talk less explored than ability/commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 3 ☐ 4 ☐ 2 ☐ 1
**Comments:** Sustain talk acknowledged without reinforcement ("it makes sense smoking helps you unwind") but rarely softened with double-sided reflections or strategic reframes. The wine comparison and minimization ("he doesn't have asthma, we're okay for now") passed without linking to her parental values — a missed opportunity to develop discrepancy.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 3.9/5**

**Summary:** A solid, well-structured MI session that successfully moved the patient from ambivalent contemplation to a concrete, self-generated plan (smoke-free car Monday, if-then coping, environmental changes, values-based dash note, reward, slip plan, and follow-up). **Strengths:** consistent respect and affirmation, strong autonomy support, effective confidence rulers (4→7), evocation of her own solutions, and a clearly directed, collaborative planning process culminating in spoken commitment. **Areas for improvement:** (1) reduce stacked, multi-part questions in favor of one query at a time; (2) deepen reflections on emotional content; (3) actively soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections to develop discrepancy; (4) elicit her ideas before offering suggestion menus; (5) more explicitly normalize slips to reinforce self-efficacy.
