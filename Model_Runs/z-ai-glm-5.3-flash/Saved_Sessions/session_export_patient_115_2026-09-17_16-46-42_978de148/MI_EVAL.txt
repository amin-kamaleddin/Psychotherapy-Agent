# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental, supportive tone throughout, with Samir explicitly praising the non-lecturing approach. Warmth was somewhat formulaic, with limited explicit normalization of struggle beyond "slips happen."

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of Samir's fear, his role as a father, and smoking as "your one break after long taxi shifts." Reflections were occasionally brief and list-like, with limited deeper reflection of his emotional weight and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, consistent focus on asthma-related smoking from the first utterance, funneling into a specific 3-shift experiment, tracking, contingency planning, and a Friday follow-up. No drift occurred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with permission asked before offering tools and Samir retaining control of tasks like calling the chemist. Occasionally the psychiatrist supplied lists of ideas and checklists rather than drawing them fully from Samir.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: Samir's "three shifts only" limit was honored, choices were repeatedly offered, and he explicitly noted "You let me pick what I could actually do." Safety advice was given appropriately without controlling his decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from the client, eliciting values ("be there for my kids") and his own reasons and ideas (wife, cabin filter, inhaler refill). Samir articulated intrinsic motivation and initiated additional supports himself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently invited Samir's expertise ("Which of those feels most doable?") and adapted the plan to his job realities, with Samir's contributions visibly shaping the plan. Fell short of 5 because some suggestions came as pre-packaged counselor-generated menus rather than elicited ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation through values questions, repeated confidence rulers (0–10 twice), and "What would move you one point higher?" Samir's change talk strengthened from ambivalence to "I do want to breathe properly again," with confidence rising from 5 to 8.5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not argued with; partial progress was affirmed and relapse was preempted with a non-shameful slip plan. At times the psychiatrist reinforced sustain talk by repeatedly accepting the "three shifts, not forever" framing without gently exploring the broader goal.
