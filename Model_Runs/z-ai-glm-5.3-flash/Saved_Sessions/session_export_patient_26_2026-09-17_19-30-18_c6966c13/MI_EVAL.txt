# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, non-judgmental, and affirming ('reaching out took real courage,' 'That's not a small shift, Daniela'). The psychiatrist normalizes Daniela's struggles and validates her feelings throughout without any disapproving language.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately capture surface content and deeper meaning, including her fear of owing people and anxiety about waiting. The clinician even anticipated her worry ('I'll send it, then stare at my phone all day'), which she confirmed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains steady focus on reliable childcare as the root stressor, moving from problem definition to a concrete, client-owned plan (Tuesday text, Head Start backup). Every exchange advances the arc without losing focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is fully co-constructed from Daniela's stated preferences (text vs. stairs, meal trade, Head Start number). The clinician provides structure while the client supplies all content, in a tone of two equals problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decision authority is repeatedly returned to the client ('That's your expertise talking, not mine,' 'What feels like the best first step to you?'). No advice is imposed; solutions are explicitly attributed to her own strengths and authorship.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocative questions draw out her own motivations and reasons for change ('I'd like to find out,' 'I want one of those evenings to be real'). Commitment language and confidence emerge entirely from her, not from clinician-supplied rationale.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician actively locates expertise with the client and explicitly honors her contributions ('you did all of this today — you figured out the plan'). All content — meal trade, text idea, coping strategy — comes from Daniela herself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions consistently evoke and amplify change talk, from the imagined-evening question to 'What would make this week the week you actually ask her?' Commitment-strength language is reflected and amplified, leading directly to a concrete plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally redirected effectively, such as pivoting from distrust of her sister to 'What would knowing for sure look like?'. The minor gap is that some sustain talk (mom's unreliability, financial limits) is acknowledged without an immediate pivot back to her agency.
