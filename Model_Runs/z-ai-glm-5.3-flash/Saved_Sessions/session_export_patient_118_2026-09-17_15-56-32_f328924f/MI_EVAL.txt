# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental with repeated 'no lecture' assurances and affirmations like 'Nice work.' Falls short of exemplary because warmth was brief and functional, with little normalization of his failed medication attempt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate content and emotion reflections, e.g., capturing his frustration and dental goals. Reflections stayed at a practical level and did not probe the emotional layer beneath his resistance, such as shame or provider fatigue.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully maintained focus on the dental goal, producing concrete actions (held appointment, itemized estimate, practiced script). However, direction toward the clinical target of smoking cessation was abandoned entirely.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Model collaboration — plans were co-constructed around the client's stated constraints, and his request for a script and role-play became the session's core activity. The phone role-play exemplified skill-building with the client doing the work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support throughout — permission-seeking, explicit choices, and ceding control of the agenda visibly reduced resistance over the session. The client moved from guardedness to active engagement and appreciation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation for the dental goal was genuinely evoked, but for smoking cessation — the clinical target — almost nothing was elicited. The client's spontaneous change talk ('I guess I got to really want to quit') was deflected rather than explored with evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as decision-maker via permission questions, offered choices, and role-play rehearsal. Not a 5 because key expertise (cost figures, scripts) remained counselor-delivered rather than drawn from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's spontaneous change talk about quitting was met with deferral ('one thing at a time'), effectively closing the door on emerging motivation. His admission that smoking stained his teeth was another unexplored opening.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or persuading when the client dismissed the pills, keeping him engaged. However, this was topic avoidance rather than skillful softening — no double-sided reflections or linking sustain talk to his dental values.
