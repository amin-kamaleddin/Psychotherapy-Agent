# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental responses like 'You're not flaky for asking — life happens' directly countered Amina's self-criticism. Affirmations were supportive but somewhat generic and could have been more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening reflection was accurate and emotionally attuned, but the counselor quickly shifted to problem-solving with few subsequent reflections. Deeper feelings like fear of judgment and rejection were addressed with scripts rather than reflective understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every counselor turn advanced the clear focus on securing a rescheduled interview and solving childcare. The session ended with specific, time-bound commitments (send message tonight, text mom tomorrow).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction functioned as joint problem-solving with choices offered at each step and Amina's preferences shaping the agenda. However, the counselor frequently supplied scripts and plans rather than co-creating them with Amina's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regular options were offered without coercive language, and Amina's choices drove the session. Autonomy could have been strengthened by explicitly affirming her capacity for self-direction rather than positioning the counselor as plan-generator.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain — the counselor largely provided scripts and solutions rather than eliciting Amina's own motivations and ideas. Despite her volunteering strong confidence ('it sits about at a 10'), no evocative questions explored or built on it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated check-ins ('Want me to personalize the message now?') positioned Amina as decision-maker, and her preferences shaped the agenda. The strong expert role in providing fully written scripts slightly limited true power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina generated substantial change talk herself, but the counselor did not consistently evoke or deepen it with open questions or importance/confidence scaling. Affirmations of action were present, but most change talk was client-generated rather than counselor-evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not amplified and was generally redirected, with reframes and proactive coping preparation for rejection fears. A brief double-sided reflection or deliberate pivot toward her stated confidence could have strengthened this further.
