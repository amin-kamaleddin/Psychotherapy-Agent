# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, with explicit validation of guilt and normalization of napping ('Napping when she naps is not only okay—it's smart'). However, affirmations tend to be formulaic and attached to transitions into advice rather than standing alone as genuine, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening in the opening and accurate captures of Nadine's self-blame and guilt. Reflections were often shallow and quickly followed by information-giving, missing opportunities to explore the emotional weight of her guilt and identity shift as a new mother.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the target concerns throughout, with each turn advancing toward concrete outcomes (night tactics, partner script, home visitor note, follow-up plan). Nadine left with a specific plan and defined next steps, demonstrating excellent directional skill without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used collaborative framing and joint activities like role-play, but the power balance tilted toward the expert with the counselor generating virtually all content. Nadine's role was largely selecting among options rather than co-creating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong use of menu options and explicit deference ('Check with the pediatrician if unsure'), with Nadine clearly experiencing choice and her requests accommodated without resistance. Score limited by heavily prescriptive advice (specific scripts, tactics) and little emphasis on affirming her capacity to direct her own parenting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Operated primarily from an advice-giving stance, conveying that solutions come from the clinician rather than from Nadine. Questions were information-gathering or menu-prompting rather than true evocation—core opportunities to explore her hopes, values, and confidence were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partially conveyed client expertise through menu-based choices and inviting Nadine's edits to the note. However, the bulk of the session was expert-to-novice information delivery, and Nadine never elicited for her own ideas ('What have you already tried?' was never asked).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nadine generated meaningful change talk largely on her own, aided by menu options and selective questioning ('Which of these feels doable?'). The counselor rarely deepened change talk with evocative questions or strategic responses, and never explored confidence/ability explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Pivoted reasonably well from sustain talk without amplifying it, and reframed despair toward options. Responses were delivered as advice/corrections rather than double-sided reflections, and sometimes argued against sustain talk ('You're not failing'), which was persuasive rather than MI-consistent.
