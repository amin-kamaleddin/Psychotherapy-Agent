# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance, affirming the patient's self-knowledge and honoring her 'trying rather than promising' framing without pressure. Falls short of 5 because affirmations are somewhat generic and moments of vulnerability (e.g., 'bad at sticking with stuff') aren't explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong throughout, accurately capturing the patient's surprise, ambivalence, and values. However, reflections sometimes pivot to questions rather than sitting with emotional disclosures, such as her self-doubt about not finishing courses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, progressive focus on smoking with visible momentum from the dental visit to a concrete, self-generated plan and identity change. Each turn builds on the last without drift, opening and closing coherently on the same thread.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor works in a genuinely consultative mode, treating the patient's judgment as decisive and co-constructing the plan from her suggestions. The score is limited because the counselor fully controls pacing and agenda, never explicitly inviting the patient to set direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is explicit and consistent — 'Whatever pace you choose from here, that choice is yours' — with zero pressure toward full cessation. All strategies came from the patient, and ownership of both decisions and setbacks is reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All reasons for change originate from the patient (teeth, gran's gum problems, proving wrong the 'can't stick with stuff' voice), which the counselor systematically elicits and develops. The final identity-level exchange is exemplary; the counselor only reflects, affirms, and asks.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positions the patient as the authority ('You know yourself and your habits better than anyone') and her contributions shape the plan. Falls short of 5 because partnership is expressed through formulaic phrases rather than explicit agenda negotiation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength: change talk escalates from 'maybe I should pay attention' to 'that I can do,' 'worth a shot,' and identity-level language, deliberately attributed back to the patient ('those are your words, not mine'). Closure questions defining success on her own terms are exemplary evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids amplifying resistance, affirming the honest 'trying rather than promising' framing and acknowledging social-smoking fears without reinforcement. Not a 5 because double-sided reflections are never used, and one question ('which feel hardest to imagine giving up?') invites elaboration of sustain talk.
