# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently shows unconditional positive regard—affirming Samir's cutting back, validating his refusal to commit to quitting completely, and never judging his habits. A warm, respectful tone is maintained throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate, capturing both sides of ambivalence and the emotional weight of symptoms ('a moment of peace you've earned'). The counselor spotlights Samir's own insight ('maybe it's actually making things worse at night') in a precise complex reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, progressive focus on asthma-related lifestyle change, building from ambivalence to a concrete two-week experiment, backup plan, and commitment summary. Guidance is consistent without drifting, tied directly to Samir's stated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session occurs between equal partners, with the plan co-constructed from Samir's own ideas (walking, chamomile tea, phone notes) and follow-up framed as joint review. His contributions demonstrably shape the direction of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is reinforced throughout: 'you decide what comes next,' framing change as a client-run experiment, and explicitly welcoming a negative outcome ('either way, I'll have my answer'). Samir's closing comment that 'this feels like my plan, not someone else's' confirms its effectiveness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within—the brother's visit recollection prompting Samir's own conclusion that drinks aren't helping, and questions about family impact surfacing deeply personal reasons for change. All reasons for change come from Samir's own mouth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly conveys that expertise resides within the client ('You know your body better than anyone here,' 'you'll be the one telling me what they mean'). Samir explicitly notes 'this feels like my plan, not someone else's'—direct evidence of power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evokes and deepens change talk through evocative questions about family impact and 'better nights,' eliciting strong desire, need, and commitment language. The closing commitment summary consolidates Samir's own articulated plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently pivoted away from without dismissal—autonomy is affirmed while redirecting to Samir's own concerns about nighttime symptoms. Resistance is transformed into a client-owned behavioral experiment rather than argued against.
