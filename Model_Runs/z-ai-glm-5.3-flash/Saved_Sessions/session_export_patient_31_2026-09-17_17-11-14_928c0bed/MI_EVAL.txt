# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a warm, respectful tone and accepts David's boundaries (non-negotiable evenings, refusing partner check-in) without judgment or pressure. Warmth is somewhat formulaic, and statements like 'Smart to keep evenings for now' edge toward endorsing the status quo rather than balanced unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, consistent reflections capture David's dual pulls (partner/cost vs. smoking as a way to unwind) and validate his confidence rating. The psychiatrist occasionally drifts into information delivery rather than exploring the deeper emotional meaning of evenings as David's protected recovery time.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, sustained focus on the target behavior (smoking reduction) with no topic drift. The session moves systematically from exploring reasons to a concrete one-week trial, safety netting, logging, and follow-up, with each turn tied back to the practical plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-created, building on David's own suggestions (gum in pocket, alarm) with options offered and input sought. However, long recaps and clinical instruction blocks tilt the exchange toward counselor-led delivery, with David's contributions sometimes only briefly acknowledged.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is actively supported—menu options, emphasizing the plan is reversible, and honoring David's preferences for the 16-hour patch, self-set alarm, and refusal of partner involvement. Scores fall short of 5 because instructions are sometimes delivered as a directive stream without checking preferences, and the evening boundary is affirmed without engaging David's ownership of it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited at key moments (reasons for cutting down, confidence scaling, what would nudge him higher), producing David's own tentative hope. A substantial portion of the session is counselor-provided instruction rather than drawing out and expanding David's motivations, and deeper meanings of smoking (stress relief, protected evenings) are left unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good partnership moves include asking permission, offering a menu, and asking which support sounds most useful. However, the counselor frequently adopts an expert stance with detailed dosing and safety monologues, and David's passive compliance ('Yeah, alright... show me that chew thing quick') indicates power-sharing is only partial.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocative questions ('What are the biggest reasons you want to cut down?', confidence scaling) elicited David's own reasons and tentative optimism about the number rising. Change talk is not consistently deepened—David's 'maybe we'll see' about evenings is left unexplored—and much of the session shifts to prescriptive planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with sustain talk and respects David's evening autonomy, but never actively softens it. No double-sided reflections pair sustain talk with change talk (e.g., linking cost motivation with keeping evenings), and ambivalence is accommodated rather than used to develop discrepancy.
