# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, but warmth is thin; key moments calling for affirmation (tolerance admission, the 'sting') and validation of the stigma complaint went unaffirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A few accurate early reflections ('mostly' ambivalence), but major vulnerable disclosures — the sting of secrecy and fear of using alone — were met with checklists rather than reflective exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed consistently focused on meth use, tolerance, solo use, and monitoring despite deflections; lost points for drifting into stigma psychoeducation and never summarizing to consolidate direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing (offering choices, asking which to try), but the plan and log were largely counselor-generated and delivered as expert monologues; client ideas were only implicitly incorporated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, accepted the client's rejection of the hotline and refusal to commit to quitting without pressure; fell short of 5 by not crediting the client for his own good ideas (Sunday-off, casual check-in).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence question evoked meaningful insights, but motivation surfaced mostly by accident amid information delivery; the counselor rarely asked about the client's own observations, reasons, or values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-adjacent questions reflect some power-sharing, but the dominant mode was expert delivery of lists and a pre-built log rather than co-created planning built on the client's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence question elicited real change talk and commitments (logging, avoiding solo use), but the counselor never deepened, reflected, or affirmed these statements, missing key moments like Adrian's discomfort with secrecy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk wasn't argued with, but it was also never strategically softened via double-sided reflections; repeated risk-warning lists kept focus on danger language rather than pivoting to the client's motivations.
