# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, affirming Linda even when she admits avoidance ('honesty with me — even about chickening out — is still showing up for yourself'). There is no judgment or disapproval anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are deeply accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both content and feeling ('woven together... part of who you are,' 'I hear how exhausting this is'). Fears like getting hopes up are validated rather than challenged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking cessation from start to finish while progressively building momentum from ambivalence to a concrete commitment (calling the doctor Thursday morning) and barrier rehearsal. Each exchange moves deliberately toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely collaborative — the counselor frames the idea of a small step, but Linda supplies the specifics (doctor's call, mornings, written list). Decisions emerge from dialogue rather than prescription.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor reinforces Linda's ownership throughout ('I'm not pushing either way,' 'yours to own') and asks permission before gently challenging the six-month timeline. The final reframe ('I don't need luck') is Linda's own autonomous conclusion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within via skillful questions ('What makes this time feel different?'), yielding Linda's own reasons, plan, and self-efficacy statement. The counselor never lectures or persuades.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Linda is explicitly positioned as the expert on her own life ('You know yourself and your patterns better than anyone'), and her contributions shape the plan. This reflects exemplary power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strategically evokes and amplifies commitment language ('call my doctor's office this week,' 'one ring at a time') and reinforces growing self-efficacy. Change talk deepens progressively across the session, culminating in Linda's 'I can do that.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk about 'the girls' and the six-month timeline is acknowledged through double-sided reflections, then gently redirected toward change. The counselor surfaces the avoidance function of the timeline without confrontation, leading to Linda's own insight about moving the finish line.
