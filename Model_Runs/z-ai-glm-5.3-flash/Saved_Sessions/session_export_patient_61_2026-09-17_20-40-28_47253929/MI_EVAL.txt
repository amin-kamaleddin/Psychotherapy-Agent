# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, including warm, non-defensive responses to the patient's admission of saying what he thought the counselor wanted to hear. Ambivalence was consistently normalized without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening that repeatedly surfaced emotional meaning, prompting the patient to say 'I hadn't really put that into words before.' The counselor skillfully used the patient's own metaphors ('handing in blanks') to deepen understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on diabetes self-management from open to close, with each exchange advancing toward an actionable plan. No topic drift; the session progressed from overwhelm to a specific Thursday test and scheduled Tuesday follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership with 'we' language, understanding checks, and plan elements shaped almost entirely by the patient. The patient's comment that this wasn't 'another lecture' confirmed the counselor departed from the expected expert-driven dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of patient ownership and choice, explicitly de-pressurizing and never prescribing. The patient's shift from 'report card' to 'something I actually laid out myself' demonstrates successful autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within the client—no lecturing or supplied reasons—with evocative questions eliciting his own strategies and insights. The patient explicitly noted the novelty of being asked these questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise consistently located within the client, with all plan components (meter placement, self-recording, self-forgiveness) originating from him. The counselor's contributions were limited to reflecting, affirming, and asking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful selective reflection caught and amplified minimal change talk ('smaller pieces') even after the patient disavowed it, producing progressively stronger commitment. The session moved the patient from pre-contemplation to a specific behavior and follow-up appointment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally not reinforced, with effective double-sided reflections and direct but gentle handling of all-or-nothing thinking. One point short because an early reflection restated barrier content, briefly keeping focus on obstacles.
