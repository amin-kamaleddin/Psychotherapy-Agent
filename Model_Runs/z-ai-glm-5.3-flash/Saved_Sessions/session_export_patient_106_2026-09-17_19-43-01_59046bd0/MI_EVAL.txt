# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, with affirmations and reframing of past failures as lack of preparation, not weakness. No shaming even when the patient minimizes harm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, deep reflections drawn from the patient's exact words, capturing her ambivalence, exhaustion, and identity as a single mother.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking with each exchange moving progressively toward change. No drift; patient-led yet purposeful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit partnership ('let's think like partners here') with the plan co-created entirely from the patient's own ideas. Equal-partner dynamic modeled throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated reinforcement of patient choice and honoring her refusal to commit to full quitting. All change framed as her decision on her terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary—never provides reasons for change; all motivation drawn from the patient's own values, experiences, and solutions. Culminates in her own commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Patient positioned as the expert on her life, with the entire plan coming from her rather than the clinician. Active, consistent power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustained evocation of desire, ability, and commitment language, with each burst of change talk immediately reflected and reinforced. Builds to genuine self-affirming statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk acknowledged without amplification and redirected effectively via double-sided reflection. Minor deduction for validating cigarettes as her pause, which could subtly legitimize continued smoking.
