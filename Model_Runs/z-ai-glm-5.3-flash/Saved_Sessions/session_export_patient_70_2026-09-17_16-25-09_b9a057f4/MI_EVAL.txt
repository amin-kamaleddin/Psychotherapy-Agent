# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and affirming throughout, normalizing embarrassment about literacy and convictions without judgment. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic, and Lars's 'stomach drop' about convictions was acknowledged only briefly before moving to logistics.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections are accurate, but the counselor's listening thins in the second half. Emotionally loaded disclosures (shame about reading, fear of the convictions line, the pull of the lads) are mostly met with checklists and logistics rather than reflective validation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus: every exchange ties back to the concrete target of polytech enrollment, interview prep, and Friday planning, with no topic drift and steady momentum toward action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided, with Lars driving key decisions and co-constructing the plan. The counselor sometimes front-loads his own expertise (checklists, sample lines, excuse ideas) before eliciting Lars's own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Next steps are consistently framed as Lars's choice, with no pressure or moralizing about Friday drinking. Counselor supplies menu options and wording himself at times, and ownership-reinforcing affirmations are limited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions and having Lars craft his own convictions line effectively elicit client-generated motivation (5→7 confidence). Deeper values—Donna, fatherhood, a different life—are mentioned but never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lars is repeatedly positioned as decision-maker and his contributions substantially shape the session. The counselor unilaterally converts the call into a role-play without checking Lars's preference.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation of change talk via open questions and confidence scaling ('I don't wanna go back inside', 'I'm at about a 7 now'). Change talk is not systematically deepened by linking it to Lars's broader values and reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is briefly normalized and reframed toward change without being amplified. Double-sided reflections are not used to hold ambivalence (mates vs. Donna), with the counselor jumping to problem-solving instead.
