# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with warm acknowledgments ('Thanks — appreciate you being open', 'well done') even when Evelyn minimized her drinking. Warmth was somewhat procedural and affirmations formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate but largely cognitive restatements; emotional cues (son's stinging comments, fear about the fall, self-consciousness during rehearsal) were missed and the session shifted quickly to advice. Empathy was inconsistent and feelings were rarely explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept clear focus on alcohol-related concerns and progressed logically to concrete commitments (stairs rule, earlier stop, GP call, son's text). However, focus early settled on the son's concerns rather than drinking itself, and hedged framing ('sensible changes') was never redirected to the drinking target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered menus, asked permission, co-edited the son's text, and rehearsed the GP call jointly; Evelyn's preferences genuinely shaped the plan. Falls short of 5 because much content came from pre-packaged advice lists and scripts rather than jointly generated ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support — repeatedly offered choice ('Which would you prefer?', 'Want it firmer or softer?') and accepted Evelyn's edits and self-set stop time without pushback. No pressure or coercion evident anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reasons for change emerged (keeping promises to her son, sleep improvement), but the session relied heavily on counselor-supplied solutions and scripts. Key evocative questions about what matters to her, the fall's meaning, and her confidence were missing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for Evelyn's preferences and incorporated her edits, showing responsiveness. But long advice lists, pre-drafted texts, scripted calls, and rapid-fire questions positioned the counselor as the expert rather than an equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited change talk through low-threat options — Evelyn generated meaningful commitments ('the stairs one, definitely', 'I'll ring them this week'). However, change talk wasn't deepened with evocative questions, and hedged language ('sensible changes') was left unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue with or reinforce Evelyn's minimization, but also rarely softened it — no double-sided reflections or strategic reflection of ambivalence. Sustain talk was mostly sidestepped, leaving 'I still don't think I'm some heavy drinker' unaddressed at session end.
