# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, normalizing ambivalence and slips, with Claudia explicitly noting she didn't feel judged. Affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic rather than deeply individualized to her strengths and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Claudia's core ambivalence (comforting ritual vs. foggy, low mornings) using her own words. Reflections are often followed quickly by multiple questions, and deeper emotional content around depression is acknowledged but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focus stays consistently on reducing nightly wine intake from opening through planning, safety check, and a scheduled follow-up. The session moves efficiently to a specific, measurable goal with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session feels like joint problem-solving, with the plan built from Claudia's choices and her requests for the check-in honored. The plan's scaffolding (strategy menus, safety check, check-in structure) originates largely from the counselor rather than both partners equally.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions are framed as Claudia's, advice is offered as optional ideas, and safety questions are framed as checks. Rapid-fire suggestion menus occasionally steer before she has generated her own options, and explicit ownership language is missing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions and open prompts successfully evoke Claudia's own reasons and success criteria, raising her confidence from 5 to 7. Evocation weakens in the second half as the counselor shifts to supplying ideas and logistics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Claudia's contributions shape the plan and she selects her own strategies, but frequent unsolicited suggestion menus make the cadence feel more counselor-driven. A top score would show more client-led idea generation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaled questions reliably evoke change talk, and Claudia articulates concrete commitments and her own success criteria. Emerging change talk (e.g., 'one bad day would prove something to me') is not always reflected back or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids amplifying sustain talk, reflecting ambivalence and responding to anticipated slips with nonjudgmental, plan-oriented framing. Remaining sustain talk (e.g., wine makes low times 'nicer') is moved past rather than actively reframed.
