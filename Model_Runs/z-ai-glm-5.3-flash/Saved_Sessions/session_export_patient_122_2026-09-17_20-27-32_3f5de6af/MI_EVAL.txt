# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard; ambivalence was framed non-judgmentally (e.g., 'Both voices make sense, and you don't have to pick a winner right now'). The closing affirmed Sofia's worth independent of outcomes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, capturing experiences like lost routines and the 'tug-of-war' of ambivalence, and reflecting her core value ('I want to feel like me again'). Sofia repeatedly confirmed being understood ('Yeah, that's exactly where I'm at').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on inactivity and evening emotional eating, progressing from vague dissatisfaction to a concrete plan (ten-minute after-dinner walk) and commitment ('I'll start tonight') with a confidence rating. No topic drift occurred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned between equal partners, with permission-style questions and co-constructed plans built from Sofia's own ideas. The counselor positioned her insight as the engine of change rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of Sofia's ownership through choice-offering language ('not as a promise, but just as something to observe'; 'which feels like the most natural one to start with?'). Her closing remark that the plan 'is mine' confirms the autonomy-supportive impact.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key motivations were elicited rather than supplied: her value ('feel like me again'), her own solutions, barrier strategies, and self-generated fuel ('prove that doubt voice wrong'). Reflections amplified these, deepening ownership.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located within the client ('Since you know yourself better than anyone'), and the walk, cue-replacement idea, and 'messy is okay' framing all originated from Sofia and were honored and built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation and development of change talk through open questions and a confidence ruler, drawing out commitment language ('I know I need to move more'; 'So—just the walk. I can try that'). Self-motivating statements were strategically amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubt was consistently reframed as protective, and fear of quitting was met with her own solutions. However, some reflections restated the doubt side (e.g., 'another part braces for failure'), giving repeated airtime to the ambivalent voice.
