# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with effective normalization of slips ('it's information, not a failure'), and Mei explicitly noted she didn't get the lecture she expected. However, warmth was functional rather than individualized — few explicit affirmations of Mei's strengths and insights kept this from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accurately tracked key data (importance 8, confidence 4) and reflected Mei's fear at the start. Reflections were sparse, though — emotional content like her shame about condoms, family-history pain, and 'drunk-me makes the mess' went largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, excellent focus on reducing risky drinking and protecting contraceptive reliability from risk screening through birthday-night planning. Mei's own comment that she leaves with 'an actual plan instead of a wish' confirms strong direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction occurred in the second half (Priya as buddy, midnight exit, code word, practicing on a regular weekend). The first half was uneven, though, with prescriptive bullet-point advice delivered before mutual exploration, leaving Mei mostly approving pre-made lists.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently offered choices ('short concrete plan or longer-term supports?', 'Want this as a phone note?') and Mei's choices shaped the session. Some early strategy lists were offered unprompted without first asking what Mei had already considered, slightly limiting the score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation resided with Mei, and the counselor built plans around her stated danger zone and accountability idea. Evocative questions ('what matters most?', 'what moved you from 4 to 5?') were rarely asked — the counselor supplied strategies and rationales instead.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission was asked lightly and Mei's contributions were incorporated (code word, midnight exit, wallet condoms). Much of the session was the counselor generating lists with the client reacting, an advice-heavy, checklist-driven stance rather than treating Mei as the expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged largely from Mei herself, and her confidence rose from 4 to 5, which was noted. The counselor did not deliberately evoke or deepen it — few open questions like 'why does this matter?' or 'what would success look like?'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubts about self-control were met with concrete harm-reduction strategies targeting those barriers, and the reframe 'a slip is information, not a failure' effectively softened all-or-nothing thinking. Doubt-based planning occupied significant airtime, though it was channeled productively.
