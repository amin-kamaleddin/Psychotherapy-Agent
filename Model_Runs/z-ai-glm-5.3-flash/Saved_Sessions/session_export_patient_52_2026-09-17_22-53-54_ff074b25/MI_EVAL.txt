# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, affirming Irina's successes, meeting her minimization with curiosity, and validating her guilt without judgment. Warmth felt genuine and individualized throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and dual-sided, capturing both her need for evening quiet and the guilt beneath it. Reflections tracked surface content and deeper emotional meaning, including the 'nine o'clock emptiness.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear, progressive focus on evening drinking through a logical arc: understanding the behavior's function → desired evenings → first step → Friday plan → coping → reward. Momentum was purposeful and never pressured.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated between equal partners, with every planning decision (one drink, tea ritual, cupboard note, reward) originating from Irina. The counselor reflected, organized, and invited rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly reinforced, with the plan framed as Irina's own choice ('not because anyone told you to') and her 'experiment, not forever promise' framing explicitly endorsed. No directives or pressure appeared at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client via open questions, eliciting her own reasons (her son, pride, better mornings) and coping strategies. Change talk deepened from vague desire to commitment language ('I start this week').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Irina was explicitly positioned as the expert from the opening line, and the entire intervention was built from her own ideas. The counselor added nothing prescriptive, honoring her assessment of what was 'small enough to stick with.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoked and deepened change talk, from 'the wine being a choice rather than a need' to self-efficacy statements recalling her success quitting smoking. Change talk was reinforced by reflection and built toward a concrete plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (e.g., 'my whole family drinks like that,' 'the second drink calls my name') was consistently acknowledged briefly and pivoted toward her values and reasons for change via double-sided reflections. The session never argued against or dwelled on reasons against change.
