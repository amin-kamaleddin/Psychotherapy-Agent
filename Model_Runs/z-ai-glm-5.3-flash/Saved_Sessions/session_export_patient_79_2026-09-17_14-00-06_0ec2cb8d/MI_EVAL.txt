# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout despite patient sarcasm and defensiveness. However, warmth felt somewhat formulaic, and repeated recaps despite the patient's wish to leave subtly undercut acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were generic and mostly served as transitions to advice rather than deepening understanding. The patient explicitly said 'I don't think you totally get my situation,' yet his overwhelm and feeling nagged were never explored, and repeated safety check-ins despite clear irritation showed limited attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on inhaler adherence and produced a concrete, specific plan including a safety pathway. Falls short of 5 because direction was achieved through directive structuring and repetitive recaps rather than collaboratively derived goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and let the patient set check-in terms, but the plan framework, tracking method, and tips were counselor-supplied and only trimmed after pushback. Collaboration was reactive rather than proactive joint construction, and the patient's own ideas were never integrated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was regularly affirmed—'Try 14 days, no pressure to continue' and acceptance of the patient's refusals and boundaries. The mounting pile of tips and thrice-repeated recaps, however, conveyed subtle pressure, and the patient's autonomy statements were managed rather than celebrated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied by the counselor rather than drawn from the patient; evocation was limited to one readiness-scale question. The patient's own hooks (avoiding the ER, getting others 'off my back') went unused, and his change talk arose despite rather than because of counselor technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered options and implicit permission-asking, but early interactions carried an expert-driven tone, with the counselor adding tips the patient had to push back to remove. The patient's own expertise from his reading was never acknowledged or built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited useful change material including a concrete commitment, a readiness rating (4/10), and a self-generated rationale ('maybe it's worth a shot'). These statements were never affirmed or deepened—the counselor moved to advice rather than developing the patient's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with minimization and acknowledged resistance without reinforcing it (e.g., 'Two weeks, not forever'). However, sustain talk like 'you guys blow this out of proportion' was sidestepped rather than reframed or used as an opening for evocation.
