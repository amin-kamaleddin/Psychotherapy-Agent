# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, notably normalizing potential relapse ('even if you caved — giving it one honest try would still count'), showing deep unconditional acceptance rather than regard tied to success.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections go beneath surface content to emotional meaning, e.g., distinguishing belonging from smoking and naming the wish to be 'seen trying.' Elise repeatedly confirms the reflections landed ('That's it, exactly').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains unwavering focus on smoking cessation while progressing strategically from imagining social situations to swap strategies, success criteria, and support building. Each turn advances the change process without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates fully between equals, with every plan element client-generated and the counselor acting as a reflective sounding board. Summaries integrate the client's own words, showing her voice drives the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively reinforces autonomy ('That's your pace, and it makes sense,' 'Either way, you get to decide') and honors the client's chosen slow pace without pressure or pushing for a quit declaration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client — no lecturing; Elise volunteers her own 'why,' success definition, support needs, and key insights. Every major realization originates from her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise in the client ('That's your expertise about your friends showing'), and the entire plan originates from Elise with each question built from her last statement. Textbook power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions ('what would make it feel like it went well?', 'where does this one night fit?') evoke progressively stronger change talk, consistently reinforced with affirmations rather than the counselor's own arguments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well with double-sided reflections and reframing belonging as separate from smoking. A point short of 5 because the loneliness fear surfaced late and was left mostly to the client to resolve rather than being explicitly reframed by the counselor.
