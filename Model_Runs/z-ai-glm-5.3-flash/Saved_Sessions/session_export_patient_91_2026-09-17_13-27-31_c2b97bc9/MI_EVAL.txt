# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout; Marcus explicitly felt unjudged. However, affirmations are generic rather than specific to his strengths, and his fear of bad numbers is not explicitly validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; counselor responses jump to questions and instruction checklists rather than mirroring feelings. Notable emotional disclosures (fear of bad numbers, feeling less lonely) go unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays firmly focused on blood sugar monitoring with a logical progression from hardest task to concrete plan to follow-up, with no drift. Direction is achieved via directive instruction rather than MI-consistent guiding, and Marcus's concern about eating right is set aside.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration — Marcus's ideas (one check/day, wife's involvement) are incorporated and follow-up is negotiated. But power balance is uneven, with nearly all content delivered in lecture format rather than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking before tools and letting Marcus set his timing and follow-up are strengths. Overall posture remains prescriptive, with multi-step directives and few genuine choices; ownership is only affirmed at the very end.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor rarely asks what Marcus knows, values, or why managing diabetes matters to him; motivation emerges from Marcus's own circumstances rather than skilled evocation. The stance is closer to health education than MI.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional invitations for input allow Marcus to shape some details, but the session is dominated by counselor-delivered instruction blocks. His own knowledge and ideas are not actively drawn upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges and commitment to a concrete first step is secured, aided by the small-steps framing. However, the counselor does not intentionally evoke, reflect, or deepen this language — the commitment develops largely spontaneously.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of falling off, habit-building anxiety) is not reinforced, and breaking tasks down indirectly reduces overwhelm. But no double-sided reflections or strategic pivots are used, and the fear of falling off is never directly addressed.
