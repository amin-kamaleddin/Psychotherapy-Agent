# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard sustained throughout with no judgment or moralizing despite the high-stakes prenatal context. Minor imprecision: 'nobody dragged you here' slightly misattunes given the physician referral.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently reached meaning level, e.g., 'saying it out loud makes it real' and naming her internal shift about her mother's blessing. Counselor also heard implicit material like her half-stated anxiety about an imperfect weekend.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation was clearly focused on whether/how wine fits during pregnancy and moved purposefully toward a concrete plan. Client's change talk was consistently capitalized on and consolidated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Counselor explicitly positioned the client as the expert ('I won't prescribe anything') and framed every question around her terms and authority. Decisions, plans, and scripts were all generated by the client herself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was affirmed repeatedly ('this is yours,' 'your way, not mine'), and the client recognized this as why change felt possible. The counselor honored her right to decide and normalized imperfection without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently drew out the client's own motivations, plans, and coping strategies (recipe, Marco's support, her script for her mom). Change talk was elicited, reflected, and owned by the client rather than imposed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor operated as a curious partner, asking rather than advising and crediting all insights to the client. The tone of joint exploration ('What do you think would help...') was maintained throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk grew from tentative ambivalence to strong commitment language ('I decided,' 'my plan I actually believe in'), reinforced through selective reflection. Counselor skillfully reflected and amplified sustain-adjacent worries without triggering resistance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ambivalence was held with double-sided reflections rather than argued against, and the client's defensive stance dissolved naturally. Family resistance (mom's opinions, Marco's grumbling) was reframed as expected and manageable, not as barriers to argue away.
