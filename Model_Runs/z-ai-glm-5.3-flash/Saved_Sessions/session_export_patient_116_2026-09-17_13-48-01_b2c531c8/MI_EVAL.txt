# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, honoring Samir's humor and resistance without disapproval. Warmth sometimes feels formulaic and advice bundles occasionally come across as procedural rather than personally attentive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections ('grey, flat, and slower can feel awful') elicit Samir's most open disclosures, but they are brief and quickly buried under question batteries and advice lists. The poignant uncertainty 'is it the pills, or is it just me getting older?' was never deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays loosely tethered to adherence but wanders across side effects, safety checks, mood, and behavioral activation. Re-offering the declined script twice suggests persistence rather than clear direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and option-offering signal collaboration, but clinician-generated checklists and pre-drafted scripts dominate. Samir's request to 'just listen' indicates he experienced the session as overly solution-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A relative strength — repeated affirmations of choice ('It's your choice,' 'your choice is respected') clearly landed, with Samir noting 'someone actually not pushing me.' The repeated abrupt-stopping caveat edges toward pressure but doesn't undermine the overall support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The open question about why he skips doses was the best intervention, but most of the session supplies clinician-generated reasons and solutions rather than drawing out Samir's own motivations. The strongest change talk was never strategically evoked or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist functionally assumes the expert role — psychoeducation, drafting scripts, proposing strategies Samir never requested. Samir's lived experience and preference for being listened to were only incorporated late in the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine change talk emerged ('maybe talking to the doctor wouldn't kill me') but only spontaneously when pressure was removed. The psychiatrist failed to reinforce or deepen these statements with affirmations or further exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument, but the psychiatrist repeatedly re-engages it by restating medication risks and withdrawal warnings after pushback. No double-sided reflections were used to pivot toward change.
