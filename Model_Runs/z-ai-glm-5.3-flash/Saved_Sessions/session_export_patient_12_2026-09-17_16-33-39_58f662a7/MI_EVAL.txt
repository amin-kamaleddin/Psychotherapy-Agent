# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and non-judgmental affirmations of Amira's efforts ('reaching out shows real courage') without criticism of her drug use or flakiness. Falls short of top marks due to somewhat formulaic praise and limited explicit normalizing of her withdrawal as self-protection.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured her fear of rejection and flat affect ('an emoji is small but still risky if she ignores you'). Reflections often stayed surface-level, missing deeper exploration of her shame and hopelessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on concrete, achievable steps (message, walk, grounding, follow-up) with appropriate safety checks. Momentum toward the substance use itself was slightly diluted, as it was addressed only indirectly through isolation and coping.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were co-constructed ('We can draft together here with no pressure') with repeated input-seeking. However, the counselor supplied most option menus and techniques, leaving Amira in a selecting rather than co-generating role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: menus of options at every step, explicit permission to hold the message rather than send it, flexible walk timing, and no pressure. All commitments were self-generated by Amira.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully drew motivation from Amira herself—her message wording, walk plan, and her own recognition that things felt 'slightly less heavy.' Missed opportunities to ask 'What do you think might help?' kept the counselor in a proposing rather than evoking role at times.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Amira as the decision-maker ('What feels most important to you?'), and her choices directly shaped the session plan. Would benefit from more explicit acknowledgment of her expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforced Amira's evolving change talk, from choosing the one-line message to committing to the walk and grounding. Kept at 4 because the counselor sometimes generated options for her and didn't explore her own reasons or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Validated Amira's helplessness and doubt without amplifying them, gently pivoting toward tiny choices. Some reflections dwelt on the helplessness at length, and double-sided reflections weren't systematically used.
