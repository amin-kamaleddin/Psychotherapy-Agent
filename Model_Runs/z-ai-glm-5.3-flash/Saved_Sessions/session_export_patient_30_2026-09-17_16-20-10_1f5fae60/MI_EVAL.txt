# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated consistent unconditional positive regard; the guarded patient explicitly noted 'Nobody made me feel stupid about the muscle thing.' Concerns were validated as 'totally reasonable' with no judgment or pressure, and advice was offered only conditionally with permission-seeking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening tied the patient's health concerns to his core values (energy for work and family). Reflections occasionally pivoted too quickly to problem-solving, and deeper emotional nuances (fear, feeling dismissed by doctors, wife's pressure) could have been explored more.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on cholesterol medication adherence and lifestyle change from opening to concrete follow-through (booking appointment, Thursday 12:30 debrief). Patient tangents were skillfully folded back into the target behavior with structured next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The doctor question list, script, and follow-up logistics were all co-created and edited by the patient, who actively negotiated terms ('That fair enough for now?'). Genuine power-sharing was evident throughout the agenda and planning process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was pervasively supported ('Which option do you prefer?', offering multiple small steps). No pressure was applied; even medication acceptance emerged from the patient's own conditions rather than clinician persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within, with rulers and open questions eliciting the patient's own reasons and conditions for change. Every element of the final plan was articulated in the patient's own words.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient was explicitly positioned as expert and decision-maker throughout. The final plan was entirely his own construction, and his requests directly shaped session content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent use of readiness and confidence rulers ('What would make your confidence move from a 6 to a 7?') moved the patient from sustain talk to concrete commitments. Commitments ('I'll book the appointment this week') were consistently reinforced with summaries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about muscle weakness was acknowledged and validated without argument, then met with corrective information and effective pivots (if/then plans, confidence questions). A few exchanges risked dwelling on side-effect concerns before redirecting.
