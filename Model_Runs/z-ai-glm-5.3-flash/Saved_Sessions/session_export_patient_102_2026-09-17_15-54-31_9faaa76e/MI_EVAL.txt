# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Polite and nonjudgmental, with some acceptance of Milan's defensive stance, but affirmations are sparse and formulaic. Little deliberate normalization of his ambivalence or coerced attendance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate summaries and reflections of his parental-confession worry are present, but mostly at a surface level. Misses deeper feelings like his frustration about unfairness and nervousness about his father's reaction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Well-organized and purposeful, moving from agenda to concrete conversation planning and rehearsal. Weakness is that focus shifts from the drinking behavior to the collateral parental conflict, which is never revisited.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menus and Milan's feedback shape the final script, which shows some power-sharing. However, the counselor supplies nearly all content while Milan merely approves or adjusts, rather than co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice-offering throughout, and Milan's negotiated 1:30 curfew is accommodated. Could be stronger given his coerced attendance and mildly persistent pressure toward role-play.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Beyond one importance ruler and the opening question, motivation is assumed to come from the counselor's scripts rather than drawn from Milan. His own glimmers of ambivalence ('lying makes it worse') are never amplified, and values are unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menu options and preference questions convey some power-sharing, and Milan's ideas are incorporated. But the session becomes expert-driven skills coaching, framing the counselor as the authority on handling his dad.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** An importance ruler is asked but never followed up ('why a 2 and not a 0?'), and Milan's one moment of ambivalence is not deepened. Change talk is neither elicited nor reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I really don't think it's that bad') is met with neutral agreement and bypassed rather than strategically softened. No double-sided reflections or skilled redirection toward change; the session simply shifts away from drinking.
