# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with respectful affirmations that position the client as capable. However, some affirmations feel slightly formulaic or effusive (e.g., 'I love that...'), leaning toward praise rather than deeply individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captures the client's core ambivalence, with meaningful deeper reflections about guilt and all-or-nothing thinking. Falls short because deeper interpretations are rarely checked for accuracy and emotional territory like guilt is reflected but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on increasing physical activity with a clear progressive arc from ambivalence to concrete planning. Lacks explicit agenda-setting at the outset and periodic summaries at transitions to mark progress.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuine partnership of equals: the psychiatrist asks rather than tells, every substantive idea originates from the client, and follow-up is co-created rather than directed. No moment of expert authority asserted over the content of change.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly and constantly reinforced — the plan is entirely self-generated with no prescribed actions, and ownership is repeatedly affirmed ('this feels like mine'). Free of any pressure, persuasion, or controlling language; a model demonstration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key insights — 'something is better than nothing,' pride replacing guilt, writing it down 'makes it real' — are drawn from the client rather than supplied by the psychiatrist. The closing 'You got there yourself — I just held the space' embodies the evocative stance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is explicitly positioned as the expert ('You know your rhythms better than anyone'), and the entire plan originates from her. The psychiatrist builds reflections on the client's exact words, actively fostering power-sharing rather than merely permitting it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematic, skillful evocation of ability, commitment, and mobilizing change talk that lands every time, with the client's language escalating from 'maybe' to concrete plans. A strategic, client-owned, model performance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through redirection toward the client's own solutions and reframing ('honest is the real goal, not perfect') without arguing or amplifying doubts. Not a 5 because softening relies on redirection rather than explicit double-sided reflections, and some sustain talk (low energy) is never revisited.
