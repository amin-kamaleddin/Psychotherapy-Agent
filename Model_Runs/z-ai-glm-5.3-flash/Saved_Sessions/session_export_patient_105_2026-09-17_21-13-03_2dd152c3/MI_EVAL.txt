# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveyed warmth and unconditional positive regard, reassuring Naomi when she worried about being judged and reframing her choice to practice as 'real self-awareness, not weakness.' No judgment or disapproval appeared anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were highly accurate and emotionally attuned, notably linking her post-shoot despair to photography mattering deeply — which Naomi confirmed she had 'never really put into words before.' The double-sided reflection on her ambivalence was precise and validating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, progressive focus on managing social anxiety during photography work, moving from exploration to motivation to a concrete, rehearsed action step. There was no topic drift; every exchange served the change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned between equal partners — the counselor asked rather than told, checked in regularly, and offered suggestions as choices rather than instructions. Naomi's ideas visibly steered each next step of the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supported Naomi's ownership with choices like practicing in session versus at her next shoot, and never prescribed solutions. All decisions, including her request for a curveball, came from Naomi.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully drew motivation from within Naomi via open evocations like 'What makes even part of you want to try?', surfacing her own reasons of protecting the career she loves. Her growing engagement — asking for a second rehearsal when she 'usually looks for the door' — demonstrated successfully evoked intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly located expertise within the client ('You know your work better than anyone'), and every plan element — the script, 'small feels doable,' the rehearsal — originated from Naomi. His questions ensured her contributions substantially shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor evoked and deepened change talk throughout, eliciting 'trying feels slightly less scary than everything getting worse' and explicitly naming and amplifying her hope. Naomi's self-generated commitment language, including requesting a harder practice round, showed strong cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced and was well handled with double-sided reflections and skillful reframes (e.g., pounding heart as evidence of the skill, not failure). However, doubts like 'is that really going to help?' could have been met with a more explicit pivot back to her own stated reasons for change.
