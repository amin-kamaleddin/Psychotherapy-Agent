# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental communication with unconditional positive regard, directly countering the client's minimization without labeling ('You're not here because you're a drinker'). Affirmations are specific and individualized throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and frequent, including strong meaning-level reflections capturing the emotional core. However, some emotional nuance is underexplored — the fear of the police encounter, worry about his mother, and peer-pressure anxiety ('I don't wanna seem like a baby') go unvalidated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, progressive focus on the target behavior (avoiding alcohol to protect athletic goals), with each exchange advancing toward a concrete, rehearsed plan. Even the party-Saturday tangent becomes the focal behavioral application.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functions between equal partners — the psychiatrist offers options rather than directives and invites the client's evaluation at every step. Decisions are fully co-constructed, with the client's expertise treated as the operative knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced ('which of those feels most natural for you?') and the psychiatrist never prescribes. The client selects the strategy, edits his own refusal line, and generates his own reminder plan, leaving with full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client — the psychiatrist never argues for abstinence or lectures on risks. The client's self-motivators (scouts, scholarships, his mother, avoiding fear) are his own discoveries, evidenced by his closing statement of realization.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly locates expertise within the client ('That's your plan, in your words, and it came from you'). The client's contributions genuinely shape the session, and he leaves owning the entire strategy.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is evoked, reflected, and amplified consistently using open values questions, a confidence ruler, success visioning, and future-self projection. These techniques produced strong desire, need, ability, and commitment language from the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimization is met with double-sided reflections and nonjudgmental reframes rather than arguing, and sustain talk is redirected toward goals and choice. Minor issue: the reflection 'this isn't a regular habit for you' somewhat reinforces the minimization before pivoting.
