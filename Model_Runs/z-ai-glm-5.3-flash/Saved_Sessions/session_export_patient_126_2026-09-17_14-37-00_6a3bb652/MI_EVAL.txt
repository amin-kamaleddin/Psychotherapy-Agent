# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating Thomas's defensiveness rather than judging him. Falls short of 5 due to formulaic warmth and missing explicit affirmations of Thomas's strengths (e.g., never driving after drinking).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture factual content well (hangovers, family worry), but deeper emotional reflections are sparse. Does not explore the meaning of drinking as Thomas's 'one release' or the marital strain beneath his minimization, and reflections are often diluted by follow-up questions or advice menus.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains consistent focus on referral concerns (smoking, drinking) and progresses logically to a concrete behavioral plan with a scheduled check-in. Brief drifts into marital communication scripts slightly dilute the primary behavioral focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely works as equals—asking permission, offering menu-based choices, and co-constructing the plan Thomas shapes (script tone, tally, check-in terms). Some expert-directed advice blocks and underuse of Thomas's own social context expertise ('everyone's buying rounds') keep it from a 5.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly supports choice—offering opt-outs, honoring refusals of tracking, the app, water, and blunt scripts without pressure. Some advice delivery retains a prescriptive flavor and Thomas's ownership of outcomes is not explicitly emphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation is elicited (tally 'to prove the numbers,' two-week trial), but much relies on counselor-supplied reasons and solutions. Missing evocative questions connecting change to Thomas's own values, hopes, or meaning of drinking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Thomas's preferences genuinely drive the session—scripts, tone, and tracking method all reflect his choices, indicating real power sharing. Falls short of 5 because advice and information blocks are delivered in a somewhat expert-driven manner before exploring Thomas's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Progressively elicits meaningful commitments: 'I'll do the tally this week,' 'Book it in, sure,' and 'I'll do my two weeks properly.' Held at 4 because change talk is elicited mainly through menu options rather than deeper evocative questions about values and reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and reframes some of it ('not to call you an addict'), but allows substantial unchallenged minimization at session's end ('I'll prove I was right all along'). A double-sided reflection pairing his skepticism with his tally commitment was a missed opportunity.
