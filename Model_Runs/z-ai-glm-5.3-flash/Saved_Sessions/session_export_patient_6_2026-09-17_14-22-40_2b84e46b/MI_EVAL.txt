# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains basic respect and avoids judgment, with brief affirmations like acknowledging Alexei's honesty with the clinic. However, warmth is largely transactional and generic, with minimal explicit affirmation of his strengths, feelings, or initial defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate perspective-taking is shown early, but reflections are infrequent and shallow throughout. Missed opportunities to explore Alexei's rich emotional material, including fear of his mother, performance anxiety, and pride in soccer, limit empathic depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well-directed toward alcohol use and its impact on Alexei's performance goals, sleep, recovery, and scholarships, concluding with concrete next steps. Some initial focus is misplaced when the psychiatrist misreads the questionnaire-based screen as lab results.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers menus of options and asks for preferences, supporting some shared decision-making. However, plans are pre-packaged and delivered rather than co-constructed, with Alexei's input adjusting details rather than genuinely shaping content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices at several points and respects Alexei's refusals (e.g., not involving his mother or role-playing). However, directive elements like prescribing detailed plans and scripts without eliciting Alexei's own ideas press against deeper autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist relies heavily on providing information rather than eliciting Alexei's own motivations. The most valuable change talk emerged spontaneously from the patient, with no open questions, rulers, or value-based queries used to draw out motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist frequently assumes the expert role, dispensing rules, scripts, and plans with limited elicitation of Alexei's own ideas. Alexei's contributions only shape session details after options have already been proposed, resulting in limited power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Substantial change talk emerged (scholarship goals, fear of addiction, desire to play as long as possible) largely because alcohol was linked to performance goals. However, the psychiatrist does not actively reinforce, deepen, or reflect this change talk using evocative questions or strategic cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** When Alexei minimizes ('I barely drink,' 'this info isn't for me'), the psychiatrist responds with information and directives rather than double-sided reflections. The sustain talk is left unaddressed, missing opportunities to resolve ambivalence by linking Alexei's own values against his minimization.
