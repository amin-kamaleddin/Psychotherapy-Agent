# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental stance, treating Daniela's fear as understandable and offering genuine, individualized affirmations. Positive regard remains unconditional even while the client describes months of avoiding disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex, double-sided reflections accurately capture the client's ambivalence and deeper emotional meaning, repeatedly landing as evidenced by her responses. Reflections are occasionally long and lightly interpretive but remain client-anchored and precise.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains unwavering focus on disclosure to friends, moving progressively from safety conditions through to commitment and a concrete plan. There is no drift, and even emotion-focused reflections tie directly to the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is fully egalitarian, with co-exploratory questions that present the client's ideas as options rather than prescriptions. The final plan is co-constructed from her own stated preferences.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces client ownership ('on your own terms,' 'a completely yours kind of victory') without imposing advice. When the client expresses uncertainty, the clinician does not push, allowing commitment to emerge naturally.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits the client's own motivations and strategies through open questions rather than providing reasons for change. Change talk escalates naturally from desire to commitment language, demonstrating masterful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Daniela is explicitly positioned as the expert, and her contributions substantially shape the session direction. Each next question builds directly from the client's own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoke and deepen strong client-generated change talk, which is then reinforced and deepened. The session culminates in the client initiating rehearsal herself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged, validated, and gently pivoted toward change through double-sided reflections. Notably, the clinician highlights the client's own choice not to plan a fallback, shifting energy toward commitment.
