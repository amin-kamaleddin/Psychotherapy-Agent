# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental, normalizes struggles including incarceration and literacy issues without any labeling. Affirmations remain somewhat generic rather than individualized to Lars's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes adequate but brief reflections that often pivot quickly to problem-solving. Misses the emotional core Lars raises — guilt about Fridays, fear of being a 'lost cause,' wanting to be a different father.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains consistent focus on reducing reoffending through employment/education with clear progression from goals to barriers to rehearsed action. Focus narrows too much to forms and logistics rather than deeper risk drivers like Friday drinking and mates.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative texture with role-play and co-created plans; Lars's preferences shape the plan. Idea generation is asymmetric — nearly every strategy originates from the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choice and avoids coercion, letting Lars select options and actions throughout. Choices are offered within counselor-constructed menus, with little reinforcement of Lars's ownership of the change itself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The 'why now' role-play elicited a genuine self-motivating statement, but evocation is superficial and inconsistent. The counselor supplies motivation through advice and scripts rather than drawing out Lars's values and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally positions Lars as the expert and his choices shape the plan, but the counselor largely occupies the expert role. Lars selects from the counselor's menu rather than generating his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicited change talk, including strong self-motivating statements via the 'why now' role-play. Does not reinforce or deepen it — Lars's 'felt weirdly good, like proof I mean it' goes unaffirmed and unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk (pride, boredom, embarrassment) with validating but neutral reflections. No active amplification of arguments against the status quo, and the pivot to change is driven by the counselor's agenda rather than strategic softening.
