### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently validates Jordan's pessimism and sustain talk without judgment, using affirmations like 'that takes something.' Warm, unconditional positive regard is maintained throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening captures both content and feeling, including the double-sided reflection balancing skepticism with what the call does provide. Understanding remains precise and emotionally attuned.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session moves strategically from hopelessness exploration to envisioning, concrete action, and barrier planning. Slightly patient-paced early on, with exploration preceding target crystallization.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with inclusive language ('Maybe together we can spot it coming'), though collaboration remains somewhat asymmetric, with fewer moments of co-constructing ideas.

### Autonomy Support

**Comments:** Every forward step originates from Jordan, with the clinician only asking what he thinks would help. The confidence rating is accepted without pressure, and the plan remains entirely his own.

### Evocation

**Comments:** The standout strength: questions elicit desire, ability, envisioning, and confidence at every turn. Jordan's motivation visibly grows from 'what's the point' to a concrete plan.

### Partnership

**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located within Jordan ('You'd know better than anyone'), and key ideas—the sister calls, listings plan, 7 a.m. insight—all come from the client. Active power-sharing at a high level.

**Comments:** Strategic highlighting of Jordan's own change language, evocative questions, and a confidence ruler draw out increasingly strong change talk ('I could look at listings tonight'). Each expression is reinforced and built upon.
