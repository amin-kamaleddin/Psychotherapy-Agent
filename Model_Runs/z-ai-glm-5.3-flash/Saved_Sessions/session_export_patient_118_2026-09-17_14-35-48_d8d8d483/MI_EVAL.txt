# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, honoring the promise not to lecture despite the client testing him. However, warmth felt formulaic with repeated scripted safety disclaimers rather than individualized attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ('being lectured is exhausting') were present, but responses were largely practical checklists. Missed a key moment to reflect the emotional weight of the client's failed medication attempt and family pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately followed the client's dental agenda, but as smoking-cessation MI, the target behavior was never explored. When the client opened the door ('the fags aren't doing me any favors'), no gentle guidance toward change followed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction was largely unilateral, with the counselor delivering plans, scripts, and tip lists with minimal back-and-forth. The client's own ideas and preferences were never solicited or co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's clearest strength: explicit, repeated support of client choice ('on your terms') with no persuasion despite natural openings. The client explicitly valued this approach, which directly built trust.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no evocation occurred—no open questions about the client's motivations, values, or confidence. The counselor supplied external content (scripts, checklists) rather than drawing motivation from within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor acted as an advice-giver, handing over a prepared package while the client passively received it. There was no acknowledgment that the client knows best what would work for him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client spontaneously offered significant change talk ('the fags aren't doing me any favors,' 'I'd have to really want it,' willingness to return), but none of it was reflected, deepened, or reinforced. These opportunities died on the table.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing or reinforcing sustain talk by not raising smoking at all, but sidestepping is not softening. No double-sided reflections or reframing of the failed medication trial as useful information were offered.
