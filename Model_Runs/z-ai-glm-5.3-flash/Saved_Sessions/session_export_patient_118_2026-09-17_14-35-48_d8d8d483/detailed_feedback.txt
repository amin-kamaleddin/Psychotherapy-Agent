                                Motivational Interviewing Feedback Criteria (5-Point Likert Scale)

Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)
------------------------------------
1. Acceptance: Extent to which the counselor communicates unconditional positive regard for the client.
   1 = Judgmental, disapproving, labeling
   2 = Inconsistent respect, occasional judgment
   3 = Neutral stance, basic respect
   4 = Generally respectful and supportive
   5 = Consistently respectful, warm, supportive

2. Empathy: Extent to which the counselor understands and/or makes an effort to accurately understand the client’s perspective.
   1 = Little effort to understand, minimal reflective listening
   2 = Limited perspective-taking, mostly factual focus
   3 = Adequate but inconsistent effort to understand
   4 = Good reflective listening and perspective-taking
   5 = Highly accurate, consistent understanding and reflections

3. Direction – Degree to which the counselor maintains appropriate focus on a specific target behavior or concerns directly tied to it.
1 = No clear focus; session wanders aimlessly
2 = Minimal focus; frequent topic drift
3 = Occasional focus on target behavior but inconsistent
4 = Generally keeps discussion directed toward target behavior
5 = Consistently maintains clear focus and guides toward change

---

4. Collaboration – Extent to which the clinician behaves as if the interview is occurring between two equal partners, both of whom have knowledge that might be useful in the problem under consideration.
1 = Authoritarian or expert-driven stance
2 = Limited collaboration; frequent direction by counselor
3 = Partial collaboration; uneven power-sharing
4 = Strong collaborative stance most of the time
5 = Fully collaborative; client’s ideas shape session direction

---

5. Autonomy Support – Extent to which clinician supports and actively fosters client perception of choice as opposed to attempting to control the client’s behavior or choices.
1 = Directive or pressuring; minimizes choice
2 = Occasionally acknowledges choice but inconsistently
3 = Neutral regarding client autonomy
4 = Regularly affirms client freedom to choose
5 = Consistently reinforces client ownership of change process

---

6. Evocation – Extent to which the clinician conveys an understanding that motivation for change, and the ability to move toward that change, reside mostly within the client and therefore focuses efforts to elicit and expand it within the therapeutic interaction.
1 = Provides reasons for change without exploring client view
2 = Limited interest in client motivations
3 = Some evocation but superficial
4 = Frequently draws out client’s own reasons for change
5 = Consistently elicits and develops client change talk throughout session

---

7. Partnership – Extent to which the clinician conveys an understanding that expertise and wisdom about change reside mostly within the client

1 = Clinician actively assumes the expert role for the majority of the interaction with the client. Collaboration or partnership is absent.
2 = Clinician superficially responds to opportunities to collaborate.
3 = Clinician incorporates client’s contributions but does so in a lukewarm or erratic fashion.
4 = Clinician fosters collaboration and power sharing so that client’s contributions impact the session in ways that they otherwise would not.
5 = Clinician actively fosters and encourages power sharing in the interaction in such a way that client’s contributions substantially influence the nature of the session.

---

8. Cultivating Change Talk – Extent to which the clinician actively encourages the client’s own language in favor of the change goal, and confidence for making that change.
1 = No effort to evoke change talk
2 = Rare or weak efforts
3 = Occasional evocation of change language
4 = Frequent evocation of change talk
5 = Consistent, skillful evocation throughout

---

9. Softening Sustain Talk – Extent that the clinician avoids a focus on the reasons against changing or for maintaining the status quo.
1 = Reinforces sustain talk
2 = Rarely shifts focus away from sustain talk
3 = Occasionally redirects toward change
4 = Often redirects effectively
5 = Consistently pivots client language toward change

- All domains are rated on a 1–5 Likert scale.
- Ratings should reflect overall quality and consistency of behavior.
- Focus on both counselor skillfulness and client speech as indicators of MI process quality.

 # Motivational Interviewing Feedback: Psychiatrist Evaluation

## Global Ratings (Counselor Behaviors)

### 1. Acceptance
Score: 4/5

Explanation:
The counselor consistently communicated respect and nonjudgment. He explicitly promised 'I won't bring up smoking unless you want to' and honored it despite Seamus testing him directly ('you're not going to slip in the lecture at the end, are you?'). Affirmations like 'you did well prioritizing what matters to you' and 'no lectures' convey unconditional regard. However, the warmth is formulaic, with repeated boilerplate crisis disclaimers ('If you're feeling unsafe...') that feel scripted rather than personally attuned, and the counselor never explicitly normalized Seamus's ambivalence or frustration in a warm, individualized way.

---

### 2. Empathy
Score: 3/5

Explanation:
There are some accurate reflections ('being lectured is exhausting') and the counselor acknowledged Seamus's frustration with repeated provider lectures. However, most responses are practical checklists rather than reflective listening. When Seamus shared meaningful vulnerability ('I tried the pills once and they made me feel worse than the smoking ever did. So I'd have to really want it, and I'm not there yet'), the counselor responded only with 'It's normal to feel ambivalent about quitting smoking' and immediately pivoted to logistics — a significant missed opportunity to reflect the emotional weight of failed attempts, side effects, and his wife's pressure.

### 3. Cultivating Change Talk
Score: 2/5

Explanation:
Seamus spontaneously offered notable change talk: 'I know the fags aren't doing me any favors, with the gums and all,' 'I'd have to really want it,' and 'your way of doing things — not nagging — I wouldn't mind coming back to that. Maybe.' The counselor did not reflect, deepen, or reinforce any of these statements. He never asked 'What would make you really want it?' or explored what coming back might look like. The change talk was left on the table and died there, representing a major missed MI opportunity.

### 4. Softening Sustain Talk
Score: 3/5

Explanation:
The counselor largely avoided engaging with sustain talk by not raising smoking at all, which prevented reinforcing arguments against change. He avoided arguing or persuading. However, avoiding the topic entirely is not the same as skillfully softening it; there were no double-sided reflections, no reframing of Seamus's failed medication trial as useful information, and no strategic pivots when he voiced reasons against change. The sustain talk was sidestepped rather than softened.

---

### 5. Partnership
Score: 2/5

Explanation:
The counselor positioned himself as an advice-giver rather than drawing out Seamus's own expertise. The bulk of the session consisted of the counselor handing over scripts, question lists, and coping tips ('bring music/headphones, ask for breaks, use a hand signal'). Seamus's contributions shaped little of the content; he passively received a prepared package. There was no acknowledgment that Seamus knows best what would work for him in the dental chair or what his own ideas might be for managing the visit.

### 6. Direction
Score: 3/5

Explanation:
The counselor appropriately followed the client's stated agenda (dental concerns) and kept focus there consistently, honoring 'I won't bring up smoking unless you want to.' However, as a smoking-cessation MI evaluation, the target behavior was never explored at all; when Seamus opened the door with 'I know the fags aren't doing me any favors,' the counselor did not guide toward change or even gently explore it. ### 8. Collaboration

The interaction was largely unilateral: the counselor delivered plans, scripts, and tip lists with minimal back-and-forth ('Sounds good — here's a short practical plan,' 'Want a one-sentence script?'). ### 9. Evocation
Score: 1/5

Explanation:
There was virtually no evocation. The counselor never asked open questions about Seamus's motivations, values, confidence, or reasons for considering change. When Seamus offered his own motivation ('I'd have to really want it'), the counselor did not explore it. ---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **2.8/5**

This session succeeded as rapport-building and autonomy support: the counselor honored his promise not to lecture, which earned genuine trust from a resistant client ('Fair play to you... more than I expected'). The autonomy support (5) and acceptance (4) are commendable, and following the client's dental agenda demonstrated respect. However, the session was practically a transactional information-delivery exchange with weak empathy depth, no evocation, minimal collaboration, and no partnership. Most critically, when Seamus spontaneously volunteered significant change talk ('I know the fags aren't doing me any favors,' 'I'd have to really want it,' willingness to return), the counselor let every opportunity pass unexplored. Key improvements: (1) reflect and deepen client change talk when it emerges; (2) replace checklists with open questions and reflective listening; (3) solicit the client's own ideas and expertise rather than prescribing solutions; (4) use the hard-won trust ('I wouldn't mind coming back to that') to gently invite exploration of ambivalence at the client's pace rather than avoiding the target behavior entirely. Also, the repeated verbatim safety disclaimers and four-fold repetition of the same script list felt robotic and eroded the human connection; tailor closing statements instead.
