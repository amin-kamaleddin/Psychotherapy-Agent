# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer affirms the client repeatedly and unconditionally, highlighting strengths like maturity and self-knowledge (e.g., 'I hear a lot of maturity in what you just said'). Affirmations feel genuine and specific rather than generic praise.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture the emotional core of the client's statements, including the frustration of being misperceived and the painful double-bind with his mother. The client visibly responds with deeper disclosure after each accurate reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer skillfully focuses on change-relevant material (trust, mother's fears, small steps forward) without confronting the substance use directly or prematurely. Each transition builds toward the client generating his own plan for repairing the relationship.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is highly collaborative, with the interviewer positioning the client as the expert ('You know her better than anyone') and co-constructing direction through questions rather than advice. There is no expert-confrontational stance anywhere in the exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported throughout: the client chooses what to share, at what pace, and with whom ('easing in... tell her some stuff, not everything at once'). The interviewer explicitly frames decisions as the client's own ('for you, not just for her').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer consistently draws out the client's own motivations and solutions, eliciting his reasons to rebuild trust and his concrete ideas for the conversation with his mother. Change talk emerges naturally from open questions rather than being imposed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dyadic partnership is evident in shared decision-making about what to disclose to the mother and the joint exploration of risks and strategies. The client actively problem-solves and even self-discloses fears, indicating a strong working alliance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer uses targeted questions that reliably evoke change talk (e.g., 'what would it take, for you, to feel trusted?'), producing strong desire, ability, and commitment statements. The client articulates specific intentions and a plan by the end of the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (denial of addiction, defending friends, resistance to disclosure) is met with reflections and reframes rather than argument. The interviewer takes the client at his word and redirects sustain talk into exploration of values and relationship goals.
