# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm and nonjudgmental, normalizing her experience and handling safety screening respectfully. However, warmth is brief and formulaic, with few individualized affirmations of her strengths as a mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections of exhaustion and feeling judged, but reflective listening is sparse relative to the volume of advice. Deeper feelings of self-doubt, fear of failure, and hurt from comparisons are left unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on sleep deprivation, overnight logistics, and family support, ending with concrete follow-up steps. The emotional dimension — maternal self-doubt and reported crying spells — is noted but never followed up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration exists, as Nadine chooses between options and shapes the plan. However, the counselor behaves largely as a consultant dispensing expert solutions, with rapid-fire advice blocks making her own problem-solving secondary.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers options rather than directives, and Nadine makes all implementation decisions. Many offerings, however, come pre-packaged with prescriptive detail and advice is sometimes front-loaded before she's asked for it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies heavily on providing information, scripts, and plans rather than eliciting Nadine's own motivations and ideas. Her best solutions (e.g., her mom taking the baby for a walk) emerged despite, not because of, the counselor's technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites input with questions like "Which option feels doable?" but the pattern is predominantly advice-dispensing. Her expertise is incorporated only after she volunteers it, not actively drawn out.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nadine generated genuine change talk and the counselor capitalized somewhat by offering a plan. It is rarely deepened with reflections, evocative questions, or confidence scaling — notably missing her insight that she "just hasn't let him" help.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about failing and nervousness is left unsoftened — no double-sided reflections or affirmations of her capability. The counselor jumps straight to logistics, leaving ambivalence unresolved.
