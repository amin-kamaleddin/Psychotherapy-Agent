# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout; sustain talk was legitimized rather than challenged, and affirmations were personalized to Jordan's actual values (his health identity, Sundays, grandparents). The patient explicitly confirmed feeling accepted ('thanks for not just lecturing me').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and operated at the level of meaning, including a double-sided reflection that captured both his health pride and his care for his grandparents. Minor growth edge: deeper exploration of the needle aversion's emotional weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear guiding direction toward exploring vaccination while following the patient's lead, never pushing or dragging. Each pivot (decisional balance, evocative question, planning) advanced the conversation purposefully.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Jordan as the expert and decision-maker ('you're the expert on your own life,' 'that's yours to own'). Every plan step was framed as the patient's own choice rather than a recommendation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Emphasized ownership at every turn ('that's you weighing it out, not me'), offered options rather than directives, and linked the plan to the patient's own values. The patient's spontaneous commitment ('doc's getting a text tonight') demonstrates effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong use of decisional balance to elicit Jordan's own reasons, then strategic deepening of change-side content and a pivotal evocative question that produced commitment language. Consolidation questions ('what shifted for you today?') generated insight and further commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power stayed with the patient throughout; the clinician's reflections, affirmations, and questions were invitations rather than steering maneuvers. Jordan's increasing openness and humor reflect a strong working alliance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook escalation of commitment language across the session: 'maybe' → 'I should get it for them' → 'I'll call tomorrow' → 'doc's getting a text tonight.' The clinician skillfully reseeded sustain talk ('not the worst bonus') into change talk and closed the loop with planning and consolidation questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently met with validation and reframed into ambivalence rather than confronted ('that's fair — you've built a healthy life and that's yours to own'). No arguing, no correction — sustain talk faded naturally as the patient resolved his own ambivalence.
