# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a nonjudgmental, respectful stance despite Jamal's dismissiveness, with positive regard communicated from the start. Deduction for somewhat formulaic warmth and few individualized affirmations of Jamal's own strengths beyond compliance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeated reflections acknowledged Jamal's perspective but stayed summary-level and slid back into agenda-driven questions rather than deepening understanding. Emotional nuance was rarely explored, though moments like the 'not thirsty' concern showed some attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened with good directional questions about marijuana's fit with Jamal's goals, but the session drifted into administrative problem-solving and never returned to the target behavior. The session ended without revisiting marijuana use at all.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing demonstrated as Jamal's ideas directly shaped the plan, including the probation-call script. Deduction because collaboration concentrated on logistics rather than collaborative exploration of the substance use issue itself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: consistent genuine choices, permission-seeking before actions, respect for Jamal's preferences, and no pressure, persuasion, or coercion anywhere in the transcript.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions successfully elicited Jamal's motivation around shortening probation and taking sessions seriously. However, when the key change talk surfaced, the psychiatrist summarized and moved on rather than developing it, and never probed Jamal's values or concerns about marijuana.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Jamal as the decision-maker through menu options, permission requests, and co-creating the call script from his own questions. Deduction because practical logistics dominated over exploring Jamal's own expertise about his use and goals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged organically around getting off probation early, but the psychiatrist never reinforced, reflected, or deepened these statements. No commitment or confidence language was evoked regarding substance use itself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Neutral summaries acknowledged Jamal's minimization and peer normalization without strategically working with them. No arguing occurred (good), but no double-sided reflections or active redirection toward change either.
