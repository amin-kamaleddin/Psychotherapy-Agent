# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard through validations like 'that's understandable' and affirmations such as 'that's a solid, honest plan.' Never judged the client's fears, reliance on his wife, or skepticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, accurately capturing core ambivalence ('You're torn — you loved the music and want to try, yet worry it's not real'). Reflections occasionally paraphrased rather than deepened into the emotional layers of fear of failing and feeling 'silly.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on the target behavior, moving every exchange toward a concrete plan with day, time, duration, movements, and safety rules. Session closed with clear follow-up and contingently scaled next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely joint session with menu options, iterative checklist co-editing, and active incorporation of client ideas. However, the counselor front-loaded solutions and detailed prescriptions before the client articulated his own.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced autonomy ('I'll support whichever you choose') and explicitly gave permission to change his mind or report failure honestly. No pressure or persuasion; the client owned every element of the final plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within, eliciting the client's value of being there for his wife and progressive hope. Mid-session, relied more on persuasion and reassurance than continued evocation, missing chances to ask what success would mean to him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as decision-maker, and his contributions (backup lines, wife joining) materially shaped the final checklist. The counselor still supplied most concrete content, keeping an expert hand on the plan's mechanics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk with questions like 'What matters most to you?' and built the plan around the client's own choices, strengthening his commitment language. Some momentum was counselor-supplied, and change talk wasn't always deepened with follow-up about confidence or meaning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying sustain talk, instead reframing with 'Ten minutes of music... absolutely counts' and framing safety rules as kindness. Some sustain talk was met with reassurance rather than exploration or double-sided reflection, risking dismissal of the underlying doubt.
