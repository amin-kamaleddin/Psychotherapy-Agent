# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard is maintained throughout, with past failures reframed as useful wisdom rather than judged. Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental from open to close.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing ambivalence precisely and naming emotion beneath content. Each response builds directly on Marcus's exact words.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on physical activity and sustainable change with zero topic drift. Each turn advances toward concrete, action-oriented commitments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is fully co-constructed, with open questions inviting Marcus's ideas to shape every turn. Decisions emerge jointly, with his contributions determining content and direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership are repeatedly reinforced ('You get to choose the pace,' 'on your terms, not the scale's'). No advice is prescribed; the change process is consistently attributed to Marcus.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is systematically drawn from the client rather than supplied, producing genuine insights, confidence, and self-defined success. Evocative questions generate sustained change talk from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client ('You're the expert on your own life'; 'You wrote that, not me'). Every plan element originates from Marcus, powerfully fostering power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and amplified at every opportunity, with questions consistently eliciting commitment language. The counselor reinforces self-generated commitments immediately and warmly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and met with effective double-sided reflections and reframing. The score is 4 rather than 5 because early exploration of past failures briefly dwelt on reasons against change before pivoting.
