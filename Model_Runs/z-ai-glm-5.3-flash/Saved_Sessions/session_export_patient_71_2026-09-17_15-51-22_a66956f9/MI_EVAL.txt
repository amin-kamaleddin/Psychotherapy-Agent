# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with genuine affirmations of Laura's self-awareness and realistic hope. Affirmations lean competence-focused and slightly formulaic, limiting individualized depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture the value-behavior gap and 'just one night' pattern, often consolidating multiple statements. Reflections stay summary-level rather than emotionally deep, and Laura's initial guilt is only implicitly acknowledged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus moving from values to a concrete if-then plan, confidence rating, environmental cue, and scheduled check-in. Multi-question turns and brief logistical drift slightly diffuse focus at times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is substantially co-constructed, with Laura generating the cue, self-prompt, and check-in format. The counselor's habit of offering pre-packaged option menus and scripts creates a mildly expert-driven flavor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of Laura's ownership—she selects the plan, cue, prompt phrasing, and check-in structure, and reports accountability 'without feeling like pressure.' No directive or pressuring language appears anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions successfully elicit Laura's core reasons for health, her confidence, commitment, and hope. Some motivational content (reframes like 'one night, not a failure') is supplied rather than evoked, and the entry guilt goes unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura's expertise is repeatedly deferred to and her input genuinely shaped the plan. However, reliance on pre-formatted option lists and mini-scripts positions the counselor as idea-supplier rather than fully equal partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocation of change talk via values exploration, a confidence ruler, and invitation to choose a plan; Laura voices commitment language and generates her own solutions. Could deepen further by exploring her 'small, but hopeful' statement more explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mild sustain talk ('just one night,' 'five minutes is pointless') is reframed with the all-or-nothing dynamic named collaboratively. No double-sided reflections are used, and some doubt moments go without skilled redirection toward change.
