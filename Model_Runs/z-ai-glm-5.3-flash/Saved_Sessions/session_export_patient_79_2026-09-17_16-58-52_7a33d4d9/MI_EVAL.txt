# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental even when the client was sarcastic, with no lecturing or labeling. Affirmations were sparse and formulaic—missed chances to affirm Luis's self-management efforts like reading about asthma.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Luis's perspective and frustration, but many were restatements of the plan rather than deep emotional reflections. The counselor missed exploring the fear Luis admitted ('Scared me a little') and grew repetitive in closing turns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on asthma self-management, moving from values to barriers to a concrete behavioral plan plus safety signals. Focus never drifted, though the mechanical repetition of plan summaries in the closing turns felt rigid.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-created from Luis's own routines and constraints, with permission-seeking before suggestions. Repeated re-offers of drafting the text after Luis declined created occasional one-sidedness.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength—choices were consistently framed as Luis's own ('It's your call'), advice was permission-based, and refusals (alarms, question lists) were accepted without pressure. The final plan explicitly reinforced his ownership ('That keeps you in control').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong early evocation drew out authentic change talk and motivations, but evocation was front-loaded—after the plan emerged, the counselor shifted to advising and summarizing. Missed opportunities to deepen his 'maybe I'll tell the doctor I did' into stronger commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Luis's expertise and choices were repeatedly honored, with ideas offered conditionally and the plan built from his routines. However, re-offering to draft the text multiple times after explicit refusal mildly undermined trust in his competence.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective open questions ('What would managing your asthma better let you do?') drew out genuine change talk, a 6/10 readiness rating, and plan talk. Missed opportunities to deepen change talk when it surfaced, e.g., after Luis's surprised optimism about the coffee plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally then redirected forward, and Luis's resistance ('no promises,' 'not coming back every month') was not reinforced. Some sustain talk was re-quoted repeatedly in summaries, keeping it slightly in focus.
