# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'You sound ready and more confident — that's huge.' However, affirmations are somewhat generic and ambivalence is never explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate content reflections early on, but emotional reflections are thin. Tyler's fears (DUI 'wrecking everything,' roommate teasing) are met with advice rather than emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use and the conduct referral throughout, building toward a concrete harm-reduction plan. The caveat is that direction rushes past exploring ambivalence into action planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission and offers choices, giving surface-level collaboration. However, the counselor generates nearly all ideas, plans, scripts, and drafts while Tyler mainly selects and tweaks.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine autonomy support (permission-seeking, Tyler choosing options, tweaking the draft text). But heavy scaffolding and directive questions create pressure toward a counselor-defined plan, and 'I'm not promising anything' goes unaddressed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and barrier questions elicited Tyler's own change talk ('School matters more than a little teasing'). But much content is supplied rather than evoked, and deeper motivations are never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor rapidly assumes the expert role, supplying nearly all options, scripts, drafts, and step-by-step instructions. Tyler's choices are invited within counselor-built structures rather than drawing out his own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of importance/confidence scaling moved Tyler from 5 to 8–9 confidence, and planning questions elicited commitment language. However, change talk is technique-driven rather than drawn from Tyler's deeper motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('everyone does it,' 'it's just college') appears repeatedly and is mostly left unaddressed rather than softened with double-sided reflections. The counselor avoids arguing and reframes occasionally, but ambivalence is largely bypassed.
