# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard through frequent, genuine affirmations and nonjudgmental acceptance of partial change and reluctance toward abstinence. Client directly validated this: 'thanks for not lecturing me about the beers — feels like you actually get that I'm trying, not failing.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening captured both content and emotional meaning, e.g., reflecting that beer fills a gap on empty days. Reflections consistently mirrored Luka's own voice and ambivalence without distortion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained unwavering focus on reducing alcohol-related risk, progressing from current changes to triggers to a concrete actionable plan. No topic drift; even casual topics were connected back to the drinking pattern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative: 'What if we looked at that gap together?' with the plan entirely authored by the client (fishing, texting his sister). The counselor's role was limited to reflecting, organizing, and affirming rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership was consistently reinforced, with ideas offered only as questions and no imposed advice. The counselor accepted Luka's boundary of continued moderate drinking without pressuring toward abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from Luka's own values — peace at home, being an uncle figure, and his father's story — rather than supplied by the counselor. His self-generated conclusion that change is 'worth a bit of effort' is the hallmark of effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Luka was explicitly positioned as the expert ('You're the expert on what's actually workable for you'), and all solutions originated from him. Follow-up questions ensured his contributions shaped the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was evoked systematically via open questions targeting desire, ability, and commitment, then deepened and amplified ('That's not just talk, that's a plan'). Self-motivating statements about family patterns emerged from skillful questioning about the future.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled skillfully — reflected neutrally and pivoted to strengths or solution-focused exploration without reinforcement. A minor deduction because sustain talk appears in nearly every turn, though redirection was always immediate.
