# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, affirming Liora without judgment about self-harm and screening safety matter-of-factly. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and repeated ('Still feeling okay?'), lacking depth of individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate reflections (e.g., about exhaustion and the pressure cutting quiets), but they are limited and quickly followed by task-focused prompts. Missed opportunities to validate her hopelessness, shame about roommates, and ambivalence beneath her flat, brief answers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior and safety throughout—risk assessment, warning signs, coping, means restriction, and a concrete plan with consolidating summaries. No drift off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed—Liora picks ice, names Dana, sets her own time, and declines elements she isn't ready for, with the psychiatrist adapting. Slightly limited because the agenda and safety-plan structure are clinician-defined, and her passivity is accommodated rather than rebalanced.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly asks permission, offers options, and respects boundaries ('not ready to have anyone hold them,' 'not yet' on the roommate script). Falls short because scaffolding is heavily clinician-proposed, with less explicit reinforcement that Liora owns the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Draws out meaningful change talk ('I don't want to keep going like this either') and self-generated commitments, but mostly through concrete task elicitation. Little exploration of her own reasons, values, or hopes for change beyond plan compliance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking supports partnership, but the session becomes increasingly directive with verbatim scripts, prescribed steps, and a clinician-structured plan. Liora's contributions are incorporated, yet the expertise framing tilts toward the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits commitment and action language ('I'll move the tools tonight,' 'I'll send Dana the text,' 'I did more today than I thought I would') from a precontemplative client. Thinner evocation of desire/ability talk and exploration of her own reasons or hope for the future.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('cutting is the only thing that helps,' 'I don't know if any of this will work') is acknowledged but not actively softened or reframed. Responses pivot quickly to logistics rather than using double-sided reflections that honor both sides of her ambivalence.
