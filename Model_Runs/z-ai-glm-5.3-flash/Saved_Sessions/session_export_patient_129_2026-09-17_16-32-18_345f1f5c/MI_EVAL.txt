# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Tyler's frustration and his compromise idea. Warmth occasionally stayed at pragmatic acknowledgment rather than deeper individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captured Tyler's perspective, and role-play his mother with accurate emotional depth ('weirdly accurate'). Reflections sometimes stayed at summary level rather than deepening vulnerable emotional moments like his reaction to 'I want you alive'.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus throughout, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete one-month written agreement with specific terms, role-play rehearsal, and commitment to sign. Each exchange moved toward a tangible change step without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine partnership in co-constructing the agreement term by term with Tyler's edits incorporated, and collaborative role-play. The counselor sometimes drove the structure with numbered options and pre-drafted scripts rather than letting the plan fully emerge from Tyler.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent strength: explicitly stated 'You're in charge of this,' offered menus of options, checked willingness repeatedly, and negotiated rather than pressured when Tyler objected. Tyler's closing commitment reflects genuine self-selected choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Conveyed that motivation resides in Tyler and elicited the compromise from his agenda choice, but intrinsic motivation was rarely probed — no values exploration, rulers, or questions about Tyler's own reasons beyond the mother conflict. Relied more on negotiation structure than drawing out the client's own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Tyler as decision-maker, and his contributions genuinely changed the plan (passenger clause, sober-ride provision). Sometimes provided fully formed options and scripts rather than drawing the plan primarily from Tyler's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evoked some change-relevant language and Tyler reached emerging commitment ('worth a month'), but change talk was never deliberately deepened with evocative questions, confidence rulers, or reaffirmation. Momentum came largely from practical negotiation rather than cultivated intrinsic motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided reinforcing sustain talk, acknowledging it neutrally then pivoting to solutions, and rolled with resistance rather than arguing. Redirections were somewhat transactional — double-sided reflections to keep ambivalence visible were not used, and some sustain talk was accommodated into the agreement.
