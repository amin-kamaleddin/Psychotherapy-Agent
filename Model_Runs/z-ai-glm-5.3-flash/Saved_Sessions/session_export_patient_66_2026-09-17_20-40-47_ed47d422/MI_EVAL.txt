# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard was maintained throughout ('However you feel about it right now, that's okay'), including explicit acceptance of the client's slip concerns. No judgment was expressed even when the client opened with 'I'm not gonna quit smoking.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, emotionally attuned reflections captured the client's experience, including the double-sided reflection balancing enjoyment of smoking with emerging change readiness. Naming the underlying fear about sustaining the change demonstrated deep understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation from start to finish, progressing logically from exploring ambivalence to a concrete, dated plan. Every question and reflection tied back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully egalitarian, with the client generating his own swap strategies, timeline, and motivation while the counselor remained facilitative rather than directive. The check-in was negotiated jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly reinforced ('the choice is still yours after all,' 'No pressure either way'), and permission was asked before proposing a follow-up. The pace and plan were framed entirely as the client's to determine.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently elicited from within, drawing out powerful intrinsic motivators (his father's death, his daughter Layla). The client explicitly credited the non-lecturing approach: 'Thanks for not just lecturing me.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned the client as the expert ('You know your own patterns best'), and the session's direction was visibly shaped by the client's own contributions. The coffee swap, eased timeline, and family-based motivation all came from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation of progressively stronger change talk through strategic questions, moving the client from 'I'm not gonna quit' to 'I can quit. I actually think I mean that now.' Each instance of change language was amplified and reflected skillfully.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally acknowledged without reinforcement, with effective double-sided reflections pivoting toward change. Early reflections occasionally restated sustain content at length, which slightly amplified focus on reasons against change before redirecting.
