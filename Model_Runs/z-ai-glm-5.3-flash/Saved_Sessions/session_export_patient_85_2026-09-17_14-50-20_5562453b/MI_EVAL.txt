# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, even when Marco discusses relapse and juice habits. However, affirmations feel somewhat generic ('nice work,' 'strong start') rather than individualized to his specific strengths like problem-solving and self-discovery.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections are accurate, but many responses quickly pivot to stacked questions or advice, cutting reflections short. Emotional layers — Marco's pride, frustration with regain, and fear of ending up 'back where I started' — remain unexplored, with reflections staying at summary level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains exceptionally clear focus on juice consumption and weight management from start to finish, with every response tied to building the plan. No topic drift, and the session progresses logically from exploration to a concrete, time-bound commitment starting tomorrow.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers options and asks Marco's preferences, but frequent multi-question barrages and counselor-supplied strategy/script lists position the counselor as agenda-director rather than true collaborator. Marco shapes details, but the structure remains counselor-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes Marco's choice ('Which of these feels most doable?') and advice is permission-based. Pre-packaged strategy lists and scripted phrasings slightly constrain client-generated solutions, with less explicit affirmation of his ownership than ideal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions successfully elicit motivation — Marco produces strong change talk and his own reasons emerge. However, much of the session's content (strategy menu, if-then suggestions, celebration ideas, scripts) is counselor-supplied, and deeper exploration of Marco's values and own ideas is superficial.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission before advising and invites Marco's input, reflecting some partnership. However, stacked questions (three to four at a time) and counselor-provided solutions make the session resemble a coaching checklist more than genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through importance/confidence rulers (both answered '10'), commitment-strengthening questions, and slip planning, and reinforces it well. Loses points for occasionally supplying the change content himself rather than having Marco generate it, and for not exploring the meaning behind his confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco's 'boy, do I love that OJ' is acknowledged but not strategically softened — the counselor responds with a batch of questions rather than a double-sided reflection. Sustain talk isn't amplified (a strength), but active pivoting is minimal and the client resolves ambivalence mostly on his own.
