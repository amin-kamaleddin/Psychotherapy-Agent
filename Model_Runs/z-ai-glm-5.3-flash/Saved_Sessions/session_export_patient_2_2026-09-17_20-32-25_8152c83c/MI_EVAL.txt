# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard without any judgment about smoking or drinking. Aisha's ambivalence was met with acceptance, and her guardedness visibly softened into cooperation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured both the functional role of smoking and Aisha's ambivalence, with complex reflections validating competing feelings. Reflections occasionally parroted the client's exact words rather than deepening emotional meaning, and the fear of illness and year-long loss of control went under-explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear, consistent focus on reducing evening smoking, with each reflection and question building momentum toward a concrete one-week plan. Even when drinking was raised, the session stayed anchored without dismissing the client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned between equal partners, with every element of the final plan originating from Aisha rather than the counselor. There was no advisory or directive content at any point; the session was fully co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly reinforced Aisha's ownership and choice, accepting her self-imposed limits without pressure to commit to more. Her freedom to define her own pace was evident in her willingness to commit to the three-a-night plan on her own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor operated from the premise that motivation resides within the client, systematically drawing out Aisha's own reasons for change through open questions. Her shift from guarded ('just here for a checkup') to committed ('I'll tell you straight how the week went') demonstrates evoked, internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently treated Aisha as the expert on her own life, and the emerging plan (three cigarettes, pre-made tea, telling her sister, Friday check-in) was entirely drawn from her own suggestions. Her ownership was evident in her closing commitment to report honestly at follow-up.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully and repeatedly evoked client-generated change talk through questions like 'What would a real break look like to you?' and 'What would make this week feel like a success?'. Aisha responded with increasingly concrete change talk, which the counselor then reinforced with affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections held ambivalence without reinforcing it, and the counselor avoided arguing with minimizations, preventing defensiveness. Opportunities were missed to strategically soften the peer-comparison minimization ('nothing next to people at work'), and the alcohol topic was sidestepped entirely, leaving sustain talk unaddressed.
