# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard communicated consistently, from the warm opening to normalizing relapse ('a slip is part of how this works, not proof you can't'). Ambivalence and resentment are accepted without any hint of judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate throughout, with complex reflections capturing nuanced content (the unfairness vs. quieter voice, the 'knot in my stomach,' the reframe of willpower as relational rather than heroic).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange ties back to the shoplifting target, moving progressively from ambivalence toward a concrete, values-linked plan with no off-topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Jordan's contributions demonstrably shaped the session — the Declan insight, the 'meet them after' plan, and the slip-as-information framing all originated with him and were built upon rather than redirected.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces client ownership: asks rather than tells, never directs, and explicitly credits Jordan's progress to Jordan ('That's you doing that, not the court, not me').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within: Jordan's own reasons (less stress, sleep, the knot gone), his own solutions, and his own past evidence of change (the jacket moment). Moved Jordan from 'a bit of a joke' to 'I actually want to try now.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Jordan is explicitly positioned as the expert ('You're the expert on your own weekends and your own world'), which enabled him to generate his entire plan himself and recognize it as his own work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation and development of change talk: eliciting desires/needs, exploring possibilities, and prompting Jordan to name his own motivation ('The "want" bit... that's mine'), including surfacing past successes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged briefly and redirected effectively, with a skillful double-sided reflection pairing it with change. Minor area: occasional follow-up questions (e.g., probing how 'all-or-nothing' the peer group is) briefly kept barriers on the table before pivoting.
