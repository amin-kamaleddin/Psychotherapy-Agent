# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout — explicitly permits Elena to change or scrap the plan 'without that meaning anything bad,' validates her protectiveness, and normalizes ambivalence. No judgment or disapproval anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including the double-sided opening reflection and the insightful capture of meaning: the cigarette is 'the one thing that's yours.' Consistently reflects Elena's fears about dependency, her patience, and failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking reduction while honoring Elena's pacing, progressively narrowing from ambivalence to a specific first step and check-in plan. Direction is achieved without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely co-constructed plan: Elena generates the coffee-at-the-table idea while the psychiatrist reflects and supports rather than prescribes. The check-in terms ('no lectures, no guilt') are explicitly negotiated and accepted as an equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy actively and repeatedly reinforced — 'you're allowed to change your mind, scale it back, or scrap it entirely,' 'you call it' — and the clinician honors her limits (evenings off the table, no walking advice). Ownership of the plan is framed as entirely hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation elicited from within: Elena generates her own reasons ('maybe it's not purely selfless') and her own success criteria ('doesn't make me a worse mom'). No lecturing; the clinician draws out her expertise rather than supplying advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Elena as the expert on her own life, and her contributions shape the session — the plan, the adjustment rule, and the check-in format all come from her. The clinician repeatedly asks permission and defers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk skillfully evoked and developed, from 'I could try that this week' to a self-designed contingency plan. The clinician amplifies Elena's own change language ('maybe it's not purely selfless') rather than imposing it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely avoids reinforcing sustain talk, using effective acceptance-based redirection ('I won't push the walk thing'). Minor area for growth: some reflections dwell on sustain content (e.g., evenings being untouchable) without more active double-sided pivots toward change.
