# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, treating Maria's skepticism as legitimate rather than noncompliance and never labeling her resistance. Warm, individualized affirmations ('You did well turning frustration into a concrete plan') persisted even when she arrived hostile and hurried.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured both facts and feelings throughout. Minor deductions for pivoting quickly to information-giving and for not explicitly reflecting Maria's deeper emotional disclosure that her attacks 'scare me more than I let on'.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on the target behavior (inhaler adherence) was maintained despite the client's resistance and hurry, with every turn connected to the goal. The session concluded with a specific, actionable plan including a review date and contingency guidance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor worked as an equal partner, asking permission and jointly building the plan with Maria's input (3-week preference, phone-based logging, stairs goal). Deduction: counselor-produced deliverables (trial plan, script, note) left Maria in a reviewer role rather than co-authoring word by word.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with genuine choices at every step and explicit exit ramps built into the plan. Maria's own framing ('it's my experiment, my decision') reflects a successful perception of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was effectively drawn from Maria, eliciting her own reasons for change (fear of attacks, climbing stairs, chasing her kid). Deduction: heavy reliance on menu-based options limited deeper evocation of values and meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Maria as the expert and incorporated her ideas (stairs goal, phone diary) into the plan. Deduction: occasional slips into information-delivery mode meant Maria largely chose among counselor-generated options rather than driving content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Structured choice questions skillfully evoked strong change talk ('a trial thing, maybe', 'maybe it's worth a try', 'I'll be there'). Deduction: emerging change talk was not always deepened before moving on to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was validated without argument and resistance was effectively softened by reframing the trial as Maria's own experiment. Deduction: substantial session time on side-effect risks kept some attention on reasons against change, though responsively to her questions.
