# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained consistent warmth and nonjudgmental regard, validating the client's fears about standing out with his friends and explicitly normalizing a potential slipped Saturday ('Not at all—and I want you to hear that from me clearly').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and consistent, using the client's own words and metaphor ('Saturday Marek and Sunday Marek'), capturing the emotional core of the rounds fear, and honoring subtle admissions like the 'blurry' Saturdays and the importance of remembering the laughter.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained unwavering focus on weekend drinking, advancing systematically from values to envisioning, a self-defined limit, barriers, strategies, accountability, and a specific start date. No topic drift occurred; even reflections served forward momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated as a full partnership, with open questions at every juncture, a co-constructed check-in plan, and all solutions originating from the client. The client's closing remark about leaving with 'a plan I actually kind of like' directly evidenced successful collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership was consistently reinforced ('you're not signing a contract here'), with the plan explicitly attributed to the client and both outcomes framed as acceptable for the follow-up. The Petr-style abstinence model the client feared was never imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key insight—the limit of three, the squash court, the Sunday accountability, the past success story—came from the client, drawn out through evocative questions. The final self-authored plan with a start date demonstrated fully internalized motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was repeatedly positioned as the expert ('You know your guys,' 'You know your life best'), with every solution originating from him and the counselor's role limited to drawing those ideas out. The plan was explicitly framed as 'designed by you, not handed to you by anyone.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic open questions ('What would a good Sunday look like?', 'What has ever helped you talk back to it—even once?') elicited progressively stronger change talk, from 'three... that doesn't sound impossible' to a concrete commitment with a start date. The counselor deliberately highlighted change talk when it emerged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I can't just drop the beers and sit home like some hermit') was acknowledged via double-sided reflections and consistently redirected toward client-generated solutions. Fears of slipping were met with catastrophe-minimizing rather than dwelling on failure.
