# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated respect, nonjudgment, and affirmations of Jordan's efforts, maintaining a warm and supportive tone. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and there was limited normalization of setbacks or deep validation of his discouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Early reflections of Jordan's emotional state were accurate, capturing exhaustion and discouragement effectively. Deeper emotional reflections became sparse once planning began, missing opportunities to explore the underlying hopelessness and sting of rejection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained an exceptionally clear focus on a single concrete target behavior, progressively narrowing to one specific step with timing, cue, and contingency plans. Every exchange advanced the plan without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Jordan taking the lead on key decisions, demonstrating real power-sharing. Collaboration was uneven at times, as the counselor defaulted to step-by-step instructions and scripts rather than consistently eliciting Jordan's own strategies first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently presented choices and deferred to Jordan's judgment, including offering help only if wanted. However, supplying ready-made solution menus before asking Jordan's own ideas subtly constrained client-generated choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some meaningful motivation was evoked via open questions and the confidence ruler, including Jordan's key insight about self-defeating patterns. However, the session's momentum relied heavily on counselor-supplied ideas and scripts, and key change talk moments were not reflected back or deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan's input directly shaped the intervention (the $10 alert, Saturday timing, alarm cue), conveying a strong sense of shared decision-making. Occasionally, the counselor front-loaded option menus and advice before Jordan generated his own ideas, slightly reducing his role as primary expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful questions elicited meaningful change talk, including a specific desired outcome, a wish to not feel stuck, and confidence rising to a 6. The counselor did not consistently deepen this talk once it emerged, such as exploring what made confidence a 6 or amplifying Jordan's self-sabotage insight.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided reinforcing helplessness narratives and pivoted toward small doable steps. However, redirects were often instructional rather than reflective, with sustain talk met by problem-solving advice rather than double-sided reflections or affirmations of past persistence.
