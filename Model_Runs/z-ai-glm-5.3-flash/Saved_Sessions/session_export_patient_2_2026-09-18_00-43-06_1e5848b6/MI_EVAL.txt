# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Affirms the client's resilience, self-knowledge, and autonomy throughout; explicitly normalizes ambivalence ('Both of those can be true') and never argues with sustain talk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Deeply reflective listening, repeatedly naming the underlying needs (the break, the loneliness, the quiet) behind surface content in the client's own language.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully follows the client's cues from 'just a checkup' to a concrete, self-generated change plan without ever imposing direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently defers to the client as the expert ('You know your evenings best,' 'What could you say to him, in your own words?'), letting the client author the entire plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforces control resting with the client, honors her refusal to commit beyond evenings, and frames change as 'getting something back' rather than sacrifice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits the client's own motivations (her son, reclaiming evenings, company from her husband) and even her own relapse-prevention script ('If Thursday goes wrong, it doesn't mean I failed').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co-built and client-owned; the clinician's contributions are reflections and invitations, not prescriptions or instructions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Draws out strong, specific change talk ('three feels doable,' 'it's more like getting something back,' 'that I want to try') and deepens it with open questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Meets each piece of sustain talk ('it's not that bad,' defensiveness about policing) with reflective, non-confrontational responses that keep the client engaged and moving forward.
