# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with effective normalization of relapse and validation of shame. Affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic, lacking specific affirmation of her strengths as a resilient single mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections early on, but emotional material (stigma as a 'leper,' daughter not wanting to hug her, shame of failing twice, wobbly voice) is glossed over rather than reflected or explored. No deep complex reflections of the emotional meaning of her ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with logical progression toward a defined plan (quit date, prep steps, daughter conversation). Momentum is sometimes psychiatrist-driven, and the rapid pace of multiple options per turn dilutes focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative gestures exist (role-play at her request, incorporating the 'quit mug' idea), but the power balance tilts toward the psychiatrist, who proposes nearly every strategy. Key decisions are largely psychiatrist-generated with Anisa agreeing rather than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choices offered and pacing respected on the quit date, but the style is highly prescriptive with directives and little explicit reinforcement of her ownership of the change. When Anisa sets a boundary ('don't set me up to fail'), her self-knowledge is not affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is frequently supplied by the psychiatrist through dense advice lists rather than drawn from Anisa. The readiness ruler is the main evocative move; notably, Anisa asks the best evocative question herself ('why is this time different?') and it is not turned back to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite inviting collaboration, the psychiatrist repeatedly takes the expert role with rapid-fire recommendation lists and closed questions. Anisa's contributions shape the plan despite, not because of, the psychiatrist's facilitation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness/confidence ruler elicited strong change talk ('I want to do it, I truly do'), and her meaningful commitment (quit for daughter's birthday) was affirmed. However, change talk is not deepened — no follow-ups like 'What makes it a seven?' and her direct invitation to explore what makes this time different is missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk (past failures, patches not working, rejection of morning routines, needing to feel more ready) is answered with prescriptive lists rather than double-sided reflections, keeping the conversation anchored on barriers. Sustain talk is neither reinforced nor effectively resolved — it is simply moved past.
