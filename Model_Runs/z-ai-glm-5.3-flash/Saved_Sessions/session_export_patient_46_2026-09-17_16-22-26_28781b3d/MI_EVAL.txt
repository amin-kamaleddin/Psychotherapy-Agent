# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental stance with no lecturing or moralizing, even amid the teen's defensiveness. Validation phrases feel somewhat repetitive ('totally understandable'), diluting their impact.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A solid double-sided reflection showed good reflective listening, but most reflections stayed at the summary level rather than exploring the emotional core (peer fear, parental surveillance). One accuracy slip occurred in re-asking the confidence question after Ethan objected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and harm-reduction, progressing logically to a concrete plan with a follow-up check-in. Somewhat narrow and occasionally stalled in repetitive recaps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed from Ethan's own ideas (skull signal, drink swap, sister as backup), but multi-question, advice-heavy turns put the counselor in the lead. His mild pushbacks suggest the counselor sometimes steers rather than follows.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong permission-seeking, choice-framing, and acceptance of Ethan's limits (dropping the corny refusal line, accepting his non-quitting stance). Some turns push too hard, including prescriptive safety directives and the repeated confidence question.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited Ethan's own motivations and self-generated hope, but evocation was superficial—shifting to supplying options and scripts rather than drawing out his values. The mechanical confidence scaling reduced evocation to a technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ethan's ideas substantially shaped the plan, which reflects good partnership. However, the counselor often defaulted to expert mode with bulleted option menus and scripted texts, missing Ethan's social reality until he corrected it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk was evoked (tired of puking and trouble, drink swap, confidence rising 6 to 7), but it was rarely deepened with evocative follow-ups. The repeated confidence scaling was mechanical, and pivotal moments like 'maybe that's a good thing though' went unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One appropriate double-sided reflection early on, but sustain talk ('I'm not saying I'm quitting') was largely tolerated or mirrored back rather than acknowledged and redirected. No strategic pivoting away from the sustaining side.
