# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing setbacks with a non-shaming slip plan. The client explicitly confirmed feeling unjudged, stating this is usually why she avoids discussing her drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client's ambivalence and perspective (e.g., wanting relaxation without escalation). Reflections were at times brief or fused with questions, and deeper emotional exploration of the mood-drinking link and self-trust was left underdeveloped.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained an exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior from open to close, with each turn advancing toward a concrete, fully specified plan. The psychiatrist guided efficiently without drifting into unrelated territory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with the client supplying most strategies and the clinician providing structure and logistics. Some turns were checklist-like and logistically dense, making the clinician at times more of an efficient plan-manager than a fully mutual dialogue partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions were consistently framed as the client's own, with advice offered as options rather than directives and the client's chosen modifications adopted without alteration. The clinician never pressured her toward the seven-drink guideline, fully respecting her self-selected goal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence rulers effectively drew out the client's own reasons and self-efficacy statements. Evocation tapered in the final third, which became largely logistical, and emerging commitment language was not fully exploited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client was positioned as the expert, and her ideas (pantry storage, restart note, check-ins) shaped the plan. The psychiatrist occasionally supplied dense option lists that slightly led the agenda, though the client remained free to select.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence rulers skillfully and repeatedly evoked client-generated motivation, which she expressed clearly ('I could see trying two a night'; 'I'd like to prove it to myself'). The clinician reinforced this change talk by echoing and building on it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally and reframed toward a smaller, realistic step rather than challenged. The clinician occasionally restated limits on the client's behalf and could use double-sided reflections more deliberately to link sustain and change talk.
