# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Eric's autonomy and terms. Lacked explicit affirmations of his strengths (work ethic, willingness to show up).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected Eric's ambivalence, including his frustration and the value of smoking as his break. Reflections were often followed quickly by options rather than deepening the emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking throughout, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete, time-specific plan. No drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were co-constructed with menus of options and incorporated Eric's preferences (lozenge, keys cue, check-in format). Sometimes filled space with technical instructions in an expert register without checking if Eric wanted that detail.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently emphasized choice ('Your choice—I'll support whatever you decide') and accepted Eric's limits without pressure. Advice was framed as information tied to Eric's own decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some genuine evocative moves (importance ruler, pros/cons), but moved quickly to structuring an experiment rather than persistently drawing out intrinsic motivations. Missed chances to explore what the 'calm and break' means to Eric or his own reasons beneath 'I feel fine.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed expertise as residing with Eric, whose contributions visibly shaped the session (lozenge, keys reminder, check-in). Option menus sometimes leaned toward the counselor's own solutions rather than drawing out Eric's ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk via the importance ruler and behavioral experiments, moving Eric from a '3' to concrete commitment language and planning. Missed chances to reinforce his self-generated reasons (money, curiosity) before pivoting to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, using brief acknowledgment and effective double-sided reflections pairing sustain and change themes. Occasionally reflected sustain talk back nearly verbatim, slightly amplifying external motivation.
