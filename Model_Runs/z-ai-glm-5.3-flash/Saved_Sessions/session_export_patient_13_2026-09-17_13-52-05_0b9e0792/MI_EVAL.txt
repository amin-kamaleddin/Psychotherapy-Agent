# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance maintained with affirmations like 'You did that—big step.' However, acceptance stayed brief and procedural, rarely personalizing the courage behind the client's disclosures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections were present, but most turns pivoted quickly to advice and checklists. Her deepest fears ('what if they find something really bad') were never reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Crystal-clear focus on the target behavior (seeking medical evaluation) throughout, with every response tied to concrete next steps. The session ended with a completed role-play call and a written note.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration in asking which steps felt doable and offering to fill in the note together. But the plan (labs, telehealth, scripts) originated almost entirely from the counselor rather than being co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some choices, respecting agency at key junctures. Yet the interaction was heavily advice-saturated, with the volume of options itself feeling pressure-inducing ('Whoa, that's... a lot all at once').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied by the counselor through structured tasks rather than drawn from the client. Key evocative questions about her own reasons and hopes for seeking care were absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role, prescribing labs by name, delivering ER criteria unprompted, and voicing the receptionist's full intake. Anara received the plan rather than co-creating it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The role-play effectively elicited strong self-affirming change talk ('I actually did that,' 'maybe knowing is better than sitting here wondering'). Missed chances to deepen this change talk at the end.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ('maybe I'm fine and I'm worrying over nothing,' 'I can't un-make it') was consistently ignored rather than acknowledged. The pivots to options felt abrupt and dismissive of legitimate fears.
