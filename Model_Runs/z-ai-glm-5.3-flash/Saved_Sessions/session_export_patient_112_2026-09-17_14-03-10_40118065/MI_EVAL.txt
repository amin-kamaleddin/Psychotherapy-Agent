# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Rosa's fears throughout, with no labeling or criticism. However, affirmations were sparse and generic, lacking specific recognition of Rosa's strengths (e.g., her past quit attempt, her honesty about ambivalence).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections captured Rosa's emotional experience well, but as the session progressed, reflections gave way to checklists and advice. Emotionally rich material (ritual loss, her doctor's warning, emerging hope) was largely met with plans rather than reflective exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on smoking cessation with no topic drift, ending with a concrete committed next step. A point was lost because direction was driven by counselor-structured agendas rather than collaboratively derived focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa's input shaped the plan (choosing the ritual swap, identifying Carmen as support), but the counselor frequently delivered pre-packaged content — checklists, scripts, and pre-rated decisional balance scores — with limited joint construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently offered menus and genuine choice points without any pressuring language, and respected Rosa's pacing. It fell short because advice was sometimes front-loaded before permission and Rosa's ownership of the decision was not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The dominant mode was informing — medication options, tools, scripts, and counselor-drafted ratings — with few open evocative questions about Rosa's reasons or confidence. Her strongest motivational material (health fears, money realization, hope) emerged spontaneously rather than through counselor elicitation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa's choices were incorporated at key decision points, but large portions of the session featured expert-delivered content, and her own expertise emerged from her rather than from counselor power-sharing. The balance tilted toward the expert stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa generated meaningful change talk ('I know I've got to stop,' 'the hard part is short'), aided by the experiment framing and decisional balance. But the counselor did not reinforce or deepen these statements, missing chances to elicit commitment language or confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor acknowledged sustain talk without amplifying it and offered a normalizing reframe of ambivalence. However, extensive session airtime was given to cons via the decisional balance, with no double-sided reflections pivoting from Rosa's concerns back to her motivations.
