# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout with specific, genuine affirmations ('You designed that yourself'). No judgment or moralizing despite the court-mandated context.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening consistently accurate and goes beyond content to meaning, e.g., 'the pull is the ritual, the being-in-it-together.' Tracks subtle shifts in the client's motivation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior with clear progression: exploring peer pull → building reasons → concrete strategies → rehearsal → a specific, time-limited commitment. Skilled 'one Saturday, not forever' narrowing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving; the plan was entirely client-generated with the clinician reflecting and organizing. Client's closing comment confirms the egalitarian stance was felt.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices consistently framed as the client's, with options offered as menus rather than prescriptions. Accepted the client's 'one Saturday' limit without challenge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key motivational element was elicited from the client via open questions and strategic hypotheticals, with no advice or imported reasons. A model demonstration of drawing out intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in the client ('You're the expert on your life') and the client's contributions genuinely shaped the session. Client's closing remark ('Thought you'd just lecture me') is direct evidence of experienced power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematically evoked and reinforced change talk, culminating in commitment language and a self-generated plan. Textbook sequencing from desire to rehearsal to commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely avoids dwelling on sustain talk and effectively reframes doubt (e.g., fear as a usable tool). Minor gap: the client's residual doubt ('part of me still wonders if I'll actually do it') was normalized but not actively pivoted away from.
