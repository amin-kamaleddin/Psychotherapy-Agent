# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard—affirming Samuel's progress, fully respecting his boundary around nicotine gum, and matching his humor warmly. No judgment or disapproval appears anywhere, including around slips and sustain talk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and consistent, including a double-sided reflection that validates both the value of journaling and its burden, and reflections that capture the twenty-year work habit and the deeper identity meaning behind change talk. Reflections track both content and emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on smoking reduction and builds progressively from journaling barriers through concrete planning, success criteria, risk anticipation, and closing commitment. Tangential concerns (wife's nagging, coworker ribbing) are folded back into the target behavior, ending with a specific follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is fully egalitarian—the clinician invites Samuel's ideas, co-creates solutions from his suggestions, and frames the plan as Samuel's own design. Samuel's engagement (offering lunch on him if he hits five a day, committing to honest logging) shows the power-sharing felt genuine.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice is actively reinforced throughout: the gum boundary is respected without pushing, options are framed around what 'feels most doable' for Samuel, and every plan element is self-generated. The clinician explicitly reinforces Samuel's ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice or persuasion is offered; open questions draw out Samuel's own reasons (breathing easier, proving himself to his wife, becoming 'someone who's getting somewhere') and his own risk-management solutions. Samuel's transformation from ambivalence to 'I'll try it. One break at a time' is strong evidence of evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located with the client ('You know your days and rhythms better than anyone,' 'You came up with all of that yourself'), and every plan element originates from Samuel. His ideas substantially shape the session—a hallmark of top-level partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked through questions about what worked in the successful first week, defining success criteria, and an evocative future-oriented question about hitting five a day that draws out powerful statements about identity and proving himself to his wife. The clinician then reinforces this talk and consolidates commitment ('slipping isn't quitting').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without amplification—doubts about 'swapping one habit for another' are met with a double-sided reflection and pivot to choice, and failure predictions are not dwelt upon. A point is withheld only because the social-pressure sustain talk from coworkers is acknowledged but not deeply explored or reframed until Samuel does it himself.
