# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, affirming Marcus's honesty and normalizing his ambivalence without lecturing or judgment. The client's own comment ('less annoying than I thought... You actually asked what I think') directly confirms acceptance was conveyed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently captured underlying meaning, e.g., drinking as 'the price of admission to the party' and the fear of being 'quiet without drinks.' Each reflection was accurate enough that Marcus built on it rather than correcting it, visibly softening his guardedness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on alcohol use from opening question to closing plan, progressing from precontemplation to a specific self-authored behavioral experiment. Directional guidance was strong without ever becoming directive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was a genuine exchange between equals — Marcus proposed the sobriety experiment, buddy system, and soda hack, while the psychiatrist contributed reflections and questions. The check-in was negotiated as a 'deal' rather than imposed, confirming co-created terms.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly reinforced ownership and choice ('It's completely your call'), framed even a failed attempt as useful, and echoed Marcus's boundary ('not expecting a promise about forever'). Marcus left with self-generated confidence ('it's gonna go fine').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No reasons for change were supplied by the counselor; all motivation emerged from Marcus — fear of the friend's dad path, wanting to know he's fun sober, being 'in charge.' The session shows a complete evocation arc from 'never thought about it' to a self-authored plan and personal reason.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('You know your world better than I do'), and the plan came entirely from Marcus, amplified rather than supplied by the psychiatrist. Asking Marcus to state his own reason at the close is a textbook partnership move.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic open questions and comparisons drew out Marcus's own reasons, culminating in the pivotal shift ('quiet-with-a-friend isn't bad') and commitment language ('I could probably do that'). The final reason — 'in charge, not the other way around' — was fully client-authored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk, using double-sided reflections and re-anchoring to Marcus's values rather than debating. The score falls short of 5 only because a few early reflections gave extended airtime to the status quo before pivoting.
