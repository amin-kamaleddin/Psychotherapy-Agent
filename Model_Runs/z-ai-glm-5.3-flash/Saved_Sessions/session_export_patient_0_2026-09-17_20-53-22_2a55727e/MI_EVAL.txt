# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently shows unconditional positive regard, affirming Adrian's honesty ('That takes guts') even about lying and hiding use. Adrian explicitly notes the absence of judgment, saying he expected a lecture but instead 'we actually talked it through.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate throughout, capturing ambivalence from the opening reflection onward and nuanced emotions like fear of Marcus's judgment. Each reflection deepens disclosure, drawing progressively more vulnerable material from Adrian.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on meth use, progressing from ambivalence to a one-month break, coping strategies, accountability, and a first action step. It closes with a specific, client-chosen commitment: texting Marcus before the next session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is a genuine exchange of equals — permission-style questions, co-constructed rehearsal ('You bring the words, I'll play Marcus'), and a plan built entirely from Adrian's ideas. Adrian initiates the closing contract ('Deal?') himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All choices remain with the client, with no directives or pressure, and autonomy is explicitly affirmed ('you're not leaving this to willpower, you're building a plan'). Adrian's closing comments confirm he experiences the change as his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every motivation — healing, avoiding contaminated supplies, escaping shame, wanting Marcus's respect — comes from Adrian's own words, skillfully elicited and amplified. Selective reflections of change talk demonstrate masterful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions Adrian as the expert ('you know yourself and your friends better than anyone'), and every plan element originates from the client. The conditional role-play offer reinforces shared work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is evoked and developed skillfully — from imagining sober weekends to a concrete one-month commitment and specific coping strategies. Follow-up questions build a full client-generated change plan culminating in strong commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fears of boredom, being 'left out,' Marcus judging him) is held lightly and reframed as material for change rather than explored at length. It is never reinforced or dismissed.
