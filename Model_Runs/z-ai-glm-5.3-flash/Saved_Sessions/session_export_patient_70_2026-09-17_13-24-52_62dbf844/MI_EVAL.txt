# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, including around literacy struggles and offending history. Warmth is present but affirmations are somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some effort to acknowledge Lars's feelings, but much of the session is dominated by rapid-fire practical scripts. Emotional disclosures about shame, guilt, and loss are met with quick pivots to options rather than reflections of their weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the target behaviors of avoiding Friday-night trouble and progressing toward education/employment. However, the focus remains on immediate tactics without linking back to Lars's larger values or consolidating a broader change plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements exist, such as inviting choice of focus and tweaking scripts to his voice, but the flow is largely counselor-delivers-advice with Lars complying. When asked for his opinion, the counselor immediately prescribes rather than exploring Lars's reasoning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers menus and asks for preferences, acknowledging choice, but the style remains prescriptive with numbered lists and instructions implying the counselor owns the plan. Lars's own ideas are adopted but not amplified as his ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely draws motivation from within Lars, instead supplying external solutions and scripts while his powerful motivations (fear of prison, the car dream, Donna) go unexplored. Even evocative questions are followed by counselor-generated answers rather than exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choices and asks Lars what he wants to rehearse, showing some power-sharing, but the session is heavily expert-driven. Lars's expertise about his own life is minimally acknowledged and does not reshape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Lars generates substantial change talk and the counselor reinforces it lightly, but rarely uses open questions designed to evoke it and never deepens or summarizes his motivations. The change talk comes largely from the patient rather than skillful evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Repeatedly engages with sustain talk through extensive planning around refusal scripts and pressure scenarios rather than pivoting away. When Lars voices doubt and embarrassment, the counselor provides scripts instead of double-sided reflections or strategic reframing.
