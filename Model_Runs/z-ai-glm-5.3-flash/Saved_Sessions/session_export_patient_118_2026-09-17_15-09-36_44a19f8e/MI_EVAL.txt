# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicated respect and nonjudgment throughout, with genuine honoring of the client's agenda and requests. Some closing scripts felt slightly formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Early accurate reflections (e.g., 'you feel nagged... that's upsetting') were pivotal in de-escalating defensiveness. Missed deeper emotional reflections later, especially around the neighbor's dry socket story, as reflections became sparser once advice-giving began.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept focus on smoking as it related to dental treatment while respecting the client's agenda, landing on a concrete plan. Minor drift toward general oral hygiene advice in the information-giving section.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission before informing and co-developed a plan from the client's own selections, with decisions confirmed rather than imposed. The middle section relied more on clinician-delivered information than back-and-forth shaping.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength — consistently offered genuine choices, framed advice as options the client selected from, and reinforced client ownership ('no pressure,' 'you kept control of the choices'). The client's own responses confirm he felt respected and not talked down to.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged via choice-based questions ('maybe I'll think about stretching it out'). However, the counselor missed the key opportunity to deepen this spontaneous contemplation language and relied more on providing options than eliciting the client's own reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly signaled that the client's preferences shaped the session, and trust clearly grew ('I appreciate you asking me'). When asked for suggestions, delivered a clinician-driven list rather than first eliciting the client's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Indirectly elicited change talk through choice questions, and the client volunteered contemplation language. Did not actively deepen this spontaneous change talk — no exploration of confidence, vision, or what made this conversation different — and closed with scheduling-type questions instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to sustain talk with brief, accurate reflections and returned to the client's agenda without arguing or pressuring on NRT. The reflection 'don't want to quit now' restated sustain talk without a double-sided pivot toward the client's own motivation.
