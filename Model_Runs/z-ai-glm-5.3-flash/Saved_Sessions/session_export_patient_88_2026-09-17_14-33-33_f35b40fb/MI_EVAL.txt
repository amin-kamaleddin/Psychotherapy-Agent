# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with genuine affirmations ('trying absolutely counts') while acknowledging Marcus's defensiveness. However, repeatedly asked the safety question despite Marcus's clear irritation and did not respond to his request for confidentiality about the checklist.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some accurate early reflections about defensiveness and peer perception, but most responses were rapid-fire advice lists rather than reflective listening. Emotionally meaningful content (walking home at 2am, parental pressure) went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on alcohol use and risk reduction, redirecting drift effectively and ending with a concrete, actionable plan. Points deducted because the target behaviors were counselor-chosen rather than collaboratively defined, and Marcus's core concern (parent conflict) was left unaddressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration appeared when Marcus shaped the plan (max of 4 drinks, Uber backup, checklist), and the counselor checked in before offering tools. However, the default mode was delivering menus of expert suggestions, and the agenda was largely counselor-set rather than jointly negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed options as Marcus's choice and respected his refusals, with Marcus himself noting the counselor 'didn't preach.' Undercut somewhat by unsolicited prescriptive lists and failure to explicitly affirm Marcus's ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied almost entirely on providing information and advice rather than eliciting Marcus's own motivations, with no open evocative questions about what matters to him. Marcus's willingness to change emerged despite, not because of, the counselor's technique and was never deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission-style questions and let Marcus select plan elements, conveying some power-sharing. But the interaction was heavily advice-driven, with multiple pre-packaged bullet-point lists delivered in an expert-prescriber manner and no acknowledgment of Marcus as the expert on his own social world.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited change talk indirectly through the 'one less than last time' framing, and Marcus produced commitment language ('four max,' 'I'll try the four-drink thing'). However, the counselor never explicitly evoked or deepened Marcus's own reasons for change or reflected his change statements back to strengthen them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus offered substantial sustain talk ('everyone does it,' 'I'm not that guy,' 'that proves my point') that was never reflected, reframed, or worked with via double-sided reflections. The counselor consistently bypassed these statements by pivoting straight to harm-reduction lists, leaving core rationalizations unaddressed.
