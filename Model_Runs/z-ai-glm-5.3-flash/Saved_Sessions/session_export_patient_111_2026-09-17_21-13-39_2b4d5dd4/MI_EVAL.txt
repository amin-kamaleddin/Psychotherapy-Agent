# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard and validation of Richard's autonomy, even during overt resistance; affirmations were specific and genuine. Never judged, labeled, or pushed despite defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening throughout, capturing both content and emotional nuance (e.g., the morning moment with his wife). Reflections consistently deepened the conversation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking behavior and its relational meaning with no topic drift despite deflections. Each exchange built progressively toward the client's shift in stance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked explicitly as an equal partner ('let's figure this out together, as equals'), redirecting questions back to Richard's own assessment. Next steps were entirely co-constructed and client-generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced ownership ('that's your call, and it will stay your call', 'yours, at your pace'). The client explicitly confirmed this landed: 'But it's my maybe. Nobody forced this part.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently elicited motivation from within using skilled evocative and comparison questions. The client's own values (his marriage, shared mornings) became the engine of change rather than external arguments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Richard as the expert on his own routines and trouble spots, with the plan driven by his contributions (porch chair, keeping it outside, talking to his wife). The counselor only helped shape what the client generated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and amplified change talk, from 'I don't want to lose her' through 'That's the life I want. Her in it.' Named and affirmed the client's shift ('maybe I'm not dead set against it anymore'), moving him from precontemplation toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk and accepted it non-judgmentally, but sustain statements ('I like my smokes') were validated more than strategically reframed. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections and for exploring the prior failed quit attempt to build confidence.
