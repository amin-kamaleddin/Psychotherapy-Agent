# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with genuine positive regard throughout. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and tied to the counselor's plan rather than deeply individualized, and her fear/anger was never explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, but empathy was inconsistent — deep emotional disclosures (son's tears, pre-send anxiety, temper worries) were met with checklists rather than emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on reducing co-parenting conflict and building stability, with no drift off-topic. Direction was partly achieved through steering, with emotional concerns set aside quickly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (adopting her schedule-first suggestion, offering role-play), but the pattern was largely advice-delivery followed by client acceptance. Her expertise was used to tailor scripts rather than co-construct strategy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and accepted her modifications, supporting genuine ownership ('I can do that myself'). Autonomy was undermined by the volume of pre-written messages and steps, with little affirmation that she is the expert on her ex.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Weakest domain — the counselor supplied reasons, plans, and scripts rather than drawing motivation from within. Her motivations surfaced organically but were never deliberately evoked or expanded with open questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Expert-driven default with prescriptive plans, checklists, and templates dominating the session. Amina shaped content at moments, but the counselor rarely elicited her ideas before offering solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Substantial change talk emerged ('I want to do this. My boy deserves it'), but largely organically rather than through deliberate evocation. Strong change statements were not deepened or consolidated into a summary of her motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of conflict, 'I don't want to make things worse') was not reinforced and was partially reframed, but ambivalence was often bypassed with scripts and breathing techniques. No double-sided reflections or exploration of the sustain talk's meaning.
