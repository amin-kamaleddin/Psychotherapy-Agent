# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, with affirmations like 'That's a powerful thing you just noticed' and 'I actually believe that.' Avoidance and prior rescheduling are framed compassionately rather than judged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, opening with a complex reflection that captures the ambivalence ('That push-pull sounds exhausting'). Reflections consistently land, tracking subtle shifts like the move from self-worth attached to the exam toward a 'gentler story.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains steady focus on the target behavior (taking the exam) with each response moving the conversation forward—study blocks, past success evidence, commitment style, supports. No topic drift; even the sister discussion stays tied to exam accountability.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative, with ideas offered as reflections and questions rather than expert solutions, and the plan emerging entirely from dialogue. Hana explicitly experiences it this way: 'Thank you for not letting me brush past the small stuff.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Hana's ownership: 'Is there a version of commitment that still leaves you some room to breathe?' and letting her script her own disclosure to her sister. No directives, pressure, or prescriptive advice appear.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key insight originates from Hana—'fear doesn't always mean I'm right,' 'doing the work made the fear quieter,' telling one person. Open questions consistently elicit and deepen her own reasons and commitment, culminating in 'I'm really making it this time.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Hana as the expert ('You know yourself and your patterns better than anyone'), and the entire plan—twenty-minute blocks, written reminders, telling her sister—comes from her. No advice-giving; questions follow her material rather than a preset agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk with questions about what made the recording experience work and how to hold the gentler story. Selectively reinforces shifts ('That's a real shift') and consolidates with 'hold onto that for a second.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged briefly with empathy ('the exit serves a real purpose') then pivoted toward change each time. Fear is reframed as a signal rather than a verdict, and the session ends focused on strengths and plan.
