# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with genuine affirmations (protecting the mother-son connection, quitting smoking) and honoring Irina's request to preview the referral text. To reach 5, warmth could be more individualized, e.g., explicitly normalizing her struggles as a stressed single mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, notably 'those two drinks are your breath, but you also feel guilty,' which captured her core ambivalence. Reflections were sometimes quickly followed by suggestions or scaling questions, moving to problem-solving before fully absorbing emotions like guilt and loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly oriented toward alcohol use from the opening line through the concrete plan and accountability check-in. Structured tools (rulers, smallest-doable-step, defined follow-up) provided strong forward momentum with only on-target drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Irina's ideas (childcare solution, meet-and-greet) substantially shaping it and confirming the partnership in her own words. Some ideas still originated from clinician-supplied option menus, keeping collaboration below 5.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Irina retained control at every decision point — approving the text, choosing the follow-up method and time — and a slip was explicitly framed as non-failure. More explicit affirmation of her ownership of the change process at closing would reach 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from the client via rulers and the smallest-step question, yielding strong change talk ('Maybe that's my test week'). At times example lists were offered before Irina fully exhausted her own ideas, slightly diluting the elicit-don't-tell principle.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Irina was treated as the expert, and her testimony ('Nobody's offered me real help before... it's actually about me') confirms effective power sharing. The frequent supply of example option menus rather than purely eliciting her ideas kept this from a full 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation produced the session's strongest change talk ('one drink instead of two — I could maybe try that,' 'Maybe that's my test week'). Her closing observation about arriving defensive and leaving with a plan was a rich moment left only lightly touched.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with empathic reflection rather than argument, which is good technique. However, double-sided reflections linking sustain talk to her stated values (e.g., 'drinking is my escape' paired with not wanting it to take time from her son) were underused, and 'I deserve two after bad shifts' was not explicitly revisited.
