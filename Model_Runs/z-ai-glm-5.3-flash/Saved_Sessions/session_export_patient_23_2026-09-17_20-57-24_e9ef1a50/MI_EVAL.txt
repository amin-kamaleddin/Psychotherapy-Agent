# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with no hint of judgment even when describing heavy drinking. Slips were reframed as 'information, not proof you can't do this,' and her modest goal was validated rather than pushed toward guideline adherence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both surface content ('the ritual matters to you') and deeper meaning ('not the wine itself, but that little lift on a rough day'). The reflection normalizing the anticipated strangeness of the first tea night showed deep understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol use, with every reflection and question tied to the target behavior. The session progressed naturally from exploration to commitment to a specific first step this week.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative, with Claudia's ideas (tea ceremony, bath nights, moving wine out of sight) shaping the entire plan. Permission-style open questions and the closing question about success exemplify shared agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly reinforced ownership ('at your pace,' 'built by you') and never pressured toward the doctor's guideline. Claudia's closing remark that she expected a lecture but got a plan 'that's actually mine' confirms effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited almost entirely from within the client, with questions like 'was there ever anything else that gave you that feeling?' surfacing the tea-ceremony insight. Prospective questioning ('What do you imagine the first tea-and-bath night might feel like?') strengthened commitment and motivation visibly grew across the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Claudia was consistently positioned as the expert ('That's genuine expertise about yourself'), and the core plan originated entirely from her ideas. No expert-driven recommendations were imposed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions like 'What is it about those ideas that feels like they'd work for you?' drew out commitment language ('I could actually see myself doing that this week'), which was then reflected and amplified rather than left alone.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was largely skillfully pivoted — exploring what would be 'missing' led Claudia to conclude she'd 'rather have one kind of flat than the other,' and bad-day talk was normalized as information. Occasionally the counselor invited elaboration of difficulty, slightly amplifying barrier focus, though productively.
