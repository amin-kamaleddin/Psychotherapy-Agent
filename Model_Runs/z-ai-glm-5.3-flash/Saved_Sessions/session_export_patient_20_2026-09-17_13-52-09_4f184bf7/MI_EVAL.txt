# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tone is respectful and non-judgmental, including a good reframe of the 'hazardous' label ('not a moral verdict'). However, warmth is thin and functional, with few genuine personal affirmations and a boilerplate crisis disclaimer opening rather than a personalized welcome.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is limited; reflections are superficial ('That makes sense') and the counselor mostly asks screening questions and gives advice. Key emotional content — defensiveness about the label, pride in his life, ambivalence about his sister's criticism — goes largely unreflected, with no complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, efficient focus on alcohol use throughout, producing a concrete reduction plan with a two-week trial and follow-up. Loses a point because the direction is checklist-driven rather than anchored to Brandon's values, and emotional exploration is skipped entirely.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration — Brandon's choices (Tuesday, NA beer, 2–3 drink limit) shape the plan, but the interaction alternates between his talking and the counselor dispensing expert guidance. Strategies like counting, smaller glasses, and exit plans are delivered rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Brandon's autonomy is consistently honored — options are offered, he picks his drink-free day and limits, and his refusal to commit beyond two weeks is accepted without pressure. The dense pre-packaged tip blocks, however, subtly imply the counselor knows best, diluting full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change motivation arises spontaneously from Brandon rather than through skillful evocation. Questions are predominantly screening and logistics, with no scaling questions or exploration of his own reasons, confidence, or vision for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor frequently slips into an expert/advice-giver stance with prescriptive limits and long tip lists. Brandon's concrete choices do shape the plan, but his expertise is rarely acknowledged and his ideas are not explicitly sought.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Brandon spontaneously offers change talk ('I do want to stop feeling rubbish on Wednesday mornings', the gym idea), but the counselor rarely reflects, amplifies, or builds on it. Instead of developing his motivations, the counselor pivots to tips and scheduling — many missed opportunities.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced, and the 'hazardous' label is gently reframed. However, statements like 'I'm not giving up my social life' and 'everyone's overreacting' are mostly left unaddressed rather than softened with double-sided reflections, leaving ambivalence unresolved.
