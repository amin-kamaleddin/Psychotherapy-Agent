# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated Mai's skepticism ('You're right to want to know — that makes a lot of sense'). Affirmations were somewhat generic ('nice job') rather than deeply personalized, keeping this short of a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captured Mai's attachment to routine and her emotional state accurately. Reflections were brief and often pivoted quickly to information-giving, missing deeper exploration of her feeling 'in the dark'.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on understanding and adhering to the new statin with no drift. Every exchange circled back to medication purpose, interactions, dosing, and follow-up with concrete closure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Built the plan around Mai's stated routines (night dosing, nightstand storage) in a shared problem-solving feel. The counselor frequently dominated with expert content, with collaboration expressed more through offering services than co-constructing the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support through repeated option menus, and Mai's declining offers was respected. Some informational exchanges were directive, with less emphasis on Mai's ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opening questions showed evocative intent and Mai articulated her own reasons ('my health matters more than juice'). Motivation was primarily generated through education rather than deliberately eliciting values, reasons, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, but the session often shifted into an expert/educator stance with dense clinical information dumps. Mai was positioned primarily as a recipient of information rather than an expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged naturally ('I'll switch to orange juice,' 'I'll take it tonight') and was acknowledged. The counselor rarely actively evoked or deepened this language through questions about readiness or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai's doubts about needing the medication and reluctance to give up grapefruit were acknowledged without reinforcement, and a useful reframing via statin alternatives was offered. No double-sided reflections were used; sustain talk was mostly resolved through information provision rather than MI technique.
