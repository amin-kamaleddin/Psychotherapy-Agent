# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, respect, and non-judgment with affirmations like 'you've thought this through' and 'nice work choosing a clear win.' Somewhat formulaic and brisk, rarely slowing down to fully honor the emotional weight of Mateo's discouragement and resignation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening capturing his frustration and exhaustion ('sounds really frustrating to keep showing up... still feel stuck and in pain'). Reflections are brief and the counselor quickly pivots to action, missing chances to deepen understanding of his resignation and relational strain with his wife.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout—every exchange advances the target behavior, from defining 'getting somewhere' as the mailbox walk to a specific plan, contingency strategy, and follow-up. No drift, with each response ending in a question tied to the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative language with co-constructed decisions on phrasing, timing, and check-in format. Collaboration is diluted because the counselor pre-packages most solution content rather than genuinely brainstorming from Mateo's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently strong autonomy support: asks whether a reminder 'would feel like pressure,' offers scripts as options, respects his refusal to role-play again, and honors his request for 'no lectures.' Mateo retains full ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and defining a personal 'win' elicit his perspective, but the counselor frequently slips into advice-giving mode, supplying steps, scripts, and tracking methods. His own motivations and past successes are never deeply explored, leaving motivation partially counselor-supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mateo is positioned as decision-maker ('Which of these feels most doable this week?'), but the substantive plan content is largely generated by the counselor. His own solution knowledge (afternoons are better) is incorporated but not expanded through his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk, from the concrete mailbox goal to confidence scaling (4 to 6) and hopeful closing ('maybe there's a way through it'). Motivation is channeled quickly into task planning rather than deeper exploration of his own reasons and values for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (pain, 'maybe that's just how it is at my age,' fear of quitting) is acknowledged briefly and consistently redirected toward doable steps without amplifying pessimism. Normalizing and double-sided reflections could have been used more strategically than brief acknowledgment followed by redirection.
