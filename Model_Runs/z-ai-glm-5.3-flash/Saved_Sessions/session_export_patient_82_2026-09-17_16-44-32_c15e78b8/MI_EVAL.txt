# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, explicitly addressed confidentiality limits upfront, and offered regular affirmations without moralizing about alcohol use. However, affirmations were somewhat generic and formulaic rather than tied deeply to the client's values and character.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections appeared early on, but empathy was inconsistent as the session shifted into skills-training and advice mode. Lukas's most vulnerable statements ("I don't love who the weekend version of me is becoming" and "That's kind of sad, right?") received no emotional reflection, representing significant missed opportunities.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Direction was exemplary: from the first turn the session stayed tightly focused on alcohol use, peer refusal, and social strategies. Every exchange built logically toward a concrete action plan (texting Marek), with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with the client inventing key language and selecting role-play targets, and the clinician asked permission before each phase. Still, content generation was asymmetric — option menus came predominantly from the counselor, who never asked what ideas Lukas already had.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offered choice and accepted the client overriding a suggestion without resistance, a strong autonomy indicator with no evident pressure. It fell short of a 5 because options were always clinician-generated rather than client-generated, and ownership of the decision to change was not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early evocation was strong, producing genuine client motivation (the regionals goal, the "two lives" insight, the Marek realization), but this skill faded as the clinician increasingly supplied solutions rather than eliciting them. Lukas's closing statement about honesty and self-respect begged for evocative follow-up but went unaddressed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician honored the client's expertise, and Lukas genuinely shaped the plan by choosing the regionals reason, phrasing, and the text to Marek. However, the overall stance was expert-driven, with nearly every counselor turn delivering numbered menus, scripts, and tactical instructions, skewing the ratio of telling to asking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Conditions for change talk were created, and Lukas produced meaningful change talk ("I don't want to be the guy who peaks at 17," "locked on regionals"), which was well-reinforced. However, key chances to deepen commitment language were missed — the powerful closing reflection received no amplification, summary, or move toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk like "It's not like I need it" was not dwelled upon, and rationalizations were redirected toward strategies rather than validated. However, redirection often occurred through rapid pivots to advice rather than MI-consistent means like double-sided reflections or strategic reframes.
