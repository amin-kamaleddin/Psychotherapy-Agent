# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating Eric's skepticism and offering choices without pressure. Warmth was somewhat task-focused, with limited affirmation of Eric beyond the smoking topic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective listening was present ('That makes sense — you're curious, not ready to change') but often brief and surface-level. Emotional undercurrents — wife friction, father's death, guardedness — went largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking throughout, returning to the target behavior despite deflections. Direction was diluted by over-emphasis on producing deliverables rather than consolidating motivation or linking content back to Eric's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission before informing, invited Eric to shape content, and offered choices at nearly every juncture. Fell short because the counselor tended to generate plans and options himself rather than explicitly eliciting Eric's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding autonomy support — constant menu options ('a/b/c... Your choice'), 'no pressure' responses, and graceful acceptance of Eric's refusal to pick a quit date. No coercive or pressuring language appeared anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The endurance/stamina hook successfully evoked some of Eric's own change talk ('something to test, I guess'). However, the session relied heavily on counselor-provided information with few open-ended evocative questions about Eric's values or reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated Eric as the expert — his input visibly shaped the note (skip cancer, emphasize endurance) and checklist (sweat/physical labor concerns). Occasionally slipped into information-delivery mode rather than eliciting Eric's solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked client-generated change talk via the stamina timeline ('I do get a bit more winded on stairs,' 'I might actually pay attention to that at work this week'). Reinforced openings without pushing, but missed chances to deepen ('What would it take for you to test it?').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well with neutral, accepting reflections rather than rebuttals, and reframed toward Eric's own interests (endurance). Sustain talk was never strategically softened via double-sided reflections.
