# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, affirming the smoking quit and validating the courage of talking with his wife. Affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic, limiting deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate at surface level but thin on emotional depth; counselor turns often pivot quickly to options and advice rather than reflecting feelings. Key emotional disclosures—vulnerability about telling his wife and the sting of social pressure—were met with reassurance and scripts rather than deep reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains unwavering focus on reducing alcohol overuse at social events, progressing logically from exploration to rehearsal to concrete commitment without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor seeks Daniel's input at each step, role-plays at his request, and builds the final plan from Daniel's own words. The structured, menu-driven style with ready-made options and advice blocks slightly tilts the power balance toward expert.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices rather than directives, with Daniel's selections driving the plan. However, the counselor frequently supplies pre-written scripts as the content of choices, constraining Daniel's generation of his own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ruler questions effectively elicit Daniel's intrinsic reasons for change, and role-play lets him discover his own capability. After initial elicitation, the counselor relies more on supplying techniques than continually evoking Daniel's own motivations, and does not explicitly elicit commitment language at the close.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniel's expertise is positioned as central—he selects strategies, refusal lines, and exact wording for the wife conversation, directly shaping the plan. Dense, prescriptive turns (lists of refusal lines and planning tips) at times lean toward an advice-giver stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and reinforces change talk through ruler questions, reflection of prior smoking-cessation success, and invitations to commitment behaviors. Reinforcement is sometimes brief, with quick movement to scripts rather than deepening Daniel's own motivational statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (social pressure, 'that's usually when I cave') was neither reinforced nor actively softened—effectively bypassed rather than strategically resolved. Missed opportunities to explore the fear of social judgment and reframe it with Daniel.
