# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout ('Both of those make complete sense'; 'your enjoyment of smoking doesn't make you any less capable'). No judgment even when the client defends smoking or warns of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, emotionally attuned reflections, including the double-sided 'both things are true at once' and catching subtle shifts like 'I could figure it out if I really put my mind to it.' Consistently captures the client's deeper feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking and role-modeling while progressively building toward a concrete, client-owned step (smoke-free drop-off runs for a week). Moves the conversation forward without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Agenda set by asking what the client wants; ideas co-explored; the final plan is entirely Linda's creation. The client's contributions drive every subsequent step.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy explicitly and repeatedly reinforced ('no commitment today. That's completely your call'; 'You don't have to promise me a thing'). The client confirms the choice-protective stance: 'nobody's really asked me that.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn entirely from within the client — her daughter's question, her values, her confidence, and her own plan ideas. The psychiatrist never argues for change or supplies reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys expertise resides with the client ('You know yourself and your family best') and frames her self-designed plan as 'you being smart, not wishy-washy.' The plan comes entirely from Linda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation throughout, producing self-affirmations ('I'm more capable than I give myself credit for') and commitment-adjacent language ('I could maybe do that'). Questions reliably draw out and deepen change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently pivots away from sustain talk without dismissing it, reframing warnings as hope ('I could maybe do that') and normalizing non-commitment rather than arguing against it.
