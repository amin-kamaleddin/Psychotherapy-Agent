# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone appropriate for a court-mandated client, but regard is largely implicit and task-focused. Affirmations are minimal and perfunctory, with little explicit warmth or validation of how difficult the situation feels.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; the counselor pivots to logistics instead of reflecting feelings. Julian's disclosures about anxiety and self-doubt ('I don't want to mess it up') and his relief at the end go unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear and consistent focus on completing 40 hours by the deadline. Every turn advances the goal—confirming requirements, drafting messages, building a week-by-week schedule, and preparing backups—without any drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration exists through preference-checking and incorporating Julian's corrections, but the counselor generates virtually all content and expertise. The interaction resembles a case manager working for a client rather than equal partners.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some decision-making space is offered through choices among options, but the counselor controls the process, order, and all drafted content. There are no explicit statements reinforcing Julian's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is treated as residing in the counselor's plan rather than the client. No evocative open questions, confidence rulers, or attempts to connect compliance to Julian's personal values—even when he offered an opening ('I like dogs').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes an expert/driver role, drafting every message and building the entire schedule while Julian mostly approves. Little effort is made to draw out his own expertise or have him generate solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Notable change talk emerges spontaneously ('I think I can actually do this'), but the counselor does nothing to evoke or deepen it. No reflections of commitment language, open questions about his reasons, or consolidation of his hopeful statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not amplified, but neither is it actively softened or reframed. Worry about messing up and feeling overwhelmed is skipped past rather than addressed with double-sided reflections or affirmations of capability.
