# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'You handled that perfectly.' However, regard was somewhat formulaic and tied mostly to task performance rather than warm acknowledgment of Alessia's feelings or the emotional weight of the situation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Responses were overwhelmingly informational, with a dense list of risks delivered before exploring what Alessia wanted to know. When she expressed fear, the psychiatrist moved straight to task without reflecting her worry; empathy appeared only as superficial validation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained excellent focus on the safe use of citalopram with phenelzine, with every response tying back to the interaction, washout plan, and concrete next steps. There was no topic drift, and the session closed with a clearly restated plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was largely one-directional, resembling expert consultation more than an MI partnership. Alessia's ideas rarely shaped the session, and the drafted messages were written solo rather than jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive offers were made ('Want help wording a message?'), and Alessia's choices were respected without pressure. However, language was largely directive with repeated imperatives, and ownership of decisions was rarely explicitly returned to her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation, questions, and solutions originated almost entirely from the clinician, with virtually no effort to draw out Alessia's own motivations or wisdom. Even her confident, proactive statements ('I feel good about it — confident, actually') went unreinforced and unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist assumed a strongly expert-driven stance, delivering directives, risk lists, and instructions in nearly every reply. Even the drafted messages were produced unilaterally rather than co-constructed with Alessia.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No open questions were used to elicit commitment language, and Alessia's volunteered change-relevant statements were not reinforced or deepened. 'How are you feeling about sending it?' was the lone evocative query.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ambivalence was minimal, and the psychiatrist did not amplify sustain talk while gently affirming her decision to hold the citalopram. However, her initial uncertainty was never actively reframed into a strength beyond brief praise.
