# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations ('that already takes guts') and reassured the client without lecturing. Warmth was somewhat formulaic and identity/community concerns were not deeply validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent accurate reflections captured both content and emotion, such as the tension between rent pressure and social identity. However, reflections on deeper fears of losing belonging stayed surface-level and pivoted quickly to options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus, moving logically from rent to a concrete payment plan and a two-week tracking experiment tied to meth use. Pacing felt slightly rushed early when steps were proposed prematurely.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong permission-seeking and genuine integration of the client's own numbers, wording, and terms into the final plan. Collaboration was uneven early, with counselor-generated menus triggering client resistance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were consistently offered, steps framed as small and optional, and the client's conditions accepted without pressure. Heavy reliance on menus subtly constrained possibilities, with few explicit affirmations of the client's capacity to direct change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation emerged through tasks like adding up spending, producing insights such as 'solving the symptom.' However, the counselor relied on structured menus rather than open evocative questions about the client's values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked for preferences and incorporated the client's wording, but the interaction had an expert-driven flavor with counselor-generated options. The client's pushback ('we're jumping straight to steps?') suggested proposals occasionally outpaced readiness.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong conditions for change talk produced notable self-motivating statements about spending and 'solving the symptom.' Pivotal moments were rarely deepened or reflected, moving instead to new options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frequent sustain talk about identity and belonging was acknowledged but often met with menus rather than strategic reframing or double-sided reflections. Autonomy-affirming language softened resistance somewhat, but redirection was inconsistent.
