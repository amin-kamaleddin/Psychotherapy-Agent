# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard. Opening affirmation ("it took some real commitment") and explicit permission for all feelings ("it's okay to feel however you feel"). Normalized overwhelm without criticizing resistance, minimization, or sustain talk; remained warm even at "I'm not promising anything."

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Excellent reflective listening throughout. Accurately mirrored core burden ("diabetes landed on your plate uninvited"). Double-sided reflections captured ambivalence precisely (dad's history AND the "little voice"), and subtle cues (father's feet, leg tingling) were picked up and connected to unspoken worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4 / 5
**Comments:** Good focus moving progressively from engagement to a concrete actionable step (soda swap with environmental cue). Point deducted: early exploration of overwhelm and the wife's nagging lingered without a bridging summary, and the session lacked a closing summary confirming next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Session operated between equal partners. Explicitly disclaimed the expert role ("I'm not here to add to your list. You're the expert on your own life"). Final plan (water bottles by the desk) was entirely Jonathan's idea, co-constructed through invitation rather than imposed advice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of ownership: "on your terms rather than the doctor's sheet," "You don't have to have it figured out today," "you know your routine better than anyone." Accepted limits (diet, no promises) without pressure, honoring what stayed off the table.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from Jonathan, not supplied. Open questions elicited intrinsic concerns (father's suffering, leg tingling, dislike of mindless habits), then developed change talk into ability and commitment language. No advice-giving; every reason for change originated with the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing. Explicit partnership language ("You're the expert on your own life"). All substantive ideas—the morning check, the soda swap, the Sunday-night bottle strategy—came from Jonathan. Asked rather than told throughout, and his contributions shaped the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 5 / 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation. "What would make it feel doable rather than exhausting?" produced Jonathan's own vision; questions about the "little voice" elicited genuine concern. Sustained commitment talk followed ("maybe swapping one thing... the soda"), culminating in a concrete plan with a specific trigger and timing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ 4 / 5
**Comments:** Effective double-sided reflections acknowledging sustain talk without amplifying it, then pivoting toward change. Did not reinforce diet resistance or "I'm not my dad" minimization. Point deducted: some reflections restated sustain talk at length (e.g., "easier to just push it aside"), and limited explicit affirmation redirected its legitimacy.
