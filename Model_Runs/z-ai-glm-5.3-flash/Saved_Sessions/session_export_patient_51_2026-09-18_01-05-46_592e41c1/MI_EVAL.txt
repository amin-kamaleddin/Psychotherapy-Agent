# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with genuine affirmations of Ingrid's judgment and instinct. Deducted for repetitive, formulaic affirmations and repeatedly failing to let her leave despite four farewell signals.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured her situation, preferences, and emotional reactions at key moments. However, her alarm and sense of betrayed trust about the medication interaction were only briefly reflected before pivoting to logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Once the agenda was set, focus on safe medication use was exceptionally tight, moving clearly toward concrete action. The opening question was broader than her stated need, and the session lost directional discipline with an unnecessarily extended close.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Agenda was negotiated, information was provided responsively to her request, and the plan was co-constructed and confirmed. The closing exchanges abandoned collaboration, becoming unilateral affirmations delivered to a passive client trying to exit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong ownership language consistently credited Ingrid's own judgment, and final decisions were appropriately deferred to her and her prescriber. Clinically necessary directives were rarely paired with explicit choice language, and overriding her repeated goodbyes undermined her control over the session's ending.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited her motivation and action plan in her own words at the critical moment. Evocation stayed at the surface of the immediate task, missing opportunities to explore deeper values, her self-advocacy instinct, and confidence in follow-through.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ingrid was positioned as the expert on her own life, and her answers shaped the session agenda and plan throughout. Partnership degraded in the closing phase, where one-sided affirmations made her a passive audience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The well-placed question 'What feels most important for you to say when you call them?' produced a detailed client-generated action plan, and her strong commitment language was reinforced. Much change talk flowed naturally from the safety information, with no exploration of confidence or barriers to follow-through.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully reframed her time constraints ('a couple of focused minutes can be worth more than an hour of guessing') and never reinforced her barriers. Minimal sustain talk limited the test of the skill, and her call-shortening tendency was left for her to solve alone while the counselor's long closing monologues added to the very time pressure he had validated.
