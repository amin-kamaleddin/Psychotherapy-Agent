# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm and nonjudgmental throughout, with affirmations of Derrick's capabilities and no lecturing. Falls short of a 5 as affirmations occasionally feel formulaic and his ambivalence isn't normalized with the same warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing nuanced content like his discomfort with blackouts and the friends dilemma. The final summary reflects Derrick's reframing without correcting it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tethered to drinking, driving risk, and DUI consequences, building toward a concrete plan. Falls short because no explicit target behavior is contracted at the outset, leaving the focus slightly diffuse between 'legal situation' and drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative — Derrick's ideas drive the plan and the clinician builds through questions rather than telling. However, the clinician shares almost none of his own clinical perspective (e.g., BAC risk), making the exchange one-directional in elicitation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly frames everything as Derrick's choice and never advises or pressures; even the Friday deadline emerges from Derrick's own realization. Ownership of change is fully transferred, as shown by 'the license part, that's mine.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from Derrick through evocative questions, never supplied by the clinician. He spontaneously generates reasons, strategies, and commitment language ('Friday it is... non-negotiable even with myself').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Derrick is positioned as the expert, and the plan originates entirely from him with the clinician amplifying only. His closing 'you get it' and voluntary plan-setting confirm the power-sharing was effective, not superficial.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked with open questions targeting Derrick's own motivations, then reinforced and escalated progressively — from cab money, to the Marcus talk, to a concrete timeline that produced 'Friday it is.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never argued with or reinforced, and the clinician reflects it neutrally before pivoting to ownership. Some sustain talk is restated in summaries, briefly keeping the negative framing in the air rather than pivoting seamlessly.
