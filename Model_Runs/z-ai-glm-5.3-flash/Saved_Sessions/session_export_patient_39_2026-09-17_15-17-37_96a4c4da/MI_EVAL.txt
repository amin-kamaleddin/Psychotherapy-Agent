# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicated unconditional positive regard through consistent affirmations ('That's understandable, and it's brave to want to try') and accepted the client's pushback without defensiveness. No judgment, labeling, or disapproval appeared in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both sides of the client's ambivalence, including his fear of falling and not wanting to be babied. Points were deducted because reflections sometimes summarized rather than deepened exploration of emotional undercurrents like shame and fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained unwavering focus on increasing physical activity, progressively narrowing from exploration to a specific, time-bound commitment (Tuesday 10am hallway walk). The client left with a clear plan and defined success criterion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor framed the work as joint problem-solving and the client's input drove all plan decisions. Long, counselor-generated suggestion menus occasionally made the client feel pressured, prompting his 'Whoa' response.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was offered at every turn, and the counselor immediately adapted when the client set boundaries ('No script needed'). The client's closing realization ('Now I've got a Tuesday plan') confirmed ownership of the change resided with him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently drew out the client's own reasons, solutions, success criteria, and check-in preferences rather than lecturing. The client's self-generated insight about using his legs as an excuse and his commitment to return with 'the real version' demonstrate excellent evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client was positioned as the expert and substantively shaped the session, choosing the day, time, location, success criteria, and check-in format. Rapid-fire option menus at times shifted toward suggesting rather than purely evoking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions, a skillfully used confidence ruler with a follow-up, and probes for the client's own solutions progressively elicited commitments from 'I suppose I could agree to try it once' to 'I'll come back and tell you how it went — the real version.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged briefly and pivoted toward change, but the history of failed attempts was met with quick redirection rather than deliberate double-sided reflections. A more strategic exploration could have deepened resolution of ambivalence.
