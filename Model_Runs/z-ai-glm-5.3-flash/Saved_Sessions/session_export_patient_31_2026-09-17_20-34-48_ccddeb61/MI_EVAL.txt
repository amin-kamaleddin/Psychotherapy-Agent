# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout — no lecturing or disapproval, with explicit promises like 'no nagging, I promise.' David's closing comment ('I came in thinking this'd be a lecture. Wasn't half bad') confirms he experienced acceptance as genuine.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing David's core experience ('that ten minutes of peace that's just yours') and tracking subtle shifts like his first genuine hopefulness. Each reflection deepened the conversation and was confirmed accurate by David's elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking while progressing logically from ambivalence exploration to a concrete, time-bound plan (patch before first coffee, gum in the van, preserved evening cigarettes, casual follow-up). Every turn built momentum toward change without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative between equals — David's own insights (coffee trigger, safety-net follow-up, Friday start) shaped the session's direction. The counselor invited his ideas at every turn and accommodated his negotiation of a casual check-in arrangement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy robustly supported with explicit permission to avoid all-or-nothing thinking and genuine choice about follow-up. Information (21mg patch) was offered without prescribing, and David left feeling full ownership of both the plan and any future quit decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within David rather than supplied, with evocative questions eliciting his own concerns, reasons, and progressively stronger commitment language. David volunteered unprompted future-oriented change talk ('maybe those evening ones end up getting the chop too one day').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned expertise as residing within David ('you know yourself best', 'you did the hard thinking here, not me'). David's contributions visibly shaped the session while the counselor acted as a resource rather than a directing expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked desire, ability, reasons, and commitment through targeted questions ('What would your girlfriend notice first?'), moving David from 'I don't really wanna quit' to concrete commitment. Change talk was consistently mirrored and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk and pivoted effectively toward change, though evening cigarettes were affirmed at length as 'yours' and 'your real break' at times. Slightly more active reframing or double-sided reflections would have shifted focus away from reasons for maintaining the status quo sooner.
