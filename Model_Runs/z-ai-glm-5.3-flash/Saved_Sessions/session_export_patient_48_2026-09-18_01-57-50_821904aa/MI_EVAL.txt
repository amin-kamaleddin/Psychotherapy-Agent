# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with validating reflections like 'That's a heavy load to carry' and 'Both are worth listening to.' Falls short of 5 only because explicit affirmations of Farid as a person are somewhat limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that names emotional weight and captures ambivalence accurately. The vulnerable self-doubt layer ('I don't know if I'm really being lazy') is touched but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on increasing physical activity and channels conversation toward concrete steps. Occasional broad questions were kept on track partly by the client's cooperative responses rather than deliberate steering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided exchanges with reflections followed by open questions handing the agenda to the client. Ambivalence is treated as legitimate and the interaction reads as an equal partnership co-constructing meaning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy strongly reinforced with no persuading, convincing, or should-language anywhere. The plan is entirely Farid's, framed as his choice with emphasis on 'small' starts protecting against imposed agendas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicits rather than supplies motivation, drawing out Farid's own reasons and ideas through open questions. Evocation stays at the level of good open questions without deeper layered development of personal meaning or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook power-sharing: expertise explicitly located in the client ('You know your schedule and your life better than anyone'). Session direction is shaped entirely by Farid's own ideas with no prescriptive advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evokes and amplifies change talk, strategically reflecting statements like 'My health does matter to me' back to the client. To reach 5, deeper follow-up (confidence scaling, elaboration on why health matters now) was needed before pivoting to barriers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without amplification via well-constructed double-sided reflections. A more explicit reframe (e.g., reframing 'laziness' as exhaustion) would have loosened the sustain talk itself.
