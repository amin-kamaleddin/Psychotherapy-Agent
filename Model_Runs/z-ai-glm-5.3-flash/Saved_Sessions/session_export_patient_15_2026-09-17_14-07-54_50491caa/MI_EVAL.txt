# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with no labeling of drinking. However, regard feels procedural — generic affirmations ('well done') and canned recaps rather than warm, explicit positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is nearly absent; the only partial reflection ('I hear your doubt about family history') is immediately countered with psychoeducation. Andrei's meaningful disclosure about the after-dinner wine routine with his wife was met with a generic strategy list rather than empathic understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly and coherently focused on hypertension and alcohol reduction with a structured flow. However, focus was maintained prescriptively rather than through guiding toward internally motivated change, so buy-in remained shallow.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is largely clinician-directed: the psychiatrist supplies the agenda, plan, and strategies in checklist form while Andrei passively complies. No joint brainstorming — the wine ritual was answered with a list of changes rather than exploring options together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine gestures include permission-seeking, offering a menu of options, and inviting choice of start day and swaps. The core plan is still delivered as directives, and push-back is met with persuasion rather than affirming his right to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Essentially no evocation occurred — no questions about what matters to Andrei, his reasons for change, or his confidence. The session's engine is external education and a prescribed plan, with Andrei's own motivations surfacing spontaneously but never developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positioned himself as the expert delivering a pre-packaged plan, and Andrei's passivity ('So, what do you suggest?') reflects never being invited into shared expertise. Advice was delivered without first asking Andrei's own ideas about what might work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Andrei offered natural change talk (quitting smoking years ago, agreeing to one glass and a Monday start), but it was never elicited or amplified. Closed questions dominated, and the smoking-cessation history was a major missed evocation opportunity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk was frequent ('That's normal, isn't it?', 'probably in the family') and was not reinforced but was also not softened — responses were corrective or generically reassuring. No double-sided reflections or reframing of his smoking cessation as evidence of capability, and ambivalence remained unresolved at session close.
