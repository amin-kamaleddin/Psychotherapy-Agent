# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, defusing Erik's expected criticism by normalizing his resentment. Affirmations were present but deeper individualized regard was underused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections tracked Erik's ambivalence well (routine attachment vs. complication fears). Reflections became sparse later as the session shifted to logistics, missing emotional content like fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical focus on alcohol reduction and diabetes management from engagement through to a concrete plan with safety parameters. Closing segments became somewhat checklist-like rather than tying back to personal motivations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions framed as shared, with Erik's input genuinely shaping the plan and an egalitarian tone ('haven't lectured me'). Safety thresholds and monitoring instructions were delivered didactically without checking existing knowledge first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced choice with permission-seeking and honored Erik's decision to focus on drinking only. Early option menus were somewhat counselor-generated rather than drawn from the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions effectively drew out Erik's own motivations (uncle's complications) and self-generated plans. Evocation tapered in the final third, replaced by counselor-driven problem-solving.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik shaped the plan (delay strategy, phone notes), but the counselor frequently supplied menus and structure in information-delivery mode. Erik's expertise, like quitting smoking via social accountability, was surfaced but not leveraged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Importance/confidence rulers and open questions elicited genuine change talk ('don't want to end up in a wheelchair or blind like my uncle'). Change talk was not consistently deepened once planning began.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally without reinforcement, and smaller steps were respected rather than pushed. Sometimes merely acknowledged rather than actively reframed with double-sided reflections.
