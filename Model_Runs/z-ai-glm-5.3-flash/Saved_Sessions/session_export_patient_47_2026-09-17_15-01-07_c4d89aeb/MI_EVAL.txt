# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with validation of Evelyn's defensiveness ('that makes sense'). However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic and there's a missed opportunity to explicitly affirm her honesty and courage in acknowledging the fall and her son's worry.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening accurately captures Evelyn's ambivalence between her routine and the fall/Danny's concerns. Emotional nuances like pride and fear of losing independence are present but only partially explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol-related fall risk with no drift. The conversation progresses logically from exploration to a concrete, specific plan and scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently asks permission before advising and co-constructs the follow-up plan around Evelyn's preferences. Falls short of full collaboration because the counselor dominates option generation and poses most agenda items.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly frames change as Evelyn's choice and honors her declines of suggestions without pushing. However, menus of options subtly direct, and explicit affirmation of her ownership of the plan is absent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses the confidence ruler and open questions to elicit Evelyn's own reasoning and motivations. But the counselor frequently supplies the reasons and solutions rather than consistently drawing them out, and deeper exploration of her values (independence, self-sufficiency) is largely absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Seeks Evelyn's input at key junctures, but turns are often list-heavy with counselor-supplied ideas rather than her own solutions. Risks an expert-providing-choices stance rather than positioning Evelyn as the primary source of expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits change talk through open questions, the confidence ruler, and reinforcement of commitments. Could have deepened desire/need language (e.g., 'What makes this worth it for you?') before moving to planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with minimization by using permission-seeking and pivoting to what Evelyn values. However, sustain talk is sometimes left standing without double-sided reflections, and information-giving slightly invites counter-argument.
