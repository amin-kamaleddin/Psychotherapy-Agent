# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with genuine affirmations of the client's strengths and past success. However, affirmations occasionally feel formulaic and are often paired immediately with advice, slightly diluting depth of acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on that accurately captures ambivalence and emotional strain. Reflections become brief lead-ins to advice later, limiting exploration of the shame and exhaustion beneath her worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains unwavering focus on managing co-parenting conflict away from the son, with every exchange building toward a concrete plan. No topic drift, concluding with a well-defined, actionable safety plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed, with the client supplying essential ingredients (kitchen, sister, cue, wording). Power balance tips early when the counselor pre-empts idea generation with tactic lists, though collaboration strengthens as the session progresses.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly reinforces the client's choice through option menus and accepts her tweaks without pushback. However, she is mostly choosing from the counselor's menu rather than generating her own options, and ownership could be more explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some self-motivational statements through good scaling questions, but often supplies reasons and tactics rather than drawing them from the client. Missed opportunities include exploring why protecting her son matters and what success would look like.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Explicitly positions the client as decision-maker and her experience shapes the plan, but frequently assumes the expert role with pre-packaged tactic lists before eliciting her own ideas. Asking 'What have you tried?' first would strengthen power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicits change talk through well-placed questions and confidence scaling ('I know I can do it,' 'My son's worth a 7'). However, strong commitment statements are met with a quick pivot to suggestions rather than evocative reflections that deepen the change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Doesn't reinforce sustain talk and briefly normalizes the freeze response, but never uses double-sided reflections to pair doubts with the client's change talk. In one instance, plan-giving effectively talks over her expressed worry rather than exploring it.
