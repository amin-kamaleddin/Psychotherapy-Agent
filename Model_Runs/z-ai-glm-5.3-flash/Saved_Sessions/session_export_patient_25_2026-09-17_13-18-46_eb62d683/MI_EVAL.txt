# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with repeated genuine affirmation of Daniel's smoking cessation and his plan. Praise is somewhat formulaic, and deeper emotional meanings of his achievements and parenting stress go unexplored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A few decent early reflections ('sounds exhausting') and validation of ambivalence, but most responses leap into advice and question checklists without reflecting back. Key emotional disclosures — wife's unhappiness, fatherhood fears about drinking creeping up — are never reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Once alcohol was identified, the session stayed consistently focused on limits, tracking, refusal lines, and follow-up, with appropriate redirection after brief drifts. Stronger agenda-setting and linking the plan to his values (fatherhood) would improve this.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The plan incorporated Daniel's feedback (walking vs. driving line, skipping the 7-day plan, self-chosen reward) with a negotiated follow-up. However, the counselor mostly delivered monologue-style expert content rather than co-constructing the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choice-offering early and permission to skip the 7-day plan, but large volumes of prescriptive advice were delivered unasked. No explicit affirmations that decisions belong to Daniel, and the counselor controlled much of the agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely asserted by the counselor ('you've got this') rather than elicited from the client. Almost no open-ended evocative questions appeared, and Daniel's spontaneous change talk was met with tips rather than development.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel's input shaped some plan details, but the overall stance was expert-driven with unsolicited advice bundles and templates. His own expertise (what worked when quitting smoking) was never explicitly drawn upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good goal questions elicited meaningful change talk ('I don't want it creeping up, especially now I'm a dad'). But this talk was never deepened or reinforced with reflections — the counselor pivoted to strategy lists instead, and no confidence or scaling questions were used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mild sustain talk ('it's just the baby phase,' 'not a huge deal,' 'let's not get ahead of ourselves') was largely ignored while pivoting to strategies. No double-sided reflections or reframing were used to resolve ambivalence — a neutral, missed-opportunity approach.
