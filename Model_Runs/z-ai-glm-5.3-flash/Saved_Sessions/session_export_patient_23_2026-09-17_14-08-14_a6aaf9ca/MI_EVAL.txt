# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'that takes honesty' and 'well done.' However, praise is formulaic and lacks deep, individualized affirmation of Claudia's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some decent reflections, but empathy is inconsistent and often overshadowed by information delivery. Key emotional moments — Claudia's shock at the guidelines, weekend low mood, and fear of failure — went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongest domain: the session stays tightly focused on alcohol reduction with no drift, moving from assessment to a concrete, measurable plan (two drinks/night, Tue/Thu alcohol-free, tracking, Friday check-in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration at key junctures (Claudia chooses the goal, nights, check-in day; her strategies are incorporated), but large stretches are monologue-like advice lists delivered with minimal client input.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers menus and asks permission, and accepts Claudia's partial goal without overriding it. Falls short because choices are clinician-generated options rather than reinforcing her ownership, and her final fear-of-failure question goes unaddressed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good evocative moves like the confidence rating and accepting the self-set goal, but the psychiatrist often answers their own questions with information and supplies reasons for change rather than eliciting Claudia's. Deeper questions (why does this matter now?) are absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A consultative stance with repeated permission-asking and genuine incorporation of Claudia's ideas, but the expertise flow is heavily clinician-directed with pre-packaged strategy lists rather than co-created plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerges, partly via the confidence-rating question, but it is rarely reflected back or deepened — ideal moments like 'I do actually want to feel better' pivot to logistics instead of evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk appears repeatedly ('not ready for seven,' weekends are tricky) but is merely tolerated, with no double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic redirection. When seven feels like 'a big jump,' the psychiatrist moves straight to a smaller goal without exploring ambivalence.
