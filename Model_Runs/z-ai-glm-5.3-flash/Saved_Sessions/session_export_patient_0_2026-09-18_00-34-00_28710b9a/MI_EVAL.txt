# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician unconditionally accepts the patient's ambivalence, explicitly affirming he doesn't have to quit today and honoring his autonomy ('that's your call entirely'). The patient's reluctance and slow movement are met with respect rather than pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is frequent and accurate, capturing both sides of ambivalence ('caught between two things... both can be true'). The clinician consistently reflects meaning beneath surface statements, e.g., sitting with 'habit at this point.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Each response builds toward change talk — from 'maybe I don't need it every weekend' to a concrete plan and openness to telling Marcus — while following the patient's lead rather than pushing an agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly positions the patient as the expert ('You're the expert here') and asks rather than tells. The plan emerges entirely from the patient's own ideas and resourcefulness.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and self-direction are emphasized throughout: 'on your terms,' 'not promising anything, though,' and 'if you ever chose to say something.' No commands or directives are issued.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully draws out the patient's own motivations — the misery of the infection, desire for 'fun without hospital crap,' and wish to not hide — using open questions that elicit sustained and mobilizing change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange is genuinely collaborative, with the clinician following the patient's agenda (friends, disclosure to Marcus) and co-constructing next steps rather than prescribing them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is consistently elicited and reinforced: 'this whole thing has got me thinking,' 'maybe skipping one weekend,' and 'it's the first time it hasn't sounded completely terrible.' The clinician highlights these shifts without overstepping.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('not saying I'm quitting,' 'failing in front of someone') is met with double-sided reflections and normalization rather than argument. The clinician rolls with resistance, e.g., accepting 'not promising anything' and reframing it as openness.
