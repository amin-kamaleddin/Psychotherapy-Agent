# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations ('that's a brave step', 'that was a great first try') and normalization of nerves. Kept from 5 by warmth being brief and task-focused rather than expressing deeper unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate understanding shown, but reflections are sparse and generic. Missed opportunities to reflect deeper feelings — shame about broken promises, fear of self-sabotage, and stomach-pain anxiety — often jumping to planning instead.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on community engagement throughout with no topic drift. The session builds progressively to a concrete, time-specific commitment (message today, Thursday at 10, backup plan).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Inclusive language ('let's pick one step together') and check-ins before advancing, with plans adjusted to her feedback. However, the counselor designs frameworks while Isabella mostly ratifies them rather than truly co-constructing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirms choice — leave anytime, no pressure if she doesn't go, backup plans normalize imperfection. Rapid menu-presentation gently steered toward counselor-preferred options, slightly outpacing her hesitant style.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine elicitation (her own script wording, doability questions), but motivation is largely drawn through counselor-generated options. Little exploration of her own values, reasons, or hopes, so evocation stays surface-level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Isabella as decision-maker, and her choices genuinely shaped the session (message selection, backup plan). Counselor still generates most content, with Isabella selecting from menus rather than authoring solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective progression from 'Yes, I guess' to concrete commitments (Thursday at 10 'before I can change my mind', sending the message) plus self-affirming language by the end. Change talk driven partly by counselor options rather than freely evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Doesn't amplify sustain talk and provides helpful safety nets (exit cue, backup message), but rarely uses double-sided reflections or pivots ambivalence toward change. Responses tend to skip past ambivalence with more planning steps.
