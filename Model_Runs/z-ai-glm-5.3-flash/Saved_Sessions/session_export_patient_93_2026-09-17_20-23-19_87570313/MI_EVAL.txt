# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with frequent individualized affirmations (e.g., 'There's real wisdom in that'). No judgment or disapproval anywhere, even when the patient minimized her drinking or dismissed her son.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including an accurate double-sided opening reflection and emotionally attuned responses to the fall. Some reflections stayed at content level, missing deeper emotional themes like retirement loneliness ('Retired life does get a bit samey').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on reducing home drinking with logical progression from ambivalence exploration to a concrete, time-bound first step (bottle in cupboard, tea tomorrow night). Side topics (Tom, Dorothy, pub) were consistently folded back into the change plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely a joint enterprise—the entire plan was co-developed from Margaret's own ideas with teamwork-oriented language. However, a redundant closing question about the first step (already answered) briefly disrupted attunement, and the agenda was never fully negotiated at the start.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced the patient's ownership with phrases like 'on your terms' and 'this isn't about quitting.' All plan elements originated from Margaret, and the choice of first step was explicitly left to her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reliably drew motivation from within, eliciting self-motivational statements ('Because it's mine, I suppose') and evoking discrepancy between habit drinking and her values. Did not use confidence/importance scaling or explicit commitment-strengthening techniques that would have deepened evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned Margaret as the expert and repeatedly asked for her ideas rather than prescribing. A redundant question about the first step—asked right after she had already stated it—revealed a moment of not fully tracking the client's contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently evoked and deepened change talk through open questions ('What do you think would feel like a sensible change for you?') and barrier exploration. Did not explicitly elicit confidence or importance ratings, and one late question ('Which part are you most curious or nervous about?') added little strategic value.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk skillfully, especially pivoting away from abstinence framing ('this isn't about quitting') after 'I'd never give up my Friday nights.' However, markers like 'I'm not made of willpower at my age' and 'I'm not promising anything grand' were left without affirming reframes.
