# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirmations, but they are generic and formulaic, lacking explicit unconditional positive regard beyond praising her competence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections occur, but the session is dominated by information delivery; Alessia's expressed anxiety about the near-miss drug interaction is never explicitly reflected or validated emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on safe medication management of the MAOI–SSRI interaction, staying on safety education and preparation for the psychiatrist call; limited only by following a concrete educational task rather than the client's broader values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The role-play and script drafting were genuinely interactive, but the overall structure was expert-to-patient education with long counselor monologues and a counselor-set agenda rather than true co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong support for Alessia's choices through options, deferring decisions to her psychiatrist, and appropriate safety-critical directives; falls short of 5 because ownership of the change process is implicit rather than explicit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and plans came almost entirely from counselor-supplied information and scripts; the counselor never elicited Alessia's own reasons, confidence, or ideas for next steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor positioned himself as the expert delivering didactic information, with session direction largely counselor-driven and Alessia's expertise in her own care acknowledged only implicitly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no deliberate effort to evoke or deepen commitment language; Alessia's change-oriented statements were spontaneous and went unreflected and unamplified by the counselor.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not inadvertently reinforced, but the counselor did not engage with ambivalence at all — no double-sided reflections or strategic pivoting were used.
