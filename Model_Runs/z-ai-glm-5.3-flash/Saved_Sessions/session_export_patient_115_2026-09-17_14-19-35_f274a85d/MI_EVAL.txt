# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with strong affirmations like 'cutting from 20 to 10 shows effort' and validation of harm reduction. However, warmth is somewhat sparse, and opportunities to affirm deeper values (e.g., seeing his kids grow up) were missed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens with an accurate, caring reflection of Samir's frustration, but reflective listening largely disappears as the session shifts into advice/checklist mode. Emotionally rich material — fear of judgment, the effort behind cutting down, wanting to see his kids grow up — is never reflected or deepened.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, steady focus on asthma and smoking/lifestyle throughout, building logically toward a concrete plan. Direction is pursued through the counselor's own agenda and information delivery rather than helping Samir articulate his own direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is functionally unequal: the counselor provides, the client receives, with pre-assembled lists offered wholesale rather than built jointly. Samir's own plan is endorsed but then substituted with the counselor's materials rather than integrated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms choice — 'gradual change is valid,' opening menus of options, declining to pressure when asked, and offering choices at decision points. Falls short by not reinforcing Samir's ownership during his moments of doubt, and solutions are largely counselor-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little active effort to draw motivation from within Samir — no open questions about reasons, importance, or confidence. The counselor's primary mode is provision of information and tools, and Samir's spontaneous change talk is never developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and pacing respect are present, but most contributions are one-directional expert content — bulleted lists, pre-written scripts, and a handed-over tracker. Samir's own ideas rarely shape the session content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Samir generates meaningful change talk on his own and the counselor validates it well. However, there is little deliberate evocation — no questions about why quitting matters, confidence levels, or what success looks like — leaning on information delivery instead of deepening the client's own motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids amplifying sustain talk by reframing reduction as legitimate progress and normalizing harm reduction without arguing for total quitting. The main weakness is responding to ambivalence with information rather than reflective pivots toward change.
