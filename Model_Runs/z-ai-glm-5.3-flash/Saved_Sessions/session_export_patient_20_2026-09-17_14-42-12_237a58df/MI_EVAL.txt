# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, from the nonjudgmental opening to the warm closing, never pathologizing Brandon's minimization or guarded humor. The client's final thanks for 'not lecturing me like everyone else' confirms he experienced genuine acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured Brandon's ambivalence well (e.g., valuing relaxation while noticing sleep and mood costs). However, explicit emotional reflection was limited—the counselor did not explore his irritation at being nagged, work stress, or the deeper meaning of wanting to prove something to himself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, consistent focus on reducing weeknight drinking from the first exchange through concrete planning (timer, start night, fallbacks, Friday check-in). Every turn tied back to the target behavior with no drift, progressing logically through ambivalence exploration, change talk, and planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were genuinely co-constructed from Brandon's own insights, with the counselor checking agreement at each step and fully incorporating his final tweak. The client's 'I came in ready to argue and instead I've got a plan' confirms success, though rapid multi-question turns occasionally limited true back-and-forth dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor respected autonomy by asking permission, offering choices rather than prescriptions, and accepting Brandon's limits ('not touching weekends') without argument. The score falls short of 5 because some solutions (tactic menu, text-to-Mike script) were counselor-generated before Brandon had the chance to generate them.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently drew motivation from the client—eliciting both sides of ambivalence, using the confidence ruler effectively, and drawing out Brandon's own reasons for change. Evocation was strong early but shifted toward logistics and planning later rather than deepening the personal meaning of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing was evident through permission-seeking, choice-offering, and letting Brandon's ideas shape the plan (his 'shower first' insight and fancy-beer tweak). The score falls short of 5 because the counselor introduced the tactic menu fairly quickly and sometimes front-loaded multiple questions at once.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled use of open questions and a readiness ruler elicited genuine commitment language ('Starting tonight, why wait,' 'That I can do'), and confidence rose from 4-5 to 6 during the session. The counselor could have more deliberately amplified Brandon's strongest change talk, such as his desire to show his sister and himself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (unwinding, social connection, weekends off-limits) was handled with neutral, double-sided reflections without argument. However, the counselor missed opportunities to strategically pivot sustain talk toward change—for example, after 'I'm not promising miracles'—moving instead to logistics.
