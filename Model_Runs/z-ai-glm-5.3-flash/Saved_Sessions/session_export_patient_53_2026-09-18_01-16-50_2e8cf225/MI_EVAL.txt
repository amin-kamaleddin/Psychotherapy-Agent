# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed Isabella's autonomy, repeatedly validating that she didn't have to commit to anything and that her hesitations made sense. Affirmations like 'that's real wisdom, not just hesitation' reinforced her worth without conditional approval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were deeply attuned, capturing both sides of her ambivalence ('that push and pull') and specific fears like being 'the awkward one.' The clinician consistently communicated understanding of her internal experience, including her anxiety about feeling trapped.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintained a clear focus on exploring conditions under which social connection might feel doable, without drifting or lecturing. Each exchange built progressively toward her self-generated plan of coffee with one coworker.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly positioned Isabella as 'the expert here' and invited her to design 'your version from scratch.' The plan was entirely hers, contrasting with past experiences of 'failing at someone else's version.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No directives or pressure were used; instead, the clinician repeatedly emphasized 'no promises,' 'your pace,' and 'you're the expert.' Isabella's increased motivation emerged from ownership rather than external persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicited change talk through questions like 'was there ever a time when stepping out of your comfort zone turned out okay?' and 'what would make it worth trying?' Isabella's own words — 'less impossible,' 'I kind of want to try' — demonstrate strong evocation of her motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange was a genuine dialogue, with the clinician building on Isabella's words and asking open questions about her insights ('what do you think made the difference?'). Decisions and language choices were consistently returned to her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician noticed and amplified every instance of change talk, reflecting 'if I did, those things would need to happen' as 'not a no.' Questions about past successes and conditions for success drew out sustained, self-motivated DARN statements culminating in commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'd probably chicken out,' 'no promises') was consistently accepted and reframed rather than argued against. The clinician normalized her hesitations, allowing ambivalence to coexist with change ('both of those can sit together').
