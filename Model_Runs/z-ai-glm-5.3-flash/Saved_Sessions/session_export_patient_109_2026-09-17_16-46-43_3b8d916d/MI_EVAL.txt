# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout. Effective affirmations ("Good thinking," "That's a practical plan," "You've got this"). Inactivity normalized without criticism; ambivalence accepted ("celebrate or troubleshoot if the couch wins"). Minor weakness: praise is somewhat formulaic and plan-focused rather than person-focused.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflective efforts ("exhaustion after work plus going alone are the key barriers"), confirmed by the patient. However, reflections remain surface-level and cognitive; emotional undertones (discouragement, shame about the unused membership, tendency to "dig in") were not reflected.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session tightly focused on returning to the gym; every turn advanced the plan (barriers, options, texting, bag, reminder, backup day, check-in). Specific, measurable commitment achieved (Tuesday 6:30, 10 minutes). Deduction: opening turn overloaded with four questions at once rather than paced.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong co-construction — Rafael designed his own hybrid plan; counselor asked what nudge would "feel supportive" and offered rather than prescribed. Patient explicitly validated the approach ("Appreciate you not making this into some big overhaul thing"). Slight deduction: counselor supplied the two-option set rather than eliciting options first.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent. Choices consistently framed as Rafael's ("would you be open to that?", "Which feels more doable, or is there another tiny step you'd prefer?"). Declined drafts accepted without pushing; "celebrate or troubleshoot" framing presumes neither success nor failure. Patient noted the plan fits him — genuine autonomy respect.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers for importance and confidence, questions about past success, barriers, and coping all elicited patient-generated reasons and solutions ("stop deciding, just start"). Incomplete: never explored why importance was a 6 rather than lower/higher; offered coping strategies (reminder templates, spouse nudges) rather than fully drawing them from the patient.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Patient treated as expert on his own life — asked what derailed him, what reminder style felt supportive, what he wanted from the check-in. Rafael's contributions (hybrid plan, wife-as-nudge, "shoes on" trick) substantially shaped the final plan. Close to a 5.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Importance/confidence rulers, past-success questions, and concrete follow-through questions generated clear change talk ("I could try it this week," "I'll give it a real shot"). Missed opportunities to deepen: rulers not followed with "why a 6 and not a 3?" and change talk was restated rather than strategically reflected.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ("the couch wins," "work just wipes me out") handled acceptably but not skillfully. No double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence; doubts ("what's the point of ten minutes?") not addressed directly — the patient resolved them himself. Adequate redirection but lacks deliberate technique.

---

### Overall Global Rating: **4.1/5**

Strong, well-structured MI session with excellent autonomy support and direction. Patient left with a self-generated, concrete plan and explicit change talk. Key growth areas: deepen emotional reflections, use rulers strategically ("why not lower?"), actively soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections, pace the opening turn, and reinforce change talk when it occurs.
