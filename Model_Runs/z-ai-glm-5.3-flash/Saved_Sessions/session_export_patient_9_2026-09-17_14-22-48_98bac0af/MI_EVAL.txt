# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with encouraging affirmations. However, regard is somewhat formulaic and does not deeply normalize her failed attempts or her fear of being 'the failure' she mentions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One strong early reflection on exhaustion and isolation, but the counselor then shifts to problem-solving. Emotional disclosures (feeling like nothing sticks, being 'the strict one,' feeling like a failure) go unreflected and unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with a menu of options early on and concrete progress toward action. Direction is achieved through fast task-completion rather than anchoring to the client's values, and emotional threads are dropped for next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration via the opening menu and invitations for input. The session frequently becomes advice delivery (messages, scripts, charts) handed over by the counselor, leaving the client receiving rather than co-creating a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is offered repeatedly and decisions are consistently left with the client. Autonomy is mainly over counselor-generated options, with little reinforcement of her ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor rarely draws out the client's own reasons, abilities, or hopes; her spontaneously expressed values ('chances I didn't') are never explored or affirmed. Solutions are supplied instead of evoking her motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role, prescribing wording, scripts, fallback plans, and chart designs. The client's contributions fill in a pre-set framework rather than shaping the session, and her expertise about her son and ex-partner is not credited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change language comes almost entirely from the client but is rarely evoked or deepened. The counselor responds to change talk with checklists and drafted scripts instead of exploring motivations, hopes, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately does not reinforce pessimism, but moments of doubt ('might just ignore it,' 'is it even worth it') are met with problem-solving rather than double-sided reflections or reframing to her values. Sustain talk is largely bypassed.
