# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, with some early validation; patient explicitly appreciated the absence of lecturing. However, affirmations were formulaic and few individualized strengths were acknowledged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections about her fears and desire for normalcy, but reflections were shallow and infrequent relative to the volume of instructions. Meaningful disclosures (mother's complications, 'planning around being sick') went largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on medication adherence and secured a concrete, achievable plan with follow-up. Direction was achieved largely through premature advice-giving rather than guiding her own goal clarification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Polite interaction with some power sharing (choice of options, agreeing to her trial plan), but the style was expert-driven with ready-made checklists. Her own ideas (kettle placement) surfaced despite, not because of, the counselor's approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered options and accommodated her preference to keep things simple, but frequent directive instructions ('set your alarm now,' 'put your phone across the room') felt controlling. Patient twice had to push back to maintain her own plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied almost entirely on providing information rather than drawing out the patient's own motivations. Her strongest motivators — her kids and her mother's story — emerged spontaneously but were never elicited, deepened, or reflected.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine choice ('which feels easiest to try?') and respect for her decision to go solo for a week, but nearly every turn ended in prescriptive instructions. Her expertise about her own routines was rarely solicited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Patient offered rich spontaneous change talk ('I want to stay healthy for my kids,' 'part of me wants to prove I can do this'), but the counselor did nothing to elicit, deepen, or reinforce it. No open questions about her own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify or reinforce sustain talk, which is a strength. However, minimization ('it's not a big deal,' 'I feel fine') was met with immediate problem-solving rather than double-sided reflections linking ambivalence to her values.
