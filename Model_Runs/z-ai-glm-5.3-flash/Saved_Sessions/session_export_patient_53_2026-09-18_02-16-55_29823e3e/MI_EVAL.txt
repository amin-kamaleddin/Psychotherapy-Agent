# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with specific, individualized affirmations. Her self-criticism ('It's silly') was neither colluded with nor punished, but a brief explicit reframe/normalization of the self-judgment would have deepened unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is a clear strength — accurate double-sided and complex reflections that track her micro-shifts in language. The truncated evaluation suggests minor gaps, but demonstrated empathy is consistently precise and individualized.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Questions and reflections consistently steer toward the client's own goals (drawing again, feeling less stuck) without imposing an agenda. Focus stays on achievable change while respecting her pace and autonomy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is honored throughout: 'You know yourself best here,' 'you're the expert on you,' and soliciting her own success criteria. Plans are co-constructed and drawn from her own ideas (ten-minute rule, sketchbook setup).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client owns every decision — solo drawing 'for now,' Saturday morning timing, her own definition of success. The counselor consistently elicits rather than prescribes, and explicitly avoids pressure ('You don't need to promise anything').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong use of open questions to evoke her own reasons for change ('What would it mean to you... about the kind of person you're becoming?') and her self-generated coping plan. Evocation is thorough but occasionally relies on leading phrasing when exploring next steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent 'side-by-side' stance — curiosity-oriented questions ('I'm curious what you think'), shared exploration of barriers, and joint problem-solving. The client actively participates in shaping the plan and reflects that being listened to made the difference.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is actively elicited and reflected back, with skilled highlighting of her shift from 'nothing really' to concrete commitment. Sustaining talk ('maybe,' 'we'll see') is acknowledged without pushing, though some change talk was left unconsolidated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubts and ambivalence ('that's still too much,' 'not promising anything') are met with validation and normalization rather than challenge. Double-sided reflections gently hold both sides, though direct engagement with sustain talk could occasionally be deeper.
