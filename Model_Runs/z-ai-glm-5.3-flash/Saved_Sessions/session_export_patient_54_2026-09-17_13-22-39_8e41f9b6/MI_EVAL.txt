# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with validating affirmations (e.g., 'You've already cut back — that's a big, important step'). Warmth was somewhat sparse and formulaic, with limited unconditional regard when the client minimized risk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made adequate reflective efforts, but reflections were brief and often followed by psychoeducation or question barrages. Missed opportunities to reflect the emotional core of her ambivalence between family tradition and maternal identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in pregnancy from assessment through planning and behavioral rehearsal, culminating in a concrete commitment. Lost a point for stacking multiple agenda items (safety screening, drinking history, options) into single messages.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed decisions as joint ('Which would you prefer?') and co-constructed tools like the partner handout and role-plays responsive to her requests. Agenda-setting and risk information remained counselor-led, limiting fuller power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A genuine strength — repeatedly affirmed choice, asked permission, and offered options rather than directives, reflected in the client leaving 'with a plan instead' of an argument. Heavy factual confrontation and rapid option menus occasionally pressured toward a predetermined 'quit entirely' conclusion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler and open questions drew out some motivation, but her strongest change talk emerged from her own reflection rather than skillful evocation. Relied on information-giving and pre-formatted option menus instead of deepening her intrinsic motivations (e.g., what being a good mom means to her).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Conveyed some power-sharing and permission-seeking, but occupied the expert role substantially with two extended psychoeducational blocks and largely counselor-generated plan elements. Client contributions shaped the session more despite than because of deliberate power-sharing structures.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk by supporting autonomy and linking to values, yielding substantial commitment language ('what I really want is a healthy baby and to be a good mom') and concrete planning. Did not deepen change talk with follow-up questions about what success or confidence would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Neither amplified nor dwelled on her frequent sustain talk, which is a strength, but countered with repeated facts ('no known safe amount') rather than strategic double-sided reflections. Softening was passive rather than skillful, risking reactance in an autonomy-driven client.
