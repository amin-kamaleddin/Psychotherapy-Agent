# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, from opening with 'I'm here to listen without judgment' to closing with 'setbacks don't erase progress.' No judgment or disapproval anywhere in the session, even when discussing the relapse and Danny.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening accurately captured Elias's shame and concern about internal motivation. Reflections were brief, and the counselor quickly pivoted to exercises and plan-building, missing chances to explore shame, the father comparison, or loneliness more emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on relapse prevention and strengthening internal motivation throughout. Each exchange ended with a concrete next step tied to the target behavior, progressing logically from exploration to a relapse-prevention plan without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elias's contributions substantially shaped the session, including his values becoming the motivation statement and choosing his contacts. However, the counselor often supplied pre-formatted plans and scripts rather than building them jointly line by line.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered choices at every juncture and never pressured or directed. Elias explicitly confirmed ownership: 'This felt like my plan, not yours.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The values exercise effectively elicited deeply personal reasons ('not just for them, but for me') and confidence talk ('I think I can actually do this part'). Evocation was concentrated in that exercise, and Elias's core ambivalence about whether his stopping was truly chosen was left partly unresolved.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Elias's input and let him rewrite the motivation statement in his own words, with Elias noting the plan felt like his own. Slightly tempered because Elias chose among counselor-generated options rather than co-creating from scratch.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Elias's own reasons for change via values clarification, producing strong change talk and a self-authored motivation statement. Missed opportunities to deepen commitment language further, such as explicitly exploring importance/confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged Elias's ambivalence without reinforcing it ('Ambivalence is normal') and redirected toward internal reasons for staying stopped. Some sustain talk was left unexplored rather than strategically reframed with double-sided reflections, but focus stayed on change.
