# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with validation of the patient's framing. However, affirmations like 'That's great' and 'That's a strong plan' were formulaic and lacked individualized depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections showed reasonable understanding but became perfunctory restatements of the plan over time. Emotional opportunities were missed, including the meaning behind the patient's alcohol minimization and his understated openness to revisit it later.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The walking plan was well developed with triggers, backups, and rewards, but the clinically central alcohol–hypertension link was dropped after initial minimization. Even the patient's own conditional opening about alcohol at the end went unaddressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were framed as joint decisions, and the patient's contributions genuinely shaped the final plan. The counselor sometimes pre-empted collaboration by offering ready-made solution lists before eliciting the patient's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient chose his own options and the plan was accepted as-is, but repeated offers of help after explicit refusals ('You don't need to write anything for me') subtly undermined autonomy. Advice-heavy lists early on also tilted toward directing rather than inviting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely presumed rather than evoked, with the counselor supplying reasons and solutions while the patient complied and selected from menus. The patient's own values, ideas, and emerging ambivalence about alcohol were never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient's input shaped the plan details, showing some genuine power sharing. However, the stance drifted toward expert-driven problem-solving, offering unsolicited tips and help three separate times despite clear declines.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some successful evocation drew out commitment language ('Monday it is,' confidence rising to 8), but these moments were not deepened. The patient's tentative change talk about alcohol in the final exchange was left entirely unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor wisely avoided arguing with the patient's minimization, but sustain talk was sidestepped rather than strategically softened. No double-sided reflections were used, and the alcohol topic was dropped instead of gently revisited when the patient opened the door.
