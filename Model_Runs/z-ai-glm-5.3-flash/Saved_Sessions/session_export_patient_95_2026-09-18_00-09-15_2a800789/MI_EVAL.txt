### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician unconditionally valued the patient, affirmed her strengths ('a mess that shows up,' her fight), and remained non-judgmental about her skepticism and ambivalence throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently deep, accurate reflections ('weight pressing down,' 'the door in your head') captured both pain and ambivalence, and the patient explicitly said she felt like 'a person today, not a problem.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation was clearly focused on safety planning and surviving tonight, with the clinician skillfully guiding toward a concrete plan while following the patient's lead.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as the expert ('You're the expert on your nights'), co-constructed the safety plan, and asked permission rather than prescribing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient generated her own coping ideas (staying out of the bathroom, Biscuit, writing the list), and the clinician reinforced that the plan was built by her, respecting every 'maybe' and 'no promises.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Powerful evocation of change talk — drawing out the patient's hesitation, her niece, and her own reasons for staying ('the nights always end'), with the clinician explicitly noting she supplied none of it herself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The relationship was genuinely collaborative: joint planning, open questions about what she thought would help, and a scheduled follow-up that solidified the working alliance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently noticed, reflected, and amplified change talk ('part of you wants to try,' 'a tiny bit lighter'), strengthening commitment toward the safety plan and tomorrow's meeting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (plans falling apart, skepticism about crisis lines, 'I'm not fixed') was met with double-sided reflections and validation rather than argument, allowing the patient to move toward change herself.
