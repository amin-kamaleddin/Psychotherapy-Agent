# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirmed Sofia's honesty and skepticism without shaming her use. However, affirmations were brief and formulaic, missing deeper affirmations of her values like family expectations and resourcefulness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate simple reflections were used, but they were often mechanical restatements of the client's words. Deeper emotional content — fear of failing, family pressure, and her anxiety identity — received little empathic exploration or validation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus was consistently maintained on cannabis use and its connection to money and stress, tethered to a concrete next step. Minor drift occurred when logistics dominated the final third of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine power sharing occurred in execution, with menu options and plans built from Sofia's own ideas. The core swap intervention originated from the counselor, preventing full co-discovery of the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was repeatedly reinforced through permission-seeking and framing the plan as a low-pressure experiment, with Sofia's 'no promises' respected. Some options felt engineered, as if guiding toward a predetermined outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and eliciting Sofia's own reasons (money) were done effectively. However, deeper values like family expectations and future goals went unexplored, missing key discrepancy opportunities, and much of the session was solution-generating rather than motivation-eliciting.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission was sought before exploring, and Sofia was offered choices about focus. The counselor's expert role emerged in the action phase, prescribing the experiment structure, breathing protocol, and drafting the text message to Marcus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tentative change talk was elicited, including a commitment to the swap and good use of confidence scaling. The counselor failed to consistently reinforce or deepen the change talk when it appeared, moving to logistics instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was neither argued with nor amplified, and was responded to neutrally. However, few double-sided reflections were used, and compromises like 'taking half as much' inadvertently kept focus on maintaining use rather than pivoting toward change.
