# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, even when the client was dismissive or set limits. Genuine warmth and affirmation of the client's persistence and boundary-setting were evident.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were consistently deep and accurate, capturing both content and emotional meaning, including the 'heavy' theme and the double-sided ambivalence. The evolving reframe of 'heavy' as grief over a lost goal showed deep, dynamic understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session followed a clear trajectory from disengagement to a concrete, self-generated next step (the kettle). Summaries explicitly tracked progress, and the session ended with a specific agreed follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session direction and action plan came entirely from the client's own ideas and interests, co-created rather than imposed. The client's growing engagement and humor suggested he experienced the interaction as between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor affirmed rather than resisted the client's 'no worksheets' boundary and framed the task as low-stakes and fully the client's own. The follow-up was framed as a preference, not an assignment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client — his lost interest, his own conditions ('not all or nothing'), and his self-generated plan. Open questions elicited change talk culminating in desire, curiosity, and a firm commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned the client as the expert and built on his contributions ('some small first step'). All counselor contributions served the client's agenda rather than directing it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation produced the course interest, the kettle commitment, and desire/confidence language ('a bit curious, maybe'). The counselor actively highlighted change talk when it emerged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Barriers and fear of failure were acknowledged and legitimized via double-sided reflection without being argued against. The counselor consistently pivoted from sustain talk toward what would make change possible.
