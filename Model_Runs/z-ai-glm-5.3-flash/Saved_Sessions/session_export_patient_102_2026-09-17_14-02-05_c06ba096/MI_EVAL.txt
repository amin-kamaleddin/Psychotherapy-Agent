# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone without moralizing, but warmth is formulaic and repetitive (e.g., 'Totally fair to feel frustrated'). Genuine affirmation of Milan's strengths and normalization of his ambivalence are largely missing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; the counselor ignores Milan's key disclosure that fear of parental discovery outweighs any crash concern. Redundant safety checks and pivoting to scripts show poor tracking of the conversation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Keeps a loose focus on alcohol-related safety and lands on concrete outcomes (code word, tracking plan), but the focus shifts repeatedly without collaboratively anchoring to Milan's values or stated concerns. The target behavior is addressed mainly through side-channel safety tips.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is largely one-directional with the counselor delivering advice and scripts; the emoji plan was drafted for Milan rather than with him. Milan explicitly noted feeling coached, and his contributions (declining the minister message) were barely incorporated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor's relative strength: clear confidentiality assurance, genuine menu of choices, and optional framing led Milan to self-directed decisions. Points lost for redundant safety checks and offering advice before seeking permission.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies almost entirely on providing tips and scripts rather than drawing out Milan's own motivations. Milan's emerging motivation (calling his cousin, agreeing to track, fear of parents' reaction) is never developed, and no open questions explore his values or goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor assumes the expert role with prescriptive lists, scripts, and templates in nearly every turn, while Milan's own ideas are rarely explored or incorporated. The exchange resembles consultation delivery more than an equal-partner interview.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine successes: Milan embraced the code word and agreed to track his drinking. However, change talk is never deepened—no exploration of importance or confidence—and Milan's direct invitation for the counselor's honest opinion was squandered on generic red-flag lists.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with Milan's minimization, sidestepping confrontation, but never uses reframes or double-sided reflections to soften sustain talk. At times it reinforces the minimization ('You're right to feel frustrated—partying is normal') without pairing it with genuine safety concerns.
