# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a neutral, respectful, non-judgmental stance throughout. However, affirmations are minimal (only one early 'that's huge'), missing chances to affirm the client's motivation, honesty, and insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; vulnerable disclosures like 'I don't trust myself in the moment' and 'cigarettes are my built-in break' are met with strategy lists rather than reflections. The client's ambivalence about his two identities is never reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays firmly focused on smoking cessation, triggers, medication, and stress management without drift, converging on a concrete action plan. Direction is maintained through prescriptive advice rather than MI-guided focus, but the directional rating itself is strong.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The exchange functions as a consultation in which the client states problems and the expert delivers solutions, with closed-ended checklist questions. Plans arrive as finished lists (e.g., the restaurant plan) rather than being co-constructed from the client's knowledge of his buddies, rituals, and schedule.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** No pressure or coercion, and medication options (patch, varenicline, bupropion) are offered for the client to consider. However, only transactional choices between pre-packaged products are given ('Want a checklist?'), with little explicit reinforcement that the client owns the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation is assumed rather than evoked—no questions about reasons for quitting, what quitting would mean for him or his marriage, past attempts, or his confidence. Only factual explorations occur (cigarettes per day, medical history), drawing almost nothing out from within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely expert-driven, with the clinician positioning himself as the source of plans, scripts, and checklists. The client's own valuable insights (needing a replacement ritual, keeping the outside walk) are generated by him but not built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client generates substantial spontaneous change talk ('I'm ready,' 'I'll commit to one outing this week'), but the clinician does little to evoke or deepen it. No open questions about reasons for quitting, elaboration or scaling questions, or summaries that collect and amplify commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not amplified and doubts are not argued against; normalizing slips ('one slip isn't failure') effectively softens relapse fears. However, softening relies on reassurance and technique rather than reflective handling of ambivalence, with no double-sided reflections and the 'two different people' struggle left unexplored.
