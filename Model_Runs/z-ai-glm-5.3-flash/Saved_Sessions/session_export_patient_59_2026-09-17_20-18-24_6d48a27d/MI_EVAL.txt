# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental stance throughout, normalizing reluctance and never punishing sustain talk or minimization. Genuinely adapted to an adolescent in a juvenile justice setting without lecturing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflective listening, including a skilled double-sided reflection honoring both his friendships and his goal. Reflecting his own insight back produced visible self-recognition.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus on avoiding future legal trouble, moving from goal to antecedents to past success to concrete plan and future projection. No aimless drift; even friend discussions were tied to the target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine equal-partner exchange; the plan emerged entirely from Jamal and the psychiatrist explicitly credited him ('That's you talking, not me'). Every forward step was co-created or client-initiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced ownership of choices ('That's yours to decide', 'I'm not pushing you toward anything'), with the final plan framed entirely as Jamal's. No coercive or pressuring language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematically drew out Jamal's own motivations — his mom's stress, his clear-headed insight, and his 'smart versus lucky' framing — rather than supplying reasons to change. Drove a precontemplation-to-preparation shift through client-generated language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned Jamal as the expert on his own life throughout, and his input genuinely shaped the session's substance. Explicit statements like 'You're the expert on your own life' and a plan 'with your name on it, not mine.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully highlighted Jamal's spontaneous goal, elicited the parking lot success story, and developed it into strong preparatory change talk and a commitment ('I could try that. Once.'). All change talk was client-generated and strategically amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk and effectively used double-sided reflections to honor it without amplifying it. Deducted one point for allowing some extended sustain talk ('it's California, no one cares') without more active strategic pivoting.
