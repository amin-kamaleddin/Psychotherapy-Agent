# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and genuine affirmations tied to Mateo's strengths (determination, wisdom), with no judgment. Falls short of a 5 only because warmth is occasionally formulaic and his long history of quiet endurance isn't explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures Mateo's ambivalence, including a skillful double-sided reflection. The emotional layer — his shame about seeming weak and a lifetime of quiet endurance — is never directly reflected or validated, a significant missed opportunity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on the target behavior (speaking up with therapists to manage pain) with progressive momentum from exploration to commitment to defining success markers. No topic drift; even the mention of his wife is woven into the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions are clearly co-made, and Mateo's voice dominates the plan, but the exchange skews elicitive — the counselor offers few of his own ideas, so Mateo never gets to react to or modify counselor input. A small deduction rather than more.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: the counselor offers choices, accepts Mateo's decisions without pressure, and repeatedly attributes the change to him ('you got there yourself'). Mateo's closing 'Thanks for not just telling me what to do' confirms the stance landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Full-spectrum evocation of desire, ability, reasons, commitment, and taking-steps language: questions like 'What would you want him to know most?' draw out Mateo's own reasons and success criteria rather than supplying them. Mateo produces the entire plan himself and names his own transformation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is repeatedly and explicitly located within Mateo, whose 'How's the back today?' idea becomes the centerpiece of the plan. Mateo's own closing feedback ('Thanks for not just telling me what to do') confirms an exemplary partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook cultivation: open questions, evocative hypotheticals, and forward-looking prompts generate escalating change language ('Guess it couldn't hurt to try,' 'I'll bring it up next session'), each amplified with reflections. Mateo even defines his own success markers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('pain was part of it at my age') is handled without reinforcement, using double-sided reflections that reframe endurance as a strength-turned-obstacle. However, the 'lifetime of dealing with things quietly' is steered around rather than acknowledged and gently resolved.
