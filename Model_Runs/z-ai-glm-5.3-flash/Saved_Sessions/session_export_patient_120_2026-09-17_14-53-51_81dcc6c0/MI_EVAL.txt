# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations of Sofia's courage; disclosure of drinking during pregnancy met without judgment. Some affirmations feel slightly formulaic and there's no deep statement of unconditional regard beyond her progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture her core ambivalence between protecting the baby and keeping friends. However, her fear of already having harmed the baby and her grief over losing her old identity are acknowledged once but never explored or revisited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on stopping alcohol use throughout, with logical progression from drinking patterns to strategies, scaling, scripting, and a concrete time-bound plan (telling Dani Friday). No topic drift; each step ties back to Sofia's commitments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative: ideas offered then immediately checked for doability, and the plan pivots to Sofia's preferences without resistance. Her choices—driving excuse, telling Dani—become the session's final structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced—'I'm here to support whatever you decide,' options offered as menus to choose or tweak. Sofia arrives at key insights herself (e.g., 'I can't control that') and her decisions fully drive the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within Sofia rather than lectured: scaling questions grew confidence from 5 to 8, and she generated her own change talk ('I need to do this for my kid') and self-insight about telling Dani forcing her to be a new person.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia positioned as expert ('Which feels more true to you?') and her contributions visibly shaped the session. Score falls short of 5 because dense counselor-supplied option menus and scripts slightly tilt expertise toward the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation: confidence scaling moved Sofia from 5 to 8, and open questions drew strong client-generated commitment language, rehearsal, and concrete planning throughout.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of losing friends, wanting to 'live that life out') acknowledged with double-sided reflections before pivoting to change, preventing rationalization. Ambivalence is never explicitly validated as legitimate, and fear of having already harmed the baby is left unprocessed.
