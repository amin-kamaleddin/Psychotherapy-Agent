# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; Marco's resistance and sarcasm were met with acceptance and his autonomy affirmed. Falls short of 5 due to limited explicit affirmation of Marco's personal strengths beyond praising the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening that accurately captured Marco's perspective and lived experience (e.g., the awful-tasting old gum). Reflections were sometimes followed immediately by facts or options rather than deepening the emotional content of his resignation and ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, progressive focus on smoking cessation from importance ruler through to a concrete, actionable plan. Every exchange advanced the target behavior while staying within Marco's tolerance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely egalitarian, with Marco's corrections and preferences genuinely reshaping the plan. The unfulfilled check-in offer and occasional dense, one-directional information delivery slightly disrupted the collaborative flow.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively and consistently reinforced ('no pressure,' 'you're keeping control,' 'no lectures, agreed,' 'information, not failure'). Marco was positioned as the owner of every decision, including the optional check-in.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew motivation from Marco via importance/confidence rulers and open questions, eliciting his own reasons (grandkids) and solutions (coin strategy, moving the packet). Early reliance on providing facts about quitlines/NRT and underdeveloped personal reasons (grandkids, teeth) kept it from a 5.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly framed choices as Marco's ('which sounds easier?'), and key plan elements (chew-and-park correction, mint flavor, coin strategy) originated from his input. Information was sometimes delivered in an expert-ish format, and the check-in was offered before clarifying logistics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and developed change talk, moving Marco from 'a 3' and 'no promises' to a specific self-designed plan (4 mg mint lozenge, coin in pocket). Missed opportunities to deepen his own reasons (teeth/staining, grandkids' comments) before moving to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally without arguing, and the 'information, not failure' framing preempted catastrophizing about setbacks. Several sustain statements (e.g., 'none of that scared me into quitting') passed without a double-sided reflection or gentle reframe.
