# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone maintained throughout ('Thanks for being straight with me'). However, warmth is formulaic; no meaningful affirmations of Ethan's strengths or autonomy. Repeated safety questions after Ethan expressed frustration read as dismissive rather than unconditionally accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening minimal; counselor pivots to advice-giving rather than reflecting ambivalence. Missed rich change-talk moments (e.g., 'when I start throwing up... I hate that part'; 'it IS fun with my friends... I don't want to be that guy').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed consistently focused on alcohol use and reducing negative consequences. Focus was somewhat narrow (harm reduction rather than Ethan's own change goal), limiting broader motivational exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Largely counselor-driven, with repeated advice lists and checklists. Ethan's own ideas (playoff line, enlisting Marcus) shaped the plan, but the counselor treated these as additions rather than building a shared plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional choice acknowledgment ('Want to try one of these?'). However, prescriptive tone and repeated checklists leaned toward directing; Ethan had to assert autonomy himself ('no promises, alright?'). No explicit reinforcement that change is his choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation came almost entirely from counselor tips rather than Ethan's own reasons. No open questions, importance/confidence scaling, or summaries used to draw out Ethan's spontaneous motivation ('hates throwing up,' 'half my weekend wasted').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor assumed the expert role with bulleted advice lists across multiple turns. Rarely asked for Ethan's ideas before offering solutions; power-sharing minimal. Ethan's contributions emerged despite, not because of, the approach.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ethan produced genuine spontaneous change talk ('Maybe limiting it wouldn't be the worst thing'; 'It's worth a shot if it means no more Sunday morning death'), but the counselor failed to reflect, deepen, or reinforce it — responding with prescriptive tips instead. No commitment language elicited or consolidated via summaries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it's just fun,' 'my friends would give me crap') was not reinforced or amplified, keeping responses action-oriented. However, no double-sided reflections were used, and sustain talk like 'no promises about Jake's birthday party' was never explored or reframed toward change.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **2.6/5**

**Summary:** A well-intentioned but largely non-MI harm-reduction session. Strengths: consistent focus, no confrontation or labeling, concrete age-appropriate suggestions, and a self-generated plan. Weaknesses: pervasive premature advice-giving, minimal reflective listening, missed change-talk opportunities, and mechanical repeated safety questioning. Recommended improvements: replace checklists with open questions and complex reflections, use double-sided reflections for ambivalence, employ summaries and scaling questions, reinforce change talk, and reduce repetitive safety screening once clearly denied.
