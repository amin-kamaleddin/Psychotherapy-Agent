# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and tolerated pushback without defensiveness. Falls short of 5 because affirmation was generic with little individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent, accurate reflections captured the client's minimization and the value of his ritual. Depth was limited — emotional meaning, stress, and personal context were not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed focused and produced a concrete, measurable plan (weekend start, six weeks, weekly yes/no, mid-point check-in, gum-based success measure). Not a 5 because focus was maintained partly through persistent re-offering of options rather than smooth guiding.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with the client choosing the start date, measures, and check-in timing, and refusals being accepted. The counselor initially dominated with information and predefined option lists rather than the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language throughout, including asking permission before sharing information and accepting the client's right to judge results. Repeated option menus after client refusal risked feeling subtly pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was client-generated (confidence rating, wife's feedback, desire for healthier gums), but the counselor relied heavily on providing information and menus. Deeper evocation of the client's own values and reasons for change was underdeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions were consistently framed as the client's, and his rejections of diary and substitutes were accepted. Early heavy information-giving and lukewarm incorporation of some suggestions indicated incomplete power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some commitments were elicited (shorter puffs, rinsing, six-week trial, confidence rating, wife's complaints as motivation), but many change steps were counselor-proposed rather than client-evoked. Little deepening of change talk around why health matters or what success would mean.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (cigarettes as the real killer, uncle's longevity, scare-tactic skepticism) was acknowledged without endorsement or argumentation, and pivoted toward client-accepted steps. Some risk of amplifying sustain talk by repeatedly offering menus the client rejected.
