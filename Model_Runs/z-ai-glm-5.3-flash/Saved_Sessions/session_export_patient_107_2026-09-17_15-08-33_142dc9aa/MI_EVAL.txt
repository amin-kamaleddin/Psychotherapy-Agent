# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard with no judgment or moralizing. Affirmations were specific and genuine, and framing slip-ups as 'information, not failure' exemplified deep acceptance, which the client explicitly noticed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was solid, accurately capturing the core ambivalence between enjoying coffee and health concerns. However, most reflections stayed at surface-level content summaries, missing opportunities to reflect deeper emotions like anxiety about blood pressure or attachment to the coffee ritual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior and moved purposefully from exploration to a concrete, time-bound plan. Every exchange tied back to the caffeine goal, culminating in an actionable protocol with measurement, barriers, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was largely egalitarian, with the client's ideas genuinely integrated into the plan. However, substantial information-giving (BP technique, monitor brands, coping strategies) occurred without explicit permission-seeking, briefly shifting toward an expert-driven frame.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforced the client's ownership of decisions, never pushing for reduction as a goal and explicitly preserving choice at every step. Menu-based options and the 'guilt-free' framing if nothing changes were standout features the client appreciated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** While the counselor elicited some motivation through confidence ratings and doability questions, much of the session consisted of supplying strategies and psychoeducation rather than drawing them from the client. Open-ended questions exploring deeper values and the discrepancy between feeling great and high BP were missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positioned the client as the expert, and his contributions genuinely shaped the session—he selected the intervention, designed weekend safeguards, and chose the check-in schedule. It fell slightly short of 5 due to delivering expert information chunks without first asking how much detail the client wanted.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evoked client-initiated commitment language through experiment options and confidence questions, drawing out clear commitment ('I'll give it an honest shot'). However, change talk was not always deepened with reflections afterward, with the counselor sometimes pivoting straight to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoided reinforcing status quo reasons, neither arguing with nor dwelling on skepticism, instead reframing it as compatible with experimentation. Double-sided reflections acknowledged both sides appropriately, though stronger reflections emphasizing change could have been used at times.
