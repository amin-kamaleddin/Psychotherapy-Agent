# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with normalizing statements. However, affirmations are generic and there's little validation of the difficulty of Amina's ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; feelings like the 'weirdly scary' fear of cutting back and stress-relief needs were met with option lists rather than empathic reflection. No complex reflections were offered.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays well-focused on the target behavior (nighttime phone use, sleep, grades) and moves toward a concrete plan. Direction was somewhat premature, rushing to solutions before ambivalence was fully explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor invites Amina to pick options and give feedback, but the 'pick one of my options' format is only superficially collaborative. True co-creation occurs only at the end when Amina drafts her own text message.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally affirms choice and accepts Amina's limited commitment, but the advice-heavy, option-menu style subtly shifts control to the counselor. Amina's own solutions were quickly rewritten rather than reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to draw out Amina's own motivations or values; her reasons for change surfaced spontaneously and were met with plan-building rather than exploration. The session's engine is the counselor's suggestions, not elicited client motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, generating nearly all ideas and rewriting Amina's contributions. Her question about alternatives was answered with a counselor-generated list rather than turned back to her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Amina's change talk emerged spontaneously but was never strategically elicited or deepened. No open questions evoked change talk, and her change statements were not reflected back to amplify them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it's kind of pointless,' 'I don't need to quit') was met with simple agreement rather than double-sided reflections linking her concerns to her values. FOMO was bypassed with option lists.
