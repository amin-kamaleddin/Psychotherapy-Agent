# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard with no judgment or disapproval, repeatedly affirming 'I'll support whatever you decide' and validating the patient's persistence despite hopelessness. Warmth was genuine and individualized, including offering to problem-solve 'without judgment' if plans slipped.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing the patient's ambivalence and reframing past failures as persistence. Later reflections became compressed summaries, missing chances to explore the emotional weight of repeated failure and the meaning of dancing as 'living a little.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained tight focus on physical activity throughout, folding the dancing idea into the activity goal rather than letting the session drift. Guided toward commitment with specific planning questions, progressing logically from contemplation to concrete preparation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element was co-constructed — the walking plan, daughter-as-companion idea, check-in terms, and text message were all developed together with the patient's ideas substantially shaping the session. The final plan was entirely the patient's construction with counselor scaffolding.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: 'I'm here to listen and support your choices' and 'I'll support whatever you decide' stated repeatedly. All plans originated from the patient, with menus of options rather than directives, and the check-in framed as the patient's choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within the patient using open questions and confidence rulers (used twice, including the advanced follow-up 'what would raise it?'). The patient generated his own motivation — 'I'm kind of excited' and 'less like exercise and more like living a little' — with no lecturing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated the patient as the expert on his own life, offering options rather than prescriptions and collaborating on the text message draft. Slightly short of 5 because some question bursts (five planning questions in one turn) felt agenda-driven rather than fluid.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through scaling questions and open-ended prompts about what had helped and what would make follow-through easier. The patient produced commitment language ('I'll make it happen,' 'I'll send it during my walk') and ended visibly motivated with 9/10 confidence and three specific commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well by pivoting from barriers (gym time, past failures) to client strengths without arguing or dwelling, avoiding reinforcement of all-or-nothing thinking. Could have used more strategic double-sided reflections to explicitly resolve ambivalence rather than moving quickly to problem-solving.
