# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed Mateo's autonomy and strengths (e.g., 'That's you leading this, Mateo'). Acceptance was strong, though occasional leading phrasing slightly limited full unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured ambivalence, e.g., naming the pull between hope and fear of getting his hopes up. Empathy was consistently reflective rather than merely sympathetic.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange steered toward change talk—vision, ability, commitment—moving smoothly from 'why bother' to 'I got this.' Direction was exemplary throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly elicited Mateo's own ideas ('what do you think that might look like?') and positioned him as the expert. The plan was entirely patient-generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Statements like 'You know your situation better than anyone' reinforced personal agency. Minor deduction for occasionally pre-shaping the plan (texting tonight) through successive questioning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation of change talk across all DARN dimensions—desire ('I want to send that text'), ability ('I can do that much'), reasons (future vision), and need (not being stuck). The 'kind of person my kid could look up to' moment was a standout.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and reflective listening created a genuine two-way conversation. A slight pattern of stacking follow-up questions occasionally shifted the rhythm.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent use of questions and reflections to deepen commitment language, culminating in strong mobilizing change talk with a concrete, self-chosen plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('don't want to get my hopes up,' 'things come up') was consistently reflected neutrally or reframed into dual-sided ambivalence rather than argued against.
