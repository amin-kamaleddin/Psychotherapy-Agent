# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Steady nonjudgmental regard throughout; never labeled the client noncompliant and absorbed pushback without defensiveness. Kept from 5 by formulaic affirmations and the mortality question that pressed past Samir's stated readiness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening including a textbook double-sided reflection and accurate early reflections of Samir's worldview. Several reflections led rather than followed, and one overstated his position despite his recent link of pills to work.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Every major move circled back to medication, including the skillful re-connection of the work/money topic to pills. No consolidating summary, focus, or next step emerged, and the mortality detour consumed momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Joint inquiry framed from the outset with genuinely shared topic control — Samir chose work/money and the psychiatrist built on it. Samir twice perceived a hidden agenda, indicating some strategic questions carried the clinician's imprint.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The standout domain: repeated explicit affirmations of client control ('you get to set the pace here,' 'nothing needs to change today') with control handed back after every pushback. No persuasive, directive, or coercive language anywhere in the transcript.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from the client himself — work routine, mother's words, family role — with no supplied reasons or medical consequences. Evocation was occasionally seeded rather than purely elicited, and no rulers or scaling adapted tools to his precontemplative state.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise repeatedly and explicitly located in Samir, and his contributions genuinely redirected the session. However, some moves imposed the clinician's frame (the mother interpretation, mortality imagery), and the client twice sensed an agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Numerous structurally sound evocative questions yielded minimal change talk, and the valuable fragments that emerged ('I take the pills so I can work') were never deepened or consolidated. No confidence exploration or commitment-strengthening occurred.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided arguing and reassurance traps, using normalization, double-sided reflections, and permission-giving skillfully. Two moments amplified sustain talk (the mortality question), and no strategic reframing of 'I feel fine' or 'habit, not conviction' was attempted.
