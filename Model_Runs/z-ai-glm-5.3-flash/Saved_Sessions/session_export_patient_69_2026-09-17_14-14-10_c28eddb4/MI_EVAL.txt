# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with a flat, resistant client, validating his resentment and normalizing his mandated attendance. Affirmations were sparse and formulaic, lacking individualized warmth or explicit positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections captured his frustration with being forced to 'engage.' However, emotional content such as his resignation, fear of being watched, and low expectations went largely unreflected as the counselor quickly pivoted to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the compliance concern and progressed logically from exploration to a concrete plan and follow-up. Direction was achieved mainly through rapid task-focus, moving to options early and risking skipping deeper engagement work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A functional working partnership existed, with Kieran selecting and modifying options (reducing applications to two, adding a trial period). Collaboration was uneven, however, as the counselor generated nearly all ideas and Kieran acted more as approver/editor than co-creator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Kieran's ownership through menu-based choices, accepting his edits without pushback, and framing the plan as keeping 'control with you.' No pressuring or directive language was used; his pace and preferences were respected throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely drew motivation from within Kieran, supplying solutions and drafts instead of exploring his values or reasons for change. Prime evocation moments like 'first time in months something went my way' and 'they just want to watch me jump' passed without reflection or deepening.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly framed expertise as residing with Kieran, offering choices and allowing him to renegotiate the plan's details. The counselor nonetheless did most of the drafting and strategizing, so the session direction was more counselor-generated than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively channeled minimal motivation into concrete action, eliciting genuine commitment language ('I'll send this today, I mean it') and reinforcing ownership by letting Kieran modify the plan. Lacked deliberate evocation of Kieran's own reasons for change and confidence talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided amplifying sustain talk, meeting dismissiveness with acknowledgment and redirection to agency rather than argument. Redirections were somewhat abrupt, with sustain talk not always gently processed before pivoting.
