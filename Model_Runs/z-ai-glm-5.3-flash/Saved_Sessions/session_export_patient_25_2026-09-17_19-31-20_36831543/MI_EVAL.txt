# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warmth and unconditional regard are consistently conveyed through individualized affirmations and nonjudgmental framing of accountability. Falls short of 5 due to one moment of mild positivity inflation that edges toward cheerleading.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurate, with complex reflections capturing Daniel's ambivalence well. Reflections occasionally mirror his words rather than deepen them, missing chances to name and validate the emotional weight of the foggy-morning guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on drinking in social contexts and progresses logically toward a concrete plan. It lacks explicit agenda-setting at the outset, and one transcript exchange shows brief role confusion between counselor and patient voices.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functions as a genuine exchange between equals, with the intervention built entirely from Daniel's own material and expertise redirected back to him. Daniel explicitly confirms the experience was collaborative rather than a lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is explicitly located with Daniel throughout, with no advice, directives, or pressure anywhere in the session. Autonomy support extends even to potential failure, framing a slip as data rather than defeat.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key therapeutic movement evokes Daniel's own motivations, values, and strategies, linking his smoking-cessation success to the new behavior. Daniel constructed the change narrative himself, confirming the evocation landed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Daniel is repeatedly positioned as the expert on his own life, and the plan originated entirely from his own ideas and experiences. His closing comment confirms the power-sharing was genuinely experienced, not just rhetorical.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is systematically evoked through open questions tied to Daniel's values and past successes, then reinforced with affirmations and reflections. His commitment language was clearly evoked rather than supplied by the counselor.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without argument or amplification using double-sided reflections that tilt toward change while respecting Daniel's framing. Occasionally sustain-laden content is mirrored neutrally without an active strategic pivot toward the change side.
