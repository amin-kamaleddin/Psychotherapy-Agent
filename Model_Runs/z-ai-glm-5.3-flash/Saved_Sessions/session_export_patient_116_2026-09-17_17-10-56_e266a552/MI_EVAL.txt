# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Samir's frustration and affirming his commitments without criticizing nonadherence. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and plan-focused rather than deeply individualized to Samir's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated consistent, accurate reflective listening that captured Samir's core complaints about pills, privacy, and family interrogation. Reflections occasionally moved quickly to options and planning rather than deepening the emotional weight of lifelong medication.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on medication adherence from opening question to closing summary with no aimless drift despite Samir's deflections. Each turn advanced toward a concrete, actionable plan including habit anchoring, missed-dose planning, weekend troubleshooting, and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Operated largely between equal partners—asking permission, soliciting troubleshooting input, and honoring Samir's preferences (text check-in, single alarm, opaque case). Fell short of a 5 because the initial framework and most solution options originated from the counselor rather than emerging jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offered choices and framed the plan as a small, declinable test while affirming Samir's reasonable change. At times presented his own solution quickly rather than first eliciting Samir's ideas, and did not explicitly state that taking the meds was entirely Samir's decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from Samir rather than lecturing, eliciting client-generated goals like 'mom not interrogating me' and 'most mornings would be something.' Rarely explored deeper personal reasons or long-term benefits, and confidence was left unexamined after Samir voiced doubt.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly signaled that expertise resided with the client by soliciting input on anchoring, reminders, and troubleshooting, with Samir's contributions substantially shaping the final plan. The core strategy content (alarm, pills at coffee station) was largely counselor-generated rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change language with questions about what 'not being about pills' would look like and what would count as a win, converting resistance into a self-endorsed one-week trial. Change talk was not consistently deepened with confidence questions, and opportunities were missed when Samir said he wasn't feeling 'confident.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged Samir's sustain talk without reinforcing it, using effective reframing ('you want your life back, not to be defined by meds') and offering choice rather than arguing. Redirection was sometimes accomplished mainly by moving quickly to practical options rather than explicitly rolling with the resistance.
