# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, with specific, genuine affirmations (e.g., 'honesty—it's one of your real strengths'). No judgment, even when the client is blunt or dismissive; identity concerns are honored rather than redirected.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening with reframes that consistently land and elicit elaboration (e.g., 'less about avoidance and more about competition'). The counselor also adapts tone in response to the client's request to keep things light.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior with logical progression through engaging, evoking, focusing, and planning. Every question and reflection ties back to charging without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ideas are co-constructed and the plan is fully client-generated (the armrest idea). The client's shift from passive listening to actively designing his own solution and follow-up terms demonstrates genuinely shared power.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of client ownership: 'not as a promise, just as an experiment' and asking permission before offering direction. The client's low bar is affirmed as his own, and his request for a non-heavy follow-up is honored rather than negotiated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from the client with no lecturing or persuasion—the client explicitly thanks the counselor for this. The question 'what would having a charged device actually give you back?' elicits a deeply personal reason (peace of mind) that is then reflected at just the right depth.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client ('You're the expert on your routine'), and the client's contributions genuinely shape the plan. The closing framing makes the partnership stance explicit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions elicit progressively stronger change talk ('maybe I move it tonight' → 'that's the bar. It's low, but it's mine'). Each change statement is selectively reflected and amplified, with motivation visibly escalating across the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Outside one anomaly, sustain talk is briefly acknowledged and pivoted toward change, and is never otherwise reinforced. However, the third counselor turn consists of an extended first-person sustain-talk monologue ('I still don't need it that much... I feel fine'), a technical error that risks amplifying anti-change language.
