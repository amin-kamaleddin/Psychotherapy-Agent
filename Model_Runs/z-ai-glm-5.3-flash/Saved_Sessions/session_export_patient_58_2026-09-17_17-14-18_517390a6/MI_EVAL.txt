# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance with genuine affirmations ('That's you taking control'), though responses occasionally feel formulaic and his ambivalence/frustration with authority is never explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections, including reframing lateness as FOMO rather than laziness and validating that one bad night doesn't cancel progress. Misses deeper exploration of feeling policed and the pivotal 'telling you makes it feel more real' moment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on punctuality and the late-night gaming driving it, moving steadily from problem exploration to a specific, dated action plan with a Wednesday check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative: asks before advising, co-constructs texts, and incorporates Jamal's pushback (no charts, no scripts). Falls short due to pre-packaged solutions offered even after Jamal said he didn't need help planning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently emphasizes Jamal's choice and frames the plan as him taking control, accepting his rejections without pushing. However, rapid-fire option menus feel like steering, and some advice comes before asking permission.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Draws out genuine intrinsic motivation and commitment language ('I'm ready,' 'if I tell you I'm doing it, then I kind of have to do it'). Evocation often takes the form of choosing among counselor-generated options rather than open exploration of his values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly signals that expertise lies with Jamal, whose contributions clearly shape the plan (the 'last game' message, check-in timing). Frequent pre-formatted option menus slightly limit full power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and amplifies change talk, eliciting Jamal's own plan and strong commitment language. Some of his best change-talk moments were met with new options rather than deepened reflection.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges and reflects sustain talk (FOMO, mom nagging, 'feels like a punishment') with some effective reframing ('slip-ups are data'). However, softening is inconsistent and often overwhelmed by quickly pivoting to menus of advice options instead of exploring resistance.
