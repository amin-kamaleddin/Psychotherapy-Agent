# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently validates Nabil's anger and affirms his agency without judgment; warmth occasionally stays at the level of technique, and moments of vulnerability (his shame about being 'managed,' his father) could have been met with deeper acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections capture the emotional core ('the unfairness of it') and the ambivalence precisely, but some emotional depths—shame about being 'managed,' hypocrisy guilt—are reflected briefly then quickly pivoted to questions rather than sat with.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on anger in medical settings with no topic drift; the session progresses logically from validation to a concrete, time-bound action plan directly tied to the presenting concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely egalitarian session with inclusive language, no expert stance or advice-giving, and ideas flowing from client to counselor; even Nabil's challenge ('I know you're trying to help, but...') is received non-defensively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly affirms Nabil's ownership with the plan entirely of his construction, but could more explicitly honor his freedom to choose at moments of renewed reluctance (e.g., 'Monday-morning me is a coward') rather than leaning on the commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every motivational element originates from Nabil—his values, ideas, and commitment language—with no external reasons supplied by the counselor; evocative questions produce the session's most powerful motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Nabil as the expert ('You know these situations better than anyone'), and every plan element originates from the client, visibly redirecting the session each time.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's greatest strength: the counselor skillfully evokes and deepens change talk (e.g., 'What would your kids see if you did it this new way?'), culminating in Nabil's explicit, self-generated commitment ('Monday. Early slot. I'll ring.') with his own accountability plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk without amplifying it, using double-sided reflections and reframing (e.g., 'taking charge rather than needing help'), though some objections like job worries receive only neutral acknowledgment rather than more active, strategic softening.
