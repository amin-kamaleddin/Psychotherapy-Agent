# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations and validation of ambivalence; some affirmations felt formulaic, and one response confusingly restated the client's experience in the counselor's first-person voice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently accurate, complex reflections captured the client's ambivalence and self-insight well; reflections sometimes summarized rather than deepened the emotional layer.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the coffee target behavior with no drift; the session progressed logically to a concrete, client-owned two-week experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — the client defined success criteria, co-created the plan, and chose the check-in schedule; the client explicitly noted getting 'something I can actually live with' rather than a lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the client's freedom of choice, accepted his refusal of total cessation without pushback, and asked how much support he wanted; 'rooting for you, whatever the verdict' conveyed unconditional support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was entirely elicited from the client through open questions; his closing insight that he 'talked himself into something I might actually do' is direct evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert and redirected advice requests back to him; all key plan elements (2pm cutoff, halfway check-in, two-week experiment) came from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions evoked progressively stronger change talk, from tentative to concrete commitment; each step was reinforced with affirmations and reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without amplifying it and used effective double-sided reflections; sustained sustain talk (3pm crash fears, 'morning coffee is sacred') could have been more strategically pivoted toward change.
