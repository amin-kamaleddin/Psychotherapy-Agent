# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, consistent affirmations and a nonjudgmental stance toward the relapse and past drinking. However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic and lack deeper, individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, but emotional material (shame, self-criticism, the diner flask memory, the 'selfish or smart' question) was largely bypassed in favor of logistics as planning took over.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on a concrete target — getting through Thursday 8-9pm — with a specific plan anchored to Elias's values (daughter's Saturday performance) and no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine back-and-forth exists (Elias shaped details, rejected suggestions), but the counselor repeatedly supplied pre-built solutions and option menus rather than jointly generating ideas, revealing an expert stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choices and accepts Elias's modifications, but steering shows through rapid option menus and suggestions offered before needs were identified (e.g., the alarm Elias declined). Ownership of the change is never explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong opening question evoked Elias's intrinsic motivations (kids, shame avoidance, daughter's event). However, the counselor quickly shifted to plan-delivery mode, with no exploration of confidence or deepening of meaningful change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias's input is invited ('pick or tweak'), but the counselor positions themselves as the expert problem-solver — drafting scripts and backup options unprompted. Elias's 'I'd never word it that way myself' shows the plan is counselor-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocation produced strong change talk ('This time I'm sitting inside, eating pancakes with my kids'), and the plan was tied to values. But change talk was rarely deepened with follow-up evocative questions or amplified reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never amplified, which is a strength, but it was sidestepped rather than acknowledged and reframed. No double-sided reflections were used, and Elias's fear of the alarm 'reminding me I'm an addict' went unexplored.
