# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental, including warm handling of sensitive disclosures; affirmations are somewhat formulaic and the emotional weight of the family history isn't deeply honored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captures both sides of the client's ambivalence; reflections remain surface-level and cognitive, with deeper emotional themes (fear, shame, family history) left unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the linked targets of drinking and contraceptive reliability throughout; no final summary of the plan, and some exchanges linger on logistics rather than advancing change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction — the client's ideas (IUD, Sarah text, Thursday timing) shaped the plan and were adopted; the clinician still generated most option menus and drafts rather than drawing them from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language throughout, with genuine client choice honored without pressure; mid-session rapid presentation of six pre-packaged options subtly steered toward the clinician's agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation evoked early, but most of the session consisted of the counselor supplying options, drafts, and scripts; evocation tools like follow-up rulers and elaboration questions were underused and key change reasons were never revisited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client positioned as expert and decision-maker, with her ideas substantially driving the plan; option menus still carry an expert-driven, pre-packaged flavor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good evocative questions early produced strong change talk, but spontaneous change talk was acknowledged rather than deepened or amplified, and commitment language was never consolidated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about self-efficacy was responded to neutrally without amplifying or resolving it; no double-sided reflections or reframing of failed attempts as learning, often redirected to logistics instead.
