# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, non-judgmental tone with no labeling of the client's anger, but responses were formulaic and safety-focused, with no explicit affirmations of Nabil's strengths or honesty. Quick pivots to option menus undercut warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ('the wait was disrespectful'), but reflective listening was shallow and infrequent. Emotionally rich material — the anger admission, his wife's feedback, his vulnerability — was bypassed in favor of tasks and recaps.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on appointment delays and concrete actions with no topic drift. However, focus stayed on external problem-solving and never connected to the referral's actual target — Nabil's anger.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration existed (client chose options, contributed the 8am idea, edited email tone), but the counselor generated virtually all solutions in a rapid-fire, checklist manner resembling case management rather than joint exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine choices offered throughout with no pressure, and the client exercised real control over the plan (e.g., declining further drafts). Falls short of 5 because options were pre-framed by the counselor and client ownership of change was not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely drew out the client's own motivations, values, or strategies — no open exploratory questions, confidence rulers, or exploration of anger's costs/benefits. The counselor did the change work for the client rather than evoking it, a fundamental MI style error.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menus and scheduling choices gave the client some input, but the interaction was task-driven and counselor-led, with nearly all content (drafts, timelines, strategies) supplied by the counselor without drawing on Nabil's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged (8am slot interest, 'anger hasn't fixed anything yet'), but it appeared incidentally — the counselor never deliberately evoked, reflected, or deepened these pivotal moments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not argued against, but also not actively softened — 'You're right to be angry' validated external blame without double-sided reflections. The client's own ambivalence ('the anger makes me worse... isn't it?') was a missed opportunity to guide toward personal agency.
