# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone despite sarcasm and defensiveness, with clear regard for the client. Affirmations were sparse and somewhat formulaic, lacking explicit normalization of ambivalence or autonomy affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections like 'You're fed up with time-consuming appointments' accurately captured the client's core grievance and ambivalence. Emotional depth was occasionally thin, not exploring deeper frustration or fears beneath the asthma minimization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, clear focus on appointment adherence and asthma management throughout, funneling every exchange toward concrete outcomes. Progressed logically from exploration to specific, measurable next steps with a set send time and contingency plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the session as joint work and sought the client's input at each decision point, co-constructing the plan. At times moved quickly to producing artifacts (emails, reminders), making collaboration feel somewhat counselor-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice ('You're in charge here') and offered options without pressure or persuasion about asthma severity. Repeated offers to do drafting work slightly undercut ownership, and autonomy was less reinforced at moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and the confidence ruler elicited the client's own reasons and commitment ('I'll send it. Monday'). Evocation stayed logistical, never drawing out deeper values that could sustain motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission before offering options and incorporated the client's contributions, but frequently assumed the doer role by drafting emails, reminders, and replies. Power-sharing was not consistently fostered despite the client showing capability.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk via the confidence ruler and solution-oriented questions, yielding commitment language and self-generated optimism ('Seven might turn into an eight'). Deeper development of the client's 'why' beyond logistics was missing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or dwelling on sustain talk like 'Oscar hasn't killed me yet,' pivoting instead to workable elements and using double-sided reflections effectively. Could have more deliberately affirmed the client's desire to avoid re-litigating missed appointments as a change lever.
