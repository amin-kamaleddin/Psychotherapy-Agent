# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor was polite and non-judgmental, but acceptance remained superficial — immediately pivoting to safety tips after disclosures without affirming honesty or normalizing ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was nearly absent; emotionally significant statements about caving to pressure and fear of exclusion received no reflection and were passed over with tips or praise.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on alcohol-related behavior, but direction was entirely skill-focused, with no agenda-setting, summary, or goal tied to the screening concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was a checklist interview followed by didactic skill delivery; Sofia's contributions were praised but never incorporated, and decisions were counselor-proposed rather than jointly made.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered choices and asked permission, but autonomy support was shallow — pushing the tips agenda despite Sofia saying she didn't need them, with the structure counselor-chosen throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No attempt was made to elicit Sofia's own motivations, values, or reasons for change; her most revealing statements emerged only accidentally during role-play and were not built upon.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite asking permission, the session was fundamentally counselor-driven: all content, scripts, and strategies came from the counselor, with Sofia passively accepting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerged only accidentally through role-play; the counselor never deliberately evoked or deepened Sofia's own reasons for limiting drinking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clear sustain talk ('it's not a big deal,' 'I don't really need tips') was largely passed over rather than softened — no double-sided reflections, reframing, or strategic redirection toward her own motivations.
