# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with strong positive regard from the opening and an affirmation of Rafael's realism as a strength. Fewer explicit, individualized affirmations of his persistence and honesty kept this from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured both barriers and past success (e.g., the "I'll go tomorrow" loop), and the counselor named Rafael's frustration. Reflections sometimes shifted too quickly into questions or suggestions, and the shame implied in "I've broken enough promises to myself" was never reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, consistent focus on returning to exercise, progressing from barrier exploration to a concrete, action-oriented plan without topic drift. It closed with a specific commitment (Wednesday 6:30, 8:00 check-in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Rafael supplying most specifics (day, time, accountability, note idea) and the counselor contributing structure like rulers and backup planning. Reliance on pre-packaged option menus rather than truly open brainstorming slightly diminished this.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly reinforced from opening to close — options rather than directives, permission-seeking on check-in format, and respect for Rafael's self-defined goal. His final comment ("This feels more real") confirms he experienced genuine ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn largely from Rafael himself — his reasons, his confidence booster ("proving to myself I can still do it"), and his own accountability strategy — via rulers and open questions, without lecturing. Could have evoked more about why exercise matters to his identity and values beyond past benefits.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor positioned Rafael as the expert, asked permission about the check-in, and let his own ideas substantially shape the plan. Frequent multiple-choice suggestion lists occasionally led rather than followed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled evocation through rulers and questions (importance 8; confidence rising 4→5→6) prompted Rafael to articulate his own reasons for change. Less emphasis on evoking desire/need language or connecting the plan to deeper values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers were acknowledged briefly, with double-sided reflections ("yet you clearly remember how much better you felt") pivoting sustain talk toward change. Dismissive comments were met with forward-moving planning rather than reinforcement, though occasional troubleshooting questions dwelled on barriers.
