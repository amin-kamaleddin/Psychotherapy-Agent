# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance with no disapproval, framing slips with 'no self-judgment.' Affirmations were somewhat generic and formulaic, missing targeted affirmation of Linda's specific strengths like her self-awareness about the slip-shame cycle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were generally accurate and captured ambivalence, such as acknowledging habit, social ties, and health fears. Reflections often stayed at content level and missed deeper emotional meaning, particularly around the morning cigarette feeling like 'the only thing that's mine' and her fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on tobacco cessation and progressed from exploring ambivalence to a concrete action plan with follow-up scheduled. Direction was driven more by the counselor's structured agenda than by weaving Linda's stated values into a co-created trajectory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda made meaningful choices (tea, Tuesday at 10, Carol as buddy), but the exchange was question-heavy and menu-based, resembling a structured protocol rather than fluid dialogue. The agenda was largely counselor-determined.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was consistently offered and Linda was never pressured, with options presented only when she was ready. Autonomy was conveyed through menu-offering rather than reinforcing Linda's ownership of the change, and the slip protocol was prescribed rather than authored by her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client motivation was elicited and confidence rose from 3-4 to 5-6, but ruler scores were never explored ('Why an 8 and not a 5?') and the counselor frequently supplied reasons and scripts. Linda's most powerful motivations were not mined for meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda's input was invited and no authoritarian directives were used, but the session repeatedly shifted into expert-driven planning mode with counselor-supplied menus and plans. Linda's expertise was incorporated only after the counselor framed the solution.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged frequently but was largely client-generated rather than strategically cultivated; ruler scores weren't followed up with questions to deepen them. The counselor moved quickly to solution-listing rather than eliciting Linda's own reasons and ideas.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced or argued against, which was a strength, but there was little active softening. No double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk were used, and the 'only thing that's mine' statement was met with a pivot to problem-solving rather than resolving the ambivalence underneath.
