# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, with notable normalization of the client's need for peace and validation of her fear. The tone remains respectful and non-judgmental, even when the client describes relapse or calls herself 'an idiot.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurate, capturing ambivalence and emotional nuance well. Some reflections remain at content-summary level rather than deepening into underlying emotions, e.g., after the client's disclosure about her mother's death.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, progressive focus on smoking cessation with no topic drift, moving from values through high-risk situations to a concrete next step. The counselor guides without dictating, building strong momentum toward preparation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely between equals, building on the client's ideas and co-developing the plan from her input. However, there is little explicit negotiation of the agenda or transparent offer of the counselor's own knowledge, so true two-expert pooling is not fully explicit.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly hands choice, pace, and timing to the client, and no advice or pressure is imposed. Ownership is not explicitly reaffirmed during moments of doubt, and when the client asks 'Does that sound sensible?' the session ends without returning the decision to her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within, eliciting the client's own reasons, strengths, and hope without arguing for change. A higher score would require deeper elaboration of change-talk moments and explicit confidence exploration or scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly frames the client as the expert ('You're the expert on your own life') and consistently locates answers within her. The client's own contributions substantially drive the session and shape the emerging plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strengths-based and future-oriented questions skillfully evoke change talk, which is then reinforced through immediate reflection and affirmation. More systematic eliciting of confidence and ability language would be needed for a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced and fear is reframed as courage, but some counselor reflections ('steady companion through life's hardest moments,' 'a thin swap') unintentionally amplified the case for the status quo. Few deliberate double-sided reflections pivot toward change.
