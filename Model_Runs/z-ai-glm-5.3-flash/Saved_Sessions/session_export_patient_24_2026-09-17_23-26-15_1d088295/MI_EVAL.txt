# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warmth and unconditional positive regard are consistent throughout, including accepting the patient's skepticism and continued doubt without challenge. Statements like 'Both make sense' and affirmations of his self-honesty convey deep respect for the person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are precise and capture both content and underlying values, such as the wind-down ritual meaning and the 9pm habit rhythm. The counselor repeatedly demonstrates accurate understanding of the patient's data-driven, self-competitive identity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on alcohol use and progresses logically from ambivalence to a concrete, committed plan (Saturday start, measurement criteria, 9pm coping plan). Every response advances the trajectory without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently asks rather than tells, and all substantive plan elements (experiment design, NA beer, 9pm plan) originate with the patient. Power-sharing is genuine throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is reinforced via open choice ('What do you think would work for you?') and never prescribing. The patient explicitly states he'll stick with it because it's self-directed—clear evidence the autonomy framing succeeded.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the patient's own reasons—marathon investment, self-competition, wanting truth—rather than supplied by the counselor. Strategic affirmations evoke strong change talk culminating in commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor positions the patient as the expert ('judged by fair standards you set yourself,' 'real ownership'). The counselor's contributions mainly organize and affirm the patient's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled questions ('What would the results need to show for you to say, okay, this matters?') evoked commitments, ability statements, and anticipation of further change ('maybe I look at the weeknight beers next'). Affirmations reinforce the patient's own momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is briefly acknowledged and reframed toward change without argument—skepticism becomes an asset channeled into the self-designed experiment. No reasons against change are ever reinforced.
