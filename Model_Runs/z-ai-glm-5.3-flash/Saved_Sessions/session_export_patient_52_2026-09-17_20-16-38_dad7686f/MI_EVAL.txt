# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, non-judgmental, and affirming; no lecturing or disapproval even when the patient minimizes her drinking. Unconditional positive regard is evident throughout (e.g., 'it's okay to hold hope lightly').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures deep, non-obvious layers of meaning (e.g., the need for an 'earned' reward). Reflections occasionally bend toward advancing the counselor's agenda, and emotional themes like guilt and her mother's legacy could be reflected back more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use, progressing logically from exploring coping alternatives to a concrete experiment and success markers. Every exchange advances the arc with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed—the ritual idea comes from the patient, the counselor adds scaffolding, and the final design (cup, photo, one Thursday) is explicitly hers. Both parties' knowledge visibly shapes the outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Patient's ownership is repeatedly reinforced, her self-imposed pacing ('one night a week') honored, and advice offered only tentatively and conditionally. No coercive or persuasive language appears.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client, eliciting her core 'why' (clear-headedness for Miroslav) and her own change mechanisms. Her closing insight that 'it's not really about the tea at all' demonstrates self-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Patient is explicitly positioned as the expert, and her contributions substantially shape the session. The closing summary explicitly returns ownership of the plan to her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes strong client-generated motivation and commitment language ('I want to be clear-headed for him,' 'I can do one Thursday'). Success criteria are articulated by the patient herself, a hallmark of well-cultivated change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids amplifying sustain talk and uses effective double-sided reflection and strategic problem-solving. However, sustain statements (e.g., 'no tea fixes that') are not always explicitly validated before pivoting toward change.
