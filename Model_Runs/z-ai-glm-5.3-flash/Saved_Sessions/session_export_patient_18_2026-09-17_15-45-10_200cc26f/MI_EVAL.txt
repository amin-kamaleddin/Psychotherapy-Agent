# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and respectful, helping Anya feel accepted. However, praise was somewhat formulaic and fears were not deeply normalized beyond 'That sounds really tough.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Anya's core ambivalence about belonging vs. safety. Missed chances to reflect deeper emotions like her fear of embarrassment or caving under pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful focus on managing alcohol-related social pressure from exploration through rehearsal. Ended without a summary or explicit commitment step for the next party.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consent-based advice sharing and co-created strategies (soda trick, buddy plan) supported genuine teamwork. Reliance on pre-packaged lists kept the power balance slightly uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly handed control to Anya, who exercised real choice throughout. Advice-giving still framed the counselor as the source of solutions rather than affirming her ownership of outcomes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence ruler and role-plays elicited Anya's own motivation and self-efficacy. Much content was counselor-supplied, with few questions exploring her values or what matters to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client input shaped the plan, but the counselor often shifted into an expert role with prescriptive lists and coaching tips. Genuine power-sharing was limited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence scaling showed measurable progression (6→7), and role-play produced commitment language. Over-relied on offering options rather than open-ended evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly without reinforcement, pivoting to strengths. Providing 'hiding' tricks may have implicitly validated avoidance rather than exploring openness about being alcohol-free.
