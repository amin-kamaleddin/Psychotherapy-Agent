# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with genuine acceptance of Diego's harm-reduction (not abstinence) goal and affirmations of his plan. However, warmth feels somewhat functional and checklist-like, with limited explicit normalization of the difficulty of his disclosure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reasonable reflective statements were made, but the session remained content-focused and solution-driven rather than emotionally exploratory. Diego's deepest material — his fear that saying it 'makes it real' and the emotional function of using alone — was met with logistics and scripts rather than reflections of the emotional weight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (heroin-related safety), with every exchange advancing concrete steps and culminating in rehearsed scripts and role-play. Focus occasionally narrowed prematurely into logistics at the expense of exploring underlying motivations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration appeared in role-play and script customization, with Diego shaping the language and identifying backups. But the balance tilted counselor-led: the counselor drafted all scripts and supplied Marco's hardest reaction and its reply before Diego could generate his own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego's choice was repeatedly emphasized ('It's completely up to you'), and his modifications to scripts were welcomed without correction. However, advice and options lists were sometimes front-loaded before asking what Diego already knew, and autonomy was affirmed more through menu-offering than ownership of his change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation originated almost entirely with Diego rather than being elicited by the counselor, who rarely explored why change mattered or how confidence could be strengthened — even the twice-stated '10 out of 10' readiness was never unpacked. The counselor supplied information, scripts, and anticipated conversation content rather than drawing out Diego's own reasons and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego's expertise was partly honored ('Which feels most helpful next?'), but an early expert-driven information dump preceded exploration of his considerable existing knowledge. The counselor also provided Marco's anticipated reactions rather than eliciting them from Diego first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego generated substantial change talk, aided by forward-looking questions about timing and obstacles, but much arose from his own pre-existing readiness rather than deliberate evocation. The counselor rarely deepened commitment with reflections, affirmations of confidence, or exploration of why the steps mattered — notably leaving the '10 out of 10' rating unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about heroin's relaxing/escape function and preferring solitude was acknowledged once but then largely bypassed rather than strategically softened. No double-sided reflections connected his relief motives with his safety goals, leaving his ambivalence unresolved as the session drifted to task focus.
