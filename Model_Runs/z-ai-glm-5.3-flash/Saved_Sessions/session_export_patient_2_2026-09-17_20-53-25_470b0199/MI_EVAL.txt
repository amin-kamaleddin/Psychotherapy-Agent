# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed unconditional positive regard, responding to boundary-setting without judgment or pressure. Aisha explicitly noted she wasn't lectured, and affirmations were genuine and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was consistently accurate, capturing both content and emotional nuance (e.g., 'the automatic part is real'). Reflections landed well and Aisha repeatedly built on them.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session progressed purposefully toward a concrete, specific change plan focused on evening smoking. However, the alcohol topic was fully dropped per client choice, leaving a clinically relevant target behavior unaddressed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interview occurred between equal partners, with every element of the final plan generated by Aisha. The counselor checked preferences rather than dictating, framing the plan as 'entirely yours.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently reinforced ('your way, your pace') with genuine choices offered about firming up the plan and follow-up format. The counselor accepted her preferences for reduction over quitting and messaging over an appointment without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions were elicited almost entirely from the client — tea, coursework, sister accountability, and coping self-talk all came from Aisha. The counselor never argued for change or supplied reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client from the first utterance ('You know your life better than anyone'). Aisha's contributions substantially shaped the session and the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evoked and developed change talk through open questions and a confidence scaling query, drawing out her son's mimicry, 'maybe three instead of six,' and 'yes, I'll try.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement and pivoted toward change, often by reframing her own past successes ('one bad week didn't mean I quit school'). The alcohol topic was gracefully dropped per her choice.
