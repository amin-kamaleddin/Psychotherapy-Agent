# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with repeated affirmations and explicit support for Anya's choices. Loses a point for a formulaic feel at times, dense multi-question openings, and missed opportunities to affirm her vulnerability about needing parental rides at nearly eighteen.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured both content and nuance, including the perceptive 'go along to avoid explaining yourself' that helped Anya articulate ambivalence. Emotional undercurrents like her embarrassment and relief at the session went largely unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol-related choices with strong progression from exploration to concrete plan. Stacked questions in the opening and lack of a collaborative agenda summary early on diffused focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative with menu options, joint rehearsal, and a co-constructed plan from Anya's own ideas. Counselor-generated advice blocks and occasional double questions tipped toward an expert-driven stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: permission-seeking, menu options, stepping back when Anya declined help, and accepting her statement that she wasn't promising abstinence without pressure. Reinforced that change ownership resides with her throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine evocation of desire and commitment talk, but most strategies (refusal lines, safety tips, text message) originated from the counselor rather than being elicited. Her strongest motivations were not deepened as the session shifted to teaching mode.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently treated Anya as the expert with permission-seeking and deference to her agenda, and her ideas shaped the plan. Not a 5 because advice blocks were counselor-generated rather than first drawn from her own strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked desire talk ('I'd rather not drink when I don't actually want to') and a commitment-like statement about acting 'on purpose,' plus her own concrete plan. Change talk wasn't consistently deepened or summarized as her own motivation before moving to advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argued with sustain talk; used a skilled double-sided reflection acknowledging comfort while pivoting to her discomfort. Some sustain talk ('mostly it's fine') was left unaddressed rather than gently reframed toward her values.
