# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirmed concrete steps, with the client explicitly noting he felt listened to. Warmth was somewhat brief and clinical at times, with limited validation of his shame about proving himself as a father.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections captured Wiremu's core conflict, but empathy often stayed surface-level. Painful disclosures about remorse, fear of testing, and grief over limited visits were frequently bypassed in favor of task lists.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol reduction, the violence program, housing, and reunification. Wiremu left with a concrete, sequenced plan and practical tools he found genuinely useful.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Planning was invited at each stage and adjusted to the client's feedback, including script revisions. However, co-construction was one-directional, with the counselor drafting and the client approving rather than brainstorming his own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice points were offered, and the client freely shaped content such as honesty about drinking. Rapid-fire suggestion lists carried a mildly prescriptive flavor, with little explicit affirmation of his ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong client change talk was tapped early but rarely deepened or amplified. Much of the session ran on counselor-generated advice rather than eliciting the client's own reasons, strengths, and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Support was framed collaboratively and the client had meaningful influence on decisions. Yet the counselor generated nearly all content, positioning themselves as the plan's author rather than drawing on the client's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Wiremu volunteered strong commitment language ('For them, I can do anything') that went unreflected and undeveloped. Open questions were useful but opportunities to amplify confidence and commitment were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was gently redirected toward action, such as reframing freedom concerns as a trade-off. But there were no double-sided reflections or affirmations linking his fears to his values, and redirection was sometimes abrupt.
