# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with consistent validation, though affirmations leaned generic ('That's great,' 'That's solid') and missed chances to affirm Erik's specific strengths like perseverance after the Thursday slip.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured both pride and frustration and Erik's ambivalence, but emotional empathy lagged — his disclosure of self-comparison to younger gym-goers and self-criticism was quickly bypassed toward planning rather than reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange advanced toward the chosen dietary goal: trigger time, food swap, procurement, reminder, backup plan, wrapper-count metric, and midweek check-in. Gym talk was integrated rather than allowed to drift the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions were co-constructed at every step — Erik generated the swap, metric, wife's role, and check-in details, while the counselor asked his input on how future sessions should run. Power-sharing was exemplary.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated choice language and options framing left Erik owning the entire plan, demonstrated by his closing 'Small steps, actual plan, wife keeping me honest.' No directives or pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully evoked Erik's own reasons, math, solutions, and success measures — scaling raised confidence from 6 to 8, and the closing self-generated insight ('Six months ago I couldn't climb stairs... that's not nothing') is the core evocation outcome.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik's contributions genuinely shaped the session and process, but the counselor occasionally offered pre-formulated solution menus (Sunday prep, 2:30 reminder, tea/nuts pairing) before fully drawing out Erik's own ideas, tilting the expertise balance slightly clinician-ward.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open agenda-setting, confidence scaling, and evocative questions consistently produced rich client change language, each affirmed and built upon. The final evocation even prompted Erik's own motivational insight about self-talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly and reframed as solvable barriers, with slips normalized and self-compassion framed. Asking 'What might realistically get in the way?' produced two rounds of obstacle talk without a double-sided reflection leveraging Erik's own resilience ('I still went' after the Thursday slip).
