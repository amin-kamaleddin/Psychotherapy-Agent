# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist is respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, validating frustration and offering affirmation ('I'm proud you're protecting those last three weeks'). Acceptance is slightly undermined by the premature 'You didn't cause this,' which contradicted the client's emerging ownership.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections (frustration, dad's birthday), but the session quickly pivots to problem-solving before exploring emotional layers. Tariq's partial self-insight was met with reassurance rather than an empathic reflection of his ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the phone privilege dispute and staying write-up-free until release, producing concrete, useful steps. Forward-looking goals (school/job) were deferred rather than connected as motivational material.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaboration exists (asking about changes, incorporating Tariq's added line), but the counselor largely supplies plans and scripts while the client reacts. Little joint problem-solving or co-creation from the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options are offered and Tariq's choices respected ('I'll say it my way'), but the approach is heavily prescriptive with ready-made scripts and specific instructions. Autonomy support is weakened by the controlling, solution-driven framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation came almost entirely from the client; questions were logistical rather than motivational. No open questions explored why staying out of trouble matters or his hopes post-release despite him raising them.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor operates from an expert stance, drafting scripts and directing the pace. Tariq's input shapes the drafts somewhat, but the agenda and solutions are primarily counselor-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged organically (mom counting on him, staying clean, school/job plans) but was not strategically evoked or deepened. The counselor redirected to logistics instead of developing commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided reinforcing blame and redirected toward action, which was appropriate. However, Tariq's partial ownership ('maybe I popped off at him') was dismissed with reassurance rather than engaged with via double-sided reflections.
