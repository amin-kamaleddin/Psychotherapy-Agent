# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental and unconditional regard throughout; validates her choice to keep the piercing and even helps her craft a dentist script. No disapproval expressed despite the risk discussion.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured her autonomy theme and defensiveness ('it felt dramatic', 'that makes sense'). Reflections were sometimes brief and could have gone deeper into the emotional/identity meaning of the piercing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session anchored to oral health and the piercing with forward-moving focused questions. Early broad menu options and lack of an explicitly negotiated change target kept it from a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Joint problem-solving with permission-seeking before sharing information and a client-selected plan. Falls short of 5 because risk information was delivered as a list rather than drawn from her existing knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding — repeatedly affirms choice ('You're in charge', 'without pressuring you') and uses a harm-reduction approach that honors her decision. Patient's closing remark ('you just... let me decide') confirms success.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from within through option menus and a confidence scaling question that produced her own reasoning. Limited evocation of deeper personal values — stayed at the practical behavioral level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the patient as the expert with questions like 'Any of those feel doable?' Her preferences shaped the final plan. Occasionally moved quickly to offering information and strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited commitment language ('rinsing is easy enough, I could do that') and her own reasoning via the confidence question (8/10). A menu of options let her generate her own plan; earlier desire/need talk could have been stronger.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not argue with strong sustain talk; pivoted skillfully to harm reduction and used double-sided reflections. Could have more deliberately reframed sustain talk toward values-based change language.
