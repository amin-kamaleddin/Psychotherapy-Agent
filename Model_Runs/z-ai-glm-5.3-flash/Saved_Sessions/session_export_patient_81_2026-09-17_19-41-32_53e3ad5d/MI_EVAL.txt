# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental throughout, including in response to shameful disclosures of weekday solo use. Affirmations such as 'You came up with both yourself' and 'You leave with a plan that's yours' reinforce unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately captured ambivalence, identity dilemmas, and the client's emerging sense of agency. Missed an opportunity to briefly sit with the emotional weight of the 'least proud of' disclosure before moving to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, purposeful focus on meth use from start to finish, progressing logically through patterns, costs, desired future, obstacles, and a concrete plan. Well-timed scaling and narrowing questions kept momentum without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction read as a genuine partnership, with co-constructed plans and negotiated mutual agreement ('That's fair, yeah?' — 'That's more than fair'). The client's ideas demonstrably shaped every element of the outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy framing throughout — no pressure toward abstinence and explicit honoring of the client's choice to remain in the scene. Minor deduction for the closing imperative directive about transferring rent money payday morning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within the client, with no persuasive reasoning or unsolicited rationale until the closing summary. The 'what would that prove about you' question elicited particularly rich change talk and insight.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('You're the expert on your life and your scene'), and both concrete strategies originated entirely from the client. Exemplary power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful use of open questions targeting the client's own motivations produced rich change talk, including 'getting my career back on track matters more to me than any party does' and 'it's a choice I'm making.' The meaning/confidence evocation was textbook.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled effectively through reflection and pivoting toward what is workable, without arguing or dwelling. Minor deduction because some sustain talk was echoed back verbatim and occasionally in full before redirecting.
