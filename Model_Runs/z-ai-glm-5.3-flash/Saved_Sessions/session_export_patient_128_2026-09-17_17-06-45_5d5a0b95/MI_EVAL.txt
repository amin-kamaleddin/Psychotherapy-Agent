# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance with explicit affirmations ("smart to use Marcus," "Nice — that sounded natural"), and Tyler confirmed feeling respected ("I thought this was gonna be a lecture, and it wasn't"). However, warmth occasionally felt procedural, with rapid question stacking limiting space for deeper individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured Tyler's ambivalence between school and social life, but empathy thinned as the session became plan-driven. Vulnerable admissions ("I'm not sure I'd actually walk out," "that sounds so lame") received logistical solutions rather than emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the target behavior throughout, with every element — drink limits, refusal lines, buddy plan, prebooked ride, check-in — tied to reducing risky alcohol use and protecting the scholarship. A clear strength of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Real participatory moments existed (Tyler co-constructed the plan and set his own check-in), but power tilted counselor-ward through stacked questions and supplied scripts, prompting Tyler's boundary ("don't need you ghostwriting my friendship"). Collaboration was present but uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice was affirmed at several points and Tyler ultimately owned key decisions, but dense multi-part instructions and micro-choices within a pre-set menu conveyed a counselor-designed protocol more than genuine client-directed exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Importance and confidence rulers successfully drew out Tyler's own reasons (scholarship, education) and self-generated strategies (not pregaming, buddy plan), and confidence rose from ~5 to 7. However, the habit of immediately supplying solution menus short-circuited development of his own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited collaboration and incorporated Tyler's choices (Marcus, casual refusal line, Sunday check-in), but repeatedly adopted an expert stance with scripted lines, message rewrites, and pre-built plans. Tyler's ideas were incorporated as add-ons rather than as the foundation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective use of importance and confidence rulers evoked values-based change talk ("School matters to me — that's not BS") and commitment language ("I'll actually try this"). However, change talk was often answered with counselor-generated solutions rather than deepened, and motivation was never consolidated through a summary of Tyler's own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was initially reflected neutrally without amplification, but no double-sided reflections linked social concerns to change talk. The plan quietly colluded with sustain themes, and Tyler's self-generated idea (not pregaming) went unreinforced.
