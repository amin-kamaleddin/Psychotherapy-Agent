# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirmed the client's proactive behavior, but warmth was formulaic and repeated nearly verbatim across closing turns, suggesting scripted rather than individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reflections were accurate but shallow; the counselor did not reflect the emotional impact of learning about serotonin syndrome or the tension between safety concerns and time pressure, pivoting instead to information delivery.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed on the medication-safety topic appropriately, but there was no MI-directed movement toward exploring readiness, ambivalence, or a collaborative goal, and the final turns were redundant, wasting the client's limited time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor dominated in an expert role, delivering pharmacological guidance and a pre-built checklist with little shared agenda-setting; the client's time constraint was accommodated but not used to negotiate a joint plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered limited genuine choice and never pressured the client to stay, but overall the stance was advisory, with directives repeated as commands rather than options and the client's decision-making never explicitly affirmed as her own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no evocation of the client's motivations, values, or reasons for change; all substantive content came from the counselor, who assumed information-giving substituted for drawing out her perspective.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was largely expert-driven with a detailed information package dominating; the client's contributions did not meaningfully shape session content, and the collaborative opening was quickly abandoned in favor of advisement.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client spontaneously expressed preparation-oriented change talk, but the counselor only praised it without deepening it; no open questions were asked about her motivations or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not reinforce reasons against change and gracefully accepted the client's time constraints, but made no active effort to strategically use or double-side the sustain talk to pivot toward her own reasons for change.
