# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, including affirming the smoking quit and validating the client's choices without judgment. Ambivalence and minimizations were met with steady respect and affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured core values ('present for your kid') and concerns ('control without feeling policed'). Reflections were somewhat sparse and compressed, often bundled with rapid pivots to strategy, limiting deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on reducing drinking at social gatherings, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete, rehearsed plan. Every turn advanced the target without coercion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving with menus of ideas, Daniel selecting and modifying, and collaborative role-play. The client's closing comment that it 'turned into an actual plan' confirms the collaborative feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength — Daniel set his own limit, chose his tactics, and authored his own scripts. Rejected suggestions were accepted without resistance, and all supports were framed as optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from Daniel's own reasons, strategies, and confidence ratings. However, the psychiatrist occasionally supplied option lists and scripts rather than evoking them with open questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniel's expertise was treated as central and his contributions substantially shaped the plan. The flow remained slightly expert-driven, with the psychiatrist supplying option menus and scripts rather than fully co-creating them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of desire, need, and commitment language from the opening invitation through scaling questions and role-play. Confidence rose from six to seven with explicit self-efficacy statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and de-emphasized without being dismissed, with effective pivots to practical solutions. The 'boring one at the party' concern was never explicitly reframed, only implicitly addressed.
