# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained even at peak resistance — 'I respect that. You're the one who knows what's negotiable and what isn't' — with no disapproval, labeling, or moralizing when the client withholds commitment or voices hostility toward prior care. Warmth was never withdrawn, even welcoming the client's self-announced negativity at the close.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and frequent, including complex reflections that captured meaning, failure, and hope ('patches sat there doing nothing, but you're curious gum might work differently') and correct identification of the social core of the barrier. Held at 4 due to thin emotional reflection: the client's health-fear and social embarrassment threads were repeatedly pivoted to practical problem-solving rather than having the feelings underneath named.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every clinician turn advanced the target behavior with a clear progressive arc — ambivalence, negotiable boundaries, concrete outcomes, barriers, solutions, product selection, trial day, consolidation. Focus was maintained while tactfully re-routing through the client's own readiness level rather than imposing a full-quit agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Work was consistently framed as joint ('That honesty helps us figure this out together'), with permission sought before offering observations ('Can I hold something up?'). Professional knowledge was offered as 'what usually fits' with the decision immediately returned to the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Pervasive and explicit autonomy support: choice menus at every decision point ('Fruit versus mint is your call'), reassurance that nothing is imposed ('No one's signing you up for anything — evenings stay yours'), and the emerging motivation protected from clinician capture ('That thought's yours to keep or drop'). Minor stylistic quibble: the ownership formula repeated nearly verbatim in the final turns, approaching formula.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was evoked systematically across nearly every turn via vision questions ('What would taking the edge off actually look like for you?') and strength evocation ('What's already in you that's handled tough days before?'). Client language visibly escalated from 'maybe during the day' to 'Monday it is,' with only a minor missed opportunity to deepen the spontaneously offered money motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('You're the one who knows what's negotiable,' 'You'd know what fits your week'), and the entire plan — quiet approach, Kofi, flavor, strength, Monday trial — was client-generated, with the clinician reflecting, organizing, and affirming. This is active power-sharing in which client input substantively determined the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The strongest domain of the session: skillful evocation reinforced with agency-attributing affirmations ('it came entirely from you') and a textbook discrepancy summary contrasting the client's entry and exit positions. Commitment escalated visibly across the session, culminating in unsolicited spontaneous change talk ('maybe Monday's the start of something').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ('I don't really wanna quit — I like smoking,' 'not promising anything') was never argued with, debated, or amplified. Handled through double-sided reflections ending on the change side, respectful side-lining of the evening boundary, and strategic redirection toward domains of openness — with no questions eliciting more reasons to keep smoking.
