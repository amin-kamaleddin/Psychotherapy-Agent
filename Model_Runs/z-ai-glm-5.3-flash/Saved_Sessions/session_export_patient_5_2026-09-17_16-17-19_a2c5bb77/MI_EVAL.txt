# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor is consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental, affirming Alessia's carefulness and validating her anxiety about the washout. However, affirmations are brief and formulaic, with limited explicit normalization of her uncertainty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate, tracking Alessia's safety concerns and preference for practical wording. Reflections remain factual summaries rather than deeper exploration of emotional meaning, such as fear of relapse during the washout or worry about second-guessing her prescriber.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on safely transitioning from phenelzine to citalopram, progressing logically to a concrete plan and a stated commitment to book the prescriber visit. Some segments drift into exhaustive symptom lists and repeated script refinement without checking whether more detail was needed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers to rehearse together, tailors deliverables to Alessia's requests, and lets key decisions emerge from her own reasoning. The stance is weakened by repeated expert corrections of her rehearsed script, tilting the exchange toward evaluation rather than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly emphasizes Alessia's choice and control, offering options and directing medication decisions back to her prescriber rather than issuing directives. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit affirmation of her ownership of the decision-making process and her capability.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client motivation is elicited at the outset and Alessia's priorities surface naturally. Much of the session consists of the counselor providing reasons and information rather than drawing out Alessia's own values, hopes, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Alessia's input is invited at several points and shapes the content, but the majority of the session is counselor-driven information delivery with the counselor in the expert role. Expertise is not consistently framed as residing within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Alessia spontaneously produces change talk and the counselor opens with a change-oriented question. Opportunities to deepen commitment language (e.g., when she says she'll call the prescriber) are missed, and much of the session is informational rather than deliberately evoking and consolidating change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without reinforcement and reframed as reasonable caution. Strategic double-sided reflections to pivot toward change are absent, and repeated serotonin syndrome symptom lists may inadvertently amplify anxiety rather than resolve ambivalence.
