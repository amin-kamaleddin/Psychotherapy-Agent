# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validating her coping and normalizing slippage. However, the repeated prescriptive checklists felt like pressure, with the client noting 'You really don't let up, do you?'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Included a standout reflection of the cigarette as a 'five-minute nobody needs me reset,' but empathy was inconsistent. Key emotional cues about isolation and fear of judgment ('everyone just gives me the look') went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking in the context of her son and coping, progressing to a concrete, time-bound experiment. Focus was somewhat diluted by escalating micro-instructions in the closing exchanges rather than consolidating the single agreed plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ideas flowed almost entirely from counselor to client, with the client positioned as evaluator of pre-packaged options rather than co-creator. No joint agenda-setting or exploration of her own knowledge and past attempts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respected autonomy by affirming not wanting to quit and using a choice-point experiment design. Undermined somewhat by re-issuing instructions after she signaled she had it, reinforcing an external locus ('I'm doing this because you asked me to').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain: the counselor supplied rationales and tool menus rather than eliciting her own motivations or values. Spontaneous change talk ('He's my whole world') was passed over without reflection or deepening.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for preferences repeatedly, but the expert-driven style meant nearly all ideas came from the counselor. The client's own expertise and solutions were never solicited, and the final checklist overrode her stated 'I've got it.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully engineered a clever choice point that produced change talk, but missed chances to deepen it. No open questions linking her parental values to potential change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing against sustain talk and gave brief acknowledgments before pivoting. However, responded to doubt with more solution lists rather than double-sided reflections connecting her values to her concerns.
