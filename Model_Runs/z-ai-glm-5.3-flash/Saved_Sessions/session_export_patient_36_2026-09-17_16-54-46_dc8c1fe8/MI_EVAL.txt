# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard, affirming Elena's choices without judgment or pressure. Statements normalizing imperfection ('Be gentle with yourself — awkwardness is still progress') reflect genuine acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** While early reflections are accurate, most counselor turns skip reflection and jump to advice or planning questions. Emotionally significant material — such as Elena's pattern of automatic agreement and her fear of guilt — goes unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains an exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior (saying no at work) from the first message through the close, with every turn tying back to the script, rehearsal, or check-in plan. The session progresses logically with excellent momentum and no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is largely co-constructed — Elena picks the step, sets the day, designs the check-in, and creates her own validating line. However, several turns present fully formed structures (prompts, if-then script, rating instructions) that Elena accepts rather than negotiates, giving the interaction a slightly pre-packaged quality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is regularly supported through offering choices, asking permission, and honoring Elena's preference to write her own validating line. Points are deducted for frequently supplying unsolicited tips and detailed plans without first asking whether Elena wants that level of guidance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes motivation effectively at the start (vision question, confidence scale), but then shifts almost entirely to providing structure and checklists. Rich evocative material — Elena's insight about automatic agreement, 'learning to back myself up' — is met with logistics instead of deeper exploration, making evocation feel front-loaded and superficial.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly conveys that expertise resides within Elena by offering options, inviting her to create her own validating line, and asking which steps feel most aligned. However, rapid-fire multi-question turns and prescriptive tips at times lean toward the expert role rather than fully letting Elena's contributions shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is elicited early through the confidence scaling question and vision question, drawing out rich desire and reason statements. However, Elena's strongest statements receive no reflection or amplification, the confidence rating is never explored (e.g., why 8 and not lower), and most later turns shift to logistics rather than evoking deeper commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elena shows almost no sustain talk, and the counselor redirects obstacles toward solutions like rehearsal and if-then plans. A minor deduction applies because repeatedly asking 'what might get in the way' subtly rehearses resistance without actively pivoting back to Elena's strengths and readiness.
