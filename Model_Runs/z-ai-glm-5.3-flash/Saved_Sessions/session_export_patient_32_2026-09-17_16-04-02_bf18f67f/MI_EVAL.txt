# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental toward Derrick despite defensiveness and minimization, with affirmations offered. Warmth remains somewhat functional rather than deeply personal, with limited validation of his emotional struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects Derrick's perspective, including his insight about needing ride money before drinking. However, reflections lean practical and the emotional layer—loneliness, the breakup, feeling like an outcast—remains largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains tight focus on preventing repeat DUI throughout, with every exchange advancing a concrete plan. The session progresses logically from values to strategies to specific commitments with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint effort: co-writing the Marcus text, offering option menus, and building every plan element from Derrick's own ideas. Derrick responds as an active partner refining the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices and lets Derrick set his own confidence ratings and select strategies without pressure. Could more explicitly affirm Derrick's ownership and past success, especially at moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently draws motivation from Derrick himself rather than lecturing, culminating in his self-generated insight about isolation driving his past relapse. Confidence scaling is used skillfully to strengthen commitment rather than pressure.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Derrick as the expert, asking which options feel most doable and letting his ideas drive the plan. The counselor supplies most of the option menus, slightly constraining Derrick's generative role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through open questions, graded commitments, and repeated confidence scaling, producing strong client statements like 'Maybe that's the difference this time.' Commitment is built incrementally in textbook fashion.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argues with or reinforces sustain talk like 'just beer' and 'bad luck,' instead acknowledging briefly and pivoting to change. Minimization sometimes passes without explicit double-sided reflection before pivoting.
