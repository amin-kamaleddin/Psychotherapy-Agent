# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, normalizing the client's struggle without judgment. Affirmations like 'That persistence says something about you' conveyed genuine warmth and respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing the ambivalence and self-blame nuance. The client explicitly validated the reflections ('Yeah... exactly').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, progressive focus on increasing physical activity from the first question onward. Each exchange advanced toward a concrete commitment with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was fully co-constructed, with the client's own ideas (brother call, shoes by the door) genuinely shaping the outcome. Questions framed as joint inquiry fostered a shared enterprise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly reinforced client ownership ('That's your own judgment forming') and actively offered choice about pacing. Every plan element originated with the client, and the counselor never prescribed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was systematically drawn from within via ideal-week visualization, projected feelings, and a confidence ruler. All reasons, safeguards, and commitment language were self-authored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the client as the expert on his own schedule and rhythms. The client's contributions substantially determined the session's content and outcome.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was evoked and deepened through open questions and the confidence ruler, yielding strong client statements ('One walk tonight wouldn't hurt'). The counselor amplified it via affirmations and future visualization.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (couch temptation, fear of being a cop-out) was acknowledged but consistently reframed toward agency rather than amplified. Doubts were resolved by the client himself using the counselor's 'small and real' framing.
