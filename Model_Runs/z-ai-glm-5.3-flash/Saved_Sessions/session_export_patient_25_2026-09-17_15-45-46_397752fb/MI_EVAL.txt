# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and respectful, with strong affirmation of the patient's smoking cessation success and no judgment about the alcohol disclosure. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and could have been more individualized to the patient's specific values (e.g., fatherhood, the wife's 'look').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captured the patient's ambivalence and emotions accurately, including pride, guilt, and the sting from his wife. Reflections shallowed later in the session, with key emotional layers (fear of social identity change, fatherhood guilt) never explicitly reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in social settings throughout, with each turn building toward a concrete, rehearsed plan. No topic drift, with effective guidance toward commitment (Marco's text, rough-week backup plan).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions about strategies and scripts were largely made with the patient's input, and role-play was offered and accepted collaboratively. The psychiatrist supplied most concrete tools rather than drawing them from the patient, making the power balance uneven at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and let the patient select strategies, tone, and scripts, supporting genuine ownership. Some language was slightly directive, and the rapid-fire option menus sometimes channeled the patient toward pre-framed solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong elicitation of the patient's own motivation through scaling questions and open questions that produced his reasons and plan ('I don't want my son seeing me hungover'). Weakened by the psychiatrist frequently offering solutions rather than consistently drawing them from the patient's own experience.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient's expertise was respected by asking what felt most doable, but the middle of the session became expert-driven, with the psychiatrist generating all strategies and scripts. The patient's role was largely selecting among options rather than co-creating ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of scaling questions evoked concrete change talk, with confidence rising from 5 to 7–7.5 and the patient articulating his own plan and reasons. Opportunities were missed to deepen the patient's own articulated reasons with further evocative follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('the guy who can't have a beer with friends,' 'the guys might rib me') was never reinforced—instead it was normalized and met with supportive pivots. Responses occasionally jumped to problem-solving rather than MI-consistent softening.
