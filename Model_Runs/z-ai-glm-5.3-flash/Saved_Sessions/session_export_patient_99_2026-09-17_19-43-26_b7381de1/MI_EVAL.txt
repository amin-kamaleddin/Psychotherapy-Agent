# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard from start ('I'm not here to judge') to finish ('I'll be genuinely glad to hear it'). Validated Matteo's self-presentation without labeling, affirming his capacity and welcoming any outcome either way.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening captured both surface content and deeper meaning, e.g., reflecting the value beneath the practical concern ('you don't want a whole semester on crutches over one night'). Complex reflections like 'drinking is baked into how you socialize' were consistently accurate.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed anchored to the target behavior and built progressively toward a concrete plan and follow-up. Early pacing was entirely client-led; a timely consolidating summary could have consolidated focus sooner, though no drift occurred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session read as two equals problem-solving together, with every strategy co-constructed from Matteo's own ideas and no directives offered. The plan was assembled out loud from his contributions and he was invited to judge the results himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Matteo chose whether and how to change, which strategies, and what follow-up looked like, with the plan framed as 'something you're choosing on purpose.' Matteo's own comment that this 'was better than getting a lecture' shows the choice-preserving stance was felt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All reasons, strategies, success markers, and commitment language came from Matteo, drawn out through questions like 'What would make it feel doable?'. Motivation was never supplied—Matteo even volunteered an unsolicited follow-through commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located in the client ('You know your life better than anyone'), with Matteo's contributions demonstrably shaping the session. The checkpoint idea originated from him and became the plan's centerpiece.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open evocative questions and a confidence ruler elicited rich desire, ability, and commitment language ('worth a shot,' 'I can do that'). Change talk was reinforced and moved from vague to concrete and self-authored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('college is college,' roommate teasing) was never reinforced or judged, but handled via double-sided reflections and affirmations of coping ideas. The arc from 'bad luck' to a self-authored plan shows sustain talk softened without confrontation.
