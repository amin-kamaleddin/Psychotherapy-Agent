# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone despite hostility, affirming the client without judgment. However, warmth at times felt formulaic and solution-focused rather than deeply accepting.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections early on, but they were brief, repetitive, and pivoted too quickly to option menus instead of lingering in the client's emotional experience. A key moment of hopelessness ('I get in trouble either way') was missed in favor of offering solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kept a loose, appropriate focus on the household conflict, but repeated three-option menus created fragmentation and the grounding exercise was a detour. Real direction only emerged late with the caseworker question, which was not developed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tone was collegial with frequent options offered, but the rapid-fire menu format replaced genuine joint exploration. The counselor supplied answers (scripts, note drafts) rather than co-creating solutions with the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear strength: consistent choices, honored refusals, and explicit statements like 'We can keep this on your terms.' Deducted for proceeding without re-offering autonomy when the client deferred ('you pick') and for somewhat prescriptive scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and ideas were largely supplied by the counselor rather than elicited, with almost no open questions inviting the client's own reasons or solutions. The single client-generated element (the caseworker question) emerged despite, not because of, skillful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, supporting partnership, but simply took over the grounding exercise when the client deferred rather than negotiating shared ownership. Offering pre-written scripts positioned the counselor as the expert rather than drawing out the client's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A small but meaningful moment of change talk emerged ('Maybe the caseworker thing'), yet it was not deepened. There were no deliberate evocative questions or affirmations of the client's own agency.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue with or reinforce sustain talk, and reflections acknowledged it without amplification. However, no strategic use of double-sided reflections or deliberate pivoting toward change; sustain talk was mostly sidestepped via option menus.
