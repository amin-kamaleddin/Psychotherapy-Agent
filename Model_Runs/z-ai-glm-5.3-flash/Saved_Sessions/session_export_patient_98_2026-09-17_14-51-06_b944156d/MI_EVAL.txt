# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and validation of the patient's skepticism. Falls short of a 5 as affirmations are somewhat generic and the depth of his discouragement is not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurate, capturing the ambivalence between effort and discouragement. Reflections occasionally stay at the surface of the plan rather than exploring the emotional weight of loss and fear of disability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on a single target behavior — noticing small progress via a nightly one-line log. Every exchange narrows concretely toward a specific, testable commitment with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-created, with the patient's ideas (notepad placement, 'number first') directly shaping it. Occasionally the counselor front-loads solution menus rather than eliciting the patient's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session opens by handing control to the patient, with genuine choice menus and permission-seeking; the patient freely adapts or rejects suggestions. Reinforcement of the patient's ownership of the change itself could be more consistent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions and win-definition prompts effectively draw out the patient's own motivations. The counselor does not linger to amplify the most powerful motivations (fear of disability, wife's support) before shifting to plan mechanics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient's expertise is repeatedly positioned as central, with the plan shaped largely by his own solutions. The counselor at times supplies option menus in a slightly leading way.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Active evocation through importance/confidence scaling and defining a 'win' produced meaningful change talk from the patient. Change talk could be deepened by selective reflection rather than moving quickly to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is skillfully reframed neutrally ('just data, not judgment') with preemptive problem-solving of barriers. Little use of double-sided reflections to pivot the patient's own words toward change.
