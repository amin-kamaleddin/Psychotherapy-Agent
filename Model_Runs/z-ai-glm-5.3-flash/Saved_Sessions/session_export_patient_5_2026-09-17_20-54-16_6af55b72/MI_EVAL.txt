# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicates respect, positive regard, and validation with no judgment or disapproval. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and repetitive ('You should feel proud' appears multiple times).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening, accurately reflecting the client's values and emotional states. Reflections occasionally serve as preambles to questions or information delivery rather than deep emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior throughout every turn, with each response tied to the safety question, the plan, or appointment preparation. The session progresses logically with no drift, consistently guiding toward concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaves as an equal partner by asking the client's views, checking in, and co-constructing next steps around her preferences. Key decisions like holding the medication were presented as conclusions rather than negotiated jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support through offering choices and respecting the client's decision-making about contacting her doctor. The safety directive ('Please don't start the citalopram') was delivered without the client's own reasoning, though justified by the danger.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally draws out the client's own motivations and readiness, but inconsistently. Much of the session consists of the counselor supplying reasons for change rather than eliciting the client's own values and personal reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly locates expertise in the client and asks rather than tells at key junctures. In the critical safety moment, the counselor shifted into expert mode delivering directives without checking the client's understanding or inviting collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some preparatory change talk through open questions that drew out commitment language ('I'll call my doctor's office tomorrow,' 'I feel ready'). This was not consistent or strategic, with no deliberate elicitation of confidence/importance or deepening of commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledges the client's uncertainty, fear, and frustration without reinforcing them, and reframes the frustration as legitimate. Sustain talk was mostly absorbed rather than actively softened using double-sided reflections that pivot toward motivation.
