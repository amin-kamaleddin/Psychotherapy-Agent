# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and non-judgmental, normalizing Oliver's conflict and affirming his progress. However, affirmations are generic and formulaic, and the counselor repeatedly offered scripts after Oliver stated he preferred finding his own words.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; feelings like guilt and ambivalence were met with reassurance and bulleted plans rather than reflections. Concerns about freezing up and not wanting to threaten the flatmate were not reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed firmly focused on the target behavior of asking the flatmate to leave, from opening to close. Focus was maintained consistently, though driven more by the counselor's agenda than collaboratively clarified goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The exchange was largely expert-prescribes / client-responds, with options presented as the counselor's lists rather than elicited. The role-play was a genuinely collaborative exception, but client-generated ideas were rarely built upon.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite permission-seeking questions, the counselor repeatedly offered scripts after Oliver declined them and prescribed consequences, deadlines, and exact wording. Oliver ultimately asserted autonomy himself ('no, I've got it') rather than because the counselor supported it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplied reasons, strategies, and language rather than drawing motivation from within, with virtually no evocative open questions or exploration of values. Oliver's motivation emerged spontaneously despite, not because of, the counselor's approach.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role with prescriptive lists of steps and scripts, offering to draft a script three times despite Oliver explicitly saying he needed his own words. Oliver was not treated as the expert on his own life, and his contributions rarely shaped the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Oliver generated substantial change talk on his own, but the counselor made no deliberate effort to evoke or deepen it, missing opportunities for questions like 'What makes now the right time?'. Change talk development was accidental rather than skillfully cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not argue for the status quo or dwell on sustain talk, but also failed to actively soften it with double-sided reflections. The advice-driven strategy list risked entrenching Oliver's fear of being harsh rather than helping him reframe firmness as caring.
