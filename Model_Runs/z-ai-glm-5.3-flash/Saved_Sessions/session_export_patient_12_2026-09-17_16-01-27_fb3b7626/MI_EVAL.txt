# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental ('I'm here without judgment') and accepts Amira's limits and privacy requests without pushback. Warmth is somewhat formulaic and affirmations lack individualization beyond her small step.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections capture Amira's core themes of helplessness and isolation ('being alone with your thoughts is the hardest part'). Reflections sometimes restate content rather than deepening the emotional hurt beneath 'people give up on me fast'.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains unwavering focus on one target behavior—a small evening walk and 15-minute urge strategy—from options through closing summary. The plan crystallizes concretely ('9:30, one loop, headphones') with no topic drift despite the client's hopelessness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine joint planning—Amira's preferences (evenings, music, privacy, no tracking) demonstrably shaped the final plan. Counselor front-loads multiple suggestions per turn, occasionally tilting toward advisor rather than equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit, repeated reinforcement of Amira's control: pace, privacy, no reporting, choices at every step. Her boundary against tracking is honored without resistance, and follow-up is explicitly left to her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Draws motivation from Amira via choice menus and open prompts, eliciting her own reasons ('maybe waiting is better than repeating that') and commitment ('I said I'd try, so I'll try'). Evocation of deeper personal values and hope beyond the behavioral plan was limited, with suggestion-heavy turns sometimes replacing open questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions Amira as expert ('Which feels least risky or most tolerable?'), and her input shaped the plan's specifics. Some key elements (options menu, grounding technique, coping phrase) were therapist-generated rather than drawn from her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced commitment language ('I can try it tonight,' 'Nine-thirty tonight. One loop') from a precontemplative client. Missed opportunities to deepen commitment via confidence rulers or exploring why the walk matters to her.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively uses double-sided reflections to acknowledge sustain talk without amplifying it, then pivot to willingness ('you think a walk won't solve the bigger stuff, and still you're willing to try tonight'). Reflections occasionally restate the negative more than necessary, diluting the pivot.
