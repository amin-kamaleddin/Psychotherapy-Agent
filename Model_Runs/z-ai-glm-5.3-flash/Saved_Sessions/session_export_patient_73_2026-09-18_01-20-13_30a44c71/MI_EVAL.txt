# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed the patient's efforts and reframed her perceived failures as strengths, never criticizing or judging her smoking history.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated, accurate reflections of the patient's fear of failure, ambivalence, and hopes (breathing easier, more money) showed deep understanding throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions about the imagined future and desired support kept the conversation clearly focused on change toward quitting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was explicitly co-designed with the patient choosing session frequency, check-in calls, and first steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized 'your call,' honored the patient's preference for small steps, and avoided prescriptive demands.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions drew out the patient's own motivations, worries, and solutions, resulting in strong change talk like 'it feels possible in a way it didn't before.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange felt like a genuine partnership, with the clinician echoing 'let's figure this out together' and crediting the patient for building the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully highlighted and reinforced the patient's change statements, from hopes of breathing better to booking the appointment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fear of failing and doubt about capability were met with normalization, reframing, and affirmation rather than confrontation.
