# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warmth and nonjudgment are conveyed through affirmations like 'That doesn't sound dumb at all—it sounds like real courage' and 'You did meaningful work today.' Falls short of 5 because mid-session affirmations are somewhat sparse and the weight-loss plateau is never explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate and emotionally attuned, e.g., 'outside, it's a loneliness you're choosing, on your own terms,' which the client explicitly confirms. The summary 'the lightness sounds like it's winning a little' precisely captures the ambivalence shift.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused and converges on a concrete behavioral step (texting Aina), with each turn building toward it. However, the presenting weight-loss/plateau concerns are never explicitly reconnected to the closing plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is fully power-sharing, with 'we could borrow' language and check-ins like 'Is that how it feels?' The Aina thread emerged entirely from the client, and the follow-up plan was jointly negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client selects the path ('pride moment or talking to Aina'), and the plan's timing and wording are entirely her own. No advice, persuasion, or directives are offered at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from the client via open questions ('how do you imagine you'll feel afterward?'), eliciting her own reasons, hope, and solutions. The client's shift from 'maybe nothing really helps' to 'I'm going to send that text' demonstrates evocation at its best.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly positions the client as the expert ('You're the expert on Aina and on what you can handle'). Every plan element originated from the client, substantially shaping the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and deepened, with commitment escalating from 'Maybe I'd text her this week' to 'I'm going to send that text.' Each step is reinforced without the clinician ever suggesting the plan herself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is strategically reframed, e.g., highlighting 'you're wondering if maybe that assumption isn't the whole truth' and treating spiral-planning as self-awareness. Misses a 5 for lack of explicit double-sided reflections tying sustain talk to change motivations.
