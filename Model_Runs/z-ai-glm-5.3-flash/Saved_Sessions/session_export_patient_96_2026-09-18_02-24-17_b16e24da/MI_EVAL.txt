# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout; validated Marisol's perspective even when she dismissed the dentist's concerns, with affirmations like 'I respect that' and no judgment or pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening capturing both content and Marisol's autonomy-focused worldview, including complex reflections like 'you're not closing any doors, you're just staying the one in charge.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained steady focus and progressively guided toward a concrete monitoring plan, but the session lacked an explicit summary of the final plan or a clear closing step, leaving the ending slightly loose.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative session, explicitly framed as 'let's figure it out together,' with choices offered rather than imposed and the final plan built almost entirely from Marisol's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support - 'I'm not here to tell you what to do,' optional framing of suggestions ('only if it appeals to you'), and repeated 'your call' messaging with no directives issued.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently elicited from the client rather than supplied - no lecturing, with open questions drawing out her own reasons (protecting her appearance, avoiding wasted money, maintaining ownership).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine power sharing that positioned Marisol as the expert ('you already know your own mouth better than anyone'), with her preferences directly shaping the plan - not token collaboration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and developed change talk in a pre-contemplative client - questions like 'What would tip it for you?' drew out her own thresholds, and questions about past consistency generated commitment language ('I'll do the checks').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided amplifying sustain talk using double-sided reflections and strategic pivots, though occasionally reflecting sustain talk back in detail (e.g., 'You're not taking it out over a sore spot') slightly extended focus on status quo language.
