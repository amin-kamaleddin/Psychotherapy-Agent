# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone with warm responses to the patient's skepticism. However, few explicit affirmations of the patient's strengths (e.g., marathon discipline, his own insights), which would be expected for a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate early reflections but understanding stayed shallow; the counselor normalized briefly rather than deeply reflecting the patient's surprise and ambivalence. Key moments (feeling overwhelmed by the plan) were skipped in favor of moving to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use throughout, with logical progression from assessment to readiness to a concrete plan and troubleshooting. No topic drift, even when the ankle came up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some input-seeking and check-in negotiation, but the plan's structure was largely dispensed by the counselor rather than co-constructed from the patient's motivations. Friendly interaction, but not fully between equal partners.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and respected the patient's boundaries, accepting his smaller commitment after pushback. The volume of prescriptive advice at times tilted toward expert-driven recommendations rather than reinforcing ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler and follow-up question effectively drew out the patient's own reasons, and his best motivational material (ankle, training) emerged partly from the counselor's information. However, the counselor relied on providing solutions rather than consistently asking the patient to generate his own reasons and ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and offered choices, but often assumed the expert role with prescriptive risk explanations, pre-packaged plans, and recipes. Missed opportunities to explicitly credit and build on the patient's own good ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Well-executed readiness ruler with a follow-up about moving from 3 to 4 produced meaningful change talk that was consolidated into goals. Missed chances to reflect and deepen change talk when it appeared, and rarely used open questions to amplify commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, and the 'data, not failure' reframe was effective. However, no double-sided reflections were used to actively pivot sustain talk toward change, leaving ambivalence less resolved than it could be.
