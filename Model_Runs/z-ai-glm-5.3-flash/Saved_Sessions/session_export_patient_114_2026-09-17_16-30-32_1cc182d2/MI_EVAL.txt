# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with respectful opening and closing affirmations. Ryan's defensiveness ('I don't get why I'm the one sitting here') wasn't fully addressed, which was a missed chance for unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections showed decent perspective-taking, but emotional depth was missed. Ryan's fears about internships and social judgment were only briefly acknowledged rather than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused and moved steadily forward, producing tangible outcomes. However, direction narrowed toward the hearing task rather than the underlying drinking behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative moments like co-editing the statement and confirming preferences, but the counselor authored the materials rather than eliciting Ryan's ideas first. It resembled a consultant refining a product more than two equal partners building understanding.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered choices about tone, next steps, and pacing, and accepted Ryan's decision to practice at home. However, unsolicited advice about party strategies was given without asking permission, which pressures a mandated client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor generated reasons and strategies himself rather than drawing them out of Ryan. Ryan's own motivations around education and his record were touched once but never developed into deeper exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Despite collaborative language like asking which options felt doable, the counselor largely took the expert role by drafting documents and structuring the session. Ryan's edits were incorporated, showing some genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Open questions early did elicit some change-adjacent language, but when Ryan offered tentative change talk ('maybe the buddy thing...'), the counselor jumped to advice instead of reinforcing it. Motivational energy went into drafting documents rather than evoking Ryan's own reasons to change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** I think you're right that when Ryan offered chance-adjacent things like the buddy system and exit plan, these were not explored deeper or reflected back. There was some accommodation of his minimizing frame ('out of character,' 'one rough night') rather than examining it.
