# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with explicit affirmation of the client's agency and self-expertise.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent reflective listening, including accurate double-sided reflections that hold the client's ambivalence (e.g., tiredness vs. still wanting this to work).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays focused on the health target and follows a natural arc from discouragement to the client's own vision, though direction is at times very client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is fully co-constructed from the client's own words — schedule, wording, and back-up plan all come from her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy actively reinforced throughout; the psychiatrist consistently emphasizes the client's choice and ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the client rather than supplied; key insights (mirror-based goals, emotional eating focus) emerge from her own words.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within the client, with open questions inviting her knowledge of her own schedule, patterns, and feelings.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is evoked skillfully and consistently, including asking what helped in past hard moments and exploring what 'this working' would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of settling, fear of rejection) is acknowledged briefly and redirected toward change rather than reinforced, with minor room to soften further.
