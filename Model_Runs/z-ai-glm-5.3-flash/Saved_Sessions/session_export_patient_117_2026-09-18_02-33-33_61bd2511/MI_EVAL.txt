# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, affirming Samuel's efforts and honoring his honesty about doubt without judgment. Sustain talk and self-criticism were accepted as legitimate rather than challenged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately captured both sides of Samuel's ambivalence, advancing understanding rather than parroting. The patient's responses ('Never really thought about it that way before') evidenced deepfelt understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained unwavering focus on smoking reduction, progressing logically from reviewing progress to identifying obstacles to co-developing a concrete plan, with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session functioned as a genuine partnership, with the counselor acting as a thinking partner who refined Samuel's half-formed ideas without taking over. The final plan was fully co-constructed from the patient's own contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice was imposed; the counselor consistently drew ideas from Samuel ('What ideas do you have...?') and affirmed his capacity for self-directed change. Every plan element originated from the patient.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently elicited Samuel's own reasons, values, and solutions, producing key insights such as pride as the true reward and the notebook as proof of the hopeful voice. The patient persuaded himself; the clinician never argued for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly located expertise within the patient ('You're the expert on your own patterns') and every plan element came from Samuel. His contributions demonstrably shaped the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoked rich change talk ('I actually want this,' confidence ratings of six to nine, a concrete plan), which the clinician then reinforced and amplified. Commitment language built progressively toward a self-generated next-step plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly and neutrally, then pivoted toward change without amplification — e.g., reflecting 'both are true right now' before a forward-looking question. No clinician utterance reinforced reasons against change.
