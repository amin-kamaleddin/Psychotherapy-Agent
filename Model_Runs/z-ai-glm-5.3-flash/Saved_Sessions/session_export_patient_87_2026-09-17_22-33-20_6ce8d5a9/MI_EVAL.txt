# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard is conveyed throughout, including nonjudgmental framing of potential setbacks and gentle reframing of Marco's request for luck into confidence in his own capability. No labeling or disapproval appears anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately capture both the practical and relational dimensions of Marco's ambivalence, including his deeper values around his father and sons. Reflections consistently land, with Marco building on them rather than correcting them.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear focus on eating habits is maintained with a structured arc from generating ideas to a concrete, dated plan. Emotionally rich material (his father's illness) is tied directly back to the target behavior as motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session proceeds between equals, with decisions co-constructed—Marco's ideas shape the direction while the counselor serves as a reflective sounding board. Marco explicitly confirms this, noting he wasn't handed 'another diet plan.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ownership is consistently reinforced: Marco selects the strategy, sets his own timing, drafts his own words, and names his backup plan. Even his request for luck is redirected back to his own agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every change element originates from Marco—his strategy, script, timing, and motivation—drawn out through evocative questioning rather than persuasion or advice. His self-efficacy grows visibly across the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power sharing is explicit, with the counselor emphasizing Marco's expertise on his own household and framing outcomes as shared ('we'll find out together'). The resulting plan is entirely Marco's own content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked through targeted questions (e.g., what to tell himself Thursday night, what it would take to move from rehearsing to doing) and amplified via reflections. Marco's confidence language visibly grows from 'a six' to 'more than a six next time.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I don't know if I'd stick to it,' 'it might all fall apart') is briefly validated and then pivoted toward agency with solution-focused questions and nonjudgmental reframes. It is never reinforced or dwelt upon—a textbook approach.
