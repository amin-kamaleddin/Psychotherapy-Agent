# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Non-judgmental and validating of defensiveness, but warmth is sparse and affirmations are generic and repetitive ('That's a solid choice — good job'). Little explicit unconditional positive regard or normalization of Marek's attachment to his beer-centered social identity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections, but the session is heavily advice- and information-driven. Marek's key emotional statements about the social meaning of drinking and fear of peer teasing are met with psychoeducation rather than reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on the target behavior and produces a specific, measurable, time-bound plan with a Monday follow-up. Falls short of 5 because direction comes mainly through counselor-driven instruction rather than guiding Marek to articulate his own goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marek's preferences shape the plan (four beers, water alternating, Tomas as backup), but long didactic blocks create an expert-to-layperson dynamic and the two-week trial and check-in structure were proposed rather than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change is repeatedly framed as Marek's decision and his rejection of tracking is honored ('It's my call in the end'). Falls short of 5 because frequent unsolicited tip lists subtly press toward the counselor's preferred solution.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marek's preferences are elicited at decision points, but motivation is largely supplied by the counselor through risk lists and strategies. No evocative open questions or importance/confidence scaling, and his nascent commitment language ('better to sort it now') is never deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission is loosely sought and options offered, but extensive counselor-generated advice blocks position the counselor as the expert. Marek's own ideas emerge despite rather than because of structured elicitation of his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful spontaneous change talk is secured into a concrete commitment (two-week trial, Monday check-in), but change talk is never deliberately evoked or deepened — no open questions exploring Marek's own reasons or benefits of cutting back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is prominent ('That's normal, isn't it?', 'part of me thinks it won't last') and is rarely redirected, usually answered with more facts or new tactics. No double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to Marek's stated concerns, leaving ambivalence unresolved.
