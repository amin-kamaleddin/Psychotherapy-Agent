# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, non-judgmental tone with occasional affirmations ('You're doing well,' 'Be gentle with yourself'), but warmth is sparse and formulaic. Missed opportunities to affirm Claudia's honesty, courage, and self-awareness at key disclosure moments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only one brief early reflection is offered, with no complex reflections anywhere in the session. Rich emotional material—the mood-drinking link, perfectionism-based fear of failure, and skepticism—goes unreflected and unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on reducing alcohol with concrete goals, structured self-monitoring, safety screening, and a specific follow-up. Loses a point for a prescriptive rather than collaborative delivery and leaving the mood-drinking connection unexplored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Preference questions are asked and the plan is adjusted to Claudia's suggestions, but the intervention itself was pre-packaged. The session resembles clinician-prescribing with client-tweaking rather than joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers choices (cut back vs. stop, check-in timing, coping alternatives) and normalizes slips. However, the core plan is delivered as near-verbatim directives across multiple turns, reading as instruction rather than fostering genuine ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no questions are designed to elicit Claudia's own reasons, confidence, or readiness for change. Her spontaneous hopefulness surfaces on its own and goes unexploited, while the clinician supplies reasons and tactics from an expert position.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The psychiatrist quickly assumes the expert role, presenting a pre-formed plan with prescriptive tactics rather than drawing strategies from Claudia's ideas. Her contributions are treated as logistics and her expertise is never explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerges almost entirely spontaneously from the client and is never deepened or reinforced. When Claudia expresses hopefulness, the psychiatrist ignores it rather than evoking more.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about alcohol-free nights, the water substitution, and potential slips receives no double-sided reflections, reframes, or strategic pivots. The psychiatrist simply moves on to logistics, missing chances to resolve ambivalence.
