# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms the patient's autonomy, explicitly stating 'I'm not here to tell you what to do' and honoring the patient's pace ('One step, on your own terms'). The patient's own change plan is endorsed as 'yours, not mine.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated, accurate reflective statements capture both the patient's ambivalence and deeper concerns, including the wish for trust rather than surveillance. Reflections are consistently well-timed and deepen the dialogue.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is anchored on exploring the patient's goals and first steps toward change, guiding toward self-motivated action. It remains responsive rather than prescriptive, which slightly limits a more explicit direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The tone is consistently a partnership — open questions, joint problem-solving ('would you want to figure that out together?'), and plans negotiated on the patient's terms.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is emphasized throughout: 'You get to decide your own pace,' and the patient's insistence that change must be 'my idea, for my reasons' is validated as self-respect.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's own motivations for change are skillfully drawn out — the vision of respect a year from now, the realization that drinking 'doesn't fit the guy I want to be,' and a self-generated concrete plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interactions are markedly collaborative and egalitarian, with the patient actively problem-solving. The guidance occasionally feels skillfully strategic, though the patient explicitly notices and responds well to it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk emerges progressively and naturally — from 'is that what you want to hear?' to the patient voicing a desire for respect, envisioning success, and committing to a test run, with the clinician reinforcing each step.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled without confrontation — the clinician reflects and holds both sides ('You don't have to pick a side right now'), and minimization ('That's not the same as saying I have a problem') is met with acceptance rather than argument.
