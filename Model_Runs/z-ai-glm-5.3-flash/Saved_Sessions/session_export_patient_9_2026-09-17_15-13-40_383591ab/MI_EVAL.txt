# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor communicated consistent warmth and respect, affirming Amina's efforts ('That was really good — warm, loving, brief') and validating her dilemmas without judgment. Affirmations were somewhat generic ('that makes sense' repeated) and deeper individualized positive regard was limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured her core fears and internal conflicts (e.g., honesty vs. burdening her son). Empathy was inconsistent, however, as reflections repeatedly gave way to stacked advice lists and multiple questions that crowded out deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained good focus on the target behavior — shielding her son during co-parenting conflict — and honored her stated goal through script rehearsal. Stacked multiple-choice questions fragmented momentum, and the session ended with an unanswered question and no closing summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interactive role-play format invited Amina's active participation ('Type what you'd say and I'll reflect and coach'). Collaboration was uneven, often following a counselor-proposes-options → Amina-selects flow rather than genuinely joint problem-solving, with prescriptive responses to her own thoughtful questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly framed the session around Amina's choices ('I'll follow your lead and support your choices') with no coercive language. Autonomy was subtly undermined by advice-heavy menus and prescribed scripts rather than helping her develop her own authentic wording, which she demonstrated she could do well.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening question about the most important outcome elicited her core value ('I want him to feel safe'), which was excellent evocation. Afterward, the counselor shifted to supplying scripts and tips rather than evoking — no scaling, confidence, or past-success queries — leaving her best insights self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening and choice-offering signaled partnership, and Amina's choices shaped the session. The counselor frequently slipped into an expert-driven coaching stance with repeated bulleted scripts and techniques, casting Amina as the trainee rather than acknowledging her own expertise about her son.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina's change talk was largely self-generated and the counselor effectively elicited value-based change talk early. However, her commitment to practicing this week was never consolidated with a summary, confidence question, or specific plan, and no deliberate strategies (scaling, importance/confidence queries) were used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided reinforcing her hopelessness ('why set up routines if he won't hold up his end?') and reasonably pivoted toward what she could control solo. The softening was largely implicit — no double-sided reflections, no reframing of her frustration as commitment, and her anxiety about 'we're still working on it' was handled with a substitute phrase without exploring the underlying fear.
