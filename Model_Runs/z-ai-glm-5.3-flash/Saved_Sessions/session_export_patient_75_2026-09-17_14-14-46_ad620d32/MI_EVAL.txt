# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with validation ('That's understandable') and affirmation ('You're making a good plan'). Affirmations were somewhat sparse and formulaic, with limited explicit warmth toward Linda's deeper fears.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were occasionally accurate but responses leaned heavily toward information-delivery rather than reflective listening. Emotional disclosures (fear of dependence, feeling 'managed' by her husband) were repeatedly met with plans and lists rather than deep reflection of underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed well-focused on asthma management and adherence, guiding toward concrete outcomes (question list, tracker, technique practice). Direction came primarily from the psychiatrist's agenda and structured deliverables rather than being linked to Linda's own values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda's requests directly shaped the session and choices were offered, but the interaction was largely question-and-answer with dense educational monologues. The collaborative structure was present while the spirit of equal partnership was uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered options ('printable tracker or reminder text?') and never pressured adherence, supporting her right to bring questions to her doctor. The heavy emphasis on instruction and checklists crowded out explicit ownership language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited Linda's priorities and her motivation grew, but largely through information and tools rather than evocative questions about her own reasons or confidence. Change motivation risked being externally anchored rather than drawn from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeated choice-offering supported some power-sharing and Linda's contributions shaped the session, but the overall stance remained expert-driven with long didactic blocks. Little explicit acknowledgment that Linda is the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda generated meaningful change talk ('I can commit to that much'), but the psychiatrist rarely explicitly evoked or reinforced it with confidence questions or amplifying summaries. The momentum was largely the client's own rather than skillfully cultivated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk and provided balanced information that softened some fears. However, expressions of ambivalence ('part of me still hesitates') were met with more information rather than double-sided reflections or reframes toward her desires for control.
