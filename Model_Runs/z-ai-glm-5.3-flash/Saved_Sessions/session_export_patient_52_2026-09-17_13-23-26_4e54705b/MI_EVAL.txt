# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, honoring requests like 'don't lecture me' and treating slips as 'data, not failure.' Falls short of top marks because warmth comes via checklists rather than individualized affirmations of her strengths as a working mother and student.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflective moments, but emotional content was often skipped in favor of option menus—most notably the client's guilt about choosing her drink over playtime, her core motivational lever. Verbatim triple repetition of the closing plan suggested formulaic rather than attuned listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the alcohol target and related coping throughout, with appropriate deferral of the daycare issue. Direction was maintained mechanically through checklists rather than collaborative goal clarification tied to her own values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative in form (choice menus, adapting check-in to text at her request) but expert-led in substance—the counselor generated all ideas via bullet lists while the client's role was limited to picking one. The plan's best elements were actually hers, yet this was never credited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed next steps as optional and client-chosen, explicitly releasing pressure ('data, not failure'). Falls short due to the triple repetition of instructions reading as gentle nagging and little affirmation of her capacity to direct her own change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little sense that motivation resides within the client; the counselor supplied reasons and solutions rather than eliciting her own. Her powerful intrinsic motivation (guilt, wanting to be present for her son) emerged spontaneously but was passed over each time without evocative follow-up.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Superficial preference-seeking ('which one feels easiest?') masked an overall expert-driven style of pre-packaged menus and dictated plan structure. Her own strong contributions (shower substitution, one-drink compromise) were never explicitly acknowledged as her expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Most change talk arose spontaneously from the client and was rarely deepened; responses to it were tips and praise rather than evocative exploration. Questions were mostly multiple-choice menus rather than open evocative questions about desire, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was mostly client-initiated and responded to neutrally, which avoided reinforcing it, but without active strategy. No double-sided reflections, reframing toward values, or gentle redirection—e.g., the 'not saying it's a huge problem' remark was simply bypassed.
