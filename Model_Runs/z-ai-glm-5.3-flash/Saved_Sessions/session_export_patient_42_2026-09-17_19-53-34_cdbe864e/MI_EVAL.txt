# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence ('smoking has been giving you something valuable'; 'it's not silly at all'). Warmth is genuine and never judgmental about Elise's smoking or hesitation to commit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing ambivalence ('real tug-of-war') and subtle shifts ('I hear some real hope in that'). Reflections consistently build on the client's exact words, e.g., 'instant friends' and 'be braver.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking and its social reinforcement, guiding progressively from exploration to a concrete plan (Wednesday ask, Saturday coffee) with a follow-up agenda. No topic drift; every exchange serves the change goal while respecting the client's pace.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully egalitarian stance: inclusive framing ('here's what I'm curious about'), check-ins, and a jointly built agenda—the client proposed the coffee plan and follow-up topic. Her closing comment ('You didn't lecture me') confirms the collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms choice and ownership ('on your own terms, at your own pace'; 'you don't have to solve that today'). Explicitly honors the client's boundary of 'not quitting yet' without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Draws motivation from within via open questions that elicit the client's own insights ('it's the belonging'; 'maybe I don't need the smoking as much'). Each piece of change talk is developed, culminating in a self-generated plan and hope.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions the client as expert ('You're the expert on your own social world'). Her contributions—the Marta example, the coffee idea, 'one brave thing at a time'—genuinely shape the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk with strategic questions and affirmations ('those were your words, and you earned them'). Amplifies momentum without pushing beyond the client's readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rolls with sustain talk rather than arguing—reframing 'the cigarette does the work' as 'the cigarette is just the doorway.' Validates difficulties ('that's real') and pivots to actionable steps while honoring the client's pace.
