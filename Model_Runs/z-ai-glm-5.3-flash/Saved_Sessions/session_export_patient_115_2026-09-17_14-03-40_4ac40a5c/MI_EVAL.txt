# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations like 'That's a solid start' and no disapproval despite continued smoking/drinking. Warmth was somewhat brisk and checklist-like, lacking explicit affirmation of Samir's deeper strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections, but the counselor frequently pivoted to advice lists rather than reflecting emotional content like wife's concerns or fear of chest tightness. Complex reflections were rare.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on asthma management, nocturnal symptoms, and trigger reduction with concrete follow-up on commitments. Early focus was more on general medical advice than on Samir's own goals, with no agenda-setting on change versus harm reduction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Planning was genuinely interactive — the cutoff alarm, shirt by the door, and 'early airport run' excuse all came from Samir's own ideas, with a mutual 'Deal?' close. Collaboration was uneven due to multi-item expert recommendation blocks rather than joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed actions as Samir's choice and accepted his refusal to quit without pressure, offering harm reduction instead. Dense directive advice blocks felt prescriptive and Samir's ownership of the plan wasn't explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Relied on information-giving and menu options rather than drawing out Samir's motivations, which mostly emerged on their own. No exploration of values, importance, or confidence, though the role-play practice was a good evocative technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered Samir choices, conveying his input mattered, but slipped into an expert stance with prescriptive medical and lifestyle lists before exploring his ideas. His expertise was incorporated mainly when he volunteered it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk leading to commitments ('the cutoff thing... might actually be worth trying'), but pivotal moments like 'maybe she's got a point' went unexplored and unaffirmed. No rulers or reflection of commitment language used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rolled with resistance effectively by not arguing and offering harm-reduction framings when Samir refused to quit. No double-sided reflections to reframe sustain talk toward his own goals, and advice-heavy responses risked implying the status quo was being managed.
