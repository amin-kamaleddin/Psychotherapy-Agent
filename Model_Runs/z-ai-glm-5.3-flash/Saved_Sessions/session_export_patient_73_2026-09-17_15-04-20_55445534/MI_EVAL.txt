# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance, reframing slips as 'information, not failure' and affirming Linda's strengths. However, affirmations were somewhat generic and the emotional weight of her repeated failures was not explicitly validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate, capturing Linda's ambivalence, fear, and hope early on. Reflections occasionally repeated the same two-sided summary without deepening, and the shame around past failures was only briefly acknowledged rather than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focus on tobacco cessation was clear and consistent throughout, with every exchange advancing the target behavior. The session progressed logically toward a structured, actionable plan with a concrete start date and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently solicited Linda's input on the start day, supports, and check-in time, and she actively shaped the final plan. However, the mid-session plan was presented as a counselor-generated bullet list rather than fully co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda's choices were respected — she chose her step, start day, and declined rehearsal without pushback. Autonomy was occasionally diluted by prescriptive, checklist-style advice and repeated urge-strategy reminders.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was repeatedly drawn from Linda via rulers, open questions, and exploration of past successes, eliciting strong change talk. Confidence-building remained surface-level, with her self-doubt ('I've let myself down before') not deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Linda was positioned as the expert, choosing her own step, supports, and check-in time, which clearly shaped the plan. The counselor-generated plan list in the middle session slightly tempered this partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked throughout via importance/confidence rulers, asking for her most important reason, and inviting a specific doable step. Linda responded with strong change talk ('Monday... I can do one morning'), which was then reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced, and the reframe of slips as 'information, not failure' was helpful. However, sustain talk around smoker identity and social norms was acknowledged but not strategically pivoted toward change with double-sided reflections.
