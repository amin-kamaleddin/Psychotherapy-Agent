# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with explicit affirmations ('That's a big insight,' 'That's real wisdom'). Secrecy is treated as understandable, and support is reinforced at closing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture the emotional core, mirroring her ambivalence ('wishes you'd said it... being seen differently is scary') and highlighting her own word 'lighter' with precision.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tight focus on peer disclosure, progressing logically from worry → safe person → content → barriers → timing → first step → commitment, with no meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conversational and egalitarian, building on the client's ideas rather than directing. Agenda-shaping is largely driven by the counselor's question sequence, and the follow-up plan is counselor-initiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** All decisions rest with the client, and the clinician explicitly avoids prescribing, with choice honored even around potential setbacks. Slightly more explicit reinforcement that she controls the pace would strengthen this.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from the client — the cake party memory, the exhaustion of secrecy, her own 'recipe' for disclosure. The counselor never argues for change, and the client spontaneously articulates reasons, ability, and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is explicitly positioned as the expert ('You know Maya better than anyone'), and every next step, including the plan, is her own design.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation through questions about almost telling Maya, what she'd want her to understand, and first steps. The client produces strong commitment language ('this month,' 'I think I can do that') developed through the counselor's prompts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged with double-sided reflections and pivoted toward change (e.g., Maya as a safe person, the 'lighter' feeling). Minor deduction for the early reflection giving equal weight to the risky side.
