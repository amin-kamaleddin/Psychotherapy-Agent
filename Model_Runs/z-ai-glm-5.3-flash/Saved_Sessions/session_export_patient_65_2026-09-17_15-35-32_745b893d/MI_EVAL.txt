# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validating Julian's external pressure and normalizing his worry. However, warmth was somewhat instrumental and task-focused, with few explicit affirmations of Julian as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections were made but they were brief and quickly pivoted to problem-solving. Emotional nuance like Julian's anxiety, guardedness, and admission of not listening to his caseworker was passed over without deeper reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the concrete target of completing 40 court-mandated hours by month's end. Every exchange advanced this goal — sites, scripts, verification, and scheduling — with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative, with the counselor checking in before each step and incorporating Julian's constraints and preferences into the plan. However, the counselor still supplied most of the substantive content and solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered menus of options, respected Julian's preferences (texting, evenings-only), and let him retain control of next steps. Fell short of a 5 because choices were among counselor-generated options rather than Julian's own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Virtually no evocative questions about Julian's values, reasons, or confidence; the counselor solved problems for him instead. Julian's motivation remained purely external, missing the core MI opportunity to build intrinsic buy-in.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frequent requests for Julian's input supported power-sharing, but the counselor generated most of the session's content while Julian stayed in a passive, choosing role. The expert role was only partially shared.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Spontaneous change talk was occasionally reinforced but rarely deliberately evoked. No open questions about Julian's own reasons, confidence scaling, or the meaning of completing the hours.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged and practically reframed but not skillfully pivoted toward change. Reassurances were offered rather than strategic double-sided reflections.
