# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, normalizing Tyler's shame and ambivalence without lecturing. Falls short of 5 due to occasional shifts into teaching/advising mode rather than deep individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflects Tyler's ambivalence and validates his failed plan appropriately, but reflections remain formulaic and surface-level. Misses deeper emotional material around shame, GPA fears, and drinking being tied to friendship.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains clear, consistent focus on preventing further alcohol-related consequences, moving systematically from harm reduction to refusal skills, exit planning, and live role-play. Steers steadily toward a workable change plan without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative moments exist, including role-play and choice points, but the session structure is largely counselor-directed with pre-written scripts and checklists. Tyler shapes details but not the overall agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers menus of options, asks permission before advising, and frames boundaries as protecting Tyler's own values. Falls short of 5 because options are counselor-generated rather than inviting Tyler to author his own strategies.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is minimally drawn from within the client — no importance/confidence rulers, no 'why does this matter to you' questions, and Tyler's own change talk goes unamplified. Tyler's motivation surfaced largely despite, not because of, the counselor's technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power sharing through permission-seeking and choice questions, but the expertise flow is largely counselor-to-client. Long lists of tips, scripts, and checklists position the counselor as the knowledge holder, with less exploration of Tyler's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tyler generates change talk himself ('my GPA can't survive another conduct thing'), but the counselor rarely elicits or amplifies it. The 'which feels most doable' question supports engagement but is not classic change-talk evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is tolerated without arguing or reinforcing, but never strategically engaged. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections tying 'everyone drinks' back to Tyler's stated priorities, or leveraging his own discrepancy about the police report.
