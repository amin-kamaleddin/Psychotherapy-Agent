# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized Daniela's hesitance, even promising to problem-solve without judgment. However, no explicit affirmations of her strengths or courage were offered, keeping warmth somewhat transactional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal beyond one opening empathy statement; Daniela's deeper fears (the 'diabetic girl' label, pity, autonomy loss, self-doubt) were never reflected back. Responses were almost exclusively advice, scripts, and options rather than feeling-focused.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on peer disclosure of diabetes with no topic drift — every response tied back to the target behavior. However, the session moved briskly through checklist-like steps without consolidating what mattered most to Daniela.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative moments existed (Daniela chose the text format, privacy line, and co-created the plan; the counselor role-played flexibly). But the structure was largely advice-dispensing, with the counselor generating most content while Daniela reacted.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: explicit affirmations of choice, option menus at every step, and honoring Daniela's timeline, yielding a plan with genuine ownership ('on my terms'). Falls short of a 5 because prescriptive scripts were offered before exploring her own approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely counselor-supplied via pros/cons lists, scripts, and plans rather than drawn from the client. Beyond one good early question, there was no values exploration, scaling, or summarizing to elicit and consolidate Daniela's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor solicited input and responded flexibly to Daniela's preferences, and her contributions shaped the final plan. Still, the expert role dominated — pre-written scripts and lines were supplied rather than eliciting Daniela's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerged (wanting to stop sneaking off, 'it doesn't sound as scary,' her own drafted text, a concrete plan). However, these moments arose from exercises rather than skillful evocation — no reflections of change talk, evocative follow-ups, or confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never judged or amplified, and waiting was validated ('totally okay to wait until it feels right'). But double-sided reflections were absent, and her ambivalence ('is that still progress or am I just stalling?') was sidestepped rather than explored.
