# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opens with non-judgmental support and normalizes slips ('Slips are normal; they don't wreck progress'). However, warmth is somewhat generic with few individualized affirmations of Ahmed's specific strengths or honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening early on, acknowledging Ahmed's defensiveness, rationalizations, and fatigue. Reflections occasionally stay surface-level and some responses shift quickly to advice rather than deepening emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior from start to finish, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete, tracked plan with a closing review. Every response ties back to the weekday-reduction goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the work jointly ('we can map small steps') and Ahmed's preferences shape the final plan. However, early and mid-session responses deliver pre-packaged advice blocks before checking what Ahmed wants.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language throughout, offering options rather than prescriptions and accepting Ahmed's refusals and weekend-cigar condition without pressure. Option lists occasionally feel menu-driven, and ownership isn't reinforced when he sets up his 'dust theory' escape clause.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently draws motivation from the client via open questions, the self-experiment frame, and a confidence ruler, with Ahmed's own motivations driving the plan. Some evocative questions are diluted by bundled advice, and commitment language isn't consolidated with a closing summary.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for Ahmed's input, but several responses are advice-dense lists positioning the counselor as the expert on solutions. Ahmed's ideas do substantially shape the plan, yet the counselor doesn't consistently frame him as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently evokes change language through open questions and a confidence ruler, drawing meaningful client change talk ('Maybe I could try fewer during the week'). Change talk isn't always deepened strategically, and the '5 to 7' question arrives late.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without amplifying it, using neutral or double-sided reflections and a self-testing frame rather than argument. Some reflections paraphrase sustain talk at length, but pivots back to change are consistent.
