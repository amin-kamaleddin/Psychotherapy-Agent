# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental opening and helpful reframing of slips as 'data, not failure.' However, warmth is brief and formulaic, with quick pivots to checklists rather than sitting with her expressed shame, and little explicit affirmation of her as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; responses are dominated by psychoeducation and strategy lists. Emotionally significant disclosures (shame driving her smoking, feeling like a leper) go unreflected and unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on smoking cessation with logical progression from assessment to a concrete morning plan, and appropriately accepted her choice to focus only on mornings. Direction is achieved through task-driven momentum rather than MI-consistent guiding.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration: counselor asks preferences, accepts her tracking suggestion and morning-only focus, and plans joint review. But the counselor generates nearly all ideas in lecture-style lists, making Anisa an implementer rather than co-creator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices and avoids pressure or coercion. However, framing is menu-selection rather than deep autonomy support, with counselor-supplied options and subtle steering toward the counselor's preferred path.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is structured around rather than elicited; her spontaneous change talk and core motivators (daughter, shame) are never developed. No open-ended evocative questions, and the readiness ruler answer ('a six, a seven') is not explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, prescribing techniques and directing next steps. Her choices influence the plan only via menu-style questions, and her deeper expertise—that shame sustains her smoking—goes unused.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Anisa spontaneously offers change talk ('I can do that,' 'it feels a bit more possible'), but the counselor never deliberately evokes or deepens it. No questions about her reasons, hopes, or confidence, and the readiness ruler is a clear missed opportunity.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strength: sustain talk is not reinforced, and slips are reframed as information rather than failure. Weakness: no double-sided reflections or deliberate pivoting of doubt toward change—doubts are simply passed over with reassurance.
