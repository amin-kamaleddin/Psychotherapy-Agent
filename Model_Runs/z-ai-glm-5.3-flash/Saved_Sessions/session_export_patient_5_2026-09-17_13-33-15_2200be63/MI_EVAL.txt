# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect and warmth are present, and safety concerns are taken seriously, but affirmations are generic and the client's distress about the prescribing error goes largely unacknowledged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is nearly absent; the client's fear, relief, and worry are never reflected, and responses consist mostly of instructions and information delivery.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains an exceptionally clear focus on preventing a dangerous MAOI-SSRI interaction, with every element — scripts, role-play, washout education — serving that goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is primarily counselor-led education; the client shapes only the format of practice, not the content of the plan, despite some role-play responsiveness.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Instructions are largely imperative, and the client's own resolve and decisions are never explicitly affirmed as her own; permission-seeking offers are the only autonomy-supportive elements.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions are almost entirely supplied by the counselor; the single evocative opening question is quickly abandoned in favor of psychoeducation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes an expert, directive stance throughout; the client's own expertise and preferences are rarely solicited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client generates strong commitment language naturally, but the counselor never reflects, reinforces, or deepens it with evocative questions or affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is minimal; the counselor handles the client's expressed worry neutrally with safety-netting but never strategically explores or reframes ambivalence.
