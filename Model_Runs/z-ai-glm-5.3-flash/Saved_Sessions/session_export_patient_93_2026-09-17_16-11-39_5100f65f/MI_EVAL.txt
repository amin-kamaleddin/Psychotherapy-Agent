# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout with several affirmations, but praise is somewhat generic and lacks individualization of Margaret's specific strengths (e.g., her persistence and past success refusing top-ups).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening accurately captures Margaret's perspective and reframes ambivalence nonjudgmentally, but the emotional layer of loneliness and loss beneath her at-home drinking is met with practical suggestions rather than deeper reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange ties back to reducing alcohol risk, from the readiness ruler to the concrete plan and follow-up, with closed loops on GP and son calls and no meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely an interaction between equals — Margaret's ideas shaped the plan and feasibility was checked — but the 1-week plan template, safety list, and scripted refusal examples were counselor-provided, tilting the agenda slightly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly reinforced: permission sought before information, choices offered, her cut-back-not-quit boundary respected, and she authored her own refusal line and timing, resulting in clear ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is effectively drawn from Margaret via open questions, the readiness ruler, and her own refusal language, but later evocation becomes more directive with counselor-supplied coping lists, and deeper values like independence are never explicitly evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise is framed as shared, permission is repeatedly sought, and Margaret's contributions substantially shaped the plan, though the plan structure, medication rule, and several option lists were clinician-provided rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is effectively elicited via the opening question, readiness ruler follow-up, and concrete planning questions that consolidate commitment, but reinforcement comes more through planning than deeper evocation or amplifying her own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist avoids arguing with sustain talk, instead pivoting to consented information and options preserving the pub, but occasional near-verbatim reflections of sustain talk lack an active pivot or strategic double-sided reflection linking drinking to the fall.
