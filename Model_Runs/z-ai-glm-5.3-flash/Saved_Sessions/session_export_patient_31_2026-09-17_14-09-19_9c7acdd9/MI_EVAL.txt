# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and the client explicitly felt accepted ('Cheers for not just lecturing me'). However, affirmations were generic rather than personalized to David's strengths or honesty, keeping this below top marks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured David's tiredness and ambivalence, but empathic responses tapered off quickly. Key emotional stakes—the cherished after-dinner smoke, poor sleep, and fear of failure—went unaddressed as the session shifted to instruction.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on smoking reduction with a coherent, actionable plan including a concrete tonight-experiment. Direction was achieved through counselor-driven prescription rather than guiding toward change David defined himself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was predominantly counselor-led, with the plan delivered as expert recommendations rather than jointly developed. David's valuable self-knowledge (minimal daytime smoking, the meaningful evening ritual) was never incorporated into a co-constructed plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were genuine autonomy supports early (menu choices, permission to keep the evening cigarette), but later turns became repetitive, pressuring instruction-delivery. David's ownership of the plan was never explicitly emphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was treated as something to install via information rather than elicit, with almost no open questions about David's own reasons, values, or confidence. The counselor's reliance on prescriptive protocols reflects the opposite of MI's evocative spirit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite initial collaborative questions, the counselor quickly assumed the expert role with monologues of medical information. David's contributions were minimally acknowledged and his expertise about his own smoking patterns went unexploited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David offered genuine change talk ('I'll give it a shot tonight,' 'maybe the cigarette won't feel quite as good') that was consistently met with dosing schedules instead of reinforcement. No open questions were used to deepen his own desire, ability, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor was neutral toward David's substantial sustain talk but made no active efforts to soften it—no double-sided reflections or reframing toward values. The harm-reduction framing accommodated sustain talk without leveraging it toward change.
