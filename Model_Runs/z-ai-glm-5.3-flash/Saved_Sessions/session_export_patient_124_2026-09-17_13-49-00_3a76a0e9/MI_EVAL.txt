# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor was consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, and Sofia appreciated being spared lectures ('thanks for not lecturing me'). Affirmations, however, were sparse and somewhat formulaic, missing chances to validate her courage in experimenting despite peer pressure and family stress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accurately summarized Sofia's substance use and practical concerns early on, but reflections stayed shallow and few. Rich emotional material — fear of her mother finding out, family expectations, social belonging, and fear of white-knuckling without weed — was bypassed in favor of moving to next steps.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on cutting back marijuana use and progressed logically to a specific, measurable first step (skip one solo Thursday session, track it, check back). It fell short of a 5 because underlying anxiety and Sofia's values were not also incorporated into the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was genuine partial collaboration — Sofia chose the tracking task, co-selected Thursday, and shaped the refusal line. But the counselor frequently slid into an expert posture, delivering multi-step protocols and checklists as instructions ('Try this for 7 days') with little negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly emphasized choice ('you can choose, not guess'), offered menus of options, accepted Sofia's boundaries ('not quitting, just so we're clear'), and framed slips non-judgmentally. Some turns still imposed pre-packaged plans rather than genuinely asking what Sofia thought would work for her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Pros/cons framing successfully drew out Sofia's own doubts, financial strain, and a values-based change talk moment ('good future thing'). During the action phase, however, the counselor shifted to supplying techniques instead of evoking her own solutions, confidence, or hopes for the future.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sofia was offered choices and her expertise shaped some of the plan, but the session was markedly advice-heavy, prescribing extensive protocols (4-4-6 breathing, grounding, urge-surfing, refusal scripts) without first exploring her own ideas. This expert-driven stance undermined power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Exploratory questions elicited meaningful change talk — doubts about effectiveness, money concerns, tentative commitment, and a pivotal values connection. Yet this key values-based statement was met with a checklist rather than a deepening reflection or affirmation, and confidence was never explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided reinforcing sustain talk and lecturing, and the late 'slipping isn't failure' reframe was a good exception. Otherwise, sustain talk about anxiety and peer belonging was acknowledged briefly and bypassed with practical steps rather than reframed or met with double-sided reflections.
