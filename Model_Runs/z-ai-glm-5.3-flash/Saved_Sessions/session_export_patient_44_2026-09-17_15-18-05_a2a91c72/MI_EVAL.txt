# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates respect and support with affirmations like 'That's great — you've done something meaningful' and 'I'm with you on this.' Warmth is somewhat formulaic and brief; deeper, personalized affirmations of Erik's resilience would strengthen it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections track Erik's frustration and compassion themes and validate struggles like the candy-bar snag. Some reflections are brief or wrapped into questions, and the emotional depth of his discouragement about comparison is not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on exercise and diet, with every turn advancing concrete goals: specific days, backup plans, and negotiated check-ins. It concludes with firm commitments and follow-up, showing skilled goal-directed guidance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor works as a partner, jointly negotiating goals and check-in logistics, with Erik's contributions driving the plan's content. The counselor still drives process decisions (scales, option lists) with limited agenda negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice language is consistent ('pick what feels right for you'), with no pressure or coercion. Frequent counselor-generated option menus and reminder offers straddle suggestion rather than fully eliciting the client's own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence rulers and open prompts elicit Erik's own intrinsic reasons and self-generated insights (e.g., competing with his past self). Evocation relies heavily on structured tools rather than deeper open-ended exploration of values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Erik's own ideas substantially shape the plan, and the counselor invites his input ('Anything else you'd like me to ask?'). The counselor still offers several pre-packaged option menus, subtly steering rather than purely eliciting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence rulers and stumbling-block questions effectively draw out strong change talk ('I'm competing with the version of me who never showed up'). Some change talk emerged spontaneously rather than through deliberate evocation, and desire/why-based talk was not deeply amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged briefly then redirected toward competence and planning, without reinforcing discouragement. However, redirection is sometimes abrupt, moving quickly to solutions rather than using double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
