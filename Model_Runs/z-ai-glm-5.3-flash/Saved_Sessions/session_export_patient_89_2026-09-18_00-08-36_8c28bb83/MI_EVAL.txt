# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly rejecting judgmental framing ('I'm not here to hand you another plan that fails') and normalizing setbacks. No labeling, disapproval, or conditional approval anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including complex reflections capturing ambivalence and deepening Marcus's own words ('ten minutes beats every plan you never started'). Reflections were emotionally attuned and consistently went beyond surface content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on physical activity with purposeful progression from exploring enjoyable movement to concrete commitment and relapse planning. Session closed with Marcus leaving for a walk — evidence of effective guidance without rushing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully equal partnership: the counselor disclaimed the expert role, and Marcus's ideas (sneakers, dancing, calendar commitment) drove the plan. Success criteria were co-constructed with the counselor deferring to Marcus's own definition.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every decision was framed as Marcus's own ('you're the expert on your days'), with explicit affirmation that 'nobody handed you this.' No pressure or directive advice; even the closing question was offered with 'no answer needed.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation emerged from Marcus himself — walking, dancing, his wife, 'living again' — via strategic evocative questions. Commitment language was elicited and amplified, culminating in 'I'm actually looking forward to it.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook power-sharing: Marcus was repeatedly positioned as the expert, and his contributions demonstrably shaped the entire plan. The counselor affirmed this directly ('Every piece came from you').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked through open questions and amplified each time it appeared, including the pivotal 'What would it mean to you to have dancing back in your life?' Session culminated in concrete commitments and Marcus's own summary of his plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced while the client was still validated — gym barriers were defused ('I won't ask you to find one') and past failures reframed as 'someone else's plan.' Relapse thinking was preempted in advance ('a week goes sideways, that's just life').
