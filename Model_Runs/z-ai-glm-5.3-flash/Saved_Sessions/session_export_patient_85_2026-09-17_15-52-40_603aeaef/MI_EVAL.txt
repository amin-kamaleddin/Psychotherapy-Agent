# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with genuine affirmations and compassionate normalization of slips ('Missing it doesn't mean failure'). Attachment to OJ was validated rather than pathologized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Feelings were reflected accurately, including frustration and relief. Kept from a 5 by reflections that were sometimes brief restatements rather than deeper exploration of the emotional weight of prior relapse and self-blame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every turn advanced a clear agenda from exploration to insight, psychoeducation, scaling, planning, barriers, tracking, and follow-up. No topic drift; strong focus on the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction: permission-seeking, option menus Marco selected from, and plan elements he added himself (50/50 dilution, wife's role, river-walk reward). Marco's contributions substantially shaped the final plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ownership consistently reinforced through permission-based advice, choice framing, and acceptance of Marco's decisions (e.g., skipping rehearsal). Slips framed with self-compassion rather than control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong use of open questions and scaling that drew out importance (9) and confidence (10), with Marco generating his own insights and plan. Occasionally supplied content (calorie facts, option menus) rather than evoking, and deeper personal reasons beyond weight went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marco was repeatedly positioned as the expert, with permission-seeking and client-designed plan elements. Kept from a 5 because the counselor supplied most option menus rather than eliciting Marco's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Active evocation and reinforcement of change talk via scaling, first-step questions, and planning, yielding strong client commitment language ('This is happening'). Could have deepened commitment further by exploring long-term vision and why this attempt differs.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully handled Marco's 'boy, do I love that OJ' with validation plus a planned-treat reframe rather than amplifying sustain talk. A brief window of sustained discussion slightly limited the score.
