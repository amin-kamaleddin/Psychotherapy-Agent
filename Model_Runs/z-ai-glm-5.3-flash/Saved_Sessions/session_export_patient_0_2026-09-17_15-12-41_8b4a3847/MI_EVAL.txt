# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, thanking the client for his honesty despite defensiveness and minimizing, and the client's openness visibly increased. Fell short of a 5 because warmth was somewhat procedural, with missed chances to affirm his courage in disclosing shame-laden material.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate complex reflections that captured the client's ambivalence between enjoyment/social connection and health worries. Empathy remained somewhat information-dense and surface-level, leaving the emotional core of his shame and isolation unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and developed change talk—the client moved from 'not much to change' to a self-generated commitment to a month-long break with confidence language ('I can actually do that'). Framing the break as 'a good experiment' and a self-directed choice strengthened his commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided arguing with sustain talk, using neutral double-sided reflections that pivoted toward concern and respecting the client's stated boundaries. Softening relied mostly on neutral reflection, with little strategic reframing (e.g., of his claim to have it 'under control').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as the expert through permission-asking and menu-based choices, with the client's selections demonstrably shaping the session direction. Long expert informational blocks at times shifted power toward the clinician without checking if that depth was wanted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on meth use and health consequences throughout, converging on concrete target behaviors (saline spray, month-long break, alternatives, refusal lines, self-checks). Early parallel agenda options and broad harm-reduction information momentarily dispersed focus before converging.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructed the plan from the client's own ideas (basketball, the 'early game' excuse, designated driving) and invited him to steer with questions like 'Which part do you want more detail on?' Didactic monologues at points reduced the client's role to receiving information, and the agenda was not explicitly negotiated in shared terms at the start.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support—asked permission before advising, honored the client's limits ('without being told to quit'), and offered menus rather than directives. The client clearly experienced ownership, authoring his own plan and stating 'I can actually do that.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from within rather than supplying it, never arguing for change and mirroring the client's own concerns back. The client generated his own refusal lines, rewards, and self-monitoring plan—clear evidence that reasons and ability for change were elicited, not imposed.
