# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly normalizing self-harm as survival ('There's no judgment here; it's kept you going') and affirming self-protective efforts. No judgmental language appeared at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally deep, capturing metaphoric language ('weight... down to your bones') and ambivalence precisely. Reflections consistently landed, evidenced by the patient's progression from monosyllables to vulnerable disclosures.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, strategic focus on self-harm throughout, guiding from 'different' to a concrete plan and success criteria. Fell short of 5 due to gradual momentum built mostly through sequential questioning, without mid-session summaries to consolidate.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly framed problem-solving as joint ('brainstorming together,' 'plan around your reality'). The plan was composed entirely of the patient's own ideas and materials.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Honored the patient's self-set terms ('one time, no promises beyond that') without pushing for more or imposing safety demands. Reinforced ownership: 'you set the terms yourself.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivations for change (sleep, fear of deeper cuts, 'quiet that doesn't leave a mark') came from the patient's own words, elicited through open questions. No reasons for change were supplied by the clinician.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly located expertise in the client ('you're the expert on what fits your life'), and the patient's contributions genuinely shaped the session. The intervention strategy was built directly from her stated values.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully selected and developed change talk, pausing deliberately on 'maybe I'm not totally against trying something' to deepen it. This culminated in a client-generated commitment ('I'll try once') that was then consolidated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well with double-sided reflections and no challenging of 'I'm not saying I won't do what I always do.' Slightly prolonged focus on the status quo by repeating sustain themes in reflections.
