# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout — explicitly counters Hana's fear of lectures and positions her as the expert on her life. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and could be more individualized, e.g., validating her fear of failure or her honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is consistently accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both sides of her ambivalence (the treasured five minutes vs. her mum's outcome). Counselor honors her nuances, like 'never' scaring her but weekday breaks feeling doable.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on smoking cessation and progresses to a concrete, client-generated plan. Momentum occasionally pauses, and mid-session summaries could have better consolidated the themes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is fully co-constructed from Hana's own ideas (coffee, flatmate, weekday breaks), with the counselor asking rather than prescribing. Hana herself notes it felt like 'me talking myself into it, with you listening.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Hana sets her own scope of change, including keeping weekends off-limits, and the counselor explicitly endorses it without pressure or advice-giving. Ownership is reinforced throughout — 'it's yours' — as the strongest kind of motivation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key change elements originate from Hana in response to open, evocative questions; the counselor never supplies reasons or sells the goal. Her shift from 'I wouldn't say I want to quit' to 'I'm more ready than I said' demonstrates excellent elicitation of intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly frames Hana as the expert, and her contributions visibly shape the session — the plan elements all originate from her. She defines success on her own terms, demonstrating that expertise resides within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions skillfully evoke change talk — separating the break from the cigarette, exploring what would make it matter enough, and defining a successful first week. Hana generates commitment-level language herself, confirming motivation was client-produced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections pair sustain language with change language without amplifying it, and the counselor pivots quickly with evocative questions. Sustain talk is occasionally restated verbatim, slightly extending focus on reasons against change.
