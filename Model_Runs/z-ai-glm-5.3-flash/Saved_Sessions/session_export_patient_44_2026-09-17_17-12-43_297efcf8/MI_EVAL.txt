# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone with genuine affirmations of Erik's progress. However, warmth occasionally feels formulaic ('Nice—' recurs), and there is little explicit normalization of his struggles with self-comparison and discouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurately summarizes Erik's situation and reflects key content, but reflections remain content-level restatements. Significant emotional material—the 'sting' of comparison, years of broken promises, self-criticism—is never met with reflection of the underlying shame, frustration, or hope.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior across every turn, moving logically from focus selection through barriers, times, fallbacks, coping lines, supports, and check-in. Even when Erik drifts, questions keep the session anchored to concrete behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Highly collaborative in structure, with Erik's ideas shaping every element of the plan and a jointly negotiated check-in. However, several turns read as rapid-fire interrogation with up to four stacked questions, shifting toward assessment rather than two-way dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice language is pervasive with no pressure or coercion, and the plan is fully owned by Erik. Option-menu questions slightly constrain the space compared with fully open evocation, and ownership of potential setbacks is not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently draws motivation from within Erik, with his own reasons (back pain, looser pants, self-integrity) driving the plan and a personally meaningful coping line elicited. Frequent multi-question barrages occasionally replace evocation with checklist-gathering, and his most meaningful statements go unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Erik as the expert, building the plan entirely from his own ideas and offering choice in nearly every turn. Falls short because suggestions are sometimes bundled as leading option-lists rather than open invitations, and his expertise is never explicitly named.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk via confidence rulers and future-oriented questions, moving Erik from 5–6 to 7–7.5 with strong commitment language ('Bag by the door tonight. I'll do it.'). The main gap is that motivation statements are rarely amplified with reflective reinforcement, often met with another question instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not amplify sustain talk and offers harm-reduction fallbacks, but little active softening occurs. When Erik expresses discouragement ('I feel like quitting'), the counselor responds with logistical questions rather than double-sided reflections acknowledging difficulty while pivoting to his motivations.
