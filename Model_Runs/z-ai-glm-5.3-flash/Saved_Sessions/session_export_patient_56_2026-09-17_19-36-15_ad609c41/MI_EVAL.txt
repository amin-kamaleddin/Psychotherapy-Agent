# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with genuine affirmations like 'That's a wise distinction' and 'You've got a real strategy forming here.' No judgment or labeling; the client explicitly noted being helped without being handed answers.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing the core value-behavior gap and its emotional stakes. Key reflections like 'an hour of real attention matters more to you than a whole day of proximity without connection' demonstrated deep understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, focused thread on aligning daily life with family values; each turn advanced a logical arc from naming the gap to envisioning, planning, anticipating obstacles, and defining success. No drift, and summaries consolidated momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership — the client's ideas (phone basket, values list as bridge) shaped the session, and summaries were co-constructed from her words. The follow-up plan was negotiated, with the client's preference determining the outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client's ownership emphasized at every step ('what might work for you and your family,' 'in a way that feels true to you'). The closing choice about check-ins honored her decision as support on her own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice given; every open question drew out the client's own motivation, plans, and definitions of success. Her closing statement — 'you just helped me hear my own' — directly confirms masterful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treated the client as the expert on her own life, integrating her contributions (anchor habit, values list, self-managed follow-up) into the plan. Explicit power-sharing at the close confirmed full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematically evoked desire, ability, commitment, and vision language through questions about what success would look like and how the husband might respond. Client's responses built from wanting to close the gap to a firm commitment to return 'either way'.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Briefly acknowledged the client's worry about a dismissive response, then effectively reframed it into problem-solving ('what do you think you'd want to do in that moment'). Only minor deduction because sustain talk was minimal, leaving less opportunity to demonstrate more active softening.
