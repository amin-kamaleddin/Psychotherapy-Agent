# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms the patient's self-directed choices, including his plan to involve his wife on his own terms. There is no judgment of his pride or past avoidance; his perspective is treated as valid throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician accurately reflects the patient's split feelings about the pills versus finger pricks, his pride, and his fear of seeming weak. Reflections consistently capture both content and emotional nuance, including normalizing doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician keeps a clear focus on blood glucose self-monitoring and the barriers to it, gently steering away from the 'easy' pill discussion. Each exchange moves deliberately toward a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation feels like a joint exploration, with open questions, reflective responses, and the clinician explicitly deferring to the patient's knowledge of his own life and marriage. Decisions and goals emerged from the patient himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly emphasized—'that's your idea, which matters,' offering choices, and letting the patient design his own routine. The clinician frames involving his wife as his decision and a strength, not an imposition.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician draws out the patient's own reasons, goals ('no more secrets, one check done'), and solutions rather than prescribing them. Questions like 'what would that look like if you designed it?' evoke self-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician acts as a partner, inviting the patient's input on every step and reinforcing that 'that shift came from you, not me.' The tone is collaborative rather than directive or expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully amplifies the patient's own change talk by reflecting and highlighting shifts from 'I got it' to naming concrete steps. Reflections consistently spotlight the patient's self-generated motivation and plans.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it's always tomorrow with me,' doubt about sticking with it) is met with empathy and reframing rather than confrontation. The clinician normalizes doubt ('doubt and determination can sit side by side') and lets the patient resolve ambivalence himself.
