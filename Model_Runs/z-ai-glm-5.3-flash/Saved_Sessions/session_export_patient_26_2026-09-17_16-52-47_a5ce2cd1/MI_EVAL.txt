# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with frequent, specific affirmations ('that takes real strength,' 'great job cutting back'). Falls short of 5 because affirmations are somewhat formulaic and don't fully convey unconditional regard for her deeper fears and self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening that captured both content and emotional weight, and validated her phone anxiety with support rather than dismissal. Missed opportunities to reflect deeper fear of failure when she voiced 'what if she says no?' and 'like I usually do,' instead moving quickly to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, unwavering focus on childcare stability and cutting back smoking/drinking from the opening question. Every exchange advanced the agenda toward a concrete, time-bound plan (text at 9:30 tonight, Head Start call at noon tomorrow) with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely equal partnership with check-ins before each step and genuine co-work in the role-play rehearsal. At times unequal because the counselor supplied the message drafts, scripts, and option lists that Daniela refined rather than co-developed from her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Daniela's choice with options, permission language, and a trial-week framing; she exercised real choice throughout. Heavy scaffolding of pre-written materials positioned the counselor as solution-generator, with limited explicit crediting of her ownership of successes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened with good evocative questions and Daniela articulated her own motivations, but the session's momentum came largely from counselor-supplied solutions. No scaling questions, minimal mining of her spontaneous reasons for change, and her expertise was drawn out only superficially.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniela's input directly shaped the session — she chose strategies, reworded the text, and set her own times. The counselor retained a fairly directive role in generating options, so her expertise emerged more in reaction than in co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited substantial change talk ('I'll send it tonight after my shift,' 'I can do hard things in small pieces') via targeted open questions and reinforced it with celebrations and concrete commitments. Some change talk came implicitly through advice and structured planning, and spontaneous change talk was rarely deepened with reflections or scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged barriers briefly without amplifying them and pivoted effectively toward solutions, with the trial-week framing skillfully softening commitment fears. Sometimes rushed to problem-solving rather than using double-sided reflections, and backup-plan discussion gave some air to failure scenarios.
