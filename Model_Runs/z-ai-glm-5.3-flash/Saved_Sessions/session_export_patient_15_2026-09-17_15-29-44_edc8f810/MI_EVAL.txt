# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirmed the patient warmly despite minimizing language and passive compliance, accepting his rejection of several options without disapproval. Affirmations were somewhat generic and professionally warm rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and concise, capturing the patient's perspective and minimizing tone without challenging it. Emotional layers—work stress and the father comparison—were acknowledged but not explored for their emotional meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently focused on alcohol reduction and blood pressure with a clear progression from permission, to a test plan, to concrete strategies and a two-week recheck. No topic drift despite the patient's brief, passive style.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was built largely from the patient's own ideas, showing genuine power-sharing. The initial agenda (BP check, options list) was set unilaterally and the patient's early deference was not explicitly renegotiated as a shared agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary use of choice-offering, permission-seeking, and explicit statements like 'You're in charge of any decisions.' The plan was genuinely co-constructed and client-owned, with rejected options accepted without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler and planning questions drew out some commitment, but much of the patient's motivation remained externally driven. The counselor did not probe for personal values or reasons for change beyond the BP test.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positioned the patient as the decision-maker and solicited his own coping ideas, which shaped the final plan. Early 'you're the doctor' deference was redirected but the patient's own expertise was never explicitly reinforced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk from a precontemplative patient through permission-seeking, offering a low-stakes test, and a well-used confidence ruler. Did not deepen the change talk (e.g., why trimming matters), leaving motivation somewhat compliance-based.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some sustain talk was handled well with double-sided reflections and reframing the change as a small test. Key statements—the father's longevity, 'twenty years of habits,' 'a bit much all at once'—were passed over or met with information rather than strategic exploration.
