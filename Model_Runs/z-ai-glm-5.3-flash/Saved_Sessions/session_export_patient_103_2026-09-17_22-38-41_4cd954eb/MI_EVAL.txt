# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard maintained toward an initially hostile client, with validation of his anger, skepticism, and irritation without defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening throughout, including catching the shift to powerlessness and hearing emotional meaning beneath content (control, fear of weakness).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Laser focus on anger in medical waiting situations with clear logical progression from validation to a concrete commitment-ready plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction with every plan element client-generated; frequent 'let's figure it out together' framing and active patient buy-in.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent ownership framing and explicit respect for Nabil's authorship, but choice-affirmation is mostly implicit rather than explicit statements that change is entirely his choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from the client via open-ended evocative questions, connecting his strengths and realizations to the change plan rather than delivering advice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Patient positioned as expert and generated most content; counselor's occasional framing of strategy options keeps it slightly below a 5, though these remained open-ended.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation and amplification of change talk, culminating in strong commitment language like 'A plan might help' and 'Can we actually write it down now?'.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk never argued with; deep-breathing skepticism defused with humor and 'easier said than done' taken seriously and pivoted into actionable insight.
