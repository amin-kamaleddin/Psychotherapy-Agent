# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance despite Milan's defensiveness and minimization, notably avoiding the moralizing tone of the referral context. However, warmth occasionally felt procedural, with limited explicit affirmation of Milan beyond his cooperation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections captured surface content accurately but missed deeper emotional material, including Milan's shame, fear of community/parental discovery, and discomfort with being judged. The counselor rarely lingered on feelings, quickly pivoting to facts and logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on weekend alcohol use, logically progressing from exploration to values to concrete steps and a scheduled follow-up. Not a 5 because unresolved points (brain facts, drink-limit resistance) circled back, and some turns over-focused on logistical minutiae.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative moments, such as asking permission and offering menus of choices, but problem-solving was largely counselor-driven, with Milan mostly selecting or approving pre-made options. Milan's own expertise was incorporated only when he volunteered it, making collaboration partial rather than full.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly emphasized Milan's choice, asked permission, and accepted refusals without pushback, resulting in an entirely client-selected final plan. Stronger autonomy support would have explicitly affirmed Milan's ownership of the outcome rather than the counselor supplying most options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One strong evocative question about values produced the session's most meaningful change talk, but beyond this, motivation was evoked implicitly through menus and counselor-supplied content. There were no questions about confidence, and emerging change talk was not developed into fuller commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission before sharing information and invited Milan's input, but the session frequently shifted into advice-giving with detailed, counselor-supplied plans and scripts. Milan noted he could have generated much of this himself, indicating uneven power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively linked drinking to Milan's own values, yielding genuine client-generated motivation and commitment to a concrete plan plus a follow-up. Fell short because change talk about the drinking itself was only tentatively embraced and not deepened with confidence or success-look questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and used some double-sided reflections, but often sidestepped it by pivoting to facts and menus instead of strategically reframing it. Milan's dismissal of the brain science and flat declaration of continuing to drink were never explored or revisited.
