# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, repeatedly affirming Jonathan's skepticism and his 'one thing only' boundary. However, warmth occasionally felt formulaic, with repetitive plan recaps undermining a genuinely relational close.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some effective reflections validating Jonathan's overwhelm and frustration, but key emotional disclosures—especially about his father's feet—were acknowledged yet not explored. The counselor often pivoted quickly to solutions rather than lingering on the emotional core.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on diabetes self-management, narrowing to one concrete behavior with a scheduled one-week check-in. The focus was anchored well but arrived somewhat prematurely, before motivation was fully built.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine power-sharing occurred through offering choices and agreeing to Jonathan's terms, but the counselor often slipped into an advisory posture—prescribing menus, delivering education, and recapping the plan repeatedly. The plan felt more counselor-managed than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong respect for Jonathan's autonomy was central to securing his tentative commitment: no added tasks, multiple options, and honoring his conditions. The score is limited by the initial prescriptive menu and persuasion about why the numbers matter.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor relied heavily on information and persuasion rather than eliciting Jonathan's own motivations. His insights about his father and the 2pm slump emerged spontaneously and were left undeveloped, and his motivation remained largely external.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jonathan's choice and his wife's role were incorporated into the plan, but the overall style was expert-driven, with the counselor structuring the plan and drawing only superficially on Jonathan's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice-offering successfully elicited change talk and a one-week commitment, including Jonathan's spontaneous observation about the 2pm slump. However, fertile openings—like 'maybe that's the chips doing that?' and the father connection—were not amplified or reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not reinforce sustain talk and effectively defused resistance by agreeing to Jonathan's boundary, but sometimes responded to doubt with logic and persuasion (e.g., explaining the numbers after 'I feel fine'). This risked deepening defensiveness rather than letting ambivalence resolve naturally.
