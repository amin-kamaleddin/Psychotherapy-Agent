# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth with effective normalization of Jordan's discomfort and affirmation of his courage. Falls short of 5 due to somewhat generic affirmations and lack of explicit acknowledgment of the weight of his circumstances.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening, accurately capturing Jordan's core ambivalence and the peer-pressure dynamic. However, responses often jump quickly to advice, and deeper emotions like guilt and fear of social isolation go unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on the target behavior from opening to close, with a logical progression from ambivalence to a concrete actionable plan. No drift into unrelated territory.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frames the work jointly and checks Jordan's preferences, and role-play was client-initiated. True two-way idea generation is limited, as the counselor supplies solution menus while Jordan merely selects.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly and repeatedly reinforces Jordan's ownership with optional, choice-framed suggestions and no pressuring language despite the court-mandated context. Jordan's own selections drive the plan throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation is present, but the dominant mode is advice-giving with solution menus rather than drawing motivation from within. Jordan's intrinsic reasons for change are never directly explored via values questions or rulers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites Jordan's input and his choices shape the plan, but the counselor takes the expert role by generating nearly all ideas. Jordan's role is reduced to picking among counselor-authored options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively invites change-oriented thinking that successfully elicited meaningful change talk, which the counselor reinforced. Deeper reasons for change weren't systematically drawn out, with practical options favored over sustained evocation of values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Doesn't amplify sustain talk and offers a brief reframe, but much of the session anchors on barrier-management strategies. Missed double-sided reflections tying Jordan's 'growing up' insight to his fears could better resolve ambivalence.
