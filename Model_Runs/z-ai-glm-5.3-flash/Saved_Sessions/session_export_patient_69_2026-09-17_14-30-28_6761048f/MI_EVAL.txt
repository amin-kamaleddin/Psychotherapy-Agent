# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental toward Kieran's mandated, disengaged presentation, validating his reluctance without labeling. Affirmations were somewhat generic, with no explicit acknowledgment of his strengths or capacities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured both content and emotional tone, particularly the apt reflection that slides-read-aloud felt 'boring and belittling.' Reflections occasionally stayed at surface level without exploring the deeper emotional meaning behind his resignation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good movement from passive sitting to identifying a specific drain (the job workshop) and a concrete, client-owned action (the message to the caseworker), impressive given his extreme reticence. Early menu options drifted toward generic coping, and the action was never tied back to Kieran's broader values or goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Egalitarian, choice-driven stance with genuine co-construction of the caseworker message incorporating Kieran's edits. True two-way collaboration only emerged near the end; earlier, the counselor mostly generated options unilaterally while Kieran remained minimally involved.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly reinforced Kieran's choice at every step through numbered options, permission for minimal engagement, and statements like 'your choice,' with no pressure or directive language anywhere. Kieran's picks directly determined the session's course — a strong demonstration of autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation occurred when Kieran identified the workshop as the main drain and drafted his own wording, and his shift to 'worth a shot I guess' showed motivation surfacing. However, the counselor relied heavily on menu options rather than open questions drawing out Kieran's own reasons, hopes, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively positioned Kieran as the expert on his own experience by offering menus and having him edit the message, deferring to his judgment. Never explicitly acknowledged his expertise, and his frequent 'doesn't matter' responses meant power-sharing was sometimes one-sided in practice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The collaboratively drafted message and Kieran's 'worth a shot' response represent emergent change talk, which the counselor supported by co-authoring. However, change talk was never strategically evoked or deepened — no questions explored what difference change would make or how Kieran would like things to be different.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('whatever,' 'they never listen') was consistently met with acceptance and gentle redirection rather than argument or amplification. No strategic double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk were used to elevate this further.
