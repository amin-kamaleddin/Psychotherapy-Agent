# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with specific affirmations ('takes real honesty,' 'you taking care of two people at once') and no labeling, moralizing, or scolding, even when the client minimizes her drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, emotionally attuned reflections, including catching embedded change talk ('I'm not saying I'd never change anything') and sensitively reflecting the client's guilt about 'stealing from him.' Reflections land on emotional meaning and normalize her guardedness without repeating content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays focused on alcohol use and stress coping, building progressively from exploring relaxation needs to a concrete, client-authored plan with success markers and contingency planning for hard nights.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The work is genuinely co-constructed — the tea experiment, one-mindful-drink allowance, and check-in plan all originate from the client, with the clinician proposing the check-in as a mutual 'Deal?' The client experienced the relationship as collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly affirmed ('no pressure on the wine part, just testing it out,' 'a rough night isn't failure, it's just information'), with the client defining her own success criteria and backup plans. Her comments about 'not being lectured' confirm the effect.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the client herself — her reasons, her plan, her hope — capped by asking 'What do you think made that possible?' so she names her own change process. Her language shifts from defensive to clear commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located with the client ('You know your life better than anyone,' 'your own evidence — that's worth more than anyone's advice'), and her contributions directly shape the session. Textbook power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is selectively reflected and evoked through open questions ('What would a real replacement need to feel like?'), with the client's agency repeatedly named. Her language develops into explicit commitment: 'here's my plan, out loud so it's real.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (alcohol as reward, 'two drinks is manageable,' family comparison) is never reinforced; double-sided reflections hold ambivalence without amplifying the status quo. Relapse risk is reframed as 'information, not failure,' preventing all-or-nothing discouragement.
