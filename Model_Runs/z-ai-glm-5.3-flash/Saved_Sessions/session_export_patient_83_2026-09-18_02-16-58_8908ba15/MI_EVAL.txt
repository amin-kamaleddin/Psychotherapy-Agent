# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently validated Mai's frustration and reframed her skepticism as sensible self-advocacy rather than resistance, with no judgment or labeling. Warmth was consistent, if occasionally slightly formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong, accurately capturing both content and emotional undertone, including a good double-sided reflection about her mother. Reflections occasionally paraphrased rather than deepened into underlying feelings, such as the vulnerability of a health scare at 34.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the adherence goal with a clear, collaborative agenda and no aimless drifting, moving from confusion to a concrete commitment. Direction was somewhat softer during the extended lab-number exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was genuinely two-way: permission was sought before sharing information, and Mai's priorities shaped the agenda (mechanism, grapefruit, lab numbers, plan). Joint inquiry language ('Let's look at them together') reinforced the partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly offered choices, framed the decision as Mai's own, and presented facts so Mai could draw her own conclusions; her plan was fully self-generated. Only minor deduction because information-giving occasionally preceded explicit permission-seeking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician drew motivation from Mai rather than persuading her, showing her the lab values and letting her talk herself into the medication. Her final plan was entirely self-generated, and her diet ideas were treated as legitimate.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Mai was consistently positioned as the expert on her own life, and her knowledge of her routines was actively incorporated into the plan. The clinician avoided the expert stance even while holding the medical information.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Well-timed open questions elicited strong change talk ('maybe the medication isn't such a crazy idea') and concrete commitment language with a self-generated plan. A 5 would require deeper strengthening of commitment/confidence language, such as confidence scaling or exploring obstacles.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never argued against or amplified; instead, double-sided reflections and reframing let the lab data persuade Mai to resolve her own ambivalence. The diet interest was validated rather than framed as opposition; occasionally sustain talk was reflected back at length before redirecting.
