# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard and warmth throughout; no judgment even when Linda voices doubt or sustain talk. Doubt is framed as 'a fair question, not a failure,' and her courage is affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently land accurately and deepen meaning, e.g., capturing how the three-day record shook her self-trust. The counselor mirrors the client's own language ('nervous with a purpose') effectively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focus remains steadily on tobacco cessation while each response advances momentum—from what matters, to support, to a concrete first step and follow-up. The session never drifts from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Inclusive language ('working alongside you') and genuine power-sharing; all plan elements (Carol, the note, the calendar, the two-week timeline) originated from Linda. The counselor positions herself as a resource the client chooses.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership is repeatedly reinforced—'what feels doable, not overwhelming' and 'all of that came from you, not me.' Linda's own comment that she doesn't feel lectured confirms successful autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from the client via open questions rather than imposed; her own words ('I owe myself one more real try') carry the session's inspiration. The counselor amplifies these with minimal direction.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the expert throughout ('You know yourself better than anyone'). Every plan element originates from Linda, with counselor contributions building on rather than directing her statements.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Evocative questions elicit progressively stronger change talk, from 'maybe I owe myself one more real try' to concrete commitments (telling Carol, calendar, two-week return). The counselor explicitly highlights the shift from 'maybe' to planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('why bother when I've failed so many times') is acknowledged without amplification via double-sided reflections and reframing, then gently redirected. The client's language progressively shifts toward hope and planning.
