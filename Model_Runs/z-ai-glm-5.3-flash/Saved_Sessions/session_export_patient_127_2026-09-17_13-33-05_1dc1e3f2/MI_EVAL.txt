# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist showed consistent warmth and nonjudgment, opening with congratulations and validating progress throughout. Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, with emotional content occasionally rushed past toward problem-solving.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was largely absent, with responses jumping to advice rather than reflecting feelings like loneliness or fear of rejection. Timur repeatedly had to restate his own perspective, suggesting his viewpoint wasn't being tracked.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on remaining crime- and drug-free and building employment, consistently steering toward concrete steps like the Tuesday PO meeting. Direction was somewhat rigidly action-oriented, pulling focus away from identity and meaning toward logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was largely expert-driven, with pre-packaged menus, 30-day plans, and scripts delivered in lecture format. Timur's ideas shaped the plan mainly through his pushback rather than genuine co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor occasionally offered choices and accepted Timur's decision to write his own texts, but much of the session was directive with unsolicited advice and prescribed techniques. Pushing a full 30-day plan against Timur's preference for small steps undermined his autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was rarely drawn from within Timur; almost no open questions explored his values, hopes, or reasons for change. His most evocative material—quiet mornings and being worthy of Fiona—surfaced spontaneously and went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role, delivering prescriptive plans, scripts, and checklists before Timur asked for them. Timur had to push back to retain ownership, and his expertise about his own life was rarely incorporated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Timur generated meaningful change talk spontaneously ('Maybe this is how I get there,' 'Might as well see how far I can push it'), but the counselor rarely recognized, reflected, or deepened it. No evocative questions were asked, and the final commitment statement was met with closing logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoided amplifying sustain talk and offered a mild reframe of 'lazy' as masking burnout or hopelessness. However, there were no skillful double-sided reflections or strategic pivots using Timur's doubts as a bridge to resolving ambivalence.
