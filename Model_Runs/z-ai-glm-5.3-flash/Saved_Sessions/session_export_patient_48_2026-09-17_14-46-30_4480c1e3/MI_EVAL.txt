# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and normalized ambivalence throughout. Affirmations felt somewhat formulaic and tied to plan-compliance rather than deeper unconditional regard for the client as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured the client's ambivalence and values, though reflections were often brief summaries bundled with questions. Missed opportunities to explore the emotional weight of his all-or-nothing self-criticism and discouragement over past failed attempts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on increasing physical activity from the opening question, with every exchange building progressively toward a concrete plan. Strong direction maintained while staying client-paced, with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely a joint problem-solving process, with genuine two-way exchange and client buy-in. However, stacked questions (2-4 per turn) and repeated menus of options tilted the power balance toward the counselor's agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed choices as the client's own with no pressure or coercion, and accepted his honest confidence rating. Frequent pre-formulated options and drafted scripts occasionally did the generative work for the client rather than trusting his own choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions successfully drew motivation from within, with the client's confidence rising from 4 to 7 through his own insights. Occasionally supplied content (drafted texts, self-talk scripts) rather than letting the client articulate everything in his own words, and deeper values exploration was never pursued.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the client as decision-maker, and his contributions demonstrably shaped the plan. At times steered with pre-packaged options and suggested scripts, slightly undermining the message that expertise resides within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through rulers, commitments, and implementation planning, with the client's confidence rising from 4 to 7. The client closed with genuine commitment language and gratitude for being pushed beyond vague intentions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to sustain talk with neutral reflections and quick pivots to realistic options rather than amplifying barriers. However, repeated failure-focused questions ('what if you miss') inadvertently kept attention on possible non-adherence.
