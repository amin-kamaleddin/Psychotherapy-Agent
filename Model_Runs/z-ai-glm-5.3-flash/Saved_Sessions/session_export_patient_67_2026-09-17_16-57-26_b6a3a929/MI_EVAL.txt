# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; Kenji felt accepted ('thanks for not making me feel like an idiot'). Affirmations were somewhat formulaic ('good,' 'solid,' 'brave').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several accurate reflections captured Kenji's surprise and ambivalence. Reflections stayed brief and factual, missing deeper emotions like his fear and preference for directness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on evaluating and reducing alcohol use throughout, progressing logically from exploration to commitment to a concrete review plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Work framed as joint ('we'll map next steps together') and the plan was co-constructed. The counselor tended to supply menus of options and left Kenji's final question about 'fine' unanswered.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kenji made all key decisions: starting tonight, logging immediately, choosing delay and walk strategies. The counselor consistently offered choices and reinforced his ownership of the agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocative moves like the readiness ruler and 'what would move you from 4 to 5 or 6?' elicited genuine motivation. Missed chances to explore values and deepen his emerging curiosity.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kenji positioned as the expert with questions like 'which feels more doable?' and his suggestions shaped the plan. The counselor occasionally slipped into a prescriptive expert role with tips before asking Kenji's ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation and reinforcement of commitment language; Kenji moved from avoidance to voluntary commitment ('starting tonight... I'll actually do it'). Could deepen change talk further by exploring what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used neutral double-sided acknowledgments without arguing, but did little active reframing of sustain talk like 'a bit much' or 'what if the numbers are fine,' missing chances to explore values and pivot toward change.
