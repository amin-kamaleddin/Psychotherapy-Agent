# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout despite Thomas's dismissiveness and minimization, never lecturing or pushing. Could deepen acceptance with more explicit affirmations of his strengths (e.g., having quit smoking before, protecting family time).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent, accurate reflections capture both content and ambivalence (e.g., feeling lectured, priorities of family steadiness). However, empathy sometimes stays summary-level and misses emotional exploration of the morning cough, fear of being 'one of those dads,' and his wife's concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking and drinking and progresses logically through exploration, rulers, options, and a concrete time-bound plan. Withheld from 5 due to slightly broad early exploration and occasional multiple-question turns that dilute focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely an equal partnership: asks permission, co-constructs the plan, and treats Thomas's practical concerns (round-buying) as legitimate expertise. Occasionally slips into service-provider mode by supplying drafted texts and notes, and the four-option menu is more counselor-generated than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength: consistently reinforces choice ('no pressure, your choice,' 'go at whatever pace feels right'), offers advice as an option menu, and accepts Thomas's resistance without pushback. Frames Thomas as the owner of the change process throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Deliberately draws motivation from within via rulers, conditional questions, and values exploration, eliciting Thomas's own reasons and plan. Evocation weakens later in the session, where the counselor fills space with options and drafts rather than deepening his motivations (e.g., being a dad his son can model).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Thomas as the decision-maker, and his ideas substantially shaped the plan (text to Dave, doctor's note). Not a 5 because the counselor supplies most options rather than drawing them primarily from Thomas or explicitly naming him as the expert on his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk from a precontemplative client using rulers, hypotheticals, and open questions, producing commitment-adjacent language ('I'd start this weekend,' confidence 7). Thomas's change talk was often qualified, and the counselor could have amplified and consolidated the commitment language more deliberately.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids reinforcing sustain talk but reflects minimizations ('it's normal, under control') neutrally, risking amplification of the status quo. Misses double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with Thomas's own concerns (cough, hangovers, wife, son) and deliberate pivots toward ambivalence.
