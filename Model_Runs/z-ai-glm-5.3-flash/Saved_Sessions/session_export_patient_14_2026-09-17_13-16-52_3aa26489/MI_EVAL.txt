# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged the client's frustration appropriately but offered almost no affirmations and maintained a businesslike, checklist-like tone throughout; the one late affirmation was generic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections were sparse and formulaic; opportunities to reflect the emotional weight of breathlessness at 47, fear of a health scare, and the client's pride in efficiency were largely missed in favor of lists and safety caveats.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on weight/health despite time-pressure deflections and progressed logically to a concrete one-week plan; slight deduction for drift during the email exchange and direction achieved via task-assignment rather than collaborative focusing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The plan was adjusted to the client's constraints and options were offered, but the counselor dominated content with lists and menus, with no joint agenda-setting or negotiation of the goal itself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine choice points were offered, but the counselor frequently defaulted to directives, prescribing exact plans, targets, and scripts without being asked; client ownership was never explicitly emphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplied reasons, education, and solutions rather than eliciting the client's own; almost no open questions were used to draw out motivation, and the client's motivation emerged despite the counselor's style.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally asked what sounded doable and offered choices, but quickly shifted to an expert stance with unsolicited education and drafting the client's email; the client's expertise was never explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Strong spontaneous change talk ('get hold of this weight', 'starting tomorrow', fear of a health scare) was never deepened with open questions or confidence probing; the counselor responded with prescriptions instead of expanding motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never amplified, but it was also never engaged with strategically—barriers were routed around with practical problem-solving rather than reflected upon or used to resolve ambivalence.
