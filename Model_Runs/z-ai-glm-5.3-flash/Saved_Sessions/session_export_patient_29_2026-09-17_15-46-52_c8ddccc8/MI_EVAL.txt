# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with no labeling even when past failures are discussed. Affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic; deeper affirmations of perseverance would strengthen the score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture both content and emotion (e.g., determination mixed with frustration over stress and social triggers). Deeper emotional material — fear of repeat failure, wife's skepticism, shame of caving — is acknowledged but not deeply validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, consistent focus on quitting smoking with each exchange moving toward a concrete outcome. Client leaves with a medication plan, quit date, coping scripts, an ally, and scheduled follow-ups.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client preferences are actively solicited and plans co-constructed, especially in the second half. However, large blocks of expert information are delivered before inviting the client's input, making collaboration uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Client choice is emphasized repeatedly, with options offered at every decision point and advice framed as choices. Frequent pre-packaged recommendations (scripts, checklists, routines) implicitly steer toward counselor-preferred solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Readiness/confidence rulers and preference questions successfully evoked strong change and commitment talk. Missed opportunities to explore the 'why' behind the readiness of 9 and confidence of 6, or to elicit the client's own coping ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens collaboratively and offers choices, but substantial portions shift into expert-driven information delivery (titration schedules, checklists, scripts) with limited inquiry into the client's own ideas first. The balance tilts toward counselor expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of rulers and preference questions drew out commitment language ('I'll call my doctor tomorrow,' 'I feel better about this attempt than any before'), which was reinforced. Could deepen change talk further by elaborating on motivations and summarizing them explicitly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (vivid dream worries, Mark's ribbing, work stress) is never dwelt upon; the counselor briefly validates then pivots to concrete solutions. Minimal validation and no double-sided reflections limit ambivalence resolution.
