# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated respect and avoided judgment about the piercing, affirming the client's autonomy. Explicit affirmations of the client as a person beyond her autonomy were somewhat limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including a double-sided reflection and accurately tracking the shift in the client's language. Deeper emotional reflection on her frustration and the meaning of self-expression was only lightly touched.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The oral health focus was maintained, but repeated 'weigh both sides' questions circled without progress for several exchanges. Direction only became effective after the pivot to the middle-ground question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor acted as an equal partner, co-constructing the middle-ground approach and building on client-generated ideas. The repeated pro/con questioning early on felt more like a technique applied at the client than a negotiated agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support — options were framed as the client's to generate, ownership of change was explicitly attributed to her, and removal was never pushed. The client directly credited this approach: 'it felt more like my idea.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within, eliciting authentic reason talk and client-generated solutions. Early evocation attempts (repeated weighing questions) failed and were repeated rather than adapted.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client, which she confirmed worked well. More collaborative agenda-setting early on would have strengthened the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The middle-ground question skillfully evoked a client-generated plan, which was amplified by asking 'What made you open to that possibility?' Some change talk appeared to mimic counselor phrasing, slightly muddying attribution.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and double-sided reflections were used appropriately. However, the repeated 'weighing both sides' question let the client rehearse anti-change arguments several times before the effective pivot.
