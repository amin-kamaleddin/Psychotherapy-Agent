# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard ('there's no judgment,' 'you've got someone in your corner'), and never criticized Marcus even when he was defensive about the screening.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that tracked Marcus's ambivalence and peer concerns using his own words. Slightly less depth when exploring the emotional weight of his 'knot in my stomach' worry.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, progressive focus on alcohol use throughout, moving from exploring the pull of drinking to a concrete first step and follow-up plan. No topic drift; each exchange built toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Framed every step as joint ('you tell me if it works') and co-created the check-in plan conditional on Marcus's agreement. Power-sharing was genuine and consistent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced choice ('this is your call, your life') with no pressure or persuasion. Marcus's final commitment was fully self-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never lectured or supplied reasons to change; questions like 'is that more of an assumption?' drew out Marcus's own insights and spontaneous change talk, including his desire to report back.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned Marcus as the expert ('you know your world better than I do'). His own ideas—Jake's example, the driving excuse, one sober night—shaped the entire plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through open questions about what sobriety would actually be like. Marcus produced strong change talk ('part of me is kinda curious if I can actually do it') and a commitment that was then reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not argue with sustain talk and used double-sided exploration effectively, turning peer pressure into an examination of assumptions via Jake's example. Minor deduction because the early pros/cons question deliberately amplified sustain talk, though it was well managed.
