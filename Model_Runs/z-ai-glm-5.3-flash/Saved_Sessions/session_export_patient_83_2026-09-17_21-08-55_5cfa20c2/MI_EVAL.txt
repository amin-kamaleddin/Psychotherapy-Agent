# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Mai's frustration and affirming her plan-building. Deeper affirmation of her emotional experience of feeling dismissed could have strengthened this further.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening that captured Mai's core concerns and ambivalence throughout. Reflections occasionally summarized rather than explored deeper emotions like feeling dismissed by her physician.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Quickly realigned after a misattuned opening reflection and kept the session focused on medication adherence with clear summaries and forward-looking questions. The initial mismatch and a slightly muddled final exchange prevent a top score.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the encounter as joint work and co-constructed the adherence plan from Mai's own ideas, though the informational segment was somewhat didactic and the final exchange became confusing when the counselor answered their own question.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly returned decision authority to Mai ('then you can decide what makes sense') and credited all insights and solutions to her without any pressure or persuasion. Mai's ownership of the plan reflects excellent autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reliably drew motivation and solutions from Mai rather than lecturing, driving her shift from wanting to skip the medication to feeling ready. Some motivation came from counselor information delivery rather than evocation, and personal values weren't deeply explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Mai as the expert on her own life, and her contributions materially shaped the plan. The statin information was delivered somewhat didactically, and her own knowledge defining the solutions wasn't explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked concrete commitment language through open questions about what feels manageable and what would build confidence. Opportunities for deeper commitment work, such as scaling questions or exploring values, were not fully exploited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled skepticism and anxiety well by validating, then pivoting to choice and reframing the muscle pain worry through Mai's own rule of thumb. Double-sided reflections weren't used to explicitly resolve ambivalence.
