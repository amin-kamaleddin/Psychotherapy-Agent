# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

**Client:** Wiremu | **Session Type:** Motivational Interviewing | **Reviewed by:** Clinical Supervisor

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental stance maintained, even when violence and drinking were discussed ("I hear how much your tamariki matter"). No labeling; defensiveness normalized ("That's normal, and we can plan for it"). Warmth is somewhat procedural/solution-focused; lacks explicit affirmations of Wiremu as a person (e.g., "you've already shown courage by calling your mum").

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☑3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Opens with a decent complex reflection (torn between whānau support and independence) and reflects the fear of the "disappointed" look. However, reflections are brief and pivot quickly to questions/advice. Emotional weight of remorse ("I've messed things up", "I'm not a bad person") and shame around being the "family disappointment" is never deeply explored.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 ☑5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus throughout: resolving ambivalence about the whānau move, preparing for the conversation, and building a concrete plan (trial length, boundaries, review date, written agreement, relapse contingency). Every turn advances the agenda; no topic drift; focus ties directly to the client's goal of reunification with his children.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Strong collaborative texture: Wiremu's ideas (trial period, boundaries, review) form the backbone of the plan; counselor checks in before each step; agreement offered for editing. Falls short of 5 because counselor turns are dense with pre-formatted content (scripts, tips, full agreement), leaving Wiremu mostly reacting to counselor-generated material.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Choices framed as Wiremu's ("Would you like to...?", "Want to tweak any line?"); his decisions honored (8-week trial, "remain sober" clause); plan content originates from him. Mild over-steering via ready-made scripts, stacked leading questions, and pre-structured options; Wiremu's own words ("feels a bit scripted") suggest this.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Motivation repeatedly drawn from Wiremu: importance ruler, "What would move that number up?", "What worries you most?" elicited his own reasons ("actions, not promises"; "I've come too far"). Rehearsal had him voice commitment aloud. Evocation concentrated on whānau-conversation logistics; confidence, past successes, and deeper values not explicitly explored; later turns shift to counselor-generated content.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☑3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Counselor invites input and Wiremu's contributions shaped the plan (8 weeks, own room, car, chores). However, the expert role is frequently assumed — scripts, phrasing, and a full written agreement supplied with limited space for Wiremu's own words (he noted the language "sounds a bit stiff"). Pace is directive with stacked questions and pre-selected options.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☑4 ☐5
**Comments:** Importance ruler evoked strong change talk ("Knowing it actually helps me get my kids back sooner"). Wiremu voiced repeated commitment ("I'll do anything to get them back"), affirmed by the counselor, and rehearsed commitment aloud (behavioral rehearsal). Evocation was narrow — focused on conversation logistics; confidence never explicitly assessed.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☑3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (babysitting fears, defensiveness, temptation to "hit the bottle" if refused) was not amplified; future-oriented questions used appropriately. However, no double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk, and when relapse risk surfaced, the counselor moved straight to scripted responses rather than exploring or reframing the ambivalence.

---

### **Overall Global Ratings Score:** **3.8/5**

**Summary:** A solid, well-directed MI session with clear strengths: excellent direction and focus, effective use of an importance ruler, behavioral rehearsal of difficult conversations, and a concrete, client-shaped plan. Key areas for improvement: (1) deepen empathy — reflect emotional material (shame, fear of rejection, relapse risk) before moving to solutions; (2) soften sustain talk more actively with double-sided reflections; (3) share expertise more genuinely — reduce pre-packaged scripts and stacked questions; (4) broaden evocation to include confidence and past successes, and add more explicit affirmations of Wiremu's character and courage.
