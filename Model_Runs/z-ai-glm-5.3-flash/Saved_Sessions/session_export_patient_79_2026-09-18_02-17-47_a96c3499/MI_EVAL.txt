# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental, and respectful throughout, with affirmations of Luis's self-knowledge and explicit honoring of his terms. Falls short of mastery because warmth occasionally feels formulaic and there's limited explicit normalization of his fears or past failures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including skilled double-sided reflections and a powerful response to the disclosure about his son. Some reflections lean celebratory rather than emotionally deep, and the final question came quickly after a vulnerable moment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, well-paced funneling from overwhelm to one doable behavior, then to timing and follow-up. Direction is somewhat reactive, however — no explicit summary or completeness check of the plan, with the ending drifting into process talk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — the plan (coffee maker placement, timing, one two-minute call) is co-created entirely from Luis's own words and constraints. The clinician negotiates rather than imposes, with no expert-driven advice anywhere.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: permission-style questions, acceptance of Luis's ceiling without pushing, and explicit agreements to his terms ('no lecture, no file'). Even the commitment question leaves the choice entirely with Luis.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of ability, commitment, and deeply personal reasons for change — Luis's son emerged entirely unprompted. Luis progressed from sarcasm to spontaneous change talk and self-generated confidence by session's end.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positions Luis as the expert ('You know your rhythm with Oscar better than anyone'), with every solution originating from the client. Active power-sharing shaped the entire session and outcome.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation with a resistant client — eliciting a concrete plan, a specific start day, and the intrinsic reason beneath it all, then consistently reflecting and deepening the change talk. Luis moved from 'try' to 'tomorrow' to 'two minutes so my kid doesn't see me like that again.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids arguing with sustain talk, responding with empathy and validation before redirecting to Luis's agency. Some minimization ('Oscar's been quiet lately') passed without a gentle reframe, leaving a bit of ambivalence unaddressed.
