# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and validates Laura's reduced commitment ('Two times isn't cheating'). Affirmations are somewhat generic and lack individualized recognition of her self-awareness and honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate reflections, but frequently bypasses emotionally meaningful statements (her 'failing vs. building' shift, the grounding feeling) in favor of quick logistical problem-solving. Missed opportunities to deepen reflection on the significance of her insights.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior, moving efficiently from values clarification to a specific, measurable plan with a built-in end-of-week review. A clear strength of the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly invites Laura's input and genuinely incorporates her contributions into the plan. Collaboration is partly constrained by counselor-supplied menus and frameworks rather than fully open co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offers Laura choices at every step and explicitly honors her decision to reduce commitment, with no pressuring or directing. Her closing comment ('This feels lighter') reflects genuine felt autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura's change talk emerges largely spontaneously but is not systematically elicited or deepened; questions are predominantly procedural. Rich motivational material passes unexplored while counselor expertise in action planning remains prominent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for Laura's preferences throughout, but the intervention's underlying structure (menus, if-then frameworks, scales) is counselor-designed with Laura reacting and tweaking. True power-sharing would invite her to generate options first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerges and is selectively reinforced via summaries, but the counselor rarely uses open questions designed to deliberately evoke or amplify commitment language. Energy goes into logistics rather than consolidating motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively normalizes self-doubt and pivots to achievable plans, reframing reduced commitment positively. Does not use double-sided reflections to actively resolve ambivalence, and some discussion of failure scenarios keeps attention on anticipated failure.
