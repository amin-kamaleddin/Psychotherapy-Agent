# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing anxiety and affirming small wins like keeping shifts. Warmth occasionally feels scripted and the emotional weight of incarceration and fear of failure go unvalidated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects Mateo's core discrepancy and validates his fear during the role-play. Some reflections are surface restatements, and the counselor misses chances to deepen reflection on loyalty to Darnell and underlying loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the concrete target behavior—avoiding Friday night contact with Darnell—with no drift across the session. Every exchange progresses toward a testable action plan with clear follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Meaningfully incorporates Mateo's ideas (gym, direct script, Renata, packed bag) and frames the work as joint. Rapid protocol-style suggestions dominate the 'how,' leaving Mateo mostly approving or tweaking pre-built options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices—scripts, replacements, role-play format, follow-up method—and accepts Mateo's modifications. Autonomy is partly undercut by pre-packaged menus and missing explicit ownership reinforcement ('this is your call').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits Mateo's own motivations ('staying out of trouble matters most,' 'family life bad') and builds capability evidence through small wins and role-play. Falls short due to top-down tactical supply and lack of values exploration or confidence rulers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing through permission-asking and choice invitations, but the session is heavily directive: the counselor supplies scripts, tactics, if-then plans, and menus. Mateo largely selects from pre-built options rather than co-creating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation of change talk through small-win questions and refusal-script rehearsal, producing self-generated language ('one Friday, I can do one Friday'). Slips into advice-giving that extracts less of Mateo's own motivation than a fully skillful approach.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently pivots away from sustain talk (mandated attendance, 'I'll always cave') without amplifying doubt. Relies on redirection and tactics rather than explicitly reflecting or double-sided reframing the ambivalence.
