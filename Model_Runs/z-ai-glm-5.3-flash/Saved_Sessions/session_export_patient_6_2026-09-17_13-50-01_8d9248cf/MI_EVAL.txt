# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone maintained throughout, including appreciation for Alexei's honesty and validation of his fear. However, warmth is somewhat functional, quickly pivoting to advice rather than fully affirming his courage in disclosing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal—only two genuine reflections, with key emotional disclosures (fear of his mother, shame about 'looking soft') going unreflected. The counselor repeatedly jumps to problem-solving before demonstrating understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session follows a reasonable trajectory and effectively targets alcohol once the cop incident emerges. However, the counselor drifts into generic soccer-performance advice and never consolidates an explicit plan around alcohol use itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative elements like agenda-setting, preference checks, and interactive role-play. But unsolicited expert checklists delivered before collaboration create an expert-to-client dynamic, and no change plan is truly co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice points are offered ('Which feels more urgent?') and Alexei isn't pressured to disclose after resisting. However, autonomy is undermined by heavy, permission-less advice-giving delivered as instruction and should-based framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The open question about worries successfully elicited the beer incident and goal-oriented change talk. But the counselor supplies refusal lines, benefits, and reasons for change rather than drawing out Alexei's own motivations via open questions and summaries.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asking for input and having Alexei generate his own refusal lines are genuine strengths. However, the overall structure is expert-driven, with prescriptive advice menus positioning the counselor as the expert and Alexei's contributions shaping the session less than the counselor's agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Focusing on soccer goals successfully evoked meaningful change talk about protecting his scholarship and future. However, this change talk is rarely deepened with reflections or evocative questions, and the counselor pivots to technique lists instead of amplifying it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is mostly met with acceptance rather than reinforcement, which is appropriate. However, no reframes or double-sided reflections link it to Alexei's goals, and the final 'one or two times isn't a huge deal' goes unaddressed.
