# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, accepting Richard's limits on alcohol and his pace, but regard stays neutral with no affirmations of his honesty, insight, or willingness to involve his wife.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** One good early reflection of ambivalence, after which the counselor became almost entirely informational; Richard's fears (buddy's statin problems, forgetting pills), love of wine, and emotionally rich statements were met with statistics and tips rather than reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly tethered to cholesterol management with no drift across meds, travel adherence, alcohol, and doctor communication; but focus was maintained through structured checklists and rapid topic-jumping rather than building one coherent thread.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard drove topic selection and the counselor offered options rather than dictating, with the Richard-initiated role-play a genuine strength; but pre-packaged plans and check-in structures were delivered as instructions rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and affirmed Richard's right to moderate rather than quit alcohol, letting him pick his own commitments; partly undermined by rapid-fire advice and counselor-drafted routines and scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Richard's motivations (retirement, staying active with his wife) surfaced on his own, but the counselor never actively drew them out or expanded them; questions were logistical or menu-based, with no evocative exploration of values, confidence scaling, or emotional meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Solicited Richard's input through choice menus and incorporated his ideas, and the role-play gave him an active expert role; but the counselor repeatedly assumed the expert position with statistics and scripts without checking what Richard knew or wanted.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard generated substantial change talk from his own motivation (retirement, wife), and the counselor evoked some commitment language via menu choices; however, strongest statements were never reflected back, no rulers were used, and change talk was left undeliberated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce minimization or dwell on barriers, but also did little active softening—no double-sided reflections or reframing; constant 'any worries' questions arguably invited more sustain talk, and fears were answered with data rather than acknowledged then pivoted.
