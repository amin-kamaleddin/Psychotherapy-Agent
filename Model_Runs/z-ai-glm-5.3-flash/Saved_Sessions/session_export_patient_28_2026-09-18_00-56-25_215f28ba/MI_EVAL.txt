# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed Dante's wisdom and self-knowledge without judging his struggles, e.g., calling his low-bar plan 'wisdom you earned the hard way' rather than excuses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently captured both the surface content and underlying emotion, such as naming the loss of his old identity and validating that 'starting over from zero' makes small steps feel like they barely count.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question and reflection moved the conversation toward Dante's own solutions—identifying a lunch walk, designing a plan, and anticipating barriers—rather than dwelling on problems or past failures.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was explicitly framed as Dante's own ('That's yours, not mine'), and the clinician positioned herself as a supportive partner by offering check-ins rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices came entirely from Dante—the walk, the loop, the note, telling his wife—with the clinician reinforcing his authority over his own behavior change throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully drew out Dante's motivations ('feeling clearer when I move') and fears (quitting publicly), culminating in his own change talk like 'maybe the walking comes first and the feeling follows.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue was genuinely two-way, with the clinician soliciting Dante's expertise about his own schedule ('knowing your days the way only you do') and co-creating solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician noticed, reflected, and reinforced Dante's change talk at every turn, including his pivotal insight 'maybe that's the problem, not the walk' and his commitment to starting tomorrow.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** When Dante voiced doubt ('does that sound realistic? Or am I just making excuses again?'), the clinician responded with acceptance and reframing rather than confrontation, letting him resolve his own ambivalence.
