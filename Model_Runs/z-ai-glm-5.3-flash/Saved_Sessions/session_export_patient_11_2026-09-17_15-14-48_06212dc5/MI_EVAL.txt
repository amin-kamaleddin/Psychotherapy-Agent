# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, and affirming (e.g., 'That's excellent planning and self-compassion'), with clear respect for the client's choices. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and occasionally echo the client's own words rather than tying praise to her values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening, including the attuned reflection on the jar moment and barriers like tiredness and routine. However, the emotional core of past failures and the all-or-nothing pattern is never deeply reflected or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains tight focus on building a walking routine, progressing coherently from exploration to a concrete commitment. The counselor appropriately avoids drifting into nutrition, consistent with the client's stated priority.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided, with joint wording of the reminder and client co-authorship of the plan. Power-sharing is slightly uneven as the counselor proposes most structural options, with the client selecting from counselor-generated menus more often than generating her own.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength: the counselor consistently offers options rather than directives and honors the client's pacing and check-in phrasing requests. The client explicitly experiences ownership ('Thanks for not pushing me on the food stuff'; 'I want this to feel like mine').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor relies on logistics and closed scaling questions rather than open questions exploring Amira's values, hopes, or why this time is different. Because the client arrived highly motivated, opportunities to deepen and consolidate her reasons for change were largely missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offers choices and defers to the client's expertise, and her contributions (shoes cue, fridge checkmark, check-in phrasing) visibly shape the session. Rapid-fire option menus sometimes steer the agenda ahead of the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amira generates abundant change talk, but the counselor does little to evoke or deepen it, quickly pivoting to planning logistics. Deliberate evocative questions (e.g., 'What makes this time different?') and reflections amplifying commitment language are absent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoids dwelling on barriers, reframing tiredness and past failures neutrally and steering toward solutions like the 10-minute fallback. Softening is achieved mostly by redirecting to planning rather than strategic double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
