# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, accepting Ethan's framing that drinking is social ('That makes sense') while gently addressing driving risk without alarmism. Fell short of 5 due to a business-like, checklist-driven tone with no explicit affirmations of Ethan's strengths or honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used consistent, accurate reflections including effective double-sided reflections capturing Ethan's ambivalence about drinking. Occasionally functioned as summaries before pivoting to questions, and deeper emotional content (embarrassment about vomiting, fear of losing peer standing) went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on the target behavior (weekend drinking) across the entire session with no topic drift. The session progressed logically from assessment to a concrete behavioral plan with follow-up confidence checks anchoring the direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session functioned as joint problem-solving with role-play and real-time incorporation of Ethan's feedback that visibly shaped the plan. The counselor drove the agenda and sequence without explicitly negotiating it, with Ethan passively going along.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Ethan's choice through permission-asking and framing harm reduction as his decision, with no pressuring language. Autonomy statements remained mostly implicit, and rapid-fire suggestion lists somewhat constrained the options Ethan considered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Ethan via rulers, open questions, and rehearsal, producing strong self-generated change talk and raising confidence from 6 to 8. Frequently supplied content (refusal lines, strategy menus) rather than eliciting it, and never explored Ethan's deeper values as intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed that expertise resides with the client by asking permission and letting Ethan pick which options felt natural, with his contributions shaping the plan. The counselor still supplied most of the menu of options, making power-sharing somewhat top-down.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked and developed change talk through the confidence ruler, refusal-line elicitation, and rehearsal, yielding commitment language ('I could try that this weekend') and a self-generated reason the counselor reinforced. Much of the change talk was scaffolded by counselor-supplied options rather than evoked purely from the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided reinforcing Ethan's sustain talk, using a textbook double-sided reflection and brief acknowledgment before redirecting toward harm reduction. Never explicitly affirmed the legitimacy of Ethan's concerns (fun, belonging) while pivoting, and some redirects jumped quickly to advice lists.
