# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  psychiatrist  consistently  communicated  unconditional  positive  regard  without  any  judgment  or  disapproval,  even  as  Tariq  expressed  frustration  and  externalized  blame.  Repeated  validation  ('that  says  everything  about  who  you  are')  was  confirmed  by  Tariq's  closing  gratitude  for  not  being  lectured.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective  listening  was  highly  accurate  and  consistent,  with  complex  reflections  capturing  both  surface  content  and  underlying  meaning.  The  double-sided  reflection  on  Tariq's  ambivalence  and  the  reflection  of  the  emotional  core  (family  connection)  deepened  his  openness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  clinician  maintained  a  clear,  consistent  focus  on  managing  frustration  through  to  release  while  pursuing  the  family  call,  with  every  response  steering  back  to  this  target.  The  session  followed  a  logical  arc  from  validating  frustration  through  plan-building,  rehearsal,  and  commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  session  was  genuinely  collaborative,  with  Tariq's  ideas  (the  script,  grounding  technique,  fallback  plan)  shaping  its  direction  at  every  step.  The  psychiatrist  never  lectured  or  prescribed,  instead  asking  for  his  perspective  and  building  on  his  answers.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The  psychiatrist  regularly  affirmed  Tariq's  ownership  of  the  change  process  and  left  all  final  decisions  with  him.  The  score  falls  short  of  5  only  because  the  supervisor  suggestion  was  clinician-initiated  rather  than  elicited  from  the  client  first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  clinician  operated  from  the  core  MI  assumption  that  motivation  resides  within  the  client,  drawing  out  Tariq's  own  reasons  (mother's  sacrifice,  father's  birthday,  desire  to  go  home).  Evocative  questions  led  to  rich  commitment  language  ('I'm  done  losing,'  'I'll  ask  tomorrow  morning').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  psychiatrist  clearly  positioned  Tariq  as  the  expert  ('You  know  yourself  best'),  repeatedly  asking  for  his  perspective.  Tariq's  own  contributions  substantially  shaped  the  session's  content,  the  hallmark  of  high-level  partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change  talk  was  skillfully  and  consistently  evoked  through  open  questions  rather  than  prescribed  solutions,  producing  rich  client-generated  motivation  ('I'd  rather  try  than  sit  and  guess,'  'Going  home  wins.  Every  time').  Having  Tariq  rehearse  his  exact  words  was  textbook  change  talk  cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain  talk  was  consistently  redirected  toward  change  without  dismissal—blame  and  fear  of  rejection  were  briefly  empathized  with,  then  reframed  as  a  choice  ('What  feels  riskier:  asking  or  not  asking?').  This  let  Tariq  resolve  his  own  ambivalence  rather  than  being  argued  out  of  it.
