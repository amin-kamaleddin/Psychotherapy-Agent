# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Polite and nonjudgmental tone ('I'm glad you reached out,' 'Take care'), but regard is generic and undermined by repeatedly ignoring Ingrid's stated need to leave — she said goodbye four separate times while the counselor kept adding new safety instructions.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Virtually no reflective listening; the counselor never reflects Ingrid's statements, ignoring her key clarification that she is not yet on phenelzine and her expressed relief at having a plan. Understanding conveyed is purely informational rather than relational.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Clinically relevant direction (safety information) but poor session management — four redundant re-deliveries of the same advice after Ingrid signaled the session must end, making the close drawn out and directionless.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** One-way information transmission from a fixed safety script; the counselor never asks what Ingrid knows or wants, and her contributions do not shape the responses. No shared agenda-setting or joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Advice is delivered as directive instruction ('Don't stop or switch medications without your prescriber's plan') rather than elicited choice, and twice offering to draft her questions for her subtly undermines her stated self-efficacy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Essentially no evocation — the only question is the opening one, after which the counselor exclusively tells. Ingrid's motivations and plans surface on their own and are never explored or drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Heavily expert-driven with three dense information blocks in a row; offering to draft questions for Ingrid after she stated she would write her own implicitly discounts her competence and autonomy.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ingrid spontaneously offers strong commitment language ('I'll talk to my prescriber first,' 'I'll write down my questions myself') that the counselor never affirms, deepens, or reinforces — each is met instead with another round of safety instructions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is minimal (mainly time constraints) and the counselor doesn't argue against it, but also doesn't skillfully acknowledge or redirect it — her expressed time limit is effectively steamrolled rather than addressed.
