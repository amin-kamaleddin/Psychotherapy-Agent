# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor communicates warm, nonjudgmental regard ('no failure,' 'that's a sensible, kind first step') and validates Aisha's discouragement. However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic and there is little explicit unconditional regard expressed for Aisha independent of her plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor demonstrates solid reflective listening, accurately capturing Aisha's dual state of determination and fear of failure ('You sound determined and frustrated'). Reflections tend to be brief and content-focused, and the emotional core of her body image distress ('feeling gross') is never explicitly reflected back or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a crystal-clear focus on initiating exercise, moving efficiently from readiness and confidence rulers through barriers and supports to a fully specified plan (Tuesday 7:30 walk, backup dance, childcare, Wednesday check-in). Each counselor turn advances toward the concrete target with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is largely co-constructed, with Aisha choosing the day, time, backup activity, and support person, and her answers genuinely shaping next steps. Repeated counselor offers to draft texts and write items for Aisha create a mild helper-doer asymmetry, and the opening agenda was set by the counselor rather than jointly negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently frames steps as optional ('Would you like to plan...,' 'no failure') and Aisha's choices shape the plan. Autonomy support is diluted by the counselor generating solutions and offering to do tasks for Aisha rather than reinforcing her ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evocative tools are used (readiness and confidence rulers, asking about enjoyed activities, supports, and barriers), and much of the plan emerges from Aisha. However, the counselor does not deepen her own reasons for change—the body image pain driving her motivation is never actively explored—and largely rides her pre-existing motivation rather than drawing it out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks permission and seeks Aisha's input, but drifts into a coach-like role during planning: drafting texts, listing self-reward ideas, and prescribing reminder tips rather than consistently drawing solutions from Aisha. Frequent 'Want me to...' offers slightly undercut the message that her expertise resides within her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha supplies abundant change talk naturally, and the counselor uses confidence rulers and planning invitations. However, the counselor rarely reflects or amplifies her change talk (e.g., missing 'hoping to feel proud instead of gross for once'), leaving momentum largely client-generated rather than skillfully evoked and deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is minimal and the counselor pivots effectively toward solutions when it arises (10-minute dance backup, 'no failure,' 'either one counts'). The fear of failure itself is largely bypassed rather than explicitly reframed or processed.
