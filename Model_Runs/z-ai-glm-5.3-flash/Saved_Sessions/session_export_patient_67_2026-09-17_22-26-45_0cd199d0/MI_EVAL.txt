# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor conveyed warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, honoring Kenji's skepticism and limited commitment ('That's all you need to commit to right now — one night, your terms') without any subtle pressure. No labeling, disapproval, or pushing was present at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were consistently accurate and emotionally nuanced, e.g., the double-sided reflection of ambivalence about drinking after work versus the form numbers. The counselor also mirrored Kenji's own language ('good-weird,' 'knew it, quit'), demonstrating deep listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained unwavering focus on reducing alcohol use while letting the path emerge from the client, building progressively from vision to trial plan to obstacle-solving to maintenance. No topic drift occurred; even Kenji's Friday concern was appropriately deferred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session functioned as genuine joint inquiry, with the counselor never prescribing solutions and consistently handing the problem back to Kenji. Every element of the plan (soda water, snacks, sticky note, brother) originated from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kenji's ownership and choice were repeatedly and explicitly affirmed, including validating his self-imposed one-night limit rather than pressing for more. No advice was imposed, and all strategies came from Kenji himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within rather than supplied, with questions eliciting Kenji's own reasons, ritual insight, and culminating identity-level change talk ('maybe I'm the kind of person who can actually do this'). This is textbook evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Kenji as the expert, asking for his assessment at nearly every turn and affirming that the plan was 'built entirely yourself.' Kenji's contributions substantially shaped the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked ('what would a successful first test night look like?'), amplified, and deepened into self-perception language. Each change statement was reflected back and strengthened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally acknowledged but not reinforced, with double-sided reflections embedding ambivalence within change framing. Minor deduction for occasionally echoing ambivalent language ('the quiet without it feels off') without immediately pivoting, though this was mostly strategic.
