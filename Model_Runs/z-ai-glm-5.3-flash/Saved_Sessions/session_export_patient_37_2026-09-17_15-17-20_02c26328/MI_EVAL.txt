# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance, validating Elena's fears and affirming her questions without dismissing her internet concerns. Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, lacking deeper individualized positive regard (e.g., explicitly affirming her dedication as a mother).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections of Elena's emotional state (unease, worry about harming her daughter, panic over conflicting claims) helped shift her from guarded to receptive. Some reflections remained partial and could have explored deeper fears of repeating a mistake or the guilt implied in her hesitation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained unwavering focus on the target behavior—vaccine hesitancy for Elena's 15-month-old—from the opening question through a concrete plan. Every exchange advanced the goal with no topic drift, culminating in Elena's successful practice script.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Inclusive language and a co-constructed agenda, including the collaborative role-play and jointly built script, demonstrated shared work. Information delivery was somewhat one-directional, with fact sheets and scripts presented in large blocks rather than fully negotiated, and Elena's own research not explored as a two-way exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was exemplary: asking permission before educating, offering genuine menu options, and presenting MMRV versus separate shots as equally reasonable while letting Elena decide. The counselor never pressured, and Elena's ownership of the decision visibly strengthened.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicited some genuine client-driven change talk, but the session's primary mechanism for shifting views was counselor-provided education rather than evocation. Elena's own reasons, values, and confidence for vaccination were rarely drawn out (e.g., no scaling questions or exploration of her hopes as a parent).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly conveyed that Elena's expertise mattered, elicited her daughter's age and family history to tailor advice, and positioned her as the active agent in role-play. Elena's contributions were acknowledged but not deeply integrated as a source of shared expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Well-timed questions like "What would help you feel more comfortable deciding?" elicited strong change talk, which grew notably over the session ("A week ago I couldn't even say the word vaccine"). Missed opportunities to deepen these statements with evocative follow-ups or explicit commitment language about next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor neither argued with nor reinforced Elena's sustain talk, and occasionally pivoted well, such as responding to her chickenpox minimization with risk comparisons. However, it largely let sustain talk pass and countered with education rather than using double-sided reflections or strategic reframing.
