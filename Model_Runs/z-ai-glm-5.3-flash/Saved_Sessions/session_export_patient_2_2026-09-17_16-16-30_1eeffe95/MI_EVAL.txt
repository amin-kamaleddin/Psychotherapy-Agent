# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout; Aisha noted she got no lecture and her boundary on drinking was honored. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and repetitive, without deep individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured her exhaustion and ambivalence, and her crayon disclosure was acknowledged. Emotionally powerful moments were under-explored rather than met with deeper emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on evening smoking with a concrete resulting plan; drinking deflection was handled without drifting. Sometimes slipped into checklist-style logistics, and the alcohol concern was deferred with no plan to revisit.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan was largely co-constructed (nights, tea swap, hiding spot, rhyme, check-in format). Early rapid option-listing tilted toward counselor-generated solutions rather than drawing from her own wisdom.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced ownership — choices offered, her pace validated, slips framed as acceptable. Aisha's closing comments ('thanks for not pushing me harder') confirm this as the session's strongest dimension.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence scaling and cue questions produced strong change talk, but evocation was inconsistent. Powerful motivations (crayon moment, her son) surfaced spontaneously rather than through skillful, deepening questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Incorporated Aisha's contributions (tea, rhyme, file notation), but frequently offered menus of options before she explored her own ideas. Expertise about change was rarely framed as residing with her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked change talk via doable first steps, confidence scaling, and concrete cues, yielding strong statements ('My son's worth more than these cigarettes'). The most powerful change talk was not deepened in the moment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled neutrally without reinforcement, but was never strategically pivoted. Peer-comparison minimization and her fear that gum means 'admitting it's a real problem' were not explored or reframed with double-sided reflections.
