# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout; validates ambivalence, affirms progress warmly, and reframes past failures as learning rather than judging them.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that captures nuanced meaning and emotional undercurrents, often mirroring the client's own words and metaphors back to him.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Steady focus on physical activity from start to finish, with each question progressively advancing toward action; no topic drift, every reflection pivots back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine two-way exchange where every turn blends reflection with open questions and the client's answers demonstrably shape the session's direction; positions the conversation as joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice repeatedly affirmed and all plan elements (walking, dancing, fridge note) are client-generated; the counselor never prescribes and frames commitments as self-made promises.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn entirely from within the client—questions elicit his own values and reasons, and he arrives at hope and self-efficacy unprompted, the hallmark of strong evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise consistently located in the client, who is explicitly named as the expert on his own life; every plan element originates from Marcus, not the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk at every turn, with the client's language escalating from 'maybe I can take a short walk' to 'I can do this'—direct evidence of effective cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely avoids dwelling on barriers and redirects toward what is workable, but the exchange probing the failure narrative kept attention on relapse fear for two consecutive turns; a faster pivot toward existing change talk would be ideal.
