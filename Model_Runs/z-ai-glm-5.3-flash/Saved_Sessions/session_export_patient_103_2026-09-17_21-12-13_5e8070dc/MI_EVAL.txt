# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard maintained throughout, even at Nabil's most hostile. His anger and ambivalence were consistently affirmed rather than criticized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that captured key emotions, including the fear of exploding before thinking. Fell short of a 5 by not explicitly reflecting the dignity wound and shame Nabil described, moving quickly toward solutions instead.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on anger in medical settings with well-sequenced progression from triggers to a concrete Thursday plan. However, the initial venting about job consequences was ignored rather than briefly acknowledged and redirected.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every substantive element of the plan originated from Nabil, with the counselor building on his responses rather than steering. The session felt like joint problem-solving between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was explicitly framed as Nabil's choice ('yours to try or not', 'your rules'), with no persuasive pressure or advice. Model example of supporting client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from Nabil himself — his own reasons, cost-benefit analysis, and definition of success. His own words trace the arc from resistance to self-generated commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was repeatedly located in Nabil, and all plan ideas came from him. The counselor offered only questions and reflections, with no advice at any point.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding evocation and development of change talk, from the exception question surfacing the daughter story to a concrete plan ('I'll change the wallpaper tonight'). Nabil's trajectory from 'I don't know' to specific commitment is excellent evidence of skill.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubts were generally met with validation paired with reflective pivots toward Nabil's own change language ('And yet I hear you saying: I won't know sat here'). Sustain talk was occasionally reflected fully before pivoting, and the queue-spot worry went unreframed.
