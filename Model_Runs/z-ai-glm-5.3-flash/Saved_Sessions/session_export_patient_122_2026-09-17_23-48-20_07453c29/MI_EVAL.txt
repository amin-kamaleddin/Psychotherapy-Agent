# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirmed the patient's worth ('wherever you are right now makes sense') and honored autonomy by presenting the plan as 'nothing exposing, nothing that can be failed.' The patient's counter-change talk ('I've said doable before') was received with empathy rather than confrontation, keeping the relational base intact.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician demonstrated deep empathic understanding throughout, e.g., naming the 'loop of flatness, easy snacks, guilt' and the loneliness 'underneath it, I don't have friends here yet.' Reflections consistently captured both surface content and underlying emotional meaning (e.g., the ache of eating alone versus food as an event back home).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician kept a clear therapeutic direction toward sustainable behavior change while consistently evoking the patient's own solutions. Strategic questions ('what would make it feel safe enough to actually try?') and timely summaries expertly channeled change talk into a concrete, self-authored commitment plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as the expert on her own life ('as the expert on your own days here,' 'you know your mornings better than anyone'), and every element of the plan originated from the patient. The clinician explicitly attributed ownership of the plan to the patient ('That's you designing this around who you actually are'), embodying a genuine partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported by eliciting the patient's own timing, contingencies, and safety conditions rather than prescribing them. The clinician reinforced self-directed reframes ('that's you knowing,' 'the plan is yours, built on you'), and explicitly removed external pressure ('there's no test in this room either').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently evoked the patient's own motivations and resources: what made routines stick back home, what would make the plan feel safe, which idea felt most like 'living.' This culminated in the patient articulating her own coping mantra ('it's just a day, not a test I can fail') and behavior plan without any clinician-generated prescriptions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session structure unfolded as collaborative exploration, with the clinician following the patient's lead (market vs. sister call vs. walking group) and inviting her to prioritize ('which one feels most like it would make the week feel more like living?'). The patient's closing statement — 'It's mine' and gratitude for 'not giving me a diet' — confirms the partnership was experienced by the patient herself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully strengthened change talk by asking 'what do you think it would take for one of those tomorrows to feel possible?' and reflecting preparatory language back in organized summaries. Commitment language was deepened at closing by inviting the patient to choose a carry-forward line, converting motivation into durable self-reminders.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (all-or-nothing failure, 'I'm not a morning person,' fear of repeating past patterns) was met with acceptance and reframed alongside change talk using double-sided reflections ('Both of those truths can sit side by side'). The clinician validated the guilt without amplifying it, and the patient's own reframe ('if I miss a day, it's not failure, it's just a day') emerged from the clinician's non-judgmental stance.
