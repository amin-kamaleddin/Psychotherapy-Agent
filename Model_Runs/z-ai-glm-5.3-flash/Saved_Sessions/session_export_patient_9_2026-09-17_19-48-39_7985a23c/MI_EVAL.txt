# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, with affirmations like 'That's real insight, not luck.' No labeling or disapproval even when the patient admits her own temper.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including a complex double-sided reflection capturing her conflict-and-care ambivalence ('You're carrying both the conflict AND the care'). Reflections accurately mirror both her emotional state and her history of avoidance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains clear progressive momentum: from exploring difficulties → two concrete priorities → planning → success markers → a committed first step. Every response advances the focus on improving the co-parenting environment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interaction between equal partners, with curious, permission-seeking questions and a plan co-constructed from the patient's own ideas. The writing task, two priorities, and coffee shop idea all originate from her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and self-direction repeatedly affirmed ('in your own way and on your own timeline'); no advice imposed. The pace—'not the big scary conversation yet'—is set by the client and honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within the client via questions eliciting her values, resources, and self-worth ('what would make you feel proud of yourself?'). No counselor-supplied reasons for change appear.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located with the client ('You know your son and this situation better than anyone'), and her answers directly shape the session direction. The entire plan originates from her rather than counselor-imposed advice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk, highlighting 'for my son, I know I owe it to him to try' and using elaborating questions. Culminates in a concrete client-generated commitment: writing down her two priorities this week.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (doubt, fear of dismissal, history of heated arguments) is acknowledged but never reinforced, instead reframed and redirected. The worry is used productively by asking 'what would tell you it's working?' to pivot doubt toward hope.
