# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental, respectful stance maintained throughout with no labeling despite the client's dismissiveness. However, warmth feels somewhat formulaic through repeated menu offers, and explicit affirmations of the client are sparse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflections tracked both content and feeling well (e.g., 'three hours for two seconds,' 'shrugging because it gets you the same result'). Missed opportunities to deepen emotionally significant disclosures like the mother situation and the client's loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays loosely anchored to appointment fatigue and related stressors, and appropriately honors the client's request to just listen. However, there is no consolidated focus or summary, and the session drifts without moving toward a specific target or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids an expert stance and asks permission, but the interaction resembles a service menu rather than joint problem-solving. When the client rejected the template idea, there was no exploration of what might work instead, keeping collaboration one-directional.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, explicit autonomy support with graceful acceptance of the client's refusals and no pressure. The main limitation is that autonomy is expressed mainly through repetitive option-listing rather than affirming the client's own capacity to direct change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's own motivations (dislike of lying to his mum, wanting meaningful work) surfaced on their own but were never drawn out, affirmed, or expanded. The session ends without any exploration of hope, values, or readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions the client as in charge, but the near-constant choice menus shifted the burden of structuring the session onto a depleted client ('You keep asking me to pick stuff'). The client shaped pacing but was not leveraged to shape the session's substance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rare favorable signals (not wanting to lie to his mum, wishing appointments felt like help) were not reflected back or developed. No questions explored importance, confidence, or what a better week would look like, and change talk faded after the first third.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never amplified and is met with neutral, validating reflections. However, there are few active pivots toward change or double-sided reflections—when the client says 'I stopped trying,' the counselor simply accommodates rather than gently reframing.
