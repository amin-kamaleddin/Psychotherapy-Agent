# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth throughout, with affirmations like "thanks for being honest" and explicit avoidance of judgment by framing data as "neutral info, not a judgment." Repetitive closing reminders across multiple turns slightly dilute the sense of genuine, individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, notably capturing the "day's done" ritual meaning of drinking and the "gap between self-image and a number" discrepancy. Would be higher with deeper exploration of drinking's emotional function (stress relief, "finally breathing") and reflection of underlying ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol as the target behavior, anchoring deflection back to the flagged survey and quantifying intake at 18 drinks/week. Some turns closed with repetitive logistical reminders rather than forward momentum, and tracking results weren't consistently tied back to Daniel's marathon values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative — sought Daniel's input on agenda, co-developed the tracking plan he modified, and let him set the check-in schedule. Menu-driven structure and repeated directive tips created occasional unevenness in power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent support of Daniel's choice via menu options, "7 days if you prefer," and "only if you want" language, with no pressure or coercion. Would be higher with explicit reinforcement that his decision about long-term drinking is entirely his call, beyond menu-style choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation was treated as residing in the client (Daniel even appreciated not being lectured), but the counselor relied heavily on providing information rather than drawing out Daniel's own motivations. Rich motivational material — the marathon, identity discrepancy, and "day's done" ritual — went unexplored with evocative questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and deferred to Daniel's decisions, with his contributions clearly shaping the session (chose tracking, set the day-3 check-in). Occasionally slipped into advice-giving (craving tips, nonalcoholic beer suggestion) before Daniel expressed need.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged organically ("I'm not about to let anything mess with that," "if the sleep or run scores do come back worse... I'd want to know"), but was not deliberately evoked or deepened. No open questions designed to elicit change talk, no reflecting back his strongest motivation statements, and no exploration of confidence or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was neither reinforced nor argued with, and neutral data framing was appropriate. Softening was passive rather than strategic — no double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with his marathon concerns, with the counselor sometimes simply moving on to logistics.
