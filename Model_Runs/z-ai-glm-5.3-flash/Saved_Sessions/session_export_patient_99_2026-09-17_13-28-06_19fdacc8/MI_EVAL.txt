# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, normalizing Matteo's drinking and validating his frustration without criticism. However, affirmations of Matteo's own insights (e.g., recognizing his drinking was more than he thought) were not explicitly affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were accurate reflections (e.g., validating frustration about missing outings), but empathy was shallow overall. The counselor repeatedly pivoted to advice lists rather than exploring Matteo's underlying ambivalence or his key moment of self-reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed well-anchored to the target behaviors—alcohol use in the context of the ankle injury—and each exchange returned to concrete planning for Wednesday. The direction was advice-driven and monitoring-oriented rather than guided toward Matteo's own values and goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some power-sharing (offering options, letting Matteo pick the text), but the session was dominated by unsolicited advice lists with Matteo remaining a passive recipient. Matteo's own ideas were rarely elicited before solutions were prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offered choice ('what would feel most helpful today?'), let Matteo select the text message, and accepted his refusals without pushback. It fell short of a 5 by not explicitly reinforcing Matteo's ownership of decisions during moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor rarely drew motivation from within the client—no open questions explored Matteo's own reasons, values, or confidence for change, and his spontaneous insights were left undeveloped. The approach was primarily advice-giving, a core MI deficit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing occurred (offering choices, inviting input), but long counselor-delivered advice blocks persisted and Matteo pushed back ('I don't need you to message my friends'). An expert tone overshadowed recognition of Matteo's expertise about his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was evoked ('Maybe I could do two beers Wednesday,' 'might not even miss the shots') through practical planning prompts. This occurred largely incidentally, without deliberate evocative questions, elaboration, or importance/confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not reinforce sustain talk and offered light reframes, but led with 'Totally normal — lots of college students drink socially,' which reinforced Matteo's minimization. No double-sided reflections were used to acknowledge sustain talk while steering toward change.
