# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations of Ingrid's responsibility and planning throughout. However, warmth feels somewhat formulaic, and Ingrid's expressed alarm was only briefly acknowledged once rather than deeply explored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are minimal; after the opening summary, the session becomes almost purely informational. Ingrid's alarm, time pressure, and relief were barely acknowledged, with most turns consisting of safety instructions rather than understanding statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Focus stays appropriately on the medication interaction concern, but no broader behavioral target (e.g., safe transition, GP communication) was established. Repetitive closings—the same script offer and warnings repeated verbatim three times—undermined momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration exists in co-creating the message content, but the interaction remains largely expert-to-layperson with the counselor delivering didactic information and setting the option menu. Ingrid's own ideas were never elicited or shaped the agenda beyond the message.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicit autonomy statements ('You're in charge') and no pressure on medical decisions are clear strengths, with next steps framed as Ingrid's choices. However, repeatedly re-offering scripts after Ingrid declined diluted the sense that her 'no' was fully honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is virtually no evocation—the counselor never explored Ingrid's reasons, hopes, or concerns about the medication switch, nor followed up on 'the citalopram hasn't been doing much.' Nearly everything (information, scripts, plans, warnings) was supplied by the counselor rather than drawn from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor quickly shifts into an expert, information-delivery role ('Short answer: they're not safe together'), positioning themselves as the authority. Repeatedly re-offering scripts Ingrid already declined at least four times suggests agenda-driving rather than following her lead.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is essentially no deliberate evocation of change talk; the single open question went unanswered and was never reattempted. Affirming Ingrid's plan lightly reinforced proactive steps, but her motivations, confidence, and reasons for the medication switch were never explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accommodated Ingrid's time pressure without argument and did not reinforce reasons against safety behaviors. However, no skillful reframing or double-sided reflection was used to resolve the tension between her time constraints and engagement—repeated follow-up offers after multiple declines replaced genuine resolution.
