# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with affirmations like 'smart safeguard' and validation of keeping what works. Warmth was somewhat brief and solution-oriented rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured the client's ambivalence and practical concerns. Reflections were sometimes brief or replaced by information lists, with deeper emotional content only lightly acknowledged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus on the target behavior and moved purposefully through exploration, evidence review, and a concrete two-week experiment with tracking and a booked follow-up. Strong directional work with a specific, time-limited action step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative with options offered rather than prescribed, and the plan was co-constructed around the client's preferences. However, collaborative moments often followed psychiatrist-generated menus rather than open co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently framed everything as the client's choice, explicitly honoring his autonomy in designing the slip allowance and check-in time. No coercive or persuasive language appeared.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early open questions drew out the client's own motivations, and he drew his own conclusions from the evidence review. Much of the session, however, featured psychoeducational content and counselor-supplied tactics rather than continually eliciting the client's own reasons and solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and invited client input, but much of the session featured expert content — pros/cons lists, evidence summaries, and pre-packaged coping strategies. Few solutions originated from the client himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited meaningful change talk, including the client's own pivot that the evening cup 'doesn't sound too bad actually' and commitment to an honest two-week attempt. Change talk was rarely deepened with follow-up reflections or additional importance/confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk, but did relatively little to soften it either. Statements like 'I'm still not sold this is a real problem' and hoping sleep won't improve were sidestepped and moved to logistics rather than addressed with double-sided reflections.
