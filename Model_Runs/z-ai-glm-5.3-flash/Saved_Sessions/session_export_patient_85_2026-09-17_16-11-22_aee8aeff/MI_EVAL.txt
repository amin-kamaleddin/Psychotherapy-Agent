# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently respectful, warm, and supportive stance with affirmations of Marco's effort and progress, and no judgmental language even around potential slips. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and tied mostly to task completion rather than deeper unconditional regard, keeping this from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Makes some accurate reflections early on and acknowledges Marco's realistic self-assessment of high-risk moments. Most turns pivot quickly to planning questions, with reflections largely summarizing content rather than exploring Marco's emotional experience of discouragement, pride, or hope.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains an exceptionally tight focus on reducing fruit juice from first turn to last, with every question advancing the action plan. No topic drift, and the session progresses logically to a fully specified plan with contingencies.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tone is consistently that of two equals problem-solving together, with Marco's input genuinely shaping the plan at every step. Held at 4 because the counselor still supplies most options and questions are counselor-generated rather than client-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Explicitly offers choices and frames check-ins as offers, but pacing is notably controlling with rapid-fire multi-part questions and scripted phrasing for the conversation with his wife. Autonomy is acknowledged but the counselor drives every decision, leaving little room for Marco to set his own agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some client reasoning around logistics and implementation, such as why the guest bottle is a trap. Deeper values, reasons, and desires for change go unexplored, as the counselor assumes motivation is present and jumps to planning rather than expanding it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Explicitly treats Marco's choices as his own and incorporates his contributions into the plan. However, frequently supplies the options himself, implying the counselor is the plan architect with Marco as implementer rather than a genuine belief that expertise resides in the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco generated abundant change talk ('A 10', 'I'm committed to this') and the counselor asked about preferences, confidence, and next steps. Commitment statements were rarely reflected back or amplified, and key opportunities to deepen motivation were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marco's mild sustain talk ('I love that OJ', 'I might cave') was not amplified or dwelled upon, and cravings were acknowledged as realistic. No double-sided reflections were used to pivot sustain talk toward change, resulting in neutral steering around it via logistics.
