# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician opened by disarming anticipated judgment ('I'm not here to judge or pile on') and maintained consistent warmth and unconditional regard throughout. Affirmations were specific and genuine, with no labeling or conditional regard anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and deep, articulating the client's core ambivalence with emotional precision. The client's repeated confirmations ('it has been weighing on me more than I let on') demonstrated that reflections reached beneath surface talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on reducing alcohol use and built progressive momentum from exploration through strategy, contingency, and relapse planning. Questions consistently guided without drifting while remaining client-directed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully collaborative between equals, with the check-in arrangement co-constructed through genuine negotiation ('Deal?'). The plan, support person, and follow-up structure all originated from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No directives or advice were given; all strategies were client-generated, and explicit ownership statements reinforced agency ('it's your choice, not something being forced on you'). The closing offered genuine choice about follow-up.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key motivational element—reasons, solutions, confidence, vision—was drawn out from the client rather than provided, exemplified by 'what changed for you between I don't know if that's possible and now?' The client's closing ('not just saying what you wanna hear') reflects genuinely internalized motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly located expertise and ownership within the client ('That's yours, not mine to hand you'), and every concrete plan element came from the client. The clinician's contributions were limited to mirrors and questions—exemplary power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions systematically evoked client-generated change language, and the client's arc from 'I don't know if that's possible' to 'I'll text him... probably tomorrow or Friday' provides direct evidence of cultivated commitment language. Each emergence of change talk was reinforced with affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections generally honored sustain talk while pivoting toward change, and fear-of-judgment concerns were acknowledged without amplification. The score reflects early engagement with barrier/fear exploration in some depth—while ultimately serving the plan, it slightly extended sustain talk exploration beyond crisp pivots.
