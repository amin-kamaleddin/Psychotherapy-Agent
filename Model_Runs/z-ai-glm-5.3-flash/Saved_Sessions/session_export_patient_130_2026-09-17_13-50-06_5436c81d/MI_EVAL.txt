# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect maintained and never judgmental, but warmth is formulaic with minimal affirmation of strengths and impersonal safety boilerplate.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections about the marital tension, but question batteries and checklists dominated, and key emotional themes (guest room, face-saving) went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on the target behavior with a concrete behavioral experiment and little drift, though direction was premature and agenda-imposed before ambivalence resolved.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered options and invited edits to scripts, but the structure remained counselor-led with limited two-way problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Framed the cutback as a test and accepted Viktor's boundaries, but frequently dictated the plan and repeated risk advice despite pushback, edging toward controlling.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied by the counselor rather than drawn from Viktor; his own reasons emerged spontaneously but were never elicited or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission once, but the session was expert-driven with the counselor supplying the risk assessment, plan, and scripts while rarely seeking Viktor's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Viktor generated meaningful change talk and the counselor built on it with the cutback plan, but largely supplied the steps himself rather than deepening Viktor's own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced, but it was met with advice and risk warnings rather than double-sided reflections, which fueled defensive rationalization.
