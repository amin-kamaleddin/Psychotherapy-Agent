# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a calm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone despite the client's hostile opening. Validation was somewhat formulaic and repetitive, lacking individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made adequate reflective attempts acknowledging frustration and family impact. Reflections were shallow and infrequent; missed opportunities to explore the emotional meaning of his disclosures about his wife, kids, and shame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kept the session loosely connected to the appointment-delay/anger concern but direction was driven by solution-pushing rather than a shared target. Focus only crystallized late, largely because the client chose the action himself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partially collaborative — invited preference and adjusted ('Got it — short and usable'), but the counselor supplied most content while the client resisted. Power balance tilted toward the counselor as expert provider of fixes.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered A/B/C/D menus, which superficially supported choice but felt pressured, with nearly identical menus re-presented after each refusal. Little explicit affirmation of the client's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made little effort to elicit the client's own motivations; the most meaningful change-relevant material (family impact, regret) was volunteered, not drawn out. The counselor did not explore, reflect, or build on these disclosures.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Frequently assumed the expert role, presenting pre-packaged scripts, drafts, and plans rather than drawing out the client's expertise. The client directly named this dynamic: 'everyone just hands me a menu of things I'm supposed to do.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely evoked change talk; most change language emerged only after repeated pushing of concrete options, with no strategic evocative questions. Nascent change statements were never reflected or amplified to strengthen them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and occasionally pivoted to action, but repeated menu-listing kept focus on external system change rather than resolving ambivalence about his own anger. No double-sided reflections linking sustain talk to change.
