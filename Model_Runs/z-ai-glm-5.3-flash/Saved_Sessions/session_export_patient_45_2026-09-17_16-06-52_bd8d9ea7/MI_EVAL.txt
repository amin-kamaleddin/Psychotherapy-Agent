# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and explicitly countered Erik's expectation of a lecture ('We don't have to do anything you're not ready for'). Affirmations were sparse and somewhat formulaic, with limited validation of Erik's frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captured Erik's resentment, skepticism, and ambivalence ('That sounds frustrating and heavy'). Reflections were occasionally brief and could dig deeper into the emotional weight of the diagnosis.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained tight focus on the target behaviors, with every response tying back to a concrete step. Guided consistently toward specific, measurable action (skip one beer on Tue/Wed/Thu, phone tracking, follow-up review).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used inclusive language and menu-based choices at every juncture, co-constructing a realistic plan. Several turns were advice-dense in an expert-like manner, tempering the sense of equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language throughout ('you can choose what feels right,' 'no-pressure week-long test,' 'not all-or-nothing'). Slightly reduced by fairly directive advice on specific foods and drinks rather than eliciting Erik's ideas first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partially evoked motivation via the confidence scale and planning questions, but much of the rationale for change came from the counselor rather than from Erik. No exploration of Erik's own reasons, values, or long-term goals.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently asked permission and offered menus, positioning the plan as Erik's choice. When Erik asked for direction, the counselor gave advice-heavy answers rather than drawing out Erik's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully channeled Erik's request into concrete choices, used a confidence ruler, and elicited planning language ('Tuesday, water, phone notes'). Could be stronger by exploring Erik's own 'why' rather than supplying the reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, but responded to 'I feel fine' with lengthy didactic information on alcohol risks rather than double-sided reflections or developing discrepancy. Redirection toward change was present but not consistent or strategic.
