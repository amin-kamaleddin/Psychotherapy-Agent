# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and warm with good affirmations of David's determination and plan, plus non-judgmental safety screening. Kept from a 5 by brief, formulaic warmth and lack of individualized affirmations or normalization of past failed attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections but empathy stayed surface-level, with checklist-style questioning dominating. Missed opportunities to reflect David's relapse shame, work stress, and fear about the mood side-effect warning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, sustained focus on smoking cessation throughout: history, medication, quit date, trigger coping, safety monitoring, and a concrete follow-up plan. No topic drift occurred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and incorporated David's input (Mike as accountability partner, phone follow-up), but the overall flow was counselor-led with recommendations and a prescriber note delivered rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and asked permission, but also directly advised ('I'd pick varenicline first') and heavily directed the plan with compliance-seeking confirmations. David's choice was respected but not actively fostered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite David's strong motivation, the counselor made limited effort to draw it out beyond importance/confidence rulers and quit-date setting. Reasons for quitting, values, and the meaning of a smoke-free life went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role, answering 'which one would you pick?' directly and prescribing the regimen, quit logistics, and prescriber note. David's own expertise about prior attempts was minimally elicited.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used importance/confidence rulers and elicited a quit date, but did not deepen David's already-strong change talk — no exploration of why now, elaboration on his commitment, or follow-up on why confidence was only a 6.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively reframed pre-quitting medication as 'smart,' softening David's 'cheating' worry, and did not amplify sustain talk. However, no double-sided reflections were used and ambivalence was often skipped past rather than strategically redirected.
