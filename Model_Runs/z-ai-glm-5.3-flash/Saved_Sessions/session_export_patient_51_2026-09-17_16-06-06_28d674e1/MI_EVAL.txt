# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accommodated Ingrid's time constraints without pushback, with sensitive handling of the safety screen. Warmth was somewhat formulaic, relying on repeated generic affirmations like 'sensible' and 'good plan.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections showed empathic awareness, but Ingrid's significant disclosure ('my head's not in a great place lately') was met with a pivot to screening and logistics rather than exploration. The stress of juggling work, home, and health was largely left unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept clear focus on the medication safety issue and tied the conversation to the concrete next step of calling the prescriber, with an appropriate safety screen. The session drifted into scheduling minutiae late on, and the target behavior remained narrow.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Logistics like scheduling and reminder details were genuinely negotiated, but the core clinical content was delivered in a one-directional, expert-driven manner. The initial collaborative exploration attempt was dropped after Ingrid declined, leaving the session more consultation-with-advice than joint exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered options, asked permission, and deferred to the prescriber's plan with no pressuring language. Autonomy support felt somewhat mechanical (menu-picking), and repeated safety reminders at the end bordered on directive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely drew out Ingrid's own motivations, confidence, or values—her commitment language emerged spontaneously but was never elicited or expanded. The session relied heavily on information-giving, and the self-care offer remained vague and undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Shared power on scheduling and preferences, positioning Ingrid as decision-maker on logistics. However, clinical content was delivered in an expert-driven way without inviting her own ideas, so her input shaped logistics but not the session's substance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerged spontaneously ('I'll phone them after my appointment,' 'I want the medication sorted properly') but was never reinforced, deepened, or reflected. The session was dominated by psychoeducation and closed, logistical questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce Ingrid's mild time-related resistance and appropriately accommodated her constraints. However, there was little strategic pivoting toward change or resolution of ambivalence—mostly simple compliance with the brief format.
