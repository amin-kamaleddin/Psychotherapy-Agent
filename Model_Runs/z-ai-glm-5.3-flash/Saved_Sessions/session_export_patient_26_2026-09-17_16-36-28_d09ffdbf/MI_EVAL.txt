# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth with no judgment about her smoking, drinking, or parenting, and several affirmations of her resourcefulness. Affirmations felt somewhat formulaic, and her struggles with cutbacks were not deeply normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections of her stress, but reflective listening stayed shallow while the counselor jumped to problem-solving. Key emotional disclosures — difficulty asking for help, distrust of family, nighttime stress driving drinking — were acknowledged briefly but not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on concrete targets (childcare, work backup, evening routines) with logical progression and a strong closing summary. Slightly weakened by option menus that occasionally scattered focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Text messages and reminder phrases were genuinely co-created with Daniela's input. However, the session largely followed an advisor-client pattern, with the counselor supplying option lists that Daniela selected from rather than equal joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices rather than directives, and respected Daniela's decisions without pressure. The volume of pre-generated suggestions occasionally felt persuasive, and her ownership of the change process was not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening question and 'most doable' queries drew out Daniela's preferences and readiness well. Much of the session involved supplying solutions rather than evoking her own reasons and motivations, which emerged spontaneously but undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine partnership moments occurred when Daniela's wording shaped the drafts and plans. But the counselor frequently adopted an expert stance with pre-packaged options and step-by-step instructions, making power-sharing inconsistent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong opening question elicited meaningful change talk, and repeated 'most doable' queries invited commitment to concrete steps. However, change talk was not deepened — no evocative questions about why change matters, and statements like 'it actually feels doable. For once' went unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (daycare costs, 'dead end' paperwork, fear of self-sabotage) was not reinforced, which is positive. However, no double-sided reflections or reframes were used to actively soften it — the approach was neutral redirection.
