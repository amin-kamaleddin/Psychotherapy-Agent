# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated warmth and unconditional positive regard, reframing 'nothing sticks' into evidence of competence and validating Marco's follow-through. No judgment, labeling, or disapproval appeared at any point, even when Marco anticipated failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong, accurately capturing ambivalence and deeper meaning such as protecting time with his children and the worry about his father's diabetes. However, emotional reflections around Marco's fear of repeating his father's trajectory and his 'lazy one' self-label could have been fuller before pivoting to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on dietary consistency at home and moved purposefully from barriers to strategies to commitment and follow-up planning. The closing summary and scheduled follow-up advanced the arc without ever feeling pushy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuine co-construction, with the plan, success criteria, and accountability structure all authored by Marco. The psychiatrist invited input before summarizing and negotiated the follow-up format with full power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively reinforced throughout ('You get to define what success counts as here'), with options offered rather than directives. Marco's closing comment ('Thanks for not just telling me what to do') confirms autonomy support was experienced, not just intended.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited from within, with the question about what success would mean producing Marco's deepest change talk about his father and his boys. Every motivational statement came from Marco, and his spontaneous 'this felt different' confirms intrinsic motivation was surfaced.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located in the client, with every strategy originating from Marco and no advice given anywhere in the transcript. The psychiatrist explicitly named his contributions as 'your own expertise showing up.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions and future-oriented prompts skillfully evoked client-generated solutions and escalating commitment language across the session. The psychiatrist deliberately highlighted Marco's confidence shift from six to six and a half as self-generated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced and was generally reframed effectively, converting tiredness into a planning opportunity and letting Marco define that a slip isn't failure. A slight deduction because some sustain talk (e.g., about his wife buying snacks) was engaged at length before redirection.
