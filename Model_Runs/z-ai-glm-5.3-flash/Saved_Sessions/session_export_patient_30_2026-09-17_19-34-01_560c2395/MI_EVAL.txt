# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and non-judgment, with skepticism met respectfully rather than corrected. However, an early pivot to building a proposal moved past David's suspicion slightly prematurely, and closing affirmations verge on flattery.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including a skilled double-sided reflection capturing ambivalence. Falls short of 5 as emotional undercurrents (fear of decline, irritation at lecturing) are never explicitly reflected back, with some turns relying on affirmation rather than deepening exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on cholesterol management progressing logically from skepticism to concrete next steps. However, direction relies almost entirely on the client's momentum, with few interviewer summaries or signposts, and the medication target is left unaddressed in a forward-leaning way.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed with every substantive element coming from David, and the interviewer checks in rather than directs. David's own surprise at having 'that much to say' evidences a genuine exchange between equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly and repeatedly reinforced, with choice framed at every decision point and the pill decision's deferral actively legitimized. David directly confirms the interviewer 'didn't push,' demonstrating that power-sharing was felt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is systematically drawn from David — values, plan, accountability, and definitions of success are all his own, with no reasons supplied by the interviewer. His movement from passive compliance to spontaneous commitment demonstrates genuine elicitation rather than imposed change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located in David, whose ideas demonstrably shape the entire plan. His closing reflection — 'you didn't push, you just made me talk it out' — is direct client evidence of felt power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook-quality evocation through open questions that draw out David's reasons, plans, and self-generated commitment. The interviewer strategically uses David's own doubts as springboards and amplifies 'mostly' as a sustainable vision of success.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated without reinforcement and deprioritized appropriately, including leaving the pill question deliberately open. Falls short of 5 as the interviewer re-voices sustain talk more often than necessary and makes little use of intentional reframing to diminish side-effect fears.
