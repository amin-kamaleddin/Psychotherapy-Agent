# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'I'm proud of you for starting this.' However, affirmations were somewhat generic and formulaic, lacking personalized reflections of Amira's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal; responses typically jumped to advice or checklists rather than reflecting Amira's feelings. Key vulnerable moments, like the salsa jar incident, received prescriptions instead of emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on increasing physical activity matching Amira's stated priority, with concrete planning (Mon/Wed/Fri walks, defined routine, tracking) and deferral of nutrition to a later checkpoint.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaboration existed (asking about limits and what feels doable), but the interaction was largely expert-driven with the clinician prescribing a full program while Amira reacted rather than co-authored the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The clinician asked what felt most doable and honored Amira's decision to delay nutrition changes, but dominated the session with prescriptive advice and scripts, with limited menu-of-options or exploration of her own ideas first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and ideas were largely supplied by the clinician rather than drawn out of the client. There were almost no open-ended questions eliciting Amira's own solutions, reasons, or strategies despite her demonstrated self-awareness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician frequently assumed the expert role, dispensing detailed prescriptions rather than treating Amira as the expert on her own life. Her contributions were minimally incorporated before returning to directives.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician made almost no effort to evoke or deepen change talk. Amira volunteered rich motivation ('I want to feel stronger,' 8/10 readiness) that went unreflected and undeepened, with no scaling follow-up or commitment elicitation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Amira's worry about avoiding nutrition was affirmatively reframed as strategy rather than avoidance, effectively softening sustain talk. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections, but hesitations were neither reinforced nor dwelt upon.
