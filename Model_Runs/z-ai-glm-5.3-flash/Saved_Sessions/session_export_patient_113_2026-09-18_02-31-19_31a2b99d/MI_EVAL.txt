# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental—'No promises needed; you get to set the pace here' and normalizing language never scold or label the client. Slightly less explicit validation of the client's emotional experiences (fatigue, shame) kept it from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, frequent reflections captured the client's fatigue, all-or-nothing pattern, and ambivalence with effective double-sided reflections. Some reflections repeated the client's exact words rather than voicing deeper emotional meaning (e.g., the shame behind 'dropping the ball').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on charging/adherence, moving steadily from ambivalence to plan, confidence, relapse contingency, and generalization. Slight deduction since the client's volunteered medication-refill topic went unaddressed, leaving the expanded agenda handling unverified.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were co-constructed from the client's own observations (Sunday dead time, coffee machine reminders) with the client positioned as expert. Mild deduction because the clinician's agenda is still visibly steering, though transparently.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength: 'you get to decide,' acceptance of the client's pacing, and explicit attribution of all ideas to the client reinforce ownership throughout. Only a touch more explicit emphasis on the client's freedom to choose otherwise would make it perfect.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client—no advice given, all ideas originated with him, and affirmations attributed insight to him. Missing 'why a 5 and not a 3?' scale-why follow-up kept it from excellence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Power sharing was genuine, not rhetorical—the session's direction visibly bent to the client's contributions ('that's your expertise showing'). Deduction because the clinician still framed the questions and set the general agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation via open questions, reflection of change talk, a confidence ruler, and future-oriented questioning, producing a clear DARN-to-commitment trajectory. Commitment language was not deepened further (e.g., direct intention questions) before the session ended.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (busyness, forgetfulness, fear of quitting after one slip) was acknowledged with double-sided reflections and pivoted toward change without reinforcement. Occasionally sustain talk was reflected verbatim without an immediate pivot or affirmation, giving it slightly more airtime than needed.
