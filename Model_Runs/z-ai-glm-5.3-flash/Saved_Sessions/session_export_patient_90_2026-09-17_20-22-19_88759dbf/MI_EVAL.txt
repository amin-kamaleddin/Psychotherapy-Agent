# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard with specific, genuine affirmations. Never criticizes or pressures Marcus, directly addressing his fear of judgment about the dog ('I'm not here to take him away').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening throughout, capturing both the surface content and emotional subtext. Complex reflections like 'I hear two things: your love for him, and your fear that fixing your health means losing him' precisely capture Marcus's ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on asthma management while skillfully tying drift topics back to the target behavior. Session moves logically from exploration to a specific, consolidated plan (Thursday insurance call, wife's involvement, bedroom changes).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving with permission-seeking framing and repeated solicitation of Marcus's input. Marcus's own ideas determine the plan, with the counselor structuring and amplifying rather than dictating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Marcus's ownership, validating 'doing this my way' and offering choices without pressure. Marcus retains full control over what, when, and how of his plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions consistently draw motivation from within Marcus, surfacing his wife and dog as internal motivators and his deepest values. All reasons for change are articulated by Marcus himself, never argued by the counselor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within Marcus ('You know her best'), and the plan is entirely self-generated. His own contributions substantially shaped the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk through strategic questions and explicit highlighting of Marcus's change language. Culminates in Marcus's own realization 'maybe I've got more riding on this than I thought.'

#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently pivots away from sustain talk without dismissing it, reframing avoidance as self-knowledge. The double-sided reflection on the dog transformed all-or-nothing sustain talk into concrete willingness ('keeping him out of the bedroom, I could live with that').
