# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed the patient's autonomy ('that's yours to try, at your pace') and normalized her attachment to smoking without judgment. Support for her self-direction was explicit throughout, including welcoming her resistance to promises.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections repeatedly captured both sides of her ambivalence and the deeper meaning of smoking as her only personal time. The clinician responded precisely to emotional content, including her discomfort about her son's questions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation moved coherently from exploring what smoking provides, to identifying alternative behaviors, to concrete next steps (car, then honesty with her son). Each transition followed the patient's own change talk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician used evocative questions ('what do you think could give you that same pocket of calm?') and let the patient generate her own solutions. The patient explicitly noted the difference from typical lecturing doctors.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient was repeatedly given permission to proceed at her own pace with no promises required ('you don't have to promise anything more than what you just gave'). She even stated that pressure would have made her dig her heels in.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions drew out the patient's own reasons for change (her son watching her, wanting to be honest with him) rather than imposing external arguments. The 'five minutes of quiet' reframe came from the patient's own elaboration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as the expert on her own life ('you know your days better than anyone') and co-constructed the plan. Decisions were framed as hers to make and test.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician selectively reinforced commitment language ('once, this week') and prepatory talk ('maybe that's my step—the car, and then honesty') without pushing for more. Commitment emerged naturally and was consolidated at each step.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I still don't see how I'd get through the day without it') was met with reflective holding and double-sided reflections rather than confrontation or reassurance. This allowed ambivalence to resolve into self-generated change.
