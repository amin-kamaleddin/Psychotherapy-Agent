# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, even at peak defensiveness ("I'm gonna do what I want, it's my body"). No judgment or labeling. Warm, individualized affirmations ("Listen to you—'that's their problem, not mine'"; "You're going to be a good mom, Isabella"). Notably, client was affirmed even in resistance—true unconditional regard rather than compliance-tied warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **4**
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening—complex reflection on wine as family identity and double-sided reflection capturing ambivalence ("the tradition you love and the pregnancy") were excellent. Mirrored her core autonomy concern ("no scare tactics, no pressure"). Slight deduction: mid-session reflections became fewer and more affirmation-driven; deeper emotional material (nighttime worry, fear of failing as a parent) was passed over rather than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use in pregnancy from first utterance to last. Each turn advanced the arc: opening reflection → values question → consolidation of self-generated plan → specification of husband conversation → closing summary with concrete, behaviorally specific next steps (brochure, wine rack, partner conversation tonight). No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **4**
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative—open questions, client-authored agenda, fully co-constructed plan, no unsolicited content. Minor deduction: closing phase was somewhat asymmetric (largely affirmations and farewells), and follow-up planning ("maybe I'll check back in") was left vague with no explicit negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Exceptional and tailored to this client's defining value. "You're the one in charge of your decisions here, always"; "if it were fully on your terms"; "no one told you, you chose." Brochure and follow-up handled by permission, not persuasion. Defensiveness about control was converted into the engine of change—client's own closing ("nobody did [tell me what to do]. I talked myself into it") is direct evidence of success.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Motivation clearly located within the client. Rather than supplying reasons for abstinence, the psychiatrist asked, reflected, and affirmed—eliciting her values (healthy baby, being a good parent), her confidence, and her commitment ("my kid, and my choice"). Client's closing "I talked myself into it... which is how it should be" confirms the evocation succeeded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in the client throughout. Every substantive idea (tea, sparkling grape juice, wine rack, family dinner boundaries) originated from Isabella. No educating, correcting of evidence claims, or supplied content—only structure and affirmation. A model demonstration of the client-as-expert stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Skillful and consistent. The pivotal values-based question ("your version of 'doing right'... fully on your terms") directly elicited "doing right would mean I didn't take dumb chances," "I don't actually need it every night," and later commitment language ("we'll sort the wine rack this weekend"). Change talk reinforced via affirmation of agency and consolidated in the closing summary; all plan steps were client-voiced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **4**
**Comments:** Sustain talk abundant and handled well—acknowledged briefly without amplification, then pivoted via double-sided reflection ("So you're clear this has to be your decision... And I also hear that wanting to do right by her is genuinely there"). Never argued with her evidence skepticism, avoiding the righting reflex. Deduction: no explicit reframe of minimizing statements; some sustain talk passed without strategic redirection.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.8/5**

An exemplary MI session, particularly strong in autonomy support and evocation—the two most important domains for this autonomy-driven client. A single well-crafted values question unlocked the client's own change process, and every subsequent turn affirmed her agency while consolidating change talk into a concrete, client-authored plan. Growth areas: deepen reflections into emotional undercurrents and close the loop more explicitly on follow-up planning.
