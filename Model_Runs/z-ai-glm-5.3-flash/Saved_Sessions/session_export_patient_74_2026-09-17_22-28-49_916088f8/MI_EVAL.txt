# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveyed warmth and unconditional positive regard, validating both sides of Linda's ambivalence ("That's real — you enjoy it") and honoring her pace without judgment. Her enjoyment of smoking and stated lack of plans to quit were treated as legitimate rather than resisted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured both poles of ambivalence well, such as the tug-of-war between smoking as a break and as a poor model for her kids. Reflections occasionally stayed at a summary level rather than exploring deeper emotions like fear of failure or shame beneath her self-doubt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on smoking and progressed logically from exploring ambivalence to importance, barriers, a concrete first step, and coping planning. Every utterance advanced toward change, closing with a clear behavioral commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Inclusive, non-authoritarian language was used throughout, and Linda's ideas shaped the session's direction, including her self-generated plan. Agenda-shaping remained subtly counselor-led in places, such as the structured introduction of the readiness ruler.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was reinforced repeatedly ("on your terms and in your own time"), and Linda's preference to keep her husband out of the plan was respected without challenge. She explicitly named this as central: "That it's mine. Nobody's making me do this."

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from the client through open questions and follow-ups, with the counselor never supplying reasons to quit or advice. Linda's own reasons (her daughter, her mother's example) and confidence-building ideas were elicited and deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Linda was explicitly positioned as the expert ("You're the expert on your life"), and the plan was generated entirely by her with the counselor crediting her ownership. Her preference to figure things out alone was honored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked through open questions, a readiness ruler, past-strengths exploration, and future-oriented questions, producing rich client statements like "I want to see if I can do it." The counselor then reinforced it ("listen to what you just said — you might actually do this").

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never amplified, and the counselor consistently pivoted toward change, such as asking what would make quitting feel more possible after Linda said she had no plans to quit. Some reflections restated sustain talk verbatim ("The other 4 points are the enjoyment and the doubt") without strategically softening it.
