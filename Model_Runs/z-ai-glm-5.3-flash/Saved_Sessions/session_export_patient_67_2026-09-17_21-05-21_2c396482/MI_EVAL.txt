# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated unconditional positive regard, normalizing Kenji's defensiveness, thanking him for his honesty, and responding to his 'is that cheating?' question with curiosity rather than correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and deep, with complex reflections capturing meaning (e.g., 'wanting to do this in a way that feels honest to yourself') and emotional nuance, including validating the vulnerability of the dad concern.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on alcohol reduction through purposeful sequencing (awareness, projections, barriers, solutions, plan), with each reflection building toward change and no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully collaborative, with every plan element originating from Kenji and the counselor facilitating rather than prescribing — confirmed by Kenji's 'thanks. For not lecturing me.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership were consistently reinforced ('you set the pace'), and Kenji's reluctance about zero drinking was accepted without pushback, with even setbacks framed as legitimate.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from the client with zero advice-giving, eliciting powerful intrinsic reasons like the father concern ('I'm not him yet') and his own recognition that 'not thinking was the whole problem.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly placed with the client ('You're the expert on your own life'), questions solicited Kenji's own standards and definitions, and his contributions visibly shaped the entire plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and reinforced (year-two projection, 'genuine progress in your own words'), producing a genuine motivational arc from 'never really thought about it' to 'I actually kind of want to try' and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification, addressed with double-sided reflections early on, and reframed into problem-solving (e.g., turning barrier talk into 'what would make it even a little easier') rather than dwelling on obstacles.
