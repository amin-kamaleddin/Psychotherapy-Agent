# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout with no labeling or disapproval. However, warmth is generic and formulaic, with no explicit affirmations of Naomi's courage or honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; responses are dominated by psychoeducation and bulleted advice lists. Vulnerable disclosures about shame are met with one-line sympathy followed immediately by technique instructions, with no complex reflections or reflections of feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on Naomi's social anxiety and moves steadily toward a concrete, agreed behavior change with a follow-up appointment. Direction is achieved through steering and prescribing rather than collaboratively clarifying goals rooted in Naomi's own values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor sets the agenda, supplies the techniques, drafts the message, and prescribes the exposure plan while Naomi's role is largely to consent. Her own ideas never substantively shape the session, with no joint brainstorming or co-construction of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine autonomy supports exist, such as offering choices and normalizing the backup plan. However, prescriptive instruction dominates, and autonomy is respected only at the level of consenting to the counselor's plan rather than Naomi authoring her own change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is treated as something to install via technique rather than elicit; the counselor never asks why change matters or explores why she kept making excuses. Naomi's change talk emerges spontaneously despite, not because of, the counselor's approach.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor operates largely from an expert stance, dispensing prescriptive strategies with nearly every turn. Naomi's contributions are incorporated only superficially, with no acknowledgment that she holds expertise about her own anxiety.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Naomi spontaneously generates meaningful change talk, but the counselor does almost nothing to evoke or deepen it. There are no open evocative questions, elaboration requests, or strategic summaries consolidating her commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does not reinforce sustain talk and offers a genuinely helpful reframe of anxiety as 'useful data, not failure.' However, sustain talk is often met with more instructions rather than double-sided reflections or use of Naomi's own change talk to counter doubt.
