# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout; Liora's skepticism was met with respect and her efforts affirmed. Minor issue: 'I'm really proud you' centers the clinician's feelings, and warmth felt somewhat formulaic given her discouraged presentation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections (quick relief, skepticism), but key emotional openings were missed — especially Liora's disclosure that bodily pain 'matches what's going on inside,' as well as her shame, hopelessness, and surprise at her own engagement. Reflections were brief and the session moved quickly to logistics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Crystal-clear focus on self-harm and safety with no topic drift; every turn tied back to urges, means restriction, coping, or follow-up. The session progressed logically from assessment to a concrete, confirmed plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-built (Liora edited the note, chose Maya, set timing), but the counselor supplied most ideas while Liora's passivity went unaddressed. Her own strategies were rarely elicited before offering menus.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was consistently emphasized ('no pressure,' 'you don't have to use the crisis line'), and Liora could decline options without pushback. Falls short of 5 due to rapid-fire offers with implicit push and no explicit affirmation of her ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One good evocative question yielded music as a coping idea, but evocation was otherwise thin — her reasons for change, hopes, and the meaning of 'see if I even want it back' went unexplored. Motivation mostly resided in the counselor's suggestions rather than her own.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menu-based choices respected her input, and she selected and modified options. However, the interaction had an expert-driven texture: nearly all ideas came from the counselor, with Liora positioned as a selector rather than a source of expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful client-generated commitments emerged (leaving the note before she 'could talk herself out of it,' 'I'll try it tonight'), partly via concrete offer-based questions. But change talk was rarely deepened with open evocative questions or explored for confidence and reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not argued with and received some acknowledgment, but there were no double-sided reflections and limited strategic pivoting. Missed opportunities to work with ambivalence, e.g., blades feeling 'pointless' vs. not wanting to explain to her roommate.
