# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect and nonjudgment maintained, with some affirmation of Wiremu's commitment to being a better dad. However, key moments went unaffirmed — the courage of calling his mum, his remorse, and his identity as a father — and warmth remained minimal and transactional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor accurately reflected the core ambivalence once and showed adequate understanding. However, reflections were sparse and shallow, with emotionally loaded openings (shame, fear of 'going backwards,' inadequate contact with kids) repeatedly skipped in favor of practical planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus throughout on safe contact with children, sober housing, and behavior change. The session progressed logically from exploration to planning to relapse preparation with no topic drift — the counselor's strongest domain.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine two-way exchange occurred, with Wiremu shaping the script in his own words and choosing his own mantra. However, the overall structure was counselor-led, with pre-formulated options and scripts, and Wiremu's own ideas for solutions were not invited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered (e.g., 'Which of these feels most doable?') and no pressure applied on the Kirsty timeline. However, much of the session was directive instruction framed as recommendations rather than possibilities, with little explicit reinforcement that Wiremu owns the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor showed limited interest in drawing out Wiremu's own motivations — his children, remorse, and desire to be 'a father they can look up to' were left unexplored. The approach predominantly supplied content (options, scripts, mantras) rather than evoking the client's reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role, delivering pre-written scripts, mantras, and step-by-step plans rather than co-creating them. Wiremu's contributions shaped things somewhat, but there was no acknowledgment that he knows best what will work for his whānau context.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was frequent, largely client-generated, and some questions elicited commitment ('The trial stay idea... that actually sits okay with me'). However, the counselor never deepened or reflected Wiremu's strongest change talk back or amplified his family-driven motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about freedom and reluctance to live with whānau was not reinforced but was largely sidestepped rather than worked through. No double-sided reflections were used to link his desire for freedom to his family values and resolve the ambivalence.
