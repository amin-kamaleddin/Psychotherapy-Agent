# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations of Marcus's autonomy and efforts, though affirmations are somewhat generic and missed chances to normalize his history of failed attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured his ambivalence and discouragement well, but reflections stayed at brief summaries without deeper exploration of the shame and frustration of repeated failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on physical activity tied to weight and blood pressure, guiding toward a concrete commitment, though the ending felt redundant with repeated plan summaries and closing questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan was genuinely co-constructed with Marcus choosing activities, backup plans, and check-in content, but rapid-fire multiple-question clusters occasionally felt interrogative rather than paced dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Marcus's ownership: permission-seeking, offering choices, tailoring reminders to his preferences, and honoring his self-defined success criteria, resulting in genuinely internalized change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence rulers and win-focused questions drew out intrinsic motivators like the 'lighter feeling,' but evocation was concentrated in the planning phase and values could have been elicited earlier.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus was consistently positioned as the expert with his preferences substantially shaping the plan, though the style was at times checklist-like and his expertise wasn't explicitly affirmed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of confidence rulers and planning questions elicited strong commitment language and excitement, but the counselor sometimes rushed to planning rather than savoring change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled barriers without arguing, pivoting quickly to realistic options and what works, but never used double-sided reflections or reframed past failures as useful data.
