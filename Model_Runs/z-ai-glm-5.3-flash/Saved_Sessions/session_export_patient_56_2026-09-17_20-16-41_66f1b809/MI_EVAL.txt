# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated warm, unconditional positive regard with affirmations like 'That clarity is a strength' and 'that persistence of yours... is already at work.' There was no judgment anywhere, even when the client admitted the phone habit was hard to confess.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured both content and emotional stance, such as the accurate reflection about picturing Sunday and leading with missing them rather than correcting them. One weak point: the counselor echoed the client's first-person words verbatim ('Um, I suppose I'd remind myself...'), which read as an odd mirroring artifact and slightly disrupted the felt sense of understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained an exceptionally clear focus, building progressively from values alignment to shared dinners, a concrete Sunday plan, contingency planning, and defining success. Each response contributed to a coherent arc from exploration to commitment without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor worked as an equal partner, with the client's ideas driving the agenda and summaries serving to reflect her own material back to her. Phrases like 'I'd love to hear how Sunday goes... whatever shape it takes' exemplified non-hierarchical engagement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was consistently reinforced through questions like 'What feels most within reach right now?' and 'whatever shape it takes,' with no prescriptions offered. Ownership of success and setbacks was repeatedly affirmed as belonging to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The entire session drew motivation from within via open questions about alignment, readiness, and success, with the client generating her own reasons for change. The counselor strategically amplified this language, producing strong commitment talk by session's end.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('Since you know your family best,' 'not me handing it to you'), and the plan, definition of success, and key framings all originated with her. The counselor built upon rather than redirected the client's contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation throughout, with questions that elicited and amplified change talk at every turn, escalating from possibility to firm commitment ('Sunday's going to happen'). The counselor deliberately mirrored the client's commitment language back as her own achievement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal given the client's maintenance-stage clarity; when mild doubt appeared (fear the first conversation won't land), the counselor proactively reframed it into resilience planning. The score is 4 rather than 5 simply because few genuine sustain-talk moments allowed demonstration of advanced softening technique.
