# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental regard throughout, with consistent affirmations ('that's a meaningful step,' 'that's a real strength') and normalization of ambivalence. No criticism or labeling anywhere.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, often complex reflections tracking the client's values and patterns. Falls short of excellent because reflections frequently pivot quickly to question strings and the guilt about breaking self-promises was touched but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tight focus on the target behavior from values clarification through to a concrete plan, barriers, backup, accountability, and scheduled follow-up. No drift; every exchange builds momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely built from the client's own ideas (morning classes, Priya, laid-out clothes), with contingent offers of help. Some turns stack three or four questions, and the check-in structure was more counselor-proposed than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong choice support — the client picks the plan, backup options are offered as choices, and commitment language comes from her ('I'm willing'). Occasionally the rapid question strings edge toward steering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from the client rather than supplied, including confidence scaling and outcome questions. Evocation stayed focused on the practical plan; deeper exploration of core values (self-love, growth) was underdeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise is clearly located with the client and her solutions are affirmed and built upon. The agenda, however, is driven heavily by the clinician via stacked questions, making pacing feel structured rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocation of change talk via scaling, 'what was different then,' and willingness questions, yielding rich client commitment ('I'd like to test it'). A key self-awareness moment ('maybe that's part of the problem') was missed rather than deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced and is reframed adaptively ('choosing mornings shows you're adapting to real life'). Handling is somewhat light, however — doubt about past broken self-promises was softened rather than fully processed.
