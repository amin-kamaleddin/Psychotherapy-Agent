# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor opened with 'I'm here to listen, not judge' and maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance despite Milan's defensiveness and minimization. Milan's closing ('less painful than I thought it'd be') reflects feeling genuinely accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured Milan's perspective, including his defensiveness and face-saving concerns. Reflections sometimes summarized rather than deepened the emotional core, e.g., not exploring the social anxiety behind 'it's easier than explaining.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus on the target behavior (weekend alcohol use) was maintained throughout, with each turn tied to concrete change. The session progressed logically from exploration to a specific, actionable plan for Saturday with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely co-constructed, with the final plan combining counselor suggestions and Milan's own ideas (signal, eating first, Luka's role). Several turns presented pre-packaged option lists rather than joint brainstorming, giving a slightly expert-driven flavor mid-session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support with permission-seeking for information, explicit choices, and acceptance of Milan's limits ('not making any big promises,' 'just a trial'). The counselor never pressured toward abstinence, honoring Milan's framing of a one-weekend experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor elicited Milan's own motivations by connecting drinking to his values and asking what would build his confidence. Milan articulated personal reasons (blackouts, protecting grades) and self-generated solutions, but deeper values were touched only briefly and change talk was not amplified further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor positioned Milan as the expert, and Milan's contributions (the Luka signal, eating first, keeping a drink in hand) substantively shaped the final plan. Later portions became somewhat advice-dense, shifting the expertise balance toward the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation with questions like 'what would have to change for you to consider cutting back' led to Milan's own plan and commitment language ('I can probably do this'). A slight deduction because some concrete plan elements were counselor-generated rather than client-evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently acknowledged Milan's sustain talk briefly and pivoted toward change without arguing, accepting 'maybe pacing' rather than pushing abstinence. Some responses reflected the sustain talk back at length ('nothing bad has happened') before redirecting.
