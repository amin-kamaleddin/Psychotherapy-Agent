# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☒4 ☐5
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout. "Not a failure — an experiment" and "8 minutes counts" communicate unconditional regard; no labeling of the patient as lazy or noncompliant. However, warmth is somewhat procedural — reads more as coaching than genuine personal regard, with little individualized affirmation of the patient's courage or insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☒1 ☒2 ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal. After meaningful disclosures ("getting back out feels impossible"; "it's all or nothing with me"), the counselor repeatedly pivots to instruction lists rather than reflecting emotional weight. One decent empathic statement ("That makes sense — feeling tired after work and dreading the return is normal"), but the shame disclosure ("not feeling guilty about it") received no reflection and was redirected to alarm times.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☒4 ☐5
**Comments:** Session remains clearly focused on the target behavior (returning to the gym) throughout, with no topic drift and a concrete next step (bag tonight, Tuesday 6:15, follow-up template). Weakness: direction pushed via pre-set agenda, and momentum lost in final exchanges with the same reminder list repeated three times.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☒1 ☒2 ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Largely counselor-directed; plans, templates, and interpretations offered for ratification rather than co-created. The patient's own good ideas (guilt-free reward; morning timing to avoid end-of-day fatigue) were absorbed into the counselor's template rather than used to shape the session. Tone is closer to advice-giving coach than equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☒3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive moments ("Which of those feels doable?" "Which reward feels right to you?") and the failure-as-data reframe. However, these are overshadowed by heavy prescriptive instruction and multi-item directive lists; the triple repetition of reminders at the end reads as controlling rather than trusting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☒1 ☒2 ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions come almost entirely from the counselor. No open questions exploring the patient's reasons, values, or confidence. Past gym success ("I used to go pretty regularly") was never explored as a source of self-efficacy. Importance/confidence rulers absent. Change talk surfaced despite, not because of, the counselor's approach.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☒1 ☒2 ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Counselor assumes the expert role for most of the session; plans are counselor-generated. Little acknowledgment that the patient — a former regular gym-goer who identified his own pattern and diagnosed his all-or-nothing thinking — holds real expertise about what will work for him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☒1 ☒2 ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Patient spontaneously offered valuable change talk ("exercise matters at my age," "maybe Tuesday," "more than I did last month"), but none was deepened, reflected, or amplified. No evocative questions or importance/confidence ratings; counselor moved immediately to logistics, missing repeated opportunities to strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☒3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk and offers useful reframes ("an experiment, not a test," "8 minutes counts," naming "all-or-nothing thinking"). However, little active work with ambivalence — no double-sided reflections; sustain talk ("plans never stick," "not convinced this sticks") was bypassed by jumping to the next instruction rather than explored and resolved.
