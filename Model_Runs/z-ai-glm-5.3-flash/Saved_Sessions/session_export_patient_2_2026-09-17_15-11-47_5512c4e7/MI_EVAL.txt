# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, with regular affirmations and no confrontation of defensiveness. Affirmations became formulaic and repetitive, and validation of Aisha's identity as a working mother and her fears was limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections demonstrated solid reflective listening and acknowledged her ambivalence. Emotional depth was limited—her fear of cancer, 'my son needs me,' and the evening cigarettes as her 'only quiet moment' were not explored, with reflections pivoting quickly to planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on evening smoking reduction, progressing to a concrete plan without wandering off-task. The focus narrowed excessively into micro-logistics near the end, and the plan was never tied back to her deeper values via summarizing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed from Aisha's own ideas, with agreement sought at each step. The later third became multi-question, checklist-like exchanges that shifted the tone from equal dialogue toward structured coaching.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha's choice was repeatedly emphasized, her own solutions were affirmed over prescriptions, and slips were framed compassionately. The rapid-fire, menu-driven questions created mild pressure, and there was no explicit acknowledgment that she could change at her own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early motivations (fear of cancer, her son) were drawn out and scaling questions elicited her own confidence assessments. Evocation was superficial and front-loaded—her deeper reasons were never explored, and most of the session focused on plan mechanics rather than intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha's input was invited and incorporated into the plan, showing some power sharing. The later coaching/checklist style with tip-heavy menus of options edged toward leading rather than fully fostering her expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, scaling, and reinforcement of her ideas effectively strengthened Aisha's change talk over the session (readiness 5, confidence rising to 7). Change talk was rarely deepened through evocative follow-up about why cutting back mattered, and later exchanges shifted to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not argued with and was occasionally acknowledged ('I hear the habit of reaching is real'). There was little active softening—no double-sided reflections, no reframing of her peer comparison, and the alcohol issue was dropped entirely without exploration.
