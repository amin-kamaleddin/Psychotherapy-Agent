# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and respect with affirmations like 'that takes a lot' and 'that's a meaningful step.' However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic and repetitive, lacking deeper individualization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including an excellent complex reflection of Mateo's ambivalence about easing up versus giving up. Some reflections were brief or skipped in favor of problem-solving, and the emotional weight of his fears wasn't deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior with zero topic drift, moving coherently from pain exploration to a concrete, measurable plan and Friday check-in. Every exchange built momentum toward a specific commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative moments included co-constructing the text message and asking which fallback Mateo preferred. Collaboration was uneven, however, as several turns delivered packaged advice with only token consultation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices ('Would you like...', 'casual or more clinical?') and let Mateo make key decisions. Autonomy was slightly undermined by rapid-fire, pre-packaged suggestions, and Mateo's ownership wasn't explicitly credited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within Mateo using open-ended questions and confidence scaling, generating substantial client change talk. Missed opportunities to deepen change statements, such as exploring what shifted since 'a month ago' rather than moving to logistics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited input but frequently assumed the expert role, supplying the script, fallback lists, and specific logistics rather than drawing strategies from Mateo's experience. Mateo's own expertise on his pain patterns was not explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through the confidence scaling question and barrier planning, drawing out commitments like 'I do want to keep at it.' Evocation was sometimes directive, with the counselor proposing goal frameworks rather than letting Mateo generate options first.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, and delivered a strong reframe of pacing as staying in the game. However, statements like 'a week won't change much' and the closing 'you'll hear about my back' went unaddressed, with limited double-sided reflections.
