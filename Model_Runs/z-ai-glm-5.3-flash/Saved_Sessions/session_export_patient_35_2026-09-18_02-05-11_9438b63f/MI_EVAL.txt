# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently affirmed patient autonomy and expertise ('you know your days, your stress, your son better than anyone'), explicitly avoiding blame and validating ambivalence ('both are true, and both matter'). 

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections repeatedly captured both sides of the patient's ambivalence, including her fear that 'maybe it's your fault' and the value of her ten-minute breaks, conveyed with nonjudgmental warmth. 

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician kept focus on the target of finding doable ways to reduce her son's exposure to smoke while staying anchored to the patient's own goals and pace. 

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan emerged entirely from the patient ('everything in that plan came from you'), with the clinician inviting her to choose strategies and even offering to scale back ('Does stacking all three feel like support, or like pressure?'). 

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Patient's self-generated ideas (cutting to six, tracking, podcast, clean clothes before pickup) were reinforced as her own, and her hesitance about the patch and her sister's involvement were fully respected. 

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions ('what would that actually look like?', 'what would make six feel real?') drew out the patient's own motivations, from the 3am guilt to the desire to 'finish something I start.' 

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conversation flowed as back-and-forth problem solving, with the clinician responding honestly to her question about cutting down and co-designing contingency plans for failure. 

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently highlighted and strengthened change talk ('cut down to six came from you, not me'), and patient moved from 'maybe when things calm down' to a concrete same-night commitment. 

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('not the right time,' 'the after-bedtime one stays') was accepted without argument, which allowed the patient to soften on her own ('maybe the after-bedtime one isn't untouchable forever either').
