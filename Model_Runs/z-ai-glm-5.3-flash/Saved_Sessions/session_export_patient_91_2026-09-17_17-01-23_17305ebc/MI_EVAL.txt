# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout ('glad you're here,' 'Complain away if it stings'), with normalizing statements and genuine, individualized affirmations. No judgment, labeling, or disapproval appears anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening ('getting a list feels like unexpected homework') and an accurate closing summary. Not a 5 because some reflections stayed at brief restatement rather than exploring the emotional meaning of Marcus's fears (quitting after three days, his wife seeing him fail).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior (blood glucose monitoring), moving steadily from worry identification through hands-on practice to a concrete routine and 7-day follow-up. Clear momentum maintained without being pushy despite Marcus's short, hesitant answers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — the first check was done together, problems solved jointly, and every plan element came from Marcus's own routines and preferences. Power remains evenly distributed, and Marcus explicitly valued working together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy repeatedly affirmed: pacing to fit his life, respecting his decision to delay telling his wife, and asking permission before adding elements. Not a 5 because option sets were sometimes tightly structured (A/B choices, before/after work), slightly constraining the space of exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within: 'What about today felt most helpful?' elicited Marcus's own insight ('My brain made it way bigger than it is'), and the confidence ruler prompted self-assessment. Not a 5 because fewer open-ended questions explored deeper values — why managing diabetes matters to him and his family — leaving evocation somewhat task-focused.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise consistently located within the client: his coffee routine, privacy preference, nightstand placement, and cheat-sheet request all came from him and became plan elements. This is genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocation via 'What one small step feels doable?' and a textbook confidence ruler, producing change talk ('kind of a relief,' 'a six... maybe seven'). Not a 5 because questions were often bundled and commitment language was never deepened or confirmed through an evocative summary.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hesitancy ('I don't feel ready yet') was met with reframes ('slips are normal; we'll problem-solve them together') and the meter fear was dissolved through low-pressure in-session practice. Not a 5 because sustain talk was mostly sidestepped rather than explicitly softened with double-sided reflections.
