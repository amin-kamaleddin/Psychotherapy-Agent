# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with genuine warmth (e.g., honoring the mother memory, validating anger). Repeated safety-check questions despite Maria's frustration slightly undermined unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, evocative reflections at key moments (naming her anger at being 'a procedure') that visibly helped her open up. Empathy was inconsistent, with frequent pivots back to safety protocols that Maria experienced as not listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session maintained focus on suicidality and suffering with concrete outcomes (memory paragraph, one song, return visit). However, direction was reactive — safety-check interruptions fragmented flow and protective motivations were not deliberately linked to target behaviors.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely joint framing ('Want to edit this together?') and immediate integration of Maria's contributions. Occasional fallback to expert-mode checklists and partially unaccommodated requests to slow down kept it from a 5.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent choice-offering and consistent respect for refusals (breathing exercise, 'Don't push it' honored). Autonomy was constrained by rigid repeated safety directives and the counselor drafting the memory paragraph rather than letting Maria own it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some space for Maria's motivations to surface through open prompts about her mother. Relied heavily on menu-style solutions rather than drawing out her own reasons for living, and key change statements were acknowledged but not developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Power was partially shared through invitations to edit the paragraph and shape next steps. The counselor writing the paragraph for her and overriding her stated preferences with repetitive safety questions weakened true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerged ('a tiny part of me wants to see what happens') but largely organically rather than through skillful evocation. Missed opportunities to amplify, deepen, or reflect back her change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was gently acknowledged without reinforcement, but little active redirection occurred. Missed chances for double-sided reflections pairing hopelessness with her emerging reasons for living left sustain talk largely unaddressed.
