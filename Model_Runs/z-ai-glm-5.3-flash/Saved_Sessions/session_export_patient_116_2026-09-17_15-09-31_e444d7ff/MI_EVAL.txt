# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirmed the client's autonomy ('that's your call'), while normalizing his frustration. Affirmations of strengths were sparse, and the safety check felt abrupt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captured core concerns (feeling sluggish, not being heard) and connected them to his value of 'feeling like himself.' Reflections occasionally stayed at surface level and missed deeper feelings like fear of new side effects and underlying resentment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on medication nonadherence, progressing logically to a concrete, achievable plan with a role-played call. The long medication option lists briefly diffused focus, and explicit summarizing at key transitions was lacking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed work as joint through the co-created script and role-play honoring the client's own words. Information-giving was one-directional, delivering long lists without first eliciting what the client knew (elicit-provide-elicit).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supported choice with permission-seeking, options framed without pressure, and acceptance of the client's hedged commitment. The role-play honored his own words and style ('three sentences max').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within, eliciting the client's value of 'feeling like myself' and his self-chosen first step. Missed opportunities for scaling questions and deeper exploration of the meaning behind his values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited input and preferences, but the two consecutive expert-style lists of medication options reduced the client's role to selecting from a menu. Did not affirmatively acknowledge the client's expertise about his own body and experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk from a guarded, precontemplative client, moving him toward commitment ('I can do that'). Change talk remained hedged ('I'm not promising anything'), and explicit consolidation via scaling was not used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well by acknowledging it neutrally without amplifying, then redirecting toward possibilities. More double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with the client's own reasons for change would strengthen this.
