# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive, with affirmations of the patient's courage and care for her daughter. Slightly formulaic at times and lacked deeper normalization of past slips.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured her ambivalence, fear of letting her daughter down, and identity tied to smoking. Reflections were occasionally brief and could have explored the shame and stigma more deeply.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation with no topic drift, progressing logically through rulers, past successes, and a concrete plan. Strong guidance toward specific actions and commitments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong joint creation of the plan, support text, reminder card, and phone script, with check-ins before drafting. Occasionally presented option lists rather than jointly generating ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced the patient's ownership, accepting her 'one week, not promising more' boundary without pushing. All materials were drafted from her own words, strengthening investment in the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out her own motivations (daughter, three-week success, house smelling better) through open questions and rulers. Could have evoked more around her values and self-efficacy beyond practical planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed decisions around the patient's expertise, and her tweaks (five-minute walks, sister as support) were adopted directly into the plan. Somewhat quick to offer options rather than drawing out more of her own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance/confidence rulers, the confidence-boosting question, and eliciting past successes, producing strong change talk. Could have used deeper commitment-strengthening follow-ups and more deliberate amplification sequences.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to doubts with empathy and pivoted to small, doable steps without reinforcing sustain talk. Redirections were somewhat brief and could have used more double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence explicitly.
