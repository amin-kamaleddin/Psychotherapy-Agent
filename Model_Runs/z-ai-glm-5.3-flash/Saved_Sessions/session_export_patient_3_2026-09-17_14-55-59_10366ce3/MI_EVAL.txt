# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with consistent affirmations ('Nice work', 'That's a really thoughtful plan') and no labeling or disapproval. Affirmations focused on the plan's cleverness rather than deeper, individualized regard for Aisha's courage in confronting her harsh self-criticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Aisha's emotional state ('feeling stuck in your body and disconnected') and her fear of failure ('past slips can feel heavy'). Empathy stayed mostly at a content/planning level, missing the opportunity to explore the deeper emotional pain of mirror avoidance and chronic self-harshness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on the target behavior — starting walking again — maintained from the opening question through concrete planning. Every turn moved toward action with specific days, cues, accountability, and a backup plan, ending in a fully concrete plan without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha's own ideas (5-minute rule, shoes by the door, text choice) substantially shaped the plan, and the counselor offered expertise as options rather than prescriptions. Occasional long menu-style suggestion blocks slightly reduced the sense of equal exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently reinforced — 'support your choices', repeated choice offerings, and framing the walk as 'an experiment, not a test.' Aisha owned the plan in her own words ('like you said'), and the counselor never pressured or directed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited (past-success question, confidence ruler), but much of Aisha's change talk emerged spontaneously due to her readiness rather than skillful evocation. The counselor moved quickly to logistics without exploring the intrinsic emotional drivers of 'feeling gross' and body image distress.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offered choice menus and co-constructed the plan from Aisha's own solutions, clearly honoring her expertise. Some turns provided dense prescriptive lists of steps before asking for her input, slightly tilting toward expert-driven structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evocative questions ('What would starting again look like?') and a confidence ruler amplified Aisha's commitment language ('I think I can actually do this one'). Reinforced her own solutions rather than imposing them, but evocation of deeper values and reasons for change beyond logistics was limited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded well to Aisha's minimal sustain talk (fear of failure, 'I don't feel like it') by normalizing ('starting is often the hardest part') and reframing a miss as 'data, not disaster.' Slightly more explicit double-sided reflection of her harsh self-talk could have strengthened this.
