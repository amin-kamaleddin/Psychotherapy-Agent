# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental toward a guarded, mandated client, with affirmations like 'showing up is a solid first step' and nonjudgmental handling of his record. Warmth is somewhat sparse and affirmations brief, keeping this below a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate simple reflections capture content and barriers, but reflections stay surface-level and are quickly followed by questions. The emotional weight of shame, fear of failure, and guardedness goes largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on employment goals with good momentum from exploration to concrete deliverables (resume, disclosure script, interview practice). Falls short of 5 because focus narrows to resume mechanics without reconnecting to Mateo's larger values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine co-construction in resume drafting and role-play, but the interaction tilts toward the counselor as doer of tasks, including a six-question intake barrage and pre-written scripts. Mateo's ideas influence the work, but the counselor often occupies the expert chair.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choices and honors Mateo's requests (moving the cert up top, initiating role-play), with permission-seeking framing of advice. Options are sometimes counselor-generated menus rather than evoking his own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits motivation via goals, barriers, and confidence scaling, and Mateo articulates his own reasons. However, the counselor frequently shifts to supplying content (resume text, scripts) rather than sustaining evocation of Mateo's own reasons and capacities.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative language and responsiveness to Mateo's input, but several turns are expert-driven with pre-packaged solutions and question barrages. Power-sharing is uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is elicited through goal questions and confidence scaling, and Mateo offers commitment language ('less excuses'). However, the counselor does not consistently reinforce or deepen it with strategic reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without reinforcement and occasionally reframed, but no double-sided reflections pivot toward change. Redirecting is present but not consistent or skilled.
