# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard was maintained throughout, with sustain talk consistently met with acceptance ('that's completely okay') rather than judgment or correction. The client's pre-contemplative stance was never pathologized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and emotionally deep, capturing the functional meaning of smoking ('the one moment in your day that's yours') and the deeper fear of becoming an irritable mess. Double-sided reflections tracked her ambivalence with genuine understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on smoking around her children, with each question building strategically toward a self-generated action step. No topic drift occurred, and the conversation progressed logically from pre-contemplation to commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully collaborative between equals, with the psychiatrist framing exploration as joint thinking and following the client's lead. All plan ideas originated from Linda's own world, and she explicitly recognized the collaborative process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice was actively and consistently reinforced, with the clinician explicitly rejecting the expert role and honoring her refusal to commit ('Nothing is being signed today'). Advice was never imposed, exemplifying maximal autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited from within via open questions about what would make small change possible and imagined outcomes for her daughter. Linda's own reasons and readiness visibly grew through the session, with the clinician never arguing for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client's expertise was explicitly and repeatedly honored ('You're the expert on your own life'), and every plan element originated from Linda rather than the clinician. The psychiatrist explicitly rejected the expert role, fully meeting this criterion.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked through open questions, progressing from 'maybe' to a concrete commitment ('take the pack out to the porch tonight'). The psychiatrist reinforced this with reflections highlighting Linda's own words and evolving desire into commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well without amplification—doubts were acknowledged respectfully and gently pivoted toward possibility, with the key reframe coming from the client herself. The score is kept from 5 only because reflections occasionally restated sustain talk before redirecting, though this appeared strategically ambivalence-honoring.
