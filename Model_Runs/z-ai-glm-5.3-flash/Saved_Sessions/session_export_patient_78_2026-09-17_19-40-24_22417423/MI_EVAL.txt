# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, unconditional positive regard throughout; validates resistance and accepts the patient's rejection of scheduled check-ins without defensiveness. Warmth is genuine and never contingent on readiness to change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, increasingly deep reflections that mirror vulnerable disclosures (daughter's comment, fear of failure). The first-person reflection style occasionally blurred speaker attribution, and some emotional nuance could have been named more explicitly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on smoking with strategic momentum from ambivalence to a concrete self-generated plan. A consolidating summary of key change statements before closing would have strengthened direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every decision is co-constructed, with questions rather than directives and no unsolicited advice. The patient's closing comments directly evidence the collaborative feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional — change is framed as fully the patient's, and her rejection of follow-up structure is endorsed and built into the plan. The patient's own words ('If it's mine, it's got a chance') confirm its impact.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from the patient's own material (daughter, past 3-month quit, curiosity) with no persuading or health lecturing. Strategic questions elicit progressively stronger client-driven change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is repeatedly located within the client, and the entire plan originates from her. The clinician defers to her self-knowledge and asks permission at each step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and develops preparatory change talk, leading the patient to generate her own plan and reframe her past quit as a success. Commitment language ('I might actually try that') emerges organically from the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged without reinforcement and gently pivoted toward change; fear of failure is explored rather than argued away. Textbook double-sided reflection and non-confrontational handling of disengagement.
