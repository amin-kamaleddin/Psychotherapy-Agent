# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard despite court-mandated, stigmatized behavior. "You're here, and that counts for something" and "Both of those can be true at once" normalize ambivalence without judgment. Warmth feels genuine and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Highly accurate, emotionally attuned reflections capturing both content and feeling ("carrying the court fees, your mom finding out, and still being pulled in"). Skillfully mirrors client metaphors ("the math changed," "Friday me isn't thinking straight") and tracks emotional shifts, including hearing hope.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Steady focus on target behavior throughout; each exchange advances momentum — from ambivalence, to a specific alternative, to planning and commitment ("one clean Friday"). No aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Genuine exchange between equals; plan co-constructed entirely from Jordan's ideas (text-to-self, pre-decision, Marcus conversation). Counselor positioned as supporter ("what would you need from me?"), not expert.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 ☑
**Comments:** Regularly supports choice and ownership ("What feels like something you'd actually do?", "What makes sense to you?"). Falls short of 5 only because Jordan's request for external accountability ("maybe a little pressure's not bad") was not explicitly reframed as his own free choice, and control over outcomes was not overtly emphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from within the client — open evocative questions elicit intrinsic reasons (weariness, mother, pride). Counselor never argues for change; all motivations originate from Jordan's words and are deepened through reflection.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Expertise consistently located in the client ("You know your life and your crew better than anyone"). Jordan's contributions visibly drive the session; counselor attributes all solutions to him ("it came from your own read on Marcus, not anything I suggested").

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Change talk skillfully evoked and highlighted throughout ("maybe I could just not go into the stores — you said it yourself, not me"; "you went from maybe to I could probably actually do that"). Strategic questions elicit desire, ability, and commitment, culminating in "I think I'm ready to try."

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 ☑
**Comments:** Sustain talk largely not amplified; acknowledged briefly then pivoted toward change ("what might help you get through that moment?"). Not a 5 because early reflections gave substantial airtime to reasons against change (peer pull, "nothing standing where it used to be"), though double-sided and ultimately serviceable.

---
**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.8/5**

**Summary:** Exemplary MI session. Strengths: exceptional empathy and reflections using the client's own metaphors; consistent cultivation of client-generated change talk; strong partnership and collaboration; clear direction culminating in a concrete, client-owned plan (texting Marcus, the "thin ice with the courts" line, one clean Friday). Areas for growth: more efficient pivoting away from early sustain talk, and stronger explicit affirmation of client autonomy when he invited external pressure. Near-model MI practice appropriate for a challenging, court-mandated, ambivalent client.
