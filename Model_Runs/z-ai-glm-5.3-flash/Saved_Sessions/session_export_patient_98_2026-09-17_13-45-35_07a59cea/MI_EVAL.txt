# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout; the patient explicitly felt accepted and not treated like a complainer. However, affirmations were generic and his specific strengths (e.g., persistence with rehab despite pain) were never explicitly affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections validated his tiredness and discouragement accurately, but empathy stayed surface-level. The emotional weight of feeling hopeless, fearing another failed attempt, and feeling isolated was never deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on increasing manageable activity and produced a concrete, time-bound micro-goal with follow-up. Direction was achieved through prescription rather than collaborative momentum, and deeper motivational exploration was missing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Patient's input shaped some details (timing, tracking, reminders), but the plan's core architecture was delivered as instructions rather than co-constructed. Repetitive recaps resembled a checklist handoff more than joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine choices were offered (grounding vs. goal, modifying the goal, reminders), and the patient exercised real autonomy. Falls short of 5 because the plan's specifics were counselor-designed with little explicit reinforcement of his ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivationally rich material surfaced ('feels pointless,' 'maybe it'll surprise me') but was never explored or built upon with open questions about his values or confidence. The psychiatrist supplied reasons and the plan himself rather than evoking motivation from the patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Inviting the patient to choose options and suggest alternatives honored his expertise, but the overall posture leaned expert-driven with prescriptions offered before drawing on his own rehab knowledge. Collaborative moments were real but shallow.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Space was created for the patient's own change language ('I can do five minutes. Today, even.'), and offering choice supported engagement. However, the psychiatrist did not strategically deepen or amplify this change talk with evocative questions about why persistence matters.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced, and reframing pacing as a strategy gently repositioned the pain narrative. But no double-sided reflections acknowledged his ambivalence, and doubt statements ('won't matter,' 'small potatoes') were skipped rather than strategically resolved.
