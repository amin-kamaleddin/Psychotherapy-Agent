# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, even when the client was dismissive or minimizing. No judgment appeared; respect was explicitly conveyed (e.g., affirming his desire to be there for his daughter).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, precise reflective listening, including effective double-sided reflections that captured the client's ambivalence. Reflections deepened self-exploration, as shown by the client's 'I hadn't really thought about it like that before.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, progressive focus from ambivalence to values, to practical strategies, to a concrete commitment and follow-up plan. No aimless drifting occurred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operated as a genuine partnership, with the psychiatrist asking rather than telling and co-creating the follow-up arrangement. The client's own comment about expecting a lecture but finding something manageable confirms the non-directive stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support: choice was explicitly honored, resistance to check-ins was respected, and all strategies came from the client. The closing affirmation framed past success as quitting 'on your own terms.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from the client rather than supplied, with no lecturing or imposed reasons to change. The client's own values (fatherhood, family history, past mastery) became the engine of the plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly positioned the client as the expert on his own life, and the client's own ideas directly shaped the plan. The follow-up structure was left for the client to define.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful open questions elicited increasingly strong commitment language, culminating in a concrete Saturday plan. Change talk was then reinforced by reflection and by having the client voice his feelings aloud.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally handled well via double-sided reflections and decreased steadily as change talk grew. The score is slightly lowered because early reflections briefly gave air to minimizing language, though this was strategic rapport-building and quickly pivoted toward the client's own concerns.
