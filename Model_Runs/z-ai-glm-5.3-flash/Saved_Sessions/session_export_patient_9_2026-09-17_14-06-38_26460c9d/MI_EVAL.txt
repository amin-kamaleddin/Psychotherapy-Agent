# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful,  ```nonjudgmental``` tone with safety screening;  however,  affirmations are generic and warmth is diluted by quick pivots to advice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections,  but deep value-based material (guilt,  fear of being  ```that mom,```  distrust of Dad) went unreflected and was met with bullet-pointed advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear,  consistent focus on co-parenting conflict and sleep/behavior,  building to a concrete plan;  direction came through premature problem-solving rather than client-guided goal clarification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Largely expert-driven with counselor-generated scripts,  templates,  and options;  Amina's idea to start smaller was folded into the counselor's plan rather than co-developed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choices offered and taken,  but frequent prescriptions and a directive closing checklist meant the plan felt more counselor-authored than client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions were mostly closed and fact-focused;  Amina's motivation (love for her son) emerged spontaneously rather than through skilled evocation of her reasons,  values,  and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role with numbered advice and directives;  Amina's contributions were accommodated but often overwritten rather than shaping the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina generated genuine change talk,  and the weekends-differ reframe was captured well,  but change talk arose from the structured plan rather than deliberate open questions evoking desire,  ability,  or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some sustain talk was neutralized (reassurance,  the effective reframe),  but no double-sided reflections were used and doubts were often bypassed in favor of the next practical suggestion.
