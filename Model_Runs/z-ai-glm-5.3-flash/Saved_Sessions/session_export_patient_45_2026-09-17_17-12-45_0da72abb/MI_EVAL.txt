# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, welcoming Erik's skepticism and offering affirmations like 'Nice clear plan.' Warmth was somewhat formulaic and brisk, with limited individualized affirmation of Erik beyond his role as patient.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Produced some accurate reflections (e.g., 'annoyed by the fuss but not wanting things to get worse'), but empathy was thin overall—frequently stacking 3-5 questions per turn crowded out reflective listening. Deeper feelings such as resentment about lifelong medication and the beer as his wind-down ritual were acknowledged once but not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a very clear focus on alcohol reduction and diet changes tied to diabetes management throughout, sequencing diet appropriately as optional. Every turn tied back to the target behaviors, progressing logically from assessment to concrete planning with a closing recap.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interacted largely as equals, co-constructing the fridge tally and letting Erik's expertise about his shifts and routine drive the plan. Collaboration was diluted by brisk, checklist-like delivery and multiple suggestions per turn that at times felt like steering rather than joint pacing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Supported Erik's choices well—he rejected suggestions without pushback—and the counselor asked permission and framed slips non-judgmentally. Occasionally bundled recommendations with autonomy language and used rapid-fire questions that reduced Erik's felt choice about session pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from Erik via rulers and evocative questions, culminating in a strong elicit for his self-generated commitment sentence to Linda. Fell short because Erik's motivation remained externally attributed ('cause the doc said so') and the counselor did not connect change to Erik's own values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly deferred to Erik's expertise through option menus and invitations to shape the plan, and his choices visibly determined the outcome. Occasionally slipped into prescriptive suggestions ('tell Linda', 'set a 15-minute timer') rather than always eliciting first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully used readiness/confidence rulers and choice among options, eliciting meaningful change talk ('that I could probably manage', 'One less each night') and a concrete commitment statement. Could have deepened change talk further by exploring why confidence was a 6 and what would raise it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk ('I feel fine', 'this is all a bit much') without argument or amplification and gently reframed slips as information. Reflections were sometimes brief acknowledgments rather than strategic double-sided reflections that resolve ambivalence.
