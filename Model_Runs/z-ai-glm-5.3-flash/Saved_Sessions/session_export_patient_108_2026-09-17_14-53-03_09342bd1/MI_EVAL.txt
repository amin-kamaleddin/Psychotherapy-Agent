# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with warm affirmations ('You've done hard work') and validation of Oliver's compassion, without labeling despite his self-criticism. Warmth was at times brief and formulaic, and deeper unconditional positive regard (e.g., explicitly normalizing his guilt) was not articulated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered accurate reflections of Oliver's ambivalence that captured both sides of his conflict ('You're juggling care and guilt while needing your space'). Reflections were sometimes brief and quickly paired with questions or options, and emotional nuances (seventy/thirty confidence, fear of sad eyes) were not consistently reflected with full depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (flatmate moving out by Friday and strengthening assertiveness), with no topic drift. Progressively built from exploring ambivalence to rehearsal and a concrete plan, with repeated confidence checks tracking momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Included collaborative elements such as inviting Oliver to choose activities and seeking his safety input, but the interaction tilted toward directive coaching with pre-made plans and step-by-step instructions. Oliver's own insights (e.g., identifying the promise pattern) were not consistently built upon, making power-sharing partial rather than equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opened with 'You're in charge of what we focus on' and consistently offered menus of choices, with no coercive or pressuring language. Autonomy was somewhat undermined by providing ready-made scripts rather than eliciting Oliver's own phrasing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence scaling effectively drew out self-generated change talk ('I've got more backbone than I gave myself credit for'), and several open questions were genuinely evocative. However, much of the session involved the counselor supplying reasons, scripts, and counter-arguments, shifting the source of motivation away from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sought Oliver's input ('Which would feel most useful?'), but functioned largely as a script-provider and coach, supplying multiple pre-written scripts, a backup plan, and canned rebuttals. Oliver generated his best material himself, which the counselor could have leveraged far more.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively invited and reinforced change talk through open questions and confidence scaling, with Oliver's confidence rising from 3 to 7+ across the session. Change talk was not always deepened once it emerged (e.g., exploring what drives the confidence rise or what success looks like).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk (guilt, 'I'm weak in that area') briefly without reinforcing it, then redirected toward actionable change via scripts and role-play. A 5 would require more skillful double-sided reflections explicitly pivoting from sustain to change language rather than redirection.
