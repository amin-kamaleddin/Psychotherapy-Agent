# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed the patient's autonomy, insight, and efforts while avoiding pressure or judgment. There were occasional missed opportunities to explicitly normalize the patient's ambivalence about peer norms.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections captured the patient's feelings and values well, including ambivalence about peer expectations and wanting control over drinking. Responses were attuned, though not always emotionally deepened beyond surface content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintained a clear focus on reducing drinking and building a concrete plan for the weekend, with no sidetracking. Each exchange built toward the patient's own change goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasized the plan as the patient's own ('That's yours, not mine') and elicited strategies from the patient rather than prescribing them. The partnership tone was strong throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's own choices were centered — identifying Danny's hangout, pacing strategies, and deciding to try the plan without promises. The psychiatrist explicitly reinforced self-direction and avoided controlling language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Powerful evocation of change talk — the patient articulated wanting control ('drinking kinda runs me'), identified his own reasons (Sunday mornings, the lake), and generated his own coping strategies. Questions like 'what would tell you it worked?' drew out meaningful motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The pacing felt equal and respectful, with the psychiatrist following the patient's lead and inviting collaboration ('I'm curious what you think'). The patient explicitly noted the session felt unlike a lecture, confirming the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully developed change talk through open questions and reflective consolidation, resulting in strong patient commitments ('Danny's Saturday, no racing anybody') and self-motivational statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'm not some alcoholic,' 'the guys might give me crap') was met with balanced reflections ('both of those can be true') and reframing rather than argument. Some peer-pressure sustain talk was left unexplored in depth.
