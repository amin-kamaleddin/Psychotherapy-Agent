# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, non-judgmental stance and affirmed Wiremu's concrete efforts (cutting back on drinking, booking the program, contacting his mum). However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and missed opportunities to individualize praise, particularly around his cultural context and the courage of reconnecting with his estranged mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was some accurate reflection early on ("how much your kids mean to you"), but emotionally loaded disclosures—grief over fortnightly visits, fear of being seen as 'better than' Tristan, ambivalence about whānau living—were largely bypassed in favor of jumping to bullet-pointed advice. Feelings were rarely reflected back or explored before problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on target behaviors (alcohol reduction, trigger management, the Tristan boundary conversation, the restorative letter) with every turn advancing a concrete agenda. The counselor kept tangents tied to change goals and closed with a clear action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The role-play of the Tristan conversation was genuinely collaborative and well-matched to Wiremu's request. However, the default pattern was pre-packaged expert plans delivered as bullet lists, with little explicit 'we' language or joint agenda-setting, leaving Wiremu in a reactive rather than co-creating position.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were autonomy-supportive moments, including offering choices, asking permission, and respecting Wiremu's decision to talk to Tristan directly rather than via written message. But dense prescriptive advice (lockboxes, journaling, meds) was frequently delivered before exploring his preferences, so autonomy was tolerated rather than actively fostered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Wiremu arrived highly motivated, but the psychiatrist made almost no effort to elicit or develop his own reasons, confidence, or vision for change—no open evocative questions, confidence rulers, or invitations to voice his motivations. The counselor supplied reasons and solutions rather than drawing them out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice questions at several turns showed some power sharing, but the dominant mode was expert-driven with rapid-fire recommendations and minimal inquiry into Wiremu's own ideas. His expertise (script adjustments, his decision about how to approach Tristan) was never explicitly acknowledged or leveraged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Wiremu offered abundant spontaneous change talk ("I'll do anything to get my kids back," "my kids are worth more than my pride"), but the psychiatrist rarely reflected, amplified, or deliberately evoked it. The change talk went largely unmarked and undeveloped—a significant missed MI opportunity given the client's readiness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoided reinforcing Wiremu's doubts about moving in with whānau or reluctance about the Tristan talk. However, no active softening occurred—no double-sided reflections pairing independence with family, and no reframing that returning to whānau could be a strength—leaving sustain talk bypassed rather than resolved.
