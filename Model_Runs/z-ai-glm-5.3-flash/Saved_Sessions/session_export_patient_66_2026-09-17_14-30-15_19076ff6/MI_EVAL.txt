# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating the client's frustration and peer-pressure concerns and honoring his work-only boundary. Affirmations were somewhat generic, with missed opportunities to affirm specific strengths like his past two-week quit attempt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured the social meaning of smoking, but reflections were sparse overall. Emotionally significant statements (fear of caving, ambivalence about 'quitting by the back door') received tactics rather than emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused and built logically toward a concrete, behaviorally detailed Monday plan. Lost a point because direction sometimes took the form of counselor-driven task lists rather than helping the client clarify his own goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were genuinely adjusted to the client's break times, craving pattern, and language style, and the role-play rehearsal was highly interactive. Some steps (lozenge primer, final checklist) were counselor-delivered rather than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered menus of choices, left decisions to the client, and honored his work-only boundary without pressure. Undermined somewhat by suggesting coping options early and presenting the lozenge plan as a near-complete prescription to tweak.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation emerged through preference questions and rehearsal, but much content came from counselor suggestions. Missed key opportunities, such as exploring the past two-week quit and eliciting the client's own internal reasons beyond the workplace policy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for preferences and let the client draft his own scripts, which he owned enthusiastically. However, frequently slipped into an expert/educator role, delivering a lozenge lecture and a largely counselor-generated checklist without acknowledging the client's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively moved the client from 'I'm not gonna quit' to 'I reckon I can do this bit' through preference questions and rehearsal. Change talk was rarely explicitly reflected or amplified when it appeared, missing chances to deepen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Never argued with sustain talk and used a clever early reframe of the social meaning of smoking. However, did not use double-sided reflections to pair sustain talk with change talk, often moving past ambivalence into option menus instead.
