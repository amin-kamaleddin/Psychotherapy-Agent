# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✅
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout — "I really appreciate you laying out what you need," "you came in with thoughtful questions," "don't hesitate to come back." No judgment or disapproval at any point; respect is warm and sustained from opening to closing.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 ✅
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening ("I'm hearing how much it matters to you to have reliable information…"). Reflections capture values well, but primarily target informational needs; less exploration of the emotional layer beneath the anxiety before moving to facts.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✅
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on medication education and safety while following the patient's own stated structure (phenelzine, mechanism, side effects). Summarizes well and keeps momentum toward a concrete plan; no topic drift.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✅
**Comments:** Genuinely two-way: "we could go through those three things right now, together." Asks permission before structuring, offers options, co-creates the written summary, and repeatedly checks in. Patient's contributions visibly shape each next step.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✅
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced — "keeps you safe and in charge of your own care." Avoids coercion, supports verifying with the prescriber before stopping phenelzine, and credits ownership of the decision and plan.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 ✅
**Comments:** Elicits motivation from within ("what would help you feel ready to decide either way?", "what feels like the most important thing to confirm?"). Draws out her own reasons and plan, though much of the session is necessarily didactic, limiting depth of ongoing evocation.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ✅
**Comments:** Explicitly positions the patient as the expert: "You're the expert on what information helps you feel confident in a decision." Patient's agenda (three questions, one clear page) directly shaped the entire session.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 ✅
**Comments:** Actively evokes and reinforces change language and names readiness: "you're already thinking about starting it properly… That's encouraging." Patient leaves with concrete next steps; however, evocation relies on a few key questions rather than sustained, varied cultivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4 ✅
**Comments:** Validates hesitation ("It's been weighing on me the most") without amplifying it, then pivots to information and action. Patient moves from worry to "I feel prepared, not nervous anymore." Softening occurs mainly through information provision rather than deliberate MI techniques (e.g., double-sided reflections).

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 4.6/5**

A strong session demonstrating high-level MI-consistent practice in a medication education context. Strengths include exceptional partnership and power-sharing, consistent autonomy support, clear direction built on the client's own agenda, warm acceptance, and effective evocation that moved the client from anxiety to a concrete, self-owned plan. Areas for growth: deepen reflections to reach the emotional layer beneath information-seeking, use double-sided reflections to soften hesitation, and sustain change-talk cultivation with more varied evocative questions.
