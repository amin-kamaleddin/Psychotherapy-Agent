# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations and consistent honoring of David's preferences (privacy, wife's involvement, lozenge flavor). Affirmations are somewhat sparse and formulaic; past failed quit attempts could have been framed as persistence rather than failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured both content and emotion, including work pressure and readiness ('this plan feels doable to you'). Shame around 'talking big' about quitting and frustration over work stress were not explicitly validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary focus on smoking cessation throughout, progressing from motivation to medication, triggers, quit date, and follow-up logistics. Every exchange advanced the concrete plan without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed (quit date, medication, wife's role) with an open invitation for additions. Several turns were still dominated by psychiatrist information delivery rather than full power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong statements of choice ('You're the expert here') and honoring of David's decisions throughout. Some exchanges slid into recommendation mode before exploring his preferences, and autonomy wasn't reinforced at moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong evocative questions opened and closed the session, but the middle relied heavily on providing information and strategy lists. Missed opportunities included confidence scaling and eliciting David's own solutions for the work-stress trigger.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicit partnership framing with regular check-ins on acceptability shaped a plan driven substantially by David's contributions. Information-delivery still dominated much of the session rather than fully co-creating each element.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open evocative questions drew out sustained change talk ('This time feels different') and self-insight about spousal support. Change talk was often answered by pivoting to logistics rather than deepening it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Work-stress sustain talk ('five minutes of peace') was acknowledged but met with strategy lists rather than strategic MI techniques. No double-sided reflections, reframing, or use of doubt to elicit confidence talk.
