# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental and respectful throughout, explicitly disclaiming pressure and validating Kareem's limits ('that's valid'). Warmth felt somewhat procedural and the safety screening was formulaic, keeping it from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Kareem's resentment and the core meaning of smoking as ritual and social time. Deeper emotional threads (twenty-year bond, identity) were touched but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, steady movement from ambivalence to a concrete, confirmed plan (10:30 walk, rain fallback, follow-up). Some multi-step structure slightly imposed the agenda rather than letting direction emerge fully from the client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kareem chose the agenda, plan, invite style, and follow-up time, reflecting equal partnership. The counselor relied on pre-packaged option lists rather than joint brainstorming, which slightly limited shared idea generation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: choices offered at every decision point, control repeatedly disclaimed, and boundaries around home smoking honored. Kareem's closing 'Not for you, for me' shows his ownership was reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kareem's perspective was elicited and his final motivation was self-generated, but much content (alternatives, plan steps) came from counselor-provided menus rather than open exploration. His intrinsic reasons for change were never explicitly drawn out and developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kareem's input visibly shaped the session, with the plan co-constructed around his priorities. The counselor at times acted as the ideas source, providing complete menus instead of drawing solutions from Kareem.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk shifted into tentative change talk ('I'll try the walk loop this week', 'maybe I'll have something to report... for me'), but this emerged indirectly through planning. The counselor rarely deepened commitment or confidence language and missed the 'for me' moment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with acceptance rather than argument, and valued aspects (break, social time) were consistently preserved rather than debated. More strategic double-sided reflections could have gently loosened ambivalence about home smoking.
