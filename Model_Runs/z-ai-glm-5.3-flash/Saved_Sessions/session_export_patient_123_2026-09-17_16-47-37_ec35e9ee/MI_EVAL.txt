# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental tone throughout, with normalization of Sofia's uncertainty. However, when Sofia said she wanted to 'leave it' regarding alcohol, the counselor accepted it without affirming her honesty or self-awareness with explicit unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some accurate reflective statements, but reflections were largely factual and summary-level. Missed opportunities to reflect deeper meaning, such as Sofia's reliance on friends ('they'd never let me do anything stupid') and her subtle invitation to discuss the screening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus, but on the wrong target — nearly the entire session addressed study/test stress while the referral concern (alcohol risk) received only brief closing mention. When Sofia opened the door to the screening topic, the counselor offered it as one menu option rather than guiding focus there.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative elements like menu-based options and feasibility checks, but the counselor generated essentially all content while Sofia passively selected and consented. Collaboration was largely superficial choice-giving rather than genuine co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and respected Sofia's pace and decisions without pressure or coercion. Limited because autonomy support operated within a counselor-defined agenda, and allowing Sofia to close the alcohol discussion without follow-up edged toward avoidance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and content flowed almost entirely from the counselor; Sofia's own values, concerns, and motivations — especially regarding alcohol — were never actively elicited. Questions were closed or menu-based, inviting yes/no compliance rather than exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked for Sofia's input, reflecting some power-sharing, but overwhelmingly occupied the expert role, dispensing detailed advice and pre-packaged plans. Sofia's contributions were incorporated politely but did not substantially shape the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Virtually no effort to evoke change talk about the screening concern (alcohol); Sofia's minimal spontaneous change statements were not reinforced or deepened. Evocation efforts targeted study habits instead, a peripheral behavioral goal, with no open questions exploring Sofia's values or concerns about alcohol use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia's abundant sustain talk ('it's not really a big deal,' 'can we just leave it?') was largely accommodated rather than explored or softened. No double-sided reflections or gentle pivots addressed the discrepancy between 'it's not a big deal' and the positive screening, allowing sustain talk to end the conversation.
