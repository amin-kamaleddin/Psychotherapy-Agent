# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, including explicit validation of feeling labeled a failure ('that takes some courage'). Never criticizes the client's resistance or pre-contemplative stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate complex reflections that capture ambivalence and meaning ('feeling like yourself again—that sounds like what this has really been about all along'), which the client confirms. Could occasionally probe deeper into the fear of failure and emotional weight of feeling pre-judged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus and progressive momentum from ambivalence to a concrete commitment (sandwich choice, 'four out of five' days). Minor loss of efficiency at the close by repeating an earlier question, which the client noticed ('We've kind of circled back').

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative—goal, plan, and success criteria all originated from the client (night-before prep, wife's involvement). Occasional somewhat leading questions fall well within skilled collaborative MI.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support: 'You're the expert on your own life,' lunch framed as 'yours—no one else deciding.' Client's repeated ownership statements ('That's mine,' 'my way, at my pace') evidence this stance shaped his change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nothing prescribed; motivation drawn out through the client's own values, reasons, confidence rating, and definition of success. Client ends with self-generated openness to future change ('Maybe we'll talk about what's next').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions the client as the expert and shares power throughout; client contributions demonstrably shaped the session. Closing 'whenever you're ready to share it' reinforces shared control.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes desire, ability, reasons, need, and commitment, moving the client from 'maybe eating less junk wouldn't kill me' to 'That I could actually do' and a stated intent to return and report. Consistently reinforces change talk with affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is accurately reflected without amplification, then pivoted toward what feels 'doable' (e.g., the 'one piece, they'll add more' exchange). Sustain talk visibly diminishes over the session.
