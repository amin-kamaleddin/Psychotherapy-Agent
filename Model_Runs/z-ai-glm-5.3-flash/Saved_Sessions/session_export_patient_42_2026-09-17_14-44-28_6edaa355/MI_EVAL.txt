# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirmations like 'You've already taken a meaningful step.' Affirmations were somewhat generic ('nice work,' 'well done') and lacked individualized depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured Elise's ambivalence accurately, e.g., the tension between social connection and health concerns. Emotional nuances like fear of exclusion and her mother's illness were acknowledged but could have been explored more deeply before moving to action planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation while respecting its social function. Progressed logically from readiness exploration through ambivalence to a concrete experiment, relapse planning, and behavioral rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ideas were co-constructed, with Elise generating key plan elements (coffee, fidget ring, permission to leave after ten minutes). The counselor asked before advising and framed the role-play as joint practice, exemplifying MI collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently reinforced through 'testing, not committing' framing and choice offered at every step ('which line feels most like you?'). The experiment structure explicitly preserved Elise's sense of choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from Elise rather than lectured, with her own reasons for change emerging organically. During advice-giving segments (refusal lines, craving tips), the counselor supplied ready-made suggestions rather than evoking her own strategies first.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elise was repeatedly positioned as the expert, and her own contributions substantially shaped the plan, indicating real power sharing. The counselor still supplied most of the concrete option menus rather than drawing them primarily from Elise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully used readiness and confidence rulers alongside open questions to evoke change talk. Elise developed meaningful change talk in response, including 'I promised myself I'd try a smoke-free break' and 'First time I've had an actual plan about this.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally handled well, with the elegant reframe 'you're curious whether the connection needs cigarettes.' The approach relied mainly on reframing rather than double-sided reflections, and fear of exclusion was somewhat quickly routed to problem-solving.
