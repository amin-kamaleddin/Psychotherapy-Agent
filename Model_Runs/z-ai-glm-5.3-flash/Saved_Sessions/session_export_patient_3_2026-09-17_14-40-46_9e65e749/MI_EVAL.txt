# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with respectful affirmations of Aisha's concerns and efforts. However, affirmations are brief and formulaic, missing opportunities for deeper individualized positive regard (e.g., validating her courage in self-referring).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections consistently and accurately capture the content of Aisha's experience each turn. Reflections remain largely at the content level, missing underlying emotions such as shame about her body and fear of repeating past failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, unwavering focus on resuming exercise from first message to last, with every exchange moving the plan forward — scheduling, troubleshooting, accountability, and contingency planning. No topic drift, and the session progresses logically from motivation to a concrete, achievable plan appropriate for a client in preparation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan (schedule, backup plans, check-ins, reward) is genuinely co-constructed with Aisha's input honored at each step. However, the counselor often drives the agenda by offering menus of solutions rather than inviting Aisha to generate ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Aisha's plan elements are all self-selected, with choices regularly offered and preferences respected (gentle nudge, small treat). One moment edges toward directive territory — accepting 'don't let me shift Tuesday's time' without affirming or gently addressing the handover of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicits preferences, confidence ratings, and self-generated solutions, but evocation is primarily logistical rather than meaning-based. Powerful intrinsic statements like 'I want that back' are affirmed briefly but never deepened — no exploration of values or why this time feels different.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha's expertise shapes the plan (she proposed walking, times, and sister involvement), and her preferences are repeatedly requested. However, rapid-fire multiple questions and lists of pre-formed options delivered by the expert limit true power sharing rather than drawing out her own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of confidence rulers, realistic first steps, and concrete planning elicits strong commitment language ('I'll do that Wednesday night,' 'I won't let weather be my excuse'). Evocation relies mostly on logistical planning rather than deliberately deepening Aisha's own reasons and motivations for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The 'I'll bail' voice, fear of quitting after one walk, and rain-as-excuse are all met with practical reframes (5-minute rule, pre-planning) rather than amplified. Responses were problem-solving jumps rather than skillful MI pivots that reflect the ambivalence before connecting to her desire to change.
