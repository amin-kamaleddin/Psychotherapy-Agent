# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and never criticizes Julian. However, warmth is minimal and transactional, with generic affirmations and a formulaic, incongruent suicide-safety question inserted twice.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate reflections about deadlines and nervousness. Deeper emotional content — stigma fears, transportation worries, dependence on his mother — is deflected into more planning rather than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Very little effort to evoke Julian's own reasons, desires, or confidence for change. Questions are closed and logistical, and no evocative questions, rulers, or reinforcement of his autonomous statements are used.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not amplified and the session moves forward, but there are no double-sided reflections or reframes. Flooding Julian with contingency plans inadvertently feeds his anxiety and avoidance narrative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor positions himself as the expert problem-solver, with nearly every turn ending in a directive offer. Julian's expertise about his own neighborhood and situation is never affirmed or built upon.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is treated as residing in the counselor's advice rather than within Julian. His reasons remain external (court, mother) and the counselor's 'I can draft/search/fill in' style actively displaces opportunities for him to generate his own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the concrete target behavior and works systematically through logistics. However, direction becomes rigidity — a checklist rather than guided exploration — and the counselor never checks whether logistics are truly the core issue.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission is repeatedly asked and options are offered, showing some respect for choice. Yet the volume of prescribed steps and scripts functions as directive advice-giving, with Julian choosing only between versions of the counselor's plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although polite, the interaction is fundamentally counselor-driven, delivering plans, scripts, and numbered steps while Julian gives brief assents. Julian's local knowledge (42 bus line, Harding Street food bank) is never merged into a shared plan.
