# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone without scolding when the patient minimizes or refuses promises, and the patient explicitly thanked her for not lecturing. Deducted for warmth that feels somewhat formulaic and limited affirmation of Natalia's strengths as a single mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately captures the core ambivalence (the cigarette as her one 'me' moment vs. worry about her son's exposure) and tracks both sustain talk and change talk. Reflections are often brief and sometimes roll too quickly into advice or questions rather than sitting with emotional weight, notably after the pencil disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary focus — the session never drifts from the target behavior and each exchange builds momentum: ambivalence exploration → options → concrete plan → barrier planning → confidence assessment → commitment. The closing loop ('ask me about the gum next time') establishes forward continuity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The final plan is genuinely co-shaped (Natalia selects, modifies, and adds her own reminder strategies), but the power balance tips toward the clinician at key moments — the option menu, the gum-dosing lecture, and the drafted sister text all had limited input solicited first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly frames options as no-pressure, asks permission before exploring, and accepts the harm-reduction compromise; the patient directly reports feeling un-lectured. Minor deductions for rapidly delivered, somewhat leading option menus and lack of explicit affirmation of the patient's ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges through the confidence ruler and open worry questions, but the strongest motivational material — the pencil-mimicking moment — surfaced spontaneously rather than being deliberately evoked and developed. The clinician missed the chance to explore the discrepancy between her value of modeling for her son and her behavior.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses collaborative language but frequently shifts into an expert/advice-giving stance, generating all three options and delivering a detailed gum-dosing monologue with limited elicitation. Rarely asks 'what are your own ideas?' despite a clear opening (the sister who quit with gum).

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and the confidence ruler effectively elicit meaningful change talk ('part of me wonders if I could get through a bedtime without one'; 'I'll do the week. That I can promise.'), which is then reflected back well. Misses the opportunity to deepen the pencil disclosure — the session's most powerful change-relevant moment — by pivoting to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoids arguing with rationalizations and uses strategic harm-reduction framing ('keep the pause, cut the risk') that successfully pivots the patient toward acknowledging risk. Some reflections merely restate sustain talk ('tea feels lame', 'you're not ready to promise change') without a double-sided reframe toward values.
