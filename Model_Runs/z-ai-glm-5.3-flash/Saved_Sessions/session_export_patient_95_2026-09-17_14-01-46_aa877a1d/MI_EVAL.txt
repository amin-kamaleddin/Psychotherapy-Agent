# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Communicates consistent respect and warmth, thanking Maria for sharing and accepting her refusal to promise safety without judgment. Warmth is somewhat formulaic at times, keeping it below top marks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects Maria's crushing weight, exhaustion, and fear of nights, and prioritizes validation before problem-solving. Reflects are occasionally brief or procedural, and deeper emotional pain isn't fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on safety, means restriction, and connection, ending each exchange with a concrete next step. Direction is occasionally fragmented by stacked questions, with less sustained focus on deeper contemplative work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Invites Maria's input and incorporates her ideas (nephew picture, locking meds in trunk) into the plan. Collaboration weakens when delivering scripted blocks (crisis resources, comfort-kit list) without checking what she thinks might help.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames steps as choices, accepts partial commitments, and never pressures Maria to promise safety. Rapid-fire safety directives and the pre-written text slightly reduce her sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Notices and affirms Maria's spontaneous change talk about her nephew and wanting help, but rarely uses open questions to draw out and deepen her own reasons for living. Energy goes mostly to safety logistics rather than eliciting intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for Maria's input and co-develops steps, but much of the interaction is counselor-directed with pre-written texts and prescribed tool lists. Power-sharing is present but uneven, mostly within the counselor's framework.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Affirms Maria's volunteered change talk about wanting help and her protective choices. Evocation is largely reactive; few open questions are used to develop and strengthen this talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles 'nothing works' without argument, pivoting to her protective actions and reframing ambivalence as a lifeline. Sustain talk isn't deeply explored or softened through double-sided reflections, as safety questions sometimes shift focus away.
