# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, judgment-free stance throughout, with explicit affirmations and normalization of lapses; the client directly thanks the counselor for not making her feel like a failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong complex reflections capture ambivalence and emotional undercurrents (guilt, family motivation), though some reflections restate content rather than deepen exploration of the emotional core.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on adherence progressing from exploration to a concrete plan with contingency planning, confidence rating, and a scheduled follow-up—all without becoming directive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session operates between equals—the client set the agenda, selected and refined all plan elements, and set the follow-up interval herself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces choice ('support whatever you choose,' 'want this tweaked?'), accepts the client's rejection of suggestions, and frames small imperfect goals as legitimate.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivations and solutions came from the client's own history and words (coffee maker, purse pillbox, husband involvement), culminating in self-generated hope and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Power sharing is evident—the client rejected the alarm, declined extra reminders, and shaped the plan—but the counselor occasionally offered fairly full option menus rather than drawing ideas out first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation produced growing client commitment language ('I want to be around for my family,' 'I feel oddly hopeful'), supported by behavioral questions, a confidence ruler, and success criteria.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is redirected effectively via dual-sided reflections and past-success framing, though direct 'what might get in the way' prompts briefly invite barrier talk before being channeled toward solutions.
