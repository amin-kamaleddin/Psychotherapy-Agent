# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, with specific, genuine affirmations like 'the rusty version and the good one both count.' No judgment or disapproval anywhere; imperfection is explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening captured deeper identity themes ('being someone who does what she says she'll do again') and the all-or-nothing pattern with precision. Reflections mirrored the client's own language and values accurately.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus on building an exercise routine, moving from exploration to a concrete plan with accountability and relapse prevention. The next-session check-in adds momentum without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor framed the work as joint inquiry ('I'm genuinely curious what you'll notice') and all plan elements originated from the client. Structure was offered through questions rather than directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was consistently reinforced ('which one feels like it would give you that release feeling first?') with no advice-giving or persuasion. The 'plan bends, it doesn't break' insight was affirmed as the client's own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation was drawn from the client via questions like 'what would make you feel like it counted for you?' The counselor never supplied reasons for change; every expressed motivation came from Leila.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly located expertise in the client ('You're the expert on your life here'), and the entire plan was built from Leila's own ideas. No counselor-imposed prescriptions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoked commitment and desire language ('I'll start with one walk this week'), which was then reinforced by reflection. Change talk was deepened into future-oriented relapse prevention.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers were mostly reframed toward strengths ('removing friction before it starts,' quitting history as insight). A few reflections briefly amplified barrier language (sales pressure, the couch) before pivoting, though strategically done.
