### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, honoring Marco's honesty and 'no promises' stance without judgment about 40 years of smoking. The refusal to lecture and ending with 'Deal' communicates deep respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are consistently accurate and often complex, including a sophisticated double-sided reflection capturing both his motivations and his wariness. The clinician picks up subtle emotional content like 'something true coming from inside.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on smoking with clear forward momentum from ambivalence to a concrete first step. One minor weakness: a near-duplicate evocative question that Marco called out ('You asked me something like that already, doc').

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative interaction with permission-style questions and negotiated follow-up ('Can you manage that?' — 'Deal'). Marco's ideas shape every element of the outcome, with the clinician ceding control of pacing and disclosure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is actively reinforced at every turn — the plan is framed as 'entirely yours,' his timeline and privacy preferences are respected. The clinician never advises, pressures, or hands over pamphlet-style directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key motivation — partner peace, money, tasting coffee, grandkids — comes from Marco's own mouth in response to evocative questions. His shift from 'I don't make promises anymore' to volunteering a Monday start date demonstrates masterful elicitation of self-generated commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly positions Marco as the expert ('you know your own patterns better than anyone'), and Marco directly confirms power-sharing landed ('now you're asking me like I'm the expert'). The final plan is entirely client-generated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently elicit Marco's own motivations, and the ruler question moved him from a 3 to articulating conditions for change. Change talk is amplified and reflected back, culminating in a concrete plan and start date.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged without amplification and gently pivoted via double-sided reflections that legitimize ambivalence while emphasizing the change side. The clinician never argues with resistance, letting Marco's own change language develop.
