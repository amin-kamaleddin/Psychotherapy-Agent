# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, immediately validating the waiting-room complaint, honoring the 'no lectures' condition, and affirming the client's efforts. Affirmations were somewhat repetitive and formulaic, and scepticism was not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening, capturing the time-pressure reframe and noting the father's death as quiet motivation, showing attention to unspoken emotional content. Reflections were occasionally generic, and the deeper emotional layer (fear of his father's fate) was touched but never gently opened for exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on the target behavior (diet/weight and its link to blood pressure), with every turn advancing a concrete plan: swap, cue, fallback, self-monitoring, and scheduled check-in. Side issues were efficiently redirected back to the plan without dismissing the client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened collaboratively with an agenda question and an option menu, and incorporated the client's practical ideas (car bar, pad tally, email preference). However, plan construction became expert-driven with prescriptive directives rather than joint brainstorming, a missed opportunity given the client's stated expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered a menu of options, honored the client's boundaries, and framed advice as choices rather than commands. Once the plan was chosen, turns became instruction-heavy without re-checking preferences or explicitly reinforcing the client's ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evoked some real client motivation, including the BP number as a personal proof point and the father's death as underlying drive. Most plan content was counselor-supplied rather than drawn from the client's own reasons, ideas, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened with power-sharing (agenda question, a/b/c options), but the middle of the session shifted to rapid prescriptive advice-giving with limited elicitation of the client's own solutions. The overall shape was 'counselor designs, client approves' rather than genuine co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Capitalized on the client's change talk by moving quickly to a concrete plan and commitment-oriented questions. Change talk was met with logistics rather than amplification, with little deepening of desire or confidence language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce the client's repeated sustain talk ('haven't got time,' 'rearranging deck chairs') and generally pivoted to action. However, doubt was bypassed rather than actively softened—no double-sided reflections, strategic reframing, or affirming the legitimacy of skepticism while steering toward change.
