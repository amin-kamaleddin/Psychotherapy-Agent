# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, with genuine affirmations like 'I'm hearing some real wisdom in what you just said' and 'you're walking out as someone who's already started.' Rosa's fears and ambivalence are met with respect throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections capture both sides of her ambivalence, e.g., the opening reflection on smoking being woven into her life versus valuing her health. Rosa explicitly confirms feeling understood: 'thank you for not pushing me.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation across the entire session with a logical arc from ambivalence to a concrete plan. No topic drift occurs, and every turn guides toward change while remaining client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element originates from Rosa in response to collaborative invitations, with the counselor never prescribing. Her ideas—the Monday experiment, the note, calling Gina—fully determine the session's direction and outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforces Rosa's ownership throughout, culminating in 'this wasn't my plan or your doctor's plan. It was yours.' Rosa's closing statement—'I'm leaving with a plan I made myself'—confirms the autonomy-supportive stance landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All core motivations (nieces, Gina, seeing herself get older) come from Rosa herself, not the counselor. Open questions and strategic reflections evoke progressively stronger desire, need, ability, and commitment language, sustained across the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys that expertise resides within Rosa ('You know yourself and your routines better than anyone'), and her own contributions substantially shape the plan. The summary 'you found the reason, you picked the step, you chose your person' powerfully cements the partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful open questions ('What do you think a first step could look like?') elicit progressively stronger change talk, from 'maybe not smoking in the morning' to concrete commitment ('I'll call Gina tonight') and identity-level shift ('Maybe it's the other way around now'). Change talk is consistently reflected and deepened when it appears.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged without amplification and redirected toward change, as with the smoke-break friendships pivot. The score is not higher because Rosa's weight and social fears recurred across several turns, and double-sided reflections or reframing could have consolidated ambivalence resolution earlier.
