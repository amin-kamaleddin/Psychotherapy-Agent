# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated warmth and unconditional positive regard, reframing Rafael's fear of failure as self-protection rather than laziness. Ambivalence was normalized and his self-protective logic was honored throughout, with no criticism or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections were frequent and precise, capturing both barriers and desires (e.g., 'work leaves you drained... yet some part of you genuinely wants this back'). Rafael explicitly validated the counselor's reflections ('Yeah... that's it, exactly'), confirming highly accurate listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, progressive focus on returning to exercise, moving through a logical arc from barriers to past successes to a concrete, self-generated action plan. Each question built on the previous answer without topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned as a genuine exchange between equal partners, with plans co-constructed from Rafael's own material rather than imposed. Even the suggestion to talk with his wife was framed gently and immediately adopted as Rafael's own idea.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** There was no advice-giving or prescribing; all choices remained with Rafael, and his self-designed plan was explicitly framed as the reason it might stick. His own solution for handling slips was affirmed rather than corrected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently drew out Rafael's own reasons, definitions of success, and coping strategies, reflecting the premise that motivation resides within the client. His transformation from hesitant, short answers to commitment-oriented language ('feels doable,' 'proving to myself I'll follow through') demonstrates effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly located expertise within Rafael ('You know yourself best'), and every plan element—weekend session, packing the bag, dinner-table invitation, phone reminder—originated from him. Questions built on Rafael's own contributions, which substantially shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor strategically evoked and reinforced change talk, tracking Rafael's progression from 'I just can't get back' to 'maybe I could manage that much' and an evening commitment to talk with his wife. The shift was explicitly highlighted and deepened through success-definition and contingency-planning questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly and effectively redirected toward past successes and realistic possibilities. Minor limitation: double-sided reflections were not always used to explicitly pair sustain talk with change talk, though the implicit redirect pattern worked well.
