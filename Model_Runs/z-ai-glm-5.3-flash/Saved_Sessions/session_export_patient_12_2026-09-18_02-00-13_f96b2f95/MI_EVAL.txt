# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician affirms the patient's autonomy repeatedly ('you know yourself best,' 'that's yours to be proud of') and unconditionally separates worth from behavior ('whether she writes back or not, you are not a disappointment'). Acceptance is a consistent thread throughout, even amid ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both surface content and underlying feelings, e.g., naming the tension between wanting to reconnect and fear of being a disappointment. The patient explicitly states feeling heard and safe, confirming empathic attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation consistently steers toward change talk—reconnection, small steps, and structured evenings—while following the patient's lead. Each response selectively reinforces hopeful and change-oriented statements.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every next step originates from the patient (texting the cousin, the walk), with the clinician asking open questions and explicitly deferring to the patient's knowledge of their own rhythms. There is no prescriptive or expert-driven steering.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are framed as fully the patient's ('on your own timeline,' 'smallest step you'd be willing to take'), and imperfection is normalized ('you don't have to be certain for this to count'). The clinician explicitly reinforces self-direction and self-generated plans.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions repeatedly draw out the patient's own reasons and hopes ('what would make reaching out feel worth trying,' 'what made today different'). The patient's change talk—'it doesn't sound impossible,' 'I didn't today'—emerges directly from this elicitation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician deliberately spotlights and reflects patient change talk ('maybe she'd pick up,' 'it's mine,' 'today you didn't shut it down') and explores what made the difference. This sustains a growing arc of commitment across the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('what's the point,' 'I don't want to mess it up') is met with reflection and reframe rather than argument or reassurance-dismissal. Doubts are validated while their underlying hopes are gently amplified.
