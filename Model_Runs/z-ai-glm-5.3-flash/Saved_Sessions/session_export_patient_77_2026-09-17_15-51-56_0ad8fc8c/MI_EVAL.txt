# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and warm, with affirmations like 'that takes courage' and 'you're making a thoughtful, brave choice.' Affirmations feel somewhat formulaic, and the client's disclosure of loneliness received only brief acknowledgment rather than deeper unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, e.g., reflecting the client's discouragement and the link between skipping breakfast and dizziness. Deeper emotional disclosures—loneliness and fear of getting hopes up—were acknowledged briefly rather than explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior from opening question through a concrete closing plan with specific steps, timing, and follow-up. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames the work as joint ('a step we could try together') and co-constructs the plan from the client's own ideas. The metabolism/stress hormone psychoeducation was delivered in an information-heavy, expert-to-client manner that slightly tilted the power balance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support: asks permission, offers menu options, lets the client decide how to handle her mother's comments, and affirms her ownership of the plan. The plan is entirely client-selected and client-owned.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from the client's own words through success and confidence questions and self-generated solutions. Some motivation rests on counselor-provided explanations, and deeper values behind the weight-loss goal were not explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats the client as the expert—asking permission, offering choices, and eliciting her own obstacles and solutions that shape the plan. The mid-session educational block momentarily occupied the expert role before drawing out her understanding.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk via success, confidence ruler, and plan questions, yielding strong client statements like 'I can do a week' and 'one bad day doesn't have to ruin the whole thing.' Early desire/need language was thinner, and the confidence answer of '6' with fear was not explored further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk, gently reframing doubt ('slips as information, not failure') and pivoting toward hope. Fear of failure was met mainly with reassurance rather than double-sided reflections that more fully resolve ambivalence.
