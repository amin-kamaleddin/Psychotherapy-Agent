# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental throughout, with genuine, specific affirmations even during fragile moments. Affirmations follow a slightly predictable rhythm, but overall this is exemplary acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and increasingly deep, capturing both content and emotional meaning (e.g., independence, connection, staying on her feet). Depth of reflection is strong but not uniformly maximal across all turns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on reducing alcohol in every turn, with a clear arc from evocation to planning, commitment, and confidence-building. No drift into tangents while remaining client-paced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every turn invites Margaret's input, and the entire plan is built from her own ideas (soda water, fridge note, calling her son). Her comment 'I've talked myself into a plan' confirms the co-constructed style.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are consistently framed as Margaret's own, with no advice-giving or pushing; even follow-up is framed as optional. Her closing remark — 'You let me figure it out myself' — directly confirms autonomy was honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client — her reasons, methods, and definition of success. This is textbook evocation, culminating in pride and readiness.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Margaret is positioned as the expert on her own life, and the clinician contributes nothing prescriptive — only curious questions and reflections of her wisdom. The client explicitly acknowledges the successful power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions evoke Desire, Ability, Reasons, and Commitment, with Margaret's change talk escalating beautifully across the session. The clinician amplifies it and even evokes success visualization, producing the powerful 'proud, walking taller' response.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never argued with and is consistently redirected toward change-oriented content. Missed opportunities remain: the minimization in 'whatever it's crept up to' was mirrored rather than gently explored, and ambivalent language is occasionally echoed in reflections.
