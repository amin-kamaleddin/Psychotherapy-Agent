# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive with frequent, specific affirmations that frame carefulness as wisdom. Falls short of 5 because affirmations are somewhat formulaic and lean on echoing the patient's exact phrases.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, consistent reflective listening that captures both content and emotional meaning. Complex reflections on her safety values, self-compassion, and the mother comparison deepened the conversation meaningfully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session opens with clear focus and channels worries into a concrete plan, but the latter half drifts into extended self-compassion and stress exploration. These tangents are valuable but not consistently tied back to the medication transition goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operates between genuine equals, with the patient's ideas defining the plan's content and next steps. Suggestions build directly on her own stated ideas rather than directing from above.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located with the patient ('you're the expert on your own life'), advice is minimal and offered as options, and her right to take her time is explicitly validated. She leaves owning every decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocative questions draw out the patient's own reasons and solutions, especially in the first half. Later questions generate insight but not change talk about the medication transition itself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook power-sharing: the patient's three conditions become the plan, her self-generated strategies become the focus, and the psychiatrist's role stays eliciting and organizing. Contributions substantially shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions actively evoke strong commitment language ('I'm willing, I just need to feel safe,' 'I'll call tomorrow'). Occasional drift into process-coaching about flustered moments moves away from the target behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced; worries are reframed as wisdom and converted into concrete planning. Some anxiety tangents receive extended exploration without being tied back to the medication decision.
