# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental throughout, validating Isabella's frustration and praising her self-led plan despite defensiveness and skepticism.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate but shallow reflections; the counselor missed deeper emotional themes of ambivalence, family identity, and parental pride, often jumping quickly to information and planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every turn stayed focused on the target behavior, moving coherently from exploration to a concrete, actionable commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaborative stance with co-developed scripts, texts, and plans; slightly limited by the counselor supplying option menus rather than jointly brainstorming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support for a highly autonomy-driven client: framed the experiment as self-led, offered choices, and immediately affirmed her right to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Relied more on providing information and ready-made plans than drawing out Isabella's own motivations; her powerful change talk was never explicitly evoked or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed that decisions reside with Isabella (asking permission, letting her choose scripts and plan elements), though some turns were prescriptive rather than eliciting her ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Isabella generated substantial change talk, but the counselor rarely evoked or deepened it deliberately; no open questions, scaling, or elaboration of her values as a parent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with factual correction rather than double-sided reflections; the safety message felt didactic, though no harm was done.
