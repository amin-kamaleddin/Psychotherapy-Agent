# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, non-judgmental stance, but warmth was minimal and the clinical safety-focused opening lacked rapport. Missed chances to affirm Malik's honesty and efforts (e.g., 'I didn't yell').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Only one reflective statement early on; vulnerable disclosures (fear of being labeled an addict, the Dad threat) were met with scripts rather than emotional acknowledgment. Problem-solving consistently took priority over understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Stayed focused on a concrete, client-endorsed goal (phone retrieval, the 7 o'clock talk) with effective role-play practice. However, direction drifted from the referral concern (cannabis use and family conflict) without gentle steering back.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration—asked for preferences and agreed to play 'worst-version Mom'—but the session was structurally uneven, with advice handed over in expert blocks rather than co-created. Adjustments came only after client pushback.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered choices repeatedly and respected Malik's refusal of the honesty script without pressure. Fell short of full marks because advice was delivered top-down before asking what he already knew or wanted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was treated as residing in the counselor's scripts rather than in Malik; no open questions explored his values or reasons for change. All meaningful change-oriented statements emerged spontaneously and went undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Assumed an expert role, dispensing prescriptive tips, scripts, and breathing tactics. Malik's expertise was rarely acknowledged, and the counselor didn't build on client-generated direction like the role-play idea.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Malik offered valuable change talk ('maybe I'll tell her the real truth then,' 'worth it') that was never reflected, amplified, or reinforced. No questions were asked about his own reasons for change or goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Heavy sustain talk ('they don't think it's a problem, so I don't either') was neither reinforced nor actively softened. No double-sided reflections or discrepancy development between peer belonging and home conflict.
