# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, warm, nonjudgmental tone. Opener ("Thanks for coming in, Matteo — I appreciate you sharing this") and repeated affirmations convey positive regard. Accepted client's frame ("It makes sense you don't see a problem but still want to be cautious") when he denied a drinking problem. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic/repetitive and lacked deeply individualized validation of Matteo's specific values.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Solid reflective efforts (e.g., "It sounds like drinking is mainly social for you... the ankle gives you some pause"). However, reflections were often surface-level and skipped emotional content — Matteo's "once I've had a few, I stop caring" and "maybe I don't know my own limits" were met with practical planning rather than exploration of underlying vulnerability, self-doubt, or social anxiety.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol risk reduction across the entire session. Every exchange moved toward a concrete goal: rulers, brainstorming, rehearsal of declination lines, backup planning, defining success, and scheduled follow-up. Guided Matteo from vague contemplation to a specific, dated action plan (this Wednesday: water, brace, meds line, ride, Dave).

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative elements (co-constructed plan from Matteo's own ideas; "we'll troubleshoot what happened"). But uneven — much of the middle session consisted of bulleted advice lists and scripted one-liners, closer to advice-giving than equal-partner problem solving. Matteo selected from counselor-generated menus rather than jointly generating ideas.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated reinforcement of choice ("you clearly have ability if you choose to"), open-ended permission-seeking, and pressure-free acceptance of "no promises" and "I don't wanna overplan it." Fell short of 5 due to rapid-fire option menus and safety messaging feeling occasionally prescriptive; outcome ownership was never explicitly emphasized.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and success-defining questions drew out meaningful self-generated material ("maybe I don't know my own limits as well as I think"). However, much of the change effort came from counselor-supplied strategies and scripts rather than evoking Matteo's own reasons and resources; his motivations were never explicitly drawn out, and his key insight about limits was left undeveloped.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Solicited Matteo's input ("Which one or two sound easiest?") and respected his pacing. But the session frequently became option-listing and advice-heavy, with the counselor supplying nearly all strategies and taking the expert role. Matteo's own contributions shaped the plan, keeping this from scoring lower.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Active evocation via importance/confidence rulers, "What would move that number up?", and "What would count as success?" Elicited genuine change talk: "I can try the water thing Wednesday," "I got this," commitment to recruit Dave. Did not consistently deepen the talk — Matteo's insightful "maybe I don't know my own limits" was left unexplored.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue with or amplify sustain talk; reflected it neutrally ("It makes sense you don't see a problem"). However, responses were largely passive — no double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk, and sustain talk ("my friends all drink," "it'd be rude to say no") was often bypassed by moving to options. Ambivalence was sidestepped more than softened.
