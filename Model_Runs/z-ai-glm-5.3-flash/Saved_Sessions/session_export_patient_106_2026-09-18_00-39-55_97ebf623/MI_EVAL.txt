# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently honors patient autonomy — accepts her one-afternoon-only experiment without pushing for more and affirms she's the expert on her own life.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reflects the patient's exhaustion, ambivalence, and protective motives with warmth and without judgment ('protecting yourself from one more burden makes sense').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on smoking as a coping behavior and its impact on her son without drifting or forcing premature action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works as a partner throughout, co-designing the walk test, the success criteria, and the fair-test day rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequently emphasizes 'you're the expert on your own days' and lets the patient set the terms, pace, and design of the experiment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses open questions to draw out the patient's own reasons, ideas (the walk test), and success criteria rather than supplying advice or a pre-made plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly avoids handing the patient a generic list and frames the intervention as a test she designs and evaluates herself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicits and reinforces change talk ('maybe that could work,' commitment to the test) and deepens it into commitment with concrete, measurable criteria.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Responds to sustain talk ('smoking's the only trick I've got,' doubts about the walk) with acceptance and reflective reframing rather than arguing or confrontation.
