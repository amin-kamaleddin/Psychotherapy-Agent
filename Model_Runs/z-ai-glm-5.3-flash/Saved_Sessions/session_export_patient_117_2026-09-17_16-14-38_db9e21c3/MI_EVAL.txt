# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with slips normalized and reframed ('recovered' instead of 'failed'). Affirmations occasionally feel formulaic and stacked with questions rather than standalone moments of individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects Samuel's triggers and successes (mornings, work stress, cutting down at work). Reflections sometimes stay at summary level, missing deeper exploration of the emotional weight of his repeated failed quit attempts and harsh self-judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on tobacco reduction from journal review through triggers, goals, recovery planning, and accountability, culminating in a fully specified if-then plan. Every exchange advances the target without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plans are genuinely co-constructed: Samuel's own ideas (walking, wife check-ins, joking text) shaped the final plan, and he negotiated modifications comfortably. Consistent offering, checking reactions, and adjusting reflects true shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every decision framed as Samuel's choice with menus and explicit invitations to tweak. When he rejected breathing exercises and the serious text, the psychiatrist accepted without pushback, and Samuel expressed genuine ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Readiness/confidence rulers and questions drawing on Samuel's successes elicited strong client-generated motivation. The 7-to-8 exploration was cut short and the psychiatrist's own options occupied considerable space versus deeper evocation of values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Samuel consistently positioned as the expert, with his ideas directly shaping the plan. A fairly prescriptive menu of options (NRT, breathing, journal prompts) introduced early briefly shifted toward an advisory role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful open questions and scaling evoked strong commitment language ('three mornings — that I can do,' 'doing this for real this time'). Change talk isn't always deepened—the meaning of the five-cigarette goal was passed over for logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully reframed all-or-nothing thinking ('recovered' instead of 'failed') and redirected 'don't expect miracles' toward realistic progress. Some sustain talk ('not promising every single morning') passed without strategic double-sided reflection.
