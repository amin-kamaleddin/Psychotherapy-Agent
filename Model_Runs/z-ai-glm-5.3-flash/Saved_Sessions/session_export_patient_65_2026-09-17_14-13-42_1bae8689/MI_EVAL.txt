# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and acknowledges the client's feelings about being mandated. However, warmth is minimal and transactional, with affirmations limited to cursory remarks like 'Good choice.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There is limited effort to deeply understand Julian's perspective, with almost no complex reflections. Key emotional signals — anxiety about the case worker's judgment and the discouraging 'It's just a lot when I didn't... whatever' — go unaddressed as the counselor pivots to next steps.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains excellent, consistent focus on completing the community service hours by the deadline, with every turn advancing the goal. The shortfall is that direction is task-driven rather than collaboratively established, and the session ends with Julian's scheduling worry unanswered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are partial collaborative elements, such as asking about site preferences and engaging Julian in a role-play. However, the counselor generates nearly all content (drafts, scripts, checklists), and Julian primarily ratifies rather than co-creates.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Questions like 'What would be most helpful?' function as menu selections within a counselor-driven agenda rather than genuine support of client choice. The counselor controls the pace and content, providing solutions before Julian articulates his own preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor shows little interest in Julian's own motivations, values, or confidence, treating the task as purely logistical. Julian's self-motivated statements ('this feels more figured out') go unnurtured, with motivation supplied through structure rather than evoked from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, delivering pre-packaged solutions, scripts, and checklists throughout. Julian's contributions are mostly passive agreement, and his own ideas rarely shape the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The conversation is dominated by counselor-generated solutions, and there are no open questions designed to elicit Julian's own reasons for engaging. Julian's emergent change talk is self-generated and is not reinforced or deepened by the counselor.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor does not amplify sustain talk, but also fails to strategically soften it, responding to ambivalence with task lists rather than acknowledgment and reframing. Julian's final expression of overwhelm about fitting in the hours receives no counselor response at all.
