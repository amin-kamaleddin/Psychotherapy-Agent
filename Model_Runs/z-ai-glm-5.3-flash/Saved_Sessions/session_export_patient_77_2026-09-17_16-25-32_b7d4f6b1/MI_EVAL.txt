# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance, validating frustration and normalizing the plateau physiologically without criticizing meal-skipping. Warmth was somewhat clinical and structured, with limited individualized affirmation beyond plan choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected core affect, including the confidence-vs-stuckness gap and loneliness at home. Some reflections were formulaic echoes of ruler numbers, and deeper emotional layers (self-blame, feeling 'broken') were often passed over quickly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout, guiding from exploration to psychoeducation to a concrete weekly plan and follow-up. Each exchange built momentum toward change despite tangents, which were consistently tied back to the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sought permission before explaining and co-selected some plan elements, but the session often resembled consultation, with the clinician generating meals, scripts, and the grounding technique while the patient approved options. Inviting Liyana's own ideas before supplying answers would strengthen collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered menus of options and respected the patient's pacing, accepting her limit on not adding strength work. Deductions for psychoeducational blocks and the unsolicited text 'tweak' that imposed the clinician's agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and choice questions effectively elicited change talk, with confidence rising from 3 to 7. However, much motivation came from clinician-supplied solutions rather than drawing out Liyana's intrinsic reasons, values, and hopes, and her change-talk moments went unamplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered genuine choice points, but most content (meal plans, text drafts, follow-up scripts) was pre-formed and clinician-driven, and an approved text was tweaked without asking. The patient largely agreed to options rather than co-creating them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully used importance/confidence rulers, eliciting desire for change and tracking confidence growth from 3 to 6 to 7, with commitment statements ('I'll send it today'). Limitations came from offering most solutions herself rather than drawing out the patient's own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reframed the plateau as physiological rather than personal failure and countered sustain talk about family awkwardness with concrete options. Occasionally moved past discouragement quickly and addressed worry about waiting with reassurance rather than exploring her own coping capacity.
