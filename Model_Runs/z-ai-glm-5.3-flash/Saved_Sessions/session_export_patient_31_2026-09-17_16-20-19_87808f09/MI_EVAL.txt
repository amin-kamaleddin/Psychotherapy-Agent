# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth toward David, including accepting his refusal of walks and protecting his evening smoking. Warmth occasionally felt formulaic, with limited individualized affirmation of his strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that captured core meanings, such as the evening cigarette as a 'reset,' and the slip reflection visibly resonated. Emotional layers like his fear of the morning cough and partner stress were not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on smoking cessation throughout with no topic drift. Session progressed logically from exploration to a concrete plan with a scheduled follow-up call.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely partnership-based, adapting to David's feedback and jointly scheduling follow-up. Technical instruction segments were monologic, and recaps were counselor-led rather than letting David summarize.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated reinforcement of David's choice through menu options, check-in format preference, and normalizing slips. Medication instructions (patch dose, lozenge strength) were delivered prescriptively without shared decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong evocative opening and effective confidence ruler (5 to 6-7), but mid-session shifted to advice-giving mode. Missed opportunities to explore why the cough matters emotionally or let David generate his own strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for preferences at points, but mid-session became expert-driven with long instructional monologues on NRT. Key decisions like the 21 mg patch and 4 mg lozenge were prescribed rather than co-explored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited David's own reasons and confidence movement, plus spontaneous commitment language and a plan David verbalized back. Follow-up questions to deepen change talk were inconsistent, and some moments were rushed past.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk without arguing, reflecting it while pivoting toward what David wanted to change. Could have used double-sided reflections to link evening protection to future change.
