# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly affirms Maya without judgment ("Thanks for being honest — that makes sense"; "I'm here to listen without judgment and support your choices") and shows no labeling or disapproval despite Maya's disclosure that attendance was school-mandated. Warmth is somewhat efficient and checklist-like in the opening message, where multiple questions are stacked at once, slightly diluting personal regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate summaries and reflections capture Maya's core issue (social anxiety driving drinking): "It sounds like drinking mostly helps you feel less shy and makes parties easier... what bugs you most is your mom worrying." Reflections occasionally serve as transitions to the next question rather than deepening emotional understanding — the fear of awkwardness and social "failure" are touched but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus maintained on alcohol use and its function throughout; logically narrows to the drinking-triggering mechanism (shyness at parties) and builds a concrete behavioral plan (opener line, soda in hand, Jess as support, refill exit, refusal lines). The safety follow-up ("can you confirm that's been true?") was slightly redundant, and the plan was never explicitly linked back to reducing drinking frequency or the original importance rating.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely two-way interaction: the counselor asks before offering and co-constructs the party plan element by element, with Maya's input driving choices (she proposed the refill-bail framing). In several turns, the counselor presents pre-packaged option lists (refusal scripts, Jess scripts) with limited space for Maya to generate her own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary, consistent autonomy support throughout. The counselor reinforces Maya's ownership, lets her select strategies, refusal lines, and exit plans rather than prescribing, and affirms the smaller goal ("One sober convo is a win") when she declines to commit to sobriety. Maya's own framing — "one conversation sober is a start, not forever" — is respected and reflected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from Maya herself: scaling questions elicited her own importance (3) and confidence (6→7) ratings, and open prompts produced her core insight that alcohol compensates for shyness. Her spontaneous change talk ("I wouldn't need the beer to talk to someone") was not explicitly amplified or reflected, and the counselor did not ask Maya to articulate her own reasons for change in future/goals terms, relying instead on strategy menus.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maya is consistently positioned as the decision-maker ("Which feels most doable?"; "pick which you'll use first"), and her contributions genuinely shaped the session — her topic choice redirected the whole plan. However, the first message contained several stacked expert-style assessment questions (safety screening, frequency, importance scale) delivered at once, creating a brief interview-protocol feel.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of scaling questions, elicitation of what bothers Maya, and offering choice between directions — evoking substantial change talk ("I'd try the jacket one," "That actually... kind of feels doable," confidence rising 6→7). Maya's spontaneous insight about needing beer was not reflected back or deepened, and early assessment questions preceded evocation of motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maya's sustain talk ("everyone does it, it's not a big deal," "I'm not promising anything," "I don't want to promise to be sober the whole night") is acknowledged without argument, and the counselor consistently pivots toward change — affirming "One sober convo is a win." Some minimizations ("the grades are fine right now") were accepted without gentle double-sided exploration, and the low importance rating (3) was never revisited to help resolve ambivalence.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.1/5**
