# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout. However, the repeated safety screening after multiple clear denials reads as scripted and dismissive, and Dylan's values (loyalty, housing) are never affirmed as strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are minimal token acknowledgments, immediately followed by practical advice lists. Dylan's emotional experiences—fear of stigma, worry about the conduct board—are never deeply reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session focuses on legalistic case management (evidence, statements, advisers) rather than the MI target of alcohol use and conduct accountability. The drinking behavior itself was never meaningfully discussed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is dominated by clinician-delivered content with no joint problem-solving or agenda negotiation. Dylan's engagement developed despite, not because of, a collaborative structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choice is offered (picking between draft options, role-play). However, options are pre-fabricated and the overall style is prescriptive, supporting choice at the logistics level rather than ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dylan's motivations (housing, social life, roommate relationship) surface spontaneously but are never elicited or amplified by the clinician. Motivation is assumed to come from the clinician's advice rather than drawn from Dylan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The clinician assumes an expert, advisor role, delivering numbered to-do lists and drafted letters. Dylan's input shapes almost none of the direction beyond selecting pre-made options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dylan spontaneously offers change talk ('Maybe I just gotta be smarter about where I show up') which the clinician fails to reinforce or deepen. No open questions elicit his own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Dylan's repeated justifications ('Twenty-year-olds go to parties,' 'this isn't even my fault') are largely bypassed rather than met with double-sided reflections. Sustain talk goes unaddressed rather than strategically softened.
