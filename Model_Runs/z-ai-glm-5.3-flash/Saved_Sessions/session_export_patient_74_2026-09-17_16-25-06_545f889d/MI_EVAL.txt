# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental toward Linda's smoking and reluctance to quit, with warm affirmations. Praise is somewhat formulaic and often attached to the plan rather than Linda herself.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured her ambivalence and fear of failure, such as her torn feelings about role modeling versus enjoying cigarettes. Reflections were sometimes followed quickly by questions or menus, limiting deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Steady focus on smoking behavior throughout, culminating in a concrete, time-limited plan starting Monday. At times the session became checklist-driven with long plan recaps rather than deepening motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda's input shaped the plan (Monday start, garage overhang, 8:30 reminder), but the counselor repeatedly supplied detailed solution packages and scripts rather than co-constructing through open questions. The session felt more like a well-delivered intervention than a fully equal exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong permission-asking, choice menus, and explicit acceptance of Linda's decision to delay the car change, with no pressure to commit to quitting. Heavy provision of pre-made solutions slightly reduced her ownership of the plan's content.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation was partly drawn from Linda, including her kids as reasons and her daughter's accountability. However, the counselor often filled space with advice and menus instead of eliciting her own ideas, making evocation partial and inconsistent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-asking supported partnership, but the counselor frequently shifted into expert mode with detailed plan menus, scripts for family conversations, and suggested alternatives. Linda's ideas shaped the session, but solutions largely came from the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The importance ruler and confidence question evoked some commitment language ('Monday I can actually commit to'). This change talk was not strategically deepened or reflected, arising somewhat incidentally.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with acceptance and gentle redirection without reinforcement. However, there were no double-sided reflections linking her reasons to change against reasons to continue, and the car exclusion wasn't explored against her role-model values.
