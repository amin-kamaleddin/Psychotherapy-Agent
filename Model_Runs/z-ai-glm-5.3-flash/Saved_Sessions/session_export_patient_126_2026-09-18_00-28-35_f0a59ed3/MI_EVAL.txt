# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed Thomas's identity as a good husband and father, accepted his ambivalence without judgment, and treated his concerns (mates' banter) as legitimate.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The practitioner repeatedly reflected Thomas's perspective accurately, including subtle cues like the 'I suppose,' and validated feelings of embarrassment and defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation moved purposefully from dismissal to identifying drinking as the change target, with the patient generating his own plan (two-pint limit) by the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The practitioner positioned Thomas as the expert on his life and mates, asking rather than telling, and explicitly refused to label the drinking a problem.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change emerged from Thomas's own values, past success quitting smoking, and his self-generated solutions; the practitioner repeatedly framed change as 'yours to change, if anything.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled use of open questions evoked the patient's core motivations (being 'the kind of dad who shows up healthy') and drew out change talk about limits and repairing things with his wife.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A warm, curious partnership throughout; the practitioner followed Thomas's lead, asked permission implicitly through open questions, and let him reach his own conclusions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was systematically cultivated and reinforced ('something just shifted there'), progressing from 'maybe there's a pattern' to concrete commitments ('two pints next Friday').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (defensiveness, 'I feel fine,' fear of mates' teasing) was met with reflection rather than argument, allowing it to soften naturally into honesty and readiness.
