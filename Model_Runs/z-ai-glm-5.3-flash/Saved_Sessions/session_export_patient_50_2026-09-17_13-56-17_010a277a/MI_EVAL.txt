# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with consistent validation of Hana's fear. However, affirmations are somewhat generic and the warmth often pivots quickly to task instructions rather than lingering with genuine acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections are present, but the counselor largely bypasses Hana's emotional disclosures (e.g., 'the worst prediction part scares me,' 'I'm tired of carrying it around'), moving straight to CBT structure. Empathic understanding is thin and inconsistent.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on exam avoidance with a logical progression from prediction to evidence to action to accountability. Point deducted because direction favors CBT technique execution over exploring and consolidating Hana's motivation for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative elements like offering choices and incorporating preferences, but power-sharing is uneven: the counselor delivers numbered instructions, pre-formulated balanced thoughts, and scripts while Hana mostly selects among counselor-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently offers choices and validates Hana's modifications (three questions, gentle alarm wording), avoiding pressure around the exam date. Minor deduction as recommendations and scripts dominate within a counselor-defined framework.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplies content (balanced thoughts, scripts, techniques) rather than drawing out Hana's own motivations and resources, notably missing the rich recorded-presentation success story. Motivation develops mostly from the counselor's structure, a core MI deficit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks for preferences and incorporates adjustments, but the overall stance is expert-driven: structured CBT protocols and plans originate from the counselor, and Hana largely responds to the counselor's agenda rather than co-creating it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges from Hana herself and is reinforced at times, but the counselor rarely actively evokes it with open questions about meaning or past successes. Evocation is incidental rather than skillful.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not amplified and is occasionally reframed, but Hana's doubts are only briefly acknowledged rather than deeply explored or strategically redirected with double-sided reflections. The counselor even introduces anxiety-provoking content (nonrefundable appointment).
