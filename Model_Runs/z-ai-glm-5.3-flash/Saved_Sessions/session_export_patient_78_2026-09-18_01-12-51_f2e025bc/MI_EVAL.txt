# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates respect and unconditional regard, affirming Lucía's self-designed plan without moralizing. Warmth occasionally feels formulaic, and there is no explicit normalization of relapse or struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and consistent, capturing both surface content and deeper meaning (e.g., holding 'knowing it's bad' and 'needing something that's just yours' at once). Reflections throughout demonstrate genuine understanding of her ambivalence, her daughter's significance, and her emotional reality.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains crystal-clear focus on smoking cessation with each exchange building momentum toward change. The psychiatrist guides progressively from ambivalence to a concrete plan without ever losing the patient-centered pacing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The work is explicitly framed as joint ('let's figure this out together, your way'), and the plan is co-constructed from Lucía's ideas. The clinician even incorporates her request for non-judgmental follow-up into the collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is exemplary: 'I'm not going to push you anywhere,' no advice offered, and 'no promises' is reframed positively. Lucía's plan is entirely self-generated, confirming the clinician never controlled the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within, most powerfully through eliciting her daughter's significance ('I don't want to quit for a doctor. But for her? Maybe that's different'). All of Lucía's reasons are self-generated—the hallmark of expert evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly positions Lucía as the expert on her own life, and her contributions demonstrably shape the session. This is power-sharing at the highest level, with the session direction genuinely client-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is evoked strategically at every turn, from 'what would need to be true for it to fit your life' to capitalizing on the daughter-as-motivation pivot. Lucía responds with increasingly strong commitment, culminating in a self-designed plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced and is reframed skillfully (e.g., 'it wasn't about willpower, it was about fit'), and deflections are rolled with rather than resisted. Sustain talk is sometimes met with forward-moving questions rather than explicit validation before gently pivoting.
