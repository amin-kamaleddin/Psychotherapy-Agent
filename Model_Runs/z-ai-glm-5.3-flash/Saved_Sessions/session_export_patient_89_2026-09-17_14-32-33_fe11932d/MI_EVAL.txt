# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing Marcus's struggles ('That makes total sense,' 'your skepticism is totally normal'). Fell short of a 5 because affirmations were generic ('You've got this') rather than tailored to his specific strengths like his persistence and family orientation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate ('frustrating and exhausting,' feeling stuck), but reflective listening was thin overall. Deep emotional content — fear of quitting after two weeks, the personal meaning of dancing — was passed over in favor of instructions and checklists.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on increasing physical activity with no topic drift. Every response advanced the goal concretely, progressing logically from barrier exploration to a specific, scheduled commitment (7:00 walk, dance class search).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Started genuinely collaboratively and Marcus's own ideas (walking, dancing) shaped the core plan. However, the style shifted to a taskmaster/checklist format, with key decisions like the one-word tracking method coming from the counselor rather than joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Included some autonomy-supportive elements such as offering options and asking which barrier to tackle first. But the counselor frequently told rather than asked — prescriptive language like 'treat it like a nonnegotiable appointment' pressed toward the counselor's preferred plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied almost entirely on providing reasons, plans, and information instead of drawing out Marcus's own motivations. His powerful intrinsic motivators — dancing as joy, family support, his excitement — were spontaneously stated but never elicited, reflected, or expanded upon.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening invited Marcus's expertise and his contributions influenced the plan, but the bulk of the session was prescriptive with rapid-fire instructions, a phone script, and resource lists. Marcus's own ideas shaped the plan more than the counselor's questions did — power sharing was present but uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus generated substantial spontaneous change talk ('I'm kind of excited,' 'first time in years this hasn't felt impossible'), but this was mostly client-initiated. The counselor never reinforced, reflected back, or deepened these moments — for example, missing chances to explore what makes dancing feel different from the gym.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without amplifying it, gently countering doubts ('your skepticism is totally normal,' 'one slip isn't failure'). However, responses were reassurance and planning rather than strategic reframes — no double-sided reflections pairing barriers with Marcus's stated strengths, and Marcus did most of the pivoting himself.
