# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with consistent normalization of slips; the client explicitly stated she did not feel judged. However, acceptance was sometimes formulaic and emotional experiences were not deeply affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections (worry about her mum, hectic mornings), but reflective listening was sparse and the counselor quickly shifted to advice-giving. Emotionally salient moments (A1c fear, her mother's complications) were met with practical scripts rather than empathic exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on medication adherence and follow-up testing with no topic drift. The session ended with a clear, confirmed behavioral plan and follow-up structure — the strongest domain.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine collaborative elements (asking about her morning routine, letting her choose the coffee maker placement), but the counselor proposed nearly all solutions and structures. Selene was largely in an approving role rather than co-creating the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered at points, but the overall interaction had a strong prescriptive push with directive micro-questions closing most turns. Selene's ownership ('I want to do this for myself') emerged despite, not because of, explicit autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to elicit motivation from within — no open questions about her reasons for change, no confidence/importance scaling, and spontaneous change talk was not developed. Motivation was assumed and action prescribed, bypassing the core MI spirit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor largely assumed the expert role, delivering pre-packaged solutions, labels, scripts, and a step-by-step plan. Minimal inquiry into Selene's own ideas about what might work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Useful client commitments emerged ('I'll set the alarm tonight,' 'I want to do this for myself'), including notable spontaneous change talk. However, this was largely incidental — the counselor did not intentionally evoke, deepen, or reinforce change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not confronted or amplified, which is a strength. But there was little active work to soften it — no double-sided reflections or strategic reframing; ambivalence was bypassed rather than resolved.
