# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently honors the patient's perspective, including ambivalence and fear of failure, without judgment. They affirm strengths ('You've done it before') and respond warmly to moments of shame.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is deep and accurate, capturing both sides of ambivalence (health fears vs. decades-long habit, hope vs. fear of failure). The patient explicitly notes feeling understood and hopeful rather than ashamed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear focus on behavior change around smoking throughout. Each exchange moves progressively toward a concrete quit plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are genuinely open ('what would need to change first for it to feel possible?') and the plan is built entirely from the patient's own ideas—sessions, sister, quit date. The clinician never prescribes or directs.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasizes the patient's ownership ('That's you speaking from your own motivation') and the patient's self-chosen date ('not having it imposed'). Choice is offered at every juncture.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicits the patient's own reasons (money, health) and past success, drawing out change talk like 'I want this' and 'maybe sooner.' The patient generates all solutions themselves.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction feels like joint exploration, with the clinician following the patient's lead and building on her ideas. The patient confirms partnership: 'just sitting here talking like this helps.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent use of open questions and reflections that strengthen change talk and commitment language, moving from 'maybe sooner' to specific commitments ('this week, I'll ring her'). Evocation of ability ('three months is three months') is particularly effective.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of failure, embarrassment, 'waiting feels safer') is consistently met with empathic reflection rather than argument, reframed gently (waiting as self-protection). The patient progressively moves away from delay language toward action.
