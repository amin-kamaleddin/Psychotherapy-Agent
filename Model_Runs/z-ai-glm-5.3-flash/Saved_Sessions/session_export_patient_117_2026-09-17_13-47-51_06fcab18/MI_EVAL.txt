# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respect and encouragement are present ('Great work,' 'Love that you put the mints in your jacket'), but praise is formulaic and focused on task compliance. There is little individualized affirmation of Samuel as a person and no normalization of slips or his struggle with a 20-year habit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is nearly absent; vulnerable disclosures ('I don't know if I can keep it up,' fear of coworkers ribbing him) are met with coping lists and scripted comebacks rather than reflections. Almost no complex reflections appear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, unwavering focus on the lunch-smoking target throughout, with every response tied to concrete next steps (reminder, walk, journal line, text to Mike, confidence rating). No drift occurs across the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is expert-driven, with the counselor supplying strategies, scripts, and text drafts for Samuel to pick from. Genuine co-creation is absent; collaboration amounts to superficial choice-picking among counselor-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice menus ('Which feels doable?') and invitations to practice show some autonomy support. However, choices are followed by prescriptive multi-step plans, and the counselor never explicitly affirms that ownership of the change rests with Samuel.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nearly all strategies and rationales are counselor-supplied; the counselor tells rather than asks. The confidence ruler is the lone evocative move, and Samuel's insight that 'it's not about the walking at all' goes unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role, offering detailed plans, rejection scripts, and text drafts. Samuel's own contributions (walking, telling Mike) emerge spontaneously but are quickly converted into directive instructions rather than shaping the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor capitalized on strong change talk and the well-placed confidence ruler elicited 'a six... Last week I would've said three.' However, most change talk was client-generated, and the counselor did little to deepen or elaborate on it (e.g., why accountability matters so much).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoids dwelling on sustain talk and pivots to action. However, Samuel's doubts ('that's my spot,' 'not cocky, not hopeless') are met with problem-solving rather than double-sided reflections or reframes that would transform ambivalence.
