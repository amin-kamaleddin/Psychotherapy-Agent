# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth, including validating her pain as real ('You're not crazy — your pain is real'). However, affirmations of her strengths (self-observations, courage to consider hypnotherapy) were fewer than ideal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening capturing both content and emotion, echoing her alarm metaphor and validating fluctuating symptoms. Reflections were often brief, and opportunities to explore grief, cancer fear, and her son's worry were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on GI symptoms with a concrete plan, consistently tied to her stated priorities. The MI target behavior (engaging with the mind-body component) was touched on but never developed as a focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Agendas were negotiated and plans co-constructed, with the patient substantially shaping content. Information-heavy turns were delivered somewhat as expert recommendations, and joint exploration of ambivalence was limited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support throughout: repeated check-ins, options offered, hypnotherapy framed as optional, and letter edited at her direction. Her closing comment confirms she experienced genuine ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some views were elicited, but motivation came mainly from the patient rather than being drawn out deliberately. Her key change statement ('maybe I could try the hypnotherapy. Just to see.') was met with information rather than a deepening question.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist treated her contributions as expertise, adopting her alarm metaphor and co-editing the letter. The expert/advisor role (test lists, scripts) occupied most of the session, making power-sharing uneven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was largely self-generated by the patient and harvested rather than cultivated. Little deliberate evocation occurred — no open questions about what trying hypnotherapy would mean, no reflection of her key change statement, no importance or confidence exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective reframing of 'functional' softened resistance ('it's in the wires'), but sustain talk was accommodated in ways that reinforced it, e.g., removing the stress-timeline from the letter. No double-sided reflections were used to hold ambivalence while tilting toward change.
