# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, but affirmations are sparse and task-focused ('Great job') rather than person-focused. Missed chances to validate her honesty about ambivalence, her fear about Sofia, and her courage in attending reluctantly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; the counselor jumps to advice before reflecting Linda's ambivalence. Emotionally loaded moments ('She's got me there,' 'That's what scares me,' 'twenty years of habit') are met with coaching and technique rather than reflections of fear, guilt, or conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session stays tethered to smoking with a concrete next step agreed. However, the goal (quitting vs. cutting down vs. role modeling) was imposed by the counselor rather than collaboratively clarified with Linda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is expert-led: multi-step directives and a fully pre-built 7-day plan with minimal back-and-forth. The role-play rehearsal is a bright spot, but the counselor scripts Linda's lines instead of eliciting her own words; true joint problem-solving is largely absent.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive elements: offering choices ('Want a brief script...?') and accepting Linda's terms ('One week. I'm not promising anything beyond that'). Yet rapid prescriptive advice and pre-made plans subtly pressure toward the counselor's preferred actions, and her stated non-commitment to quitting is never explicitly honored as her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is supplied externally via scripts and plans rather than drawn from Linda. The counselor never asks about her reasons, hopes, or confidence, and her own change talk ('having her watching might actually help me') goes unreflected and undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, delivering prescriptive advice and a complete plan with little input from Linda. There is little inquiry into her own ideas or recognition of her expertise in her life; the session is noticeably counselor-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerges ('maybe cutting back a little wouldn't be the worst thing,' 'I could try that for a week'), but mostly via the role-play rather than deliberate evocation. Opportunities to deepen commitment language and explore importance/confidence are missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I really enjoy that,' 'I don't have plans to quit') is tolerated without argument, which is good, but never actively softened. No double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to her concern for her children; when she reasserts she isn't quitting, the counselor responds with more plan details.
