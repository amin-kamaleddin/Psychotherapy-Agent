# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, never criticizing Alexei's experimentation, with warm affirmations like 'That sounded like a real shift... That's real progress.' Affirmations were somewhat generic and there was limited explicit normalization of adolescent experimentation or his initial defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured both emotion and social context, e.g., 'You felt really scared that night — heart racing and imagining legal trouble that could derail your plans — while your friend treated it like a joke.' Reflections sometimes transitioned quickly into option menus rather than deepening emotional understanding of fears like failure or social pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained unwavering focus on alcohol use and its connection to Alexei's soccer/college/scholarship goals, with every exchange tying back to the target behavior. There was no topic drift, and the counselor guided steadily from exploration toward skill-building and commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction felt genuinely two-sided, with a co-created role-play where Alexei even directed the level of challenge ('Push harder — what would you say back?'). The counselor still tended to supply strategy menus rather than eliciting Alexei's own generated solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choice ('Which of those sounds most doable for you?', 'Want to role-play his pushback now, or would you prefer to rate your confidence?') with no advice imposed without input. Could more explicitly affirm Alexei's ownership of the change process rather than only offering structured choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from within the client via open questions, confidence rulers, and role-play, culminating in self-generated insight: 'Saying not my thing anymore actually felt good. It's not just an excuse, it's true.' A 5 would involve more consistent elicitation of the client's own solution ideas rather than counselor-generated strategy lists.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed that expertise resides with the client — options were offered as menus, Alexei selected them, and his contributions genuinely shaped the session, including the role-play emerging from his stated difficulty with in-the-moment pressure. Option menus sometimes predetermined the agenda rather than openly eliciting Alexei's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change talk — Alexei produced strong commitment language ('I don't wanna become addicted or mess up my body when soccer's the whole plan') and his confidence rose from 7 to 8 after role-play. A 5 would require more systematic deepening of change talk, such as exploring what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'm not even sure this stuff really applies to me') was acknowledged but not amplified — reflected once, then pivoted toward his values and goals with no dwell time on reasons to keep drinking. Some additional double-sided reflections or deliberate reframing of the minimizing would earn a 5.
