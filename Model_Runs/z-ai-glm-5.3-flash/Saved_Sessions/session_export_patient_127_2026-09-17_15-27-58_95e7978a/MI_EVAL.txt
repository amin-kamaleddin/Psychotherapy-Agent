# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with multiple affirmations of Timur's agency ('You showed up — that's meaningful'). Falls short of 5 due to somewhat checklist-like warmth without deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate integrative reflections captured Timur's core ambivalence well ('proud but torn', 'boxed out'). However, emotional nuances around shame, identity wounds, and fear of repeating patterns were acknowledged but not deeply explored before pivoting to action planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior throughout, with concrete commitments locked in (noon Thursday call, 11:45 alarm, 1pm check-in, tonight's text) and a specific follow-up appointment. The transition from ambivalence exploration to achievable action was well-timed and non-coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration early on, with Timur's own ideas incorporated into the plan. However, the second half became menu-driven, with the counselor generating scripts, backup options, and boredom strategies rather than true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions consistently framed as Timur's with no pressure or coercion, respecting his pace and 'not yet' stance. Falls short of 5 because heavy scaffolding of counselor-supplied scripts and options occasionally implied one right way forward.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good early elicitation of intrinsic motivations (porch mornings, self-respect), but the session shifted toward counselor-provided materials. Key moments like 'I want that for me too' were not deepened, and no confidence scaling or success visualization was used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Timur's contributions shaped the plan, but many concrete solutions (scripts, texts, urge routines) originated with the counselor. Choice-offering was menu-based rather than genuine co-authoring.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective open questions elicited meaningful change talk ('It's just a phone call, right?', 'I want that for me too'). However, the counselor did not consistently reinforce or deepen these moments — e.g., moving to logistics when Timur noted 'I've got a plan... weird feeling. Not bad' — and used no confidence rulers or elaboration questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful double-sided reflection countering 'I'm lazy' with Timur's own 'once I decide, I stick to it' identity, and effective reframing of identity-based resistance. Some sustain statements passed with only neutral acknowledgment, and problem-planning gave mild air to the against-change side.
