# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgment and respect, with affirmations like 'That takes some self-awareness.' Fell short of 5 due to occasionally formulaic affirmations and missed opportunities to normalize the client's defensiveness given the authority referral.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured both content and values, e.g., reflecting control and social standards. Reflections sometimes pivoted to questions too quickly, limiting deeper exploration of the client's fear of missing out and social pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on high-risk drinking contexts from the opening question through a concrete harm-reduction plan and follow-up. Every exchange advanced toward change with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session functioned between equal partners—the co-created follow-up plan and the client's choices (driving, group-chat sorting, text check-in) shaped the session's direction. Genuine power-sharing was evident throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was entirely client-generated, with the check-in negotiated to the client's preference and no imposed advice. The client explicitly noted the contrast with his lecture expectations: 'I've kind of got a plan I made myself.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions like 'What does staying in control look like for you?' and 'What would make it stick?' drew out the client's own strategies and values. The client's closing statement confirms motivation was evoked rather than installed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned the client as the expert ('You're the expert on your world'), and the entire plan originated from the client. The counselor recognized this as 'a plan that fits your life, not one someone handed you.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation of change talk moved the client from minimization to commitment language ('I could do that starting next weekend honestly'). The counselor reinforced the client's shift from 'later thing' to concrete steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification and reframed as strength (naming the 'cave' moment as self-awareness). A few double-sided reflections still gave meaningful airtime to minimization before pivoting.
