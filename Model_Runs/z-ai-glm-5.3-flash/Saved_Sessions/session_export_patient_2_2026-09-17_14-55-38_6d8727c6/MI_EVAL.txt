# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, respecting refusals to discuss alcohol or commit to NRT without pressure. The client explicitly noted she expected a lecture and got none.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captured her ambivalence and deeper motivations (son, health). The emotionally charged crayon moment received only a brief acknowledgment rather than a deeply attuned reflection on her fear and guilt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on evening smoking reduction throughout, with each turn progressing the plan despite deflections. The session ended with a fully specified, actionable commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaved as a partner, with the client's ideas substantially shaping the plan (delaying, tracking, tea, ashtray removal). Option menus were sometimes long and clinician-generated, and some affirmations felt generic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent autonomy support — refusals (alcohol, NRT, not quitting) were honored without pushback, and the plan was entirely her construction. Client's closing comment confirms she experienced genuine choice and control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ruler questions and open exploration successfully drew motivation from within, including her spontaneous disclosure about her son. Later questioning was somewhat logistics-focused, and the emotional core was not consistently deepened; her importance rating of 5 was never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as the expert, returning choice to her at each step. Some option menus were extensive and clinician-supplied rather than fully drawn from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk via rulers and concrete planning questions, culminating in her spontaneous deep motivation about being a mother. Missed chances to deepen and reflect the change talk back (crayon story, cancer scare).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with minimization and respected boundaries, preventing resistance from being amplified. Never used double-sided reflections to link sustain talk ('just one night') with her growing reasons for change.
