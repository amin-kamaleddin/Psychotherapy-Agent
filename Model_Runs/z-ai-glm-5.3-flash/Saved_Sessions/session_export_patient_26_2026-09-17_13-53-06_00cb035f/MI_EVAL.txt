# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and validation, and handled safety screening without pathologizing. Warmth occasionally felt formulaic, with sparse affirmations and little explicit normalization of her struggles or distrust of family support.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened with a decent reflective statement, but reflective listening largely disappeared after the first exchanges as the session shifted to advice and checklists. Key emotional content — fear of asking for help, guilt, the 'giant knot' — was rarely reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on childcare, finances, and cutting back, with an early focusing question and concrete steps tied to the client's priorities. Direction was strong but over-driven by the counselor's agendas and checklists rather than collaborative goal clarification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration existed — the counselor incorporated the client's edits to the text draft and validated her pacing idea — but the counselor generated nearly all content. True co-creation was mostly absent in a lukewarm, counselor-led dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some nods to choice ('What feels most urgent?') and acceptance of the client's one-change-at-a-time pacing, but much of the session consisted of prescribed quit rules, routines, and fixed plans. Autonomy was respected in tone but not actively fostered as genuine choices she weighs.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client's own motivations (her son, her culinary skills, her pacing logic) emerged spontaneously but were rarely deepened through open questions, scaling, or strategic reflections. The counselor supplied reasons and solutions instead of drawing motivation from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role, dispensing scripts, checklists, and quit plans with minimal inquiry into the client's own ideas. Her contributions shaped the plan only because she volunteered them, not because the counselor actively sought her expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Most change talk came spontaneously from the client, but the counselor rarely capitalized on it — no evocative or elaborative questions and almost no strategic reflection of her change talk. When she asked 'Am I making excuses?', the counselor answered directly instead of turning it back to her.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk and reframed pacing concerns as 'smart pacing,' which is a strength. However, barriers like cost and distrust were addressed through problem-solving lists rather than double-sided reflections helping the client resolve ambivalence herself.
