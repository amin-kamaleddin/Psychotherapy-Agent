# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional unconditional positive regard even amid initial deflection ('teeth, that's it'), consistently validating the patient's autonomy and reframing his failed quit attempt compassionately ('the method was what let you down'). Warmth was maintained throughout, closing with 'this chair's yours — your pace, always.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, capturing both surface content (visible staining, the failed medication trial) and subtle change talk ('someone actually listened'), plus deeper emotional material about his father's dentures. The patient explicitly confirmed feeling heard: 'you asked, you let me talk — that's a rare thing.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally good focus, honoring the client's agenda while keeping an undercurrent toward smoking ambivalence and anchoring the end in concrete dental planning. The soft-touch approach to smoking was strategically sound, but a brief closing summary linking his values (keeping his teeth, deciding for himself) to future change would have strengthened direction slightly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — plans framed as joint proposals open to rejection ('tell me if it's wrong'), with the patient's preferences (afternoons, straight talk, no lectures) visibly shaping the plan. The tone was that of two equals, and the patient responded as a partner ('fair play to you') rather than a passive recipient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with explicit statements like 'this stays your call, your pace, always' and 'no pressure, no sermon,' plus asking permission before offering direction. The patient repeatedly responded positively ('I can live with that'), confirming the stance landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from within the client via open questions ('what would it need to look like so it wasn't like last time?') rather than lecturing — especially powerful given the client's fatigue with lectures. Internal motivation emerged organically ('I might ask. Might.'). The resulting client statements show genuine, self-generated movement toward change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly conveyed that expertise resided within the client ('What would listened to look like in practice, from your side of the chair?'), and Seamus's contributions visibly shaped the session. His insight about being asked rather than lectured became the organizing theme of the interaction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and developed change talk through curious, non-pushy questions, eliciting statements like 'I'd need to want it for myself' and 'I might ask. Might.' These were reinforced with reflections and affirmations rather than pressure, moving the patient from 'teeth only' to 'I know where you are.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently pivoted away from sustain talk without dismissing it — reframing the failed quit attempt as a failed method and immediately pivoting to a change-oriented question. When the patient insisted on staying with teeth, the clinician honored that rather than re-engaging resistance, which paradoxically produced the client's softening ('maybe this time would be different').
