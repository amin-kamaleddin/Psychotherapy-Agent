# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, affirming Luis's efforts and explicitly supporting his choices without pressure. However, warmth was somewhat functional rather than deeply individualized, with little explicit validation of the emotional weight of his daughter's fear or feeling dismissed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening that landed well with a defensive client, capturing both his frustration and his self-management efforts. Reflections were occasionally formulaic and missed deeper emotional material, such as his daughter's worry and his pride in self-education.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on regular controller inhaler use from start to finish, steering back to concrete change-relevant content despite Luis's sarcasm. The session progressed logically to a specific, time-bound plan with defined success markers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was largely co-constructed — Luis chose the cue, start date, tracking method, and follow-up format. Repeated multi-item instruction lists at times gave the session a clinician-led, prescriptive feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforced Luis's ownership with no-pressure framing, offered options, and accepted his conditions (once daily, phone follow-up) without pushback. His comment that this was the first time someone asked what he'd actually do confirms the autonomy-supportive effect.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from within, eliciting Luis's own reasons (daughter's worry, night wakings, stairs) and genuine commitment language. Sometimes answered its own evocative questions by immediately supplying detailed plans, preempting deeper client-generated exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated Luis's contributions as central by adopting his vocabulary ('Oscar,' coffee maker) and incorporating his ideas into the plan, which he explicitly recognized. Some turns were advice-dense, slightly diluting the power-sharing stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through strategic questions, moving Luis from dismissive sarcasm to commitment and plan language with personal reasons. The client's closing commitment ('I'll give it a shot') was a direct result of effective evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally did not reinforce sustain talk and used effective double-sided reflections to pivot toward change. Missed opportunities to actively reframe risk minimization ('Oscar hasn't made me die yet') by linking it to his own admission of worsening nighttime symptoms.
