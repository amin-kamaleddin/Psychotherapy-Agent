# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard with no criticism of avoidance. The counselor normalizes fear and repeatedly affirms Anara's courage and practicality.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of ambivalence and somatic anxiety show strong tracking. However, reflections sometimes pivot quickly to planning, leaving emotions like fear of bad news less fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior (making the medical call) with logical progression from barriers to planning, rehearsal, and follow-up. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions are jointly made and the counselor role-plays at the client's request. But most concrete materials (scripts, texts, backups) are supplied ready-made rather than elicited from her.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently offered and pacing honored, with Anara designing her own accountability. No pressure or coercion at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is effectively drawn from the client's own commitment and insight about accountability. More systematic elicitation of her deeper intrinsic reasons for change would have strengthened this.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Anara is positioned as the expert and her input clearly shapes the session. A slight deduction because tools were pre-designed and offered rather than fully co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of change and commitment talk through first-step questions, timing prompts, and script rehearsal. Moving faster to practical planning rather than deepening her own reasons for change kept it from a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but not reinforced, with reframes and pivots to doable steps, and the client designs her own accountability. Slightly less than 5 because avoidance is sometimes sidestepped rather than worked through with double-sided reflections.
