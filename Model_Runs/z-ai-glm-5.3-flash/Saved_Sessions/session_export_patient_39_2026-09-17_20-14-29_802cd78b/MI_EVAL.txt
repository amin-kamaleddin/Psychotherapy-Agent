# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout ("I'm glad you came today, whatever you're feeling about it all"). Grief and fear normalized without judgment; pride respected. Warmth is genuine and individualized to Elias's experiences with his wife and the group.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening—e.g., "That gap between what your mind wants and what your body allows—that's a real grief." Reflections consistently land, and Elias builds on them, indicating deep understanding of both emotional and practical dimensions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior. Session progresses steadily from exploration to concrete planning (seating, ride, script) to a specific commitment ("one visit, script in hand"). Progress tracked and summarized; no drift despite brief client responses.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Fully collaborative—inclusive language ("let's work on what to say when you call"), co-constructed plain-words script tailored to Elias's preferences. The final plan is entirely co-created rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Ownership consistently reinforced: "that's your call entirely," "whenever you decide." Genuine choices offered (who makes the call); no coercive or pressuring language anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** All motivation emerges from Elias himself—beauty of the group, wife's hope, identity as "the old Elias." Values-based questions ("What do you imagine her seeing in you?") draw out meaningful change talk; closing insight is self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Active power sharing: "You know yourself best," "That's your own conclusion, not mine or the doctor's." Elias's contributions substantially shape the session direction, demonstrating the client's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Strategic, progressively building questions evoke escalating change talk—from "it was beautiful" to "I could try once more" to "I'll call this week." Reinforced with affirmations and summaries marking desire, ability, reasons, and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5 ☑
**Comments:** Sustain talk pivoted without dismissal—"what's the point" acknowledged then redirected to the beauty Elias noticed; fear normalized ("you can feel it and still be there"). Counselor amplifies Elias's own shift rather than arguing against sustain talk.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **5.0/5**

Exemplary MI session. Strong alliance from the opening reflection; sustain talk transformed into a concrete commitment with a clear follow-up plan. Particular strengths: values-based evocation, consistent attribution of motivation to the client, and collaborative problem-solving around practical barriers. Minimal growth areas—could plan more explicitly for what to do if the visit goes poorly.
