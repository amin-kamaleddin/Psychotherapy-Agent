# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine affirmation (congrats on quitting smoking, validating the sting of peer pressure) with a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, despite the patient's concern about a lecture.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of social drinking patterns and the sluggish morning with his son. Reflections were brief and the counselor pivoted quickly to problem-solving rather than deepening emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on reducing drinking at social gatherings, progressively building a concrete action plan with limit-setting, role-play, peer support, and a follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Plan was genuinely co-created — Daniel's ideas (grill duty, kid line, casual text) directly shaped the final plan, with interactive role-play exemplifying equal-partner exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently asked permission and offered choices. However, the counselor generated most options itself and steered decisively at times ('Confirm comeback to use'), slightly limiting Daniel's sense of authorship.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of importance/confidence rulers and linking past smoking-cessation success to current self-efficacy, which elicited strong change talk. Could be stronger by asking Daniel to generate strategies himself before supplying solutions like pre-pouring beers or phone reminders.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively solicited Daniel's preferences, and his contributions shaped the plan. The counselor sometimes front-loaded its own menu of options before drawing more from Daniel.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk via rulers and connected Daniel's smoking success to self-efficacy, producing strong statements ('if this works Saturday, I'll have proof it can be done'). Missed the opportunity to reflect back and deepen his strongest change statements before moving to tactics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal, and when Daniel worried the plan might fail at bigger events, the counselor reframed it as a 'solid, realistic plan' emphasizing small steps. Little explicit strategic handling (e.g., double-sided reflections) was needed since sustain talk was largely absent.
