# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, non-judgmental stance throughout, with affirmations like 'You did a great job making a concrete, doable plan.' However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic and evaluative rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections, especially the standout fear-of-failure reflection ('no wonder pushing through feels safer'). Deeper emotions—shame about sweating and fear of judgment—were not explored with emotional depth, and reflections gave way to rapid questions and problem-solving as the session progressed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus on managing pre-shoot anxiety, with each turn tied to the wedding shoot in two weeks. Minor drift into logistics, but stronger direction would involve linking the plan back to Naomi's broader values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Practical co-construction of the plan, with Naomi's contributions incorporated (plunger on checklist, choosing the casual greeting). The power balance tilted toward the counselor's solutions—Naomi noted the plan feels 'official' because the counselor wrote it.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice-offering and respect for Naomi's decisions, including declining the breathing script without pushback. Option menus and prescriptive plan structure subtly steered her, with little explicit reinforcement of her ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation came largely from structured prompts (rulers, option menus) rather than being drawn from within Naomi. Her own reasons—pride in her photography, wanting to believe in herself—were surfaced only incidentally and never developed; the frequent provision of solutions undermined the principle that change resides within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership via asking for Naomi's input ('Which sounds most possible?'), but the overall stance was expert-driven, with the counselor supplying the checklist, greeting lines, and backup plans. Naomi's own expertise (packing habits, knowledge of triggers) was acknowledged but not deeply leveraged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective use of evocative questions (importance ruler, confidence query, 'How will you know it helped?') elicited meaningful change talk ('I'd believe it a bit more,' 'at least I'll have actually tried something'). However, this change talk was rarely reflected back or deepened before moving to plans and options.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One strong double-sided reflection addressing the fear of failure, but other sustain talk ('push through,' 'breathing apps didn't work,' 'I might just rush out anyway') was passed over or met with more options rather than strategic reframing.
