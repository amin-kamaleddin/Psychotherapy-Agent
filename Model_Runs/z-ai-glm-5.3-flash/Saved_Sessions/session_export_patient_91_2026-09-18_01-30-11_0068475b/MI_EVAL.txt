# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms the patient's autonomy and efforts ('that came entirely from you,' 'that's exactly the kind of answer that sticks'). The patient feels respected and capable, explicitly stating the plan is his own.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both sides of the patient's ambivalence, e.g., 'the pills seem doable, while the checking and eating changes feel like too much all at once.' The clinician normalizes mixed feelings ('Both of those can be true at once') with warmth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stays anchored to the patient's goal of making diabetes self-management doable within his routine. Questions systematically move from identifying routine to troubleshooting barriers to defining success.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly positions the patient as the expert ('Since you know your routine best,' 'you came up with your own answers'). Every plan element originates from the patient rather than being prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are consistently offered back to the patient, and his ownership is reinforced throughout ('It's not the doctor's plan anymore. It's mine'). No directive or prescriptive language appears.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open-ended questions skillfully draw out the patient's own motivations, solutions, and change talk ('part of me already sort of wants to try'). The clinician elicits rather than supplies reasons and strategies.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The tone is genuinely collaborative—'maybe we can spot together where these small tasks could fit.' The clinician follows the patient's lead and builds on his ideas at each turn.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician notices, reflects, and reinforces emerging change talk ('I want to get this right,' 'I'd keep going') without overdoing praise. This helps the patient articulate concrete commitment to a one-week trial.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ambivalence ('it still feels like a lot every single day') is reflected and normalized rather than argued against, allowing the patient to resolve it himself by analogy to the pills. Resistance never emerges, partly because no confrontation occurs.
