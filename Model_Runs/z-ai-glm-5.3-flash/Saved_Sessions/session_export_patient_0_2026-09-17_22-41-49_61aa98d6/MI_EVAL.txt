# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout ('Both make sense to me,' 'It took some courage to say that here'), with defensiveness and minimization met with acceptance rather than confrontation. Approaches ideal unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening including several complex reflections capturing ambivalence ('one part of you wants to keep your weekends the way they are, and another part clearly doesn't want to go through that pain again'). The client repeatedly confirms and deepens these reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on meth use and its connection to the infection from the opening reflection through the closing question. Discussion stays tied to the target behavior even when exploring social dynamics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every significant turn in the conversation originates from the client's own ideas (knowing what's in the substance, skipping weekends), with the counselor building on them. No expert-driven advice at any point.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces client ownership of choices ('what feels like yours to decide right now?', 'in whatever way feels doable for you'), with no pressure or directives. The client confirms the plan as self-authored: 'That's not quitting. But it's... mine.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation emerges from the client himself through open questions—every key insight (connecting meth to the infection, recognizing cover stories) is elicited rather than supplied. Exemplifies that change resides within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is repeatedly located within the client, whose ideas become the working plan. Power sharing is active and sustained, not superficial.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully and consistently evokes change talk, with the client progressing from minimal ('maybe be careful') to substantial change talk ('skipping some weekends') and insight. Demonstrates advanced skill in selectively reinforcing change talk ('There it is, though...') over sustain talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without reinforcing it and pivots effectively ('without overhauling your life, what do you think would actually help?'). Minor area for improvement: sustain talk was occasionally given extended airtime (e.g., the excuses discussion) before redirection.
