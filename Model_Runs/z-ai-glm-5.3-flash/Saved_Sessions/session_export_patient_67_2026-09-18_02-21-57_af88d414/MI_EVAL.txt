# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms Kenji's autonomy and self-knowledge ('You're the expert on your own life'). Language like 'that's not stupid; it makes sense' normalizes his struggles without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections capture both surface content and underlying ambivalence ('part of you is fine most days, and part of you is asking, quietly'). Repeatedly names the emotional weight of living alone and using alcohol to quiet his mind.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Each response builds toward the client's own change talk, moving from ambivalence to a concrete, self-generated plan. The conversation stays anchored to his stated concerns (liver numbers, evenings alone, stress).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly positions Kenji as the designer of his plan ('you've just designed your own plan here, not me'). Questions are open and invitational rather than prescriptive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every commitment (walking, Wednesday timing, headphones) comes from the client, with the clinician reinforcing his ownership ('a smart, simple yardstick you built yourself'). No advice is imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions elicit change talk: 'what do you imagine would worry you most?', 'what might give you even a little of that quiet?'. The client articulates his own reasons and hope ('maybe it proves to me I can do stuff differently').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange is a genuine back-and-forth, with the clinician following the client's lead and co-constructing the plan step by step. The client does the problem-solving while the clinician mirrors and amplifies it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rich change talk is drawn out and consolidated ('the drink isn't really fixing anything', 'I could give it a shot'). The clinician highlights it at each turn ('Listen to what you just did').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it sounds lame', 'Wednesday-night-me is lazier') is met with normalization and reframing rather than argument. The clinician validates the function of drinking while opening space for alternatives.
