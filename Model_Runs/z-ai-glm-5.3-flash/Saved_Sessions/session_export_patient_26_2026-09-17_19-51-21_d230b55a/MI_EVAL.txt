# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with no labeling or criticism, even when the client disclosed smoking and drinking struggles. The client explicitly confirmed feeling unjudged ('Thank you—for not making me feel judged about any of it').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate complex reflections, such as 'hope with your eyes open' and 'a mom who wants to do this with dignity, not handouts,' that tracked both content and emotional nuance. Reflections repeatedly landed, as shown by client responses like 'That actually hits different.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, purposeful focus on childcare instability, stress, and smoking/drinking, moving progressively from need to solution to commitment and future vision. The closing summary crystallized the plan while keeping all threads connected to change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was co-created from the client's own ideas and the summary exchange showed genuine power-sharing. However, the counselor occasionally structured the agenda unilaterally without explicit negotiation or a check-in like 'Is this what you'd like to focus on today?'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently located power and capability within the client ('That's you doing this, not anyone handing it to you') without pressure or credit-taking. The closing 'I'll be here... whatever happens' reinforced agency, confirmed by the client's own 'I'll figure out the next move.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every meaningful motivation originated from the client—trade idea, one-Thursday scope, Head Start—and evocative questions drew out her values and resources. No advice-giving or motivational preaching occurred, producing strong desire, need, and commitment talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the client as the expert, with affirmations crediting her own contributions and solutions drawn entirely from her ideas. One slightly directive moment (suggesting she mention the dinner idea) edged toward leading.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and deepened change talk through open questions, amplifying commitment ('What makes it a maybe instead of a yes?'), and future-vision prompts. The strategic progression from desire to plan to commitment ('Thursday. I'm gonna try it.') was excellent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reframed sustain talk as self-protective wisdom ('hope with your eyes open') rather than reinforcing it. One point lost because an early reflection allowed elaboration of money/trust fears ('back to square one') before redirecting toward agency.
