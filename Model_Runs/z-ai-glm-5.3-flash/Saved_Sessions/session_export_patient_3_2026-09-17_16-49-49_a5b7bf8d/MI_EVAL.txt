# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, with affirmations of Aisha's courage and progress. However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic and repeated, with no deep individualized acceptance when she disclosed putting everyone else first.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture both change talk and barriers, with good perspective-taking of her family-first pattern. Limited exploration of the emotional core of her body-image distress, staying mostly at the behavioral/logistical level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, clear focus on the target behavior with a systematic transition from motivation to a concrete plan (Tuesday 7:40 walk, alarm, shoes, backup, sister text, family boundary, Wednesday check-in). Every turn advances toward Aisha's stated goal with no drift — a model of MI direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frames the work as joint from the start, and decisions like the family boundary line were co-constructed. The power balance is slightly uneven — the counselor drives the structure and proposes most concrete solutions while Aisha primarily approves and refines.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every decision is framed as Aisha's own, suggestions are optional, and her declining of polished texts is accepted immediately. Aisha explicitly experiences agency ('my walk actually matters'), and the counselor never pressures or takes control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Evocative questions (confidence scaling, upward-arrow, closing 'win' question) elicited rich client-generated motivation. Evocation centers on planning and confidence rather than deeper values and emotional meaning, and solutions are occasionally supplied rather than elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly positions Aisha as the expert, and her choices shaped the plan substantially (walk, timing, alarm, sister text, boundary approach). Short of 5 because the counselor tends to supply options and unsolicited suggestions rather than primarily drawing them from Aisha.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and grew measurably (confidence 6→7, hope and pride expressed). Change talk is elicited around planning logistics rather than deeper values or desire statements, and the most meaningful change talk isn't deeply amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers like exhaustion and guilt were handled well by pairing them with solutions and reframing all-or-nothing thinking. The session occasionally lingers on barrier discussion without consistently pivoting back to Aisha's own reasons and strengths for change.
