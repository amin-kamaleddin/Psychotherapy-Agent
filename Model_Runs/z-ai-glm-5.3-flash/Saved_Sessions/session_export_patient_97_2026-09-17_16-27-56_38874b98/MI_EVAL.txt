# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with validation of the client's wariness of mandated sessions. However, affirmations are sparse and generic, missing chances to affirm deeper strengths like courage and resilience in facing re-entry with a record.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, but empathy faded as the session became task-focused. The counselor missed opportunities to reflect the emotional weight of rejections, shame about his record, and guardedness around disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical focus on employment progressing from goal identification to resume, script practice, and weekly goals. Points off because the connection to the deeper MI target (reducing recidivism, family values) was only briefly touched and never explicitly linked.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client input shaped some deliverables, but the counselor delivered long monologues with finished resumes, scripts, and bulleted plans, leaving Mateo largely in a passive recipient role. Power-sharing was partial rather than fully mutual.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language with repeated choice menus, goal negotiation (accepting 2-3 applications over 5), and honoring the client's disclosure preference. Slightly reduced by a tendency to provide solutions and drafts quickly rather than inviting client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely assumed rather than evoked; most elicited content was logistical rather than motivational. The client's strongest motivational statements arose spontaneously and were not reflected back or expanded, making the session feel more like vocational case management than MI.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor sought preferences and adapted to choices, but the middle of the session shifted to an expert-driven checklist approach with unilateral drafting. Client contributions did not substantially reshape the session beyond initial topic choice.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine change talk emerged ('that'd be a start, right?', 'trying to do it different') but was left underdeveloped. No amplifying reflections, scaling questions, or exploration of what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('Five's a lot', 'I've been avoiding most of that') was not reinforced, and realistic goal negotiation was respectful. However, sustain talk was mostly bypassed rather than strategically softened through double-sided reflections or discrepancy.
