# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, affirming Amira's effort ('You showed up today — that matters') and responding to her relapse without criticism. Warmth was genuine but somewhat procedural; deeper individualized positive regard and normalization of her shame would elevate the score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly reflected Amira's emotional state accurately, including her numbness, shame, loneliness, and ambivalence about change. Reflections sometimes shifted too quickly into problem-solving and checklists rather than lingering on the loneliness she named as 'the real thing underneath.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus on reducing drug use, culminating in a concrete, specific plan (30-minute shower delay, Wednesday, checklist). The underlying loneliness Amira identified as the driver was deferred to a future session rather than briefly anchored in the treatment agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely between equal partners, with the plan co-constructed in Amira's own words ('just thirty minutes') and negotiated rather than imposed. The psychiatrist tended to generate option menus themselves rather than inviting Amira to brainstorm first, giving planning a slightly clinician-driven structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength: consistently sought permission, offered menu options, honored her limits ('It's okay not to promise more'), and reinforced her ownership ('your plan keeps control in your hands'). No coercive or pressuring language appeared anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicited change talk through scaled questions and Amira's self-generated coping ideas, but evocation was inconsistent — the psychiatrist often filled space with pre-packaged options rather than drawing out her reasons and hopes. Her 'I showed up today' affirmation and 3/10 confidence rating went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently treated Amira as the expert on her own life, eliciting her ideas and building the plan from her existing coping strategy (showers). However, supplying pre-made option lists slightly pre-empted her own brainstorming, and her expertise was implied rather than explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk via scaled questions and invitations to define 'use less,' yielding Amira's own plan and commitment language ('I'll try Wednesday'). Missed opportunities included not deepening the change talk after 'I showed up today, right?' and not exploring what success would feel like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged Amira's doubt without arguing against it, which is appropriate. However, sustain talk was often reflected back without a strategic pivot toward change, and no double-sided reflections paired her doubt with her demonstrated motivation (showing up, generating the shower idea herself).
