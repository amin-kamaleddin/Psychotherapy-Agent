# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout ('you're welcome exactly as you are, whether that carton thing happens or not'). No judgment or pressure, even when the client tested limits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured both sides of ambivalence and nuanced autonomy needs. Reflections consistently landed, evidenced by the client progressively opening up.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus with deliberate directional scaffolding from dismissiveness to a concrete first step. Withheld a 5 only because the session path was largely client-led rather than more actively steering toward goal clarification, though this matched the client's resistance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine exchange between equals: inclusive language, asking what felt right to explore, and letting the client end on her terms. The playful 'zero credit' negotiation showed authentic equality.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional autonomy support explicitly tailored to her resistance to lectures—pace and timing fully hers, no interventions suggested. Even the closing respected her request not to 'make it a whole thing.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key question drew out the client's own motivations (identity, cough, daughter) rather than supplying reasons to quit. The client's own surprise—'now look at me'—demonstrates successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert from the opening and credited all insights to her. Her stated conditions shaped the session's focus throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation and amplification produced rich client-generated change talk ('maybe I'll think about that carton thing this week'). Reinforced commitment-adjacent language: 'That's you talking, not me.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it, using double-sided reflections pairing doubt with past success. Sustain talk was transformed into hope ('maybe next time it's three').
