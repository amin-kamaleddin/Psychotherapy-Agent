# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, explicitly establishing a safe space and repeatedly affirming Jamal's insights without criticizing his skepticism or resistance. Jamal's closing comment ('Thanks... for not just lecturing me like everyone else') confirms unconditional positive regard was effectively communicated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening that captured Jamal's frustration and worry, but reflections were sometimes brief or skipped in favor of scaling questions. Deeper emotional themes—his autonomy struggle and the self-proof meaning of 'one good morning'—were only implicitly rather than fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained an exceptionally clear focus on school punctuality via its upstream driver (evening phone use), with every question serving the change goal. The session progressed logically from problem awareness to a concrete, implementable plan with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently collaborated, sought Jamal's input before offering ideas, and co-constructed the if-then plan using his own words. However, batching multiple suggestions and questions into single turns shifted toward counselor-led pacing, prompting Jamal's mild pushback about being asked 'a lot of details.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supported autonomy by offering genuine choice, accepting Jamal's decline of paper planning without pressure, and framing slips as a reset rather than failure. Jamal designed and owned the entire plan, with the counselor consistently reinforcing his ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within—Jamal articulated his own reasons, coping strategies, and definition of success, with confidence rising from 5 to 7.5 through elicitation. However, the counselor never explicitly evoked deeper intrinsic benefits of punctuality beyond escaping nagging, leaving some motivation tied to external pressure.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Jamal as the expert, and his own solutions drove the entire plan. At times rapid-fire questions slightly pressured the interaction, with Jamal pushing back ('You're asking a lot of details, man'), suggesting moments where the counselor's agenda outran the client's pace.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and deepened change talk through importance/confidence scaling, eliciting powerful intrinsic statements ('Proving I can actually do it') and letting confidence rise organically from 5 to 7.5. Change talk was consistently reflected and built upon rather than left hanging.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk briefly (app limits being easy to bypass, mom's nagging) and pivoted quickly to problem-solving rather than reinforcing resistance. A missed opportunity remained in strategically reframing the nagging/authority-friction theme toward Jamal's own reasons for change.
