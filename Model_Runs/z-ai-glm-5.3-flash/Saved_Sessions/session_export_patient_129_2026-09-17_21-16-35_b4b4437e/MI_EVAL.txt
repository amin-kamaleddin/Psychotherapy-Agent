# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout, even when Tyler deflected blame onto his mother or defended his crew. Affirmations like 'That's an honest thing to notice' and 'That's yours, not your mom's, not mine' honored his worth and autonomy without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening captured both sides of Tyler's ambivalence, including reframing his defensiveness into a value of control. Tyler explicitly confirmed the impact: 'I never actually thought about it that way until now.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange tied back to the target behavior (substance use within the peer group) while respecting Tyler's frame. The session progressed logically from belonging, to the incident, to refusal strategies, to a concrete plan with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was built entirely from Tyler's own observations and ideas, with questions framed as inquiries into his expertise. Tyler's comment that this was 'the first useful thing I've gotten outta coming here' reflects the success of this stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly reinforced, with Tyler framing change as 'my call — not my mom making me, not being scared into it — me deciding.' Choice was never pressured, and the psychiatrist celebrated Tyler's ownership of both the plan and potential setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was evoked rather than instilled through questions like 'what's the part you'd most want to keep?' Tyler articulated his own values, plan, and desire to change ('I actually kind of want to try it').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly positioned Tyler as the expert ('You know your crew better than I ever will'), and every plan element originated from Tyler. This reflects the highest level of power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions moved Tyler from precontemplation to commitment language, eliciting his own plan, ability statements, and desire to try it. Each instance of change talk was reinforced ('That came from you').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally not amplified, with effective double-sided reflections guiding toward change. A slight deduction as a few reflections briefly restated sustain-side content (e.g., 'territory you didn't plan for') before pivoting.
