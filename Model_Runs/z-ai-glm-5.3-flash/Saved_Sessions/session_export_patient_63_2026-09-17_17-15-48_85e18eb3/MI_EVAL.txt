# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone toward a court-mandated client, with affirmations like 'That's a really honest place to be.' Warmth at times felt formulaic, and there was little normalization or affirmation of worth independent of progress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected Jordan's ambivalence and core dilemma, including triggers and prior success. However, deeper emotional content — fear of losing peer belonging and the loneliness of idle nights — was left unexplored, and reflections sometimes parroted rather than added meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on the target behavior from opening framing through escalation planning, tying every exchange back to concrete change. No topic drift across the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session was predominantly a joint endeavor — drafts were co-edited and Jordan altered counselor suggestions. Fell short of full partnership because several turns began with counselor-generated menus, with Jordan choosing among rather than initiating options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Jordan's ownership through choices ('Which of these feels doable?', 'Want to tweak any of these?'), with his own selections driving the final plan. Jordan's closing statement — 'First time I've had one that isn't just try harder' — confirms the plan felt self-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions and evocative inquiries effectively drew out Jordan's own reasons, past success, and commitment language, driving a confidence rise from 4 to 6. However, counselor-supplied lists of steps and tactics meant some motivation and strategy were provided rather than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Jordan's expertise as central, and most plan elements originated from him (Marcus as contact, sticky note, refusal lines). At times steered with pre-packaged option menus, which mildly constrained Jordan's contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change talk — scaling surfaced importance and desire, and questions about past resistance elicited a strong success example and commitment ('I'll send the Marcus text tonight'). Change talk was partly channeled into counselor-generated option lists rather than open evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk (peer pressure, cravings, fear of seeming superior) without reinforcing it, pivoting to confidence-building questions. A top score would require more strategic reframing, such as double-sided reflections linking sustain talk to Jordan's values.
