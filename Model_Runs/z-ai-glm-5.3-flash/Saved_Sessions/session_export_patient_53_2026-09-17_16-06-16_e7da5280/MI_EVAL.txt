# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, unconditional positive regard, and honoring the client's pace ('It's okay if you only manage the porch'); she explicitly felt accepted. Falls short of 5 due to formulaic, repetitive summaries and lack of explicit affirmations of her personal strengths beyond her plan choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections of ambivalence and fear of being watched, but responses were plan-heavy rather than emotionally exploratory. Deeper feelings — shame about past non-follow-through and neighbors' judgment — were acknowledged only briefly without exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on community engagement with no topic drift, advancing each turn toward a concrete first step. Momentum was counselor-driven and behavioral focus slightly outpaced her contemplation-stage readiness, side-stepping deeper concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine permission-seeking and client-sourced plan details (11am, porch fallback, 4pm text), but the counselor set the agenda and generated nearly all strategies. Isabella functioned as a selector of pre-packaged options rather than an equal co-creator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly and repeatedly supported — pacing statements, choice offerings, and asking permission before suggestions. The density of pre-formed options subtly framed the counselor as plan architect, and reassurance occasionally substituted for reinforcing her own ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good evocative opening questions drew initial change talk, but the session quickly shifted to supplying solutions. Her own reasons for connection and values were never developed, leaving motivation inferred from logistics rather than drawn from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited choice and adapted the plan to her input, but the stance leaned expert-driven with nearly all ideas supplied by the counselor. Little explicit elicitation of her own solutions before offering options limited true power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change talk, developing vague possibility into concrete commitment ('eleven o'clock... I can do ten minutes') and building on existing behavior. Fell short of 5 because change talk was channeled into logistics rather than deeper reasons and values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification and skillfully redirected, including a strong double-sided reflection and normalization of worries. Occasionally restating her doubts multiple times slightly reinforced attention to the negative, though never arguing against it.
