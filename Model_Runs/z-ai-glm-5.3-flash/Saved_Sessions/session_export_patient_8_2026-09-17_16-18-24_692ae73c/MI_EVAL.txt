# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone and validated Amina's fear of isolation, but affirmations were somewhat formulaic and praised the plan more than conveying unconditional regard for Amina as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured her ambivalence (e.g., 'You're torn — it feels normal, but it's costing sleep and grades'), but they were brief and quickly pivoted to questions or suggestions, missing deeper exploration of her shame and social fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every turn tied back to the target behavior, moving from the opening question through concrete planning (5pm/8pm windows, timer, flatmate accountability) to a dated Friday follow-up with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly checked in before advancing and jointly shaped key decisions (window times, message wording, follow-up time), but relied on pre-packaged option menus rather than co-creating from Amina's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly affirmed Amina's choice throughout ('I'll support whatever you choose'), accepted her hesitations without pressure, and framed slips as normal, reinforcing ownership at every decision point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence rulers and open questions elicited her motivations and insights (sleep, grades, accountability), but the plan's content was largely counselor-generated with little exploration of her deeper values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina chose her windows, edited the flatmate message, and set the follow-up time, but the counselor frequently supplied the ideas (timers, grayscale, auto-reply, alarm clock), leaving her selecting from a menu rather than generating her own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence rulers effectively tracked Amina's progression from 4 to 8/10, and questions like 'What might get in the way?' elicited concrete mobilizing change talk ('Tonight, actually... I'll set the app timer'). Occasionally the counselor supplied the change ideas rather than having Amina generate them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well with a strong double-sided reflection and brief redirects toward doable steps, though there was little explicit reframing (e.g., her fear that seeking help means 'admitting it's a problem' was normalized but not reframed).
