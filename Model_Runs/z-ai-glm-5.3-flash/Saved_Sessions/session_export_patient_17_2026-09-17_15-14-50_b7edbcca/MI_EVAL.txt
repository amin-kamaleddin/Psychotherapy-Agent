# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validating Anisa's shame and affirming her bravery in choosing a quit date. Affirmations were somewhat generic, with little individually tailored positive regard beyond the bravery comment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening was shown, including an accurate summary of her readiness/confidence discrepancy and mirroring her language ('not policing you'). Reflections were occasionally dense multi-part bundles, and deeper emotional nuance — fear of repeated failure and the 'leper' stigma — was not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation throughout, progressing logically from exploring past attempts to a concrete quit date, lapse plan, rehearsed script, and written checklist. There was no topic drift, and the session ended with a dated, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were largely co-constructed, with Anisa editing the lapse plan into her own words and asking for the role-play. However, the initial planning turn presented a pre-formatted bundle of strategies rather than genuinely co-generating options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly framed support around choice and respected Anisa's modifications to the script and lapse plan. The rapid-fire menu of recommendations had a prescriptive flavor, with less explicit reinforcement that she owns the outcome even in the event of setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers, confidence-building questions, and small-step questions effectively drew out strong client motivation ('I actually feel ready. Properly ready.'). The counselor also supplied significant expert content rather than consistently eliciting solutions, and did not develop her hope ('for her, and for me') further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Anisa was repeatedly positioned as the expert, and her contributions substantially shaped the plan, including her own lapse-plan wording. Some directive advice lists delivered rapidly tilted toward an expert stance, and agenda-setting was only partially negotiated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic questions and rulers skillfully evoked strong change talk, including a concrete quit date and 'properly ready' commitment. At times the counselor answered evocation questions with suggestion lists instead of continuing to draw out Anisa's own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced and was occasionally reframed positively, but extended discussion of lapse and failure scenarios implicitly amplified fear of failure. Limited use of double-sided reflections, and her fear of 'chickening out' was met with advice rather than a strategic redirect.
