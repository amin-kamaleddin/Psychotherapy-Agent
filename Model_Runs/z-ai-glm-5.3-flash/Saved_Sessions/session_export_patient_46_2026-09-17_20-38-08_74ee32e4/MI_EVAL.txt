# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and normalizes ambivalence without any judgment or disapproval, even when the patient minimizes. Affirmations are genuine and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and emotionally attuned, including complex double-sided reflections that capture fear and minimization beneath the surface. The patient repeatedly responds with elaboration and deeper insight, showing the reflections landed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well focused on weekend alcohol use and progresses from ambivalence to concrete planning and confidence assessment. Minor redundancy (a verbatim repeat of the patient's words and a near-duplicate question the patient noticed as 'going in circles') keeps it from a 5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functions between equals, with the counselor asking rather than telling and co-constructing the plan entirely from the patient's own observations. No expert-driven or authoritarian moves appear.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor actively fosters choice, frames change as an experiment the patient controls, and gives no advice or pressure anywhere. The closing credit — 'That's yours, not anyone lecturing you' — crystallizes this stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within the patient through probing questions, the ideal-weekend invitation, and a confidence ruler with exploration. The change plan is driven entirely by the patient's own reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly and explicitly positions the patient as the expert on his own life, and every session direction comes from the patient's ideas. Power-sharing is demonstrated explicitly and repeatedly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoke desire, ability, reasons, and commitment language ('I'm kinda tired of getting in trouble,' 'I could probably actually pull it off'), plus a confidence rating of 6/10 with concrete support. Change talk is reinforced through timely affirmation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (minimization, fear of peer judgment) is acknowledged empathically and gently redirected toward change without arguing or amplifying. The Marcus/Gatorade example is leveraged as disconfirming evidence the patient discovered himself.
