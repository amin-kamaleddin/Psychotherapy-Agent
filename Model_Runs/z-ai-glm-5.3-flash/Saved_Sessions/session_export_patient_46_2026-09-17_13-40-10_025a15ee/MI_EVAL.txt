# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor maintains a nonjudgmental stance and offers a safe space, but warmth is formulaic with no individualized affirmations of Ethan's strengths. Repeating the safety script after Ethan protested feeling talked down to undermined the stated respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal reflective listening; key emotional content (fear of being kicked off the team, ambivalence about the issue being 'overblown') was never reflected or explored. The counselor also misassumed Ethan 'reached out,' which he immediately corrected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A relative strength — the counselor keeps consistent focus on alcohol use and establishes a concrete harm-reduction plan with a follow-up after Marcus's party. Deductions for jumping to solutions before resolving ambivalence and repetitive closings that wasted momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative elements exist (asking preferences, letting Ethan pick focus items, agreeing on follow-up), but the session tilts expert-driven with a long unprompted list of tips and rapid-fire multi-question turns resembling a checklist rather than joint exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine choice offered between harm reduction and a plan, framed as something to 'try and see.' However, advice delivery was directive and the plan was repeated four times despite Ethan's protest, showing little trust in his autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is treated as coming from the counselor's information rather than from Ethan. His own ideas (eating first, sticking near Dev, hating feeling like garbage) were never built upon, and pre-made decline scripts were supplied instead of evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ethan was given choice in shaping the plan, but the counselor quickly assumed the expert role with a dense lecture. When Ethan raised a real barrier (friends clowning him), the solution was supplied rather than drawn from him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ethan spontaneously offered change talk (disliking feeling sick, worry about the team, willingness to try harm reduction) but the counselor never reflected or deepened it. The importance ruler answer of 3/10 was accepted without exploring why it isn't a 0.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ethan's sustain talk ('overblown,' 'just being a teenager,' 'bingeing sounds dramatic') was left unanswered or glossed over. No double-sided reflections or discrepancy work was used; the counselor pivoted straight to advice.
