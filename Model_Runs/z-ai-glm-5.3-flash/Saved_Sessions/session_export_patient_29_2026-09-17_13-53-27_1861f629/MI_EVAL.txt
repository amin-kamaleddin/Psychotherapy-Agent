# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a neutral, respectful stance and opens warmly, but warmth fades into checklist-style directives. No affirmations of David's honesty, prior quit attempts, or valuable insights.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is nearly absent; emotionally rich patient disclosures get tactic lists or generic acknowledgments in response. The counselor never explores the meaning of the smoking ritual or David's fear of failing again.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays firmly on smoking cessation with concrete outcomes (quit date set, prescriber message drafted, plan summarized). Held at 4 because direction is achieved through information delivery rather than guiding along the patient's stated priorities.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Monologue-style advice blocks and unilateral creation of scripts and plans; no agenda negotiation. Even the well-received role-play involved counselor-scripted replies rather than David generating his own.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Consistently directive ('pick one now 7–14 days out', 'Practice this twice a day'), with choice framed as selecting from the counselor's menu. David's articulated ownership at the end goes unaffirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Standard evocative questions are entirely absent; motivation and solutions are supplied rather than elicited. David's own motivations surface spontaneously but are never drawn out or built upon.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, prescribing medications, dictating quit-date timing, and supplying scripts before exploring David's ideas. When David explicitly asks for the counselor's opinion, he gets a directive recommendation instead of exploration of his own thinking.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David generates abundant spontaneous change talk that goes unevoked and unrewarded. A prime evocation opportunity ('what do you think, pick one now or wait?') is met with advice rather than drawing out his reasoning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Significant sustain talk about triggers, permission-giving thoughts, and fear of failure is buried under tactic lists rather than worked through. No double-sided reflections or use of David's own optimism to pivot toward change.
