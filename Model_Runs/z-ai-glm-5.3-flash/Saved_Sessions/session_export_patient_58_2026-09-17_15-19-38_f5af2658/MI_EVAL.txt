# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with consistent positive regard and practical affirmations. Affirmations were somewhat generic, missing chances to specifically affirm Jamal's honesty and self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate reflections captured ambivalence and the plan's difficulty, but most reflections summarized logistics rather than emotions. Missed opportunities included Jamal's frustration with nagging, his guardedness, and his desire for 'peace and quiet.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on punctuality with no drift, building progressively toward a concrete action plan including a 4/5 success criterion. Slightly mechanical direction driven by rapid-fire closed task questions rather than the client's own values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal's input was solicited and incorporated, and he chose his own strategies. But stacked multiple questions each turn and prescriptive tip menus made the interaction feel like a coach running a protocol rather than two-way dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jamal's ownership was consistently emphasized — he set his own success criterion and reward, with the counselor positioned as a troubleshooter. Fell short of 5 due to a slight push for immediate commitment and no explicit affirmation of his right to decide.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor elicited Jamal's own reasons, plan, and confidence ratings. Evocation stayed superficial — deeper exploration of what independence from his mom's nagging meant to him, and of 'For me,' was missed in favor of logistics and repeated confidence scaling.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal generated most strategies himself and was invited to choose options, showing collaborative elements. However, the counselor often slipped into checklist-expert mode with stacked questions and prescriptive menus rather than genuine co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk was elicited ('I have to start getting to school on time. For me,' committing to act tonight) and confidence rose from 6 to 8. However, change talk was not deeply elaborated, and confidence questions became repetitive rather than strategic.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (nagging, phone temptation, weekend derailment) was acknowledged without reinforcement and lightly reframed. No double-sided reflections were used, and repeated invitations to name barriers kept focus on problems rather than motivation.
