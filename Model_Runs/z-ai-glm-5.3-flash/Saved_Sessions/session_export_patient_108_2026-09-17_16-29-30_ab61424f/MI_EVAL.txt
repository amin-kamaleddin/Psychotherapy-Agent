# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone that validated Oliver's ambivalence and guilt as legitimate. Few explicit affirmations of his character strengths (e.g., his generosity, seven years of maintaining the flat) kept this from a higher score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections, such as naming the guilt barrier and normalizing fear of caving. But poignant emotional disclosures — Oliver's sadness about losing companionship, his lifelong self-criticism, and loneliness — were largely passed over in favor of logistical next steps.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained exceptionally tight focus on the target behavior — asking Danny to move out — across the entire session with zero topic drift. Every turn advanced the goal through purposeful transitions from scaling to scripting to rehearsal to planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Oliver's choices shaped specifics like timing, delivery method, and dates, and the psychiatrist asked before moving forward. But the counselor produced nearly all the substantive content (scripts, plans, contingencies) while Oliver mainly approved and dated it — power sharing was uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered meaningful choices (which lines feel most like you, text vs. face-to-face, setting dates) and let Oliver shape key details. However, choice was offered within a counselor-defined menu of pre-written scripts, subtly constraining Oliver's space to author his own words.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Two scaling questions successfully drew out importance and confidence, and Oliver generated meaningful change talk. However, the default mode was providing ready-made solutions rather than exploring Oliver's own reasons, ideas, and words.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Frequently assumed the expert role, supplying fully drafted scripts, exact texts, and contingency language, with Oliver only asked 'want any tweaks?'. Oliver's expertise — his knowledge of Danny and his own psychology — was never explicitly acknowledged as the driving resource.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions effectively evoked strong change talk (importance 8–9, confidence 7, 'I've held this in too long'), and some mobilizing statements were reflected back. However, change talk was not consistently deepened — key statements like 'I'm tired of being weak' were never reflected or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced and occasionally reframed, but it was often bypassed rather than strategically softened. Guilt and ambivalence questions like 'is that a cop-out?' were met with plan-building rather than double-sided reflections helping Oliver resolve ambivalence himself.
