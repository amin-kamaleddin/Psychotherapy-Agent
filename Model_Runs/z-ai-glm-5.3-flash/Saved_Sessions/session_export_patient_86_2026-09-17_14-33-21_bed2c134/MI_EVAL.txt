# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Consistent respect and non-judgment despite client's guarded, sarcastic presentation ("Thanks for that honesty," "Tell me to drop it and we'll just chat"). Honored the client's no-lecture request throughout. Affirmations somewhat generic; lacked deeply personalized statements of unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening ("I hear frustration and defensiveness... scarier health stuff than stained teeth"). Sensitive to client's pace. Reflections sometimes brief; did not deeply explore emotional meaning behind fatalism or attachment to smoking as "an old mate."

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on smoking/dental health from start to finish. Progressed from opening the topic to a concrete two-week cut-down plan with specific commitments (baseline count in three days, triggers, dental booking). No aimless wandering despite deflections.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Largely egalitarian—asked permission, offered choices, tailored plan at client's request. Client's rules honored; he left with agency. Occasionally defaulted to expert mode (pre-written checklist, unsolicited advice on breathing breaks) rather than drawing solutions from the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Explicit and consistent reinforcement of client ownership ("Slip = data, not failure," menu of options, accepting his framing that slips are his call). Accommodated his conditions (bedroom not car, every-3-days check-ins) without pushback. Client repeatedly noted this approach was "a first."

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Strongly drew motivation from within—questions yielded the client's own reasons (grandkids, money, partner noticing). Returned choice to client even when he asked for advice. Occasionally supplied options before the client fully articulated his own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Positioned client as expert on his life; his contributions shaped the session (bedroom zone, check-in frequency, plan terms). Falls short of 5 as the plan was delivered largely pre-formed rather than co-constructed from the client's own ideas throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Effectively elicited and built on the client's own change language—grandkids and money motivations drawn out directly. Harm-reduction reframe shifted him from "I can't see myself changing" to "Cutting down... I could wrap my head around that" and "Alright, I'm in." Secured concrete commitments. Would need deeper commitment language and confidence exploration for a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk; harm-reduction reframe elegantly disarmed "all-or-nothing" thinking, which the client acknowledged himself. Sacred rituals acknowledged rather than challenged. Some sustain talk (fatalism, "slips are my call") was accepted rather than strategically reframed.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **4.2/5**
