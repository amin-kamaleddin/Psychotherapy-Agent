# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and normalized Tyler's concerns, though warmth was functional rather than individualized and explicit affirmations of his strengths were sparse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections were offered, but reflections were brief and bundled with pivots to questions or advice, missing chances to deepen Tyler's emotional moments (his value conflict about education, his 'more doable than I thought' realization).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on alcohol use and progressed steadily toward a concrete change plan, though early agenda options lacked a clearly anchored shared purpose tied to Tyler's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was co-constructed and language was invitational throughout, but the counselor supplied the bulk of ideas (risk-reduction list, decline scripts), making Tyler more a menu-selector than equal generator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were consistently framed as Tyler's ('What number feels right to you?'), though occasional unsolicited option lists subtly steered content before his own preferences were articulated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Tyler's own motivations and plan were elicited (his limit, reminder text, decline line), though much evocation relied on counselor-supplied frameworks like scales and option lists rather than open exploration of his core value of graduating.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and menu options support power-sharing, but the counselor supplies most of the ideas, with Tyler selecting from them rather than generating his own strategies.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong use of open questions, importance/confidence rulers, and rehearsal produced genuine change talk ('School's too important to screw up'), though evocation was somewhat formulaic and the strongest change talk went unamplified.
