# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and avoids criticizing Jason's drinking or defensiveness. However, warmth feels procedural, and there are no affirmations of Jason's strengths (calling his roommate, self-awareness, willingness to role-play).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Early reflections validate Jason's frustration, but reflective listening is quickly abandoned in favor of advice and task lists. Deeper feelings—fear of parental discovery, feeling singled out, pride in calling Jake—are never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session completely drifts from the target behavior (alcohol use) to conduct-meeting logistics and document drafting. Even when Jason invites a discussion of the drinking ('do you think what I did was that bad?'), the counselor never returns to the clinical concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Although the counselor offers choices, the flow is heavily counselor-driven with numbered advice lists, a drafted letter, and script corrections. Jason functions as a client receiving services rather than a joint agenda-setting partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menu-based choices (prep, role-play parents, stress management) and statement options respect Jason's choice. Yet advice is delivered unprompted, and Jason's ownership over his drinking decisions is never affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The counselor never attempts to elicit Jason's own motivations, values, or reasons for change—no open questions, values exploration, or scaling questions appear. All content is counselor-provided tactical advice, and Jason's stated values go unexplored as levers for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes an expert/consultant role, delivering checklists and a fully drafted statement. Jason's input shapes only logistics (choosing 'the neutral one'), not the therapeutic direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is virtually no effort to evoke or reinforce change talk about alcohol use—no questions about Jason's drinking, consequences, or goals. Even the preparatory change talk Jason offers ('walking in prepared beats mouthing off') goes unreinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Jason's pervasive sustain talk ('everyone drinks before 21,' 'one party isn't gonna ruin my life') is ignored or implicitly validated. The counselor's 'it can seem petty' response reinforces the minimization rather than developing discrepancy.
