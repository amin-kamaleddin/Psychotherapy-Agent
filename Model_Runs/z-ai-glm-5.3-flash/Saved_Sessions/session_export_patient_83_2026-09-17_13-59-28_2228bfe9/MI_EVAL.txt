# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains  a  respectful,  nonjudgmental  tone  and  validates  the  client's  concerns  about  the  new  medication.  However,  warmth  is  generic  with  no  individualized  affirmations  of  Mai's  strengths  or  proactive  stance,  and  repeated  offers  to  do  the  work  for  her  undercut  positive  regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early  reflections  were  occasionally  accurate,  but  responses  quickly  shifted  to  bullet-pointed  education  rather  than  reflective  listening.  Meaningful  client  statements  like  'it's  protecting  my  heart'  were  never  reflected  or  explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The  session  stays  tightly  focused  on  statin  adherence  with  every  exchange  tied  to  actionable  steps  around  medication  purpose,  timing,  interactions,  and  monitoring.  Direction  is  maintained  largely  through  advice-giving  rather  than  collaborative  goal-setting,  keeping  it  from  a  5.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The  interaction  is  predominantly  counselor-as-advisor  with  bullet  lists  and  pre-packaged  scripts  rather  than  joint  problem-solving.  Mai's  own  solutions  (juice  swap,  nighttime  routine)  drive  the  plan  but  are  never  framed  as  shared  discovery  or  co-created  accomplishments.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers  some  genuine  choice  (drafting  questions  vs.  pharmacist  script)  and  respects  the  client's  decisions  without  pushback.  However,  the  overall  style  is  prescriptive  ('Don't  stop  the  drug,'  'set  a  reminder'),  and  the  counselor  never  explicitly  reinforces  Mai's  ownership  of  the  change  process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The  counselor  operates  from  an  information-deficit  model,  supplying  facts  rather  than  drawing  motivation  from  within  the  client.  Mai's  intrinsic  motivation  ('it's  protecting  my  heart')  emerged  spontaneously  but  was  never  elicited,  reflected,  or  expanded  upon.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The  counselor  repeatedly  assumes  the  expert  role,  delivering  lectures,  instructions,  and  offers  to  draft  scripts  on  Mai's  behalf.  Her  own  contributions  to  the  plan  are  never  acknowledged,  and  there  is  minimal  inquiry  into  what  she  already  knows  or  believes  would  work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No  deliberate  effort  to  evoke  change  talk  through  open  questions  designed  to  elicit  commitment  language  (e.g.,  'How  do  you  see  this  fitting  into  your  routine?').  Mai's  commitment  statements  emerge  spontaneously  from  education,  not  from  strategic  MI  questioning,  and  are  never  reinforced  or  deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai's  mild  sustain  talk  ('why  am  I  even  taking  this?',  'I  probably  would've  quit  after  a  week')  is  neutralized  effectively  through  direct  education,  avoiding  amplified  resistance.  However,  there  is  no  strategic  MI  handling  —  no  double-sided  reflections  or  reframing  of  skepticism  as  a  strength  —  and  one  directive  ('Don't  stop  the  drug')  could  subtly  invite  reactance.
