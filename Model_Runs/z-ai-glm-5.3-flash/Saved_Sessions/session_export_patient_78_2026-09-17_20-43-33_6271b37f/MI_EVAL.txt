# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  psychiatrist  consistently  communicates  unconditional  positive  regard,  from  the  opening  'I'm  not  here  to  lecture  you'  to  accepting  Lucía's  terms  at  the  close.  Defensiveness  is  validated  as  understandable,  her  courage  is  affirmed,  and  her  confidentiality  request  is  honored  without  judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective  listening  is  highly  accurate,  with  complex  reflections  capturing  her  ambivalence  and  the  emotional  core  of  her  motivation.  Reflections  repeatedly  land,  as  seen  when  Lucía  deepens  her  disclosure  after  each  one.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  session  maintains  clear,  progressive  focus  on  smoking  cessation,  moving  from  frustration  to  the  daughter's  promise,  past  failures,  her  mother's  success,  and  concrete  first  steps.  The  clinician  guides  without  pushing,  confirmed  by  Lucía's  comment  that  she  left  with  'more  than  I  walked  in  here  with.'

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  interview  operates  as  genuine  partnership:  the  clinician  offers  choices,  negotiates  terms  transparently,  and  accepts  Lucía's  conditions  as  a  co-created  agreement.  The  client  visibly  shapes  the  session,  even  setting  the  deal's  terms  herself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy  support  is  exemplary,  with  phrases  like  'Your  pace,  your  call'  and  'that's  yours  too,  in  your  time'  woven  throughout.  The  clinician  accepts  refusal  to  commit  today,  honors  confidentiality  demands,  and  reinforces  that  the  decision  and  pace  are  entirely  hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  psychiatrist  never  argues  for  quitting  or  lists  benefits;  instead,  strategic  questions  draw  out  Lucía's  own  reasons,  confidence,  and  plan.  Her  comment  'Nobody's  asked  me  that  before'  and  spontaneous  insights  reflect  evocation  at  the  highest  level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The  clinician  explicitly  positions  Lucía  as  the  expert  ('You  know  yourself  best,'  'You  did  the  hard  part,  not  me'),  and  her  freedom-first  priority  becomes  the  organizing  frame  of  the  plan.  Her  observation  'you  didn't  lecture  me  once'  confirms  the  power-sharing  was  felt.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open  questions  skillfully  evoke  rich  change  talk  from  a  resistant  client  ('maybe  asking  for  help  means  being  smarter  than  last  time,'  'I'm  actually  thinking  about  it').  The  clinician  reinforces  momentum  without  rushing,  and  Lucía  ends  with  concrete,  self-generated  first  steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain  talk  is  acknowledged  but  not  dwelled  upon,  with  effective  double-sided  reflections  and  reframes  of  resistance  as  self-knowledge.  Slightly  more  explicit  pivoting  would  raise  this  score,  but  the  current  balance  is  strong  and  strategic.
