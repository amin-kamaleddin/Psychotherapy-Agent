# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout; Sofia explicitly appreciated the low-pressure tone ('Thanks for not making this a whole big thing'). Warmth occasionally feels formulaic, with limited deep, individualized affirmation beyond surface encouragement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections (steady home, good friends, enjoying English), but Sofia's minimization ('not a big deal', 'everyone does that') and awkwardness about check-ins are never reflected or explored. Understanding remains adequate but shallow, with the session leaning informational.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** When Sofia discloses party drinking, the counselor pivots to logistics and safety tips rather than exploring context, frequency, or her perspective. The target behavior stays at surface level and the session closes without any collaborative goal or reflection on her drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration via format questions and options, but core content (screening explanation, safety tips) is delivered expert-to-client without eliciting Sofia's knowledge first—e.g., her buddy system with Mia only emerges after tips were given. Power sharing is uneven though the tone is non-authoritarian.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A clear strength: repeated choice-offering ('totally your choice', 'only if that feels useful'), with Sofia's preferences directly shaping the plan. Falls short of a 5 because advice is delivered unidirectionally without first eliciting her own ideas or emphasizing ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and ideas are rarely drawn from the client; the counselor supplies reasons and tips rather than eliciting Sofia's own motivations or values. Her spontaneous commitments ('I'll remember that') come from her own initiative, and 'I don't know what there is to focus on' is not explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices are offered (summary vs. skip, text vs. in-person), conveying some power sharing, but the session is largely counselor-driven information delivery with Sofia in a passive role. Her expertise about her own situations and friendships is never explicitly recognized.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia spontaneously offers mild change talk (pre-set limits, never riding with a drinking driver), but the counselor merely summarizes rather than deepening it. No open or evocative questions are used to amplify her reasons for staying safe.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia's minimization ('I don't drink much anyway', 'everyone does that stuff') goes essentially untouched—no double-sided reflections, reframing, or pivoting toward her protective behaviors. This is a significant missed opportunity for a screening follow-up.
