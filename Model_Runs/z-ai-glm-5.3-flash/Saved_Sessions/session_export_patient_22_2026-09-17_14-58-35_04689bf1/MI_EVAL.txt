# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating Camila's ambivalence and thanking her for honesty. Falls short of 5 due to few individualized affirmations of her strengths beyond generic praise like 'nice work.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflections capture her core ambivalence well, e.g., 'You're torn — you want fewer hangovers and lost days, but you worry about social pressure.' Reflections are sometimes brief summaries rather than deeper explorations of the emotional layer (embarrassment, fear of being 'the boring one,' loneliness).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent focus on reducing drinking from the opening question onward, with every exchange moving toward a concrete plan (Thursday, three-drink limit, water alternation, midnight alarm, taco text, Uber). The session never drifts from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Builds the plan from Camila's preferences and her own insights (water alternation, roommate ally, Uber based on her 'worst calls'), checking feasibility along the way. Slightly below 5 because most strategies are supplied top-down rather than eliciting her ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames strategies as options and lets Camila select and shape the plan, accepting her rejection of cash-only and skipping rounds. Lacks explicit statements reinforcing that the change process is entirely hers, and some stacked questions gently press for commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out her motivations and self-generated solutions via rulers (importance 8, confidence 6→7) and open questions. Falls short of 5 because strategies are sometimes offered before fully exploring her ideas, and deeper values (dating, loneliness, identity as 'the fun one') remain unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites Camila's input on which strategies fit ('Which refusal line feels most like you'), but frequently assumes the expert role with prescriptive strategy lists delivered in rapid succession. Her ideas shape the plan, but the frame often feels clinician-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evokes change talk using importance/confidence rulers and questions like 'What would help raise your confidence from a 6 to a 7,' successfully eliciting desire, reasons, and commitment. Misses chances to deepen or reflect back her change talk after she offers it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk but does little active softening, mostly moving past it with strategy suggestions rather than double-sided reflections. The validating 'That ambivalence makes a lot of sense' is a good move, but the overall pattern is neutral redirection rather than skilled softening.
