# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard maintained even in response to hostility, resistance, and active suicidal ideation. Validated distrust of formal plans and accepted the client's conditions without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, capturing the client's own words and both sides of her ambivalence. Empathy was also conveyed through candor, which built trust with a client expecting invalidation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on suicidal ideation, emotion regulation, and safety, responding to risk disclosure immediately and appropriately. Pacing progressed logically from vague hopelessness to a co-created crisis plan in one session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Asked permission before structuring and treated the client's negotiations (no worksheet, texting counts as attending) as legitimate design input. Co-construction was genuine, with a warm and human tone throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy reinforced at nearly every turn: 'no worksheet, no lecture,' 'the plan is yours, not mine,' and framing even risk management as a request rather than a directive. The client's conditions were accepted as legitimate constraints.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently elicited from within rather than supplied, drawing out the client's own reasons (her niece, the hour with Denisha, 'relief. Like breathing.'). She ended the session having generated, evaluated, and committed to her own plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert, and her contributions demonstrably shaped the session — the entire safety plan was her design. Power sharing was genuine, not rhetorical.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled open questions elicited the client's own desire and need language, which was then amplified and converted into commitment and action. The client generated the entire safety plan herself and proposed her own contingency strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Abundant sustain talk ('Nothing works,' 'ER feels like admitting failure') was neither argued against nor dwelt in — acknowledged honestly, then pivoted toward exploration or folded into the client's own plan. Preemptive rejection of 'breakthrough' framing was met with agreement while re-anchoring her change language.
