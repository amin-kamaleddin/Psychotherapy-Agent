# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, including fully respecting the client's firm boundary about the dog without judgment. Repeated affirmations and a warm, non-judgmental ending ('the honest version, whatever it looks like') reinforced acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, varied reflections consistently captured the client's experience, from overwhelm ('it's a lot all at once') to ambivalence ('both of those are true') to the shift toward hope. Reflections were emotionally attuned and well-timed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained strong focus, guiding from 'everything all over the place' to one concrete behavior (preventer anchored at the kettle) with a defined trial and check-in. Related issues (appointment avoidance, costs) were appropriately deferred but left some directional threads open.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session proceeded as genuine joint problem-solving, with every plan element originating from the client and decisions co-constructed. The client's comment — 'a plan I came up with myself' — confirmed the collaborative dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently reinforced through choices ('what feels most doable?'), low-stakes experimental framing, and explicit respect for the client's boundaries. The counselor never pressured and credited the client with full ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client via open questions about what would make the biggest difference and what had worked before. This culminated in strong self-generated commitment: 'kettle, preventer, honest check-in. Two weeks.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert on his own life, with all plan elements (kettle anchor, weekend strategy, check-in timing) originating from him. The counselor's role was to help the client hear his own ideas rather than impose an agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through open questions about desired differences, past successes, and markers of progress, then reinforced it via affirmation and reflection. The client ended with strong self-generated commitment language: 'for real this time.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (inconvenience, cost, dog resistance) was acknowledged but never reinforced; a double-sided reflection validated barriers while anchoring to the change side. Focus remained consistently on actionable change rather than obstacles.
