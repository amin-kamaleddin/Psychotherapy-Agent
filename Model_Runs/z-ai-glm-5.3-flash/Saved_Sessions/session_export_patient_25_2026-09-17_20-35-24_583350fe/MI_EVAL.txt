# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates consistent, unconditional positive regard with no judgment even when Daniel discloses drinking beyond intentions. Normalizing slips as 'information, not failure' is especially skilled given his all-or-nothing relapse history.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are consistently accurate and emotionally attuned, including the double-sided reflection on fitting in vs. staying true and the 'dad you want to be at 6 a.m.' meaning reflection. The final summary accurately consolidates the plan and confirms understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, progressive focus on alcohol use at social gatherings while leveraging Daniel's smoking-cessation success as a resource. Each exchange advances the agenda (triggers, plan, obstacles, confidence, self-monitoring, recap) with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A model of equal-partner interaction—every plan element originated from Daniel, and the clinician frames follow-up as joint review. Daniel's own words ('instead it's just... my plan') confirm power-sharing was realized, not merely rhetorical.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively reinforces ownership throughout ('What feels doable there?', 'What sounds right to you?') and offers zero prescriptive advice. Framing 'no pressure to be perfect' decouples worth from adherence, which is sophisticated support for someone who abandons goals after slips.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within the client—his own reasons (6 a.m. with his child), past strategies, confidence rating, and solutions. Daniel articulates reasons, ability, and commitment unprompted, and the clinician affirms these as his own discoveries.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise within the client ('You know yourself better than anyone here') and every substantive plan element comes from Daniel. The client's recognition that the plan is his own is direct evidence the partnership was genuine.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematically evokes Daniel's own change language via open questions, producing self-generated commitments (two-drink line, 7/8 confidence, 'it feels manageable') that progress to commitment language. The clinician effectively reflects and amplifies Daniel's own shift in self-perception.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk (peer pressure, fear of teasing, minimizing) well by acknowledging it empathically and pivoting to solution-focused questions. Not a 5 only because ambivalence was low-intensity, so fewer explicit pivot opportunities arose.
