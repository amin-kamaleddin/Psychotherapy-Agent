# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirmations and normalization of discouragement. Held from 5 due to occasionally formulaic warmth and less explicit normalization of relapse/setback.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately capturing ambivalence and deeper meaning. Reflections sometimes echo the client's words rather than exploring unvoiced emotional nuance like the shame or grief in 'letting myself down'.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary focus: every exchange advances a logical arc from exploring options to values to specific, time-anchored commitments. No topic drift; each turn ties back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent check-ins and client input shaping the plan — Marcus even voiced the reality-check question himself. Long, polished counselor summaries occasionally dominate, and the agenda was set via question sequence rather than explicit negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change consistently framed as Marcus's choice, with him positioned as the expert and the plan entirely his own. Lacks explicit affirmation of his ownership over how he responds to inevitable setbacks, which would fully cement internal locus of control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn entirely from within: values questions elicited his deepest reasons and his own success criteria ('kept a promise to myself'). Marcus's shift from 'what's the point' to 'I'm kind of excited' demonstrates highly effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marcus explicitly positioned as the expert from the opening line, and the walking/dancing plan came entirely from him. Long, polished reflections occasionally risk leading him toward pre-formed conclusions rather than drawing insight from him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation and reinforcement of change talk: values questions elicited reasons for change, followed by strategic reflection and deepening of commitment language. Produced strong commitment statements plus specific, time-anchored planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled well with double-sided reflections, reframing of barriers, and invitations to problem-solve rather than argue. Occasionally restates sustain talk without immediately pivoting, though redirection is always effective.
