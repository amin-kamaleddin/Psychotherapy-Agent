# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and never criticizes the patient's skepticism or habits. However, affirmations are generic and brief rather than personalized reflections of Jamal's effort or autonomy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections are accurate, but as the session progresses they give way to logistics and planning questions. Meaningful emotional disclosures—fear of failing in front of his mother, 'don't let my mom use it as proof I can't do anything'—go largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on punctuality throughout, with every turn advancing a concrete plan (alarm time, backup, start date, check-in). The counselor even caught the practical flaw that a powered-off phone wouldn't sound its alarm.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaboration is inconsistent: the patient's adjustments shape the plan, but the counselor often fills long turns with options, scripts, and rules before eliciting his input. The power balance tilts toward the counselor generating solutions the patient merely ratifies.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers menus and asks the patient to choose, reinforcing ownership ('I hear you like that it's on you'). Deducted because pre-packaged option lists constrain choice and advice (the mom-script) was delivered before permission was granted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and the confidence-boosting prompt effectively elicit motivation and self-efficacy language. But most solutions, scripts, and backup plans come from the counselor rather than drawing out the patient's own ideas, so evocation remains superficial.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Alternates between partnership and an expert stance, with long prescriptive turns (option lists, mom-script, backup menu) foregrounding the counselor's expertise. The patient's contributions do shape the plan, but most ideas are supplied by the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled scaling questions evoke the patient's own change language ('that one's on me', 'I'd rather come back and say I did it'). However, change talk is rarely deepened with reflections—the counselor moves straight to logistics instead of amplifying intrinsic ownership.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently pivots away from sustain talk without reinforcing it, using forward-moving questions and practical reframes. Minor deduction because the sustain talk about his mother's nagging is acknowledged but never strategically reframed toward his own reasons for change.
