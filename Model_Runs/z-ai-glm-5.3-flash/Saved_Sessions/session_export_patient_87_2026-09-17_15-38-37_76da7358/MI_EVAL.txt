# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations throughout, and never criticized Marco's failed attempts or self-doubt. However, affirmations were generic and formulaic ('Nice choice,' 'nice plan') and relapse/struggle was never explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured both Marco's behavioral patterns and emotional core, including his fear of being isolated from family dinners. Later turns dropped reflection almost entirely in favor of rapid advice menus, and the shame and emotional weight of his repeated failures was never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear and consistent focus on evening eating, with every exchange tied to triggers, one concrete step, troubleshooting rough nights, and the plan with his wife. The session progressed logically from exploration to a specific commitment with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly sought permission, asked Marco to select and shape the plan, and asked how to support him, with Marco's contributions meaningfully shaping the plan. However, options were counselor-authored and the structured format (scaling, menus, scripts) gave a clinician-driven feel rather than full co-generation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marco's choices were entirely client-selected and the counselor never pressured or expressed disappointment, consistently reinforcing ownership. Autonomy support remained implicit in the menu format — the counselor never explicitly stated Marco was in charge and steered him back to counselor-generated plan details.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling and barrier questions drew out useful self-knowledge and growing confidence, but much motivational content was counselor-supplied rather than client-evoked. The counselor never explored why change mattered to Marco (his children, self-trust), his hopes, or his own ideas before offering hers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and asking which options felt doable supported power-sharing, but the counselor assumed the expert role substantially, generating all plan components and rapid-fire menus of options. Marco's own expertise from past diet attempts was never acknowledged or explored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated scaling questions reliably evoked strengthening change talk, with confidence rising from 5.5-6 to 7.5 and culminating in a genuine commitment ('I'm willing to give it a real shot this week'). Evocation relied almost exclusively on confidence scaling, with no exploration of desire, reasons, or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor never amplified sustain talk and sometimes pivoted effectively, but sustain talk was frequently left unaddressed rather than actively softened. Statements about self-doubt and relapse fear were passed over without double-sided reflections or reframing to resolve ambivalence.
