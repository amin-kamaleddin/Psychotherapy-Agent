# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard, honored the no-lecture agreement, and validated Marco's frustration without any judgment. The client's closing thanks confirms he felt fully accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, function-focused reflections (e.g., the cigarette as a 'pause', the conditioned coffee ritual). Missed deeper emotional reflection on the grief of giving up a 40-year 'mate' and the existential weight of smoking after two serious illnesses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session progressed logically from exploring the function of smoking to trigger mapping, a concrete experiment, and follow-up. Minor deductions for early menu-style prompts (grounding exercises, 'whatever else is bothering you') that briefly diluted focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as equal partners, incorporating Marco's own words and constraints verbatim into the plan without defensiveness when he pushed back. The checklist and mantra were genuinely co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary and explicit autonomy support: repeated structured choices, experiment framed as 'data, not proof you must quit,' and the client set his own timeline. Marco internalized this ('I'm the one in charge here. Not the smoke.').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never argued for change; drew all reasons and commitments from Marco through open questions about what smoking gives him and what he'd lose. Skillfully calibrated evocation for a precontemplative client, producing voluntary change talk and a behavioral commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise clearly located in the client—Marco chose the focus, the mapping target, and the mantra, and his constraints shaped every element of the plan. Client confirmed his expertise was honored ('You asked questions instead of preaching').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and amplified change talk at every step, converting faint openness into a concrete self-designed experiment. Marco's language shifted from 'can't see myself changing' to 'I could try it once... I'll let you know what the numbers say.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never argued with or dwelled on sustain talk; instead reframed delay as data collection, sidestepping the quit/not-quit dichotomy. Evidence of success is Marco's shift from 'can't see myself changing' to 'that almost sounds doable. Almost.'
