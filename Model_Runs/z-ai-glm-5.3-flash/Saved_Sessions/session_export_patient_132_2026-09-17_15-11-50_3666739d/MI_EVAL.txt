# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout ('I'm here to listen and support you without judgment'), with consistent affirmations and no judgmental language, even around the patient's persistent weight-loss desire.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture Yasmin's cycle of obsession and crash and her gym ambivalence. However, some reflections repeat content rather than deepening into the emotional layer (e.g., fear of failing again, loneliness of being either cheered or lectured).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on sustainable, intention-focused movement, with every exchange advancing toward a concrete agreed plan (two gym sessions, no metrics, a check-in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed—Yasmin chose the experiment, session count, start time, and her own intention wording. Slightly below 5 because the counselor occasionally slips into prescriptive mode with detailed pre-written plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is reinforced consistently, from selecting experiments to choosing check-in timing and format ('vent' vs. 'next step'), with no pressure and full respect for her pacing and preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong open questions elicit Yasmin's own motivations and change talk ('I'd rather try this than crash again,' 'manageable is new for me'). Menus of pre-packaged options at times substitute expert content for evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Yasmin is treated as the expert, choosing the experiment and crafting her own intention. Falls short of 5 because the counselor tends to supply pre-packaged menus rather than consistently letting her generate solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evokes change talk with questions about feeling good in her body, sustainable exercise, and defining success, producing strong patient responses. Occasional menu-offering keeps it from deeper exploration of her own reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ambivalence (weight loss, fear of failure) is acknowledged with a double-sided reflection, but never deeply explored or reframed. Repeated contingency planning for 'if it goes badly' keeps attention on anticipated failure rather than building confidence.
