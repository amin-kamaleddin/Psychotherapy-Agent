# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental and neutral throughout, with no disapproval of the client's use. However, affirmations and expressions of warmth or positive regard are almost entirely absent, leaving the stance respectful but clinically thin.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections (ambush feeling, social bind), but the session repeatedly pivots to advice-giving. Rich emotional material like 'weekdays are medicine' and rejection numbing receives only brief acknowledgment before scripts and plans.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the target behavior and its concrete consequences, tying plans back to goals and triggers. Direction is channeled heavily toward budgeting and action-planning rather than exploring motivation and ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative gestures (asking which option to start with, building on the client's numbers), but the balance tilts expert with three pre-set multi-step plans. The clinician's agenda of practical steps drives the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choice is offered within menus, and there is no pressure to quit or attend groups. However, the plan is largely prescribed with specific figures, and hesitations like '$40 for a whole weekend?' are absorbed without renegotiation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is supported through external tools rather than elicited; the one strong evocative question is never followed up. No confidence or importance exploration occurs despite the client naming meaningful motivations like acting and self-respect.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choice is offered ('which of these would you like me to do first?'), but numbered plans, dollar figures, and directives dominate. The client generates most insights himself while the clinician supplies solutions, with little acknowledgment of his own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk was almost entirely client-generated and rarely evoked or deepened. A prime opening ('does that sound manageable to you?') was answered with advice rather than exploring his own concern.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never amplified and some reframes are offered, but ambivalent statements ('am I kidding myself?', 'I'm not ready to give up that whole world') are left unexplored. Validation of plans replaced strategic reflection of the underlying ambivalence.
