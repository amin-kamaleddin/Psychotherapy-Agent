# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance maintained throughout, including around relapse and pill use; affirmations were brief and could have extended beyond honesty to Elias's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured shame and ambivalence well, but the counselor moved quickly to techniques rather than sitting with the emotional pain and fear underneath.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on relapse prevention from start to finish, culminating in a concrete co-created if-then plan with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Agendas and interventions were genuinely co-constructed; Elias selected and modified reframes, messages, and pacing throughout the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices were repeatedly reinforced, boundaries respected, and the counselor explicitly endorsed workable steps over perfection, keeping ownership with Elias.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation of change talk ('I did stop... I stopped'), though some moments leaned on counselor-supplied options rather than eliciting Elias's own solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elias was treated as the expert; when he rejected the prescribed phrase, the counselor adopted his wording ('I messed up one night, but I stopped') which became the core intervention.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was elicited and reinforced effectively, with confidence rising from 6 to 7; missed chances to deepen exploration of why he rated himself as he did.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was reframed rather than reinforced ('that critical voice is loud, but it's not the whole story'), but the core choice-vs-circumstance ambivalence remained unresolved.
