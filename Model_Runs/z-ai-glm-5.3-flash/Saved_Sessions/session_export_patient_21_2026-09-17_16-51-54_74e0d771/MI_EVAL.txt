# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with no judgment or labeling; validated current efforts, normalized fear of failure by framing missed sessions as 'data, not failure.' Patient explicitly left feeling accepted, saying she felt more hopeful than when she came in.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured ambivalence, including the dual nature of the referral. However, reflections were occasionally brief and solution-oriented — the disclosure of past failed plans deserved a deeper complex reflection of her fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on increasing physical activity from open to close, with every exchange advancing the goal. Session ended with a fully specified, realistic plan tied to the patient's values of consistency and self-kindness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-way — patient's responses visibly reshaped the plan, including her request that the check-in focus on feelings, which the counselor accepted. She experienced the session as a partnership and co-ownership of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element (days, times, stretches, contact method) was framed as the patient's decision, with no coercive language despite the referral context. She left affirming ownership: 'it's not some huge overhaul' and 'I think I've got this.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited the patient's own reasons via open questions and confidence rulers, but evocation was front-loaded and practical. Missed opportunities to explore deeper values around health and aging, and to unpack why her confidence was a 7 rather than lower.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Patient's input shaped key plan details (walk times, script style, check-in format), and the counselor repeatedly sought her preferences. Some power-sharing was limited by counselor-generated option menus rather than fully client-generated ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk through open questions and confidence rulers, with patient confidence rising from 6 to 7 and clear commitment statements. Not a 5 because the confidence ruler was not deepened (e.g., asking why a 7 and not lower).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged barriers (energy, injury worry, past failures) without amplifying them, and converted each into contingency plans while reframing missed sessions as 'data.' More explicit reframing of the past-failure narrative itself would have earned a 5.
