# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental and respectful throughout ('I'm here to listen, not judge'), validating Ethan's minimization rather than confronting it. Missed opportunities for explicit affirmations of his honesty and strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured both sides of Ethan's ambivalence well. Could have gone deeper emotionally, e.g., reflecting the feeling behind 'I'm kinda tired of getting in trouble' before moving to advice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use with a concrete, value-linked plan (24-hour rule, soda swap, Danny, refusal lines) and a defined follow-up, with no topic drift despite Ethan's disengagement attempts.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely equal-partner work: co-created refusal lines via rehearsal and used Ethan's own language in the plan. Some exchanges were counselor-provided menus, limiting Ethan to choosing among options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent permission-seeking, choice among options, and acceptance when Ethan declined backup planning. Ethan's 'then that'd be on me' reflects genuine internalized responsibility.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Ethan's own values (varsity, hating sick mornings) with evocative questions and confidence scaling. Relied somewhat heavily on supplying information and options rather than letting Ethan articulate reasons first.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sought permission, asked which options felt useful, and let Ethan's ideas drive the plan. Some safety-tips and alternatives sections leaned toward the expert role with rapid counselor-generated lists.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and developed change talk linking drinking to varsity, producing rich commitments ('skip drinking before games,' 'tired of getting in trouble'). Confidence rose from 6 to 8, with Ethan generating his own plan elements and rehearsing.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and diffused defensiveness ('weekends-only doesn't mean you're addicted'). Occasionally invited sustain talk directly ('what you like about those nights') and relied on information-giving pivots rather than double-sided reflections.
