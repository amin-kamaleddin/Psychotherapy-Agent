# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with affirmations like 'you're not wrong to honor that' and normalization of David's self-protective skepticism. Affirmations occasionally follow a formulaic pattern and cluster at turn openings rather than being woven organically throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including an excellent capture of David's core concern ('the choice gets made for you before you've even decided') and a skilled double-sided reflection. Emotional nuances like fear of failure and shame are acknowledged but not deeply explored at an emotional level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains a clear, progressive focus on smoking cessation with a clean arc: identifying needs → prioritizing social triggers → mining past success → building a concrete plan → commitment. Orienting questions at nearly every turn keep forward momentum with no aimless wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative, with David's answers demonstrably steering the session — the focus on social triggers and plan components came from his own prioritization and suggestions. Information is paired with inquiry, trade-offs acknowledged honestly, and decisions returned to David.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David is consistently framed as the author of his plan ('you're the one who chose to reach out'), and medication choice ends with a genuine choice question. However, explicit statements of unconditional permission — acknowledging he could choose differently or not start medication — are largely absent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn almost entirely from within David through evocative questions rooted in his own experience; he supplies the insights, reasons, and commitment language himself. No persuading or supplying of reasons occurs — information is given only on request and immediately re-evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within the client ('You know yourself well enough to see that Friday-tired David needs more than a good feeling from this room'), and David's insights became the session's direction. When information was needed, it was provided and the decision handed back ('Which matters most to you?').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's standout strength: open evocation, strategic prioritizing, amplifying past success (the barbecue story), and success imagery produced David's visible progression from desire → reasons → ability → commitment ('the medication, no question'). Commitment was then consolidated through concrete weekly planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of public failure, past caving) is briefly validated then consistently pivoted toward change — a textbook soften-and-redirect ('you've been burned before... and at the same time, you're describing something you haven't had before'). David resolved much of his own sustain talk, though the psychiatrist could occasionally restate sustain content more economically.
