# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard with genuine affirmations and no labeling or disapproval of marijuana use. Defensive minimization was met with curiosity, and potential relapse was accepted nonjudgmentally.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that captured both instrumental and values-level content using the client's own language. Deducted because some reflections were simple repetitions rather than deeper emotional exploration (e.g., family expectations, fear of being 'the weird one').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on marijuana use and a specific target behavior (school nights) from open to close, with no topic drift. Concluded with a concrete, testable commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely joint enterprise with inclusive language and every plan element co-developed through back-and-forth. The client's closing remarks confirm she experienced the power-sharing as real.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced ownership and choice, honored the weekend boundary without pushing, and framed the outcome as self-controlled information-gathering. No directive or coercive language anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation for change came from the client's own mouth — finances, effectiveness doubts, family values — with self-efficacy built from her existing resources. Spontaneous commitment language confirms evocation succeeded.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the client as the expert, with every plan element originating from her and no direct advice given. The final summary attributed ownership entirely to her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked escalating change talk through open questions, culminating in a strong values-based desired-state statement ('doesn't fit with the person I'm supposed to be becoming'). The counselor then reinforced and reflected this language back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk, using selective reflections that pivoted toward ambivalence and respectfully containing boundaries. Deducted for absence of explicit double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk.
