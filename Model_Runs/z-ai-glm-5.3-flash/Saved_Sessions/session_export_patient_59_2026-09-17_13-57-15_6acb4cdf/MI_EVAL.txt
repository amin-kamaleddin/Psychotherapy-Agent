# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout despite Jamal's minimizing stance, without any lecturing about marijuana use. Warmth is somewhat transactional, driven by checklists, with few genuine affirmations of Jamal as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate reflections and catches Jamal's one moment of genuine emotional disclosure about his mother. However, reflections are brief and formulaic, and emotional content is consistently met with checklists or practical advice rather than deeper exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session has focus but on the wrong target — administrative compliance (paperwork, slips, copies) consumes the conversation. The intended target behavior (marijuana use/risk) is barely explored beyond a superficial harm-reduction list.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction is largely counselor-as-provider delivering pre-packaged deliverables while the client accepts or declines them. When Jamal pushes back on suggestions, the counselor continues the plan rather than exploring his view or co-constructing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirms choice ('It's your call') and accepts Jamal's preferences without pushback, including declining the role-play and preferring texting. Choices are framed mostly around logistics rather than the actual change behavior.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no evocative questions about Jamal's own values, goals, or reasons to change; the counselor instead supplies reasons himself. Jamal's spontaneous motivation emerges on its own and goes unelicited and undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, dispensing lists, scripts, and step-by-step instructions with minimal inquiry into Jamal's own ideas. Jamal becomes passive ('Whatever's fastest'), signaling weak power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal spontaneously offers meaningful change talk ('I don't need this messing up school,' 'I don't really need a second time'), but every instance is met with a checklist item instead of a reflective affirmation or open question. Change talk is repeatedly left unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor never argues with sustain talk, which is positive, but also offers no double-sided reflections or strategic redirection toward Jamal's own reasons for change. Volunteering harm-reduction lists at times invites more defensiveness.
