# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent warmth and nonjudgment throughout, normalizing Maya's uncertainty about the referral, affirming her honesty, and repeatedly reassuring her about confidentiality. Positive regard was somewhat brief and procedural at times, missing opportunities for deeper individualized affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made adequate reflective attempts (e.g., linking late nights to seeming 'out of it') but reflective listening was inconsistent. Maya's most emotionally significant disclosure — that secrecy around drinking was 'more exhausting than the actual parties' — was not deeply reflected; the counselor pivoted to safety advice instead.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed reasonably focused on referral-related concerns (sleep, academics, alcohol) and moved toward concrete next steps including the email, study plan, and check-ins. Some drift occurred through lists of safety advice, and the drinking topic was left superficially resolved.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maya tailored key elements (chem focus, molarity email, check-in timing), but the counselor's default mode was expert-delivered plans with limited joint problem-solving. Valuable ideas like 'starting small' and recruiting Jordan as a study buddy came from Maya rather than emerging from collaborative exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated strong autonomy support with 'it's up to you,' shared decision-making about follow-up frequency, offered options, and no pressure on the drinking issue. Slight deduction because advice was sometimes delivered prescriptively before checking what Maya wanted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied primarily on providing information and advice rather than eliciting Maya's own motivations; open questions about her reasons for change or values were absent. Motivation emerged spontaneously from Maya (wanting to do well, disliking secrecy stress) but was never drawn out or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Framed follow-up decisions collaboratively ('we'll decide together what feels right') and offered Maya choices, but much of the session consisted of expert-driven advice blocks. Maya's contributions often shaped the session only after she pushed back on the counselor's proposals.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maya generated meaningful change talk organically (committing to send the email, texting Jordan, requesting accountability check-ins), and the counselor captured these in structured plans. However, commitments were rarely reinforced or deepened with affirmations or evocative follow-ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing and did not reinforce Maya's sustain talk ('it's really not a problem,' 'I don't need to stop'), which is a strength. However, no double-sided reflections or strategic pivoting to Maya's own stated concerns (secrecy stress) were used, leaving sustain talk largely unaddressed.
