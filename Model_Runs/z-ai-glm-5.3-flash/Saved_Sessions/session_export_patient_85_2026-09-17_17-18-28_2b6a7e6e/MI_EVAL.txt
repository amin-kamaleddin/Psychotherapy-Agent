# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with validating openings and affirmations of Marco's past successes. However, affirmations became formulaic and repetitive ('that matters'), with little normalization of his weight regain or deeper expression of unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening that captured Marco's emotional experience, including the sting of the hidden juice setback. Reflections often shifted quickly into advice, and Marco's shame about the scale ('thinking I'd failed') was never explicitly explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained exceptionally clear, unwavering focus on reducing juice consumption with no topic drift. The session progressed logically to a concrete, time-bound action plan with a scheduled one-week follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used collaborative language throughout—asking permission, offering choices, and co-constructing the plan with Marco's genuine input. However, the counselor tended to present pre-formed drafts and option menus rather than jointly generating ideas from Marco's own knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed choices as Marco's own ('Which feels best?', 'Want to tweak the tone?'), and he exercised meaningful choice throughout. The rapid-fire offering of specific solutions occasionally crowded his own problem-solving space.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Marco's own motivations and confidence through scaling questions and open-ended invitations, producing strong change talk. Evocation was sometimes bypassed by supplying alternatives and drafts rather than first asking for Marco's ideas, with deeper values left unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited Marco's input and asked permission early, but frequently slid into the expert role—drafting texts, composing a fridge note, and providing a seven-item shopping list unrequested. Marco's pushback ('I'll handle any pushback from her myself') signaled the counselor had drifted toward solving problems for him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully used scaling questions that elicited strong change talk (importance 9, confidence rising from 7 to 10) and cultivated commitment language. Missed opportunities to amplify change talk when it emerged—for example, moving to logistics after Marco's vision of success ('Imagine what happens when I actually fix it').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not dwell on Marco's attachment to OJ, instead pivoting to his prior successes and reframing the stocked fridge as a problem to solve. However, relied on redirection rather than deliberate double-sided reflections or strategic resolution of ambivalence.
