# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful tone throughout with validating, non-judgmental affirmations. Affirmations became repetitive in the closing exchanges, reducing the sense of individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that captured both emotional states and values, e.g., reflecting her uncertainty about conflicting information. Reflections stayed mostly at the 'informed decision-maker' identity level without deeper exploration of her phone anxiety or fear of being seen as overly anxious.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the citalopram–phenelzine interaction and building a concrete prescriber call plan. Session circled through near-identical closings, and the patient's question about citalopram's general effects was deferred without a follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided interaction with permission-style questions and a co-constructed call script shaped by the patient's own phrasing and notes. Core safety information was delivered didactically without first checking what she already knew.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced patient ownership through 'You know your prescriber best' and option-framed advice ('Some people find...'). Less emphasis on her capacity to handle setbacks herself, with decisions repeatedly routed to the prescriber.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally drew motivation from within, and her commitment to call the doctor emerged organically. Motivation was more often affirmed after the fact than actively evoked, with repetitive closings substituting for deeper evocative exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Alessia as the expert on her own life and offered suggestions as options rather than directives. Pharmacological information was still largely expert-delivered without acknowledging how her own knowledge shaped the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively evoked commitment language leading to 'I'll call my doctor today' and a self-built script. Missed chances to deepen confidence or explore what would make the call successful, especially in the closing exchanges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal and not reinforced, but also not strategically softened. Her worry about acting too quickly was redirected neutrally rather than with a double-sided reflection affirming her caution while guiding toward action.
