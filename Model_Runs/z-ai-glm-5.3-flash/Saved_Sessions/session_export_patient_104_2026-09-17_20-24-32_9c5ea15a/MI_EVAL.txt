# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, judgment-free support throughout—normalizing her doubt, reframing guilt without criticism, and checking in on how she's really holding up. Even her fear of caving at 4am is met with 'progress isn't perfection.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, emotionally attuned complex reflections, e.g., 'That feeling of failing—of being surrounded by everyone else who seems to have it figured out—is heavy.' Pivots sensitively to 'how are you holding up, Nadine?' at her emotional moment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus moving purposefully from validation of sleep deprivation to exploring the self-doubt cycle, identifying barriers, co-constructing a concrete plan, and connecting rest to her values, ending with a follow-up framework.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative—each question builds from Nadine's statements, and the client's own ideas drive the session ('I guess I just answered my own question'). Agenda-setting is handed to her at the close.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces her ownership ('What would give you the most peace of mind first?'), with no prescriptive advice—the monitor-off plan is entirely her formulation, and rest is framed as her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterful elicitation of client motivation—strategic questions drew out her own evidence, barriers, solutions, and values talk ('Hope, maybe?'). Nadine generated every key insight and decision herself, which the counselor named and amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions Nadine as the expert ('You're also your own best expert on your baby') and credits her insights as her own. Her closing reflection—leaving with a plan for herself rather than answers about the baby—confirms genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation of rich commitment language: 'I think I want to try it. This Saturday,' 'I'll talk to him tonight,' and 'Hope, maybe?' Explicitly names and reinforces her shift from 'I can't break this habit' to a concrete plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk around guilt and control is acknowledged and reframed ('that's courage, Nadine'), and redirects toward change. Minor deduction: the extensive exploration of guilt/control, while clinically productive, occasionally slows the pivot toward change talk.
