# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with good affirmations tied to Erik's own efforts and no criticism of setbacks. Some affirmations feel formulaic, and setbacks are not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some decent reflections are present, but they are limited and quickly pivot to advice or questions. Vulnerable moments (shame about asking his wife, fear of failure) are met with scripts rather than reflection of the underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on the target behaviors throughout, with every exchange moving toward concrete, achievable steps. The session culminates in a well-defined, realistic plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is genuine back-and-forth, with Erik's ideas (notepad, text wording, rejecting the home workout) shaping the final plan. But the session often takes on an expert-consultant feel, with multiple counselor-driven suggestions per turn.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices are offered and Erik's rejection of options is respected without pushback. However, most solutions, scripts, and plans originate from the counselor rather than being evoked from Erik.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik's intrinsic motivations (back pain relief, 'this one's for me') and confidence rulers draw out meaningful self-assessment. However, these moments are not deepened—e.g., 'that's the real payoff' moves straight to friction-reduction tactics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik is offered choices and his input shapes the plan, but the counselor repeatedly positions himself as the solution generator, offering scripts, plans, and wording. Erik even asks the counselor whether his own idea would work, showing reliance on expert authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively capitalizes on Erik's volunteered change talk (tracking lifts, treadmill calculation, wanting to stop coasting) and uses confidence rulers to evoke commitment. Evocation relies heavily on solution-selection questions rather than deeper exploration of Erik's own values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ambivalence is briefly acknowledged, but doubts ('Not promising it sticks', 'the couch wins', confidence of 5-6) are met with more action steps rather than double-sided reflections. Sustain talk is largely bypassed rather than softened.
