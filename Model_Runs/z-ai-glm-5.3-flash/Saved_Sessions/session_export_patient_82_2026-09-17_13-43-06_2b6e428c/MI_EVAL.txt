# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validated the client's ambivalence as normal. However, the counselor never addressed Lukas's final confidentiality request, leaving an important trust moment unanswered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured the client's ambivalence well ('both reactions can coexist'). Reflections were sometimes brief, an early question barrage overwhelmed the client, and deeper emotional reflections on guilt and identity conflict were missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stayed focused on the target behavior throughout, moving coherently from exploration to ambivalence to a concrete harm-reduction plan. Weakened by early scattered questioning and jumping to logistics before ambivalence was resolved.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and checked in ('Want to tweak the message?'), but tended to present pre-packaged answers rather than jointly building solutions. Lukas's own adaptations (casual text, 'dumb memes' tone) showed suggestions weren't initially calibrated to his world.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed client choice ('You decide what to share,' 'You pick which to start with'), and the client noted the absence of lecturing. Eagerness to supply scripts sometimes pressured toward action, and the final 'on my own terms' statement went unreinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early questions drew out the client's own values language effectively. The session then shifted to advice-and-scripts mode, supplying solutions rather than eliciting Lukas's own ideas, leaving the plan largely counselor-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited choice at several points, but frequently assumed the expert role by supplying ready-made scripts and plans. The client's contributions shaped the plan only modestly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created space for change talk that elicited meaningful client language ('that version of me wasn't really the person I'm trying to be'). Did not actively deepen these statements with affirmations or evocative follow-ups.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk and reframed it reasonably well, but pivoting was limited. When Lukas said he wasn't ready to make a plan, the counselor proceeded with concrete plans anyway rather than exploring his hesitation.
