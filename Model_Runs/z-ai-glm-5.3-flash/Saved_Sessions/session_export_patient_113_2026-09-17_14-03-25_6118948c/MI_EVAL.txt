# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect maintained without judgment, but warmth was formulaic and generic (e.g., 'Totally understandable'). No explicit affirmations of Rowan's honesty, resilience, or self-direction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections, but empathy was repeatedly cut short by pivoting to option menus and ratings. The deeper emotional core of Rowan's resistance (fear of permanent obligations and being managed) was never deeply reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed anchored to the target behavior and moved Rowan from outright refusal to a concrete agreed experiment. Minor drift into side topics and repetitive plan recaps at the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration existed (e.g., asking check-in timing preference), but the pattern was largely counselor-offers-options, client-picks. Rowan had to repeatedly reject worksheets, scripts, and pouches to reshape the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A relative strength: the experiment was consistently framed as Rowan's choice and his conditions were honored. Constant option menus slightly undercut this by making the counselor the architect of every choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation emerged but was mostly accommodated rather than actively evoked. The counselor never explored why 'not telling anyone' mattered to Rowan or what his own reasons for change were.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor repeatedly positioned himself as option-generator and instruction-giver, prompting direct pushback ('I'm not a worksheet'). Rowan's key contribution was supplied despite the counselor's structure, not because of it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk appeared ('I can do a week,' 'it's my thing') but largely by accident rather than skillful evocation. No deepening questions or commitment-strengthening follow-ups were used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly and redirected rather than dwelled upon, and it softened over the session. Offering 'accept the risk' as an explicit option somewhat validated the status quo.
