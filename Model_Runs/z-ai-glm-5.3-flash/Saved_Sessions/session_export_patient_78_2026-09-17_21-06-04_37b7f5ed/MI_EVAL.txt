# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, validating smoking as 'her one thing' without judgment and explicitly stating 'no pressure—your pace, your call.' No labeling or disapproval appeared even when Lucía was dismissive or defensive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, capturing subtle emotional content like the daughter's quiet look, guilt, and the wish to be seen as a whole person. The client repeatedly confirmed feeling understood ('You got most of that right').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on smoking behavior change with steady momentum from frustration to a concrete plan and follow-up. Every exploration (swimming, stress) was tied back to replacing smoking's function, with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was fully co-created—success criteria came from Lucía's own metrics and she negotiated terms ('No lectures though. Deal?'). The clinician asked rather than told throughout, confirming a genuinely equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively reinforced ('your choices are yours,' 'not because anyone's grading you') and the refusal to commit beyond one day was fully honored. The follow-up was framed as permission-seeking, making client ownership unmistakable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from the client herself—the swim idea, success criteria, and follow-up all originated from Lucía. She explicitly noted 'Nobody's actually asked me that before,' confirming the evocative approach.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly located expertise in the client ('you're the expert on your own life here') and let her define success criteria and terms. Lucía's own words shaped the entire Saturday plan rather than clinician prescription.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and selectively amplified, drawing out intrinsic reasons (pride, her daughter noticing) and specific commitments like Saturday's swim. Open questions such as 'What made those couple days work?' produced the session's strongest client motivation language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced, and double-sided reflections effectively acknowledged ambivalence while pivoting toward change. The score reflects only that some reflections gave substantial airtime to sustain talk (e.g., 'five minutes of breathing') before redirecting.
