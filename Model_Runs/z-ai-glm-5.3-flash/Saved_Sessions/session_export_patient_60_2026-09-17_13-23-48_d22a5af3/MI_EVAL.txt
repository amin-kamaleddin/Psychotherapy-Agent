# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 **☑3** ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental, respectful tone maintained throughout; never criticized the patient's drinking or dismissed his perspective. Basic validation present ("I hear you — being forced into this would be frustrating"). However, no affirmations of strengths, no warmth beyond procedural courtesy, no explicit positive regard. Neutral-professional rather than genuinely supportive.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 **☑2** ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Reflective listening nearly absent. Responses were mostly checklists, question batteries, and document templates (9-question intake list, statement drafts, submission checklists). No complex reflections of frustration, defensiveness, or the meaning behind minimizing ("I turn 21 in a month," "everyone was drinking too"). The single empathic statement was not followed up or deepened.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 **☑2** ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Focus maintained but on the wrong target. Sanction resolution, evidence requests, and letter drafting dominated the session. The clinical target — alcohol use and minimization — was never explored after initial screening questions. Counselor implicitly adopted the patient's framing ("I'm not a problem drinker") by not returning to it. Strong direction toward paperwork; nearly absent direction toward the target behavior.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 **☑2** ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Uneven interaction: patient provided facts, counselor produced plans, letters, and checklists in an expert-driven fashion. No joint problem-solving about drinking, no negotiated agenda beyond sanction logistics. Resembled a legal/administrative consultation more than an equal-partner interview.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 **☑3** ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Offered choices at times ("Which would you prefer?", "contest vs. mitigate", course options) and avoided pressure or coercion. However, most of the session involved prescribing specific steps and producing finished documents rather than exploring what the patient wanted for himself. Autonomy respected procedurally but not actively fostered around behavioral change.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** **☑1** ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Essentially no effort to elicit the patient's own motivations for changing his drinking. Never explored values (internship ambitions, self-image as the "responsible one" who called Derek), never asked about his relationship with alcohol, and failed to develop the spontaneously offered concern about his record. Motivation for change left untouched; session resolved paperwork, not ambivalence.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 **☑2** ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Counselor assumed a task-manager role: templates, checklists, and recommendations without inviting the patient's expertise about his own drinking or life. Preferences occasionally accommodated (softening the "accept responsibility" language), but this was drafting collaboration on paperwork, not conveying that wisdom about change resides within the patient.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 **☑2** ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Virtually no deliberate evocation of change talk about alcohol use. Never asked how drinking fits into his life, what he values, or what risks he sees. A rare glimmer of forward-looking concern ("it showing up when I'm applying for internships... that'd actually suck") was not reflected or amplified. Change talk was largely about resolving the sanction, not the target behavior.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 **☑2** ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ("It wasn't my fault," "I'm not some problem drinker," "that's not a crime... for one more month") was neither reinforced nor strategically softened. Mostly ignored and bypassed with administrative tasks rather than met with double-sided reflections or gentle discrepancies. Avoiding it is better than amplifying it, but no skilled pivoting toward change.
