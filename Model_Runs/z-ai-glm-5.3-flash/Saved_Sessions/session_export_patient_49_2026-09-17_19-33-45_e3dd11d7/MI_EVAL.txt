# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with ambivalence explicitly normalized ('both of those make complete sense'). The patient's closing gratitude reflects felt acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, mirroring the patient's own words ('unwind thing,' 'the real deal'). Complex double-sided reflections captured her ambivalence skillfully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear progressive focus on cutting back smoking, moving from ambivalence to specific plan, slip planning, and success markers. The session ended with a structured summary and concrete follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint work with phrases like 'anything you'd want to plan for together?' Expertise was shared when invited, and the plan was co-constructed from the patient's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was consistently affirmed and information was offered at the patient's explicit request. Not a 5 only because autonomy emphasis was implicit rather than reinforced with explicit ownership statements (e.g., 'it's completely your decision') in the later planning phase.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within the client, never argued for by the clinician. The patient's own reasons (job smile, hidden gum damage, real check-ins) became the engine of her commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('You know your life, your routines... better than anyone'). The entire plan—gum, coworker disclosure, reset plan—came from her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation with open questions that elicited strong change talk ('skipping that first break smoke seems doable,' 'I think I want to try it'). Each instance was reflected and amplified, guiding planning that produced commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification and consistently paired with change talk via double-sided reflections. The patient progressively resolved her own ambivalence.
