# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, reframing the cousin's acceptance as a strength and normalizing Julian's anxiety. Affirmations were at times frequent and formulaic ('That's smart'), and could be more varied and individually attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with complex reflections capturing ambivalence and internal barriers (e.g., 'asking feels like making this real'). Deeper exploration of the emotional weight and shame around the court involvement would have elevated this further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained an exceptionally clear focus on completing community service hours, progressing logically from location to barriers, social support, a specific 3:00 first step, and a one-week success marker. No topic drift; every reflective moment served the change agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplified joint work — 'If we put our heads together...' — with Julian's ideas (food bank, cousin, timing) determining every step of the plan. The tone was genuinely shared problem-solving with the client holding the expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed Julian's ownership ('What's your take on it?'), and all solutions emerged from him without advice or directives. Lacked explicit choice statements ('it's completely up to you'), and the structured closing didn't formally offer the option to decline or modify the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All reasons for change came from Julian himself ('putting it off doesn't make the deadline go away'), with no motivations supplied by the counselor. The visible shift from guarded compliance to self-authored commitment demonstrates highly effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Julian as the expert, with nearly every plan element originating from him (food bank, cousin, 3 o'clock timing). The counselor explicitly credited him repeatedly, and open questions drew out client expertise rather than prescribing solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through evocative, scaling, and future-oriented questions, reinforced each time with affirmations and reflections. Julian's language visibly shifted from avoidance ('I don't know, man') to self-authored commitment ('I'll text him right when I get home so I don't talk myself out of it').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk, reflecting ambivalence briefly and pivoting to solutions (e.g., eliciting Julian's own coping thought about volunteers in his situation). Some early reflections repeated the worry framing without immediately pivoting toward change.
