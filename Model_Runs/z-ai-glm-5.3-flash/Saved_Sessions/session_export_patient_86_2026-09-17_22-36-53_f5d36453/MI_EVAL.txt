# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental throughout—no lecture despite the client bracing for one, and his challenges were welcomed openly. Boundaries were honored and his skepticism accepted without defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured Marco's metaphors ('losing an old friend,' 'forty years living in your hands'). However, deeper emotional content—his father dying at 68 and grief beneath the friend-loss metaphor—went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on smoking and progressed from ambivalence to a concrete self-generated experiment. Some circular repetition near the end drew a call-out from the client ('We're back to this again?').

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-way: Marco set the agenda and his ideas (hands, shed, tally) shaped the session, which the counselor openly acknowledged. The client's closing comfort confirms an equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary—decisions repeatedly kept in Marco's hands, suggestion framed as curiosity and transformed by him into his own plan on his terms. No pressure or coercion anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within Marco, producing multiple novel insights he'd never voiced (the hands, untasted autopilot cigarettes, the cigarette as his only 'full stop'). Reflections credited these discoveries to him.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise consistently located in Marco, who set the agenda and whose contributions visibly determined session direction. The counselor explicitly named that the best ideas came from him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions tied to Marco's own statements elicited desire, ability, specific ideas, and self-generated commitment, each reflected and amplified. He ended with his own realization that the cigarette is really about the pause.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was rolled with rather than confronted—boundaries validated and resistance sidestepped via the 'watching yourself smoke' reframe. Some statements ('maybe nothing does help,' father's early death) received only brief acknowledgment.
