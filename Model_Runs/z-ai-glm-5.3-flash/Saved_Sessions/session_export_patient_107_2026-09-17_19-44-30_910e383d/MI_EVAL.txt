# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly removing judgment ('you get to decide if it's one'). The clinician validates the client's reality without disapproval even when he defends his habit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening on every turn, with complex reflections capturing both surface content and underlying meaning (e.g., 'caught between is this worth looking at? and I can't function without it').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, coherent focus with a strong evocative arc and no topic drift. Falls short of 5 only because the clinician leans on following rather than gently steering or consolidating with a values-linked summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine two-way exploration where client ideas (tea swap, fair test) shape the session's course. The clinician acts as an interested companion, never an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy explicitly and repeatedly reinforced with no pressure or controlling language anywhere. Client boundaries (morning cup, deadline week) are honored without covert steering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation systematically drawn from within with zero advice-giving, culminating in the client's deepest motivation ('the arguing in my head stops'). The client's progression from defensiveness to tentative commitment demonstrates masterful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise repeatedly located within the client ('That came from you, not me'), with every forward movement originating in client speech. Exemplifies power sharing at the highest level.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout domain: amplifying ambivalence via reflective wordplay, selective reflections ending on change material, and questions that elicit progressively stronger change talk ('maybe it's not perfect' to 'maybe I'll do a bit of reading').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk consistently pivoted toward change without dismissal or argument, using double-sided reflections and affirming practical wisdom. The clinician lets the client's own change talk do the work.
