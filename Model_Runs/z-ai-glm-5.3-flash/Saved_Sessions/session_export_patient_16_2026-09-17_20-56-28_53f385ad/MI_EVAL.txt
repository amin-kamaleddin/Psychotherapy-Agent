# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard with genuine affirmations ('Seven out of ten is real confidence'). Accepted the client's minimization without lecturing — especially notable given her stated fear of being lectured. No labeling or moralizing occurred.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were consistently accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both practical and emotional dimensions (e.g., stress after bad practice as the trigger moment). Skillfully reflected her athletic identity and automatic urges.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the vaping/marijuana target from opening reflection through the closing plan summary. Each turn advanced toward change with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine exchange between equals — the counselor asked rather than told, and the entire action plan was co-constructed from the client's own material. The final summary restated her plan for confirmation rather than issuing directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively reinforced throughout ('You know your schedule better than anyone'), with choices explicitly offered and the plan consistently framed as hers. Her closing surprise at not being lectured is direct evidence she experienced genuine choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All major motivations — scouts, college, fourth-quarter sharpness — were drawn out of the client through open questions, with no persuasion or external reasoning. Her internalization was evident ('saying it out loud like that makes it feel more doable').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located within the client ('that's a pretty sharp idea, and it came from you'), and every plan element (pen strategy, phone tally, Sarah loop) originated from her. She explicitly acknowledged the effect: 'you keep asking me stuff that makes me think.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and deepened change talk, progressing from tentative ('maybe not using once season starts') to committed ('I'll do the phone thing this week'). Elicited confidence, vivid desired outcomes, and a concrete first step.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally used skillful pivots and reframes rather than reinforcing sustain talk (e.g., 'What if the easy option wasn't as easy?'). Points lost for a slightly generous validation of minimization ('that's just good life') and for closing without a double-sided reflection addressing her parting 'it's mostly just fun with my friend.'
