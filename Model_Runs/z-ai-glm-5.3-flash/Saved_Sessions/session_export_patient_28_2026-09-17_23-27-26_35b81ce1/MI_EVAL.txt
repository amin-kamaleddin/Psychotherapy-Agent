# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, non-judgmental tone and normalizes Dante's struggles while affirming his self-knowledge. Affirmations are frequent but somewhat formulaic, lacking deeply individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture ambivalence and later emotional material like shame about 'talking' vs. doing and fear of his wife's skeptical 'look.' Missed opportunities to probe deeper into the emotional weight of repeated failures keep this below a 5.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear progressive focus—from past success to barriers to a concrete plan to relapse anticipation. Each question advances momentum toward exercise without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is entirely Dante-designed and co-constructed, with his insights treated as authoritative. The clinician builds around his ideas rather than clinical recommendations.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Dante generates all ideas himself and the psychiatrist never prescribes, reinforcing ownership. The clinician never explicitly affirms Dante's freedom to choose his pace, especially on the emotionally harder step of confiding in his wife.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently elicited from within the client through questions about past success, values, success criteria, and hopes. Dante's deeply personal motivation—'to feel like that guy again'—emerges from him and is reinforced each time.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client, and Dante's contributions demonstrably shape the session. The clinician builds on each of his ideas rather than directing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully and repeatedly invites Dante's own reasons and plans, evoking progressively stronger change talk ('I'll tell her this week,' 'that's really what I want'). Each instance is reflected and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk like 'what's a walk gonna do?' and 'I usually quit everything' is met with double-sided reflections and pivots toward change. The fear of his wife's skepticism is acknowledged but not deeply explored as a barrier.
