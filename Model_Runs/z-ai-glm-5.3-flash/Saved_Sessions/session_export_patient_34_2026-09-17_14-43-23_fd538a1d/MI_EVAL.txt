# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance despite Dylan's dismissiveness, never labeling, criticizing, or moralizing about the drinking or conduct violation. Warmth remained somewhat procedural, accommodating Dylan's minimal-effort framing without affirming his strengths or worth beyond the practical task.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly and accurately reflected Dylan's frustration and sense of unfairness with validating statements about the party, singling out, and upcoming 21st birthday. Reflections stayed at the surface level of annoyance and practical goals without exploring the meaning beneath the defensiveness, such as feeling controlled or anxiety about his record.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifted from the target behavior (underage drinking) to crisis-management scripting for the disciplinary meeting and never returned to the drinking or its risks. The target behavior was effectively abandoned by the midpoint after Dylan deflected with 'I don't need a strategy, I need this to just go away.'

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The interaction was polite and cooperative, with check-ins at each step and co-editing of the statement. However, collaboration occurred only on a narrow administrative task rather than the clinical problem, with Dylan dictating terms while the counselor complied instead of working as equals with mutual expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered menu-based choices and deferred to Dylan's preferences with no pressure, coercion, or lecturing. Autonomy support became compliance, however, as the counselor followed Dylan away from the clinical agenda without asserting that engaging on the drinking was also his choice, so ownership of the change process was never fostered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Expressed no curiosity about Dylan's own motivations, values, or ambivalence regarding alcohol, instead supplying practical artifacts like scripts and closers. The reasonable evocative opener went nowhere after Dylan deflected, and Dylan's mild softening at the end ('maybe if this thing somehow blows up, I'll swing back') went unreinforced.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Positioned himself as a service provider and offered menu-based choices, sharing some power with the client. But he never acknowledged Dylan as the expert on his own life or explored his ideas about what would help, and Dylan substantially steered the session with minimal reciprocal influence.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was almost no deliberate evocation of change talk about the target behavior, and the spontaneous admission ('I was there, I had a few drinks') was left unexplored with no reflection, affirmation, or follow-up. No questions were asked about downsides of drinking, future goals, importance, confidence, or discrepancies between Dylan's values and behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Absorbed substantial sustain talk ('everyone does this,' 'it's stupid 'cause I'm basically 21 anyway,' 'this whole thing's BS') without strategic redirection, reframing, or double-sided reflections. The counselor actually reinforced the status quo by drafting statements that explicitly avoided any acknowledgment of drinking, aligning with minimization rather than gently introducing discrepancy.
