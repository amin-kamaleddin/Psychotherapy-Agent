# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validating the client's defensiveness and normalizing the discussion without blame. The client explicitly noted not feeling lectured or judged, though responses were at times procedural and lacked explicit affirmations of her worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered accurate reflections capturing the client's ambivalence between being a protective mother and needing a break, and validated smoking as her coping mechanism. Deeper emotional content (guilt, fear of not forgiving herself) was acknowledged only implicitly rather than reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior (smoking/smoke exposure) from the opening menu through the closing plan, with concrete actionable steps. Minor drift on the doctor-response option and some goal flexibility kept the session from progressing more consistently toward change itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The final plan reflected the client's stated limits and contributions, but power-sharing was uneven—counselor presented fully-formed solutions and prescriptions rather than eliciting her strategies first and building jointly.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choice via menus and check-ins, with explicit 'without pressure' language reinforcing the client's control and accepting her limit of not quitting today. Some advice blocks felt like directives rather than options, with ownership not consistently handed back to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Relied heavily on information provision and suggestions rather than drawing motivation from within the client. Missed opportunities to explore her pregnancy quit experience, her values as a mother, and what her evening breaks mean to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frequently asked for the client's input, respecting her as a decision-maker, but the overall stance leaned expert-driven with prescriptive advice blocks. The counselor never explicitly framed her as the expert on her own life or explored what had worked for her before.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged ('if it helps him, it's worth it,' 'maybe we could talk about quitting sometime') but was driven by the client's own momentum rather than skillful evocation. The counselor did not strategically reinforce or deepen these moments, and practical advice shifted focus away from building motivation to quit.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled minimizations and rationalizations with brief, non-arguing information rather than debate, and validated the function of smoking without amplifying reasons to keep smoking. Extended advice-giving occasionally diluted focus, and sustain talk was neutralized rather than actively reframed toward change.
