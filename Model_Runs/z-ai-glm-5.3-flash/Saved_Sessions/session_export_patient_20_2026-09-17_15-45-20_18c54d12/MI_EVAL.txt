# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout; defensiveness and minimization met with validation. Praise was somewhat formulaic and lacked individualized affirmations of Brandon's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured ambivalence and emotion. Emotional core (sister's disappointment, Dave's comment) was acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use, moving from assessment through readiness scales to a concrete behavioral plan. Each turn built momentum without wandering off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine two-way exchange with permission-seeking and a fully co-constructed plan (water alternation, script line, reminder, Saturday 4pm check-in). Suggestions built on Brandon's stated preferences rather than overriding them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice repeatedly reinforced; Brandon controlled the pace and approach, and 'no press conference' was accepted. Option lists occasionally shifted toward a consultant role, and autonomy could be more explicitly affirmed during moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from the client via rulers, values exploration, and his own reasons (nephew's game, sister's disappointment). His closing commitment language reflects internally generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Brandon was positioned as the expert via permission-seeking and menu options, and he selected the strategy, script, and check-in time. Occasionally advice-heavy with multiple suggestions rather than fully eliciting his own solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through scaling questions and values exploration, then reinforced Brandon's own motivation ('maybe I actually want this to work'). Helped develop commitment language and a concrete, client-chosen action plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk consistently met with neutral or double-sided reflections rather than reinforcement, pivoting to client-identified costs. Some sustain talk was restated without reframing, and the counselor occasionally echoed skepticism without redirecting toward change.
