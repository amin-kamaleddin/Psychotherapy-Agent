### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist normalizes hesitation ('no deadline here,' 'taking time to think is completely okay') and frames every decision as the patient's to shape. No pressure or agenda is imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently draw out the patient's own motivations and solutions, eliciting key insights like the 'don't ask me about it' condition and the draft-now-send-later strategy. Change talk emerges naturally from the patient rather than from persuasion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The tone is that of equal collaborators ('I'd love to figure that out together, starting from your ideas'), with the patient voicing most of the content and steering the plan. The psychiatrist follows the patient's lead throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully highlights and deepens change talk ('that's change talk worth listening to') and explicitly pauses on the 'nervous-but-maybe' shift to consolidate it. The patient moves from 'maybe' to concrete behavioral commitment (drafting the text).

#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of being caught, worry about failing) is reflected without confrontation, and gentle reality-testing ('has anyone actually said anything before?') helps the patient loosen anxiety-based predictions herself. No argumentation or direct contradiction is used.
