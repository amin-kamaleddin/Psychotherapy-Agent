# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'You're doing the right thing by asking for reassurance,' and Nadine reported feeling 'not made to feel crazy.' Affirmations were somewhat brief and generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening and consistent validation of exhaustion and worry. Reflections tended to be shallow transitions into advice rather than exploring her deeper fears of 'doing something wrong.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus throughout — every exchange advanced a concrete plan (questions, log, partner-feed script, 3 PM follow-up) tied to the target concerns. The session never wandered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative — the counselor asked before offering, built on Nadine's suggestions, and co-constructed next steps. Concrete materials (question lists, logs, scripts) were counselor-generated rather than jointly created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently respected with meaningful options ('Would you like A or B?') and Nadine making all key choices. Heavy use of prescriptive content reduced her ownership of strategy design.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Weakest area — the counselor relied on supplying tools, scripts, and information rather than drawing out Nadine's own motivations, values, and confidence. Open evocative questions and confidence rulers were absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nadine's choices genuinely steered the session content, and the counselor actively built on her requests. The flow of expertise came mostly from the counselor rather than being drawn from Nadine's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively reinforced Nadine's change-relevant statements, used choice questions rather than prescribing, and she voiced increased hope ('maybe I can actually get through a night soon'). Change talk centered on concrete tasks rather than deeper personal motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about fears and doubts was acknowledged without amplification and reframed toward actionable steps. Doubt was neutralized rather than strategically softened — no double-sided reflections were used to resolve ambivalence.
