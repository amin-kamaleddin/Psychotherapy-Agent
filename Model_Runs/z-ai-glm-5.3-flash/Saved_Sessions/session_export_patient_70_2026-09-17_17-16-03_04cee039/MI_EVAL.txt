# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance with multiple affirmations, normalized Lars's shame about education, and offered non-stigmatizing options around his criminal record. Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, lacking explicit expression of unconditional regard beyond his goals.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Lars's ambivalence, including the emotional sting of literacy struggles and the risk context. Reflections were somewhat brief and action-oriented, with limited exploration of deeper emotional themes like his absent father.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained exceptional focus, with every exchange tied to reducing reoffending risk and concrete, actionable questions throughout. Tangible outcomes were achieved with no topic drift, and new topics were folded into future session plans.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was strongly collaborative—completing the form side by side, drafting texts Lars could modify, with Lars explicitly noting the lack of lecturing. However, the counselor tended to propose the structure (menus, drafts) rather than inviting Lars to co-generate solutions from scratch.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently supported Lars's choice through menu options and explicit deferral to his judgment, with Lars exercising real control over session decisions. The menus slightly constrained the range of options Lars generated himself, with less explicit affirmation of his ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor drew motivation from Lars rather than lecturing, with reflections consistently amplifying his own reasons for change and Lars articulating motivations in his own words. Evocation could have gone deeper—exploring the father theme, scaling confidence—or allowed Lars's motivations to develop before offering options.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lars's contributions were central: his choice of the car job shaped the session's focus, and repeated menu-based choices positioned him as the decision-maker. The counselor sometimes provided fairly structured options rather than open-ended co-creation, slightly limiting full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frequently evoked and reinforced change talk with an opening question eliciting desire and commitment language, then consistently reflecting and building on Lars's statements. Key change statements were not always deepened (e.g., wanting to be a different dad), and confidence/importance ratings were not elicited to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly without amplification, with effective pivots to concrete alternatives and values-based responses to self-doubt. Redirections were somewhat quick, and more double-sided reflections could have fully resolved ambivalence.
