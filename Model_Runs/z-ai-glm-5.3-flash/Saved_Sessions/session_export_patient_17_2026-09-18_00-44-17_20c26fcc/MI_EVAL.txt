# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys unconditional regard, even validating the patient's ambivalence and fear of failure as understandable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, affect-laden reflections throughout ('that mixture of shame and love for her sounds heavy'), effectively deepening her exploration of shame and hiding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation moves steadily toward a concrete, self-authored first step (telling her daughter this weekend) while remaining patient-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange is genuinely collaborative — the clinician invites her expertise, and the plan (telling her daughter, slip protocol) comes entirely from the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasizes 'you know your life best,' asking what she thinks might work rather than directing, and supports her self-generated plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's own motivations and solutions are evoked rather than provided — insights like 'shame grows in silence' and 'thinking is where I lose' are entirely hers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician reflects, summarizes, and hands the lead back at each turn; the patient does nearly all the substantive working-through.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are sequenced well to evoke change talk, drawing out commitment language ('that's real, somehow,' 'I won't hide') and even a testable verbal rehearsal of what she'd say to her daughter.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (excuses, fear of failing, 'leave me alone') is met with dual reflections holding ambivalence ('both of those can be true') rather than confrontation.
