# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard with no judgment about smoking or past slips. Warmly normalizes setbacks ('missing a day doesn't have to mean the whole plan falls apart') and acknowledges Samir's self-criticism with respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses accurate reflections throughout, including a sophisticated double-sided reflection about the quiet minutes versus freer breathing. Some reflections restate client content almost verbatim rather than adding deeper emotional or meaning-level interpretation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on asthma management behaviors from start to finish, with each question building progressively from priorities to concrete steps, maintenance, slips, and finally the smoking change. No topic drift, and pacing was respected throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely between equals—never prescribing, with all solutions originating from Samir. The counselor does most of the agenda sequencing, however, with little explicit invitation for Samir to shape the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit and consistent autonomy support: 'I'm not here to tell you what to change' and immediate respect when Samir says 'don't push me too fast.' All ideas, including patches, come from Samir himself, preserving full ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn almost entirely from within Samir—his reasons, strategies, and insights. The counselor consistently asks 'what do you think' rather than advising, and successfully elicits values, confidence, and emerging readiness for the harder change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Samir as the expert ('You know your life better than anyone'), with every plan element originating from him. The counselor's contributions build on his ideas rather than replacing them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk with open questions, strategically highlighting shifts ('Listen to what you just said—that breathing matters more than a spotless cab'). Samir moved from 'I don't know if I can give it all up' to 'maybe I'm more ready than I thought.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids dwelling on reasons against change, using double-sided reflections to honor ambivalence while pivoting toward change. Sustain talk is sometimes restated fairly fully before redirecting, which slightly extends focus on the status quo.
