# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, respect, and nonjudgment landed well — the patient noted she 'came in expecting a lecture.' Not top marks due to somewhat formulaic affirmations and limited individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections of ambivalence and 'me time,' but reflections are often thin, restating logistics rather than emotional meaning. The patient's fear for her health and her mother's cancer warnings went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, steady focus on smoking reduction with a complete, concrete plan. However, the alcohol concern was silently dropped without explicit collaborative negotiation of scope.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed — she named times, chose the tea and walk fallback, and designed the slip rule. Slightly directive when stacking pre-packaged tips (gum, alarm) rather than jointly brainstorming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was consistently reinforced ('No pressure — your choice'), her quitline refusal was honored, and the patient explicitly said the plan was 'mine, and I chose it' — a hallmark of successful autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, choices, and confidence scaling elicited strong client-generated reasons and strategies. Held back from 5 by frequently supplying solutions before fully evoking the patient's own ideas and missing deeper values exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient was repeatedly positioned as the expert, and her choices shaped the printed plan verbatim. Early menu options and practical tips had a slightly counselor-generated, advisory flavor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective elicitation produced strong commitments ('three instead of six,' maternal motivation, 'I chose it') plus confidence scaling and concrete planning. Her strongest change talk — her motivation as a mother — was passed over rather than amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Minimization ('not like people I work with') was not reinforced and was gently redirected, but sustain talk was never actively reframed with double-sided reflections. Excuse-quality statements ('am I making excuses?') were validated without exploration.
