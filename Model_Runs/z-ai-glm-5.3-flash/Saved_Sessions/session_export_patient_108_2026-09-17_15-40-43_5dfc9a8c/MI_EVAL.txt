# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, countering Oliver's self-criticism and treating his ambivalence with compassion. Warmth felt somewhat practical/scripted rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured Oliver's ambivalence and fear of backpedaling. Reflections were occasionally brief, with limited deep exploration of the guilt's emotional roots and anticipated loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained tight focus on the target behavior from the first message, with every turn advancing toward action. Moved logically from ambivalence to a concrete, paste-ready plan without drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session read as joint problem-solving — Oliver selected and modified options, and the final text was co-edited with his own additions. Counselor occasionally moved quickly to providing solutions rather than jointly generating them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices about method, timing, and next steps, letting Oliver set his own timeline with no pressure or coercion. Slight deduction because the counselor supplied script and plan content rather than always eliciting Oliver's own words first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicited Oliver's own motivations through values questions and rehearsal that drew out determination language, leaving him feeling surer of himself. Some content (scripts, after-plan) was supplied rather than drawn from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Counselor repeatedly deferred to the client's choices, and Oliver's preferences materially shaped the session, including the script, role-play, and his own edits to the final text. Excellent client-led structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively solicited values, used a readiness approach, and structured rehearsal that produced strong commitment language ('My job here is done,' 'I'll send it right when I get home'). Could have deepened commitment language further, e.g., through confidence scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (guilt, comfort of companionship) was acknowledged and redirected toward reasons for change and concrete action without dwelling on it. Missed occasional chances to reframe guilt as evidence of his kindness.
