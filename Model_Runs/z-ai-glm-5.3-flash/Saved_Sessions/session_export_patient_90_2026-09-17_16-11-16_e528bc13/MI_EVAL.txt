# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with consistent affirmations ('You're doing the right thing,' 'Nice work'), though affirmations were somewhat generic and validation of the emotional weight of the dog and missed doses remained brief.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections tracked both practical and emotional content, such as the fear of running out at night and dread of the dog conversation, but reflections often stayed surface-level and pivoted quickly to scripts and action lists.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on asthma management throughout, driving to a concrete prioritized action plan; every exchange, including finances, work, and the dog, was tied back to the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine back-and-forth with permission-seeking and adaptation to Marcus's budget and ideas, but power-sharing was uneven since scripts and lists were largely counselor-drafted.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and let Marcus select options with no pressure to rehome the dog, but rapid menu-provision subtly constrained space for self-generated choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effective evocation (the 'one specific thing' question drew commitment talk), but most solutions, priorities, and scripts were counselor-supplied rather than drawn from Marcus; confidence language was never evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus's choices were invited and incorporated, but the session followed an expert-driven pattern with counselor-generated plans that Marcus mostly reacted to rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked commitment and activation talk ('let's start with the refill thing,' 'I can do three small things') anchored to values, but ability/confidence language was not explicitly drawn out.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to ambivalence about the dog and fear of judgment with empathy and practical non-rehoming alternatives, transforming resistance into willingness, though early barrier-focused questions gave some air to sustain themes.
