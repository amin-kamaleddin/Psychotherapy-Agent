# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validating ambivalence with responses like 'That makes total sense' and 'Totally understandable to worry about a lifelong pill.' Warmth was present but somewhat procedural, with limited explicit affirmation of Richard's own strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captured ambivalence ('You're torn — feeling fine makes it easy to downplay risk, yet you clearly want to stay active with your wife') and integrated his values. Occasional drift toward information-giving slightly limited the depth of emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior (cholesterol treatment adherence) from opening line to close, with each turn advancing the plan. Richard's own statement 'I've got a plan now' confirms concrete movement toward change with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction in which the counselor offered structure while Richard supplied the content (his worries, Maria's role, question topics, format preferences). The one-pager incorporated his exact meds, wine habit, and wife's involvement, demonstrating full power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was consistently reinforced through two treatment pathways, stop/switch criteria, and building in 'a way out or down' from medication. Richard's comment that the session 'doesn't feel as heavy' reflects his perceived ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within via open questions, values exploration, and the confidence ruler, with Richard articulating his own reasons for change. Slightly below 5 because the counselor supplied considerable content (safety criteria, plan templates) rather than consistently evoking every element.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Richard as the decision-maker ('Which of those feels more acceptable to you?') and shaped plan documents from his input. More explicit acknowledgment of Richard's expertise and less counselor-provided structure would reach a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk using open questions and a confidence ruler, with Richard's confidence rising from 6 to 7.5 and statements like 'I'll give it a real shot.' Deeper exploration of success imagery (e.g., the retirement trip) was left underdeveloped.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification and reframed into safety planning, softening the 'life sentence' fear with stop/switch criteria. Minor point off because some sustain talk was only reflected back rather than actively pivoted toward his stated values.
