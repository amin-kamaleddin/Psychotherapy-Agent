# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist consistently honors Amy's autonomy—supporting the low-effort, casual approach with Sara rather than pushing a more ambitious plan. Resistance is met with acceptance ('I hear the hesitation too') rather than confrontation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, complex reflections appear throughout, e.g., naming the phone as filling silence, validating FOMO, and echoing the client's own metaphors ('silence doesn't swallow me whole'). The client repeatedly feels understood and elaborates in response.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation follows a clear evocative arc: from 'I don't know what I'd do' to a concrete plan (show night Thursday, phones in kitchen, telling Sara at 8pm). Each transition question steers toward specific, self-generated commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element originates from the client, with the therapist explicitly naming ownership ('That's your idea'). Questions are consultative ('What feels most doable?') rather than directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are consistently offered (flatmate vs. baking club, casual vs. telling Sara it matters), and the therapist frames the client's incremental 'casual tonight, deeper later' approach as a valid strategy, not avoidance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist skillfully elicits change talk—reasons (connection over scrolling), ability ('that story gets a crack in it'), and commitment (telling Sara tonight, envisioning reporting back success). Hope and self-efficacy are drawn out rather than imposed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange feels like joint problem-solving, with the therapist reflecting and building on the client's ideas at each step. The tone is curious and shared, never lecturing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Numerous strategic questions ('What would it mean if Thursday goes well?', 'What would you tell me next time?') generate rich preparatory and mobilizing change talk, which the therapist reinforces by reflecting the strongest statements back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (FOMO, 'I'm just a phone person', fear of failure) is consistently acknowledged, reframed ('trying it once isn't life-changing—nothing's lost'), and met with reflections that tilt toward the client's own change-oriented words without arguing.
