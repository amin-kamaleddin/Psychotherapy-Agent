# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Warm and supportive ('I'm proud of you,' 'rooting for you') but formulaic, with no explicit affirmation of Aisha's strengths until late in the session. Early emotional statements ('gross,' avoiding mirrors) were not validated, and the warmth was thin and repetitive rather than individually meaningful.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** One strong initial reflection captured Aisha's frustration and pain, but reflective listening was quickly abandoned for advice and logistics. Deeper feelings — mirror shame, gym embarrassment, fear of quitting, and emerging hope — were never explicitly reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on resuming exercise, moving to a concrete plan with specific days, times, durations, and follow-up without drifting off topic. Not a 5 because direction came through directive, advice-heavy steering rather than MI-consistent guiding, and narrowed to logistics while bypassing body-image concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaboration exists ('want to make a plan together?', 'we can tweak') and Aisha's preferences shaped details, but power-sharing was uneven. The counselor generated nearly all content — plan, template, mirror exposure, cues — while Aisha mainly approved or adapted it rather than co-creating.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine choice points were offered (dance vs. walk, flexible scheduling, tweaks), but the overall style was prescriptive, with plan structure, tracking, and self-compassion cues delivered as repeated checklist instructions. Aisha's ownership of the change was never explicitly emphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Beyond the single opening question, the counselor supplied information, tips, and structure rather than evoking motivation. No open questions about Aisha's reasons for change, what 'comfortable in her own skin' means, or her confidence — the session ran on the counselor's agenda, not the client's.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked for input on scheduling and offered to tweak, showing some partnership. However, the stance was expert-driven, presenting a pre-packaged plan, tracking template, and protocols largely as directives, with Aisha's expertise never explicitly located within her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Aisha generated abundant spontaneous change talk ('I'm sick of feeling this way,' 'I've got this,' 'I feel ready'), but the counselor did little to evoke, deepen, or reinforce it. There were no strategic reflections, scaling questions, or explorations of her motivation — the session benefited from a motivated client rather than skill.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal, and barriers like gym embarrassment and all-or-nothing quitting were handled reasonably well with the home/no-judgment space and 5-minute make-up rule. Responses were problem-solving rather than MI-consistent softening — no double-sided reflections or affirmation of her ability to overcome barriers, and self-criticism went unexplored.
