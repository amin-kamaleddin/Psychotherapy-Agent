# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout—affirms Rosa's presence, normalizes ambivalence, and honors her fears without judgment or pressure. Affirmations like 'That's real strength talking' convey genuine acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate, capturing both sides of ambivalence and her deepest fear (losing her people, not her cigarettes). Rosa consistently builds on reflections, indicating they resonated and deepened exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, progressive focus on smoking cessation from opening to a concrete, client-owned next step. No topic drift; every exchange moves the ambivalence forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine exchange between equals—invites Rosa's input before sharing psychoeducation, and next steps are co-constructed entirely from her own ideas. Rosa's contributions demonstrably shape the session agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Rosa's ownership ('your date, your reasons, your person'), and psychoeducation is provided only responsively to her explicit request. No directive or pressuring language appears.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every motivation voiced in the session comes from Rosa herself—hopes, reasons, conditions for change, and reframing of her past attempt. The clinician never argues for quitting or supplies benefits.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise within the client ('You're the expert on your own life'), and Rosa's ideas (Denise, quit date, support needs) drive the plan. No expert plan is imposed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through strategic open questions, and Rosa's language grows progressively stronger—from 'I've got to stop' to 'something I chose, with my name on it.' Each instance is reinforced with affirming reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally handles sustain talk well with double-sided reflections and pivots toward hope and resources. Minor deduction: a few reflections briefly restated the failure narrative ('the barbecue got you') before reframing toward strength.
