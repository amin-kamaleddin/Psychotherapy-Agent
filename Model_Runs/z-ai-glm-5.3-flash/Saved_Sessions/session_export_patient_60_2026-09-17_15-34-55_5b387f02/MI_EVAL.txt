# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with no criticism of Jason's drinking or defensiveness. Limited depth due to formulaic warmth and few individualized affirmations of his genuine strengths (e.g., looking out for friends).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate but largely simple and repetitive ('That makes sense'). Content was tracked well, but the counselor rarely deepened exploration of the emotional meaning beneath Jason's defensiveness and image concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every response stayed focused on the target behavior with no topic drift. The session progressed logically from exploring the incident to a concrete plan and commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The agenda was offered to Jason and his ideas (Tyler, guest cap, checklist) shaped the plan. Collaboration weakened late as the counselor prescribed email/policy steps and delivered finished drafts rather than inviting client-generated solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language throughout ('You're in charge of what we focus on'), with choice-based menus rather than directives. Falls short of 5 because menus still structured the agenda heavily and client-generated insights weren't explicitly reinforced as his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler and the 'move from a 3 to a 5-7' question effectively elicited Jason's own reasons for change. However, the counselor relied heavily on option lists and advice rather than evoking his values or long-term goals to build discrepancy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice was invited repeatedly and Jason's contributions shaped specifics, but the second half slipped into a counselor-driven, checklist mode with pre-packaged menus and drafted messages. The frame became increasingly counselor-generated rather than client-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of the readiness ruler, especially the evocative 'what would move you from a 3 to a 5 or 7' question, which produced meaningful change talk. Missed opportunities included not exploring values to build discrepancy and meeting one commitment-oriented remark with advice rather than reinforcement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor never amplified sustain talk and used one effective reframe (planning as smart, not an admission of fault). Most responses, however, neutrally mirrored sustain talk without double-sided reflections linking it to Jason's own goals.
