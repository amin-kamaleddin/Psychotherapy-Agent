# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing Julian's avoidance and self-criticism rather than judging it. However, affirmations were sparse and generic, lacking individualized recognition of his courage in practicing the call.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opening reflections accurately captured Julian's external pressure and anxiety, and later reflections showed decent perspective-taking. Once the counselor shifted into problem-solving mode, emotional reflection largely disappeared, missing meaningful insights about shame, fear of failure, and growing confidence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering, consistent focus on completing community service hours by the 30th, with every exchange advancing concrete steps from problem to plan to rehearsal to commitment. No drift occurred, and even the role-play directly served the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the work as joint, incorporated Julian's site suggestions and tool preferences, and ran a genuinely responsive role-play. However, the power balance tilted toward counselor expertise, with the five-step plan delivered rather than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and asked permission, with Julian's own choices (phone checklist, Friday start, three mornings a week) driving the plan and no pressure or coercion. Fell short of 5 because the counselor sometimes supplied answers before Julian generated his own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied primarily on providing information, scripts, and structured plans rather than eliciting Julian's own motivations and capacities. Intrinsic insights emerged spontaneously but were never drawn out through purposeful evocative questions, treating motivation as a logistical problem rather than something to evoke.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked permission and offered choices, supporting a collaborative stance, but the session was heavily expert-driven in content. Missed opportunities to draw out Julian's own ideas, with client contributions shaping the session only modestly.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Julian produced occasional spontaneous change talk, but the counselor did little to deliberately evoke or deepen it. No open questions explored his reasons for change, no importance/confidence scaling was used, and his commitment language was rarely reflected back or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Julian's sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement, and worry was gently reframed as understandable. However, the counselor did not strategically pivot these statements toward change, such as using double-sided reflections to link his procrastination insight to his desire to avoid court.
