# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, validating Jason's grievance without judgment and explicitly framing a relapse as 'information, not failure.' No disapproval, labeling, or moralizing about the underage drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that uses Jason's own words and accurately captures his dual concerns. Could go deeper emotionally — the humiliation of being the 'lame one' and anxiety about the blackouts are acknowledged but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use from the first utterance, building steadily from problem recognition to specific commitment and follow-up plan. No topic drift; even rapport-building ties back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — every question invites Jason's perspective, and plans are co-discovered rather than prescribed. Jason's shift from defensiveness to 'I'm kind of actually planning this out, aren't I?' confirms the shared direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced: the counselor declines to add to Jason's plan and frames outcomes as his own evidence to gather. Jason is free to define success as imperfect ('if I drink a few beers, fine').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from the client — the counselor never supplies reasons to change and highlights each instance of self-generated motivation ('Those are your words'). The arc from 'this is BS' to specific commitment language demonstrates highly effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in Jason, whose contributions shape the entire plan (no-shots, Sunday buffer, definition of success). The counselor refuses to add content even at the close, and Jason's comment about 'annoyingly good questions' confirms the power-sharing worked.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's greatest strength — systematic evocation of progressively stronger commitment language, from first admission to 'I could see myself trying the no-shots thing this weekend.' The counselor strategically mirrors and reinforces each instance ('that's you building your own evidence').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is neither argued against nor reinforced; double-sided reflections pivot effectively toward change, and 'I'm not promising anything' is accepted without pushing. Early reflections briefly dwell on the unfairness grievance before redirecting, though this plausibly served rapport-building.
