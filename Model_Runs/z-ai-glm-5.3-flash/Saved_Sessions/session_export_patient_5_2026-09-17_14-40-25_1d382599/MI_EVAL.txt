# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with genuine affirmations ("You're wise to plan this"), with no labeling or disapproval. Warmth remained somewhat brief and functional rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some accurate reflections ("I hear safety is your top priority"), but emotional content—especially Alessia's fear about the medication-free washout—was acknowledged only briefly before pivoting to practical task delivery.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on the safe medication transition with a logical progression and no drift. Direction was largely information-centered rather than motivation-centered, though appropriate for a medication-education encounter.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frequent check-ins ("Would you like...?") gave partial power-sharing, but the counselor unilaterally generated the question list, safety plan, and checklist. The client stayed in a receptive role rather than co-creating the tools.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered options rather than directives and framed the medication decision as one to make with her doctor. Could more explicitly reinforce her ownership of the overall decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely drew out the client's own motivations, values, or reasons for change; her spontaneous statement "Safety comes first for me" was never explored or developed. The session moved straight to logistics, assuming motivation rather than strengthening it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and respected the client's expertise about format and next steps, but information was delivered largely in an expert-to-layperson format. A more partner-driven approach would co-create the tools with her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client change statements emerged largely from helpful tool provision rather than deliberate evocation. Forward-looking questions supported agency, but commitment language and reasons for the switch were never explicitly elicited or deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's anxiety about the washout was neither amplified nor skillfully softened—no double-sided reflections, affirmations of her capacity, or exploration to resolve ambivalence. Concerns were neutral, essentially bypassed with a checklist.
