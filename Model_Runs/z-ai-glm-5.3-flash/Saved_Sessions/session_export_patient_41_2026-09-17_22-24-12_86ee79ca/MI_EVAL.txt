# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental throughout, with specific, genuine affirmations ('that's a powerful insight, Elira'). Normalizes fear and never frames avoidance as failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture both content and emotional nuance, including the relief-vs-pride distinction later woven back into the session. Highly accurate reflective listening throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains steady focus on follow-through despite avoidance, progressing from identifying what works to a concrete, self-authored weekly plan. Each exchange advances the arc while pacing stays patient-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing—all plan elements (five-minute rule, bad day pass, written note) originated from the client, who explicitly closes saying the plan is 'mine.' Counselor proposes co-creation and invites modification of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated choice-affirming language ('on your own terms,' 'would you shape it differently?') with no imposed advice. Even affirms her honesty about ongoing avoidance rather than pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully elicits the client's own solutions via open questions ('what made that possible?', 'what would be kind but still true?'). Consistently returns ownership of insights, and the client generates her own counter-thoughts by session's end.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions expertise within the client ('You'd know better than I would what feels fair') and credits every contribution as hers. The session's shape is driven substantially by the client's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions effectively evoke commitment language ('Let's do that this week,' 'I could do that today, actually'). Amplifies and reflects change talk, ending with approach-oriented motivation ('I actually want to see if I can do it').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Pivots away from sustain talk using double-sided reflections followed by forward-looking questions. The 'bad day pass' reframe transforms fear of failure into a change-supporting structure without dwelling on it.
