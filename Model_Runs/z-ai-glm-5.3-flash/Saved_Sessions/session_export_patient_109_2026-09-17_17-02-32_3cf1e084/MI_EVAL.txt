# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive tone with repeated affirmations and nonjudgmental pre-commitment to a backup plan if Saturday fails. Falls short of 5 due to somewhat formulaic warmth and brief early sessions limiting deeper individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Rafael's competing demands and self-sabotaging patterns, and informed tailored strategies. Slightly limited by a fast pace that acknowledged but did not deeply explore discouragement over past failed follow-through.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary focus on one target behavior, building every exchange toward a concrete, measurable plan (Saturday 7:30, 20-30 minutes, bag packed, wife informed, alarm set, backup routine, 4:00 check-in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative, with permission-seeking and ideas offered for Rafael's evaluation, and the patient experienced having an actual plan. The balance tilted when the counselor supplied idea lists and the backup workout rather than co-creating them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rafael's choices were honored throughout, including his decision to tell his wife face-to-face, and the plan was largely his own construction. Occasionally prescriptive pre-packaged solution lists slightly constrained choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was substantially drawn from within, eliciting Rafael's own reasons, solutions, accountability, and rewards, with explicit intrinsic motivation named. External scaffolding (check-ins, drafted texts) supplemented rather than purely relied on internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rafael was repeatedly positioned as the expert, and his contributions genuinely shaped the plan (7:30 time, wife accountability, 4:00 check-in). Some turns slipped toward a leading, checklist-driven style with numbered idea lists.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength: readiness and confidence rulers plus elaboration of past success systematically deepened change talk, moving Rafael from ambivalence to strong commitment language and concrete pledges (alarm, bag, wife, check-in).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers were briefly acknowledged then pivoted to constructive problem-solving and contingency planning rather than amplified. Not a 5 because obstacle exploration was repeated across multiple turns, slightly prolonging the sustain-talk focus.
