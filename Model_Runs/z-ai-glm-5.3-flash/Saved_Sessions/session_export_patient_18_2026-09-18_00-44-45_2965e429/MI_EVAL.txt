# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains unconditional positive regard throughout, refusing to pathologize a lower-risk teen and affirming strengths consistently. Minor critique: affirmations follow a somewhat repetitive formula.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and captures both sides of ambivalence, including the client's social fears. Missed depth opportunity around significant family material (the aunt's drinking, the mother's fear) that was reflected cognitively but not emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus on alcohol's role in the client's social life with a visible architectural arc and no meaningful drift. Could be strengthened by pausing to summarize and explicitly checking the agenda with the client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is structured as joint inquiry between equals, with transparent, democratizing language throughout. The counselor offers zero advice or directive suggestions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is reinforced at every juncture, with choice built into questions ('if anything?') and commitment reframed as optional experimentation. The client spontaneously adopts the permissive framing, showing genuine ownership transfer.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Evocation is skillfully woven in with open questions that elicit the client's own motivations and self-generated solutions. Strong integration of change talk throughout the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly and operationally located in the client ('You're the expert on your own parties'), with zero advice or psychoeducation. The client's contributions substantially steer the entire second half of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A near-textbook evocation arc from desire to action, with every plan idea supplied by the client and change talk consistently reflected and built upon. The first third of the session is rapport-building rather than evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled skillfully without arguing, using balanced double-sided reflections and strategic pivots to small-steps questions. The counselor re-voices the sustain side a bit too frequently, and reflections could be weighted more toward the change side.
