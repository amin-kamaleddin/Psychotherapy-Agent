# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, repeatedly validating Julian ('That makes sense') and respecting his wish not to discuss the offense. Falls short of 5 due to a lack of explicit affirmations and warmth that feels somewhat formulaic and procedural.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate simple reflections but frequently pivots from reflection to advice and logistics, cutting off emotional exploration. Julian's deeper feelings—fear of seeming incompetent and stress about deadlines—received only superficial acknowledgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on completing community service hours by the deadline, with every turn tied back to logistics and next steps. Direction sometimes becomes task-driving via counselor-generated checklists rather than collaborative goal clarification tied to Julian's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is functionally expert-driven: the counselor supplies the plans, scripts, and strategies while Julian's role is largely to approve them. Julian's own ideas and expertise never substantially shape the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early autonomy-supportive language ('You choose where we start and how fast we go') and respect for Julian's texting preference are genuine strengths. As the session progresses, however, the style becomes increasingly prescriptive, implicitly taking ownership of the process away from the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is treated as residing in the counselor's advice rather than within the client, with questions largely logistical rather than motivational. No evocative questions about importance or confidence, no affirmations of strengths, and Julian's one moment of optimism was left undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor quickly assumes the expert role, delivering pre-packaged plans and drafted texts while Julian is positioned as the recipient. Although menus of options are offered, the substance of the session is entirely counselor-generated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Few deliberate efforts to evoke change talk; Julian's spontaneous preparatory change talk ('it kind of feels more doable now') was missed rather than reflected or deepened. The counselor never explores Julian's own reasons for completing the hours or elicits confidence or commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does not amplify Julian's sustain talk, but also does not strategically soften it—responses tend to be reassurance followed by more advice, papering over ambivalence. No double-sided reflections or use of sustain talk to explore what matters to Julian.
