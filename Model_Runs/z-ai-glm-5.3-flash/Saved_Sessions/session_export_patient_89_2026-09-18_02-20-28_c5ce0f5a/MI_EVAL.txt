# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms the patient's autonomy and strengths, treating him as the expert on his own life.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections capture both the content and feeling of the patient's statements (e.g., 'I hear real hope in that,' reflecting his dread of the old all-or-nothing pattern).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on behavioral change goals, maintaining a clear direction toward an actionable, concrete plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is dialogic, with the clinician reflecting and inviting rather than directing, though he occasionally leads the conversation with his own framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices and next steps are driven by the patient, with the clinician reinforcing his ownership of the plan (e.g., sneakers in the car, wife's involvement).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong use of evocative questions that draw out the patient's own reasons, ideas, and commitment ('What would success look like for you?'), rather than prescribing solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently positions the patient as the decision-maker, asking rather than telling and building the plan together from the patient's own insights.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent elicitation of change talk through open questions about past success, hopes, and what success would look like; the patient generates his own plan and expresses excitement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician responds to the patient's doubts ('small almost feels like it's not enough') with reframes rather than arguing, effectively defusing ambivalence.
