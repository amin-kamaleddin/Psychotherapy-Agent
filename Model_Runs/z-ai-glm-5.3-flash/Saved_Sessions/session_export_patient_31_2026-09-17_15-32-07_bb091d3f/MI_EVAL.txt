# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout ('slips are normal — not a failure'), and the client explicitly noted 'Thanks for not making this a lecture.' Warmth was somewhat procedural and affirmations of the client's own strengths were sparse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the client's ambivalence ('smoking helps you unwind... you didn't come to quit'), but they were brief and often followed by information-giving. Deeper emotional reflection (the meaning of the 'sacred' first cigarette, fear of failure) was limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on smoking reduction throughout; every exchange tied back to the target behavior and progressed logically to a concrete, negotiated plan with a follow-up scheduled.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative moments existed (permission-seeking, plan tweaks), but power-sharing was uneven — extended monologues on dosing, side effects, scripts, and checklists left the client in a passive recipient role much of the time.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice about pacing, options, and boundaries ('keeping evenings yours is a fair trade'), and accepted the client's refusal to quit fully. Occasionally directive information delivery and little explicit emphasis on the client's ownership of outcomes kept it from a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good evocative questions drew out the client's own reasons (cost, coughing, partner's dislike), but motivation was largely assumed to come from information. Confidence was never assessed and the client's commitment language ('I can live with that') went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited client input on options and plan tweaks, but large expert-driven segments (pharmacology lectures, prescriptive 5-step script, pre-start checklist) made the session structure largely clinician-directed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The importance ruler and option menus elicited mild change talk ('maybe that's not crazy,' 'that might work'), but emerging commitments were not deepened with evocative follow-ups or reinforced. Change talk was elicited incidentally rather than strategically.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I like smoking,' 'evenings stay mine') was acknowledged but never strategically softened — no double-sided reflections or reframing of the 'sacred' first cigarette or slipping as information. Reassurance that smoking on the patch isn't failure indirectly addressed the underlying fear.
