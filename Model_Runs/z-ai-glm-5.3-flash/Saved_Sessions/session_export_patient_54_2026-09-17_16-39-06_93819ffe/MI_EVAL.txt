# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with permission-seeking before sharing information and no pressure despite the client's defensiveness. Affirmations were brief and procedural rather than deeply individualized, keeping this below a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured her core ambivalence (tradition/independence vs. worry for the baby) and validated social pressures. Reflections occasionally pivoted quickly to questions or advice before fully exploring the emotional layer.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, purposeful focus on alcohol use in pregnancy from the opening question through concrete outcomes. Each exchange progressed toward the change goal, with role-plays tied directly to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interview proceeded between genuine equals, with plans and scripts co-constructed from the client's own social reality and ideas offered as options she selected from. Rehearsal and the partner brochure were invited, not assigned.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support—permission-seeking, choice-framing, and 'I'm not here to tell you what to do'—ideal for this control-sensitive client. The client's own words ('If it's my idea, I can do it') confirm it worked.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within the client via values exploration, self-generated plans, and discovery through rehearsal. She ended the session self-motivated and proactively requesting materials.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power sharing was actively fostered, with the client's contributions substantially shaping the plan, scripts, and rehearsal. Questions like 'Which two feel most like you?' explicitly located expertise in the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled, sustained evocation of change talk—from values questions through concrete plans and two successful behavioral rehearsals. This generated strong commitment language ('it's an actual thing I'm going to do') and proactive help-seeking.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with neutral, permission-based information and double-sided reflections rather than argument, keeping weight on change. More explicit reframing of sustain themes (e.g., tradition preserved through the NA swap) would have strengthened this further.
