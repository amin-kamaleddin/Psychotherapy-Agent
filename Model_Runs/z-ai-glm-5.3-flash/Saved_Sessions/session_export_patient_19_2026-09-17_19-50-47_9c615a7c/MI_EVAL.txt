# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, holding Anya's ambivalence without judgment and responding to her humor with warmth and accountability. Closed with genuine affirmation: 'I have real confidence in that Anya.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex, accurate reflections captured both content and emotional ambivalence, e.g., 'the cards and the pokies are connected, even though it's easier to point to the cards' and the double-sided 'Friday nights give you joy AND Monday dread costs you.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained steady focus on gambling and financial stability, tying every exchange to Anya's values (Egypt, the jar, Friday nights). Credit cards were left open-ended ('I'll think about the cards') without a gentle follow-up or agenda check at closing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was genuinely co-created — the counselor asked rather than told, built on Anya's ideas, and negotiated the closing deal ('Ask me next session'). Anya noted 'nobody's ever asked me what I wanted before.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change was consistently framed as Anya's choice with no advice imposed; the jar plan, one-Friday experiment, and accountability structures originated from her. The counselor explicitly credited the shift: 'That shift came from you.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from within the client via open questions ('What made the difference for you tonight?'), eliciting values-based reasons (Egypt, showing her mum something different). Anya's 'Saying it out loud to you made it real' demonstrated highly effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('You know your life better than anyone'), with the plan repeatedly attributed to Anya: 'That's you, not me.' Her own contributions genuinely shaped the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional evocation produced commitment language ('Nah — I can'), ability statements, and a specific plan, then deepened it by asking what made the difference. The shift from 'I haven't managed it' to 'I can' was highlighted and reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it's just my fun') was handled skillfully via double-sided reflections rather than argument. However, minimization ('The real problem is the cards') was sometimes met with neutral acknowledgment rather than active reframing or strategic pivot.
