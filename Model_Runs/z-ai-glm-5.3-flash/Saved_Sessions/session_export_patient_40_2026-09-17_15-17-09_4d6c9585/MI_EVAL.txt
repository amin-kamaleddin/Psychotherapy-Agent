# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental responses to Elias's relapse and shame, with affirmations like "That felt brave — you said the line." However, praise occasionally feels formulaic and deeper explicit statements of unconditional regard or relapse normalization are limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Elias's shame, fear, and sense that his sobriety may be circumstantial. Reflections are sometimes brief, and turns often cluster 2–3 questions, limiting deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on staying sober, managing shame triggers, and blocking the alcohol-to-pills pathway. Every element ties into a concrete, rehearsed relapse-prevention plan with a defined cue.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong co-creation of the self-compassion line, text message, and rehearsed sequence, with permission-seeking and incorporation of Elias's edits. Several early turns present pre-packaged strategy lists before eliciting his own ideas, slightly unbalancing power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice — Elias picks strategies, authors his own compassion line and cue, and options are framed as pickable or tweakable. No pressure or coercion appears anywhere; ownership is reinforced throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of confidence rulers and evocative questions drew out genuine change talk (his kids, "maybe I did stop because I wanted to"). Evocation is occasionally supplanted by information-giving, and some ambivalence moments could have been explored more deeply.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elias's expertise is central — he generates his own coping line, identifies his own triggers and cue, and the counselor asks permission before brainstorming. A few turns offer long prescriptive strategy lists before inviting his input, slightly undercutting power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Confidence rulers and "what would move you to a 7" questions produced meaningful change talk, reinforced through rehearsal and confidence tracking (5 to 7). Deeper exploration of his reasons for change could further strengthen commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ("maybe I just landed here," "tricks work until they don't") is acknowledged briefly without amplification before pivoting to confidence-building. Acknowledgments are somewhat brief, and double-sided reflections contrasting doubt with his demonstrated strengths are not used.
