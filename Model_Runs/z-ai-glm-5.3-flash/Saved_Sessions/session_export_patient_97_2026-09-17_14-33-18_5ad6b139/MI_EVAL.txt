# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance with warm validation of Mateo's record and circumstances. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and repetitive, limiting deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate but surface-level reflections of stated problems. Deeper emotional content—fear of rejection, doubt about meaning his words, guardedness—was passed over rather than reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on employment with all exchanges tied to that goal. Missed opportunities to connect practical tasks to Mateo's deeper motivation for change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaboration in places (incorporating corrections, co-producing the disclosure script), but the counselor often handed over finished products rather than building from Mateo's ideas, leaving him passive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Next steps repeatedly framed as choices, and Mateo's declines (ZIP, commitment to warehouse) were respected without pressure. Rapid solution-giving sometimes edged toward doing-for rather than self-directed choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied externally via information and materials rather than drawn from the client. Core discrepancy material—values, family hopes, what 'things being different' means—was never elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine input-seeking and preference-taking, but much of the session was expert-driven with a checklist structure. Mateo's own expertise and connections were unexplored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Modest change talk emerged and the role-play represented a small behavioral commitment. Change statements were never reflected or amplified, and open questions about reasons and values were absent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and received one empathic reframe, but responses were informational rather than MI-based. No double-sided reflections, and 'I'm not promising anything' went unaddressed.
