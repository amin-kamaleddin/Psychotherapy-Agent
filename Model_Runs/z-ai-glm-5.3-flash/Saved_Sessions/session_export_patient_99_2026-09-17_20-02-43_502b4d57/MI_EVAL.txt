# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, respect, and nonjudgment, including when Matteo admits being drunk at injury time. Some affirmations feel slightly formulaic and ambivalence isn't explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture Matteo's content and plans, integrating his own words well. Reflections rarely touch the emotional layer beneath his minimizing and humor, such as the shame in 'That was stupid.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on alcohol use tied to ankle recovery, with each turn advancing toward concrete change, a plan, barriers, support, and a success metric. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving; Matteo generated every element of the plan (tracking, skipping Wednesday, texting Jake, hosting game nights) with no clinician directives. Matteo's closing confirms he experienced it as 'my thing.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ownership and choice consistently reinforced: Matteo defines solutions and success, and the psychiatrist commits to nonjudgmental acceptance of whatever the numbers show. No persuasive pressure; Matteo explicitly notes it wasn't a lecture.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn entirely from Matteo via skillful questions about what matters to him, barriers, and what a win looks like. Culminates in spontaneous commitment language without any clinician arguing for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in Matteo throughout ('You know yourself better than anyone,' 'That's you doing this, not me'), and his contributions drove the entire session plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional elicitation and development of change talk: Matteo's pivotal admission about the injury, self-generated solutions, and solidified commitments ('I'll text him tomorrow,' 'I'll be honest with the numbers'). The psychiatrist consistently reinforces his ownership of these ideas.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled well by not amplifying it and pivoting to change-oriented questions, plus effective reframing of 'the boring one' fear. Some minimizing ('that's just being a college student') goes unaddressed, and pivots are more structural than explicitly strategic.
