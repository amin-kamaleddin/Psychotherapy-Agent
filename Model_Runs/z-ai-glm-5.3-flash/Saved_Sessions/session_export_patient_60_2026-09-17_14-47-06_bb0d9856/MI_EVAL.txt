# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, using 'That makes sense' and following Jason's lead even when he rejected quitting. Lacked explicit affirmations of Jason's strengths (e.g., calling his roommate, his honesty), keeping acceptance somewhat formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered accurate simple reflections early, but they were brief, repetitive, and often immediately followed by advice or menus. Missed the emotional layer beneath Jason's minimization — notably his 'wrecked Saturday' admission was met with hangover tips rather than empathic exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session focus was consistent but misdirected — centered on the disciplinary appeal and harm-reduction logistics rather than the clinical target of alcohol use. Jason's Saturday-morning admission was never followed up with a return to his relationship with drinking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Structural collaboration was present via menu options and adaptable drafts, but the counselor primarily dispensed ready-made materials rather than co-creating them. Jason's input shaped only logistics, not therapeutic direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Jason's choice ('Which would be most helpful right now?') and accepted his refusal of 'limit drinks' without pressure. Leaned toward accommodating his agenda without affirming his capacity for self-directed change around alcohol.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied heavily on providing scripts, checklists, and information rather than drawing out Jason's own motivations. Virtually no open questions evoked discrepancy between his drinking and his stated goals (record, internships, not wasting Saturdays).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used collaborative language and offered choices, but the transactional delivery of products (scripts, emails, checklists) made the counselor feel like a service provider. Jason's 'What else do you want from me in these sessions?' reflects this perception.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jason's spontaneous change talk — skipping sketchy dorm parties and admitting wrecked Saturdays happen 'most weekends' — went unreflected and undeveloped. Questions were logistical rather than strategically evocative; change talk emerged despite, not because of, counselor technique.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Heavy sustain talk ('I'm not some alcoholic,' 'that's normal in college') was met with neutral acknowledgment and task pivots rather than softened. No double-sided reflections linked Jason's values to his behavior, and hangover tips inadvertently validated continued use.
