# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a generally nonjudgmental stance with some validation, but warmth felt transactional and missed opportunities to affirm Jason's strengths (e.g., his self-identified responsibility). Repeated safety check-ins that Jason noticed added to a formulaic tone.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were adequate early but shallow, quickly pivoting to problem-solving. Deeper emotional content — Jason's worry about friends' perceptions and his memory gaps — went unreflected, and his expressed frustration at repeated scripts was ignored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Major direction failure — the session drifted to legal strategy and conduct-meeting scripts for most of its middle, with the alcohol target behavior addressed only after Jason forced the issue ('If you think I drink too much, just say it').

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction was markedly uneven — counselor imposed a legal-preparation agenda through advice monologues while Jason repeatedly pushed back. Genuine collaboration only emerged late when Jason's own input shaped the small plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional choice-supportive language ('You don't have to label it', letting Jason pick lines) was undercut by prescriptive numbered lists, unsolicited scripts, and repeated rehearsal pressure despite refusals.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some open questions early drew out Jason's motivations (remembering his nights, protecting his reputation), but these were rarely developed. Motivation surfaced more through Jason's self-reflection than counselor technique.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor assumed an expert role, dispensing tactical legal-style scripts that Jason explicitly rejected multiple times without the counselor adjusting. Minimal acknowledgment that Jason holds expertise on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged (skipping shots, easing up to remember nights) but mostly from Jason self-correcting rather than skillful evocation. Closed-ended option menus replaced open questions, and the strongest change talk about memory gaps was never deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk was actively reinforced rather than softened — echoing 'bad luck' framing, endorsing 'one night shouldn't define you,' and validating heavy socializing as normal. Little effort to use double-sided reflections against minimization.
