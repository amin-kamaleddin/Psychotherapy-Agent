# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard with frequent, genuine affirmations ('That shows resourcefulness', 'Cutting back on smoking already is progress'). Immediately respected the client's fear of judgment by removing the smoking mention from the script without any hint of disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opened with an accurate empathic summary and validated coping ('it makes total sense smoking feels like relief'). Reflections were accurate but occasionally brief, leaving deeper emotional themes — distrust of family, solo parenting exhaustion, fear of losing progress — under-explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained exceptionally clear focus on childcare reliability and progressively guided toward concrete action: drafting the neighbor message, scripting and rehearsing the call, preparing documents, and scheduling follow-up. Kept the smoking topic contained appropriately without losing direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session was largely co-constructed, with drafts offered for modification and role-plays at the client's request. However, several counselor turns delivered pre-packaged content (scripts, tip lists, resource menus) that the client accepted rather than negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices, honored the client's decisions to exclude smoking from the script and postpone smoking work, and framed all next steps as hers. No coercive or pressuring language appeared anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Started with excellent evocative questions that drew out the client's own plan, but increasingly supplied answers — drafting messages, listing backup options, providing craving strategies — rather than drawing them out. Her change talk ('maybe I'll have more fight left') was met with advice instead of exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client's own ideas (meal-trade, coworker backup, Head Start) became the session's substance, with wording and pacing decisions left to her. Occasional slips into an advice-providing role (craving tips, pre-call tips) tilted the expertise balance toward the professional.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Targeted questions effectively elicited change talk ('I'd definitely want to look into Head Start') and affirmations reinforced momentum, culminating in strong self-efficacy ('That's more than I've done in months'). Some change talk was followed by content-provision rather than deepening.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers ('I can't afford daycare', 'I'm not ready to quit completely') were acknowledged briefly and redirected to client strengths without ever amplifying sustain talk. Redirections were quick problem-solving moves rather than double-sided reflections that would help resolve ambivalence.
