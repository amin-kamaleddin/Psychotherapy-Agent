# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental tone and acknowledgment of Samir's honesty were present, but affirmations were generic and warmth was undermined by mechanical safety checks and formulaic responses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal with only token acknowledgments ('Totally valid'). Samir's emotionally meaningful fear of 'not feeling like himself' was met with clinical information rather than understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, consistent focus on the referral concern (medication nonadherence/BP management) with a concrete outcome: two-week lifestyle trial, home BP monitoring, and GP script. Focus maintained well despite patient deflections.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some preferences were incorporated (Samir edited the script, 'put that in bold'), but plans were largely counselor-generated and handed over rather than co-constructed. Samir was a consumer of options rather than an equal architect of solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Explicit choice was offered ('you get to set the trial'), but the volume of unsolicited advice and pre-packaged plans conveyed a controlling stance. Samir's pushback ('suddenly it's a whole menu') suggests autonomy was honored nominally but not deeply felt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was assumed rather than elicited; few open questions explored Samir's values or reasons for change. His desire 'to feel normal' received education instead of exploration, missing key openings for intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered and agency affirmed, but the counselor frequently shifted into expert mode with rapid-fire questions and four-option menus. Samir's comment 'You really like your lists, huh?' implicitly critiqued the expert-driven style.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Samir's spontaneous openness ('Food and walking, I can do that') was never reinforced or deepened. No open questions explored his own reasons, importance, or confidence for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** No arguing against sustain talk and medication dislikes were acknowledged, but the counselor flooded the conversation with medication information rather than using double-sided reflections, keeping the status-quo struggle salient.
