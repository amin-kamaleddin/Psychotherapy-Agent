# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental throughout; validated the client's defensiveness and normalized her guilt without labeling. Affirmations like 'that took real work' reinforced respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captured the client's core tension between self-care and parenting fears. Reflections were sometimes brief before pivoting to advice, and the emotional weight of her guilt disclosure was not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on alcohol use from start to finish, converging on a specific change goal with tracking and a scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—asked permission, negotiated the plan jointly, and let the client shape key decisions. However, the middle portion leaned toward delivering advice menus rather than inviting the client's ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional choice-framing: 'You don't have to quit cold turkey,' 'Treat it as an experiment,' and acceptance of the client's conditions without pressure. The client clearly experienced ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully drew out client-generated motivation, including her powerful 'not for me, for him' statement. Relied somewhat heavily on supplying practical tips rather than consistently asking the client to articulate her own reasons and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client chose her own nights, ritual, and swap, substantially shaping the plan. At times the psychiatrist occupied an active expert role, with tip lists that crowded out the client's own problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited client-driven change talk ('maybe I could try one drink instead of two'). Powerful change talk moments were affirmed but not deepened with follow-up evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well with neutral, non-argumentative reflections rather than confrontation. Occasional double-sided reflections could have strengthened ambivalence resolution.
