# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, with a nonjudgmental opening and non-defensive responses when tested. Affirmations were specific and authentic, with no judgment or disapproval despite defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently captured the client's world, including fitting in, in-the-moment pressure, and privacy concerns. Understanding was demonstrated through the client's shift from guarded to voluntarily committing to a follow-up.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use and harm reduction from opening to close, progressing through pros/cons, coping, a concrete plan, and a follow-up. Deflections were handled honestly with immediate return to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative: asked rather than told, invited the client's own solutions, and co-constructed the follow-up around his preferences. The response to the motive question flattened the power hierarchy.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit autonomy statements ('you're the only one who gets to decide,' 'no strings attached') reinforced the client's control. Accepted the possibility of failure with no controlling language, warnings, or lecturing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited entirely from the client, who articulated his own reasons (parents, cousin's crash, bad mornings). Open questions drew out his reasoning, and he ended more motivated than he began.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client expertise was repeatedly foregrounded and every plan element originated from him. The clinician honored his single-session boundary and let him set the follow-up terms.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked both sides, capitalized on the client's own ideas, and spotlighted spontaneous change talk ('maybe it wouldn't kill me to ease up'). A concrete self-generated commitment emerged: a cap of three, eating first, and a Tuesday check-in.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk, validating then pivoting toward change. Some responses briefly dwelt on sustain themes (peer pressure, minimization) and the cousin-car comparison wasn't gently challenged.
