# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opens with 'I'm here to listen, not judge' and responds neutrally to Adrian's minimization and shame about being judged. Affirmations occasionally feel formulaic ('good call,' 'that makes sense'), keeping it from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures ambivalence, e.g., the double-sided reflection on enjoying weekends while the infection and unknown contents 'nag' him. Misses emotionally loaded moments like the friend going 'blue' keeping him up and the comment about his mother crying.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical focus on meth-related risk from the referral, progressing from importance scaling to concrete harm-reduction steps and follow-up. Excessive time spent on logistics (ZIP codes, text wording) displaced a chance to explore his 'not every weekend' remark.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan genuinely co-constructed: Adrian selects test strips and naloxone, drafts the friend script, and sets his own follow-up. The counselor at times drives the session with lists and multiple-choice offers rather than open inquiry.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently honored, including Adrian's 'not promising anything big' boundary, which is met with explicit respect. No pressure, persuasion, or shaming; his pacing on telling his mother is fully supported.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The importance ruler and strategic questions effectively draw out client-owned motivation ('maybe not do this every weekend,' 'not ending up back in that clinic matters to me'). Some content is counselor-supplied, and emerging change talk could have been amplified more before pivoting to logistics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Adrian is positioned as decision-maker ('which of those feels doable,' script tweaks), with brief, non-lecturing expertise-sharing about naloxone. Less explicit acknowledgment of Adrian's own expertise about his social context, though the counselor ultimately leverages it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled use of the 0-10 ruler with follow-up elicited key change talk, culminating in 'part of me wonders if I should maybe not do this every weekend.' Emerging change statements were often met with practical offers rather than amplification.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it's fun,' 'I'm not promising anything big') is acknowledged without reinforcement, largely via well-timed double-sided reflections. Asking what he likes about weekend use briefly invites sustain talk, though framed strategically.
