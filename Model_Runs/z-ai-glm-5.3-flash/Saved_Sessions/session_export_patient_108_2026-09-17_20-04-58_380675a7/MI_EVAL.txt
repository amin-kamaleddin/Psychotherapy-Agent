# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout; counselor affirms Oliver's care for his friend and his need for space as equally valid, reframes self-criticism ('weak', 'selfish') into strengths, and shows no judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that captures both sides of ambivalence and names underlying emotion (guilt beneath the humor). Oliver repeatedly confirms reflections ('Yeah... you're right. My gut does know.').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior of assertively asking the flatmate to leave; session progresses logically from exploration to decision to concrete action steps without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally an equal, ask-don't-tell partnership that builds on Oliver's ideas, but the counselor briefly speaks in Oliver's voice ('But you're right, I do have a plan now...'), removing his agency in voicing his own insight.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are consistently framed as Oliver's own and all plans originate from him, but the opening question slightly pre-frames the goal ('a way forward that honors both sides') and freedom to choose differently could be more explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No counselor-supplied advice or reasons for change; strategic open questions elicit Oliver's own reasons, confidence, and coping strategies. His shift from 'somebody needs to get motivated' to 'the work is on me' demonstrates evocation working as intended.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in Oliver, and his own contributions (rehearsed sentences, specific evening, calling his sister) become the working plan the counselor builds on.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and deepens change talk through projection and confidence questions, eliciting progressively stronger commitment language ('I'm going to follow through', 'I'm sticking to it'), which the counselor then reinforces.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (guilt, comfort of companionship) is acknowledged without amplification using double-sided reflections and effective pivots toward change, though the guilt theme occasionally lingers over several turns before redirecting, slightly slowing momentum.
