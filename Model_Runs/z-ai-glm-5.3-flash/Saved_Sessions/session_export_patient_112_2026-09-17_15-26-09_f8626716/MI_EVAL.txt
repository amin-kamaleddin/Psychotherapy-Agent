# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations for Rosa's courage and steps. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and deeper emotional moments (identity grief) were acknowledged only briefly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early, accurately capturing her ambivalence about identity, withdrawal, and weight. Reflections became less frequent as the session turned informational, with fears of failure and grief receiving only brief acknowledgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on smoking cessation throughout with no topic drift. Every exchange moved progressively toward change, ending in a concrete quit date, supply plan, and homework.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements like asking permission and jointly choosing strategies, but long segments were expert-driven option lists with Rosa reacting. The plan felt more provider-designed with client sign-off than genuinely co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices rather than directives, and Rosa's decisions (patches, Saturday date, casual script) drove the plan. Heavy informational delivery partially constrained exploration, and explicit ownership-of-change statements were absent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good use of confidence scaling and an actionable next-step question that elicited commitment and self-pride. However, motivation was more supplied than evoked, with little exploration of Rosa's own reasons beyond the doctor's warning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa was invited to choose among options, but the session was largely expert-driven information delivery with minimal eliciting of her own knowledge and ideas. She was positioned more as a recipient of guidance than a co-expert.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged strongly but was mostly client-generated; good confidence and action questions elicited commitment language. Missed opportunities to deepen and elaborate her reasons, self-pride, and vision of success.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and effectively reframed slips as learning opportunities, which the client echoed back. Sustain talk was often met with information dumps or option menus rather than double-sided reflections or deliberate pivots.
