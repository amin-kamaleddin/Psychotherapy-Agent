# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor validates Derrick's court-referral reluctance and affirms his effort without judgment or shaming. Acceptance feels somewhat brief and formulaic, with insufficient affirmation across the session to reach a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening, including a strong double-sided reflection on the license/independence vs. one-off DUI tension. Deeper emotional content (isolation, breakup, the 2.8 BAC blackout) is acknowledged only lightly and not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains consistent focus on drinking and DUI consequences throughout, never drifting from the target behavior. Each exchange builds momentum toward license reinstatement and safer weekends.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Agendas are negotiated, the role-play is joint, and Derrick's script edits are incorporated. Power-sharing is slightly uneven when the counselor supplies the full plan and script without first eliciting Derrick's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Derrick's choices are consistently honored, with options framed as his decision and no moralizing about drinking. Options are frequently presented by the counselor rather than fully generated by the client, limiting early ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively draws motivation from Derrick via open questions, first steps, and the confidence ruler, with Derrick's own reasons driving the plan. At times the counselor supplies reasons and solutions (interlock reframing, refusal wording) rather than evoking them, and deeper motivations remain untapped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Derrick's contributions clearly shape the plan, from his chosen day to his own refusal script and cousin text. The counselor occasionally slides into an expert role with pre-packaged plans and menus of options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked through future-oriented questions, the confidence ruler, role-play, and rehearsal, yielding sustained commitments ('calling a cab — that's not up for debate'). The counselor consistently reinforces these commitments, with confidence rising from 6 to 8 and a concrete plan resulting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is accurately reflected but never strategically softened, with minimizations ('it's just beer,' 'bad luck,' 'isolated incident') largely bypassed. The discrepancy between 'I don't have a problem' and his own safety planning is left unresolved, risking intact minimization.
