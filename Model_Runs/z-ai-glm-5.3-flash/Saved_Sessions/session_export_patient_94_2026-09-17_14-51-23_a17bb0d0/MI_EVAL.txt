# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout. Statements like "I hear you: frustrated about taking time off, skeptical about inhalers" and "You value protecting your body and managing things yourself... that makes sense" show acceptance of the client's defensive stance without labeling. Warmth is somewhat brief and instrumental; deeper individualized affirmation (e.g., validating her self-advocacy in researching her own health) was missing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Good reflective listening, accurately summarizing frustration, time pressure, and safety concerns ("You're worried about steroids—understandable given your aunt's experience"). Reflections track both emotion and values, but are sometimes terse and information-focused. Deeper emotional content (fear of bodily harm, frustration with dismissive prior care) was acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on asthma management and inhaler adherence throughout, despite resistance. Each exchange progressed logically: understanding concerns → addressing specific fears → tailored option → technique teaching → concrete trial plan with tracking, escalation criteria, and follow-up. Session concluded with specific commitments (texting doctor, 4-week trial, follow-up visit).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Collaborative stance used well ("help weigh risks and alternatives together"); the plan was co-created with client input shaping its content. However, much of the session was information delivery (evidence points, technique instructions, script) — well-tailored but somewhat expert-driven. Counselor adapted well to the client's time pressure rather than imposing a longer agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of client ownership; "I'm not promising anything" was met with respect rather than pressure. Choices repeatedly offered, and client's conditions (doctor discussion about dose first, time-limited trial) were incorporated into the plan. Client's own comment — "the first thing that hasn't felt like someone just shoving meds at me" — is strong evidence of effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Effectively drew out the client's own reasons and values rather than lecturing (what would make her feel okay, which option felt most useful). Motivation emerged from her own words ("less risky than what I was picturing," "I can do that much"). Some motivation came from persuasive information delivery rather than pure evocation; deeper values (protecting her body, her family) were left implicit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Client repeatedly positioned as decision-maker ("Which part feels most useful to try first?" / "Which of those would be most helpful?"). Plan emerged from her stated preferences. However, the counselor sometimes drove the agenda with rapid-fire multi-part questions and structured menus before the client fully weighed in, slightly limiting her influence on session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☑ **5**
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited progressively stronger change talk: from resistant opening ("I'm not saying yes") → "maybe... explain that combo" → "I might be willing to try... a trial" → concrete plan ("I'll text my doctor today"). Questions like "What would have to be different for you to feel okay using it as prescribed?" and offering structured choices effectively drew out her own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☑ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk. Fears about steroids and the aunt's bone problems were acknowledged empathically, then reframed with brief facts ("inhaled steroids at low doses aren't the same as long-term oral steroids"). The client softened her own sustain talk ("maybe it's not the same"). Occasional repeated elicitation of "what still worries you?" invited additional sustain talk, though it was usually redirected productively.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 4.3/5**

**Summary:** A strong MI session with a challenging, pre-contemplative client. Key strengths: clear direction toward the target behavior, excellent autonomy support that transformed a resistant client into one with a concrete, self-owned change plan, and skillful cultivation of change talk progressing from "I'm not saying yes" to a committed trial with follow-up. Areas for improvement: deeper, more emotionally attuned reflections; fewer multi-part rapid-fire questions; and more intentional evocation of the client's deeper values rather than reliance on information delivery.
