# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, acknowledged defensiveness ('that makes sense'), and offered transparency about confidentiality that built trust. However, warmth was somewhat procedural, with missed opportunities to affirm Malik's honesty and care for his mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently and accurately reflected Malik's perspective, tracking both his minimization and underlying frustration. Would reach 5 with deeper exploration of the emotional layer beneath his dismissiveness, such as feeling unfairly labeled or fearing his mother's disappointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session stayed focused and produced a concrete plan, but drifted toward communication coaching rather than the target behavior of cannabis use. The 'cutting back' menu option was dropped once Malik chose option 2 and never revisited.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements existed, including inviting Malik's input and incorporating his edits, but collaboration was uneven. The counselor tended to generate solutions himself and ask Malik to select, rather than jointly developing ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Malik's choice with menu options, letting him pick and edit scripts, and respecting his refusal of role-play without pushing. Malik's preferences substantially shaped the session content and plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions drew out Malik's desires for trust and fewer fights, plus a spontaneous insight about staying out later when pressured. However, motivation was elicited around family communication rather than the substance use itself—his own reasons to reconsider use were never explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mixed: Malik's input was invited and his edits incorporated, but much of the session was counselor-driven advice delivery with pre-packaged scripts and contingency replies. The counselor filled the expert role more than drawing out Malik's existing knowledge of his mother's patterns.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change-adjacent talk emerged, including Malik's desire for fewer fights and his insight that parental conflict worsens his behavior. However, these discrepancy-rich statements were never deepened with evocative questions, as the session shifted to script drafting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided amplifying sustain talk by using neutral reflections rather than arguing and pivoting toward Malik's own goals. The scripted 'I'm not doing anything crazy' line placed mild sustain language in Malik's mouth, though this reflected client-requested pragmatism.
