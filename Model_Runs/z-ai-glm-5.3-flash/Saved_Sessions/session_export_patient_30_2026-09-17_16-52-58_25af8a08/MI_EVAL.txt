# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with a guarded client, though warmth occasionally felt procedural and explicit affirmations of David's strengths were missing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured David's surface concerns and practical constraints, but deeper emotional meaning—like his wish to feel like 'a guy taking care of himself'—went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused and moved from ambivalence to a concrete three-part plan with a specific Thursday check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was visibly co-constructed with David editing details, though rapid-fire multiple-choice questions meant he mostly picked from counselor-generated options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was excellent: menus rather than directives, honoring David's pace and conditions, and explicit avoidance of pressure throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evocation stayed largely at the logistical level; the excellent opening values question was never revisited and David's self-motivational statement went undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** David shaped tactical details, but the core agenda, scripts, and option menu were counselor-generated, limiting client-owned expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited through option-offering questions, but pivotal self-motivational statements were not reinforced and no confidence/importance scaling was used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement and skillfully absorbed into a safety plan, though redirection was occasionally abrupt rather than reflective.
