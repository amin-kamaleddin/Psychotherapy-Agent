# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental with respectful affirmations ('You did fine—clear and focused'). Affirmations were brief and generic, keeping the warmth functional rather than deeply personal.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections captured her exhaustion well, but empathy dropped off as the session shifted to task mode. Emotionally significant statements like 'stop feeling like I'm drowning' and distrust of family went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on childcare, established early and honored throughout, ending with concrete commitments. The deferred smoking/drinking discussion was appropriately scheduled rather than dropped.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine give-and-take existed (e.g., Daniela editing the neighbor message), but the rhythm became counselor-provides-solution, client-approves. Long blocks of scripted advice tilted the balance toward the expert role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated permission-seeking and honoring of Daniela's choices (neighbor trade first, lunch-break calls, ending early). Autonomy support was implicit rather than explicitly affirming her ownership of the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain—the counselor largely supplied answers and scripts instead of drawing out Daniela's own motivations. Missed opportunities to explore her agency, confidence, and what she thought would work beyond willpower.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and invited preferences, but the middle of the session was dominated by counselor-generated content (drafts, checklists, scripts). Positioned herself as the resource expert rather than locating expertise within Daniela.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good evocative questions early successfully drew out change talk and commitment language. However, change talk was never deepened—no scaling questions or strategic reinforcement when rich openings like 'once childcare's steady, we work on that next' appeared.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (financial worries, distrust, 'harder than I expected') was neither reinforced nor effectively pivoted. Barriers were handled informationally without reframing or double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence about smoking and drinking.
