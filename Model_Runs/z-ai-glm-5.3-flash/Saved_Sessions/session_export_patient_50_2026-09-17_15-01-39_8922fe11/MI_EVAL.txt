# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with consistent respect and brave-step affirmations, though affirmations are somewhat formulaic and relapse history is not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captures Hana's anxiety cycle and hopeful plan, but reflections occasionally stay superficial without exploring the emotional core of fear of embarrassment and years of self-criticism.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, clear focus on the target behavior from first question through concrete action planning (Tuesday 7pm, 10-minute timer, alarm wording, fallbacks) with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed from Hana's own ideas (Monday prep, tweaked self-compassion line, gentle alarm wording), with the counselor offering options and inviting tweaking.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is consistently framed as Hana's choice with no pressure or coercion; could more explicitly affirm her ownership of both success and setbacks.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and open questions drew out some motivation and Hana's own solutions, but the counselor often supplies menus of options (self-statements, alarm texts) rather than evoking them from her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana is repeatedly positioned as the expert and her contributions substantially shaped the plan, though the counselor tends to offer lists of options rather than eliciting her ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent questions evoked strong change language ('I can do ten minutes. Tuesday I'll try it'), but deeper meaning of change (what passing would mean) is not amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('too little to matter,' 'I've bailed before,' fear of telling her sister) is acknowledged but met with quick problem-solving rather than double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
