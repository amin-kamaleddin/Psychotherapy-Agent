# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; 'I won't be disappointed' directly addresses Matteo's fear of judgment. Affirmations are present but somewhat brief and formulaic, with little explicit normalization of his college context.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate surface-level reflections, but Matteo's key emotional moment ('it sounds like a lot when you say it out loud') goes unexplored. Feelings and underlying ambivalence—like fear of losing his social identity—are never reflected in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol use throughout, connecting it to the screening result and medication safety. However, the direction is sometimes checklist-driven and rapid, making goals feel imposed rather than client-driven.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rapid-fire questions and pre-packaged plans leave little room for joint problem-solving. Matteo's contributions shape the plan only marginally, and he notes 'You basically just said it all anyway.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers menus and accepts Matteo's two-drink counter-offer, showing procedural respect for autonomy. However, specific numeric limits and scripts are prescribed before Matteo articulates his own goals, and his ownership of the change process isn't deeply reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely draws motivation from the client; his own reasons for change (avoiding blackouts, roommate feedback) are never explored with open questions. Motivation and the change plan originate mostly from the clinician, making this the weakest domain.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Frequently slips into an expert stance with counselor-generated plans while Matteo plays a passive approving role. His own ideas (roommate accountability, Tuesday/Thursday insight) are acknowledged but not substantively built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk is elicited ('the cutting-down thing doesn't sound terrible,' 'I'll try the limit this week'), but it isn't deepened. Missed opportunities to evoke Matteo's own reasons for change, and commitment language goes unreflected.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ('it feels pretty normal for college,' 'Wednesdays are like our thing') is not reinforced, but it's also not actively softened. Responses like 'social drinking is common' inadvertently validate sustain talk without double-sided reflections linking it to change talk.
