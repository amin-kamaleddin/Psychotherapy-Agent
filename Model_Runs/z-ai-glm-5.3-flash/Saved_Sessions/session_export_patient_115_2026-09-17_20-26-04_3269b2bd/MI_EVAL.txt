# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, disarming shame about ambivalence and honoring the client's decision to defer addressing drinking ('That's wisdom, not weakness'). Accepts the client exactly where he is without any judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and emotionally deep, capturing physical fear, family burden, and ambivalence precisely. The client repeatedly confirms feeling deeply understood ('Yeah, exactly').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear progressive arc from exploring the problem to a specific relapse-prevention plan with no topic drift. Every question purposefully advances the target behavior without steering off the client's agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates as a joint inquiry with the client doing virtually all the problem-solving while the counselor structures exploration. The final exchange was fully client-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced, accepting the deferral of drinking and never prescribing solutions. All behavior-change decisions were the client's own, with no persuasive or coercive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation elicited entirely from within the client through hypotheticals, values questions, and relational prompts. Ownership of change fully transferred: 'it's not just the doctor nagging anymore—it's me.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise within the client ('That's your idea, not mine') and the entire plan came from him. Power-sharing at the highest level.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and deepens change talk via a pivotal hypothetical question and amplifying follow-ups. Rich commitment language emerged: 'A plan, not a promise' and 'Tonight I want my actions to do the talking.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids amplifying sustain talk, using double-sided reflections to surface the client's own doubt. Minor deduction as the unwinding reflection briefly gave air to the status quo, though it was strategically appropriate and quickly redirected.
