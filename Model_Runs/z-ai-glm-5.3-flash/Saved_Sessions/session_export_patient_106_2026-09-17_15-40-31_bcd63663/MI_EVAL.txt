# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth, including non-judgmental responses to the client's boundary-setting ('Totally fair — I hear you'). However, warmth occasionally feels formulaic, with phrases like 'totally understandable' repeated nearly verbatim across turns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured both sides of her ambivalence, e.g., the need for a break versus worry about her son's exposure. Reflections sometimes missed deeper emotional material such as her shame about neighbors' judgment and fear of failing again.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking and its impact on her son throughout, steering each turn toward a concrete outcome. The session ended with a specific, actionable if-then plan, confidence rating, and follow-up check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with the client contributing the porch hook, habit-stacking idea, and check-in time. Frequent pre-packaged option lists (jacket, purifier, NRT, gloves, alarms) tilted toward an expert-menu approach rather than evoking her own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced the client's ownership, immediately honoring her 'no quitting lecture' boundary without pressure. Her pace ('Doesn't mean I'm quitting') was fully respected, and the slip rule normalized setbacks without judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, the small-change hypothetical, and the confidence ruler effectively elicited her own reasons and plans, producing strong change talk ('first crack in the wall'). Limited by supplying practical solutions rather than evoking them and by not exploring the deeper meaning of being 'a good mom.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly sought permission and let the client choose focus areas, treating her as the expert on her own life. The counselor supplied most concrete options rather than evoking her ideas first, and advice-giving was somewhat dense with long option lists.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective questions and the if-then planning drew out meaningful commitments and the pivotal 'first crack in the wall' statement. Missed opportunities included not deepening that pivotal statement and asking only about confidence rather than importance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with minimization ('He doesn't have asthma,' 'My mom smoked her whole life'), instead using double-sided reflections, and the slip rule wisely pre-empted all-or-nothing thinking. Repeatedly restating both sides occasionally gave equal airtime to sustain talk without strategically pivoting toward her parental values.
