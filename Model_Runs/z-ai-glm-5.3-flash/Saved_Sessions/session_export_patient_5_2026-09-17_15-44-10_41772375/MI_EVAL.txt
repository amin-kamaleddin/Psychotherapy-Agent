# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tone is respectful and nonjudgmental with occasional validations, but there are no explicit affirmations of Alessia's strengths or proactive safety-seeking behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections appear, but most responses immediately pivot to information delivery, with limited exploration of the emotional weight of her fear of destabilization.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus stays clearly on safe medication transition planning, progressing logically toward a concrete outcome (a prepared, rehearsed script). Minor deductions for front-loading information early rather than setting a collaborative agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-asking and choices are present, and Alessia's requests drive some turns, but the dominant mode is expert-to-layperson information delivery rather than joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Autonomy is supported through explicit advice to wait for the prescriber's plan and menu-based options, but large amounts of unprompted directive information subtly control what she asks her prescriber.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and content come largely from the counselor; virtually no open questions elicit Alessia's own reasons, hopes, or confidence, with prompts limited to logistics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes an expert role, delivering dense prescriptive information blocks before they're requested; Alessia's contributions do not substantially shape session content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Closing questions and rehearsal elicit Alessia's own commitment statements, but the counselor never explicitly evokes reasons or confidence for change; much change language arises spontaneously.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anxieties about the washout are validated and supported with planning rather than amplified, but sustain talk is simply moved past without double-sided reflections, and heavy risk emphasis may unintentionally heighten fear.
