# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations like 'You've got good insight,' but did not normalize Adrian's ambivalence or shame about family secrecy, keeping warmth somewhat transactional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal; emotionally meaningful disclosures (naloxone feeling 'heavy,' family secrecy) received no reflection and pivoted to logistics. Repeating the safety question after Adrian objected showed script-following rather than tracking the client.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept consistent focus on meth use and risk reduction with concrete next steps, but direction followed the counselor's harm-reduction checklist agenda rather than a collaboratively negotiated focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adrian chose between options and contributed his own ideas (the infection excuse, 'one thing at a time'), but the counselor dominated with expert information blocks, reducing Adrian's role to answering checklists.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly framed next steps as Adrian's choice and accepted his refusal to share his location without pressure, but did not explicitly affirm that key ideas came from Adrian himself, and some advice felt prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rich motivational material (the infection experience, the seatbelt analogy, family fears) went largely unexplored; most questions were about frequency, safety, or logistics rather than open questions about values and concerns.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and asked preferences, supporting some power sharing, but the bulk of the session was counselor-delivered expert content in an advice-heavy manner with Adrian choosing from counselor-generated menus.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Spontaneous change talk emerged ('the infection was genuinely miserable,' 'maybe skipping one weekend wouldn't kill me') but was client-generated; the counselor missed chances to amplify it or explore the seatbelt analogy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered a decent reframe toward practical change and never reinforced sustain talk, but ignored it ('everyone I hang out with, this is what we do') rather than using double-sided reflections to develop discrepancy.
