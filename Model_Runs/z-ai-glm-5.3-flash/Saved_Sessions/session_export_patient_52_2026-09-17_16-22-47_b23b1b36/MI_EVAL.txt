# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validated that her drinking feels like hard-earned downtime, and avoided pressure — Irina noted she would have walked out if told to quit entirely. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and not deeply personalized to her fear of failure or imperfect efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captured her ambivalence, e.g., naming the core issue as never getting a break rather than the drinking itself. Reflections occasionally restated facts rather than exploring deeper emotions like her fear of losing her one reward or her pattern of quitting when not perfect.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on alcohol reduction and the linked childcare/respite issue, with clear negotiated action steps and concrete follow-up. Some exchanges (bitters, garnish, timers) drifted into fine detail rather than core motivational work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed — Irina's rejections of alcohol-free nights and the log were incorporated without pushback, and the social worker script matched her stated preference. At times the psychiatrist moved quickly to solution lists, tilting toward advice-giving over fully equal idea exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent autonomy support with 'flexible and reversible' framing, menu choices, and a plan explicitly designed around keeping her reward. Options were presented as a fairly directive list early on rather than open exploration of her own ideas first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and questions about past successes elicited some of Irina's own reasons, including her son and prior smoking cessation. However, much of the plan content came from counselor-generated menus, and her deepest motivations surfaced spontaneously without being deliberately evoked or built upon.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Irina was consistently positioned as the decision-maker, and the plan was built from her own ideas like the mocktail ritual and shower backup. The initial strategy menu was counselor-generated, so her contributions shaped but did not drive the agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and invitations to choose strategies elicited change talk such as her mocktail idea and 'I'll try it.' Powerful values statements like not wanting her son to remember 'mom always waiting for her wine' were summarized but not amplified or deepened, and no commitment language was explicitly evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged with neutral reflections, which is adequate, but no double-sided reflections were used. The strongest sustain statements were sometimes simply restated rather than strategically de-emphasized or reframed.
