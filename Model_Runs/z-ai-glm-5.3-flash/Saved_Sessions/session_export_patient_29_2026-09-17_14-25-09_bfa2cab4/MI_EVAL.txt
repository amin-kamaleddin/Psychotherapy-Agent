# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor is polite, nonjudgmental, and ends with affirmations, but warmth is sparse and few individualized affirmations of David's strengths (e.g., his six-week quit attempt, insight into alcohol as a trigger) are offered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is nearly absent; disclosures about caving at the restaurant and losing willpower after beers receive tactic lists rather than reflections. Understanding is assumed rather than demonstrated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays firmly focused on smoking cessation with no drift, though direction is maintained through clinician-driven checklists rather than linking actions to David's own values and reasons.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction follows an expert-recipient structure with multi-point plans delivered rather than negotiated. David tweaks some details, but the counselor never asks what he thinks would work or jointly explores his questions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is dominated by clinician-directed directives, with client-chosen details (Monday quit date, friendly script) initiated by David rather than facilitated. Little explicit reinforcement that the change is his to own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Virtually no effort is made to draw out the client's own motivations, reasons, or past success; all plan content originates from the counselor. Motivation is assumed rather than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor positions himself as the expert dispensing solutions via prescriptive bullet lists, and never invites David's expertise. Some credit for incorporating David's contributions into the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David arrives with strong spontaneous change talk that is never elicited, deepened, or reflected (e.g., 'Why is now the right time?'). Affirmations acknowledge but do not amplify his commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** David's sustain talk about gum, stress weeks, and alcohol is met with problem-solving and generic tips rather than double-sided reflections or strategic reframing. Sustain talk is neither reinforced nor resolved.
