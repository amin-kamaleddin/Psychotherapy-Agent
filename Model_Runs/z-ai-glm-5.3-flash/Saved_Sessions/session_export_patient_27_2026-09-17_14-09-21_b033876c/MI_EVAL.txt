# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, with validation and normalization of Daniela's concerns and progress. Falls short of 5 because affirmation is brief and task-focused rather than highlighting her strengths, values, and courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Empathy is present but inconsistent — early reflections on fear and ambivalence are good, but most responses pivot quickly to scripts and checklists. Missed key opportunities to reflect the emotional weight of disclosure's irreversibility, fear of food policing, and her self-talk pattern, plus a moment of imperfect tracking when re-offering a completed practice.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on disclosure to Mia, with a concrete plan (Saturday, in person, follow-up text, reward) by session's end. Not a 5 due to a checklist-driven, slightly rushed pacing and briefly circling back to an already-completed practice.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine co-creation of the disclosure script and role-play, with Daniela's edits honored so the language felt authentically hers. Collaboration is uneven, however, as the counselor often supplies full scripts, checklists, and options first rather than building from Daniela's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated and explicit choice-offering with no pressure or coercion, and Daniela's decisions (tweaking the script, skipping re-rehearsal, choosing Saturday) were respected. Options were often presented as pre-packaged menus rather than open exploration, and her values-laden question about what 'counts' was answered quickly rather than supporting her own reasoning.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela's motivation emerged organically, but largely incidentally rather than through skillful evocation. The counselor rarely asked open questions about her own reasons or confidence, supplied content instead of drawing it out, and missed exploring her notable '60/40' motivational shift.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela's input was incorporated when offered (script edits, boundary, tone, plan), but the counselor frequently drove the agenda with pre-made options and delivered information from a quasi-expert stance before exploring what Daniela thought or knew.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Conditions for change talk were created and Daniela produced meaningful statements ('it felt kind of good? Saying it,' 'I think I might actually do this'). However, the counselor did not consistently recognize or deepen these moments — most notably the '60/40' shift passed without reflection or scaling — and tended to supply motivation rather than evoke it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing against change and gave brief acknowledgment of fears ('scary and freeing at once'). But it then redirected to scripts and checklists without double-sided reflections or reframing, missing chances to actively resolve ambivalence in favor of change.
