# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with affirmations that explicitly countered Elena's self-criticism ('not silly'). Slightly formulaic at times and could be more individually attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured her motivation and internal conflict. Reflections sometimes pivoted quickly into information-giving rather than deepening emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange advanced the target behavior, from clarifying concerns to preparing for the doctor visit. No topic drift, ending in concrete behavioral intent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Joint framing and Elena's choices shaped the session direction, including co-created role-play. But the counselor supplied most content (data, scripts) rather than drawing plans from Elena.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent permission-seeking, option menus, and no pressure. Heavy information delivery occasionally edged toward persuasive framing of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain — change talk emerged spontaneously but was not deliberately evoked or deepened. Relied on education and logistics rather than evocative questions about Elena's own reasons, values, and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena was positioned as expert and offered choices, but the session took an expert-led, educational character with large data blocks. Power balance tilted toward the counselor as information authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena generated meaningful commitment language on her own, which the counselor affirmed but did not strategically deepen or consolidate. No evocative questions or confidence scaling were used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hesitancy and minimizing were met with neutral, non-reinforcing reflections and gentle pivots toward action. Fell short only because pivots relied on information delivery rather than double-sided reflections or reframing.
