# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, and Isabelle explicitly said 'Thanks for not making this a huge thing.' Respect was sometimes formulaic rather than warmly individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used accurate factual summaries but little exploration of Isabelle's underlying perspective or feelings — e.g., the social meaning of drinking at her age or her discomfort with being singled out. Emotional nuance went largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept clear focus on alcohol use and safety from start to finish, with concrete follow-up plans tied to Isabelle's goals. Direction was checklist-driven rather than collaboratively negotiated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Isabelle selected safety steps and check-in logistics, shaping the final plan. However, the session opened as an expert-driven questionnaire and key content originated from the counselor; her 'Is that what you needed from me?' suggests compliance rather than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and honored refusals, including protecting confidentiality and respecting her 'no' to practicing lines. Options were pre-selected menus, with some gentle steering toward a plan she said she didn't need.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied almost entirely on supplying information, ideas, and rationale rather than drawing out Isabelle's own motivations. Her reasons for change, values, concerns, and confidence were never elicited — a core MI deficit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partially shared power by offering choices, but the opening was interrogation-style and expertise remained mostly with the counselor. Isabelle's role was largely to comply.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely asked open questions about Isabelle's own reasons for caution or values, and never deepened her minimal statements. Even the one client-owned element (the rehearsal excuse) was counselor-generated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Isabelle voiced considerable sustain talk ('I barely drink,' 'everyone I know drinks sometimes') that was neither reinforced nor softened. No double-sided reflections or reframes were used, and sustain talk was left unaddressed.
