# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard from opening to closing, explicitly removing judgment ('no promise required') and warmly engaging the patient's humor and deflections. Thomas was treated with respect despite his initially dismissive, minimizing stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, layered reflections throughout, including a double-sided reflection of ambivalence that the patient confirmed ('Yeah, I suppose that's fair'). The psychiatrist also caught subtle cues, framing 'I'd be the first to say so' as evidence health mattered to him.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on drinking with logical progression: exploring his health read, surfacing ambivalence, eliciting his own goal (pacing), a concrete strategy, and self-monitoring. Each response advanced momentum without rushing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue between equals—the psychiatrist opened by asking the patient's own assessment and let his answers drive each next question. The patient's remark that he expected 'a proper telling-off' confirms the collaborative stance was experienced, not just performed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Patient ownership was consistently reinforced ('there's no promise required,' 'no lectures, just your report'), and all ideas came from Thomas. Even follow-up was framed as optional.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from within—the psychiatrist never supplied reasons to change, using evocative questions to elicit Thomas's own motivations (his son, wasted Sundays, wife's opinion). He left with self-generated reasons, plan, and confidence ('Might surprise myself').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient was repeatedly positioned as the expert ('You know your Fridays with your mates better than anyone'), and his contributions—pacing, the Sunday report card, telling his wife—shaped the session direction. The psychiatrist explicitly credited him for authoring the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and developed, with the psychiatrist reinforcing Thomas's own language, amplifying it with targeted questions, and echoing commitment language back. Thomas moved from precontemplation to a specific, self-generated plan within one session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally not reinforced, with reflections acknowledging but pivoting toward change-relevant material without arguing. At times minimization (lecture-aversion, family history) was reflected fairly fully before redirecting; more strategic pivoting would earn a 5.
