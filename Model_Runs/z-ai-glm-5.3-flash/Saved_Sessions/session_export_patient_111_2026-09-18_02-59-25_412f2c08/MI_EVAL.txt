# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirmed the patient's autonomy and personal choice. The patient explicitly noted feeling respected: 'it's still my choice, just pointed somewhere different.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeated accurate reflective listening captured both surface statements and deeper feelings, e.g., 'her fear touches you more than you let on.' The reflections frequently drew out unspoken ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Each response purposefully moved the conversation from resistance toward the patient's own change talk. Questions were strategically sequenced to uncover personal motivation without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positioned the patient as the expert throughout ('You know yourself and your smoking better than anyone'). The plan was entirely patient-designed and patient-owned.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly protected at every turn, including confidentiality about the plan and 'it's got to be mine.' The patient's shift from defensiveness to engagement directly reflects this support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully drew out the patient's own reasons (grandkids, mornings, his father's death) rather than imposing external rationales. Change talk emerged organically from the patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The tone was consistently non-hierarchical and curious rather than directive. The patient responded with increasing openness and voluntarily requested a follow-up session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician reflected and amplified change talk each time it appeared ('maybe I'd think about it,' 'maybe even you'). The patient moved from 'leave me alone' to committing to a first-week plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (blaming his wife, 'nothing needs to change') was met with reflection rather than confrontation, allowing ambivalence to resolve naturally. The patient self-corrected toward change without argument.
