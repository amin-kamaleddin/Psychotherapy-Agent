# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed the patient's perspective, validated her frustration with the referral, and treated her as the expert on her own life, conveying deep respect and nonjudgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured both surface content and underlying feelings (family pressure, anxiety, loneliness of weeknight use), including nuanced insights like 'borrowing calm rather than finding it.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions and reflections were purposefully aimed at exploring ambivalence about cannabis use and coping with anxiety, gently guiding toward the patient's own reasons for change without pulling her off track.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasized the patient's autonomy ('you get to decide about both') and co-created a small, self-determined plan rather than prescribing one, including honoring her wish not to quit outright.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Language consistently reinforced choice and self-direction, explicitly removing pressure to quit and framing all decisions—including weeknight use—as the patient's own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions drew out the patient's own motivations and change talk (running helped before, weeknight use isn't working, money concerns), letting her discover her reasons rather than hearing the clinician's.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange had a genuine two-way feel, with the clinician building on each patient statement and inviting her input at every step, from identifying stressors to shaping a concrete first step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful reflection and strategic questions elicited strong preparatory and mobilizing change talk, such as recognizing weeknight use as avoidance, planning a Wednesday run, and considering trying weeknights without cannabis.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Rather than arguing against sustain talk ('I'm not quitting weed'), the clinician accepted and recontextualized it, which paradoxically deepened the patient's own examination of when use didn't serve her.
