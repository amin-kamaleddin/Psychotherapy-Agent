# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, treating the alcohol incident as information rather than a failing. Affirmed Tyler's honesty and self-generated plan without any labeling or shaming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured Tyler's ambivalence and emotional nuance, including the internal conflict of 'talking itself out of it.' Fell slightly short due to limited deeper exploration of frustration with past failures and the emotional weight of the police incident.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on controlled drinking in social settings with no topic drift. Each exchange built purposefully toward a concrete, self-directed plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated as genuine teamwork, with questions like 'what ideas do you have?' inviting Tyler to shape direction. The counselor provided structure while Tyler supplied all content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly returned authorship of motivation to Tyler ('That's your own conclusion, not mine') and left all decisions to him. No advice was imposed; all strategies were self-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Drew motivation from within Tyler, eliciting his own reasons for change rather than supplying them. Tyler ended the session in commitment language with a specific, time-bound action he set himself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly conveyed that expertise resides with Tyler, who shaped the session substantially. Every element of the plan—limit, reminder, signal, roommate, timing—came from him.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk through open questions, values reflection, a confidence ruler, and success-marker questions. Tyler responded with clear commitment language, demonstrating highly effective evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accepted sustain talk without amplifying it and pivoted deftly toward change with reframes like 'keep the good parts while having more of a handle.' Occasionally restated sustain talk at length in summaries, giving it slightly more airtime than necessary.
