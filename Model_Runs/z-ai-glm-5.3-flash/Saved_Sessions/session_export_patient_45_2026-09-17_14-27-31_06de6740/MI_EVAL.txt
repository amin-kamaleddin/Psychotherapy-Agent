# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and validated Erik's initial defensiveness without labeling. Affirmations were sparse and generic, with little explicit affirmation of Erik's strengths (openness, concern for grandkids).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected Erik's frustration and responsively explained his diabetes concerns in plain language. Deeper emotional reflections were missed, including his fear of amputation, grief about his father's death, and fear of 'finding out I'm worse than I thought.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept consistent focus on referral targets and moved toward concrete action: tracking week, food swaps, and GP visit with prepared questions. Some drift into tangential informational detail (extensive complication lists, tracker columns) slightly diluted motivational momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik shaped elements of the plan (food swaps with wife, written results, script tone), but the counselor mostly presented fully formed solutions rather than building them from Erik's ideas. Power-sharing was uneven, functioning more as advisor than equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, respected Erik's rejection of groups and his preference for tracking first, and tailored materials to his style. Implicit pressure from long prescriptive lists and lack of explicit ownership statements ('it's up to you') kept this from a higher score.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Intrinsic motivations (grandkids, father's early death, wife's support) surfaced, but only incidentally via the complication explanation, not through deliberate evocative questions. The counselor never explored Erik's own reasons, confidence, or vision of success, and didn't reflect the motivation back.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for Erik's input and adapted materials to his preferences, but repeatedly shifted into an expert stance with prescriptive plans, templates, and scripts delivered before Erik's own ideas were explored. Erik's expertise about his own life was underused.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik generated meaningful change talk (grandkids, tracking plan, 'proper go'), largely catalyzed by the complication explanation and small-goal offers. The counselor didn't actively deepen or reinforce it—no reflections of motivation, confidence scaling, or evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and gently pivoted toward small options. However, no double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk, and ambivalence about pub culture, peer mockery, and fear of self-discovery was largely passed over rather than resolved.
