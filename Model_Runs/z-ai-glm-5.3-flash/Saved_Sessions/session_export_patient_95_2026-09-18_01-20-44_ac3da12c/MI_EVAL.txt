# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly welcoming both sides of Maria's ambivalence and refusing to argue with her hopelessness. No judgment even when warned she'll 'scream' and push people away.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that mirrors Maria's exact language and validates pain before redirecting. Reflections land so well Maria repeatedly says 'that got me.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Progressively guides from hopelessness toward a return commitment and survival plan. However, with active suicidal ideation and dangerous nighttime hours identified, no direct assessment of immediate safety (means, plan, tonight's risk) was conducted.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely egalitarian: adopts Maria's stated conditions into the working agreement and co-constructs the safety card from her own words rather than imposing a template. Every forward step builds on her ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforces ownership by asking what would make treatment feel 'on your terms' and honoring all of Maria's specifications. All plan elements — returning, the card, when to read it — originate from her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently draws motivation from within via evocative questions rather than supplying reasons for change. Elicited rich change talk ('I want to not be scared of myself') and a client-authored survival card without lecturing or arguing for hope.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treats Maria as the expert, explicitly adopting her criteria ('let me be a mess first,' 'just ask me about it') as the session roadmap. The safety card is built entirely from her own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and amplifies faint change talk, pairing each hopeful reflection with an open question. Resulted in concrete commitment language ('I'll come back. Next week') and unprompted survival planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never reinforces sustain talk, validating then pivoting toward change. Scored 4 because some reflections restate both sides (including hopelessness) before pivoting, which is double-sided but less assertively change-oriented than ideal.
