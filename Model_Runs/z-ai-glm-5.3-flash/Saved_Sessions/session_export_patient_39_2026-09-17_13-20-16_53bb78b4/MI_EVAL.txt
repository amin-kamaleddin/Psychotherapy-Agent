# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with affirmations of Elias's courage ('That was brave — saying your limits and longing out loud takes courage') and normalization of his fears. Warmth occasionally felt formulaic, and affirmations were not always deepened into individualized regard for Elias as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected Elias's core dilemma, capturing both hope and discouragement ('wanting connection but fearing being singled out is painful and understandable'), and picked up on his concern about being 'a project.' Emotional nuances like his shame about sitting while others moved and his fear of a stinging reply were addressed only briefly before pivoting to task-oriented suggestions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on re-engagement with the group throughout, guiding every exchange toward a concrete step — from identifying what mattered, to scripting a message, rehearsing it, planning contingency replies, and agreeing on follow-up. No topic drift, and direction was maintained without becoming pushy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative: scripts were co-developed and edited by Elias, and the plan incorporated his resources (his wife's help, his knowledge of old songs). The counselor drove the structure and content, with Elias refining rather than co-creating, and the agenda was set by the counselor's opening framing rather than negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Elias's ownership of every decision — message format, wording, delivery method, and timing — accepting his choices ('No promises') without pressure. Elias's closing comment that this plan 'I can manage' unlike plans others impose confirms successful autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited motivation from within Elias, surfacing his values (connection, music, youth) and evoking genuine desire and commitment talk ('I'd love to join... that's true, actually'). Missed opportunities to evoke more deeply, such as exploring what being part of the group would mean for his identity or asking Elias to generate his own solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Elias's expertise as central, offering options rather than prescriptions and letting him choose the details — Elias explicitly recognized this: 'you helped me find words to ask for something.' Practical suggestions were counselor-generated rather than drawn from Elias's own ideas, and his contributions shaped specifics but not the overall session agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively structured the session so change talk emerged naturally, with genuine desire and commitment statements elicited rather than imposed ('I'd love to join — that's true, actually'; 'I'll go twice a month'). Key moments like Elias's 'Surprised myself saying it' were affirmed but not amplified with evocative follow-up questions about what joining would mean for his life.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias voiced substantial sustain talk ('What's the difference if I sit there again?'; 'I might not bother going back at all'), and the counselor did not reinforce it, which is a strength. However, double-sided reflections were rarely used, and his fear of a discouraging reply was sidestepped rather than actively worked through beyond providing scripts.
