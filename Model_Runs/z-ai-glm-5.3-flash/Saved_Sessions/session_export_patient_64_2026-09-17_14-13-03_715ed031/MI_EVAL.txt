# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout; Jordan felt respected and thanked the counselor at the end. However, warmth was sometimes generic and the safety screening felt formulaic rather than individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections acknowledged ambivalence, but reflections were often cut short by fact-giving and option menus. A key moment ('maybe it's not just about me') was missed in favor of transmission facts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed consistently focused on the vaccine decision and produced a specific, actionable plan (Saturday deadline, cancelable slot, symptom precautions). Direction occasionally ran ahead of the client's readiness, pushing logistics before ambivalence resolved.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan shaped key elements of the plan, but the pattern was largely counselor-proposes / client-picks. Ideas came mostly from counselor menus rather than being jointly generated, making the plan feel accepted rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong logistical autonomy support: repeated choice offers, a cancelable slot, and graceful acceptance of Jordan's pushback with his own timeline. Autonomy was underused as explicit affirmation of Jordan's ownership of the decision, and unsolicited facts carried an implicit steer.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The most powerful motivation (protecting his grandmother) emerged from Jordan himself, but was never deliberately evoked or deepened. No open evocative questions, values exploration, or importance/confidence scaling were used — the shift happened despite, not because of, skillful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan's input was invited and his timeline honored, but the session often drifted toward an expert stance with rapid option menus and unsolicited fact lectures. The counselor never asked what Jordan already knew or believed would work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan generated genuine change talk anchored in his grandparents, and the counselor capitalized with concrete next steps. However, change talk was never explicitly evoked or deepened, and a premature option menu triggered pushback ('Whoa, I didn't say I was doing it yet').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and ambivalence was normalized, but softening was inconsistent. Early sustain talk met corrective facts rather than double-sided reflections, edging toward counter-argument.
