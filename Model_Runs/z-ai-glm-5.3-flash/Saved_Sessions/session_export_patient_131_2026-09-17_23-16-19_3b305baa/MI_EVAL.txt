# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates warm, unconditional positive regard and dignity-affirming language (e.g., 'that's the decent man who was there all along'). Affirmations are specific and genuine rather than formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections are consistently accurate and complex, including a nuanced double-sided opening reflection. However, reflections often restate the client's words and could deepen into emotional nuance, such as the grief around fortnightly visits.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on reducing alcohol use, the Stopping Violence program, and the living arrangement, building steadily from ambivalence to a concrete plan and coping strategy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely between equals, with the plan co-constructed through questions and optional suggestions. Some closing summaries sound like polished mini-speeches, slightly tipping the conversational balance toward the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is repeatedly affirmed with questions about what would help most and preferred timing, and the counselor never prescribes. The client explicitly frames the plan as 'my choice, my plan,' which the counselor reinforces.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within rather than supplied, with questions evoking the client's own reasons, resources, and commitment. The client's growing self-efficacy and self-generated coping strategies reflect highly effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions the client as the expert, and every key insight originates from the client. Counselor contributions are limited to reflecting and offering gentle optional ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully elicit commitment language ('I ring her tonight,' 'I'll do anything to get my kids back'), which the counselor then strategically reflects and amplifies. This is a textbook MI pattern.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but not reinforced, using double-sided reflections and reframing questions to pivot toward change. A few reflections revisited the sustain side slightly longer than necessary.
