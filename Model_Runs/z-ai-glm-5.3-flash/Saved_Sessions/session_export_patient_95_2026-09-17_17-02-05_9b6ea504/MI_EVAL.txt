# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with careful safety screening, gratitude, and affirmations of Maria's strengths despite her suicidal ideation and resistance to safety planning. Falls short of 5 due to warmth that feels somewhat formulaic and limited explicit normalization of her ambivalence about giving up the pills.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, notably the 'patched but not seen' reflection that Maria explicitly confirmed captured her experience for the first time. Depth faltered when her existential despair ('What's supposed to change? The burning is every night') and fear of losing the pills as an 'option' were not fully reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on immediate safety throughout, achieving measurable momentum from expressed hopelessness to a completed action (texting Rosa) and a stated backup plan within one session. Not a 5 because Maria's question about longer-term change went unaddressed, leaving the trajectory beyond tonight undefined.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative — co-drafted the text message, offered role-play, and let Maria's choices (neighbor over mother, 'keep talking') steer the session. Collaboration sometimes took the form of pre-formatted option lists for selection rather than genuinely co-generating possibilities, and 'we' framing appeared less often than individual choice framing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed Maria's choice with 'no plans unless you want them' and respected her refusals of the safety plan and 988 while offering alternatives. Autonomy framing often came as a closing tag question after directive content rather than being woven into the exploration itself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Maria herself — her protective reasons (mom's face, niece's drawing), her own solution (Rosa holding the pills), and her self-initiated text, with confidence rising from 0 to 5. Evocation was strongest around immediate safety but did not extend to broader motivations for living or her unanswered question about long-term change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Maria as the expert, eliciting her perspective and adapting to her choices, which demonstrably shaped the session. Some exchanges reverted to menu-driven formats positioning the psychiatrist as option-generator, and her deeper strategic questions were not taken up collaboratively.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and amplified change talk, most notably when Maria's 'small part' wanting to get through tonight was immediately affirmed as 'a real, important crack of hope,' alongside effective importance/confidence scaling. Could have deepened change talk further by exploring what getting through tonight would mean for her future rather than moving quickly to logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing hopelessness and used reframe-like responses, but softening was often achieved by pivoting quickly to safety logistics rather than working through ambivalence. Maria's fear that removing the pills leaves her 'stuck with the noise' was only partially addressed before moving to the next concrete step, leaving key ambivalence unresolved.
