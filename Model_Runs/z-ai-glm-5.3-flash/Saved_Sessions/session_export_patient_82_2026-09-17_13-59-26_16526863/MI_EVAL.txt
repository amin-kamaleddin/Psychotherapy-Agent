# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, but warmth feels generic and formulaic. There are no individualized affirmations of Lukas's specific strengths (honors student, athlete, self-awareness), and praise like 'smart move' reads as performance praise rather than genuine positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; the counselor rarely reflects Lukas's emotional content, such as his anxiety about disclosure or fear of being 'the weird one'. Responses immediately pivot to scripts and tactics rather than exploring his inner conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use and peer-context boundary-setting throughout, never drifting off-topic. The tight focus occasionally prioritizes tactical momentum over exploring ambivalence, but direction itself is consistently on task.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are collaborative elements, such as inviting agenda-setting and Lukas shaping some content, but power-sharing is uneven. Responses read like a coach delivering playbooks with bulleted tactics and prescribed homework, limiting true two-way dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers choices and asks permission ('Want to rehearse...?'), but advice density is high, with imperative instructions crowding out Lukas's own decision-making. Lukas is never explicitly affirmed as being in charge of the change, and pushing the weekend trial pressures him beyond his contemplative stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is treated as something to be supplied through techniques rather than drawn out of Lukas. His own motivations (athletics, academics, authenticity) were largely voiced by himself, while the counselor failed to elicit and develop them with open questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor frequently assumes the expert role, with responses dominated by prescription lists of tactics. Lukas's own expertise about his friend group was accommodated but not actively leveraged as the driving source of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was elicited largely by Lukas himself, aided by the pros/cons suggestion and rehearsal prompts. However, the counselor missed key opportunities to deepen his insights, such as his observation about usually caving at parties or noticing better legs at Monday practice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does not reinforce sustain talk and offers a helpful reframe that boundaries aren't proof of a problem. However, softening is mostly passive — Lukas's ambivalence ('might blow over and I'm making it a bigger deal') was ignored rather than addressed with double-sided reflections.
