# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout; validates Elena's defensiveness without judgment or pressure. Her minimization is treated as understandable rather than confronted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, emotionally attuned reflections capturing both content and feeling (exhaustion, guilt, blame). Patient explicitly confirms feeling heard for the first time in a medical appointment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on smoking within the context of stress and her son's health, skillfully linking the ear-infection concern to motivation. Slight deduction for the middle portion dwelling on the ENT referral before reconnecting to the smoking target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine power-sharing: asks before advising, offers concrete help in response to stated needs, and lets Elena negotiate the agenda. The follow-up plan is fully co-constructed with her lead.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice and pace ('whatever pace feels right') and explicitly asks what support would look like to her. Offers information and actions rather than directives about smoking.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from the patient through open questions rather than lecturing about risks. She arrives at her own reasons ('if the ENT says it's a factor, I'll deal with it') and self-efficacy, which are then affirmed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly locates expertise within the client ('You know your life best'), and the final plan is entirely her design — her order, her pace — with the psychiatrist explicitly naming that.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation (asking about past coping strategies) produced spontaneous change talk ('the walk thing did more for me than the cigarettes ever did'). Commitment-adjacent statements are amplified and attributed to the patient herself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Uses double-sided reflections and reframes defensive statements as evidence of effort; sustain talk is met with acceptance, not argument. Slight deduction as some reflections initially restated reasons to continue before pivoting.
