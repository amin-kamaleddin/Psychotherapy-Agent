# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained consistent warmth and non-judgment throughout, including no disapproval of the client's initial minimizing. The client's closing comment ('Thanks for not just lecturing me about it') directly confirms he experienced respect and acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, moving beyond surface content to underlying values and emotions (e.g., fear of letting down the people and future he cares about). Alexei repeatedly confirmed reflections with 'Yeah, exactly.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus linking alcohol use to the client's soccer and scholarship goals. It progressed logically from exploring ambivalence to a specific, time-bound commitment plan without wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction unfolded as joint exploration between equals, with the counselor asking open questions and building on the client's contributions. Every plan element originated from the client while the counselor shaped the process through skilled questioning.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly reinforced client ownership ('it's one you're setting up entirely for yourself') with no advice-giving, persuasion, or pressure anywhere in the session. Respecting Alexei's own timeline for the conversation with his mom further demonstrated autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No reasons for change were supplied by the counselor; open questions drew out Alexei's own motivations (scholarship, his mom, athlete identity). His closing statement ('I've got too much I'm working toward to risk it') is a textbook outcome of well-executed evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Alexei as the expert on his own life, with all plan elements (training excuse, telling his friend, posting goals, mom timeline) originating from him. Client contributions genuinely shaped the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions like 'What's one small thing you could do this week?' skillfully evoked strong commitment language ('I'm not doing that again,' 'I'll keep those reminders up'). The counselor then reflected and amplified change talk each time it appeared, building momentum toward commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled skillfully with a reframe rather than argument and largely disappeared after the opening. The score falls short of 5 only because double-sided reflections acknowledging both sides of ambivalence could have been used more explicitly.
