# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone, normalizing Linda's fears and responding supportively to her humor. However, affirmations remained generic rather than affirming her specific strengths, such as her self-education efforts and honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured her core dilemma of feeling trapped between relief and reliance, including her fear of not trusting the doctor's answers. Deeper emotional moments, like her self-discovery about anxiety-driven avoidance, were acknowledged briefly before pivoting to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on medication adherence and appointment preparation with a logical progression and appropriate safety redirection about not self-tapering medications. Direction was sometimes achieved through prescriptive instruction rather than guided discovery, and valuable self-insights were rushed past.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening menu of options and script rehearsal showed collaborative elements, but most content (lists, scripts, plans) was counselor-supplied with only brief check-ins. The interaction functioned more as expert consultation than an equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The initial offering of genuine choice was strong, but the session became increasingly prescriptive with pre-written scripts and mandated plan steps. Linda's own decision was co-opted into the counselor's plan rather than affirmed as her own ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor predominantly provided information and solutions instead of eliciting Linda's own motivations and confidence. Key openings, such as her ambivalence about cutting back on her own and her realization about anxiety-driven avoidance, were missed rather than explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** While the tone was collegial and Linda found the tools helpful, the counselor drifted into expert-driven advice-giving across multiple turns. Linda's contributions did not substantially shape the session direction, and her expertise was never explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged largely from Linda herself, such as her hope that objective lung numbers might quiet her fear. The counselor did not strategically deepen it with scaling questions, confidence rulers, or deliberate evocation of commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively avoided amplifying sustain talk, reflecting her ambivalence without reinforcing it and delivering a clear safety message against self-directed medication changes. Double-sided reflections explicitly pairing her sustain talk with her change talk were absent, which would have strengthened ambivalence resolution.
