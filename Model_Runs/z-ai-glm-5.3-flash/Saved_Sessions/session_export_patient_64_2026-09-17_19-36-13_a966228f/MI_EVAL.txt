# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental, with affirmations like 'That's not dumb, that's you figuring out what actually matters to you.' Warmth occasionally felt slightly formulaic and moments of mild dismissiveness could have been normalized more explicitly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including an accurate double-sided reflection and careful mirroring of the Sunday dinner imagery. One brief overreach: asking what felt 'heavy' overshot Jordan's more casual register.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on flu vaccination with each exchange advancing toward a concrete plan. One moment of drift toward generic emotional exploration before re-establishing behavioral focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session framed as joint exploration with a co-constructed plan incorporating Jordan's preferences. Some steering was clinician-initiated and the session agenda wasn't explicitly negotiated up front.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit and consistent autonomy support throughout: 'I'm not here to talk you into anything,' 'It's completely yours,' 'your pace, your plan.' Options were offered rather than directives, with no pressure or persuasion language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan's motivations (protecting grandpa, avoidable guilt, flu season timing) were drawn out through his own words, with no lecturing. A few questions were slightly leading, and confidence/ability talk was underdeveloped relative to reasons and commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary power-sharing — Jordan was positioned as the expert and his contributions visibly shaped the session and the final plan. His preference for 'real numbers' and 'keeping it simple' drove the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked a full commitment statement ('Maybe I should just do it before flu season really kicks in. For them.') and a concrete plan. Early evocation relied on hypotheticals, and confidence talk wasn't fully deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement, using double-sided reflections and non-argumentative pivots. A few reflections re-voiced sustain talk, slightly amplifying reasons against change.
