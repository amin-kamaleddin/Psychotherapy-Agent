# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently honors the patient's autonomy and choices ('no big deal, just Tuesday,' 'this is your plan and your follow-through, not mine'). Absolutely no arguing, confronting, or imposing direction is evident.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately and repeatedly capture the patient's ambivalence and deeper meaning, e.g., naming the 'first domino' and both the pull toward family life and the pull to quit before trying. The patient explicitly says he felt heard ('thanks for not just lecturing me').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conversation is strongly oriented toward change; the patient moves from ambivalence to a concrete, self-generated plan (Tuesday session, alarms, phone off Monday).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician draws expertise from the patient throughout, asking what he has seen work for others and inviting his own solutions. The patient responds as an equal partner, noting the plan is 'actually mine.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is reinforced throughout ('that's yours to hold,' 'don't let it go to my head'). Change talk is credited entirely to the patient rather than to clinician persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently evokes the patient's own motivations and solutions — his vision of family life, his identification of steady work as the first domino, and his own plan for obstacles. Change talk is self-generated rather than led.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are posed in a genuinely exploratory, collaborative spirit ('I'm curious what you think,' 'if any'), avoiding an expert stance. The patient clearly experiences the interaction as joint problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled use of evocative questions about the envisioned future and what would make the program 'worth it' produces frequent, strong change talk, which the clinician then reinforces with affirmations and reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (record barriers, doubt, program fatigue) is met with non-confrontational reflection and reframing into strengths. One reflection ('the record keeps knocking you back down') slightly amplifies sustain talk before pivoting, but the recovery is quick.
