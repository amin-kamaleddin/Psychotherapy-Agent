# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Nonjudgmental tone landed — Marisol appreciated 'not being another person telling me I made a mistake.' However, warmth was formulaic and chances to meaningfully affirm her autonomy and self-expression were missed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was largely absent; the counselor jumped to medical instructions when Marisol shared her sore gums rather than reflecting her worry. Her frustration about being lectured was acknowledged only superficially.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifted toward assertiveness scripts irrelevant to the referral target, which Marisol rejected multiple times. A collaborative agenda was never established, and the health focus emerged only accidentally through the client's own disclosure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Offered options were counselor-generated scripts Marisol repeatedly declined, and advice dominated until late in the session. Her own ideas about her care were never invited or co-constructed into a plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session's strength: the counselor consistently respected her choice to keep the piercing and framed care-seeking as her decision, reflected in her closing 'that's still my call.' It fell short of 5 because autonomy messaging was implicit and generic rather than actively reinforced throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was provided to the client via risk lists rather than drawn out from her own concerns and values. When she made a small commitment, the counselor failed to consolidate or deepen it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role, dispensing instructions and scripted one-liners without invitation even after Marisol explicitly pushed back. Little recognition of Marisol's expertise about her own body and choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marisol spontaneously produced change-adjacent language ('maybe I should actually keep up with that'), but the counselor did nothing to evoke or deepen it. The change talk emerged despite, not because of, the counselor's technique.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor never argued against the piercing or reinforced sustain talk, avoiding confrontation, but achieved this by sidestepping the behavioral target. No double-sided reflections acknowledged her attachment while highlighting her own health concerns.
