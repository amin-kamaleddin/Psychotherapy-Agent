# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout; normalized Jordan's lost thread and validated both sides of his ambivalence without shaming the shoplifting. Would be stronger with more explicit affirmations of Jordan's strengths, honesty, and self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including accurate double-sided reflections on the tug-of-war and the loneliness of change. Missed opportunities to deepen empathy at emotional moments like the 'pathetic' self-label and fear of Marcus's rejection, which were redirected toward problem-solving too quickly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on reducing shoplifting from start to finish, with every prompt tied to the target behavior. Session progressed logically from ambivalence to a specific, client-generated action despite a brief distraction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue between equals, with Jordan's ideas driving all planning and the framing that sessions must be worth his time. Somewhat asymmetric rhythm—little explicit 'we' language, mutual planning, or invitation for feedback on the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Jordan's ownership, exemplified by 'That's you—not me, not the court—deciding this matters.' No advice imposed; all ideas came from Jordan, and his pacing was honored without pressure or correction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out intrinsic motivations—freedom from fear, protecting his mother, genuine connection—rather than supplying reasons to change. Jordan's language evolved from 'I can't keep shoplifting' to self-generated commitment ('This week. I'll actually do it.').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Jordan as the expert on his own life, and Jordan's contributions (Marcus, the text idea, the food plan) shaped the session. Little explicit acknowledgment of continued joint work ahead, such as following up on his 'maybe I talk to you about other options'.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic, high-level evocation with change talk visibly strengthening across the session—from 'I can't keep shoplifting' to 'I have to stop' to 'This week. I'll actually do it.' The pivotal reflection reframing 'I have to stop' as Jordan's own decision reinforced ownership.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally handled sustain talk well, using double-sided reflections and converting friendship-loss fears into productive planning. Recurring 'I still want stuff I can't afford' was never strategically reframed or explored with lawful alternatives.
