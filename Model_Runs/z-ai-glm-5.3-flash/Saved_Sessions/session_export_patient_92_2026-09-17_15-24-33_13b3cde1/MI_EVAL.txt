# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opens with "I'm glad you're here. I'm ready to listen without judgment." Maintains consistent warmth throughout. Responds neutrally and respectfully when client is defensive, never labeling or judging. Remains supportive even after client jokes about not keeping the appointment. Affirmations are frequent; no judgment or disapproval appears anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening accurately captures the client's stance, including reflections on pacing and "binge" drinking and acknowledgment of ambivalence. Reflections are sometimes brief and paired immediately with questions or menus rather than being allowed to land emotionally. Missed opportunities to deepen reflection on the social identity dimension (fear of being mocked) and the emotional meaning of "Maybe if I got sick."

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on alcohol use as the target behavior from open to close. Each turn connects to the drinking topic: guidelines, weekly totals, pros/cons, a concrete cut-back plan, confidence scaling, and reminder strategy. No drift; reflections stay tethered to the drinking goal, and the session progresses logically toward a defined next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided: asks permission before providing information, invites client's data, and co-constructs the plan around the client's own strategies. Menus and confidence scales reinforce equality. A few turns are fairly information-dense and counselor-structured (guidelines explanation, bulleted plan ideas), slightly tipping the balance toward the counselor shaping the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary. Repeatedly offers choice via menus; accepts refusal of a formal plan without pressure and pivots to a one-line if/then. "No promises" and "still not sure this is necessary" accepted without argument. Client edits the reminder text, which the counselor accommodates, reinforcing ownership of the change as the client's own test.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation from within rather than lecturing — "What would bump that number up?" draws out the client's own incentive; ambivalence is evoked early. Draws out the client's own coping strategies rather than prescribing them. Evocation of importance/desire language is thinner than evocation of confidence, and the counselor occasionally fills space with expert content rather than prompting the client's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently conveys that expertise resides with the client; the plan is genuinely client-authored, and the counselor explicitly affirms "practical and self-directed." Client's rejection of a drafted comeback is respected. Score is 4 because the counselor sometimes supplies substantial content (guidelines, plan elements, reminder wording) that the client then modifies, rather than consistently drawing material out first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and reinforces change talk: questions about worries and what matters most elicit "I guess I've got something to think about now," and confidence scaling draws out self-motivating statements. Client progresses from sustain talk to a concrete self-generated plan, confidence rising from 5 to 6, and a commitment to return. Could score higher by asking more explicitly about importance (not just confidence) and reflecting change talk more deliberately each time it occurred.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids reinforcing sustain talk — responds to minimization with neutral acknowledgment and pivots to information and client choice rather than arguing. The "you're surprised" reflection validates without dwelling on reasons against change. Some counselor turns invite pros/cons, giving airtime to sustain talk, and the counselor occasionally restates the client's minimizing; minor, but slightly reduces the score.

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** 4.3/5
