# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with specific affirmations ('Good work,' 'Good choice'). Affirmed tasks well but offered less affirmation of Marcus as a person, keeping this short of a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflection of Marcus's overwhelm, but reflections gave way to information delivery. Emotional openings (shame about asking, embarrassment, night fear, dog gratitude) repeatedly went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on asthma management throughout; every exchange advanced a concrete next step with a time-specified action plan at close. No topic drift despite many possible tangents.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Marcus selected his own path, but the pattern was counselor-proposes, client-picks with an agenda set by the counselor. Marcus's own solutions emerged despite, not because of, explicit co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent menu-based choices with explicit opt-outs and no pressure; pushback was accommodated. Lacked explicit ownership language ('this is your decision') to reinforce Marcus's control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strategic questions (rescue inhaler frequency) led Marcus to his own conclusions, but questions were mostly logistical. Marcus's key motivators—dog walks, wife's worry, avoiding scary nights—were never explored or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices shaped the session, but the expertise balance tilted toward the counselor delivering instruction and pre-built menus. Never explicitly invited Marcus's own ideas or acknowledged him as the expert on his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Structure allowed Marcus to generate his own commitments ('One week, I can commit to that'), ending with strong closing change talk. Rarely deepened or reflected change talk back to strengthen it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Brief validation of money stress before pivoting to action was effective, but sustain talk was bypassed rather than explored. No double-sided reflections; financial ambivalence resurfaced at session's end ('money's still money').
