# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance, affirming Wiremu's dignity and strengths (e.g., 'that shows strength'). However, warmth was somewhat task-focused, with limited normalization of his emotional fears before pivoting to strategy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered accurate complex reflections that captured Wiremu's core ambivalence (e.g., wanting support but respect as an adult). Deeper emotional content — his remorse and fear of being seen as 'the stupid shit' — was acknowledged only briefly before redirecting to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus throughout, with every exchange advancing the target behavior — from ambivalence to a rehearsed kōrero and a concrete written plan. Wiremu left with a clear next step ('I could have this kōrero this weekend'), showing sustained, purposeful direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative — agenda items were offered as choices, Wiremu's edits were incorporated, and the plan was co-developed. However, the counselor repeatedly supplied ready-made solutions rather than jointly generating options, revealing an asymmetry in expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices and returned responsibility to the client ('Puts it on me, which is where it should be'). The volume of pre-formed scripts somewhat constrained Wiremu's own authorship of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Wiremu's own values — children, dignity, accountability — via rulers, open questions, and rehearsal prompts. Frequent advice-giving pre-empted evocation in places, and the drinking behavior itself was never deeply explored from the client's perspective.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing was evident through permission-oriented questions and inviting script edits, but the counselor frequently slipped into an expert/advisor role supplying scripts, structures, and written plans. Expertise remained visibly located with the counselor rather than actively drawn out from Wiremu.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change talk; the importance/confidence rulers drew strong commitment language ('ten out of ten, no question', 'I'll do it'). However, Wiremu's confidence rating of 6 was never revisited — a missed opportunity to build self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of losing independence, feeling 'watched') was acknowledged but often met with immediate solution-giving rather than strategic softening. The counselor avoided amplifying sustain talk but did not consistently use double-sided reflections or pivot ambivalence back toward Wiremu's own change reasons.
