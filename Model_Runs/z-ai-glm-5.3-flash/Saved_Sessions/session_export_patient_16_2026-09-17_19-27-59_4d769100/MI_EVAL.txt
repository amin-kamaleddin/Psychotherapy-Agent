# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations throughout; client notes the session was less painful than expected. Falls short of 5 only because affirmations feel slightly formulaic and regard for Anika beyond her athletic identity could be more individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Several accurate, emotionally attuned reflections, including a strong opening reflection of her ambivalence. Misses depth by not fully sitting with the loneliness and fear of teasing before pivoting to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive arc from exploration to concrete commitment with no drift. Every exchange moves the target behavior forward while remaining client-led.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative, with the plan co-constructed from Anika's own suggestions and the follow-up negotiated. Falls short only because the counselor drives nearly every transition with questions and could invite Anika to steer the agenda more.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support: 'You set the pace,' no advice imposed, and every plan element originates from the client. Anika's free volunteering of the text promise confirms she experienced genuine choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation — basketball performance, Sarah's support, being 'one of the good ones' — was elicited, never supplied. Anika's own closing comment confirms the reasons were drawn from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary partnership, with the clinician explicitly locating expertise in the client ('That came entirely from you') and every plan element generated by Anika. Her recognition that she 'already had reasons to stop' is direct evidence the power-sharing landed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength: evocative questions reliably drew out commitment and ability language ('I could actually do that,' 'I want to'). The clinician reinforced confidence and even had her rehearse the text to Sarah — a skillful commitment-strengthening technique.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is neither dwelt on nor reinforced; the clinician effectively pivots with double-sided, solution-oriented questions without repeating back harm-minimizing language. A 5 would require more explicit reframing of the social fears rather than quick redirection past them.
