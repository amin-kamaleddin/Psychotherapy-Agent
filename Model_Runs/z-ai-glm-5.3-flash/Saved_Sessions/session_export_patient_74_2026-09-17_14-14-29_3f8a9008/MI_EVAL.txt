# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirmed the patient's choice not to quit while honoring her honesty. Affirmations tended to be brief and generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used reflective listening well, capturing the patient's ambivalence and noting the sting from her daughter's comment. Reflections were often brief or skipped in favor of practical tips, missing chances to deepen emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking and protecting her children throughout, never drifting. Each turn tied back to concrete behaviors and ended with questions moving toward actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements, incorporating the patient's ideas into the plan and negotiating a follow-up. However, the pattern repeatedly involved delivering expert advice lists first, with the patient reacting afterward.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed the patient's right to choose and applied no pressure despite her pre-contemplative stance. Choices were framed as selecting from counselor-generated menus rather than fostering her ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little sense that motivation resides within the client; questions focused on logistics rather than reasons, meaning, or vision. Her spontaneous spark about wondering what life would feel like without the after-dinner cigarette went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing, with the patient's ideas (hoodie, sister conversation) shaping the plan. The counselor frequently fell into an expert-driven pattern of prescriptive advice before asking what the patient knew or wanted.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely actively evoked change talk; spontaneous change language went unreinforced or undiscussed. Confidence ruler answers (7, 5) were not probed with 'why not lower / what would it take to be higher.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or dwelling on sustain talk, pivoting instead to the patient's stated priorities and validating her stance. Occasionally restated her sustain position explicitly, mildly echoing rather than softening it.
