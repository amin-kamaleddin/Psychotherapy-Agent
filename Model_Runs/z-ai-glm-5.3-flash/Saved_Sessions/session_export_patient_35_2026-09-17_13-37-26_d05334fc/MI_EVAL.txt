# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and affirmed Elena's harm-reduction efforts and need for autonomy. Deducted because warmth felt formulaic, with repeated menu-style offerings that felt mechanical rather than deeply person-centered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, but empathy was inconsistent. Emotionally significant statements (feeling judged, 'I don't even really enjoy them anymore,' 'I don't know what I'd have left') were met with brief acknowledgments or strategy lists rather than deeper reflections of hurt, grief, and loss.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept consistent focus on smoking and its stress-related maintenance, progressing logically to a concrete, time-bound experiment. Minor drift into general stress management was appropriately linked back to smoking, though stronger connection to Elena's stated value ('my son deserves me healthy') would improve direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena's choices shaped the plan, but the counselor dominated the informational content with pre-packaged technique lists without eliciting her own ideas or existing coping strategies. Interaction was closer to counselor-proposing, client-selecting than true two-way exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly emphasized choice and explicitly permitted continued smoking without pressure or scare tactics. Fell short of a 5 because options were presented as counselor-generated menus rather than open invitations, and Elena's self-direction was not actively reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence scaling and the experiment framing gently elicited self-persuasion, but the counselor relied mainly on providing information. Strong change talk and self-reflective statements were never followed up with open questions or exploration of Elena's own reasons and ideas for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Elena to choose focus areas and her contributions shaped the plan, but content remained heavily counselor-driven. More deliberate drawing out of Elena's own expertise was possible given her evident self-knowledge about her routines.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Created some conditions for change talk (confidence rating, experiment framing), and Elena produced meaningful change statements. However, these were never explicitly reflected, amplified, or deepened—no exploration of the 'why' or development of desire/need/commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with Elena's minimization but offered little active softening—no double-sided reflections, and sustain talk passed largely unaddressed. Recaps occasionally repeated sustain talk back verbatim rather than strategically redirecting it.
