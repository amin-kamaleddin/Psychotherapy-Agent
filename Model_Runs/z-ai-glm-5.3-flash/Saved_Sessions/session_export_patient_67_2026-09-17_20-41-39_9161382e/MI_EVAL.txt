# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard was communicated consistently, from normalizing Kenji's reaction to the referral to affirmations like 'you're already doing something a lot of people never do.' His ambivalence and avoidance were accepted without judgment, and tracking was framed as info-gathering rather than self-evaluation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were highly accurate, capturing subtle nuances such as the double-sided 'part of you brushed it off, and another part got bothered.' Kenji repeatedly confirmed understanding ('yeah, exactly'), showing the counselor tracked his fuzzy worry and relief-vs-fear tension precisely.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on alcohol use with a concrete outcome, progressing steadily from the referral reaction to a committed two-week tracking plan with a review point. Each exchange moved forward with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was fully co-constructed through open questions, with practical ideas (phone notes, ten-second logging) coming from Kenji himself. He explicitly recognized this ownership: 'it's the first time this whole thing's felt like my idea instead of the doctor's.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice was reinforced at every point — 'not what you should do, what would you want?' and 'what feels right?' The counselor avoided pressure toward reduction, honoring Kenji's 'not promising anything big' and his choice to start with clarity rather than change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client through open questions like 'what would that mean to you?' rather than argument or persuasion. Kenji generated his own realization and commitment language ('I think I actually want to'), which the counselor then developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly located expertise in the client ('you know yourself best'), and the plan emerged fully from Kenji's own ideas and words. Summaries integrated his language before inviting each next step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evoked desire ('maybe I'd want to cut back a bit'), ability ('that feels doable'), and commitment language ('I can do that. I think I actually want to'). Momentum was reinforced by reflecting the shift and having Kenji articulate his own plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('part of me doesn't want to look') was acknowledged without amplification and gently pivoted using the client's own ambivalence as a foothold. The 'knowing beats guessing' reframe was adopted by Kenji as his own, with no reinforcement of the status quo.
