# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validating Maria's distrust and affirming her control without labeling her hostility. Deducted for formulaic warmth — repeated menu options and boilerplate crisis disclaimers felt scripted rather than personally attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections (frustration about being rushed, wanting control), but emotional disclosures like the stairs embarrassment and husband's complaints were met with information or menus rather than reflection. Her relational challenge ('Why do you even care?') was answered briefly rather than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stayed focused on asthma management and moved steadily from concerns to a concrete, actionable plan (trial, script, follow-up) despite resistance. Minor deduction: menu offerings scattered focus across unrelated options and repeated safety recitals added drift at the close.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructed the plan with genuine incorporation of Maria's edits to the script and repeated choice throughout. However, the mechanical A/B/C menu format felt procedural, and guideline information was delivered in expert-lecture mode without engaging her own research.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongest domain: the time-limited trial with a documented stop option was built in at Maria's request, and the counselor never pressured despite her repeated resistance. Explicitly reinforced her ownership ('it's in the notes that I can stop whenever I want') throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Weakest area — Maria's motivations (stairs embarrassment, sleep disruption) emerged mostly through her own volunteering, not counselor evocation. The counselor proposed the trial solution directly and supplied arguments rather than drawing out her own reasons with open questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed Maria as the decision-maker, offering options rather than directives and honoring her demand for the stop clause in writing. Missed an opportunity to treat her research as expertise instead of assuming the expert role in information delivery.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maria progressed from 'Why fix what mostly works?' to 'maybe' and concrete commitment ('I'll actually use it during the trial'), aided by the strategic time-limited trial structure. Change talk emerged partly through counselor-proposed solutions rather than evocation of her own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argued with or reinforced Maria's substantial sustain talk; instead validated her distrust and pivoted to a low-commitment structure with a stop option. Minor deduction: repeated benefit/risk counter-arguments edged toward persuasion rather than letting her resolve ambivalence herself.
