# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout without labeling Alexei's experimentation. However, warmth was brief and formulaic, missing opportunities to normalize adolescent experimentation or affirm his honesty about the police incident and hiding it from his mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered accurate reflections but they remained at a summary level before pivoting to menu options or questions. Missed key opportunities to explore deeper emotions: the police scare, the secret from his mother, and the peer pressure behind 'not wanting to look lame.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent, purposeful focus on the target behavior (avoiding alcohol in peer situations) across the entire session with no topic drift. The session moved coherently from importance → skills rehearsal → contingency planning → concrete commitment (Friday reminder, Sunday check-in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative, with menus offered instead of prescriptions and plans co-constructed (e.g., the emoji exit code built on Alexei's buddy idea). Advice-giving was heavy in places—rapid-fire refusal lines delivered as an expert bundle—shifting some exchanges toward a skills-coach posture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently used permission-based language ('Want to practice one now?') and accepted Alexei's decline of further role-play without pressure. A 5 would require more explicit framing that Alexei owns the entire change process and outcomes, not just the selection of tactics.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers were legitimate evocative tools that elicited strong motivations ('college soccer doesn't happen by luck'), but much of the session was supply-driven, with the counselor providing scripts and plans rather than drawing them from Alexei. Open-ended evocative questions were sparse.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Alexei's input genuinely drove the plan—his chosen refusal line, buddy plan, and exit code shaped the intervention. However, the counselor repeatedly generated option menus himself rather than first eliciting Alexei's own ideas, which would have better conveyed that expertise resides within the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used importance and confidence rulers, eliciting change talk and tracking measurable movement (6→8→9) while highlighting the client's progress. Change talk could have been deepened with open questions (e.g., 'What would success Saturday look like?') rather than moving quickly into scripts and plans.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Alexei's sustain talk ('not sure this stuff is really for me,' 'I don't drink all the time') was neither reinforced nor strategically addressed—it was largely bypassed. No double-sided reflections or affirmations validating his skepticism while pivoting to his goals, leaving the minimization unexamined.
