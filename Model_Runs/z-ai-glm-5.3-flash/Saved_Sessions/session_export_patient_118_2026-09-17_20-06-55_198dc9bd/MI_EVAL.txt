# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout: 'There's nothing I want from you' and 'No other shoe, I promise.' Validated frustration with lectures, normalized resistance without judgment, and remained warm even when tested.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflective listening that went beyond surface content to underlying emotion (e.g., 'hiding your smile... that's a heavy thing to carry'). Patient responses showed deepening disclosure, confirming the reflections landed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus anchored to the patient's own agenda (his teeth), with smoking addressed only through its connection to dental outcomes. Session ended with a concrete action tied to a forward-looking question.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner: 'let's work through your list together' and co-constructed the agenda from the patient's own priorities. Expertise was offered within shared problem-solving rather than prescription.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed patient ownership ('it's your call entirely,' 'the deciding's mine') and explicitly decoupled the dental booking from any quitting commitment. This directly defused the patient's reactance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic open questions ('What would Seamus want?') drew progressively deeper change talk from the patient himself. The patient even recognized it: 'You're getting me to say the quitting bit for you' — evidence motivation was genuinely his.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positioned the patient as the expert on his own life and credited insights to him ('That idea came from you, not me'). The patient's contributions substantially shaped the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk: 'I'd like to not feel self-conscious,' 'one pack fewer a day,' and 'I'd want to protect it. Whatever that takes.' Consistently recognized, reflected, and deepened the patient's own movement language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk; when the patient preemptively rejected the 'cleaning won't hold unless you quit' message, the clinician pivoted to his practical list. Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement, keeping energy on the patient's own reasons for change.
