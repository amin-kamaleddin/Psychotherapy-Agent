# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and accepted the client's boundaries, including his refusal to quit. However, warmth was brief and functional, with rapid checklists at times feeling clinical rather than relationally attuned.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some accurate early reflections, but missed key emotional disclosures like his fear of losing his place in the social scene. Vulnerable statements were frequently met with practical option lists rather than emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus throughout, progressively narrowing from general concerns to a concrete, client-valued target: a 48-hour no-use window protecting auditions. Each exchange advanced this goal and closed with a specific commitment and follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client could select options and his framing was adopted, but most solutions (scripts, checklists, calendars) were counselor-generated rather than co-created. The interaction often resembled expert consultation more than joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed next steps as the client's choice and explicitly accepted his boundary of not quitting. Falls short of a 5 due to rapid menu-offering of pre-packaged solutions and leaving his closing insight unaffirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Drew out genuine client motivation at key points, including realizations about his use and authentic desire to protect auditions. However, often shifted into providing reasons and solutions himself rather than exploring the client's own confidence and reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited choices and honored refusals, but much of the session was expert-driven with detailed plans supplied before the client's own ideas could emerge. The client had to push back on suggestions that didn't fit his life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out meaningful client-generated change talk and a genuine commitment to the 48-hour window. However, key moments like 'Maybe that's something' and his closing insight were not explored or affirmed further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk, which is a strength, but did little active softening via double-sided reflections. Statements like 'I'm not agreeing to quit' were met with accommodation rather than reflections holding both sides of ambivalence.
