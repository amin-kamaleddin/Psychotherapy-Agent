# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms Kevin's autonomy and perspective, repeatedly deferring expertise to him about his social world. Kevin's growing comfort generating his own ideas reflects a strong accepting stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both sides of Kevin's ambivalence (fun vs. risk, worry vs. reality) and even surface the emotional undertone in 'that's not great, is it?' Kevin repeatedly feels understood and builds on the reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Each response keeps the conversation focused on drinking while respecting Kevin's pace — from the initial accident, to the Saturday test, to the wedding plan. Questions move the change process forward coherently without pushing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All ideas (eating first, shandy, cash-only, sister as ally) come from Kevin, with the psychiatrist explicitly crediting him and handing decisions back. The tone is 'walking alongside' rather than advising.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Kevin owns the plan entirely — 'these are your ideas,' 'it's yours now,' 'that's your call entirely' — and explicitly notes it's unusual not to be told what to do. No pressure, commands, or should statements appear.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently draw out Kevin's own motivations and solutions: how to pace without standing out, what would make the plan work, what would raise his confidence. Kevin articulates his own reasons (remembering nights, avoiding crutches, identity shift toward 'take it or leave it').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange is genuinely two-way: the psychiatrist asks, reflects, and steps back; Kevin plans, self-corrects, and sets his own confidence rating. There is no expert–patient hierarchy in the dialogue.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustained, deepening change talk throughout — commitment ('food first, I can do that'), ability ('six out of ten with a plan'), desire, and reasons all emerge, plus spontaneous self-motivational shifts ('maybe I'm not just someone who loses it every time').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (vibe concerns, willpower failures, 'that's not me') is met with reflections rather than argument, and Kevin resolves most of it himself ('maybe it's mostly me worrying'). Realistic caveats about the wedding are honored, not contested.
