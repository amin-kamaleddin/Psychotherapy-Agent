# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with Marco's worries and failed dieting history received without criticism. Affirmations were brief and formulaic ('Nice choice'), lacking deeper individualized positive regard for his persistence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections captured Marco's ambivalence well ('you want to be healthier... but past attempts left you feeling stuck'). Empathy was diluted by stacked questions, and his discouragement and shame about repeated failures were never explicitly validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary focus throughout — every exchange moved from goals to barriers to a concrete, self-selected plan (Mon/Wed/Sat walks, reminders, backups) with no topic drift. The session progressed logically from ambivalence to a specific, achievable commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent permission-seeking and menu-based options with Marco selecting every element of the plan. Collaboration was slightly limited by pre-packaged suggestions and scripted wording rather than drawing Marco's own phrasing first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicit choice framing ('I'm here to listen and support whatever you choose') and full respect for Marco's own timeline and decisions. Autonomy support would be stronger with more explicit reinforcement of his ownership of the plan, as some turns subtly pushed toward action.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of rulers, future-oriented questions, and barrier exploration drew out Marco's own reasons and solutions. However, the counselor frequently supplied strategies and scripts rather than eliciting Marco's ideas, and never explored the deeper 'why' behind his high importance rating.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated permission-asking and choice-ending questions let Marco's contributions genuinely shape the session. Rapid-fire suggestions created a slightly agenda-driven rhythm, and his expertise was implied rather than explicitly acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions effectively raised confidence from 6 to 8, and open questions evoked Marco's own plan and change language ('it feels kind of good having an actual plan'). Missed opportunities to deepen his rising confidence and explore his reasons for wanting change this time.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about snacks and giving in was briefly acknowledged and redirected to actionable steps. The acknowledgment was minimal rather than strategic — no double-sided reflections linking sustain talk to change talk, and barriers were resolved by the counselor's suggestions rather than Marco's own solutions.
