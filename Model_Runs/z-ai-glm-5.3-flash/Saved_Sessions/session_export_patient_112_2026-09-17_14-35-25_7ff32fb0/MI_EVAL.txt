# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations throughout. Some affirmations felt formulaic and tied to compliance with the counselor's plan rather than being deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Adequate early reflections, but quickly pivoted to advice-giving, cutting short exploration of emotional and identity themes. Rosa's fears about identity loss and friends' judgment were met with practical suggestions rather than deeper emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking cessation throughout, progressing to a concrete, time-limited experiment with a start time and follow-up plan. Occasional drift into weight-management and NRT details, but generally steered back on target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative elements included offering choices and co-constructing the final plan. However, the counselor supplied most ideas, and Rosa's own expertise informed the plan only after she pushed back.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered menus, asked permission, and framed quitting as a low-commitment test, which the patient explicitly appreciated. Frequent advice-giving slightly undercut full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited, including a good move asking what cigarettes do for her. Reliance on providing information and suggestions over asking Rosa for her own ideas limited deeper evocation of intrinsic motivation and confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power-sharing via choice menus, but the overall stance was expert-driven with prescriptive tips in nearly every turn. Rosa's contributions shaped the plan only at the margins, and she noticed the asymmetry herself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rosa offered clear change talk and a concrete commitment was secured, but largely via menus rather than deliberate evocation. Missed opportunities to deepen change talk around the doctor's warning and her hopes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk and normalized slips, which is a strength. However, responses were typically reassurance followed by advice rather than strategic double-sided reflections pivoting toward change.
