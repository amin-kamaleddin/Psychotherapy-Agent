# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental about the underage drinking; validated Jason's frustration and affirmed his responsible actions without lecturing. Fell short of a 5 because warmth remained somewhat transactional, with little affirmation of deeper personal strengths beyond case logistics.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections captured Jason's frustration and priorities well, but the counselor shifted into administrative mode as the session progressed. Emotionally meaningful moments—such as the deflection about turning 21 and 'this whole night cost more than the party was worth'—went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened with a strong framing linking the incident to Jason's goals, but quickly pivoted to conduct-board logistics. The actual target behavior (alcohol use and future risk) received almost no sustained focus after the opening turns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Engaged Jason as a decision-maker: solicited his preferred outcome, incorporated his edits, and co-edited the conduct statement. Short of a 5 because the counselor drove the agenda, with Jason's input shaping wording more than the session's substance or goals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices—contest vs. provide context, draft vs. rehearse, menu of backup options—without pressure. Autonomy support operated mainly in the procedural/administrative domain rather than reinforcing Jason's ownership of the change process itself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The initial goal-framing question was a good evocative opener, but afterward the counselor supplied virtually all content: options, draft language, and scripted answers. Jason was cast as an approver rather than a source of his own reasons and capacity for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jason's input shaped the statement and he chose among options, but the dynamic increasingly resembled a case manager/client service relationship. Jason's own expertise about his drinking and motivations was never explicitly tapped.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jason spontaneously offered significant change-adjacent statements ('parties are fun, but dealing with all this? Not it,' 'maybe I'll even pick up something useful') that were never reflected, deepened, or amplified. Substantial missed opportunities given his surfacing ambivalence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I barely drank,' 'I turn 21 in a month,' 'this changes nothing') was handled neutrally—neither reinforced nor strategically softened. No double-sided reflections or reframes linking his stated values (clean record, housing) to his drinking choices.
