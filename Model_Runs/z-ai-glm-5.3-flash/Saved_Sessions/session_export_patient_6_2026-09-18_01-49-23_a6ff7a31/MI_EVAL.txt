# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms the patient's autonomy and personal agency, using phrases like 'You came up with your own answer' and 'a plan you built yourself.' The patient's shift from fearing a lecture to feeling genuinely heard reflects strong unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections throughout accurately capture the patient's words and underlying feelings, e.g., 'the push is toughest when it comes from someone you care about.' The patient explicitly notes 'you didn't just lecture me... it helped to think it through out loud.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Each exchange skillfully steers the conversation toward change talk — the patient's goals for college soccer, reasons not to drink, and concrete refusal strategies. By the end, the patient articulates his own 'why' and a clear plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician uses open questions ('what do you think would help you feel confident?') and invites the patient to generate his own solutions. Decisions like the two-week check-in are framed as shared choices rather than directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient repeatedly arrives at his own conclusions ('a reason that's mine, not just what my mom would want'). The clinician reinforces self-generated change talk rather than prescribing advice or authority-based arguments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions like 'what would you have done differently to get there?' and 'which of those things matters most to you?' reliably draw out the patient's own change talk and values. The patient self-motivates by connecting sobriety to soccer and friendship.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation feels balanced and exploratory, with the clinician following the patient's lead. Slight clinician direction appears in weaving reflections, but the patient retains ownership throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician systematically elicits and reinforces change talk: 'Next time I'll be ready,' 'soccer wins — it has to,' and the plan to bring his friend on board. Each statement is reflected and amplified to build commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (friend pressure, wanting to fit in, temptation at parties) is met with empathy and dual-sided reflections rather than argument, transforming ambivalence into resolve. Only mild residual doubt remains, addressed through the planned check-in.
