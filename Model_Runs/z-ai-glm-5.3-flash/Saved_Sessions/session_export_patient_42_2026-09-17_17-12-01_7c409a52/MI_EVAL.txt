# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with validating statements like 'That makes total sense.' However, affirmations of Elise's own strengths (her insight, choosing Claire as backup) were sparse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong early reflective listening capturing Elise's core ambivalence and social needs. Empathy reflections thinned out mid-session as the counselor shifted to advice-giving, and the fear of invisibility was never revisited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear, unwavering focus on cutting back/quitting with no topic drift. Session progressed logically to a specific, time-bound plan Elise committed to.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Started collaboratively with menu options and permission-seeking, and Elise shaped key elements (bakery reward, Claire, her preferred line). But the middle was counselor-driven, with Elise deferring ('you'd know better than me').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicit choice at the outset, permission before advice, and full honoring of Elise's choices. Later advice on dosing and scripts was somewhat directive, without reinforcing that outcomes belong to Elise.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of importance/confidence rulers that yielded authentic client motivation and a confidence gain from 6 to 7. Missed opportunities to explore values around belonging and longer-term change beyond the one-week frame.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elise was invited into decisions, but the counselor frequently slid into an expert role, supplying dosing advice and scripted lines. Elise's 'you'd know better than me' revealed a power imbalance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers effectively evoked change talk ('I could try that,' confidence rising to 7), and Elise's own ideas were reinforced. The counselor failed to deepen her key statement about fearing loss of connection more than health consequences.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced, and a useful reframe honored the social need. However, strong sustain talk ('losing my people, that's the scary part') received only brief acknowledgment with no double-sided reflections or strategic pivots.
