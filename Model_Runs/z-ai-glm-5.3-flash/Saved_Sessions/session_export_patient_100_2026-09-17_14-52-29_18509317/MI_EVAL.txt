# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental opening with consistent validation of Maya's hesitations and no labeling of her drinking. However, explicit affirmations of her strengths are sparse, and some validating phrases read as formulaic (repeated nearly verbatim).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captures Maya's no-win dynamic with her parents and her school stress. Some reflections only summarize her words rather than probe deeper emotional meaning, such as the pain of parental distrust.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains good focus on Maya's chosen concern (parent conflict, school stress) with clear branching questions. However, the referral target—alcohol use—is continually postponed at Maya's deflection and never linked back in before the session ends.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent check-ins ('if that's okay?', 'Want to practice one now?') and responsive adjustments when Maya asks to tweak the Sunday script. Collaboration is uneven, though, as ideas are mostly pre-packaged—Maya influences delivery but rarely the substance or direction of the intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices and adapts when Maya rejects the 'boundary' script as 'too grown-up'; practice is framed as optional. The script-heavy approach subtly prescribes solutions, and Maya's ownership of the change process is affirmed less explicitly than it could be.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicits motivation from within Maya—help arrives as counselor-generated scripts and tips rather than questions drawing out her own reasons, hopes, or confidence. Maya's few self-generated insights arise on their own, not through skillful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaborative stance, but the counselor drifts into expert-driven mode with pre-written scripts and tip lists. Solutions largely originate from the counselor rather than being co-created from Maya's own ideas about her family.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges occasionally ('Sunday's probably the best day anyway,' 'Small steps, I guess') and the counselor invites practice, but never strategically deepens it—no scaling questions or exploration of what success would look like. Multi-option scripts tend to supply language rather than draw out Maya's own.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maya's sustain talk ('it's not a thing,' 'everyone my age does that') is neither reinforced nor actively softened—responses remain neutral acknowledgments while the drinking topic is repeatedly deferred. This avoids conflict but leaves sustain talk unaddressed rather than gently resolved.
