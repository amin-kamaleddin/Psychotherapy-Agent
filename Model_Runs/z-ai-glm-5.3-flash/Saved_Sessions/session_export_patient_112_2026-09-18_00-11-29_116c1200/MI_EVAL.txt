# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard with genuine, specific affirmations like 'That takes courage' and 'That's real evidence you already carry this ability.' Rosa's 'keeping it loose' decision is accepted without pressure, reinforcing warmth and respect throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and often complex, capturing the emotional core of Rosa's ambivalence, including her fears and identity attachment to smoking. The double-sided reflection ('part of you feels it might be too long... part of you knows that future isn't waiting forever') skillfully mirrors her internal conflict.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on smoking cessation while progressing from ambivalence to a concrete first action. The clinician gently redirects barrier-listing back toward motivation and capability without losing the target behavior focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is fully collaborative with open questions and co-constructed decisions, with ideas offered only as options. Rosa explicitly notes the collaborative quality: 'you didn't push. You just... made me look at what I've already done before.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently reinforces Rosa's ownership, honoring her refusal to set a date without pushing and framing the plan as her own chosen experiment. Rosa's closing statement about 'a messy morning I chose' demonstrates successfully fostered self-directed choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within Rosa rather than supplied — no lectures or directives, only evocative questions that elicit her deepest reasons (her mother's illness, her family, proving she can still change). All change talk and the final plan come from Rosa's own words and ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly locates expertise within Rosa ('You know yourself best') and the plan emerged entirely from her own ideas — the porch coffee and Linda as support. Permission-oriented questions exemplify top-level partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evokes and deepens change talk with open questions targeting Rosa's own motivations, eliciting her strongest statements ('I know I've got to stop,' 'tomorrow I can handle'). Change talk is explicitly named, reinforced, and connected to her past success, culminating in a concrete self-generated plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally handled well with reframes and pivots toward change, but some reflections (like the double-sided reflection restating 'too long to imagine changing') amplify barriers somewhat. Occasionally sustain talk is given equal voice rather than being consistently softened toward resolution.
