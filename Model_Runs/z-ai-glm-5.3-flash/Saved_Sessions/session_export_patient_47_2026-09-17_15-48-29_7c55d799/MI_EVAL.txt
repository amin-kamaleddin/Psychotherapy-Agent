# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor opened warmly and nonjudgmentally, respected Evelyn's minimization without shaming, and affirmed her practical plan. However, affirmations were somewhat sparse and formulaic, with missed opportunities to validate the difficulty of changing a lifelong routine or her fear after the fall.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening tracked both Evelyn's enjoyments and her dawning concerns throughout the session. Reflections occasionally stayed at surface level, missing the emotional weight of the fall, her son's worry, and her fear of losing her evening ritual.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with no topic drift, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete, time-limited action plan. Every exchange tied back to the target behavior, with each turn ending in a focused question advancing the agenda.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely a joint enterprise, with Evelyn's ideas (decanting, calendar tracking) shaping the plan. The counselor occasionally supplied ready-made strategy lists rather than consistently drawing out Evelyn's own solutions first, creating brief moments of uneven power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly and repeatedly supported through permission-seeking, option menus, and honoring Evelyn's pacing on the alcohol-free nights without pressure. No coercive or persuasive language appeared, and Evelyn's own choices shaped the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence scaling (5-6 up to 7-8) effectively drew out Evelyn's own reasons for change — the fall, her son, being unsteady at 52. Improvement would involve deeper exploration of her values and more development of change talk through reflective elaboration rather than rapid transitions to action planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Evelyn as the expert, asking permission before sharing information and letting her select her own strategies. At times the counselor shifted toward advice-giving with long bulleted lists of strategies, slightly diluting the power-sharing stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully elicited substantial change talk ('I can't really argue with the numbers,' 'I'm not falling again') and concrete planning language, with confidence scaling helping Evelyn move from 5-6 to 7-8. To improve, change talk could be deepened with reflections and more explicit commitment language rather than immediately moving to strategy lists.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing with sustain talk and used some double-sided reflections, but often simply moved past it to offer problem lists or options rather than strategically pivoting it toward change. More consistent reframing of sustain talk toward Evelyn's stated goals would improve this score.
