# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations like 'congrats on the interview' and 'You're doing this despite the fear.' However, warmth is brief and quickly pivots to problem-solving rather than lingering with individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening, especially 'unreliable childcare is the real obstacle, not you,' which separates competence from circumstance. Reflections are sometimes compressed and immediately followed by advice, limiting deeper exploration of shame and repeated-failure themes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, sustained focus on the concrete target behavior (childcare barriers to attending interviews) with no topic drift. The session progresses logically from exploration to a workable plan, ending with a confidence-building role-play.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina shapes the plan (wording tweaks, naming Ms. Renee, requesting the role-play), but the counselor supplies most content—message drafts, dollar amounts, strategies—rather than drawing solutions out of her. True co-creation appears mainly in the role-play and minor edits.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choice ('Would you like to use that, tweak it, or add a backup ask?') and welcomes Amina's edits without resistance. The strong advice-giving flavor of the early menu of options slightly tempers full autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicits some motivation through open questions, but much of the session is counselor-provided advice and scripts. Amina's strong intrinsic motivation—her confidence of 'a 10' and framing of the 'childcare puzzle'—goes largely un-evoked and undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for Amina's preferences and her ideas do influence the session, but the counselor frequently assumes the expert role, listing four solutions and drafting all messages. The pattern is counselor-generated solutions with client sign-off rather than genuine client-driven problem-solving.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina offers strong change talk herself ('this actually feels doable,' 'my confidence sits about at a 10'), and the counselor occasionally reflects it back. However, the counselor does not strategically deepen it—no open questions exploring what her confidence is built on or what getting the job would mean.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** When Amina voiced doubts about plans falling through, the counselor acknowledged them neutrally and redirected to solutions. Lacks double-sided reflections pairing her doubt with her stated confidence, missing chances to resolve ambivalence skillfully.
