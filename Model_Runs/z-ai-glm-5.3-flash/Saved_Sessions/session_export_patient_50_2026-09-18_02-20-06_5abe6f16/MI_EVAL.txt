# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirmed the patient's efforts and autonomy, validating that nervousness and readiness can coexist. Affirmations were specific and genuine rather than generic praise.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly reflected both sides of the patient's ambivalence, including the fear that small means 'not enough.' Reflections were accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing feelings like embarrassment, self-doubt, and hope.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every response moved the conversation purposefully toward the patient's goal of attempting the exam, from identifying barriers to concrete planning and relapse forecasting. The session had a clear arc ending in a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation was genuinely joint ('let's think together,' 'shall we check in'), with the clinician following the patient's lead on solutions like the sticky note and reminder letter. The clinician acted as a guide rather than an expert directing the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently framed choices as the patient's own ('coming from you,' 'you've built a small toolkit'), and elicited the patient's own plan rather than prescribing one. The decisional balance honored the patient's right to decide pace and scope.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician drew out the patient's own motivations and strengths—the presentation memory, the desire to keep promises to self—rather than imposing reasons for change. Open questions like 'what feels most important' helped the patient find personal meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions were consistently collaborative and non-confrontational, positioning the clinician as a partner in problem-solving. The final check-in about predicting obstacles was framed as joint planning rather than testing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicited and reinforced change talk—'shrinking the task until it fits,' 'rebuilding trust in yourself'—and had the patient articulate the plan in their own words. Confidence scaling with follow-up questions further deepened commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** When the patient expressed doubt ('twenty minutes is pointless,' 'I can't promise anything'), the clinician didn't argue or counter it, instead responding with acceptance and inviting planning around those thoughts. This allowed sustain talk to dissipate naturally without a righting reflex.
