# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains basic respect and avoids lecturing despite the patient's expressed resistance, but affirmations are generic and formulaic ('That's a great start,' 'Small steps matter'), repeated verbatim across multiple closing turns. There is no acknowledgment of the courage it takes to return after failed quit attempts or validation of her specific struggles.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal, with no complex reflections and few responses that capture the meaning or emotion behind Lucía's statements. Emotionally rich moments—smoking out of spite, wanting to tell her daughter she tried—were bypassed in favor of checklists and plans.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps the session focused on smoking and negotiates a concrete, specific plan (30-minute morning delay, dropping one afternoon cigarette, Thursday follow-up) that the patient commits to. Falls short of 5 because focus is maintained through task-completion rather than resolving ambivalence, and repeated crisis-line disclaimers add irrelevant content.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration—inviting input, offering options, and incorporating the patient's modifications into the final plan. However, the eight-question rapid-fire intake and fully pre-packaged plan tilt the power balance toward the counselor, whose supplied solutions crowd out the patient's own coping knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A relative strength: the counselor asks permission, offers choices, allows the patient to renegotiate the plan to her own terms, and explicitly permits slips without blame. Falls short of 5 because prescriptive advice lists are still delivered rather than consistently framing strategies as her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is assumed to come from the counselor's plan rather than elicited from Lucía; her own reasons for change (her daughter, 'common sense') emerge spontaneously but are never developed. There are no open questions exploring her values, hopes, or what success would look like—the opposite of MI's evocative spirit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine partnership moves are present, including asking permission, offering menu options, and co-creating a plan Lucía modifies. However, the rapid-fire questionnaire feels extractive and the counselor prescribes the full plan rather than eliciting and affirming the patient's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little deliberate evocation occurs; the patient's spontaneous change talk ('I'd want to figure out if it's even realistic for me') is not reinforced or deepened. The readiness ruler is asked but the crucial follow-up ('Why a 5-6 and not lower?') is never pursued.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively rolls with initial resistance by explicitly agreeing not to lecture, and does not amplify sustain talk. However, responses to doubt ('another plan that falls apart by day ten') simply move on to logistics rather than using double-sided reflections or affirming autonomy while steering toward change.
