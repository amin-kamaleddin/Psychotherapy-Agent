# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, including nonjudgmental framing of the client's alcohol use ('I'm not here to judge how you unwind') and affirmations of existing strengths. No judgment, labeling, or disapproval despite the client's minimization.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex and double-sided reflections captured ambivalence well ('Both of those are true at once'). Reflections occasionally ran slightly ahead of the client, and emotional undercurrents (e.g., fear related to the father-in-law's death) were touched but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on hypertension and the alcohol–blood pressure link, progressing logically from diagnosis to values to concrete options. Occasional drift into family history was redirected back toward the focal concern each time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided interaction: ideas came from the client (walks, shows), and the alcohol topic emerged from him. The agenda was largely counselor-sequenced, with no explicit agenda negotiation early on.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language framing change as optional and client-chosen ('which one feels most doable to try, if you wanted to?'). Could have more explicitly affirmed the client's ownership of the decision and his right to decide it isn't a priority.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective open questions elicited the client's own reasons (wife's worry, retirement travel) and client-generated solutions. Elicited change talk was not fully developed into commitment language—no importance/confidence exploration or motivational summary before action talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as the expert, asking rather than telling and building on his suggestions. The client's direct request for information ('Do you actually know if it makes a difference?') went unaddressed, missing a shared decision-making opportunity.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk ('I don't like that she worries about me,' 'maybe having less on some days') through well-timed open questions. Opportunities to deepen and consolidate this talk (confidence, elaboration on why cutting back matters) were only partially used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged then pivoted away from, with double-sided reflections keeping both sides in view without amplifying the status quo. Some reflections repeated sustain-talk content ('nothing extreme') more than necessary, though always paired with change-side content.
