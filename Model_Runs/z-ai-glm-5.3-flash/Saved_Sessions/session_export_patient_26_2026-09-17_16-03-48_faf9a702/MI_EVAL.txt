# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations and validation throughout (e.g., 'your worry about trust is totally valid'). However, affirmations are brief and formulaic, lacking personalized recognition of Daniela's strengths as a mother and student.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately capture Daniela's frustration and cautious hope, but the counselor shifts into task mode as the session progresses. Deep emotions like her fear of things falling through and exhaustion go largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus on securing reliable childcare from start to finish, with each exchange ending in a concrete forward step. The session progresses logically from problem identification to a written action plan while honoring Daniela's pacing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela's ideas and edits genuinely shape the final plan, but the counselor generates nearly all structures (option lists, drafts, checklists) and frames herself as the doer. True equal-partner brainstorming is limited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers explicit choices and states support for Daniela's decisions, but the service-delivery style (searching programs, making contacts, producing documents) positions change as counselor-driven. Daniela's ownership of success is not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening question skillfully evokes strong change talk, but the counselor rarely uses open questions to elicit motivation afterward. Motivation is largely assumed as the session pivots quickly to logistics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniela's input shapes the plan at key points (the meal-trade idea, Tuesday, softer tone), but the counselor frequently slips into an expert role by generating options and drafting all documents. Daniela's own problem-solving capacity is under-affirmed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The opening question successfully evokes strong change talk, and structured choices elicit commitment language ('I can text her tonight, actually'). However, the counselor does not deepen this talk with evocative questions, moving straight to task logistics instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about cost, trust, and fear of things falling through is acknowledged without being amplified, but never actively softened or reframed. There are no double-sided reflections or pivots toward Daniela's evidence of agency.
