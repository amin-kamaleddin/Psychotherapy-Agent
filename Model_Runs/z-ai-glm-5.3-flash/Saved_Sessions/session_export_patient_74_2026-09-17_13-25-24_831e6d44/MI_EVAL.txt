# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validating Linda's enjoyment of smoking while affirming her choice to protect her kids. However, when Linda pushed back ('Let's not get ahead of ourselves'), the rapid-fire suggestions could feel slightly dismissive of her stated limits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections acknowledged Linda's hypocrisy concerns and enjoyment of smoking. But deeper emotional content—her guilt about her daughter noticing the smell, her exhaustion—was never explored, as reflections were quickly followed by advice lists.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays consistently focused on reducing the children's exposure and modeling, a reasonable target given Linda's stage. However, it drifted toward cessation-adjacent suggestions (substitutes for the after-dinner cigarette) that exceeded the agreed focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The role-play was genuinely interactive and Linda-directed, and options were offered for her to choose from. However, most of the session involved the counselor presenting pre-packaged plans for Linda to approve, rather than true co-creation from her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly affirms Linda's right to keep smoking and offers menus of options rather than directives. Autonomy is somewhat undermined, though, by persistent advice-giving (tea/gum/deep breaths) after Linda explicitly declined substitutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely assumed rather than elicited; the first substantive question came only after a full advice list, and no importance or confidence exploration occurred. Linda's insights surfaced spontaneously or through role-play rather than through skillful evocative questioning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Linda was invited to choose among options and role-play at her request, but the overall stance was expert-driven, with the counselor generating virtually all ideas and plans. Linda's own existing strategies were not built upon, and her 'you're moving fast' comment revealed the pace was counselor-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged ('I actually feel a little better about tonight,' 'it's a start') and a concrete commitment was obtained, but it arose spontaneously rather than through deliberate evocation. The psychiatrist never reflected or deepened Linda's change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids arguing against Linda's sustain talk, but heavy advice-giving sidesteps ambivalence rather than working with it. Offering substitution strategies Linda explicitly rejected risked amplifying resistance rather than softening it.
