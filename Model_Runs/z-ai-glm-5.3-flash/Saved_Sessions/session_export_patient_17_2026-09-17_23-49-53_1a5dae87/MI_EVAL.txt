# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, receiving shame and stigma ('like a leper') without judgment. Tone is warm and respectful throughout, normalizing ambivalence rather than criticizing it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections capture both content and emotional core, e.g., 'the cigarettes became your only pocket of calm' and the insight about the daughter's disappointment being a real reason. Patient responses confirm she feels deeply understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains clear focus on smoking cessation with a natural progression from ambivalence to concrete this-week actions. Each counselor turn builds toward change with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly frames the work as joint ('figure out together'), which the patient explicitly names as new and valuable. Decisions are fully co-constructed and the counselor shapes rather than dictates the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Patient's ownership is consistently reinforced; quit timing is left entirely to her and no advice is imposed. Her pace and choices are fully respected throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently elicited from within ('What does that tell you about yourself?'), never lectured or supplied. All reasons, strategies, and commitments are client-generated and then developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located with the client ('You know yourself better than anyone'), and the entire plan comes from her own ideas. Counselor questions draw out her contributions, which shape the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoke client-generated motivation, which is then strategically reinforced and deepened. Patient progresses to strong commitment language ('I think I can do those two things this week').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections acknowledge ambivalence without amplifying sustain talk, and the counselor never reinforces reasons against change. Minor refinement: moments of fear of failure and 'the packet in my bag' receive somewhat extended airtime, where brief affirmations could pivot faster toward hope.
