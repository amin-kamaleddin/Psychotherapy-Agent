# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicated unconditional positive regard, refusing to judge Eric's minimization ('You're the judge of that, not me') and validating his boundaries. Eric's closing remark about 'not piling on' confirms he felt fully accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both the ambivalence ('something that's just yours') and subtle language shifts ('curious,' 'maybe'). The counselor also reframed Eric's fear of failure effectively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained consistent focus on smoking, advancing steadily from ambivalence exploration to a defined experiment with rules, success markers, and a start date. Every reflection and question tied back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was fully co-constructed from Eric's own ideas, with the counselor asking rather than telling and attributing outcomes to the client ('Either way, you get an answer'). No unsolicited advice or expert-driven content appeared.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ownership was reinforced throughout ('your experiment, your rules, your timing'), with no directives or pressure past Eric's stated boundaries. Eric explicitly noted the session felt different from 'the usual nagging' because it was on his terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within rather than imposed, eliciting Eric's personal stakes and developing them into a self-generated commitment with his own success criteria. Eric's 'guess I talked myself into something' is the hallmark of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Eric was positioned as the expert, and the entire experiment—rules, timing, and decision to delay telling his wife—originated from him with the counselor reflecting and amplifying. The closing summary attributed full ownership to the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions shifted motivation from external (wife's pressure) to internal (father's history, stair climbing), and the counselor highlighted and consolidated Eric's own commitment language. The concrete plan ('when does your week start?') emerged directly from the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently neutralized and redirected rather than argued with ('You're the judge of that'), and boundaries like 'this isn't quitting' were accepted. This paradoxically loosened resistance, leading to 'maybe I extend it.'
