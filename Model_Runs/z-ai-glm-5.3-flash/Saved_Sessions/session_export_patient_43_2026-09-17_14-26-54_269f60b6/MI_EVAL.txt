# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist was respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, validating Eric's pushback about nagging. Warmth was somewhat procedural, with formulaic crisis screening and limited explicit affirmation of his honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, capturing Eric's feelings of being pressured. Deeper emotional content (smoking as stress relief, the cough, wanting ownership of change) received logistics and advice rather than reflective responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed broadly on smoking and converged on a concrete target (two-week log and check-in), but drifted substantially into marital conflict management. Key target moments, like the winter cough, were not pursued.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Eric's preferences were invited and his contributions (own script, check-in idea) shaped the plan. However, the counselor often delivered pre-packaged scripts, log templates, and tips rather than co-creating them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered menus of options, framed the log as giving Eric 'control and facts, not nagging,' and accepted his rejections without pressure. Solution-providing occasionally crowded out space for Eric to generate his own ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely elicited Eric's own motivations, values, or confidence; no rulers or evocative questions were used. Progress came through behavioral contracting rather than drawing motivation from within the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** No lecturing and choices were offered, but the middle of the session was notably expert-driven with scripts, log formats, and delivery tips supplied unprompted. Eric's own ideas were only lightly incorporated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Eric volunteered meaningful change talk ('two weeks to actually think,' 'especially if they're ugly'), partly enabled by the plan-setting. These moments were not deliberately evoked or deepened when they occurred.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Eric produced substantial sustain talk ('I feel fine,' 'cigarettes are how I unwind,' 'I'm not saying I'm quitting') that was largely met with more options and advice rather than strategic reflection. Key openings, like the winter cough and 'I'd actually have to think about it, huh?', were missed.
