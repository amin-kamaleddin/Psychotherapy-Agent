# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicated respect and unconditional positive regard — adjusted pace when Lucía pushed back and used affirmations that drew explicit credit from her ('first time a doctor-type person hasn't just repeated the same scary speech'). Kept from 5 due to some formulaic praise ('Nice work, Lucía') and an initial four-question barrage that ignored her limited engagement style.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured Lucía's defensiveness, ambivalence, and her daughter's influence throughout. Reflections were sometimes checklist-style summaries rather than deeper emotional exploration — the meaning of her daughter's disapproval and the failed cold-turkey attempt went unexamined.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on smoking and craving management at work, progressing logically to a concrete experiment and defined follow-up, with off-topic comments gently redirected. The opening four-question barrage and repeated over-recapping of the plan scattered focus and added drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lucía vetoed options, redesigned plan parameters, and set the follow-up format, with the counselor accommodating each adjustment and Lucía ending with ownership ('Deal's a deal'). Collaboration was reactive rather than generative — pre-built menus were presented for her to accept, modify, or reject instead of true co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly and consistently reinforced Lucía's choice and ownership ('I'm here to support your choice,' 'Your call — no judgment'), fully honoring her scope, timeline, and no-lectures boundary. Lucía set the conditions of the plan and the counselor deferred to all of them without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was drawn from Lucía (daughter's disapproval, not finishing packs, tentative self-efficacy), but most strategies originated from the counselor rather than from her. Key opportunities were missed — no exploration of what quitting would mean, no confidence scaling, no elaboration of her prior two-week quit or spontaneous self-efficacy statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mixed stance — offered choices ('Which of these feels doable?', 'Your call') but frequently slipped into an expert role, reciting option lists and prescribing plan details rather than drawing strategies from Lucía. The intervention's architecture remained counselor-driven, limiting genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evoked some change talk through the options menu and captured spontaneous statements ('I don't smoke the whole pack some days'), but repeatedly failed to deepen them — glossing over that key self-efficacy moment with a generic response. No confidence exploration, no eliciting how success would feel, and the counselor often supplied plans instead of evoking them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rolled with resistance effectively — accepted Lucía's vetoes neutrally, pivoted to her priorities, and framed the plan as a 'tiny, time-limited try, not a program' with no judgment on failure. Occasionally restated sustain talk in summaries without a strategic double-sided reframe tying it back to her own change statements.
