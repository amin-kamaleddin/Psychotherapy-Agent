# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, nonjudgmental throughout, including when the patient was defensive about his weekends. Affirmations were genuine and individualized, with unconditional regard maintained even when anticipating imperfect outcomes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately captured both sides of the patient's ambivalence and the emotional nuance beneath surface statements. Reflections consistently landed, as shown by the patient progressively opening up about his uncle, future children, and never-before-spoken motivations.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on alcohol use while guiding a well-sequenced arc from ambivalence to a concrete plan and follow-up. No topic drift occurred, and the session ended with a firm commitment point ('Deal. One month').

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative between equals — decisions were framed jointly, and the plan, follow-up, and written number all came from the patient. The clinician positioned himself as a companion in the experiment rather than an authority, which the patient explicitly recognized.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was reinforced constantly ('Nothing needs to be decided today,' 'a month is an experiment, not a contract'), with ownership of outcomes returned to the patient. The patient designed his own plan and explicitly noted it 'was always going to be my call.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivational content — the uncle's death, wanting children, fogginess, weight, fairness to himself — came from the patient, and the clinician named this accurately ('I didn't put those there'). The patient's shift from braced-for-argument to committed experimenter demonstrates evocation at the highest level.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power sharing was exemplary: the patient's expertise was repeatedly foregrounded, and the entire plan was client-generated. The guideline number was written down so 'it's the guideline talking at the bar, not me' — deliberately de-centering the clinician's authority.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The standout strength: strategically sequenced evocative questions drew out the patient's deepest motivations ('What are you hoping to protect?') and his self-generated plan. Change talk clearly increased across the session, culminating in a concrete commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally acknowledged briefly and pivoted toward change, with effective double-sided reflections. However, some sustain talk received generous airtime early on — e.g., warmly validating 34 years of feeling healthy — and early responses to 'would it even matter health-wise?' did not directly redirect.
