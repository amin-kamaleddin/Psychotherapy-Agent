# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with affirmations like 'I'm impressed' and acknowledgment of quitting smoking. However, affirmations remain somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized to Daniel's emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture Daniel's ambivalence well, such as the summary about weekend drinking giving him pause. Reflections stay mostly practical and miss deeper emotional layers like the shame of 'explaining himself' to his wife.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on the target behavior with progressive movement toward a concrete Saturday plan. Slight deduction for offering broad option lists and add-ons that diluted strategic depth near the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Joint framing throughout ('let's map a recent night together') with the plan built substantially from Daniel's own ideas. Frequent pre-formatted option menus position the counselor more as idea supplier than fully equal partner.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent autonomy support: permission-seeking, repeated choices, and no pressure toward abstinence despite Daniel's fear of being told to quit entirely. Daniel's comment 'no lecture, just actual stuff I can do' confirms genuine choice and ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good evocation via open questions, the confidence ruler, and rehearsal, with Daniel's confidence rising from 7 to 8. Relied mainly on choice questions; deeper exploration of values (being present for his child, pride in quitting smoking) could have anchored motivation further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniel positioned as the expert with solutions genuinely originating from him (his wife, the self-text, sparkling water). Fell short of 5 because the counselor retained agenda control through option lists and question-driven pacing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively elicited Daniel's own solutions and commitment ('I think I can actually pull this off'), but did not systematically deepen change talk when it emerged, e.g., moving on after Daniel said he felt like an 8 rather than exploring what sustained that confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce Daniel's mild sustain talk and appropriately pivoted to his stated goals. Missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections to explicitly resolve ambivalence like 'just me overthinking it.'
