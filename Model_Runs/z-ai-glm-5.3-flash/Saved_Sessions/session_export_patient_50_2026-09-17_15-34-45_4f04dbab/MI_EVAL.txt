# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations ('That plan sounds brave and realistic') and explicit pushback on self-criticism ('You're not pathetic'). However, validation remains somewhat formulaic and does not deeply address the emotional weight of years of avoidance and fear of embarrassment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects the client's ambivalence ('a real tug-of-war') and tracks her experience well. Reflections summarize content more than deeper emotional nuance like shame around others' questions and the toll of repeated cancellations.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays tightly focused on starting to study, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete plan, confidence rating, and check-in. Falls short of 5 because direction relies on task-focused prompts rather than linkage to deeper values and the emotional layer of avoidance.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely joint: options rather than prescriptions, permission-seeking before advising, and client input materially shaped the plan. Not a 5 because the initial strategy menu was counselor-generated rather than drawn from the client's own coping experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces client ownership ('You're in charge of the pace and topics,' letting her pick time, page, reward, and check-in content). The coping phrase 'Five minutes — then I decide' actively builds her sense of choice, with no pressure or directive language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence ruler and open questions drew out client reasons and hope, but the counselor often supplied tips and scripts rather than evoking the client's own solutions. Her past success story was surfaced by her, not exploited as a springboard for deeper evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions the client as expert, and her choices shaped the plan (5-minute rule, page 12, sticky note). Falls short because the counselor tends to lead with strategy offerings rather than fully drawing solutions from the client first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Small-step questioning and the confidence ruler successfully evoked change talk ('I can do five minutes,' 'me making the first step'), but the counselor did not systematically deepen or amplify it, missing chances to elaborate on her past success and anticipated relief.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mostly avoids reinforcing sustain talk and responds to fears with acceptance and smaller alternatives. However, barrier-focused questions keep focus on doubt, and few double-sided reflections reframe failure and embarrassment statements toward change language.
