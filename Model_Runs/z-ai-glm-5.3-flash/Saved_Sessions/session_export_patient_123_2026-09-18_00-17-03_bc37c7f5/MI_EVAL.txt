# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with normalization ('that's not weird at all') and appreciation for her honesty. Slightly formulaic at times, but consistently respectful and supportive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections including her insightful self-observation about not judging herself. Reflections stayed surface-level and never touched the referral issue (alcohol use), limiting depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The stated target behavior—alcohol screening follow-up—was never addressed; the session wandered through hobbies and friendships. Pleasant rapport-building with no movement toward the clinical focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Curious, side-by-side tone with her answers shaping each next turn. Limited by withholding the referral purpose, which prevented true shared agenda-setting.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent affirmation of choice, open-ended questions, and acceptance of her 'nothing needs to change' answer without pressure. Would be higher if connected to the actual health decision at hand.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuinely evoked Sofia's values, strengths, and desires (ease, trust, reading). However, no motivation was elicited around the reason for referral, so evocation missed the target domain entirely.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong assumption that expertise lies with Sofia ('you know your week better than anyone'), with her contributions shaping the session. Not a 5 because the referral agenda was withheld rather than shared.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Elicited and reinforced some desire/ability talk about reading and slowing down. No change talk was evoked regarding alcohol, the actual target behavior.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Her mild sustain talk ('things are pretty alright') was not amplified; the clinician effectively pivoted to what she might want different. Since the target behavior was never introduced, this was neutral rather than strategic.
