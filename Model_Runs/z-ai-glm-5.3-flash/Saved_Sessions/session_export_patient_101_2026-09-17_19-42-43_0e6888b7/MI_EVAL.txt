# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 — ☑
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgmental stance; no lecturing or moralizing about drinking. Strong affirmations ("That's important self-knowledge," "That takes wisdom"). Warmth occasionally felt slightly formulaic; normalization of relapse and family-of-origin pain was only briefly addressed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 — ☑
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with meaning-level reflections ("your sober self leaving a message for drunk you"; "scared and doing nothing is worse than scared and trying something"). Limited deeper exploration of family-of-origin pain, though acknowledged respectfully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5 — ☑
**Comments:** Session stayed anchored to alcohol use and contraception reliability with no drift; each turn built progressively toward concrete next steps. Occasionally narrow—could have invited broader exploration of the "boring one" social fear.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 — ☑
**Comments:** Strong joint framing ("we'll look at them together," "everything here did come from you"). Answered the patient's direct question about tracking honestly and transparently. Power-sharing remained somewhat implicit rather than explicitly naming the client as expert.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 — ☑
**Comments:** Consistent ownership language ("Your call entirely," "you don't have to fix everything today"). Allowed the patient to select the pill-first strategy; never pressured or prescribed abstinence. At times felt like leading questions rather than open exploration.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5 — ☑
**Comments:** All concrete strategies (pill before leaving, notes-app tally, brunch instead of bars, counting drinks) originated from the patient. Excellent use of open questions to elicit her own solutions; direct honest answer when asked was appropriate.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 — ☑
**Comments:** Positioned Mei as the expert ("You know yourself best here"); did not impose a plan and explicitly credited her for it. "We'll look at them together" reinforced shared ownership of follow-through.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 — ☑
**Comments:** Good evocation and reinforcement—amplified "protecting your future self" and closed on her philosophy of change ("scared and doing nothing is worse than scared and trying something"). Missing: confidence ruler re-rate, scaling question, deeper "why now" exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5 — ☑
**Comments:** Did not dwell on doubt ("I'd probably fail at the limit thing"); appropriately pivoted to workable steps and values without challenging resistance. Mild risk of reinforcing sustain talk via "you don't have to fix everything today," though framed as permission.

---

### Overall Global Ratings Score: 4.1/5

**Summary:** Strong MI skills across nearly all domains. Key strengths: clear direction toward dual targets (alcohol and contraception), consistent evocation of the patient's own solutions, and skillful change talk reinforcement. Areas for growth: deepen exploration of family history emotional weight, more explicitly name the client as the expert on her own life, and consider a confidence re-rate at session end. Excellent concrete, measurable follow-through (pill before going out, notes-app tally, "receipts" at next visit).
