# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and never shamed the client even as he minimized his drinking. However, warmth was somewhat businesslike, with bundled questions early on and few genuine affirmations of the client's honesty or engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured surface content but stayed at a content-summary level rather than reflecting emotion. Key emotional moments—the weight of the drunk-driving realization and fear of friend pressure—were acknowledged practically but never emotionally validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept clear focus on weekend heavy drinking throughout, advancing steadily from rulers to a specific limit, concrete strategies, personalized BAC data, and a safety plan. The only drift, into drunk driving, remained directly tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client genuinely shaped the plan (cash-only, Tomas, leaving the car home), but the counselor often slid into expert mode with pre-loaded options and numbered strategy lists. The client's 'slow down' pushback signaled the counselor moved faster than the partnership allowed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice, respected refusals without pressure, and framed the plan as a client-controlled experiment. Autonomy support was slightly undercut by the density of suggested strategies and scripts, which felt somewhat prescriptive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from the client via open questions and skillful on-request information exchange that visibly moved confidence from 6 to 8. Missed chances to deepen the peak change talk ('maybe four is just how it is now') with reflective summaries.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's contributions shaped the plan, but the session often resembled expert consultation with counselor-generated lists and scripted lines. The counselor never explicitly named the client as the expert on his own social world.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used rulers and iterative confidence questions to evoke and build change talk, tracking confidence upward across the session. Fell short by not amplifying the client's strongest change talk or eliciting explicit commitment language to consolidate it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk, briefly acknowledging and redirecting it. However, missed opportunities for active softening—no double-sided reflections were used, and sustain talk was avoided more than skillfully transformed.
