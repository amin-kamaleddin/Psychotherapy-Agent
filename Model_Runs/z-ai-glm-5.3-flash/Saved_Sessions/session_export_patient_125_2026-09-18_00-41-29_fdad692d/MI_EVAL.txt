# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed the patient's strengths, values, and autonomy, emphasizing that the plan came from him ('that's exactly smart,' 'all of that came from you'). Acceptance was unconditional, offering follow-up support regardless of outcome.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly reflected the patient's core tensions (unfairness vs. caring about his record, anger vs. family commitment) and validated feelings without judgment. Reflections like 'the call is just the bonus' captured meaning, not just words.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully guided the conversation from complaint about the officer toward concrete behavioral planning, connecting the patient's stated value (family) to achievable steps. Open questions consistently steered toward change talk and solutions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The entire plan originated from the patient, with the psychiatrist eliciting ideas ('what do you think might be worth trying?') rather than prescribing. The psychiatrist repeatedly positioned the patient as the expert ('you know him and this place better than I do').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient chose the goal, the coping strategy (picturing his mom), the script, and the timing. The psychiatrist reinforced self-directedness throughout and acknowledged limits of control ('the other four? That's just him').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew out rich change talk: the patient's desire to 'walk out clean,' his mom's pride, and self-motivational statements like 'I can't be the reason for that.' Open questions about values and future vision evoked deep, personally meaningful motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue was fully two-sided, with the patient generating solutions while the psychiatrist asked rather than advised. The closing offered mutual ongoing partnership ('come back whether it goes good or bad').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Each response selectively reflected and reinforced the patient's change talk ('I want to walk out of here clean,' 'I got this') while rolling with sustain talk about the unfair officer. The confidence scaling question elicited further commitment ('my mom's face — that's never failed me yet').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist acknowledged and normalized the sustain talk (unfairness, urge to snap) without arguing, then reflected the change-oriented side of each ambivalence ('part of you is discouraged... part of you remembers your mom's pride'). This Darnell-style double-sided reflection kept sustain talk from dominating.
