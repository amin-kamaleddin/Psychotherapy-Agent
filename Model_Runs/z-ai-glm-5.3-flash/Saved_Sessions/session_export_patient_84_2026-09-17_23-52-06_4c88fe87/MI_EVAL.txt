# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveys respect and warmth, validating Malik's irritation without judgment and avoiding any labeling or moralizing about cannabis. Falls short of 5 only because warmth occasionally feels strategic and ambivalence/peer loyalty aren't explicitly normalized as legitimate values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing both content and undertone (resentment, loss, social fear). Malik's elaboration and within-session insight shift ('I never really thought of it like a cost') provide strong behavioral evidence of accurate empathy.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on cannabis use and its relational consequences despite a deflecting client, with each exchange purposefully advancing the arc from minimization to a concrete first step. The session never wanders and establishes an explicit, client-owned agenda early.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician operates as a curious partner, and every actionable idea (keeping substances out of the house, mirroring Dario, passing once) originated from Malik. The session feels like two equals problem-solving, with Malik's ideas driving direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change is consistently framed as Malik's choice ('Not hers — yours,' 'taking charge of this, on your own terms'), and his refusal to commit is explicitly accepted. His closing language ('testing it out') shows ownership was fully preserved.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited almost entirely from within via strategic open questions, moving Malik from 'I don't think it's a problem' to 'I miss how we used to be' and a self-generated experiment. This is near-exemplary MI evocation with a dramatic within-session motivation shift.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly locates expertise in Malik ('you're the expert on your life') and never advises, diagnoses, or prescribes. The plan comes entirely from Malik, with the Dario insight originating from him rather than the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation of change talk from a precontemplative client, with Malik progressing from 'maybe things would be easier at home' to a concrete commitment ('I could actually try that this weekend'). The Dario comparison strategy is masterful, letting Malik talk himself into change, and the discrepancy between peer belonging and losing his mom is textbook.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimizations are never reinforced or argued against; double-sided reflections hold both sides while tilting toward change. Not quite a 5 because early questions briefly invited more minimization and a few reflections paraphrased sustain talk before pivoting.
