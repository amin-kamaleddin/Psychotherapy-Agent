# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated respect and nonjudgment toward Maria despite her skepticism and defensiveness. Deduction: affirmations were formulaic, with 'Thanks, Maria' repeated nearly verbatim four times and little explicit normalization of her distrust given her grandmother's experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and captured Maria's core concerns about bone health, safety, and autonomy. Deduction: reflections were somewhat formulaic and could have probed deeper emotional nuance, such as her frightening nights or fear of her family hearing her wheezing.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on asthma management and moved efficiently from exploration to a concrete, achievable plan (7-day log, PCP questions, follow-up on the 15th). Deduction: the closing third became repetitive, restating safety plans and re-offering declined materials, diluting directional momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor used collaborative framing throughout and co-constructed a plan Maria shaped herself (log, phone format, appointment time). Deduction: the steroid pharmacology information was delivered didactically, and repeated re-offers of materials after Maria declined them suggested uneven power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was consistently and explicitly strong: 'facts, not pressure,' framing the log as 'just data' rather than a commitment, and respecting her refusal of the script. No coercive or pressuring language appeared at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew motivation from Maria's own values, which paid off when she spontaneously connected change to family and to gathering her own facts. Deduction: these evoked motivations were not systematically built on — no elaboration questions, confidence exploration, or targeted reflection on her strongest motivation statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise was framed as shared, genuine choices were offered, and Maria's contributions visibly shaped the session. Deduction: the counselor retained an educative expert stance during psychoeducation and repeatedly re-offered materials Maria had already declined.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open-ended questions successfully elicited genuine change talk, including her frightening nights, willingness to keep the log, and family-centered motivation. However, the counselor did not consistently deepen or reinforce it — no reflection of commitment language, no confidence or importance scaling, and her strongest change talk was met with generic summaries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally avoided reinforcing Maria's substantial sustain talk, using neutral, validating reflections and pivoting to balanced information and small actionable steps. Deduction: repeated restatements of safety concerns at session close re-centered the problem rather than sustaining momentum toward change.
