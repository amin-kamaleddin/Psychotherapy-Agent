# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated respect and non-judgment, validating smoking as her coping mechanism and meeting her where she was. However, affirmations were sometimes formulaic and lacked deep affirmation of her strengths as a mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured her exhaustion, ambivalence, and parenting concerns. But reflections were brief and the counselor missed chances to deepen emotional exploration around her fear of 'failing as a mum' and her shame about the doctor.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong focus on smoking and her son throughout, with a logical progression to a concrete micro-trial and review plan. Focus narrowed prematurely to harm-reduction, deferring deeper ambivalence about quitting without a clear bridge.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Inclusive language ('we'll decide together') and incorporation of her feedback (no nagging, no tracking) into the plan. However, collaboration was limited by the menu-driven approach—her expertise shaped selection, not idea generation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's strongest element: explicit, repeated reinforcement of choice ('I won't nag — I'll follow your lead') with her limits fully honored and no coercive language at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The options menu and trial format drew out some change talk, but the counselor relied on supplying strategies and scripts rather than asking open questions to evoke her intrinsic motivations. Her powerful admission that 'part of me already knows what they'd say is true' was passed over.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client chose from offered options, but the framework was largely counselor-generated—option lists, scripted doctor lines, and a pre-formed plan. Her own ideas were rarely drawn out beyond picking from the counselor's menu.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively created space for change talk through the options menu and small trial, with meaningful client statements ('I can do a small thing') and reinforcement ('notice any small wins'). Missed opportunities to deepen the change talk when it emerged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally without reinforcement, but double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with her change talk were not used. Her ambivalence was never explicitly leveraged to develop discrepancy.
