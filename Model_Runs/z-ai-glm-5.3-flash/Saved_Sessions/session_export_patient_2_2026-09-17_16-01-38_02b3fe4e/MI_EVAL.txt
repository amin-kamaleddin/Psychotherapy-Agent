# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, explicitly honoring Aisha's boundaries ("We can keep this as a simple checkup," "I won't bring up drinking unless you want to") and normalizing her smoking without disapproval. Warmth was present but somewhat formulaic, with limited individualized affirmations of her strengths beyond repeated references to caring about her son.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured her situation and the personal meaning of her smoking ("evenings are your time to unwind — that makes total sense") and validated her cut-back framing. However, reflections stayed surface-level and did not explore the emotional weight of her health fears as a mother or her defensiveness about drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening drifted with broad screening questions Aisha rejected, and the alcohol topic was dropped entirely without exploration or follow-up. Once smoking emerged, focus was solid, ending with a concrete plan and scheduled follow-up, but overall direction was inconsistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were offered at key decision points (tip now vs. later, timer vs. gum, follow-up timing), but the change plan was largely prescribed by the counselor rather than co-created. Aisha contributed only the tea idea, and the screening portion felt checklist-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly and consistently supported: explicit choice at every step, "Whatever you choose is fine," honoring her refusal to discuss drinking, and letting her set the follow-up time. Her "no promises" statements were accepted without any pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler effectively elicited Aisha's barriers and facilitators, and her core motivation (her son, health fears) emerged on its own. However, the counselor relied heavily on advice-giving rather than evoking and deepening her own reasons and solutions for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked for Aisha's input and offered options, but the session was advice-heavy, with the unwind routine, structured plan, and gum instructions all counselor-directed. Aisha was not explicitly positioned as the expert on her own life or asked what she had already tried.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was evoked and reinforced ("maybe three instead of five or six," her motherhood motivation), and the confidence ruler was a good technique. Much of the session, however, was counselor-driven advice rather than deepening her reasons or exploring her ambivalence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk (peer comparisons, drinking minimization) was acknowledged neutrally without reinforcement, and the counselor avoided arguing. But there were no double-sided reflections, reframes, or strategic pivots back to her stated motivations — sustain talk was mostly sidestepped rather than actively worked with.
