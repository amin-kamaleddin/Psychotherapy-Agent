# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains basic respect and neutrality ("AUDIT-C isn't a judgment, just a flag"), but warmth is thin and formulaic. The rupture when Thomas says "you seem set on finding something wrong with me" is never directly repaired with genuine unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal — responses are mostly checklist questions and advice rather than reflections. Key openings like "my wife might disagree" and feelings of being judged are never explored with complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains focus on the referral concerns (smoking and alcohol) with logical progression, but the direction is that of a medical screen rather than motivational focus. No exploration of values (family, "not 22 anymore") to anchor change, and no collaborative closing summary or next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Largely counselor-led with symptom batteries, score calculations, and pre-packaged tips. Thomas's own ideas (Dave's water trick, the money rationale) are never woven into a shared plan, and the power imbalance is explicitly named by the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor's strongest domain — options are repeatedly offered without pressure, and Thomas's rejections are accepted. However, autonomy support is undercut by uninvited prescriptive tip lists and failure to affirm Thomas's ownership of his own proposed changes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is informed into the client rather than elicited — no scaling questions or exploration of Thomas's own reasons. The only change talk emerged spontaneously, not through skillful evocation of values or concerns he himself raised.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor occupies the expert role throughout — checklists, scoring, and prescriptive strategies. Thomas's rejection of suggestions is met with more counselor-generated scripts rather than building on his own ideas, and the client explicitly experienced the dynamic as fault-finding.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Thomas spontaneously offers genuine change talk ("dial it back a touch," "skip the first round," "I don't need to be legless to have a laugh"), but the counselor fails to capitalize — no affirmations, elaboration, or deepening reflections. Advice-giving keeps motivation counselor-supplied rather than client-evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Repeated minimizations ("it wasn't really that much," "my dad drank way more") are met with facts or tips rather than validation or reframing. At one point the counselor inadvertently invites sustain talk by validating downplaying without pivoting toward change.
