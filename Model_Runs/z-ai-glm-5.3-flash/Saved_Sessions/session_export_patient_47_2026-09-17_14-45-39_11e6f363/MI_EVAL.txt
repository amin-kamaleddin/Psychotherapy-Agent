# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates unconditional positive regard and warmth throughout, never judging Evelyn's minimization, and normalizes potential slips. Closing affirmations like 'You've got this' exemplify respectful, supportive counseling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective efforts are regular and mostly accurate, including a strong complex reflection capturing her ambivalence about the fall and her son's worry. Not a 5 because reflections often stay factual and miss the emotional layer beneath the fall and her health concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focus on alcohol reduction is maintained consistently from opening question to closing plan, with every turn advancing the agenda. Even tangents are tied back to safer drinking and fall prevention—exemplary direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element—Tuesday night, drink choice, script wording, reminder line—was chosen by Evelyn, with the counselor asking permission and offering tweaks. Her remark 'You didn't lecture or nag' is strong client-voiced evidence of a non-hierarchical relationship.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently affirmed ('I'm here to support your choices') and resistance to rigid plans is respected. Not a 5 because a few turns bundle multiple questions together and the counselor sometimes supplies option menus rather than letting Evelyn generate ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and rulers successfully draw out Evelyn's own reasons—the fall, her son's worry, valuing company—leading to her shift from skepticism to a plan. Not a 5 because evocation relies on structured tools rather than deeper exploration of personal values and the meaning of change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions Evelyn as the expert ('you're the expert on your life') and her contributions genuinely shape the session. Power sharing is active and effective.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is actively evoked via open questions, rulers, and plan prompts, producing genuine commitment language from Evelyn ('maybe I'll try a second night'). Not a 5 because the counselor sometimes moves quickly to planning rather than deepening elicited change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well with neutral and double-sided reflections, never argued with, and redirected toward her values. Not a 5 because redirection is occasionally passive—ambivalence between minimization and fall risk could have been addressed more directly.
