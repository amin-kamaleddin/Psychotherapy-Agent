# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, strength-based regard throughout, with specific tailored affirmations and no judgment of past failures or self-described embarrassment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured meaning, not just content (e.g., the calendar feeling 'official,' accountability as a promise to someone else). Fell short of a 5 only because emotional layers—like the vulnerability around asking for help—could have been explored one step deeper.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on re-establishing exercise, guiding through a natural planning arc from options to commitments; session ended with two concrete self-generated commitments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative—every solution originated from the patient, with the clinician only eliciting, reflecting, and offering options rather than directing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices offered rather than prescribed; patient preferences treated as valid parameters, with success criteria and ownership consistently left with her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation and answers systematically drawn from within the client—her reasons for change, confidence, and self-defined success metrics were all elicited and amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient was explicitly positioned as the expert on her own life, with every plan element (calendar block, backup route, accountability person) coming from her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked at every turn, with strong desire and commitment statements drawn out and strategically reinforced through reflection.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well—barriers were affirmed as problem-solving and pivoted into planning. Not a 5 because the barriers question slightly expanded space for barrier talk, and responses could have leaned harder on existing change talk.
