# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, repeatedly affirming Isabella's autonomy and honoring her humor-based coping style rather than pathologizing it. However, explicit affirmations of her personal strengths and values (e.g., her commitment to being a good mother) are limited, keeping acceptance solid but somewhat procedural.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture the surface content of Isabella's perspective, including her family tradition and autonomy concerns. However, the counselor misses emotional openings—such as her expressed nervousness about motherhood and the weight of the family-tradition conflict—pivoting instead to logistics and planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays consistently anchored to alcohol use during pregnancy, with every exchange circling back to the target behavior, readiness, and concrete change steps. A higher score would require tighter linkage between her values and the behavior change, with fewer instances of logistics displacing deeper exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative elements include asking permission, offering options, and following Isabella's lead on scheduling. However, the planning phase becomes increasingly prescriptive with counselor-generated lists of substitutes, apps, and schedules, tilting the power balance away from co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy support is strong, with explicit statements like 'This is your decision, not mine,' and Isabella is free to decline the role-play, texting, and other suggestions without pushback. The top score is prevented mainly because the counselor proposes a fairly detailed pre-formed plan rather than eliciting more of her own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor evokes meaningful client-generated motivation with future-oriented questions about the kind of mother she wants to be and where she sees herself. However, evocation is sporadic rather than sustained, with Isabella's motivation emerging more from her own values than from skillful counselor deepening.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Explicit collaborative overtures are made, including 'I will follow your lead here' and asking permission before informing. Yet the session increasingly becomes agenda-driven, with the counselor proposing most plan elements while Isabella mostly agrees, negotiates, or declines.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Future-oriented and importance questions successfully evoke change talk about the baby's health and finding substitutes. However, strong change statements are passed over quickly rather than strategically deepened, reflected, and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoids arguing with Isabella's substantial sustain talk about choice and family norms. However, there is little active softening—no double-sided reflections or reframes linking the family tradition to her priority of a healthy baby.
