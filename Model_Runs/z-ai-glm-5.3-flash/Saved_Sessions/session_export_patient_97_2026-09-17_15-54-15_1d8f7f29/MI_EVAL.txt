# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently nonjudgmental and respectful, treating Mateo's record and hesitation neutrally with affirmations like 'clear and steady.' Falls short of 5 because warmth is task-focused and moments of shame weren't deeply affirmed or normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately captured Mateo's ambivalence and shame around peer pressure and money. Reflections often pivoted quickly to offering options, leaving little room to sit with deeper feelings of loneliness and having 'nothing to show' for himself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear behavioral funnel from risk triggers to money leverage to a concrete rehearsed micro-goal (calling day labor). Direction sometimes came at the cost of depth, and the final emotional moment wasn't used to consolidate change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Agendas were negotiated at each step, and the role-play was genuinely joint with Mateo's feedback shaping the final script. The power balance remained uneven in idea generation, as nearly all solution content originated from the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Everything was framed as Mateo's choice, with menu-based options he could freely accept or decline. Option lists and scripts were counselor-generated, and the closing push toward action didn't explicitly reaffirm that declining was also acceptable.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good evocative questions drew out meaningful self-reflection, but most motivation-generating content (cash options, scripts) was supplied rather than drawn out. The powerful closing statement 'I guess I could. Weird saying that out loud' was met with silence rather than exploration of hope or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo's choices genuinely shaped the session through repeated offers of direction and options. The expertise balance tilted counselor-ward, with little acknowledgment of Mateo's own knowledge of his neighborhood and peers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client-generated change talk emerged ('doesn't line up with what I actually want long-term,' 'I guess I could'), but key moments went unreflected and unaffirmed. No scaling questions, commitment language, or deeper exploration of Mateo's reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk and doubt were met with neutral, normalizing reflections rather than amplification, then pivoted to actionable options. Some reflections briefly dwelt on pain and stuckness without double-sided reframes anchoring back to his long-term goals.
