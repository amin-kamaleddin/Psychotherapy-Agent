# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, consistently affirming Hana's efforts and avoiding any labeling or pressure despite her ambivalence and low initial importance rating. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and tend to praise the plan rather than the person, missing chances to affirm her deeper strengths like honesty and self-awareness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrates good reflective listening with complex reflections that accurately capture Hana's ambivalence between smoking's benefits and her worries. Room remains for deeper emotional reflection, such as exploring the 'nagging worry' underneath her concern or the social anxiety of not joining friends.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on cutting back social smoking from the first utterance through a logical arc from exploration to rulers to a concrete, client-owned experiment with follow-up. No topic drift occurs, and every exchange tightens toward actionable change while remaining client-paced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is largely a joint enterprise: the counselor asks permission, checks which options feel doable, and co-constructs the plan through back-and-forth, with Hana's contributions genuinely shaping the outcome. Rapid-fire option lists and a suggestion-heavy planning phase slightly reduce the equal-partner feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly supports choice by framing the plan as a low-pressure experiment, using the 'slip is info, not failure' reframe, and respecting Hana's pace without pushing toward full cessation. Could go further by more consistently emphasizing Hana's ownership of outcomes and relying less on counselor-supplied scripts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is largely drawn from the client, with rulers eliciting her own ratings and reasoning, and her confidence rising from 5 to 7 through her own articulation rather than persuasion. The counselor sometimes fills space with solution menus instead of evoking Hana's own ideas and does not explore her values or what long-term success would mean to her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission appropriately, but repeatedly supplies pre-packaged solution lists and drafts text messages and reminder labels for Hana, subtly shifting expertise toward the clinician. While Hana adopts and adapts these ideas, the balance tips toward advice-giving rather than consistently drawing plans out of the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk using importance and confidence rulers, open questions, and a concrete experiment, with Hana's confidence visibly progressing from 5 to 7 and her own language ('Thursday's a start') showing momentum. Points are withheld for occasionally answering for the client by supplying options rapidly rather than letting her generate her own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged with neutral, non-reinforcing reflections and pivoted toward change, while the 'slip is info, not failure' reframe effectively defuses relapse fears. Double-sided reflections are not always used, and sustain talk about social belonging could have been softened more explicitly by connecting the walk plan to the same social need.
