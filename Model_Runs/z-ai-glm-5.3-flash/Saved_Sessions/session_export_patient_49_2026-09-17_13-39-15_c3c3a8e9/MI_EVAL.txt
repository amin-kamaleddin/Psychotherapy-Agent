# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; normalizes defensiveness and slips effectively, and the patient noted it 'felt less like a lecture.' Affirmations were somewhat generic and lacked deeper individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some factual reflections were made, but emotional content was often missed — e.g., the social identity of smoking, worry about undoing dental care, and hopeful curiosity. Reflections were sparse and quickly gave way to instructions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus throughout with a logical progression from exploration to a concrete one-week experiment, specific start time, alternative behavior, and scheduled follow-up. Every response served the change goal with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine partnership in practical decisions and co-constructing the pre-filled entry, but the counselor dominated the advisory content (templates, scripts, tips) and set the session's pace.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offered menus and choices, and accepted the patient's limits without pushing. However, heavy prescribed instruction tipped toward advising rather than eliciting her own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied primarily on information and structure rather than drawing out motivation. Key change-talk moments (e.g., 'saying that out loud is a bit interesting') were met with logistics instead of deepening questions about values, importance, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices and asked for input, but the second half was largely counselor-driven with scripted lines and templates. Most strategies were supplied by the clinician rather than drawn from the patient's expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler and menu of options evoked some change talk ('doesn't seem too scary,' 'might be doable'). Missed opportunities to deepen, such as following up on her insight that smoking is habit rather than desire; no commitment language elicited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing against sustain talk and channeled it into a low-pressure curiosity-framed experiment; pre-normalized defensiveness and slips. Didn't use strategic double-sided reflections to tilt ambivalence toward change, and sometimes implicitly endorsed the status quo.
