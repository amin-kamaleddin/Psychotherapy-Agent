# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental with a defensive teen, validating pushback and respecting privacy. However, repeated scaling questions despite visible irritation prioritized protocol over attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected Marcus's peer-oriented worldview and soccer values, and acknowledged his boundaries. Reflections were occasionally brief and formulaic, missing deeper exploration of self-image concerns.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on concrete harm-reduction steps, culminating in a specific achievable plan. Deductions for the four-question opening that overwhelmed Marcus and looping confidence questions late in the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely a two-way exchange with a genuinely co-constructed, client-generated plan. At times felt checklist-like and protocol-driven, with the counselor answering Marcus's own questions for him.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: explicit choice statements, privacy assurances, no pressure toward abstinence, and client-controlled pacing throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Marcus himself — his own reasons (soccer, not being 'that guy') and strategies, moving from denial to a 7-8 confidence plan. Relied too heavily on repetitive ruler questions and occasionally pitched solutions early.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Marcus as the expert, asking permission and offering menus he selected from. Occasional quick slips into counselor-suggested solutions before fully drawing out client ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong elicitation of change talk via rulers — Marcus voiced 'garbage at Monday practice,' 'don't wanna be that guy,' and hope to report success. Questions became mechanical later, and self-image reasons went unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk well by acknowledging without reinforcing and pivoting to Marcus's endorsed values, never arguing with minimization. The brain-fact opener invited defensiveness and sustain talk was sometimes re-summarized verbatim, giving it extra airtime.
