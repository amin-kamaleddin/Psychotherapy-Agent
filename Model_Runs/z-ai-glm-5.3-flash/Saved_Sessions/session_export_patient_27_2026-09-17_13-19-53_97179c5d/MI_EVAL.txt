# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a consistently warm, nonjudgmental tone with no shaming of Daniela's secrecy or hesitation, and validates her nerves as normal. Falls short of 5 because warmth becomes formulaic — the near-verbatim safety check at every turn dilutes genuine regard, and praise is generic rather than specific.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects her core ambivalence ('keeping it private can feel safer but lonely,' 'that secret sounds heavy') and offers in-the-moment grounding for her anxiety. Misses chances for deeper complex reflections on rich emotional material ('tired of making excuses,' the 'good flip'), instead pivoting quickly to options and drafts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains exceptional focus on the target behavior — disclosing diabetes to peers — with every response tied to the goal and no topic drift. Session progresses logically from exploration through drafting and rehearsal to a concrete, time-bound plan (tell Maya on Friday).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session is genuinely co-constructed: Daniela rewrites drafts in her own adolescent voice, the role-play is reciprocal, and the final plan emerges from her own suggestions. Each step is negotiated and her ideas substantively shaped the outcome, meeting the highest standard of MI collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly and repeatedly honors her choice ('writing doesn't commit you to sending,' 'saving it is fine') with no pressure or coercion. Falls short of 5 because persistent option menus subtly steer toward action, and explicit affirmation of her ownership ('you know your friends best') is limited at moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates conditions for Daniela's own motivation to emerge — rehearsal let her discover 'it didn't come out all shaky,' and she set her own timeline ('this week. For real.'). Some strong motivational material came from counselor structure (drafts, menus) rather than skillful evocative questions about her own reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently treats Daniela as the expert, and her choices (Maya, the shorter message, requesting rehearsal) visibly shaped the session's direction. Option menus are somewhat prescriptive, and her 'Is that okay?' permission-seeking suggests power-sharing could be more fully embodied.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively moves Daniela from 'I don't even know if I want to tell anyone' to commitment language ('this week. For real.') through open questions and skillful behavioral rehearsal in which she generated her own words. Early option offers pre-empted some of her own reasons for change, and her confidence or values were rarely explicitly evoked before jumping to drafting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Never argues against or reinforces her hesitations ('I'm not saying I'll send it today,' 'don't get too excited'), which is a strength. However, sustain talk is met with acceptance followed by rapid pivots to options rather than strategic softening — no double-sided reflections pairing her fears with her stated desires to actively resolve ambivalence.
