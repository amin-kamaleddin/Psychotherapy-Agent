# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, explicitly disconfirming the client's expectation of judgment ('There's no telling-off here'). Affirms hedging and honesty without labeling avoidance as resistance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frequent, accurate, complex reflections capture both sides of ambivalence (e.g., 'one visit feels doable... but doing it again is where the worry creeps in'). Reflections name emotions as well as content and clearly land with the client each turn.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains unwavering focus on the target behavior while progressing logically from preferred activity to a concrete first step and follow-up plan. Redirects the 'every time' worry without losing momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine joint inquiry with all substantive ideas originating from the client and no unsolicited advice. Held at 4 because collaboration is expressed mainly through questioning rather than explicit team language or transparent agenda negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Pervasive autonomy support: 'this step is entirely yours to take, at your pace,' with the client's right to limit commitment validated. Client directly confirms the effect: 'I needed someone to not push me.' No directives or pressure anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicits the client's own reasons, solutions, and motivation, crediting change talk as hers alone. Falls short of 5 because broader values and the fuller vision of her social life remain unexplored, and importance/confidence aren't assessed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise in the client, uses her own words to shape questions, and the action step emerged entirely from her, not counselor suggestion. Textbook power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strategic evocation including the well-timed smallest-step question that produced a concrete, client-owned plan, with change talk explicitly named. Not a 5 because peak change talk ('proud of myself') isn't deepened and no commitment language (importance/confidence scaling) is sought.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently acknowledges sustain talk without amplifying it, using double-sided reflections and reframing avoidance as protective. Some sustain talk is neutrally reflected rather than deliberately minimized, and the second-visit worry receives a full question that slightly amplifies anxiety.
