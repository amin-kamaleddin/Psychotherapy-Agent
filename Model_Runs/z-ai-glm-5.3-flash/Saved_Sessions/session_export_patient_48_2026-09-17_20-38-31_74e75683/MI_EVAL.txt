# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates warm, unconditional positive regard throughout, normalizing ambivalence ('it's such a human thing') and validating resistance to rules without judgment. No disapproval or labeling appears anywhere in the exchange.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate, capturing emotional nuance and underlying values, e.g., 'You're knackered, not unwilling' and the two-sided conflict between the walk being his and internal nagging not being freedom. Reflections consistently go beyond surface content into deeper meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Focus on increasing physical activity never drifts, moving progressively from past successes to a concrete plan and a specific commitment ('starting this week'). Each turn advances momentum toward the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session is genuinely joint ('what would you want to figure out together?'), with Farid's ideas shaping the entire plan (train stop, trainers by door, mate Dev). He even revises the goal downward and the psychiatrist accepts and explores rather than corrects.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Ownership is consistently affirmed ('it's yours,' 'you know yourself best'), and Farid retains final say. However, when asked about rough days, the psychiatrist offered his own opinion ('lowering the bar might be exactly right') before fully returning the question to the client — a small lapse in pure autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently elicited from within ('what's the version of you that would actually do it?'), with Farid's own words carrying all change arguments. This culminates in strong self-efficacy and commitment language ('doable,' 'I'll give it a proper go, starting this week') — textbook evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Farid is consistently positioned as the expert ('You know your life and schedule better than anyone'), and his contributions drive the session direction. Questions are genuinely open and the plan content comes entirely from his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and amplified, including reflecting Farid's own language back ('doable,' 'no drama' — 'talking yourself toward it') and future-oriented questions. The session culminates in a concrete commitment with a start date.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally handled well with double-sided reflections and effective pivots. Minor inconsistency: one question ('What feels like the shakiest piece of that?') invited more focus on barriers rather than pivoting fully toward confidence and change.
