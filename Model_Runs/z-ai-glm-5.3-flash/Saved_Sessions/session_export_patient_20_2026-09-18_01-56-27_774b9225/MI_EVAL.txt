# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clinician consistently affirmed the patient's autonomy ('matters less than whether it works for you'), displayed unconditional positive regard during ambivalence disclosures, and demonstrated accurate empathy through repeated reflections. All four acceptance elements (absolute worth, affirmation, autonomy support, accurate empathy) are prominently featured throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both sides of the patient's ambivalence, including emotional undertones like fear of 'eating my words' and discomfort with self-monitoring. The clinician's statements like 'the problem isn't you, it's the setup' reflect deep understanding without judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintains clear focus on reducing alcohol consumption, with each exchange building incrementally toward a concrete, patient-generated plan (pouring two, backup drinks, tracking, telling sister). No drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently invites the patient's own solutions ('what do you think might work for you?') and positions the patient as the expert ('you've identified your own timing, your own accountability'). All ideas originate from the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly emphasizes patient choice and ownership ('whether your sister thinks it's enough matters less than whether it works for you'), avoids prescriptive advice, and reinforces self-directed decisions throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions skillfully draw out the patient's motivations, values, and change talk, eliciting powerful self-statements like 'five nights out of seven isn't a fail' and 'maybe it's worth the discomfort.' Change talk is amplified through selective reinforcement.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue has a natural back-and-forth rhythm with the clinician building on each patient response. The patient increasingly leads the problem-solving while the clinician serves as an active, engaged ally.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician strategically highlights and mirrors patient change statements ('I want to make sure you keep hearing yourself say that'), transforming doubt into commitment language. Sustain talk is acknowledged without amplification, and change talk progressively strengthens from tentative to specific planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every instance of sustain talk ('cold turkey feels like setting myself up to fail,' 'a bad week at work could blow this up') is met with reflective validation followed by gentle redirection toward what feels doable, never confrontation or minimization.
