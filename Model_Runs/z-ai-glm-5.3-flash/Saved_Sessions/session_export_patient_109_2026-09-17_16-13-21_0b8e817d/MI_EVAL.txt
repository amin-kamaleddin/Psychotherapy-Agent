# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful tone with frequent affirmations ('solid, realistic start'); Rafael felt 'not another thing I'm failing at,' though warmth was somewhat tied to plan mechanics and his fear of failure wasn't deeply normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured his ambivalence (workday fatigue, gym feeling out of reach). Emotional depth was underexplored — discouragement about past failures and reluctance to tell his wife were acknowledged but not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on returning to physical activity; the session progressed logically from ambivalence to a concrete plan, backup contingencies, and a scheduled check-in with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely equal partnership — plans were co-constructed from Rafael's preferences and negotiated check-in format. Occasionally stacked suggestions and questions in single turns, leading the agenda early on.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every decision was Rafael's — he chose the walk, the reminders, the backup, and declined help setting reminders without pushback. The counselor accepted his choices, including deferring telling his wife.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and confidence rulers drew out Rafael's own reasons and solutions ('I don't need it to be perfect — I need it to happen'). Occasionally supplied options before fully exhausting his own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rafael was positioned as the expert and his ideas substantially shaped the plan. The early dense menu of options somewhat led the agenda before exploring his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk via open questions and twice-used confidence rulers, tracking movement from 4-5 to 7. Rafael moved from sustain talk to concrete commitment ('That's a deal, I guess') using his own solutions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argued with sustain talk, validated honesty and redirected to doable options, successfully reframing away from gym failure. Pessimism about past patterns wasn't directly softened with double-sided reflections.
