# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard throughout, even amid defensiveness and sustain talk ('that's a sharp observation,' 'You get to explore this at whatever speed feels right'). No labeling or disapproval, and the respectful stance holds through closure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate complex and double-sided reflections capturing ambivalence well. One notable flaw: the counselor briefly spoke in first person as if they were the client ('Man, that's the hard part. The beers just keep coming...'), a role-reversal that muddied attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, sustained focus on alcohol use across every turn, from the opening evocative question to a concrete success definition and follow-up plan. No topic drift; the session progressively moved from ambivalence to a testable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine between-equals working: Marek generated the strategies (pacing, water, eating), defined success criteria, and set the experiment's terms. His closing comments suggest he experienced the relationship as honest and collaborative rather than hierarchical.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding, explicit autonomy support: 'no promises, no pressure... Your call, your pace,' and outcomes framed as Marek's to define. No coercive or persuasive language anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never lectured or supplied reasons for change; every motivation emerged from Marek via well-calibrated open questions, elaboration, and affirmation. Effectively moved him from defensiveness to a self-initiated weekend experiment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Marek as the authority ('You know yourself and your friendships better than anyone'). His own ideas became the basis of the plan — exemplary power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, strategic evocation matched to a contemplation-stage client, drawing out elaborated change talk ('maybe three, four beers instead of six... I'd call that a success') and a concrete experiment plan. Tentative commitment language was well affirmed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk and used effective double-sided reflections paired with change talk. Occasionally echoed sustain talk fairly fully ('you're not seeing major fallout right now') before pivoting, and did not always affirm the change side after ambivalent statements.
