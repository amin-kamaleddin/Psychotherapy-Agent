# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated consistent warmth and respect with no judgment, affirming the client's self-compassionate restart plan. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and brief rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were consistently accurate, capturing the client's goals and acknowledging hurdles like tiredness. Reflections remained mostly content-level, with limited exploration of emotional nuance beneath the client's statements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained exceptional focus on the target behavior of increasing family presence, moving cleanly from values to a concrete, cued, time-limited behavioral plan. No topic drift occurred at any point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The tone was friendly and options were offered rather than imposed, but intervention decisions largely originated with the counselor. The client selected from counselor-generated options rather than her expertise substantially reshaping the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed choice and the client retained full ownership of her plan. Autonomy support was slightly diluted by frequently supplying ready-made scripts and options rather than trusting the client to construct her own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of a confidence ruler and success indicators drew out strong intrinsic motivation about building family happiness 'a little at a time.' Evocation was inconsistent, as prescriptive suggestions sometimes shifted the dynamic from evoking to advising.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offering choices supported some power sharing, but the dominant pattern was the counselor generating options, scripts, and rules for the client to select from. True partnership would involve the client's own ideas substantially shaping the intervention.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was well structured to elicit the client's motivations via the confidence ruler, success indicators, and open outcome questions, producing strong change talk. Evocation was sometimes crowded out by the counselor supplying options and scripts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal given the client's readiness stage, and barriers like tiredness and potential husband hesitance were acknowledged neutrally without amplifying resistance. Barrier discussions were handled by offering fixes rather than drawing out the client's own strategies.
