# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful tone with affirmations and no judgment; however, praise is somewhat formulaic and lacks deeper, individualized affirmations of the client's character or perseverance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate simple reflections were offered early, but reflections largely functioned as summaries rather than deepening emotional understanding. Missed opportunities included exploring why presence feels diluted or the meaning behind the client's 'keeping the thread going' remark.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior from the opening question through concrete planning and closing follow-up. Every exchange advanced the goal of aligning daily habits with family happiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor checked in frequently and incorporated the client's contributions, but supplied the plan skeleton, activity menu, and scripts. Isolde adjusted the counselor's plan rather than co-creating one from her own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly invited the client's choice and accepted her tweaks, but the pre-generated menus and structured plan slightly constrained choice space. Explicit affirmations of her ownership were absent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited from within via confidence ratings, but motivation was more often assumed than evoked—the counselor told the client the plan honored her value rather than asking her to articulate the connection. Deeper evocative questions about hopes and meaning were absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative questions were asked, but the counselor frequently pre-generated options, plan structure, and even scripts, positioning himself as the plan architect. Little acknowledgment that the client's expertise about her own marriage drives the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence ratings elicited strong commitment-adjacent language ('I feel ready,' confidence 9), but there was little deliberate deepening through elaboration questions. Change talk emerged from planning prompts rather than skillful evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The only wobble (fear of late work nights) was handled well by eliciting the client's own solution, which she resolved herself. Low ambivalence meant little redirection was needed.
