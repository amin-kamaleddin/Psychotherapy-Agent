# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly positioning themselves as non-judgmental and normalizing the client's confusion and defensiveness. Respect stayed consistent even when the client dismissed the guidelines.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, capturing the client's social comparisons, information gaps, and ambivalence with complex double-sided reflections. The counselor deepened into emotional meaning, particularly around the father material.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear focus on alcohol use while strategically moving the client from defensiveness toward a concrete first step, with no aimless drift. Each exchange built toward the eventual commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was a genuine meeting of equals, with permission-seeking and a plan fully co-constructed from the client's own ideas. The client's resistance was treated as information rather than opposition.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively reinforced, with repeated statements that the client didn't have to change and explicit reframing of change as the client's own call. The client's own words ('my call, not some chart's') show this support landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within the client rather than supplied, with each question eliciting progressively stronger client-generated reasons and confidence. The counselor never argued for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located within the client, and the client's contributions substantially shaped the session, from his questions driving the psychoeducation to his own idea becoming the action step. Permission was consistently requested before informing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook evocation of change talk through open-ended questions that drew out the client's own reasons (his father's trajectory, health) and a specific commitment ('I'd try it. One weekend.'). The counselor amplified and deepened change talk once it emerged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was largely managed well through double-sided reflections that paired it with change talk, and it was reframed rather than amplified. The score falls short of 5 because some reflections mirrored sustain talk fairly fully before pivoting, leaving room to redirect toward change language even faster.
