# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opens with explicit non-judgment and maintains warmth and affirmation throughout, even during defensive moments. Affirmations occasionally feel formulaic, and unconditional positive regard isn't always explicit when minimization resurfaces.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong, accurately capturing exhaustion, ambivalence, and emotional undercurrents. Some reflections are stacked with questions in a patterned way, and deeper emotions like guilt and loss of autonomy could be explored more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains steady focus on alcohol use and coping despite client deflection, without becoming pushy. Session progresses logically to clear, client-owned next steps tied to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely two-way with no direct advice offered; every plan element is generated by the client. The client's own collaborative language ('we go back to the drawing board') confirms successful power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client agenda control opens the session, self-imposed limits are honored as wisdom, and everything is framed as 'on your terms, at your pace.' No persuasive pressure appears; the referral is offered only after the client expresses willingness.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within the client through open evocative questions, with no clinician-supplied reasons for change. The client's own reasons (guilt, presence for her son, culinary identity) visibly deepen from defensiveness to commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is repeatedly positioned as the expert ('You're the expert on your life'), and every plan element originates from her. Client contributions visibly shape the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength: skillfully elicits and amplifies change talk by reflecting the client's own words and asking about obstacles and solutions. The client responds with strong commitment language and a concrete plan (Thursday, one glass, social worker call).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is validated rather than argued with, then consistently reframed toward the client's own values, with a textbook double-sided reflection. Occasionally the protective framing of drinking is amplified before pivoting, and minimization isn't always actively redirected.
