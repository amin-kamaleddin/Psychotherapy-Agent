# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard. Normalized avoidance ("given how irregular your schedule and routine already are, that makes some sense"), affirmed "it's not that you don't care about your health," and repeatedly affirmed Rowan's agency ("You're the expert on your own life," "this shift came from you"). No judgment or disapproval even during dismissiveness; accepted Rowan's gratitude without taking credit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening throughout. E.g., "It sounds exhausting, Rowan—coming home 'wrecked'... one more thing on a pile that's already too high," and captured deeper meaning: "it's not that you don't care... it's hard to prioritize something that only pays off down the road." Reflections clearly resonated ("Yeah. Exactly that," "that's basically it"), deepening self-understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on the target behavior from opening reflection through concrete commitment. Questions advanced progressively: barriers → meaning of use → reframing → first step → obstacles → future benefits. Session moved efficiently from precontemplation to a specific, self-generated action plan ("Cable out, plug it in, done") without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Genuine exchange between equals—asked rather than told, incorporated Rowan's phone-charging analogy into the shared framework, and co-constructed meaning through reflective dialogue. No expert-driven steering; session direction visibly emerged from the dialogue itself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Strongly supported throughout. Explicitly rejected pressure: "You're not promising anything, and that's okay," "at your pace," and contrasted doing it "because you decided, not because of another lecture." No advice imposed; entire plan client-generated, and self-set pace respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within: Rowan volunteered the phone-charging reframe, articulated his own benefits ("peace of mind," "not lying awake"), and self-corrected ("I've been paying way more than that in nagging letters"). Rowan explicitly noted the non-lecturing approach was "the only reason I'm still sitting here agreeing to anything"—evidence evocation, not persuasion, produced change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned Rowan as expert ("You're the expert on your own life, Rowan"). Plan emerged entirely from Rowan's own idea (cable by the bed like his phone). Counselor credited him directly: "That's a real, concrete plan, and it's yours" and "this shift came from you."

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and amplified change talk: highlighted Rowan's own words ("if it kept something bad from happening down the line, that'd matter"; "'that might actually work' is worth paying attention to"). Open questions elicited client-generated motivation, culminating in strong commitment language ("I'll actually do it tonight—not 'maybe,' not 'we'll see'").

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk never amplified; responded to "it doesn't feel urgent" with validating, change-oriented reframing and effective double-sided reflections ("part of you knows... another part keeps putting it off"). However, some early reflections restated sustain talk fairly extensively before pivoting; slightly more decisive double-sided reflections in opening exchanges could accelerate ambivalence resolution.
