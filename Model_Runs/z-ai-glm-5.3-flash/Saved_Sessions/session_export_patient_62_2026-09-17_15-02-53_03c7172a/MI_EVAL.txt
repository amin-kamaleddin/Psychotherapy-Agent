# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'Reaching out shows strength' and validated Jordan's skepticism. A 5 was withheld because warmth felt somewhat formulaic and affirmations lacked deeper individualization of Jordan's persistence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing themes of exhaustion and financial drain. Reflections were occasionally repetitive and surface-level, with limited exploration of the deeper emotional weight of repeated failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus on the target behavior of employment/income instability, with every exchange progressing logically from naming the worry to a concrete completed action and coping plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offered choices at every step and co-drafted the text to Jordan's preferred style, honoring his request to keep grounding simple. The menu-driven format kept the counselor somewhat in an option-provider role rather than true co-thinker.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jordan chose the focus, action, contact, wording, and timing, with consistent affirmations like 'You're in charge of what we focus on next.' A 5 was withheld because the counselor was quick to supply options and scripts rather than first exploring Jordan's own ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The weakest domain: the counselor relied heavily on closed menus and psychoeducation rather than open questions eliciting Jordan's own reasons, hopes, and strengths. Key change statements like 'I'd like to not feel stuck anymore' were acknowledged but not explored or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan's choices were invited repeatedly and his wording shaped the text draft, but the interaction leaned toward the counselor supplying options while Jordan merely selected from menus. More could have been done to draw out Jordan's own solutions and expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully turned Jordan's vague hope into concrete action, culminating in an in-session job inquiry text and reduced anxiety. Deeper change talk was not explicitly evoked through open, evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never amplified; the counselor validated skepticism then pivoted to a different approach, reframing past failure as a reason to change strategy rather than proof of futility.
