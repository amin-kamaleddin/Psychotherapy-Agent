# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful tone with affirmations ('you're showing a lot of care') and explicit no-lecture/no-judgment promises. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and normalization of setbacks was brief, keeping it below top marks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections (smoking as her one break, being torn between protecting her son and coping) but empathy was shallow at charged moments. When Elena expressed alarm about thirdhand smoke and said quitting 'feels like a joke,' the counselor moved quickly to options instead of reflecting her feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on smoking and efficiently reached a concrete plan (alarm +15 minutes, gum, ear-infection log) with consistent redirection to the target behavior. Direction was achieved via rapid menuing rather than collaborative pacing, which slightly compressed Elena's ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative moves (permission-seeking, offering choices, checking next steps), but structured recaps and pre-packaged option lists felt expert-driven. Elena had to repeatedly modify or reject proposals rather than co-constructing the plan from the start.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently low-pressure framing ('tiny, no-pressure test,' 'setbacks are info, not failure') and respect for Elena's limits, including rejecting full quitting and the quit line. Falls short of 5 because rapid option menus occasionally left Elena negotiating for control rather than the counselor reinforcing her ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied by the counselor through health information and option menus rather than drawn from Elena. Her own motivations surfaced spontaneously ('worth more than anything') but were not reflected, amplified, or deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission was asked and options were checked for doability, supporting some power sharing. But the agenda-driven structure and expert-style information delivery forced Elena to set boundaries herself, suggesting reactive rather than genuine co-construction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena voiced some change talk ('I've thought about quitting,' agreeing to the 15-minute delay), but this emerged largely through behavioral menus rather than deliberate evocation. The counselor missed chances to deepen commitment language when it appeared.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elena's minimization ('I don't smoke around him,' 'quitting fully feels like a joke') was acknowledged but not strategically reframed with double-sided reflections. The counselor shifted to psychoeducation instead, leading Elena to ask why the topic kept coming back.
