# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, affirming Daniela's courage and normalizing her ambivalence. Responds with warmth and curiosity even to risky behavior (skipping checks) and self-criticism, never with judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate and deepening, e.g., 'stories are you giving something, and diabetes feels like asking for something.' Reflections repeatedly land, evidenced by Daniela's 'Exactly!' and spontaneous insights.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a consistent focus on peer disclosure with a clear arc from fear to evidence to first step, timing, reasons, and rehearsal. No topic drift; closes with a concrete, rehearsed plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operates as a genuine partnership of equals—the plan (Kayla, next week, short script) is co-constructed entirely from Daniela's material. Role-playing as Kayla while deferring authorship of the words reflects true collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions the change as Daniela's own ('That's you, not your mom, not me') and avoids persuasion even when she invites it. Converts maternal pressure into intrinsic motivation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Draws motivation from within rather than supplying it—strategic questions like 'have they ever shared a need with you?' elicit Daniela's own insights. Her motivation visibly strengthens across the session, the hallmark of effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Conveys that expertise resides within the client: 'you know yourself and Kayla way better than I do.' Daniela's own analogy about Kayla becomes the turning point, and the counselor explicitly credits her insights.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and develops change talk, moving Daniela from desire to ability to commitment with a specific person, timing, and rehearsed script. She ends with strong commitment language: 'I actually want to do this. Not just think about it.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids dwelling on sustain talk, using double-sided reflections and reframing fear toward autonomy. A few reflections like 'holding this part of your life back' briefly amplified the problem-side before pivoting.
