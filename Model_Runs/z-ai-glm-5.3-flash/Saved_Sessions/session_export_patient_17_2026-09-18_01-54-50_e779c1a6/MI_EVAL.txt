# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms the patient's expertise and validates her ambivalence as a real dilemma rather than a weakness, respecting her without judgment even when exploring the hiding behavior.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are deep and accurate, capturing both sides of ambivalence ('cigarettes have been there for you... yet you're done with the hiding') and tender nuances like shame, fear of failing publicly, and the daughter relationship.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear focus on smoking behavior and toward change, with each reflection and question steering gently toward the patient's own reasons, strategies, and commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange is genuinely collaborative: ideas are generated jointly, options are offered as experiments ('what if we started smaller'), and every plan element originates from or is confirmed by the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported throughout — the plan is explicitly 'on your terms,' the patient chooses the delay experiment, whether/when to tell her daughter, and the psychiatrist explicitly declines to define what counts as 'cheating.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws out the patient's own ideas, motivations, and solutions rather than supplying them — the delay strategy, stress ball, success criteria, and self-talk all come from her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The tone is one of equal partnership, with the psychiatrist asking permission implicitly, positioning the patient as the expert, and co-constructing the experiment rather than prescribing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and amplified — the psychiatrist reflects self-motivational statements ('you're trusting yourself again'), asks about success definitions and coping strategies, and helps transform 'test to fail' into 'information to gather.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (skepticism, fear of announcing a quit date, past failures) is acknowledged and rolled with rather than confronted, reframed as a dilemma and 'curiosity, not commitment,' keeping resistance low.
