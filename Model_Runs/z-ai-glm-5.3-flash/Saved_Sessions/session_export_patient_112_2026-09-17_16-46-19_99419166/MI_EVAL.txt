# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveys warm, unconditional positive regard — normalizing ambivalence, affirming Rosa's bravery, and framing slips as learning moments rather than failures. No labeling, disapproval, or pressure appears anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate throughout, capturing Rosa's core ambivalence ('tug-of-war,' 'knot in my stomach') and deepening with complex reflections on the patch failure and need for support. Reflections build on Rosa's exact words and consistently deepen understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation from the opening reflection through the concrete action plan. Every exchange ties back to the target behavior, culminating in a specific, structured plan (Monday 7pm call, group search, friend-plan) without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session is largely a joint enterprise — Rosa's preferences (phone calls, in-person groups, knitting) directly shape the plan. However, the final third becomes counselor-driven with rapid logistical questions and listed deliverables, diluting full equal co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces Rosa's ownership by offering choices rather than directives, respecting her pace ('not ready to toss cigarettes yet'), and honoring her preference to start with behavioral supports. Rosa's closing autonomous commitment reflects genuine self-directed choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within Rosa via the importance ruler, gap questions, and problem-solving prompts — the counselor never argues for change or supplies motivation. Rosa's commitment evolves organically from ambivalence ('maybe a 6') to self-generated resolve ('scared and doing something is better').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located with Rosa, whose ideas visibly shape the session direction. The counselor occasionally slips into a service-provider/expert role in the later planning phase (listing options, offering to draft scripts and find groups), slightly reducing power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The 0–10 importance ruler and follow-up questions are textbook evocations that yielded change talk and a concrete confidence gap (6 → 8). Rosa's commitment language grew across the session ('Monday, then. I'll be waiting by the phone') and was actively reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally met with validating double-sided reflections and effective reframes (slips as learning moments, support before meds). It loses a point because worries about withdrawal, mood, and weight are sometimes simply reflected at length rather than actively reframed, giving them slightly more airtime than optimal.
