# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout; the client's autonomy-driven defensiveness is met with respect, and her identity, intelligence, and motherhood are affirmed without any labeling or moralizing about her drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, from the opening reflection on holding 'two real things at once' to pausing on 'that part actually feels good to say' and capturing the deeper meaning of 'peace of mattering more than wine.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on alcohol use in pregnancy with every exchange tied to the target behavior; tangents are channeled productively and the session progresses logically from ambivalence to a concrete Friday plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor operates as a genuine equal ('let's think together'), offers information only after inviting the client's knowledge, and the action plan is co-constructed entirely from her ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: everything framed as the client's choice ('on your terms'), the offered conversation script is declined and respected without pushback, converting resistance into voluntary commitment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within; pivotal realizations and spontaneous commitment language ('being a good mom matters more than the wine ever did') come from the client's own reasoning, not counselor persuasion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client ('You know your life far better than I do'), and the plan — brochure, Marco, alcohol-free ritual — is entirely hers, with her knowledge treated as the starting point.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation and amplification of change talk via open questions and reflective pauses; the counselor strategically elicited the value connection so the client arrived at 'I want that brochure' herself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not argued against and the client dismantles her own rationalizations, but engaging the evidence question meant extended dwell time on status-quo arguments; a faster pivot to values might have been more efficient.
