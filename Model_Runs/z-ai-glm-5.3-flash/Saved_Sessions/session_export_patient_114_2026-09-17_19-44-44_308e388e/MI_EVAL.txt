# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warm, nonjudgmental regard with frequent genuine affirmations; no labeling or moralizing even when Ryan minimizes the incident. Client explicitly felt unconditionally accepted ('Thanks for not just lecturing me').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including double-sided and meaning-deepening reflections that directly precipitated the client's insight about social rejection. Reflections consistently landed ('Never really said that out loud before').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior without pushiness; every exchange advanced toward understanding and changing the drinking pattern. Skillful transitions kept the session on track with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully joint work framed as 'figuring out together,' with all decisions driven by the client's ideas. Power-sharing was genuine, with the clinician building on Ryan's contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership explicitly reinforced throughout; the change plan was entirely client-generated and hedging was affirmed rather than pressured. No advice was imposed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within the client, who articulated his own value discrepancy (scholarship vs. drinking) without clinician persuasion. Questions like 'Has anyone ever actually cared?' produced the session's most powerful moments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was consistently positioned as the expert, and the entire change plan originated from him. His insights visibly shaped the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and amplified through open questions and strategic affirmations, culminating in strong commitment language ('That I think I can actually do'). The discrepancy was evoked through the client's own words.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never argued with or reinforced, and minimization was redirected effectively. However, some sustain talk (fear of turning Jake down, 'I'm still me') received extended airtime without active pivoting toward change language.
