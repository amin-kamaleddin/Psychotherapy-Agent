# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, normalizing the client's dismissive stance ('that 4/10 makes sense') without judgment. Respects even the final 'Don't make a big deal of it' without pushing for emotional engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, e.g., capturing the core frustration that decisions are already made and his role is just to tick boxes. Reflections sometimes serve as transitions to options rather than deepening the emotional weight beneath his resignation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintains clear, progressive focus on the target behavior, moving from vague frustration to a complete, specific plan with exact wording, delivery timing, backup line, and follow-up. Every exchange advances toward actionable change despite the client's brevity.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction is largely between equals—menus rather than directives, asking permission, and the client's contributions substantively shape the plan. Framing remains structured around pre-built options rather than fully open-ended co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces choice with genuine exit ramps at every step ('Want to pick one, change the wording, or rehearse?'). Reminders are dropped when the client finds them annoying, and his right not to rehearse or commit is respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully draws motivation from within, moving the client from 'whatever' to choosing a change line and stating he might say more next time. Early evocation yielded limited material and some phrasings were counselor-supplied rather than client-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise resides with Kieran—he chooses the sentence, rewords it, picks the delivery method, timing, and backup strategy, making the plan genuinely co-constructed. The counselor still generated most of the option menu rather than drawing primarily from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and shapes change talk, leading the client to select and refine a specific sentence, plan delivery via phone note, and note 'that's more planning than I've done in months.' Early change talk was sparse, with the counselor supplying more content than the client in places.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk briefly without amplifying it, then pivots toward change with low-effort experiments. Double-sided reflections effectively hold ambivalence while tilting toward change, though sustain content is occasionally repeated.
