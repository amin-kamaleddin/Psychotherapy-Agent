# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard through normalizing statements, affirmations, and nonjudgmental exploration of avoidance. Reframed the patient's 'It's dumb, I know' as meaningful, creating a warm, shame-free space.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong and accurate, skillfully decoding emotional meaning beneath brief answers. Occasionally stayed at the content level rather than naming deeper emotions directly (e.g., shame toward the case worker, pressure from his mom).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on completing community service hours by month's end. The session moved efficiently from location to confidence to sequencing to rehearsal with no topic drift, each exchange advancing toward concrete action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaved as an equal partner, asking rather than telling, offering choices, and letting the patient's ideas drive the plan. However, the skillfully sequenced questioning left slightly less room for the patient to co-direct the agenda at moments.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was reinforced at every juncture, with explicit options and no pressure or direction. The patient set his own timeline ('today? Or tomorrow'), which the counselor simply reflected back.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every key insight—the sequencing solution, meaning of avoidance, timeline, and closing reflection—was drawn from the patient rather than provided. Summaries consistently credited the patient as the source of change, reinforcing ownership.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned the patient as the expert ('You know your schedule and your neighborhood better than I do') and repeatedly credited him with the ideas. The patient's reframing of texting as 'telling her the plan' became the pivot of the intervention.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was actively evoked through the confidence ruler and open questions, producing self-generated solutions and organic commitment language ('I think I can do that,' 'I'll find out tomorrow'). The counselor consistently reflected and amplified this language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged briefly then pivoted toward problem-solving, with effective reframing of a 'no' as finding somewhere else rather than starting over. Some worries, like the food bank rejecting him, were left unaddressed rather than deliberately softened.
