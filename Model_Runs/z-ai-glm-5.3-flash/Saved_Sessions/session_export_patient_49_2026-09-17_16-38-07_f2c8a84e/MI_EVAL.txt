# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, nonjudgmental with genuine affirmations; slips normalized and skepticism met without disapproval. Loses a point for slightly formulaic support statements and limited explicit normalization of her ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured her ambivalence and stress-trigger/all-or-nothing pattern. Reflections were diluted by rapid question stacking, and her mother's smoking-related illness—a significant disclosure—passed without emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Laser focus on smoking reduction throughout, with a clear progression from exploration to a concrete experiment, stress-trigger contingency plan, scheduled follow-up, and escalation to the next step. No topic drift at any point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were genuinely co-constructed and framed as a joint project. Collaboration tilted counselor-directed at times, with the option menu and practical tips delivered instructionally rather than first eliciting Hana's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary support of Hana's ownership of pace, scope, and choices; her refusals and boundaries ('no promises past that') were honored without pushback. She set her own start date, tracking method, and next step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from Hana via scaling questions and open inquiries, eliciting powerful self-generated reasons (money, mum's health) and discoveries. Some motivation-adjacent content was clinician-supplied, and deeper exploration of her mother's illness was missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hana was repeatedly positioned as decision-maker and her input clearly shaped the plan—she rejected the walk option and invented her own breathing cue. Falls short because the option menu and some tips were counselor-generated and advice-heavy.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength: scaling questions reliably evoked change talk, moving her from importance 4/confidence 3 to confidence 7 with self-generated reasons and next steps. She closed with clear commitment language ('I'll actually do this one week').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally not amplified; slips were skillfully reframed as 'data, not failure,' directly softening her all-or-nothing thinking. However, some summaries restated sustain talk, and the early 'What do you like most about smoking?' question extended focus on the status quo for several exchanges.
