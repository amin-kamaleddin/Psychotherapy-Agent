# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains non-judgmental respect, and Claudia explicitly felt unjudged, but affirmations are sparse and generic, immediately diluted by checklists and safety boilerplate rather than honoring her specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is nearly absent; emotionally significant disclosures (disliking the drinking self, mood-worse-after-drinking link) are met with swap tips and repeated crisis scripts instead of acknowledgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Excellent focus on the target behavior throughout, culminating in a concrete, measurable plan; direction is sustained but somewhat rigid, pushed rather than responsively guided.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor functions as an advice dispenser with pre-packaged plans and tip lists; Claudia's ideas are incorporated only because she volunteers them, not through invited co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choice support via 'if you like' framings and picking start/free days, but the plan is largely counselor-authored and delivered as instructions without affirming Claudia's ownership or offering menu options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is supplied by Claudia, not elicited; the lone readiness ruler is never followed up, and no open questions explore her reasons, confidence, or the depression-drinking link she raises.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes an expert role with prescriptive advice dominating most turns; Claudia's contributions are acknowledged superficially while the counselor's ideas drive the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Claudia generates strong change talk spontaneously, but it goes undeeper—the readiness ruler response ('six, six and a half') is a classic missed evocation opportunity with no follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk (the 'shocking' seven-drink guideline, wine making low moods 'more interesting') is neither reinforced nor softened; no double-sided reflections or reframing, and the ambivalence is left unresolved.
