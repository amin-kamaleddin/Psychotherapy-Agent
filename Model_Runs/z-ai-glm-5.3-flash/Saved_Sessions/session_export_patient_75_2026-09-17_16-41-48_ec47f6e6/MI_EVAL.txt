# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, actively normalizing Linda's shame about being 'careless' with statements like 'you're learning, not being careless.' Slightly instructional at times during technique teaching, which keeps it from a top score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening throughout, capturing her ambivalence and frustration. However, reflections sometimes skipped deeper emotional content — discouragement about past failed attempts ('it never sticks') was passed over in favor of moving to planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on inhaler adherence from opening to closing role-play, connecting every exchange to the referral concern. Session progressed logically from exploration to concrete, graded next steps with timelines.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was genuinely co-constructed, with the final plan (rinsing tied to tooth-brushing, 9pm alarm, dog trial) assembled from Linda's own ideas and stated capacity. Counselor offered expertise while explicitly integrating her contributions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly frames everything as Linda's choice with permission-seeking and option menus, with no pressuring language anywhere in the session. Linda repeatedly perceives ownership, stating 'I'm taking charge instead of just worrying.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from Linda via open evocative questions, the confidence ruler, and support for her self-generated solutions, producing progressively stronger change talk. Advice was limited to requested factual information.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Treated Linda as the expert on her own life; her ideas genuinely shaped the plan, including the dog trial on nights her husband works late and tying rinsing to tooth-brushing. Asked permission before providing information and offered to adjust the PCP note.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation using forward-looking questions, option menus, and a confidence ruler with barrier follow-up, drawing strong change talk ('I can do the rinsing,' 'maybe I'm actually kind of hopeful'). Role-playing the PCP conversation was a skillfully elicited commitment step.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not argue against sustain talk, briefly correcting misinformation and redirecting to collaborative options. Some summaries inadvertently restated barriers rather than strategically reframing them toward change.
