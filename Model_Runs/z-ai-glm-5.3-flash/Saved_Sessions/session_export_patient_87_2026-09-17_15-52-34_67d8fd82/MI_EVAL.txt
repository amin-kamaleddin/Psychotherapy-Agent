# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, including welcoming Marco's honesty about possible failure. Warmth is somewhat formulaic, with few individualized affirmations of his specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture his ambivalence, barriers, and insights (e.g., the couch, Monday as the real test). Reflections are sometimes compressed into multi-question bundles and deeper emotional themes (diet failures, being healthy for his kids) are not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tight, consistent focus on evening eating and the walking plan with no topic drift; every exchange advances a concrete, measurable commitment (Mon/Wed/Fri, 20 min, 6:30 reminder, Tuesday check-in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely joint problem-solving with Marco's input shaping each detail, and the counselor framed as a supporter. The pre-packaged option menus position the counselor as the idea source rather than fully eliciting Marco's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Permission is consistently sought before advice, and Marco's choices (backup plan over invite, notification wording, timing, check-in format) are accepted without pushing. The plan is entirely his.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from Marco himself via scaling questions, with commitment language emerging naturally and no lecturing. Evocation becomes repetitive (same scaling formula four times) and deeper values like active fatherhood are rarely evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marco is treated as the expert, with constant choice questions and his input determining the plan's details. Early offering of a menu of strategies slightly undercuts full partnership by not first eliciting his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful scaling questions evoked rising confidence (six to seven) and self-generated motivation ('proving it to myself') plus concrete commitment language. The counselor sometimes moves past the answers too quickly rather than deepening them.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is briefly acknowledged then redirected toward solutions, and failure risk is reframed into a plan. Some early questions invite sustain talk ('what makes a diet fall apart?'), a mild missed opportunity to pivot faster.
