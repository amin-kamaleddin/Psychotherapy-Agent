# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard, normalizing the client's burden ('no judgment here') and reframing past failure as courage. No disapproval or moralizing appeared, even when the client minimized harm.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing ambivalence precisely and attending to affect (e.g., noticing the lighter tone in her voice). Reflections consistently landed and elicited elaboration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on the target behavior and progressed steadily from ambivalence to a specific, time-bound next step. Each exchange built on the last with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned as a genuine partnership, with the counselor explicitly asking how much the client wanted to decide herself. Every plan element was driven by the client's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice was reinforced throughout ('your version, your rules'), and hesitation was met with an experiment framing rather than pressure. The client's closing comment about having her own plan confirms strong autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from the client herself rather than supplied—she generated her own reasons ('permission,' her son watching) and her own solutions through evocative questioning. The counselor never lectured on health risks.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly positioned the client as the expert on her own life and credited insights to her. Her entire plan was self-designed, confirming the power-sharing landed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked through open questions, moving the client from 'I don't know' to a concrete, self-designed commitment ('yes' to trying tonight). Distinguishing choosing from agreeing deepened her commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally not amplified and was pivoted toward change effectively. However, some double-sided reflections restated sustain talk more than necessary, and one question briefly invited barrier-focused talk, though both were handled productively.
