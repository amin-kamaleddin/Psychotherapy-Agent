# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating the patient's minimizing stance and affirming his past success quitting smoking. Warmth is somewhat formulaic, however, with the same affirmation repeated multiple times rather than individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately capture Thomas's perspective on his drinking and his annoyance with his wife's comments. However, empathy thins as the session progresses, with the emotional layer of his defensiveness bypassed in favor of rapid advice-giving and planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains unwavering focus on reducing alcohol overconsumption from start to finish, with every exchange tied to the target behavior. The conversation moves logically toward a specific, actionable commitment with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The plan incorporates some of Thomas's own suggestions and offers options rather than directives. However, the psychiatrist monopolizes several turns with long tactical lists and pre-written scripts, effectively solving the problem for the client rather than drawing out his ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is repeatedly emphasized through permission-seeking questions and accepting the patient's 'no promises' stance without pressure. The barrage of prescriptive tactics edges toward directing, and the patient's ownership of the decision is not explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Standard evocative tools (importance and confidence rulers, 'what would bump that up') are used and elicit the patient's own reasons and success measures. But the motivation drawn out remains thin and surface-level, with values like fatherhood and health never explored as intrinsic sources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist offers choices and incorporates some of Thomas's ideas into the plan. Still, the middle of the session becomes noticeably expert-driven with rapid-fire tactic lists, making the overall flow counselor-directed rather than genuinely collaborative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Importance and confidence rulers are used skillfully to elicit modest change talk ('I could give those a shot'). However, evocation feels mechanical—change talk is not deeply amplified or reflected back to strengthen commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist appropriately avoids reinforcing Thomas's repeated minimizations ('it's not a big deal'). Yet sustain talk goes mostly unacknowledged rather than actively softened with double-sided reflections or reframing, missing opportunities to resolve ambivalence.
