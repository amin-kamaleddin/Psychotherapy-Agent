# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance toward a resistant adolescent, with affirming statements like 'Totally fair you won't give up your friends.' However, warmth felt somewhat instrumental, with affirmations serving to advance the negotiation rather than conveying deep unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed adequate early reflections ('That sounds really frustrating and violating') but shifted heavily into advice-giving as the session progressed. Emotionally significant moments, such as friends being 'the only people who actually get me,' went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session lost focus on the referral target entirely, with substance use mentioned only once indirectly and never explored. The session was co-opted into mother-conflict mediation, and Tyler's explicit statement that he won't quit substance use was left unchallenged.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Checked in frequently and adjusted scripts based on Tyler's feedback, with interactive role-play. Power-sharing was uneven, however: the agenda, solutions, and compromises largely originated from the counselor, with Tyler only selecting among pre-made options.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and allowed Tyler to reject unacceptable options without pressure, retaining his control throughout. Autonomy support could be stronger, as the counselor never emphasized Tyler's ownership of change or his capacity to direct his own life, and script-provision positioned solutions as counselor-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine motivation emerged ('it might be worth it just so she stops freaking out'), but the counselor relied primarily on providing advice and scripts rather than drawing motivation from within. Deeper questions about Tyler's values, friendships, or concerns about his use were never asked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited Tyler's input at several points and tuned scripts to his feedback, but the overall structure was expert-driven, with the counselor supplying nearly all content. Tyler's question about consequences was answered prescriptively rather than exploring his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited genuine movement, from 'There's nothing I wanna change' to 'I could probably live with that' and 'I actually feel kind of ready for tonight.' Change talk centered on managing his mother rather than his substance use, which the psychiatrist never evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce Tyler's substantial sustain talk and occasionally acknowledged it briefly. However, no double-sided reflections or strategic reframing were used, and sustain talk about minimizing substance use was simply bypassed and never softened.
