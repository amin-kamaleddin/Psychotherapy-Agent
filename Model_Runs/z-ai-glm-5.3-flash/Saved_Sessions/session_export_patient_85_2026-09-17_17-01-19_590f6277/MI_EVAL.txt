# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with genuine affirmations ('You've got strong reasons,' 'That's huge'), and never labeled or disapproved of Marco's habits. Falls short of 5 because affirmations were somewhat generic and formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflected Marco's frustration, fear of his father's heart attack, and desire to keep Rosa on his team. However, poignant disclosures—the childhood attachment to OJ, the sting of weight regain, and fear of 'quietly backing out'—were met with questions and suggestions rather than deep emotional reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on reducing fruit juice throughout, with every exchange tied back to the juice plan—from scaling through the Rosa conversation to the two-week reassessment. The session progressed logically from exploration to commitment with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The plan built heavily on Marco's own ideas (diluting, weekend treats, two-week trial framing), showing genuine collaborative texture. However, the counselor's pattern of affirmation-plus-question-bundles and pre-formatted scripts created a coach-driven rather than fully co-constructed feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices ('Which of those feels most doable?') and presented options rather than directives, with Marco repeatedly exercising choice. Falls short of 5 due to over-support—offering to draft texts and calendar lines even after Marco declined.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively evoked Marco's values at key moments, drawing out his fear of repeating his father's heart attack and strong self-generated motivation ('this time feels different'). But the counselor frequently supplied reasons and solutions (sugar-without-fiber psychoeducation, Saturday distraction strategies) rather than drawing them from Marco.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission and incorporated Marco's contributions, but repeatedly slipped into an expert-prescriber role with a largely counselor-generated numbered plan and drafted scripts/texts. Marco's own problem-solving drove the plan more than the counselor's facilitation of it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Used scaling questions and evocation effectively, producing strong change talk ('A 9, easy,' 'this time feels different'). However, the counselor did not systematically deepen these statements—when Marco rated confidence 8, the counselor jumped to solutions instead of asking why not lower or what would raise it to a 9.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, but also failed to strategically soften it—Marco's 'I genuinely love that OJ' was met with a solution menu rather than a double-sided reflection. Anticipated pushback from Rosa was handled with psychoeducation scripts rather than helping Marco resolve ambivalence.
