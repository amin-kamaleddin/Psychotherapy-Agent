# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard with statements like 'You're the expert on your own life' and normalizes slips proactively. No labeling, judgment, or conditional regard anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate reflective listening, including a complex double-sided reflection on the client's fear of quitting. Captures both emotional content and underlying meaning with precision each turn.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains consistent focus on resuming exercise and progresses logically from exploring motivation to a specific, time-bound commitment with an accountability structure. Each response advances the arc without drifting off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sets an egalitarian tone from the opening invitation to 'shape this together,' and every plan element is co-constructed from the client's ideas. Even the follow-up structure is negotiated rather than imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Frames every question as the client's choice and imposes no advice, with the entire plan originating from her ('it's mine, not something I'm forcing myself into'). Explicitly affirms her agency and ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivational content—reasons, solutions, and commitment language—comes from the client rather than the counselor. The counselor's questions consistently invite her to generate answers she already possesses.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly and explicitly positions the client as the expert, and her answers directly shape each subsequent question. The counselor merely reflects and amplifies her contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and selectively reinforces change talk at every turn, reflecting commitment language back ('I hear real commitment in I'll start tomorrow'). The client's motivation visibly strengthens from wanting to concrete commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles fear of quitting and self-criticism effectively with a double-sided reflection and a pivot to self-compassion. Could have probed slightly deeper into the all-or-nothing pattern before pivoting forward.
