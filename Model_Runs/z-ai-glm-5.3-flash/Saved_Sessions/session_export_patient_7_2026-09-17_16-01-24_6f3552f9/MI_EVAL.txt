# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained non-judgmental respect despite Amina's defensiveness, using validating language like 'It's brave of you to talk about it.' However, repeatedly returning to the safety question after she explicitly said 'stop asking that' slightly undermined unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was generally accurate, capturing Amina's guardedness and tentative openness ('being interrogated sounds awful'). Reflections were sometimes mechanical and missed deeper emotional content, such as her hurt about her mother 'taking it out on me.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained an appropriate focus on coping with household conflict for a precontemplative client. The safety question was asked four times after explicit objection, and focus drifted among multiple micro-strategies without asking what Amina herself wanted to be different.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor behaved largely as an equal, soliciting permission and building the plan entirely from Amina's own resources. However, reliance on structured choice menus rather than open joint problem-solving made collaboration feel uneven at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was a clear strength: 'You're in charge of what you share' and gracefully honoring her refusals of roleplay, rehearsal, and notes. Persistent safety questioning after objection and menu-heavy options created mild pressure despite the overall supportive stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor drew out Amina's preferences and her own backup plan, but never explored her motivations, values, or hopes for change. The session's forward movement was driven mostly by the counselor's option menus rather than Amina's intrinsic reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned Amina as the decision-maker, and her texting-Amara plan became the working plan. Yet the formulaic numbered menus drew direct criticism ('You keep making lists and it's giving me a headache'), suggesting the format did not fully foster genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Modest change talk emerged ('I'll try it, I guess. Next time'), and the 'tiny, practical thing' successfully elicited tentative engagement. The counselor did not deepen or affirm these commitment fragments with reflective affirmations or evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ('She never gets calm,' 'I'm not promising anything') was neither reinforced nor strategically addressed. The counselor sidestepped ambivalence by moving to new options rather than using double-sided reflections or reframing to resolve it.
