# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance, avoids labeling the use as problematic, and affirms the patient's efforts. However, validation of the patient's irritation at mandated attendance and his mother's surveillance was not made explicit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Early reflective listening accurately captured the patient's externally motivated defensiveness and frustration with his mom. Later reflections grew thinner as advice-giving increased, and the emerging ambivalence ('kinda want her to chill out') went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed anchored to cannabis use and its consequences, advancing a concrete agenda from pros/cons to a negotiated plan and rehearsal. One point docked because the focus subtly shifted from the use itself to consequence management without naming the shift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were collaborative elements such as menu-based options and co-editing the script into the patient's voice. However, the counselor frequently presented pre-formed solutions and invited ratification rather than genuine co-construction of substance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong opening autonomy statement and repeated choice-offering, with acceptance of the patient's 'not ready to quit' position without pressure. Advice-giving became fairly directive mid-to-late session, lacking permission-based framing and reinforcement of the patient's ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplied options, scripts, and strategies rather than evoking the patient's own motivations. His important admission of wanting his mom to 'chill out more than I thought' — a key piece of internal motivation — went completely unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asked preferences and incorporated the patient's rewrites of the script, showing some power sharing. Yet the expert role dominated: the deal options, script, timing, and strategy were counselor-generated, with the patient shaping only wording, not substance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions elicited some change talk ('maybe I'd follow some rules', 'I kinda want her to chill out'). This emerging talk was never reinforced or deepened; the counselor pivoted to scripts and advice, doing the motivational work for the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Abundant sustain talk ('they don't think it's a problem, so I don't either') was not reinforced and occasionally reframed. However, it was sidestepped rather than strategically softened — no double-sided reflections or discrepancy development with his stated values (team, family peace).
