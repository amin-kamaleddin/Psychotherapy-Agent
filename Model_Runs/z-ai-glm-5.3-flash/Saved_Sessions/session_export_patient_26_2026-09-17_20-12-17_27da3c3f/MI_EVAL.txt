# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, affirming Daniela's strengths without judgment of her smoking, finances, or family situation. Respect is warm and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening captures both content and emotional nuance, e.g., 'the backup plan is what would make you feel steady.' Reflections consistently attune to distrust, fear of rejection, and need for stability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on securing reliable childcare while appropriately deprioritizing smoking/alcohol concerns. Each exchange progresses toward a concrete, sequenced action plan with no aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Information is offered only in response to stated needs, and decisions about sequencing are co-constructed. Daniela's ideas visibly shape each next step of the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Ownership is consistently reinforced through questions like 'What would make you feel ready?' and offering rather than prescribing. The prioritization choice and plan are explicitly framed as Daniela's own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently elicited from within ('What would getting him into Head Start mean for you?'), with Daniela's own answers carrying the weight. Commitment language emerges naturally and is deepened by the counselor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly positioned within the client ('You know your life and rhythms better than anyone'). The Head Start-first sequencing came entirely from Daniela, and the plan was built around her ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and amplified, e.g., reframing 'I'd rather try and get a no' as 'someone moving.' Daniela responds with strong commitment language ('I'll write that down tonight,' 'That feels... doable').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Barriers are generally acknowledged briefly with empathy before pivoting to solutions, and the smoking/alcohol struggle is redirected toward prioritization. Occasionally, reflections restate barriers (e.g., 'you can't afford daycare'), though always paired with the change side.
