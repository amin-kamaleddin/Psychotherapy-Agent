# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated warmth and unconditional positive regard, affirming Nadine's honesty and validating her guilt without judgment. No disapproval or labeling appeared anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflections throughout captured emotional nuance, not just facts (e.g., reflecting the guilt about 'dropping everything else' and both hope and worry about the plan). Consistent, precise reflective listening was a standout strength.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on infant sleep, fatigue, and maternal confidence, progressively building toward a concrete plan. Each turn advanced the goal without drift, culminating in a specific client-generated plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was fully collaborative, with co-constructed ideas and decisions ('let's look at it together', 'I'm happy to think it through with you'). Nadine's input shaped each next step of the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with explicit choice ('Rather than me picking for you, which of those feels most doable?') and no pressuring or directives. Nadine's ownership of the plan was consistently reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently drew out Nadine's own insights and motivations rather than providing expert-driven reasons for change. This elicited key realizations like 'it's not me, it's the tiredness.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positioned Nadine as the expert ('You know your baby better than anyone here'), and her contributions directly shaped the session direction. Power sharing was evident throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk through open questions ('What ideas do you have...?'), leading to strong commitment language ('I'll actually bring it up tonight'). Nadine's own movement toward change was highlighted and affirmed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was largely handled well, including a double-sided reflection on the fear of 'chickening out,' but some worry language (e.g., fear of 'messing up her routine') passed without explicit redirection. Occasional missed opportunities to deepen ambivalence resolution with double-sided reflections.
