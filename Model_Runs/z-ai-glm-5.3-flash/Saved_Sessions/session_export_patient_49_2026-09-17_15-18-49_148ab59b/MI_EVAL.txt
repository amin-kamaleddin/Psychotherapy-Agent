# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with genuine, specific affirmations. Falls short of 5 because warmth occasionally felt formulaic and ambivalence was never explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used accurate reflective listening that captured both the client's behavior and stage of change, including skilled double-sided reflections. Reflections occasionally summarized rather than explored the deeper emotional meaning behind smoking and social belonging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior from opening frame through closing rehearsal, with every exchange tied to the goal. The session progressed logically from exploration to motivation to plan to skills practice with no topical drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked as an equal partner for most of the session, asking permission before offering ideas and building the plan from the client's own resources. Short of 5 because the counselor supplied most of the raw material (swap lists, scripted lines), making some exchanges feel counselor-generated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly and explicitly reinforced choice, and the client fully owned the plan—choosing the line, the day, and requesting rehearsal herself. A 5 is withheld because the counselor fronted much of the content rather than asking the client to generate her own solutions first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within using open questions, rulers, and past-success elicitation, yielding rich client-generated reasons and a confidence gain from 4 to 6. The latter half shifted from evoking to informing, with the counselor providing alternatives and scripts rather than continuing to elicit the client's own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Positioned expertise with the client at times, but the mid-session shifted toward an expert-driven mode with counselor-supplied lists, scripts, and rehearsal structure. Power-sharing was real but uneven, as the agenda and techniques originated from the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evoked change talk through importance/confidence rulers, 'one point up' questions, and past-success elicitation, producing strong client change talk and measurable confidence gain. A 5 would involve deeper elaboration of her stated motivation rather than moving quickly into brainstorming and planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk neutrally and used a skillful double-sided reflection, but directly invited sustain talk by asking what she enjoyed about smoking and then left those reasons unaddressed. Redirects were passive rather than strategic—the social ritual was never reframed as attainable without cigarettes until the client volunteered it herself.
