# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'Nice work so far' and 'you absolutely get credit.' Praise is somewhat generic and formulaic rather than individualized to Daniel's values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; the counselor jumps to advice and numbers rather than reflecting Daniel's social awkwardness or ambivalence. Emotional cues such as pride in quitting smoking and worry about his wife's concern are missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays well focused with clear structure: assessing drinking, setting a limit, weekend tracking, Monday review, and a 4-week plan. Direction is somewhat imposed via counselor-set agenda rather than negotiated, and emotional dimensions are under-explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel's preferences shape some decisions (Option B, Marco as buddy, Monday review), but the counselor supplies nearly all content and Daniel mostly chooses between pre-built options rather than co-creating plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offers real choices ('one-week plan or relapse sheet?', 'Pick A or B') without pressure; Daniel notes it 'doesn't feel like a lecture.' Choices remain within counselor-defined parameters with limited explicit affirmation of Daniel's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation emerged from the client largely unprompted; the counselor never asks evocative questions about why family, control, or his smoking-cessation pride matter. The session moves quickly to psychoeducation rather than drawing motivation from within.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor adopts an expert, advice-dispensing role with prescriptive limits and detailed sheets. Daniel's ideas are incorporated only after he volunteers them; the counselor rarely asks what Daniel thinks would work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges ('A limit might not be a bad idea,' 'worst case, I learn something') and choice questions invite it, but the counselor never deepens it by exploring why control matters or amplifying Daniel's stated motivations around family.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I don't have a problem, so why switch?', 'doable, but tight') is neither reinforced nor skillfully softened—no double-sided reflections or reframing of the social concern; it is mostly bypassed via redirection to practical strategies.
