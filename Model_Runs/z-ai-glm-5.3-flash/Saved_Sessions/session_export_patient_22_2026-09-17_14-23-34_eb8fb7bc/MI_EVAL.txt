# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor is respectful and nonjudgmental ('that's brave to say,' 'it's okay to feel worried'), but warmth remains generic and surface-level. Individualized affirmations of Camila's strengths (honesty about blackouts, insight into overly strict goals) are absent.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; no complex reflections anywhere in the session. Key emotional disclosures (fear of losing friends, drinking to cope with anxiety) go unacknowledged as the counselor pivots to plans and checklists.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays clearly focused on alcohol reduction with a concrete agreed outcome (one-week baseline tracking). However, direction is enforced through directive structure rather than collaborative goal-setting, and emotional themes surfaced are never integrated into the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction follows an expert-to-recipient structure with the counselor setting the agenda and supplying all solutions, templates, and scripts. Camila's input shapes minor details, but genuine co-construction of the plan is minimal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine gestures toward choice (Option A vs. B, app vs. paper), but the overall tenor is prescriptive with detailed instructions delivered before Camila asks. Her ambivalent autonomy statements ('feels like babysitting myself') go unexplored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation originates from Camila herself, not from counselor elicitation; questions are closed and information-gathering, quickly pivoting to advice. Her own motivations (anxiety, social fear, hangover exhaustion) are never drawn out or reflected.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, delivering prescriptive packages (seven-step plan, apps, templates, scripted lines). Camila influences minor details while the agenda, methods, and structure remain clinician-determined.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Camila generates change talk spontaneously ('I just wanna stop,' importance ~80), but the counselor makes no effort to evoke or deepen it. No open questions about what cutting back would give her, and her change talk is never reflected back or reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk appears repeatedly ('I don't wanna quit completely,' 'my friends will give me grief') but is simply ignored or bypassed with more advice. No double-sided reflections, reframing, or acknowledgment of drinking's social function.
