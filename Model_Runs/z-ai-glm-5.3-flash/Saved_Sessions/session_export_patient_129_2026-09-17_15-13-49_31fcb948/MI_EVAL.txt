# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a nonjudgmental, respectful tone ('I'm not here to judge') and validates Tyler's frustration rather than siding against him. Falls short of 5 because affirmations are brief and formulaic, with no deeper affirmation of his strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captures Tyler's perspective and names his emotional state. Reflections are occasionally surface-level — the emotional opening about his mom's crying making him 'feel like garbage' went unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains focus and progressively moves through assessment, role-play, and follow-up strategies using Tyler's felt concern as a workable entry point. Not a 5 because the substance use itself is only lightly touched and the session lacks a closing summary tying back to use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely collaborative and power-balanced — Tyler's critiques shape the session (the 'corny' script was revised, the boundary line dropped). The role-play is fully co-constructed with Tyler choosing scenarios and editing his own lines.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength: repeated choice offerings, permission-seeking, and respectful acceptance of Tyler's rejections reinforce his ownership. Tyler ends the session voluntarily attempting honest communication with no pressure from the counselor.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws motivation from Tyler's own values, and the role-play produced self-recognized insight ('that sounds defensive even to me'). Falls short of 5 because evocation centers on the mother-conflict rather than the substance use, and the counselor supplies scripts rather than drawing wording from Tyler.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently treats Tyler as the expert, inviting him to choose direction, edit suggestions, and drive the role-play setup. Tyler's contributions substantively shape the session — he rejected one script, modified another, and chose the direction himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully anchors to Tyler's own goal (earning his mom's trust), eliciting genuine change language like 'maybe I don't get grounded for the rest of the year.' Stops short of 5 because emerging change talk is never deepened or explicitly reflected back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Does not argue with Tyler's minimization ('it's just weed'), instead pivoting to what feels useful and embedding risk-reduction Tyler affirms himself. A 5 is withheld because double-sided reflections are never used to explicitly hold both sides of his ambivalence.
