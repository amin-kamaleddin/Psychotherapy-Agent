# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, never judging Julian's guardedness or justice involvement. Affirmations were genuine and specific, and the desire to be treated like a normal person was warmly validated as fair.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, capturing both content and emotional nuance throughout. Complex reflections consistently landed, with Julian confirming them ('Yeah, exactly'), showing accurate mirroring of his internal experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a crystal-clear focus on completing mandated hours, progressing deliberately from place to schedule to start date to first step. Each exchange advanced the plan while the clinician guided without dragging, using summaries to consolidate momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was strongly collaborative, with Julian's ideas (food bank, one-place preference, Saturday start) driving the plan and a visible shift from guardedness to elaborated planning. The 5 was withheld because the clinician shaped most of the conversational arc through sequenced questions and occasionally supplied framing language Julian then adopted.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice was consistently reinforced, with every decision — place, days, Saturday start, calling the case worker — coming from Julian. The clinician never directed or pressured, explicitly reinforcing ownership ('That's a plan you built'), which Julian's closing autonomy confirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from within the client; the clinician never argued for change or supplied reasons. Questions elicited Julian's own realizations ('I didn't realize I could actually do it until I said it out loud'), transforming compliance into self-generated planning and hope in a court-mandated client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly framed expertise as residing in the client, and Julian's contributions demonstrably shaped the working plan. The 5 was withheld only because suggestions like the Sunday self-check-in were introduced by the clinician rather than fully client-generated, though Julian immediately owned them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked through open questions that drew out commitment language ('call her. Probably tomorrow. I think I can do that'). Each instance was reinforced, with mobilizing change talk (specific steps and dates) developed to an exceptional degree for a pre-contemplative, court-mandated client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Julian's sustain talk was acknowledged without amplification and frequently redirected using well-executed double-sided reflections that pivoted toward readiness ('part of you is ready to get this behind you'). Sustain talk was never reinforced; a 5 would require even more systematic pivoting.
