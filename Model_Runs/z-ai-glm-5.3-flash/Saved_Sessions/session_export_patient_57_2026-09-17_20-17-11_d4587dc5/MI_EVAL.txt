**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard; even sensitive topics like keeping things from Coach are handled without judgment. Affirmations are frequent and genuine ('there's something real in what you just said').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections accurately capture both emotional and social nuance, e.g., mirroring the tension between peer pressure and athletic goals. Sustain talk is named using Jamal's own words, showing deep understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on handling alcohol pressure, with each exchange advancing a logical arc from problem identification to concrete action and follow-up. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element originated from Jamal, with ideas co-developed through questions rather than prescribed. The follow-up plan is negotiated with Jamal setting the terms.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are consistently framed as Jamal's ('which feels more doable to you'), with no advice or pressure. The check-in is offered as an option, and his closing 'I actually want to try' reflects autonomous motivation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions are drawn entirely from the client — his refusal line, the Dre idea, and his accountability framing are all self-generated and amplified through affirmation. A fully client-authored plan with strong commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client ('You know your world better than anyone'). The counselor shapes only the process while Jamal's contributions determine the session's content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation moves Jamal from 'I don't know' to mobilizing change talk ('I actually want to try') and a concrete commitment. Planning questions yield a fully client-generated plan.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged briefly and pivoted toward change (e.g., pairing 'one night won't hurt' with 'remember Monday's legs'). A few reflections occasionally restate worry talk ('whether it went perfect or got messy') more than necessary, though framed as normalizing.
