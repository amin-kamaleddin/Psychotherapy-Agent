# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, thanking Daniel for his honesty and validating his skepticism. However, affirmations were formulaic and did not individualize Daniel's strengths (e.g., his discipline and self-monitoring).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections were present (e.g., 'I get why that math felt surprising', 'Weekends are the toughest'), but reflections were limited and shallow. The counselor missed opportunities to reflect Daniel's ambivalence, defensiveness, and threatened identity as a healthy athlete, with instruction dominating over reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on alcohol use with strong directional momentum toward a concrete 2-week reduction experiment, tracking parameters, and a scheduled check-in. Only minor drift occurred into logistics and repeated recaps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was genuine collaboration — Daniel chose the option, adjusted alcohol-free days, and shaped the tracking method. However, heavy reliance on unsolicited strategy lists, guideline lectures, and recaps tilted the interaction toward expert delivery rather than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently offered choices, accepted Daniel's modifications without resistance, and respected his 'test, not a promise' framing. Score held at 4 because Daniel's ownership of the decision was never explicitly affirmed or reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor predominantly provided information rather than eliciting Daniel's own motivations and concerns, with little exploration of his values or what change would mean to him. The '18 sounds like a lot' moment was evocative but incidental rather than a consistent evocative stance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered menu options and negotiated the plan, incorporating Daniel's modification of two alcohol-free nights. However, lengthy expert-driven monologues dominated, and Daniel's contributions were largely absorbed passively rather than shaping the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel acknowledged the math surprised him, agreed to the experiment, and committed to tracking — helped by the tracking plan and anchoring to training days. The counselor did not deepen this change talk with strategic questions or reflections that expanded on Daniel's own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel expressed substantial sustain talk ('don't be surprised if nothing changes'), which the counselor neither reinforced nor actively softened. Responses like 'your skepticism is understandable' validated it without pivoting toward change via double-sided reflections or reframes linking to his values.
