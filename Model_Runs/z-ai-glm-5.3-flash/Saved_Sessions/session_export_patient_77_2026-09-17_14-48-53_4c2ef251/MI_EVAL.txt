# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with frequent affirmations and normalization of the patient's feelings. However, praise feels somewhat formulaic, and self-critical statements like 'I feel like a failure' could be met with deeper unconditional validation of her worth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening that captures her discouragement, wariness, and loneliness. Reflections are sometimes brief before pivoting to questions or advice, and the emotional core of her loneliness and self-criticism deserves fuller exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior throughout, with no aimless drift. The session builds progressively to a concrete, collaboratively agreed plan with a clear closing summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed, with the patient choosing the breakfast, tracking method, and check-in timing. However, option menus and nutrition advice are delivered in prescriptive bursts rather than jointly brainstormed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are consistently framed as the patient's, with no pressure and respect for her preferences. Some turns bundle multiple suggestions, which can feel subtly leading, and ownership of the change process could be more explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Confidence scaling and eliciting preferences draw out some motivation, and her confidence rises from 6-7 to 7. However, her deeper values, reasons for change, and hopes are never explored or amplified, with the session focusing more on logistics than internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient's choices do shape the plan, but the counselor positions himself as the source of solutions through menus of options and scripted replies. More exploration of what she already knows and her own expertise would strengthen true partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of confidence scaling and invitations to select her own options, with change talk growing from 'I can try that' to 'like a seven now.' Some change talk is elicited via yes/no or menu-style questions, and spontaneous self-motivating statements aren't deeply amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The patient's doubts ('I've been burned before,' 'what if nothing changes again?') are acknowledged briefly but often met with reassurance or factual correction. Strategic double-sided reflections and rolling with resistance would soften sustain talk more skillfully.
