# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Milan's perspective with statements like 'That makes sense' and 'totally fair.' Warmth was somewhat functional and formulaic, with few genuine affirmations of Milan as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Delivered accurate complex reflections of Milan's perspective, including his social view of drinking and fear of looking like a wimp. Missed opportunities to explore the deeper emotional weight behind his peer-image concerns and identity issues.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on harm reduction from the opening values question through the concrete final plan. Each turn advanced toward a workable outcome without topic drift, even when Milan deflected.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission before advising, invited tweaking, and let Milan select tools, resulting in a genuinely co-constructed plan. The power balance tilted somewhat toward the counselor generating ideas for Milan to choose among.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support through repeated choice offerings, explicit permission-seeking, and accepting Milan's 'not saying I need rules' stance without pressure. The frequent option menus, while choice-based, occasionally led rather than followed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some evocation present via the values/goals question, confidence ruler, and eliciting Milan's exit language, but real motivation surfaced was shallow. The counselor frequently supplied options, scripts, and comebacks rather than drawing reasons for change and strategies from Milan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Milan's expertise as central, with his contributions genuinely shaping the plan (code word 'pizza,' the 'ride' excuse). The counselor supplied most ideas and options rather than drawing more from Milan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk through the likes/differences question and confidence ruler, moving Milan from pure minimization to commitment language and a concrete plan. Could have deepened change talk by exploring Milan's own reasons for change rather than moving quickly to solutions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided reinforcing Milan's minimization, briefly acknowledging it ('That makes sense') then pivoting toward practical strategies. Sometimes moved past sustain talk too quickly rather than using double-sided reflections to help Milan resolve ambivalence himself.
