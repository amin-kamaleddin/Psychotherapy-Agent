# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth with no judgment despite the client rushing off, though affirmations are somewhat formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid complex reflections and accurate summaries of the client's preferences, but the emotional undertone of her tiredness and uncertainty was not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus advancing from preferences to a concrete plan, though centered on information logistics rather than a behavioral change goal or explicit commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed with the client's input shaping every step, with everything negotiated rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support throughout—options offered without pressure, client framed as the expert, and the future visit left entirely to her choice with no timeline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's preferences were elicited well, but questions targeted logistics rather than values, importance, or confidence, leaving intrinsic motivation superficially explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was explicitly positioned as the expert, and her contributions determined the session's direction, format, and content—full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk was evoked and affirmed, but it remained largely logistical and was not deepened with questions about what makes the change feel important.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about time constraints was skillfully neutralized via double-sided reflections, though redirection toward resolving ambivalence was implicit rather than strategic.
