# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirmations like 'Nice—short and casual is a smart call' and 'You're doing well thinking ahead.' Warmth remains somewhat task-focused, with little affirmation of Julian as a person beyond logistics.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Julian's worries ('the deadline and not knowing where/how to log hours feel biggest') and his communication preferences. Reflections stayed brief rather than exploring the anxiety beneath his guardedness ('I don't want to say something wrong').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange advanced a concrete pathway—caseworker text, follow-up, site list, log/calendar—with no topic drift. Each response ended with a clear next-step question tied to the goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Julian's input materially shaped outputs (shorter casual texts, 9th St food bank, weekend/after-3 availability). However, the counselor often supplied complete drafts and lists rather than letting Julian generate ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices offered at every turn ('Send now or tweak?', 'Want it more formal or shorter?'), and Julian's preference to wait on the hourly plan was accepted without pressure. His growing self-directed ownership ('I'll send both today') reflects effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler drew out a 7 and some self-efficacy talk ('It's just one text, right?'), but most momentum came from counselor-supplied structure. Julian's own values, reasons, and hopes beyond compliance—like his cousin's example—were unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Julian was consistently positioned as the decision-maker ('Which step feels easiest to do right now?'), and his preferences shaped the scripts and site list. The counselor sometimes provided complete answers, slightly limiting client-generated solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The confidence ruler and step-choice questions elicited commitment ('I'll send it after we're done here') and self-generated change talk ('That's actually not that bad. Okay. Yeah, I can do that'). Evocation relied on procedural questions rather than exploring personal values or reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Hesitations like 'it might be weird to text them before I even know my hours' were met without reinforcement, reframed as planning, and briefly explored rather than pushed. Some repeated 'I don't know' fillers were left unaddressed.
