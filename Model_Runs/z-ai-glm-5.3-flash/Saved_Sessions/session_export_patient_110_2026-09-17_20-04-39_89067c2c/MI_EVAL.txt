# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard was maintained throughout, with affirmations like 'You've clearly thought about how you operate, and that's real expertise.' The client's minimization was validated rather than judged, confirmed by his closing 'Thanks for not just lecturing me.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were highly accurate and consistently deepened the conversation, such as capturing the tension between feeling healthy and the silent risk. The clinician mirrored the client's shift from 'a chore' to 'a promise.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on cholesterol treatment adherence with each exchange building toward commitment and concrete next steps. No topic drift occurred; every question advanced the target behavior while staying client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operated between equal partners, with 'let's think together' invitations and every plan element originating from the client. The counselor explicitly credited him: 'Not because of anything I told you — you found your own reason here.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice was consistently reinforced with phrases like 'what feels right to you' and 'That's yours to keep.' The plan was framed as fitting who he is rather than something imposed, and he freely chose his own pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key motivations came from the client's own words — his retirement dreams and identity as a partner ('She deserves a partner who can keep up with her'). The clinician amplified this change talk into strong commitment language and a self-generated plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was actively positioned as the expert ('You've designed this yourself, really'). All plan elements — the coffee-pill association, joint walks, dinner conversation — originated from him, with the counselor limited to reflecting and asking questions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic open questions ('What would make the difference between this feeling manageable versus feeling like a burden?') evoked the client's own solutions and values. This produced explicit commitment language: 'Yeah, I'll take the medicine too. That part's settled.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently met with double-sided reflections and redirected toward problem-solving, never argument or confrontation. His ambivalence about his wife's involvement was acknowledged while keeping the conversation moving forward.
