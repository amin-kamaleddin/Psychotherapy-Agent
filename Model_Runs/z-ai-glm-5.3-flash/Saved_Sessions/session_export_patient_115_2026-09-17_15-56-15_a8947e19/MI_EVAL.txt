# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout with validation of partial progress and explicit respect for Samir's boundaries around smoking. However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic rather than individually tailored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections (frustration, feeling unrecognized), but reflective listening largely disappears as the session shifts to problem-solving. Emotional cues like resignation, fear of being nagged, and trust-building statements went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintains consistent focus on asthma management and trigger reduction with concrete, relevant steps. Momentum is heavily weighted toward logistics rather than building motivation for the broader lifestyle changes.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Samir shapes the agenda and chooses among options, but the counselor's long expert monologues (checklists, instructions, templates) dominate without first eliciting his own knowledge or ideas. Power-sharing exists at the level of choosing clinician-generated options rather than co-creating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently affirms Samir's ownership and pace, offers menus of options rather than directives, and respects his refusal to discuss quitting. Falls short because choices are mostly framed around pre-generated lists without connecting to his intrinsic capacity or personal values.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Operates primarily from an advice-giving stance, implying motivation resides in information rather than the client. Samir's spontaneous motivations (curiosity about symptom patterns, hope of reduced cigarette reliance) were never drawn out, and motivation for smoking/drinking was left almost entirely unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Samir's contributions influence some direction (choosing the filter, checklist, inhaler check), but the bulk of content is clinician-generated and unilaterally delivered. His handy, experienced-patient knowledge was bypassed rather than leveraged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Samir spontaneously generates change language (e.g., 'maybe that proves to me I don't need the smokes'), but the counselor never recognizes, reflects, or amplifies it. No open questions designed to elicit desire, ability, reasons, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk well by not debating quitting and responding neutrally when Samir states his limits. However, this is achieved through avoidance rather than skillful double-sided reflections or strategic pivots toward change.
