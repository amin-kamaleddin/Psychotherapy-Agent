# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, warm, and nonjudgmental with multiple affirmations. Warmth occasionally feels formulaic ('Love that'), and past relapses are normalized only briefly rather than with depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening capturing Marcus's frustration with yo-yo dieting, time constraints, and his growing hope. Reflections are brief summaries often followed by stacked questions, and deeper emotional exploration of shame behind repeated failures is only lightly touched.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a crystal-clear focus on increasing physical activity, with every turn advancing a concrete agenda (specific days/times, reminders, if-then backup, accountability, self-monitoring). Session progresses steadily from contemplation to preparation with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Works as an equal partner for most of the session, co-constructing the plan and integrating Marcus's ideas (flexible if-then, texting his wife). The counselor sometimes drives the process with multi-part questions and ready-made solutions rather than staying fully client-led.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly offers choice and lets Marcus select his own if-then wording and accountability method, reinforcing ownership. However, the counselor frequently supplies pre-packaged options and scripts, and rapid directive questions early on slightly pressure the pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently draws out Marcus's own motivations and reasoning via open questions and rulers, eliciting strong personal insights ('a detour, not a dead end,' mood benefits over the scale). A 5 is withheld because the counselor also supplies substantial content, menus, and scripts rather than relying purely on evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveys that expertise resides with Marcus, inviting his preferences and letting his contributions substantially shape the plan. Falls short of 5 because the counselor often presents pre-formatted menus of options rather than openly letting Marcus generate his own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully uses readiness and confidence rulers that drew out strong commitments and rising self-efficacy (confidence 6 to 7, 'Okay, I'm doing this. Starting today'). Repeatedly reinforces and deepens Marcus's own change talk, including his personal reasons of family and dancing again.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk, briefly acknowledging doubt and barriers then pivoting to small believable steps and past strengths. Acknowledgment is somewhat formulaic; double-sided reflections to actively resolve ambivalence would be needed for a 5.
