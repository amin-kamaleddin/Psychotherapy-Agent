### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, including reframing Linda's 'obsessing' as thoughtful self-care. No judgment even when describing skipped doses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, emotionally attuned reflections such as the double-sided ambivalence reflection and highlighting the weight of 'forever' and 'less heavy.' Reflections consistently deepened Linda's own insight.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on inhaler adherence with every exchange advancing toward a concrete tracking plan and troubleshooting. No aimless wandering; momentum built from ambivalence to a specific experiment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit partnership framing ('Let's plan it as partners') with decisions co-constructed throughout. The experiment, checklist, and check-in cadence all emerged from dialogue rather than prescription.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Linda set her own check-in frequency, experiment parameters, and first steps. The counselor explicitly checked for pressure and framed the plan as her 'deal with myself.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly all change ideas originated from Linda via skilled evocative questions tied to her own values (walking Brutus, being a researcher). Her words 'I'd finally know instead of always wondering' show motivation successfully evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located with the client ('You know your body; we know what to watch for'). The counselor credited Linda's contributions directly: 'None of that existed an hour ago, and it came from you.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully elicited client-generated change language, which was then strategically mirrored and amplified. Linda's shift to a self-designed experiment is direct evidence of success.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally acknowledged and reframed productively without judgment. A few double-sided reflections restated fear ('forever,' horror stories) a beat longer than necessary before pivoting to change; quicker redirection would earn a 5.
