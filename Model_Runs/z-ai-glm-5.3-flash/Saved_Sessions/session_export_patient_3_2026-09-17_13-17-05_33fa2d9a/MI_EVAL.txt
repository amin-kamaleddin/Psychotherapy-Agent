# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with validating and warm supportive statements throughout. However, warmth was somewhat formulaic and lacked explicit affirmation of Aisha's specific strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Initial reflection of feelings was adequate, but reflections largely disappeared after the second turn. Emotionally significant content — feeling gross, family weight criticism, self-criticism, pre-activity anxiety — was repeatedly left unreflected and unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on resuming exercise and moved efficiently to a concrete, committed plan. The strong direction came at the cost of collaborative goal-setting and exploring the emotional drivers Aisha surfaced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session was fundamentally expert-driven — the counselor proposed, Aisha accepted, with no agenda negotiation or co-construction. Aisha's ideas were absorbed into a counselor-generated plan rather than shaping the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission once before offering suggestions, but the interaction then became prescriptive with detailed plans, mandatory checklists, and rapid-fire instructions. Little reinforcement of Aisha's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied almost entirely on information-giving and closed logistical questions rather than drawing out Aisha's own motivations and resources. No values exploration, confidence scaling, or open evocative questions; the client's motivations passed without development.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor rapidly assumed the expert role, delivering a full exercise plan, body-image practices, and checklists with minimal client input. There was no acknowledgment that Aisha knows what will work for her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Aisha spontaneously generated abundant change talk, but the counselor never evoked, reflected, or deepened it with open questions. Her strong motivation was a product of her own readiness, not counselor skill.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Little true sustain talk was present, and the counselor appropriately avoided dwelling on mild discouragement. However, barriers like embarrassment and self-criticism were never strategically addressed or reframed — handling was neutral rather than skillful.
