# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with positive regard expressed through affirmations. However, affirmations were repetitive and formulaic, lacking deeper individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening reflection showed good perspective-taking, but reflective listening was not sustained. Missed opportunities to reflect the client's relief, ambivalence ('more or less'), and time pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately prioritized the critical safety question about the citalopram–phenelzine interaction. However, the session lost focus and dissolved into many aimless farewell exchanges without consolidating a concrete next step.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements, but the interaction was largely deliver-information-and-reassure. The client's ideas never substantially shaped the session and no agenda was negotiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly supported choice ('Nothing about this needs deciding today') with no coercive language, and the client's decision to stay on citalopram was accepted. Could have more consistently reinforced her authority over medication decisions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied on information-giving and affirmations rather than evocative questions. The one open question was dropped, and no exploration of the client's values, motivations, or confidence occurred despite openings like 'more or less'.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Positioned the client as capable and asked an open question early, but the exchange quickly became question-answer-information-delivery. Partnership remained at a basic level rather than genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The client offered mild change-related statements, but the counselor never deepened, reflected, or amplified them. Advice was given rather than drawing out her own reasons and plans.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with the client's sustain talk, which was appropriate. However, no strategic softening occurred — no double-sided reflection or redirection toward the client's own goals.
