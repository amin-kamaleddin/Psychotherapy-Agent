# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with multiple affirmations and explicit respect for Diego's choice not to quit. However, affirmations are somewhat generic, and there is little normalization of his ambivalence or acknowledgment of the emotional weight of his circumstances.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is generally accurate and captures Diego's perspective well. Reflections tend to be brief and quickly paired with questions or menu options, and the emotional pain underlying his use ('things I don't want to think about') was left unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on harm reduction—Narcan/housemate conversation and safer injecting—with each exchange building toward concrete goals and no meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative with preferences solicited at every decision point and role-play at Diego's request. Informational content (safer-injecting tips, safety sheet) was delivered didactically rather than built from his existing knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: choices offered repeatedly, permission asked, no pressuring language, and Diego's ownership of the plan (e.g., Narcan placement) reflects genuine internalization of choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation was assumed rather than drawn out—no evocative questions about reasons, no confidence exploration, and the powerful statement 'I can live with awkward. I can't live with the other thing' went unreflected. Much of the session involved providing options rather than eliciting motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego is repeatedly positioned as the expert, with menus deferring choice to him and his contributions shaping the plan. The clinician still structured most content, with limited co-construction or eliciting of Diego's own knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego volunteered strong change talk, but the clinician did little to actively evoke or deepen it—no strategic open questions about reasons or confidence, and the 10/10 statements were accepted at face value. Change talk emerged from the patient's own readiness rather than skillful evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement and effectively redirected toward harm reduction without argument. Redirection relied on multiple-choice offers rather than skilled double-sided reflections or honoring-and-reframing.
