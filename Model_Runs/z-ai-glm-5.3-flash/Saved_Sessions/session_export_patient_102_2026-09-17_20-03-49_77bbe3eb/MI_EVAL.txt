# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout, validating Milan's defensive position without judgment and normalizing his ambivalence. Affirmations were genuine and individualized, with no moralizing despite the moralistic referral context.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening captured both surface content and underlying emotion, notably the 'sober guy' reflection that prompted Milan's first genuine acknowledgment. Milan explicitly confirmed feeling understood: 'you asked me stuff today nobody's asked before.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on Milan's alcohol use, with each reflection and question tied back to drinking behavior. The session progressed logically from exploration to ambivalence resolution to a concrete, time-bound plan with scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned between equal partners, with Milan's own knowledge shaping the entire action plan (driving card, Marcus, self-monitoring). Milan's stance shift and self-proposed follow-up demonstrate the collaborative approach visibly changed his engagement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly reinforced Milan's ownership with 'no promises needed' and an explicit 'if any' permitting no change. Milan framed all commitments as his own practical choices rather than imposed abstinence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from within the client, eliciting his own reasons (social belonging, hangover annoyance) and practical strategies. Milan's surprise at 'thinking this hard' confirms intrinsic motivation was successfully activated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Milan as the expert on his friends and social world, with key questions like 'What do you think would actually land with them?' Milan directly noticed this, and the session's key plans all emerged from him rather than the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk with pivotal questions and strategically highlighted emerging commitments. Milan generated his own reasons and made concrete commitments (texting Marcus, self-monitoring) within a single session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk using effective double-sided reflections and pivoting to client-generated solutions rather than arguing. A point is withheld because Milan's minimization ('everyone's making this bigger than it is') recurred several times without more active strategic reframing.
