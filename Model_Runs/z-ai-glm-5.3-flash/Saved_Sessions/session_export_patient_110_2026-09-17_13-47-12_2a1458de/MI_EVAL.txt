# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with warm affirmations and handled safety screening calmly. However, affirmations occasionally felt generic and formulaic rather than deeply individualized to Richard's struggles with minimization and self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate, especially the ambivalence around urgency and retirement goals. As the session progressed, empathy gave way to rapid-fire advice and logistics, and vulnerable disclosures like brushing things off and fear of falling off were met with problem-solving rather than reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, unwavering focus on cholesterol management throughout, with every exchange tied to the concrete plan. Session concluded with clear commitments, tracking strategy, barrier planning, and a scheduled Tuesday follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard's choices shaped the plan (grilled over fried, wife's involvement), but the counselor frequently dispensed multi-step advice bundles and stacked 3-4 questions at once. The interaction took on an expert-driven quality with limited back-and-forth problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options were offered rather than mandates, and Richard freely chose his own steps with appropriate medical caveats. However, the volume of prescriptive tips and repeated commitment requests could feel subtly pressuring.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation largely came from the counselor's plan rather than being drawn out from Richard. His powerful own values (hiking at 70, grandkids, active retirement) went unexplored, and no evocative questions about hope or success were asked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard's input shaped the plan, but the mid-session was dominated by counselor-generated advice delivered rapidly, leaving him in a receptive role. Little acknowledgment of Richard as the expert on his own routines and motivation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective use of importance/confidence rulers elicited useful change talk, including commitment to daily pill-taking. However, change talk was never deepened (no 'Why a 6 and not a 3?') and Richard's strongest motivation went unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** No reinforcement of sustain talk and a good double-sided reflection early on. However, minimization and fear of quitting were met with practical tips rather than strategic pivots back to his values.
