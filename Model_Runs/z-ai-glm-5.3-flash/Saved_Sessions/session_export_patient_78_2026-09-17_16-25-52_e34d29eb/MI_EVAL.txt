# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and warm, explicitly honoring Lucía's request for no lectures or promises with no judgment shown. However, affirmations lean formulaic and offer little explicit recognition of her strengths beyond politeness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections throughout captured both content and her frustration with repetitive medical advice (e.g., te hartan los sermones pero tu hija te importa). Reflections tended to stay at the practical level rather than deepening into her emotional stress or health fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained an exceptionally clear focus on smoking with no topic drift, converging on a concrete, behaviorally specific plan (lighter in drawer, tea contingency). Every turn tied back to the target behavior, including triggers and relapse planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Lucía selecting and modifying options and shaping all contingencies. Occasionally the counselor offered ready-made solution menus rather than eliciting Lucía's own ideas first, and repetitive summarizing felt one-directional.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support throughout, with all of Lucía's refusals (check-ins, counting days, recruiting Marisol, promises) honored without pressure. The plan was explicitly framed as realistic and hers, reinforcing her ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Lucía's own motivation, with her daughter emerging as the emotional anchor and Lucía generating her own coping ideas (tea, drawer placement). Improvement needed in waiting for client-generated answers before offering option menus and probing hopes beyond the daughter.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Lucía was repeatedly positioned as the expert, choosing every element of the plan while rejecting alternatives without pushback. Some option-listing turns leaned slightly expert-driven, though her choices were fully honored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk via the importance ruler and the question about what matters most, yielding meaningful client language about her daughter and a willingness to try. Could have deepened the change talk (e.g., exploring what the 5 means) before moving to planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rolled with sustain talk effectively without arguing, acknowledging ambivalence neutrally and pivoting to small client-chosen steps. The work-stress/balcony sustain talk was accepted as fixed rather than gently explored and softened.
