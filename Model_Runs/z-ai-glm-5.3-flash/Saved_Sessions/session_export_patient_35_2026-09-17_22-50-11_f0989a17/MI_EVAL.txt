# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard; directly countered Elena's fear of judgment and closed with unconditional acceptance ("you're welcome here exactly as you are").

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing core fears like dreading "another failure"; Elena's responses ("lighter," "that hasn't happened in a while") confirmed she felt deeply understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus on smoking and progressed logically from exploration to commitment and follow-up; slight deduction for extended emotional validation in the middle that briefly loosened behavioral momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key decisions (car change, timeline, follow-up) emerged from joint exploration with Elena's ideas as the primary resource, with Elena setting agenda, pace, and plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly honored the client's choice at every step ("you don't have to promise anything," "that's yours to own"), celebrated the client-chosen step, and left the larger quitting decision genuinely open.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All change arguments came from Elena herself (guilt about the car, past success, curiosity about next steps); the clinician's role was to notice, reflect, and amplify rather than persuade.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly located expertise within Elena ("that was your idea, your insight") and used open questions that let her generate the change herself; she confirmed experiencing the power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and amplified commitment language ("that one I could actually do"), consolidated it by asking how it felt to say, and the change talk generalized spontaneously ("maybe in a month," telling her sister).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged via double-sided reflections and redirected toward doable change without arguing; minor deduction for the "you've earned those ten minutes" reflection briefly validating the sustain side.
