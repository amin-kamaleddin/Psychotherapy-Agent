# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard toward a guarded, externally motivated client—no judgment despite repeated minimization, and respected his refusal of roleplay. Viktor's gradual softening suggests he felt genuinely accepted rather than managed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate double-sided reflections captured Viktor's ambivalence well, but missed opportunities to reflect emotion around peer judgment ('calls me soft, I might cave') and the 'Friday me' conflict. Problem-solving sometimes came before deeper emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on weekend drinking and its marital impact, steadily advancing toward a concrete experiment. Deducted for occasional rapid-fire option checklists that derailed conversational momentum and briefly overstepped with the alcohol-free night suggestion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Viktor modifying options and selecting his own allies and deflection lines. Some turns had advisory density, delivering menus of options rather than emerging from joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent autonomy support—explicitly framing choices as Viktor's, accepting all his terms (one Friday, no announcements, no promises), and never pushing. His ownership of the plan reflects choice genuinely retained by the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew authentic motivation from Viktor—peace at home, better Saturday mornings, fixing the garage. Deducted for supplying deflection lines directly when asked, rather than first evoking Viktor's own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Viktor as the expert, and his own ideas genuinely shaped the plan. Occasional rapid-fire option menus verged on the consultant/expert role rather than open-ended inquiry.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and developed meaningful change language about fights easing and a better Saturday morning. Deducted for moving to concrete planning quickly rather than deepening desire, need, and confidence statements first.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally handled sustain talk well through brief acknowledgment, redirection, and effective double-sided reflections. The alcohol-free night proposal exceeded Viktor's readiness and unnecessarily invited pushback that a smaller step would have avoided.
