# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental, validating Elena's efforts and explicitly countering her feeling of being blamed. Respected her modest goals without disapproval and praised her realistic slip plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured both content and emotion, including the guilt, the meaning of smoking as her only quiet time, and her fairness concerns. Pace was adjusted smoothly when she resisted bigger commitments.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on smoking and her son's health, progressing logically to a concrete two-week experiment with tracking and a relapse plan. The ultimate goal of quitting was deferred to a harm-reduction subgoal at Elena's request.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as equal partners, with every decision (replacement strategy, tracking method, next-session agenda) co-created. Elena explicitly appreciated the non-lecturing approach.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support, with explicit choice from the opening and Elena's limits accepted without pressure. Slips were normalized and her ownership of outcomes reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Elena's own motivations, including her son's health and self-respect from public commitment. Deeper exploration of desire and long-term vision for full quitting was not pursued.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elena visibly drove the session, choosing the focus, proposing the car goal, selecting mints over NRT, and setting next-session agenda. Clinical information was offered only when she asked why the car mattered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited change talk via a confidence rating and the thirdhand smoke explanation, converting ambivalence into concrete commitment language. Focus remained on a modest harm-reduction step, with brief confidence exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and was consistently pivoted toward what was doable, aided by reframing information. Sustain talk received several full-sentence reflections before redirection, though this was arguably needed to reduce defensiveness.
