# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations and a promised nonjudgmental check-in; client even thanked the psychiatrist for not lecturing. Withheld from 5 as warmth was somewhat brief and the counselor didn't deeply normalize Daniel's defensiveness about being flagged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured Daniel's ambivalence, values, and wish for facts over lectures. Reflections were occasionally thin, multiple questions were stacked in the opening, and the emotional layer of feeling accused wasn't deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear directional arc from exploring alcohol's fit with goals, through rulers, to a concrete one-week tracking experiment with defined logistics and check-in. Stacked early questions briefly diffused focus, and broader longer-term next steps weren't summarized.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent joint framing with permission requests and choices; Daniel's contributions (tracking, alarm, summary format) genuinely shaped the plan. Much of the scaffolding (template fields, metrics, cadence) was proposed pre-assembled and mostly ratified rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength: explicit permission-seeking, an option menu Daniel could freely reject, honoring his request for self-generated conclusions, and inviting him to set his own check-in time. Client confirmed the approach 'made it easier to say yes.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from Daniel via rulers, open questions, and linking alcohol to his own race and sleep goals, eliciting meaningful change talk. Sometimes supplied hypotheses himself and didn't persistently ask Daniel to articulate his motivations in his own words.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively positioned Daniel as the expert, sought permission, offered options, and honored his request to draw his own conclusions. Some structure (templates, exact metrics, reminder texts) was supplied with less negotiation than would fully transfer power.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk through rulers and value-linking; Daniel generated statements about race pace, sleep, and the experiment being potentially useful. Missed the chance to deepen his readiness statement ('I guess I'd have to think about it') into commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never argued with; instead it was neutrally reflected and pivoted toward data-driven exploration tied to Daniel's goals. Softening relied mostly on neutral reflection rather than active reframing or double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
