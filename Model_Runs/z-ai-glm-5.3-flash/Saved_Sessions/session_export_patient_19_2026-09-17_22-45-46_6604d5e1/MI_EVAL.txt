# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm and nonjudgmental throughout (e.g., 'It's not pathetic at all — telling someone about the debt takes guts'). Affirmations lean formulaic ('I love how...') and shame/relapse normalization could deepen unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflections capturing both sides of ambivalence (Egypt hope alongside fear of losing friends). Reflections often pivot toward the next question rather than pausing on deeper emotional content like shame or abandonment fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, steady focus on gambling reduction with a coherent arc from one-year vision to concrete action steps. Each question advances momentum without forcing or drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — open questions invite Anya to shape the session, and the plan is entirely built from her ideas. Counselor acts as a thinking partner rather than an authority.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Anya's ownership ('that's two, both your own ideas'; 'you're not waiting to be fixed'). Never prescribes solutions; frames accountability as her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn entirely from within the client — vision, reasons, confidence strategies, and the meaning of follow-through all come from Anya. Questions like 'what would it mean to you to have one person in your corner?' deepen her own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Anya as the expert ('You know yourself best'). Every plan element — texting Jess, cutting cards, the jar, the photo — originates from the client and is amplified by the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk, from highlighting commitments to having Anya articulate the text in her own words. Her language escalates from tentative ('worth a try, maybe') to firm plans ('the scissors come out').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without amplifying it and redirects toward solutions ('What would it mean to you to have one person in your corner?'). Could use more deliberate double-sided reflections to actively resolve ambivalence rather than lightly acknowledging it.
