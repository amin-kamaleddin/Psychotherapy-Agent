# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained respectful, nonjudgmental regard despite Kieran's dismissiveness and apathy. Affirmations were somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized (e.g., missed a chance to affirm his 15 weekly job applications).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening capturing both content and emotional resignation ('It sounds exhausting and pointless'). Occasionally stayed at surface-level frustration rather than exploring deeper feelings like hurt and hopelessness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus from start to finish—each turn built logically toward an actionable outcome with no topic drift. Session ended with a feasible plan (trial with screenshots plus weekly check-in).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session was genuinely two-way; Kieran's preferences (text over calls, proof-first ordering, gentle role-play start) visibly shaped the plan. Counselor even took a subordinate role playing the caseworker.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was explicit and strong, with 'You're in charge' reinforced throughout and repeated choice offerings. The caseworker request was framed as Kieran's own low-risk choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew motivation from Kieran himself through open questions, surfacing his own reasons and solutions. Evocation sometimes centered on procedural compliance rather than deeper values, and confidence was not explicitly explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Kieran as the expert and incorporated his contributions directly into the session. Some offers were somewhat binary, and drafts were counselor-generated rather than co-authored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change language, moving Kieran from 'doesn't matter anyway' to concrete preferences and commitments ('maybe I'd actually pay attention,' 'I can live with that'). Occasionally channeled toward compliance logistics rather than Kieran's broader goals.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged Kieran's sustain talk briefly without amplifying it, then pivoted to low-risk experimentation (role-play, trial agreement). Occasionally sustain talk was passed over rather than reflected.
