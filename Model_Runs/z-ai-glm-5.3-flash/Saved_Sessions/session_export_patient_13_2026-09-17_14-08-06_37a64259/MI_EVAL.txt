# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout; thanked the patient for her "brave share," acknowledged her fear without criticism, and honored her pace (agreed to a gentle Friday check-in rather than pressure). Warmth is somewhat transactional at times, and the early rapid-fire screening block could feel evaluative rather than warmly accepting.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some effort to understand ("I'm sorry that's been so scary," "that fear is real"), but reflections are few and shallow. The disclosure "once I make that call, it becomes real" warranted a deeper reflection about fear of confirmation; missed opportunities to mirror her self-criticism and her surprise at her own progress.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on seeking medical care for physical changes; progressed from clinical information-gathering to a concrete, time-bound action (PCP message with Friday 10:00 check-in). Initial question block was broad and screening-oriented rather than MI-directed; brief shift to anxiety-coping before returning to focus.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission, checked preferences repeatedly, and co-constructed the plan (patient adjusted message content, set the deadline, shaped the reminder text). The actual content (drafted message, specific labs, reminder format) was largely counselor-generated, with the patient only editing.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices rather than directives; accepted ambivalence without pressure. Patient explicitly affirmed this: "thank you — for not pushing too hard." Falls short of 5 because options were pre-formulated and the counselor did not explicitly affirm her right to decide not to change.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation came more from structured options than from drawing out the patient's own reasons and capacities. "I want to feel normal in my own body again" was never reflected or built upon; no open questions about her values or hopes. Change talk was evoked indirectly through small-step scaffolding.

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions consistently framed as the patient's; her contributions clearly shaped the session (chose breathing, set Friday 10 AM, requested reminder wording, declined name completion). Falls short of 5 because the counselor often supplied the agenda and ready-made options first rather than asking what she thought might work.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Small, manageable options effectively evoked meaningful change talk ("maybe I might be able to do that," "Friday. I can try for Friday," "now I'm... actually thinking I might. That's new for me"), which the counselor reinforced. Change talk was rarely deepened or reflected back to strengthen it.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ("it just seems overwhelming," "part of me just wants to shut the whole thing out," "I can't promise anything") was acknowledged non-judgmentally without arguing, but not strategically softened — no double-sided reflections pairing her fear with her stated desire to feel normal, and no reframing of avoidance.

---

### Overall Global Rating
**Overall Score: 3.7/5**

**Summary:** Solid MI fundamentals demonstrated: a safe, nonjudgmental atmosphere; consistent autonomy support; strong direction toward a concrete behavioral goal; and a co-constructed, realistic plan that elicited genuine change talk, culminating in the patient's recognition that "now I'm actually thinking I might." Key strengths: graded choices, honoring the client's pace, and reinforcing ownership of final steps. Improvement areas: (1) deepen empathy with richer reflections, especially of the fear underlying avoidance; (2) increase true evocation of the client's values and reasons for change; (3) actively soften sustain talk with double-sided reflections; (4) slow early pacing, replacing the rapid screening block with open exploration.
