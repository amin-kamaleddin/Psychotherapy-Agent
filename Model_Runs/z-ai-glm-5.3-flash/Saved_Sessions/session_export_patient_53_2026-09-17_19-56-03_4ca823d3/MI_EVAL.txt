# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, e.g., 'I'm not here to judge that' and 'That part of you matters, even if it's small right now.' Tone remains warm and respectful even with hesitant answers like 'Not that I'm going Thursday.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate, deep reflections track both sustain talk and underlying desire, e.g., 'Staying home is safe and familiar, and at the same time there's a part of you that wishes things were different.' The creative 'Wednesday-night Isabella' reframe was explicitly embraced by the client.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear, progressive focus on attending a community activity, building from ambivalence to a concrete next step (viewing the website tonight). No topic drift; each question guides toward the goal while remaining client-paced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Planning is framed as joint problem-solving ('So let's think together—what would be helpful to find out?'), but ideas come from Isabella and are affirmed by the counselor. The interaction functions between equal partners throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced with invitations like 'What would feel doable, not overwhelming?' When the client says 'It's not a promise, though,' the counselor honors that without pressure, and affirms her ownership of the decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within the client via open questions ('What would small actually look like to you?', 'What would be different about you after?'), eliciting her own reasons for change. The counselor never argues for change or supplies reasons, only amplifies what the client offers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located within the client ('You know your life better than anyone,' 'That's your insight, not mine'). The client self-generates the action step, with the counselor merely reflecting it back—a hallmark of strong power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor skillfully evokes and amplifies change talk, from the library painting class idea to commitment-adjacent language: 'Tonight, maybe... First time I've said that out loud.' The client's own investment is highlighted ('yet you remembered the day, looked it up').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('Home is easier,' 'Don't want to buy paints and then never go back') is consistently acknowledged without reinforcement and gently redirected toward change. Double-sided reflections and affirming reframes ensure sustain talk never dominates.
