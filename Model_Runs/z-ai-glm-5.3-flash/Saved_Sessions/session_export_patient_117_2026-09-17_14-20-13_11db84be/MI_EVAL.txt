# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with encouraging statements throughout. However, warmth is generic rather than individualized, with missed opportunities to affirm Samuel's specific efforts, strengths, and fears of letting himself down again.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; the counselor responds to emotionally meaningful disclosures (fear of failure, self-criticism after slips, his wife's concern) with advice and logistics instead of reflections. There are essentially no accurate simple or complex reflections in the entire session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing smoking at work with no topic drift. Direction occasionally tips into excess, however, as the same instructions are restated verbatim three or four times at the session's end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is heavily unbalanced toward counselor expertise, with bulleted advice in nearly every turn and Samuel's responses mostly compliance ('got it', 'plan's locked in'). The counselor never asks what Samuel thinks would work, explore prior coping attempts, or negotiate the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers nominal choices (picking a strategy, scheduling the check-in) and uses autonomy-supportive framing like 'info, not failure'. The overall style remains prescriptive, though, with strategies presented as a directive package and Samuel's ownership of the change process minimally emphasized.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor never asks about Samuel's reasons for change, what success would mean, or his confidence, instead supplying external techniques and accountability. The one evocative move — the breathing practice with 'Tell me what you notice' — was well executed but is the only example in the session.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role with prescriptive advice lists, asking only token permission-style questions. Samuel's contributions shape the plan somewhat, but he is not explicitly honored as the expert on his own habits, triggers, or work environment.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Samuel generates abundant spontaneous change talk ('the most hopeful I've felt about quitting in years', 'I finally feel like I've got a shot'), but the counselor rarely evokes or deepens it. These statements are met with summaries or advice rather than reinforcing questions or reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately avoids reinforcing sustain talk ('Monday mornings are the worst', 'I don't know if I can hold it'), but also does not actively soften or reframe it. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections that would connect Samuel's doubts to his demonstrated success (down two cigarettes/day).
