# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, honoring Marcus's nonnegotiable dog boundary and normalizing his skepticism. However, the dog boundary was met with practical reframing rather than deeper validation of its emotional significance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects Marcus's overwhelm, financial strain, and determination ('overwhelmed by appointments, strapped for cash... determined not to lose your dog'). Reflections are sometimes brief and don't deeply explore the emotional meaning of the dog attachment or his frustration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains exceptionally clear focus on asthma management, advancing concrete steps (consolidated visits, phone script, inhaler check, action plan) without derailing. Ends with Marcus summarizing his own plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Jointly selects targets, co-develops the phone checklist responsive to Marcus's stated difficulty, and role-plays at his request. Some offerings (checklist, action plan explanation) are delivered as counselor-generated content rather than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary use of explicit choices and pace-respecting language throughout, never pressuring on the dog issue. Marcus's growing ownership ('I'll bring it... maybe ask about that spacer thing') reflects this consistent choice-affirming stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws out Marcus's values ('change or protect' elicited the dog) and self-generated commitments ('one call I can do'). Evocation weakens mid-session when the counselor provides substantial information rather than eliciting Marcus's own knowledge or confidence statements.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Marcus as decision-maker through choice questions and supports aligned with his self-identified goals. Some shifts toward a teaching mode occur without explicitly inviting Marcus's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk via structured choice questions, role-play rehearsal that built confidence ('way easier than I built it up in my head'), and linking health actions to the dog. Missed opportunity to deepen his inhaler insight into stronger commitment or confidence talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids arguing — immediately accepts the dog boundary and pivots to compatible options, and reframes doubt by normalizing and framing consolidation as 'a realistic first move.' No deliberate double-sided reflections used to actively resolve ambivalence.
