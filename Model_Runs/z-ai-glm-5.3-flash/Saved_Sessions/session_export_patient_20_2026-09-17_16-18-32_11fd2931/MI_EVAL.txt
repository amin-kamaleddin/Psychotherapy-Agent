# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor was consistently warm and nonjudgmental, using frequent affirmations and explicitly promising not to judge or 'pile on' if Brandon slips. Warmth was somewhat brief and formulaic, keeping this from a perfect score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Surface ambivalence was reflected accurately, but deeper emotional content—fear of judgment, pride, and the poignant 'nobody just... hears me out'—went unexplored, with reflections often pivoting quickly to logistics and options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear, consistent focus on the target behavior from the opening question through to a concrete commitment with a specific plan and follow-up. Every exchange advanced toward this goal without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The final plan was genuinely Brandon's, but the counselor repeatedly presented pre-packaged solutions with little invitation for him to generate his own ideas, making some exchanges feel like dispensing options rather than equal problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offered choices, respected Brandon's privacy preference, and accepted his own reminder phrase over suggested alternatives. The counselor's habit of supplying solutions and tweaks somewhat crowded out Brandon's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions elicited meaningful motivation ('part of me wants to prove I can do it,' 'that's new for me'), but these moments were met with logistical pivots rather than exploration. Session momentum relied on counselor-provided structure rather than motivation drawn from Brandon.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Brandon's contributions genuinely shaped the plan, showing respect for his expertise. However, the advice-dense style with rapid-fire menus of options felt more prescriptive than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor created conditions for change talk through rulers, plan elicitation, and commitment requests, and Brandon produced notable change statements. Opportunities to deepen this—such as exploring the confidence rating of 5 or elaborating on his curiosity about sleeping better—were missed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing or debating severity, briefly acknowledging Brandon's minimization and redirecting toward his own curiosity and practical steps. However, redirection sometimes jumped too quickly to action planning rather than helping Brandon resolve ambivalence himself.
