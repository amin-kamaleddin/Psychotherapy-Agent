# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'nice job doing this for your grandpa,' though warmth occasionally felt formulaic and early affirmations of Jordan's honesty were missing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately captured Jordan's ambivalence ('wanting autonomy but uneasy about possibly hurting your grandparents'), but deeper emotions like 'He's like my best friend' were passed over quickly.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the vaccination target throughout, with no topic drift and a logical progression from ambivalence to a concrete, time-bound plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Plan elements were co-created (pharmacy choice, text to Mom), but the counselor frequently offered unsolicited solutions, and Jordan pushed back ('neither, honestly'), signaling an uneven dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered menus of choice with no pressure or coercion, though the volume of options felt somewhat controlling and Jordan's ultimate decision authority was never explicitly affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The well-timed question about making his grandfather sick elicited intrinsic motivation and pivotal change talk, but outside that sequence the counselor leaned on information-giving rather than drawing out Jordan's own reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session repeatedly slipped into an expert-driven mode with evidence summaries, a six-step plan, and drafted scripts that Jordan did not request, positioning him as a recipient rather than a partner.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The visionary question effectively elicited commitment language ('I should get it for them,' 'I could probably book it this week'), but these statements were not systematically deepened or reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally, but the counselor early on invited and amplified it ('say more about why you don't think you need the shot') then shifted into corrective/educational mode, and end-of-session sustain talk went unreframed.
