# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and offers practical help, but warmth feels transactional and never affirms Jamal's honesty or normalizes his frustration.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are minimal paraphrases of wanting a quick process; emotional cues like Jamal's defensiveness, his mother's stress, and his fear about the coach are missed and never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifts from MI toward administrative case management of citations and court steps, never developing discrepancy about the marijuana use itself despite Jamal's basketball priority.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites preferences and adjusts scripts to requests, but acts mainly as an information provider while Jamal merely selects among options rather than shaping the session's substance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong procedural autonomy with 'You're in charge,' menus of options, and respect for Jamal's choices, though autonomy is limited to logistics rather than the deeper change decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor never asks about Jamal's motivations or values; the meaningful basketball-related shift emerged incidentally rather than being deliberately evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers of choices and preferences suggest some partnership, but the counselor functions as a checklist provider rather than treating Jamal as the expert on his own change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal produced genuine change talk tied to basketball eligibility late in the session, but this was incidental rather than strategically evoked or reinforced by the counselor.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk dominates and goes largely unaddressed; the counselor even accommodates it ('Totally reasonable'), reinforcing the status quo rather than developing discrepancy.
