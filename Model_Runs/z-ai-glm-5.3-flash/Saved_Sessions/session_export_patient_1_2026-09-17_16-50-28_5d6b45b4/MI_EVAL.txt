# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with consistent validation of Ahmed's perspective ('that makes sense,' 'that sounds fair'). Affirmations were brief and formulaic, missing explicit affirmation of his strengths and honest engagement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured Ahmed's core experience, including his desire for proof and skepticism. Reflections were occasionally shallow, missing deeper emotional meaning such as the significance of his wife's nagging and identity tied to work.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on tobacco use and gum/throat health with no topic drift. Every exchange tied back to the target behavior, converging on a specific, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint enterprise — Ahmed chose the walk, day, format, note content, and check-in time. The counselor's responsiveness to Ahmed's requests showed his contributions directly shaped the session output.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission before sharing information, offered choices, and accepted Ahmed's refusal to quit outright. Fell short because the counselor proposed specific replacement behaviors (walk, tea) rather than first eliciting Ahmed's own alternatives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evocative questions drew out some of Ahmed's own reasons (wife, relaxation value, openness to testing alternatives). Motivation was more often supplied externally via risk education and suggested experiments rather than elicited from Ahmed's values and own ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Ahmed as the decision-maker, asking permission and letting him shape the concrete plan. The informational content and experiment suggestions largely originated from the counselor rather than being co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good evocative questions successfully elicited commitments (smoke-free home, Thursday walk, reading the leaflet). Change talk was not deepened — Ahmed's stated reasons were not amplified, and no confidence/importance scaling occurred.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with Ahmed's minimization and used some reframing, but his sustain talk received substantial airtime without consistent pivoting back to change. Double-sided reflections linking sustain talk to his own change statements were absent.
