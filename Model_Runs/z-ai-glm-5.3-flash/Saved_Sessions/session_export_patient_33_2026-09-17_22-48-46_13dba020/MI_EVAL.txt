# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently shows unconditional positive regard, never judging Diego's continued use or his decision not to quit, and explicitly affirms his self-knowledge. The client's closing feedback ('you didn't flinch... you actually listened') confirms he experienced genuine acceptance and warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, complex reflections appear throughout, capturing both the relief function of heroin and Diego's core pattern of shutting down. Each reflection deepens insight, as evidenced by Diego realizing aloud that reaching out had actually helped.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains consistent focus on heroin use and harm reduction, moving logically from exploration to planning to commitment without drift. Every question ties back to the central goal of keeping Diego alive and safer.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operates between equal partners, with the plan fully co-constructed from Diego's own ideas and the psychiatrist responding to his requests as a resource rather than an authority. The agenda emerges entirely from Diego's stated priorities.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly frames choices as Diego's own, affirms his self-designed plan, and explicitly accepts his 'I'm not ready to talk about quitting.' Diego recognizes this: 'Nobody's making me do this. I chose it.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly every turn is an open question drawing out Diego's own reasons, resources, and solutions — he generates all the content, including the check-in system and readiness rating. The psychiatrist never lectures or supplies reasons for change, instead amplifying Diego's own words.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positions Diego as the expert ('You know your life and your use better than anyone'), and the client's own contributions directly shape the plan. Diego's closing feedback confirms effective power sharing: 'treated like someone who can figure his own life out.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoke Diego's own commitments, culminating in strong client-generated change talk ('10 out of 10, I'm ready,' 'maybe this safety stuff is a start of something bigger'). The psychiatrist deliberately amplifies and reflects Diego's emerging language back to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced and is generally reflected briefly in service of balance, with pivots toward safety goals. However, the psychiatrist occasionally restates the relief function of heroin and does not use explicit double-sided reflections to actively resolve ambivalence — Diego largely resolves it himself.
