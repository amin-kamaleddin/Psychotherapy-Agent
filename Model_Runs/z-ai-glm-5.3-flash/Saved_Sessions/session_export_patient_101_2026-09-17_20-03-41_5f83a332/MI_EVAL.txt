# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with frequent, specific affirmations. Imperfect nights were normalized without any judgment or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening captured both sides of ambivalence and emotional nuance accurately throughout. Reflections were complex and built on the client's own words.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on alcohol reduction and contraceptive reliability, progressing strategically toward a concrete plan. Ended with a defined follow-up in one month.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine partnership with the client's ideas driving every decision and the counselor never prescribing. Summaries faithfully integrated the client's own language.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice was reinforced throughout, with open questions rather than directives and no pressure applied. Successes were explicitly framed as the client's own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within, eliciting the client's own reasons, strategies, confidence ratings, and definition of success. The client's closing realization confirmed successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client, and the entire change plan originated from her. The counselor's role was to reflect and amplify rather than direct.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully elicited substantial change talk and the client's own reasons and plans. Confidence was tracked numerically, growing from a 4 to a 6.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally met with pivots toward the client's own insights rather than amplification. A brief exploration of worry/barrier language ('what makes it feel risky') spent some time on barriers instead of actively pivoting toward change.
