# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with genuine specific affirmations and immediate ownership of her pushback rather than defensiveness. Honored her limits (crisis line, pills) without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Recovered exceptionally well from an opening misattunement; the subsequent complex reflection visibly moved the conversation forward and captured deep emotional nuance. Deduction for the initial miss and occasionally pivoting to questions before reflections fully landed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on suicidal ideation, means, and coping, with strategic summaries to resolve ambivalence. Only misstep was the opening misattunement requiring redirection.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maria's ideas (voicemail, paper by the bed, painting) became the actual intervention content via co-construction and permission-seeking ('Could we...?'). Agenda-setting wasn't always explicitly negotiated, e.g., risk questions were asked rather than offered.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly framed ideas as the client's own and explicitly respected her limits, including non-coercive framing around lethal means and the crisis line. Minor deduction for gentle incremental persuasion ('Could we put that number in your phone too?').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Drew out Maria's own reasons (sister, niece, painting) rather than supplying them, with the closing exchange yielding genuinely evoked meaning. Some suggestions remained therapist-originated and some despair was left undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned Maria as the expert, and her contributions substantially shaped the session. On a few occasions the therapist supplied the frame (crisis number, code word) before she contributed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicited and developed Maria's fledgling change talk about wanting things to be different for her sister. Evocative questions were skillfully built on her own material, including what her note paper would say.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided amplifying heavy sustain talk, instead pivoting toward protective and hopeful elements. Minor deduction for reflecting some sustain talk verbatim in summaries.
