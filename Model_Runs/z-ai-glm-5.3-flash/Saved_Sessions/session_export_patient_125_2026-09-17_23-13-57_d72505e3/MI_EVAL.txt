# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout ('you're still the same person who's earned respect here') with genuine, individualized warmth and no judgment, even when the patient blames staff.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captures emotional weight ('feels crushing — and unfair') and vulnerability ('that gap is scary'), but reflections sometimes stay summative rather than exploring deeper pain (separation from his father, anxiety beneath his anger).

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target issue and moves the session progressively from validation to options, contingency planning, and a concrete action plan with follow-up, without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly frames the work as joint ('let's put our heads together') and co-constructs the plan — Tariq writes the request while the counselor offers to review and vouch, a genuine partnership of equals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirms the patient's ownership and choice ('what feels like the right first move for you?', 'you got there yourself'), offering suggestions as options rather than imposed advice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every major idea and commitment originates from the patient via evocative open questions; no lecturing or arguing for change, confirmed by the patient's closing gratitude for not being lectured.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the counselor as backup rather than director ('how can I back you up?'), with the patient's contributions substantially shaping the session plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces change talk, highlighting the patient's own motivations ('I hear real hope in that... you got there yourself'), leading to concrete commitments ('I'm gonna write it tonight').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Redirects blame and fear talk toward agency and problem-solving effectively, though occasionally restates the unfairness framing without a change-oriented counterweight.
