# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, using specific, genuine affirmations ('that honesty is what makes plans stick') and validating Andrei's frustration with time pressure rather than challenging it. No judgment or disapproval appeared at any point, even amid the client's initial irritability.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, with the opening reflection ('back-to-back calls, deadlines, eating whatever's quick at your desk') precisely capturing the client's world. The reframing that 'the problem isn't knowledge; it's that your days leave no room to breathe' was confirmed by the client ('exactly'), and subtle cues like 'I'm not saying no' were skillfully reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on the target behavior from start to finish, progressing logically from barriers to a concrete plan and follow-up. The counselor guided each step without drifting off-topic, even absorbing the parking complaint gracefully.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was fully co-constructed, with ideas emerging from genuine dialogue — the client proposed, the counselor reflected and asked, the client refined. Andrei's closing thanks 'for actually listening instead of just telling me things' confirms the collaborative experience from his perspective.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was exemplary: no advice was imposed, every choice point was returned to the client, and the counselor explicitly removed performance pressure ('whether it's four mornings or one, I'll take your honest report however it comes'). Andrei responded by taking increasing ownership of the plan ('the deal is the deal').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited entirely from within the client rather than supplied, with open questions drawing out his own reasons and solutions — and visible growth from 'nothing fits' to 'probably four' mornings. The counselor never lectured, a point the client explicitly praised.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned Andrei as the expert from the first utterance, and every plan element (stairs, mornings-only, escape hatch, coffee routine) originated from him. Power-sharing was so effective that the client's contributions substantially shaped the entire intervention.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully and consistently evoked, producing concrete commitment language ('definitely the stairs,' 'probably four [mornings]'). The strategic use of the client's own change talk replayed back as a motivational mirror was an especially advanced technique.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('nothing fits,' 'a drop in the ocean,' 'my doctor would probably laugh') was consistently acknowledged without debate, then redirected toward agency and possibility. Sustain talk steadily diminished across the session — a strong indicator of effective softening.
