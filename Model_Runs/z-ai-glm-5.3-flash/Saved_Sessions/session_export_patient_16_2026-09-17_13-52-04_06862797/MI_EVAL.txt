# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with repeated affirmations of Anika's honesty. Warmth is somewhat formulaic, and off-target suicide/self-harm screening questions misattuned with her presentation and briefly undermined acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes some accurate reflections (relaxation/social benefits, annoyance at the school referral). However, checklist-driven screening questions clearly mismatched her presentation, and her deeper ambivalence ('what happens after the month?') was rarely reflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays coherently focused on vaping, moving from use patterns to triggers to a concrete if/then plan and follow-up. Not a 5 because early off-target safety screening derailed focus and long-term goals after the month remain vague.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans are genuinely co-created, with Anika's preferences shaping strategy selection and the check-in format. The power balance tilts toward the counselor during bulleted advice lists, where she picks from pre-packaged options rather than generating them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices, supports her modification of the plan (garage instead of parent holding the pen), and frames quitting as a trial she controls. Occasionally prescribes solutions via advice lists rather than fostering her ownership of generating options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Implies motivation resides in the client and affirms her spontaneous change talk (basketball motivation, 7/10 confidence), but explicit evocative techniques are sparse. Never explores importance, confidence, or values to deliberately elicit and deepen change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly positions expertise with the client, inviting her to co-build the if/then plan and accepting her modifications. Falls short because much of the session follows an advice-delivery format with Anika reacting to counselor content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges largely spontaneously and the psychiatrist affirms and builds on it (trial framing, rewards, check-ins). Rarely deliberately evokes or deepens it—no importance exploration, elaboration questions, or strategic exploration of her 7/10 confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not reinforce sustain talk, which is a strength, but also does not actively soften it. Key sustain statements (worries it won't hurt her, whether the plan is just a season break) receive brief normalization followed by advice lists rather than double-sided reflections.
