# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a neutral, respectful stance without judging or labeling Derrick, but warmth is minimal and formulaic, with no genuine affirmations of strengths or normalization of his relapse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; meaningful emotional disclosures (feeling like an outcast, breakup, feeling like a ghost) are met with logistics and scripts rather than reflection. Only one genuine empathic summary ('trying to stay safe without losing your friends').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on the target behaviors (drinking and driving, high-risk contexts, DUI prevention) with concrete, actionable goals throughout. Focus stays on harm-reduction logistics rather than deepening motivation, but direction is competent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration exists (asks what Derrick wants, lets him choose options and refine scripts), but the counselor generates nearly all solutions and doesn't build on Derrick's own ideas like cab money or the gym.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some choice (session focus, which option to try), but the overall style is advice-heavy with back-to-back prescriptive plans that subtly impose the counselor's agenda rather than fostering Derrick's ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Operates from an advice-giving model with virtually no open questions to draw out Derrick's reasons, values, or confidence. Derrick volunteers substantial motivation on his own, which the counselor never explores or deepens.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Repeatedly assumes the expert role with bulleted plans, drafted texts, and scripts; Derrick's expertise about his friend group is underused and his corrections ('that won't work at the bar') are needed to make the counselor's suggestions workable.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Asks one motivation scaling question but never processes Derrick's answer of '5,' and repeatedly steps over spontaneous change talk ('I want my license back,' 'smarter to skip hanging with them') in favor of delivering more plan steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Derrick's sustain talk ('I don't really think I have a drinking problem,' 'it's just beer') is neither reinforced nor strategically softened; no double-sided reflections are used, so the ambivalence is never actively resolved.
