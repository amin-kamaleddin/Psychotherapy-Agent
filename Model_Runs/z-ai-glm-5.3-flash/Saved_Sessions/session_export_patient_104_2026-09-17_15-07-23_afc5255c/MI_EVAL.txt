# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated respect and warmth, normalizing exhaustion and reframing lowered standards without judgment. However, warmth was at times formulaic and deeper affirmations of the client's own capacities were limited.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was generally strong and accurate, e.g., capturing the fear behind 'You're scared you broke her.' Reflections sometimes stayed at surface content and missed deeper emotional themes like loneliness and fear of being a burden, which were met with advice rather than validation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a clear, consistent focus on target behaviors (infant sleep, maternal rest, support-seeking), with each turn ending in a focused next-step question. The session progressed logically from normalizing to sleep strategies to a concrete if-then plan with accountability.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was largely between equals, with permission-seeking, option-offering, and acceptance of the client's self-imposed limits shaping a co-constructed plan. The counselor occasionally assumed the expert role with tip lists and scripts rather than first inviting the client's own problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was consistently offered ('tips now or help sorting signs?', 'draft now or set the if-then plan?') with no pressure and respect for her pacing. Some advice-heavy turns with pre-written scripts diluted the client's sense of ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was drawn from the client ('maybe I'm not fine, actually'), but the counselor relied heavily on providing information and scripts. No confidence rulers, exploration of past attempts, or systematic eliciting of her own reasons and ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor positioned the client's choices as central, letting her contributions genuinely shape the plan (window sit, 20-minute alarm 'middle ground'). Some responses still slid into expert/advice mode without checking what she already knew or had tried.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew out commitment and planning language, converting curiosity into a concrete plan with clear change talk ('I can do tonight,' 'Three things. That's my limit'). Opportunities to affirm and deepen her change talk were sometimes skipped in favor of logistics.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk (despair about coping, guilt about being a burden, fear of napping) was neither reinforced nor strategically softened. Responses jumped to practical advice rather than using double-sided reflections or exploring the ambivalence.
