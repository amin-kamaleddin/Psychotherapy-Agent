# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and supportive tone throughout, but few explicit affirmations of Marcus's strengths, and praise is generic. Deeper unconditional positive regard around his ambivalence and self-doubt is not communicated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal reflective listening; Marcus's emotional content (overwhelm, fear of failure, attachment to his dog) is never reflected or validated. Responses pivot immediately to checklists, scripts, and options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Clear momentum on a concrete micro-goal (affording the inhaler, making the call, setting a reminder). However, direction is narrow and task-focused, never connecting to broader asthma management or the referral concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor generates nearly all content (scripts, checklists, reminders), with Marcus mostly agreeing. No joint agenda-setting or co-creation of the plan; the session reads as doing for rather than with.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongest area: choices repeatedly offered, permission consistently requested, and Marcus's pacing honored. Held below 5 because option-offering is mechanical and his ownership of the change is never affirmed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Weakest domain: no open questions exploring Marcus's reasons, values, or vision of success; motivation is outsourced to external tools (scripts, reminders, a coffee reward). His own emerging motivation statements go undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Fundamentally expert-driven stance supplying scripts and tasks rather than drawing on Marcus's expertise. His stated priorities (go slowly, one thing at a time, address the dog later) do not shape the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk emerges incidentally from Marcus but is passed over in favor of offering the next task. No evocative questions or deepening of his hopeful statements about ability, desire, or commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ('one call isn't gonna fix everything,' 'good intentions that fall apart') is neither reflected nor softened. No double-sided reflections, reframes, or affirmations to resolve ambivalence.
