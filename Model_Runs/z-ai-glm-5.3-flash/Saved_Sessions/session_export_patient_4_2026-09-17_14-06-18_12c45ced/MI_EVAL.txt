# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental despite the patient's initial anger and defensiveness; validated her self-advocacy and fear of cancer. Fell short of top marks due to warmth that was efficient rather than individualized and limited explicit affirmation of strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered accurate acknowledgments ('exhausting and scary'), but the session was heavily procedural, leaving little room for reflective listening. Emotionally significant disclosures—nighttime loneliness, fear of normal results, her sister's remark—met with checklists rather than complex reflections of feeling.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, concrete agenda (referral, diary, script) anchored to the client's own concern without forcing psychological interpretation. Direction was driven by task completion rather than guiding toward behavior change, and the emerging stress-symptom connection was never framed as a focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Polite and consultative with check-ins before adding content, but not truly between equals—the counselor generated nearly all clinical content while the patient approved wording. Never invited her own ideas about what might help or jointly built a plan from her knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and respected the patient's boundaries, even adapting referral wording to avoid 'stress.' Short of top marks because coping strategies and diary requirements were delivered as directives, with little reinforcement that she owns the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Gave the patient space to voice her own insights, and meaningful change talk emerged ('I'll mention it to the specialist myself'). However, evocation was passive—rarely using open questions to draw out her motivations, hopes, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for the patient's input on options, conveying some power-sharing, but the session's substance was expert-driven with long lists and prescribed scripts. Little acknowledgment that she herself held expertise about her condition.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully created conditions for self-generated change talk by legitimizing trigger reporting; the patient voiced clear intent and growing insight. Missed opportunities to deepen these statements deliberately with evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing resistance, using the patient's biomedical values as a bridge and carefully framing symptom-trigger data as evidence for the specialist. Did not use double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence and occasionally left sustain talk unaddressed.
