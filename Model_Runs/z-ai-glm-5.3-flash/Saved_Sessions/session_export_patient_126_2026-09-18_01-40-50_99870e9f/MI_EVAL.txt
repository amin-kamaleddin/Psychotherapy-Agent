# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer consistently affirmed the patient's autonomy ('you know your life best'), validated his perspectives without judgment, and respected his reluctance about the checkup and the doctor's approach.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections repeatedly captured both surface content and underlying ambivalence, including subtle meanings like the embarrassment over rough mornings and the fear of not being a good father.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation moved purposefully from initial minimization toward the patient's own concerns about drinking, using his family goals and weekend mornings as the organizing focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer positioned the patient as the expert, invited his own take on the doctor's findings, and consistently elicited rather than prescribed solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change emerged from the patient's own values and self-generated ideas (driving, telling his wife), with the interviewer explicitly crediting him for knowing what would work.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully drew out the patient's own motivations: being present for his son, not becoming like his dad, quitting smoking as self-efficacy evidence, and his wife's support.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient was highly engaged, generating his own plan and confidence rating; the interview was fully collaborative, though at times the interviewer's reflections slightly led the framing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions about the father he wants to be in twenty years and what would need to change elicited sustained change talk, including commitments ('I could mention the driving thing to my wife tonight') and reasons for confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'm not some monk,' confidence of five) was reflected non-judgmentally and paired with change talk using double-sided reflections and 'five, not zero' reframing, though some minimization early on was only lightly explored.
