# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm and nonjudgmental throughout, with consistent affirmations. However, affirmations were generic ('that definitely counts') rather than deeply individualized to Marco's specific efforts and relapse history.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately captured Marco's core dilemma and trigger ('tired Thursdays + lasagna = slip point'). Reflections were often quickly paired with advice and multiple questions, limiting depth of emotional understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on the target behavior, producing a concrete if-then plan (water on arrival, glass by the front door) with scheduled follow-up. Late-session questions about Sunday grazing opened new problem territory rather than consolidating the plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructed the plan and follow-up agenda, with Marco contributing key ideas like the daughter as accountability. Option lists were counselor-generated rather than brainstormed together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Marco's ownership with repeated choice without pressure. Honored his decision to keep just water this week and to defer the Sunday changes without pushing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence scaling question produced useful change talk (an '8'), but overall the counselor relied on offering menus of ideas rather than drawing out Marco's own solutions. Missed the opportunity to explore what would raise a confidence of six to an eight.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited Marco's input and let him choose his strategy, but frequently adopted an expert posture with pre-formulated option lists and tips. Marco's own expertise was not explicitly recognized.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged (past success, confidence of 8, 'I think I can do one week'), but somewhat incidentally after the counselor supplied options. No deliberate evocative questions about why change matters or what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify sustain talk and offered a helpful reframe countering all-or-nothing thinking ('drink a glass as soon as you remember — no all-or-nothing'). However, some questions invited barrier talk, keeping focus on problems rather than pivoting to solutions.
