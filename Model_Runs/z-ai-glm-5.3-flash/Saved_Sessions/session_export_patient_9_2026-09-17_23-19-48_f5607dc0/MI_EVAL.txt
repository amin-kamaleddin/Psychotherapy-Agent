# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, unconditional positive regard throughout, with affirmations like 'That's no small insight' and non-judgmental normalization of her struggles; no labeling or disapproval present.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently accurate, emotionally attuned reflections capture both surface content and deeper meaning, such as framing the son's quiet as what weighs most and routines as safe ground versus a conversational minefield.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear focus on co-parenting communication for her son's wellbeing while following Amina's natural progression from routines to a concrete plan, with each turn connected back to the central concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint exploration: the counselor offers reflections and invites Amina's assessment at every step, with her contributions actively shaping the session's direction and eventual action plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly reinforces Amina's ownership and choice ('chosen by you,' 'no one's permission needed'); all decisions come from her, which she explicitly recognizes ('it came from me').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within via questions like 'what would need to shift before a conversation could feel safer?' No advice is imposed; Amina's reasons, resources, and plans emerge anchored in her love for her son.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Counselor explicitly positions Amina as the expert ('you know him best') and every element of the plan—note, sister, timing—originates from her, with questions consistently deferring to her wisdom.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and deliberately reinforced—highlighting 'I could try that' as her own decision—culminating in her concrete self-generated plan and commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor acknowledges worries briefly and pivots well (e.g., 'what would you need to feel like trying wasn't stupid?'). Some sustain talk about the father's dismissiveness and tone is reflected at length, slightly extending focus on obstacles.
