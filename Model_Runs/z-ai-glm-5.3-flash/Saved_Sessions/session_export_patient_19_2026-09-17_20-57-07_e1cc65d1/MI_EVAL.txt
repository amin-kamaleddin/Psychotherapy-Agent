# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard with frequent, specific affirmations ('That's you problem-solving', 'The stubborn Anya... she showed up today too'). No labeling, shaming, or moralizing about the gambling or theft history; doubt is validated without disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, timely reflective listening, including the opening ambivalence reflection and subtle naming of internal battles ('the voice saying just one quick go'). Reflections are emotionally attuned and capture both her fears and her resources.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior (gambling and its financial consequences) while remaining client-centered. Progress moves through vision, strategies, concrete steps, and success criteria via strategic evocative questions rather than steering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving: 'I want to build on it with you', asking permission to consolidate, and negotiating the follow-up plan. Anya's insights (rent vs. someday) become the session's conceptual centerpiece.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice consistently reinforced — her success threshold ('two out of three') is honored and ideas are offered as options, not directives. The closing invitation to review 'whatever the week looked like' fully supports her ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited almost entirely from the client; no arguing for change or supplying reasons. Her 'I actually kind of want to try. That's new' and 'I've never had a why before, only a shouldn't' demonstrate activated intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in the client ('You know your patterns better than anyone', 'That's you building this, not me'). The entire plan is composed of her own ideas, with the counselor actively naming this power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open evocative questions reliably draw out desire, reasons, and mobilizing change talk, with each burst reflected and deepened. Concrete commitments (leaving cards home, booked taxi, banking app background) emerge from the client herself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally met with brief acknowledgment followed by strong pivots to change ('I also hear you already sketching ways around it'). However, some questions directly invite sustain talk (biggest obstacle, the persuasive voice), which is well-handled but focuses time on the against-change side.
