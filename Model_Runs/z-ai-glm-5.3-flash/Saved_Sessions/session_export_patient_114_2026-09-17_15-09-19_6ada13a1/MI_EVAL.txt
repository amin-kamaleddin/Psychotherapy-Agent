# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with unconditional regard, validating Ryan's defensiveness rather than challenging it. However, warmth is somewhat formulaic ('That makes sense' repeatedly) and lacks individualized affirmations of his honesty and self-insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captures Ryan's ambivalence and the pain of his mom's disappointment. Reflections remain largely content-level, with little exploration of deeper emotion (e.g., the fear behind 'that scares me') and some recycling of the same themes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the alcohol use and conduct referral, moving logically from exploration to a concrete plan and rehearsal. Momentum occasionally stalls in repetitive A/B/C option checklists, and progress is not explicitly summarized or tied to Ryan's core value.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely a joint enterprise with permission-seeking before advice and a co-constructed role-play. Weakened by the counselor handing over complete plans and scripts rather than building them from Ryan's own ideas ('pacing myself,' 'knowing when to call it a night').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Ryan's ownership through choice of topics, scripts, and pacing, with advice offered only as options with explicit permission. Ryan visibly responds with increasing ownership ('I'm sticking to it').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers effectively moved Ryan's confidence from ~4 to 7, but the counselor frequently supplied reasons, strategies, and scripts rather than drawing them from the client. Ryan's degree-vs-drinking discrepancy was reflected but never actively evoked or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ryan is positioned as expert and chooses intervention targets, showing real power-sharing. Yet the counselor repeatedly provides menus of options and pre-written scripts before asking what Ryan already knows works.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness and confidence rulers successfully evoked change talk (readiness 4–5, confidence rising to 7). Evocation is inconsistent, with ready-made options offered instead of elaborative questions or strategic deepening of the confidence jump.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged neutrally without amplification, which is adequate. But it is rarely reframed toward Ryan's values, and the key ambivalent question ('is it?') was passed over with generic validation rather than used as an evocation opportunity.
