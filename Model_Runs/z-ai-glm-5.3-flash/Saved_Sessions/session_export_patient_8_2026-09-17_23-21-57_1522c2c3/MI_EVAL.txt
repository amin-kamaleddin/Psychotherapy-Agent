# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI
**Patient:** Amina Farouk – Social Media Overuse
**Evaluator:** Supervisor Review

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental, respectful stance. Strong affirmations ("It takes some courage to say that out loud," "That's a real insight, Amina"). Normalizes ambivalence ("Both make sense"). Fell short of a 5 because warmth occasionally leaned toward cheerleading, and there was no explicit moment of unconditional regard for the patient if she chose *not* to change at all.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening throughout. Excellent double-sided reflection capturing ambivalence (coping function vs. cost of 2am scrolling) and the nuanced reflection of the patient's own metaphor ("2am-you doesn't have to answer to morning-me"). However, the emotional layer beneath "I don't care about morning-me" (possible self-worth/self-trust wound) was never explicitly explored.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the target behavior with clear momentum: ambivalence → costs → function of behavior → small experiment → accountability → follow-up planning. No drift off-topic, never overly directive. The closing check-in question established a concrete next step.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Two equal partners co-constructing a plan. Clinician offered observations as curiosity; the final plan (charger placement, flatmate accountability, experiment mindset) was entirely patient-generated. Patient's closing line—"thinking about this like an experiment"—demonstrates ownership of problem-solving was successfully transferred. Exemplary.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly disclaimed control ("I'm not here to take your phone away"), reinforced ownership ("you'll have an answer"). Held at 4 due to mild leading ("So here's my thought…") and the clinician-generated "mine time earlier" reframe. Autonomy over the ultimate goal itself (reduce vs. manage) was left assumed rather than explicit.

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine belief that motivation resides within the patient; nearly every insight and plan element was elicited, not provided. Questions ("What did you learn about yourself?") drew out self-motivational insights ("I have to decide before I'm tired"). By session's end, the patient voiced intrinsic motivation ("proof I can actually follow through").

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated power sharing ("You know your situation best," "You know your life and your friends best"); patient's contributions clearly shaped the session. Fell short of a 5 because the clinician still subtly steered toward its own framing at times, though the "mine time" suggestion was skillfully offered as a question.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongest domain. Systematic evocation and reinforcement of change talk—patient's language progressed from sustain talk to clear commitment ("that's the one I'd actually try," "I'll have an answer"). Clinician consistently reflected and amplified change talk. Textbook-quality evocation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective double-sided reflections paired sustain talk with change talk without amplification; the "move the 'mine' time earlier" reframe elegantly softened sustain talk. Held at 4 because sustain talk occasionally received near-equal airtime, and ambivalence resolution was not strategically deepened (e.g., weighing the two selves explicitly).

---

**Overall Global Rating: 4.4/5**

**Summary:** A high-quality MI session demonstrating strong command of the MI spirit and core skills. Greatest strengths: evoking change talk, maintaining direction, and true collaboration. Areas for growth: (1) deepen emotional empathy around the self-trust wound beneath "I don't care about morning-me"; (2) allow the patient's material to lead rather than subtly steering via reframes; (3) explicitly affirm autonomy over the ultimate goal rather than assuming change.
