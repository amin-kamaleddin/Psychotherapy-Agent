# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with repeated genuine affirmations. However, affirmations often rely on echoing the client's own phrases rather than independent positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening, including an accurate complex reflection of her ambivalence about information sources. Reflections sometimes merely repeat her words back, and the client herself noted the echoing, signaling a lack of added depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session is well-structured around the target behavior (safe citalopram use), but momentum is lost mid-session with circular negotiation over the checklist format. The client had to intervene to get the conversation back on track.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided: the psychiatrist elicits the client's preferences and solutions, and her ideas visibly shape the session. Collaboration weakens during the unproductive format-negotiation loop and a shift toward information delivery.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently supports choice with no pressure to take the medication, and the safety question is posed collaboratively. However, options are offered as clinician-defined menus and there is no explicit affirmation of the client's ownership of the final decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist elicits the client's own solutions about trustworthy information (pharmacist leaflet, written questions), which is good evidence of evocation. However, motivation and confidence for the core behavior—starting the citalopram—is never explicitly evoked, with no importance or confidence questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly frames the client as the expert and her contributions clearly shape the session structure. Several turns became circular discussions about the checklist format without delivering it, and deeper client-driven exploration of the decision itself was not pursued.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions successfully elicit the client's own solutions for information-gathering. But evocation is inconsistent: her motivations, confidence, and commitment regarding actually starting the citalopram are never explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist does not amplify the client's hesitation and generally pivots toward her goal of being informed. There is little active softening, however—no double-sided reflections or strategic reframing of her reluctance as a strength.
