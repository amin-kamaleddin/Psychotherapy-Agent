# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, validating Tyler's reluctance and normalizing his desire for autonomy without disapproval of substance use. Warmth is somewhat procedural — few specific affirmations of Tyler's strengths like his honesty or willingness to rehearse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections early and later, but reflections are brief and often followed by stacked questions (four questions in one turn, which Tyler called out). Emotional layers beneath his defensiveness — fear of losing belonging, feeling betrayed — remain largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, steady thread from Tyler's chosen focus to goal, script, agreement, rehearsal, and timing, ending with a concrete workable plan. Direction drifted away from the referral target behavior of substance use, which is never meaningfully reconnected.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely between equals: Tyler's choices drive the agenda, his edits are incorporated, and he contributed the grade-based deal idea himself. The counselor tends to hand over finished scripts and plans rather than building them jointly, and Tyler initially perceived the counselor as having an agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** A genuine strength — consistent emphasis on choice, options over directives, and no preaching, which Tyler explicitly recognizes and appreciates. Counselor never asks permission before offering scripts/advice and misses chances to affirm Tyler's capacity for self-direction during his doubts.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is largely counselor-supplied through scripts, plans, and solutions rather than evoked from Tyler. His values (friends, autonomy) are never developed into change talk about substance use, which the counselor explicitly sidesteps with 'I'm not here to make you quit.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Inclusive language and repeated choice offerings honor Tyler's agency, and his contributions visibly shape the plan. The counselor frequently slips into an expert stance with pre-drafted scripts and multiple-choice menus that substitute counselor structure for co-creation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tyler generates his own solutions, rehearses the script, and shows tentative ownership ('I can do that'), showing effective elicitation around the mother-negotiation. No change talk is ever evoked about the actual target behavior — substance use — as the counselor avoids it entirely.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('It's my business what I do') is acknowledged without reinforcement or argument, which is good. No double-sided reflections are used, and the counselor sidesteps substance use rather than gently pivoting Tyler toward examining risk.
