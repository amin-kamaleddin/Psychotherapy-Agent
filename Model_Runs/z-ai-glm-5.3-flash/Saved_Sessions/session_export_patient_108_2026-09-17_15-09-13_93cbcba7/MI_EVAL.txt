# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with consistent affirmations and normalization of anxiety. Falls short of a 5 because affirmations are brief and formulaic, with little validation of the deeper emotional difficulty of prioritizing his own needs.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, well-landed reflections of ambivalence early on ('torn—caring for them but wanting your space'). Deeper emotional reflection largely stopped once skills practice began, missing the significance of Oliver calling himself 'weak', his pride, and his shift toward excitement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on the target behavior—asking the flatmate to leave—throughout the session, with every exchange moving toward a concrete outcome and no topic drift. Session concluded with a complete, dated action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Oliver set the agenda, chose practice formats, and edited the notice and timeline, with the counselor checking in on accuracy. Power balance tilted, however, as the counselor produced most concrete material, acting more as a skills coach than a co-equal explorer.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered menus of choices and respected Oliver's decisions (in-person delivery, check-in addition, choosing the notice). Not a 5 because menu-style offers with counselor-crafted content subtly steered toward preferred solutions, and multiple questions at once reduced genuine steering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited (confidence ruler, 'what would move you from a 5 to a 7?'), drawing strong self-generated change talk. Much of the motivational content—scripts, guilt responses, the notice—was counselor-supplied rather than evoked, so Oliver's intrinsic resources were tapped only intermittently.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Positioned as a helper and asked Oliver's preferences, but frequently assumed the expert role by supplying scripts, the notice draft, and coping lines. Oliver shaped logistics, keeping this from being lower, but core words and strategies originated from the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced change talk via open questions, a readiness/confidence ruler, and highlighting progress, with Oliver's change talk strengthening across the session. Did not consistently deepen change talk with follow-up reflections on Oliver's own reasons, often pivoting quickly to materials and planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged guilt and comfort in companionship without amplifying it, using brief empathic reflections and strategic role-play to build coping responses. A 5 would require more consistent pivoting back to Oliver's own motivations rather than counselor-supplied counter-responses.
