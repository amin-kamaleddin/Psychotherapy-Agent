# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicated consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, affirming Samir's partial progress ('cut down rather than quit') explicitly without any judgment. Partial efforts were treated as genuine achievement rather than falling short.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist demonstrated highly accurate understanding, opening with a precise double-sided reflection of Samir's ambivalence and repeatedly naming the emotional core ('they're filling a need'). Complex reflections consistently captured the meaning beneath his words, including the honest doubt alongside hope.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained clear, unwavering focus on smoking reduction and asthma control, with every exchange advancing the agenda from triggers to concrete commitments. The session progressed steadily from exploration to a two-week plan with self-defined success markers.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was fully co-constructed from Samir's own ideas, with joint agreement on follow-up ('let's put our heads together', 'we'll look at them together'). Samir explicitly noted this felt different from the usual lecture, evidencing the egalitarian approach was felt by the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was reinforced continuously ('cutting down on your own terms', 'tell her the short version, my way'), with no pressure toward complete quitting. Setbacks were framed as adjustment rather than failure, and Samir's conditions were fully accommodated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently drew motivation from within rather than supplying it, using evocative questions that elicited Samir's own reasons, solutions, and commitment language. Samir's closing statement — 'my chest has been getting worse... I can't keep pretending' — is powerful evidence of successfully elicited intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positioned Samir as the expert on his own life ('You're the expert on your own life, Samir'), and every solution originated from the client. This is a textbook power-sharing stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and reinforced each time it appeared, with the psychiatrist spotlighting it ('that's you finding your own solution') and deepening it with questions that produced rich elaboration of Samir's own reasons. Commitment language emerged naturally ('I'll try it this week', 'That I can do').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('No promises about the long nights') was consistently acknowledged without reinforcement and paired with pivots toward change. Double-sided reflections ('Both of those can be true at once') legitimized ambivalence while keeping momentum on change.
