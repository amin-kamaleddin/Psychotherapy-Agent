# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental opening with affirmations of Lars's steps, including no judgment about his literacy struggles. Affirmations were somewhat generic and formulaic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections of his conflicting feelings, but emotional content (guilt, shame, the 'different dad' aspiration) was touched on without deep reflection. Much of the session leaned toward problem-solving rather than reflective listening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus: every exchange tied back to concrete targets (forms, telling Donna, Friday peer pressure). Session moved steadily from ambivalence to a plan Lars could articulate himself ('text, forms, course, Fridays sorted').

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Co-constructed key elements (Lars picked the heartfelt text and contributed 'for us'), and offered to sit with him on forms. Large blocks of counselor-delivered scripts and advice tilted the session toward expert problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choices without pressure, and Lars made all final decisions. Choices were framed within counselor-generated menus rather than openly inviting his own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied heavily on supplying solutions and scripts rather than eliciting Lars's own motivations. His strong change talk emerged spontaneously and was met with logistics instead of exploration of meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, conveying some power sharing, but content was counselor-driven (scripts, drafts, checklists). Lars's own expertise about his life did not substantially shape the interventions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Lars produced strong change talk, but the counselor rarely evoked or amplified it, often pivoting quickly to scripts and logistics. Rich entry points like his dream ('that dream keeps me going') were left unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and gently redirected, but used little strategic softening. Ambivalence (boredom, fear of looking soft) was sidestepped rather than actively resolved with double-sided reflections.
