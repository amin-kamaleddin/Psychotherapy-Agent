# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warmth and respect are consistent, with well-placed affirmations and no judgment, but affirmations are occasionally generic and lean toward encouragement rather than deeply individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurate, including the double-sided reflection on the wife's encouragement vs. fear of setback, but opportunities for deeper emotional reflection are missed at vulnerable moments like 'I usually just nod and go along' and 'I've earned the right to ask questions about my own body.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains unwavering focus on engaging with rehab while progressing logically from pain and discouragement to concrete advocacy planning and next steps, with a well-timed transition to action and clear closing commitments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Decisions about who to talk to, what to write, and follow-up are co-constructed from Mateo's own ideas, explicitly framed as 'figuring out two things together,' and directly produce his self-authored advocacy script and support plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently reinforced with no directives; the clinician asks Mateo what he believes and what role, if any, he wants the clinician to play, helping him build his own case in his own words.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly every meaningful change statement comes from Mateo himself, elicited through open questions about goals, his ideal session, and his own wording—never argued for or supplied by the clinician—and culminates in genuine client-generated commitment and self-efficacy.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positions Mateo as the expert on his own body and genuinely shapes the session around his contributions; Mateo's final statement ('You actually asked what *I* wanted') confirms he experienced the power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is the session's standout strength: change talk is systematically evoked (grandkids at the park, 'challenging but not crushing,' 'consistent instead of crashes and recovery'), explicitly highlighted, and rehearsed into a concrete plan, with Mateo's own closing shift from 'pointless' to having a plan demonstrating effective evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without amplification, often within double-sided reflections, and self-doubt is skillfully reattributed as expertise rather than laziness; however, a few sustain statements (e.g., pain improving before exercise can work) are left relatively unaddressed rather than strategically redirected.
