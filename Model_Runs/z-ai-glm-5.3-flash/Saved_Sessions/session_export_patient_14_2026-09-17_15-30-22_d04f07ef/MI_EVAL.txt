# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect is maintained and the client's anger over the 45-minute wait is acknowledged, but affirmations are sparse and generic ('Good call,' 'that's smart'). Repeated sarcasm and skepticism were not met with explicit validation of the client's character or efforts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some reflections capture the client's practical perspective (need for efficiency, treating client time as sacred), but they remain brief and functional. The emotional weight of health worries and fear of repeated failure was never deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor keeps good focus on the target health behaviors despite the client's time pressure, moving from identification to structured follow-up. A point is deducted for prioritizing logistics over clarifying underlying motivation and ending without a values-tied summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client chooses options, sets times, and shapes the accountability plan, which shows partial collaboration. However, the interaction is predominantly counselor-as-advisor offering menus rather than genuine two-way brainstorming, and the client's own expertise went unleveraged.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are offered repeatedly with no pressure or coercion, and the client explicitly notes the plan 'fits my life.' Autonomy support is somewhat procedural, however — menus of options rather than affirming the client's ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor relies heavily on dispensing information and options rather than eliciting the client's own motivations. Important client-initiated change statements ('I do want to get hold of it') were never explored with open questions or values exploration; the confidence ruler was the one genuinely evocative move.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice-based questions convey some power-sharing, but the overall stance is expert-driven with pre-packaged advice lists dominating. The client's self-identified expertise was dismissed rather than used as a collaborative resource.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The confidence ruler effectively elicits commitment and leads to structured change talk (calendar entry, assistant accountability, fallback plan). However, the counselor misses chances to amplify and deepen the meaning behind the client's stated desires for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about work barriers and past failures is acknowledged but engaged with through more problem-solving lists rather than strategic MI responses. No double-sided reflections pairing barriers with the client's own change statements were used.
