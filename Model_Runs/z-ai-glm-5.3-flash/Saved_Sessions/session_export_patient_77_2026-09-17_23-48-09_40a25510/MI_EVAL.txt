# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI
**Client:** Liyana Putri (Adolescent Weight Loss)
**Overall Score: 4.7/5**

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth toward a vulnerable adolescent; strong affirmations ("that takes real resilience," "a real step, not a small one") and no judgment. Affirmations occasionally formulaic/gilded; normalization of the plateau only briefly touched. Deeper unconditional acceptance of worth independent of the scale would elevate this.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is a genuine strength — accurate, emotionally attuned reflections (e.g., "carrying this completely alone... lands as dismissal, not help"). Slightly uneven: loneliness was reflected once but not deepened; the clinician moved to solution-finding when Liyana flagged the lonely material as "harder to talk about," missing an invitation for deeper empathic exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus: confidence → what worked before → what changed → eating connection → testable plan. Deliberate agenda-setting with a choice of three threads, followed by client selection. No drift; a specific, time-bound behavioral experiment (two weeks of regular meals) emerged by session's end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Genuine between-equals tone. "Together we can figure out what's really going on"; co-constructed agenda; hypotheses tested through Liyana's own reasoning. The experimental plan originated entirely from her. Tone would be experienced by a 16-year-old as respectful rather than adult-imposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Exemplary. "Absolutely okay — this is yours to guide" directly affirms ownership; decisions framed as hers; no advice imposed even when directly asked ("Could skipping actually mess it up?"). Client's pacing on the loneliness material honored ("Maybe later" → "Absolutely okay"). Textbook autonomy support with an adolescent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Woven into every turn — motivation and answers drawn from the client. Liyana moved from helplessness to self-generated insights (non-scale victories), a self-discovered hypothesis (meal skipping), and self-authored commitment. Reframing worked by evoking ("quitting would feel worse") rather than installing motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Expertise consistently located within the client ("You probably know more about what helps you than anyone"). Liyana's contributions substantially shaped the session — exercise focus, the meal-skipping discovery, the experiment. Clinician deliberately withheld information (e.g., adaptive thermogenesis) to let her arrive at insights herself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** The standout strength. Every major change statement ("I don't want to quit either," "trying something different can't be worse than this") was elicited, not volunteered. Skillful questions evoked her own progress markers, her own hunches, and her own proposed experiment; the double-sided reframe actively amplified commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk consistently redirected without dismissal — "maybe I'm just meant to be like this" paired with change talk via double-sided reflection; "It's gotta be me" reframed toward curiosity. Minor weakness: fear of gaining weight ("what if I gain instead") was not directly reflected before pivoting to the "fair test" question.

---

**Summary:** An exceptionally strong MI session, particularly impressive given a vulnerable, discouraged adolescent. Masterful evocation, consistent power-sharing, and disciplined direction moved the session from vague discouragement to a concrete, client-authored behavioral experiment. Areas for refinement: deepen exploration of the loneliness theme, add explicit normalization of plateaus, and reflect fear-based sustain talk before pivoting to problem-solving.

**Overall Score: 4.7/5** — A near-exemplary session demonstrating MI fidelity well above trainee level.
