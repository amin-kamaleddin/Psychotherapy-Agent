# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, notably responding to the client's pushback with validation rather than defensiveness. A few more explicit affirmations of her as a person would strengthen this further.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening with accurate complex reflections that elicited vulnerable material, such as her son's question about smoking. At times the counselor moved quickly to a question rather than sitting with the emotion, e.g., after her fear of failure disclosure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays focused on smoking and its impact on her son, with each exchange building toward a concrete first step. Some exchanges circled briefly before regaining momentum toward behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Work is framed as joint problem-solving, with the counselor offering frames while the client supplied the actual plan content. The counselor explicitly credited her authorship, and the interaction reads as two equals with complementary expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy repeatedly and explicitly reinforced ('this is your call, and your timing'), with no prescribed plan or quit date. The commitment was entirely client-authored, and even the follow-up was initiated by Natalia herself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from within rather than supplied, with open questions eliciting deeply vulnerable material. All major motivations came from the client's own mouth and were strategically reflected and deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client repeatedly positioned as the expert, and the counselor's approach genuinely shifted in response to her pushback. The resulting client-designed plan and the affirmation that it 'came entirely from you' reflect material power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength: change talk systematically evoked, deepened, and amplified, from the gut-lean question to the deep value about being there for her son. Resulted in client-generated spoken commitment with a witness ('I said it, so it's real now').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk handled skillfully by exploring the function of smoking and using double-sided reflections rather than arguing. Some reflections could have been followed by a quicker pivot toward change rather than dwelling on the status quo.
