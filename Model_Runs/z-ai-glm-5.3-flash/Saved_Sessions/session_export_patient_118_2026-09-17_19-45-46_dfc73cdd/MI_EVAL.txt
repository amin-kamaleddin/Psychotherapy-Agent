# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, respectful, and non-judgmental throughout. Responded to defensiveness ('Don't go writing that down as a commitment') with acceptance and playful regard rather than correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Deep, accurate reflections, e.g., on how cigarettes wove through grief and how quitting attempts had cost him — visibly shifted the client ('nobody's put it like that before'). Held dual feelings ('it's yours, and it's scary') with attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed focused on smoking and built steadily toward clarifying motivation and a first step. Slight deduction for not pinning down specifics of the cutting-down plan (how many, by when) and a closing that drifted into pleasantries.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Asked permission ('if you're willing') and asked rather than told ('what would a first step look like?'). The plan was co-constructed entirely from the client's ideas, keeping his dental priorities central.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced client ownership ('your decision, your timing'; 'you don't have to have it all figured out') and declined to push medication. Client confirmed the effect: 'This feels like something I'm choosing.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never supplied reasons for change; all motivation was elicited from the client — his da's dentures, his granddaughter, 'I got to really want to.' He left with self-generated reasons and steps.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly located expertise in the client ('You're the expert on your own life'), and client contributions shaped the session's direction. Client confirmed: 'Nobody handed me a leaflet... You actually listened.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful open questions ('What was it about seeing your own teeth that made this feel different?', 'Which reason would carry you furthest on a hard day?') drew rich change talk and a concrete self-generated plan. Reinforced it without supplying the motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally effective — avoided arguing and used dual-sided reflection ('I hear your caution AND I hear something shifting'). Minor deduction: occasional sustain talk (e.g., 'Don't tell the family doctor') was accepted gracefully without a deliberate pivot.
