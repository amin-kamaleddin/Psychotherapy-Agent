# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirming closers ('no shame,' 'you've got this'). Warmth felt somewhat formulaic, and Maya's normalizing statements about teen drinking were never explicitly validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasional accurate reflections ('It makes sense this feels normal to you'), but quickly pivoted to checklists and advice. Key emotional moments—Jess's driving scare, dad's embarrassing lecture, her need to belong—were never reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept consistent focus on alcohol use at parties from intake through a concrete plan (drink cap, refusal line, safe ride, check-in). Direction sometimes outpaced Maya's readiness, pushing a change plan while she was at 2/10.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maya's ideas shaped the plan and permission was asked once, but the counselor delivered stacked questions and bulleted expert recommendations with token 'want to?' check-ins. Backup steps were counselor-generated rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicitly affirmed choice, respected Maya's refusal to role-play, and framed relapse non-shamefully. Maya chose her own goal and text wording, though polished rewrites and rapid advice slightly reduced ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely counselor-supplied; advice followed Maya's explicit 2/10 readiness and ambivalence. Open-ended evocative questions were rare, and her own reasons for change (belonging, Jess's scare) were never developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some inclusive framing ('we'll problem-solve') and one permission request, but the session was largely expert-driven with stacked closed questions and prescriptive tips. Polished rewrites of Maya's message conveyed expertise sat with the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness ruler and confidence ratings elicited Maya's own commitments ('I can do that,' text to Jess). However, the counselor never explored what would raise her 2/10 rating or reinforced her self-generated reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not argue with sustain talk ('nothing bad really happens,' 'I'm not saying I have a problem'), which is a strength. But no double-sided reflections or reframes—ambivalence was bypassed with tips rather than worked with strategically.
