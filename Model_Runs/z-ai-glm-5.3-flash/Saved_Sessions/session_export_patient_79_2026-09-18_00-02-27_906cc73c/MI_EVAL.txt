# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms the patient's autonomy and self-knowledge ('You know your body and your routine better than anyone,' 'your wisdom'). Even resistant statements are met with respect and reframed without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly reflects both the patient's stated concerns and underlying feelings, including pride, guilt, and caution. Reflections like 'no promises, no weekly check-ins — that makes sense' capture the patient's meaning accurately and non-judgmentally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is clearly oriented toward the goal of finding a workable asthma management plan. The clinician steers the patient from resistance toward a concrete, self-designed behavior change (inhaler with morning coffee, two-week test).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is explicitly framed as the patient's own design ('you designed it yourself,' 'my proof, my lungs, my call'). The clinician asks rather than tells and honors the patient's stated conditions (two weeks, no lectures).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently supports patient choice and self-direction: 'what's the one small thing you'd actually be willing,' 'what day this week do you want to start,' and emphasizing the patient as the one running the test and judging results.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully draws out the patient's own motivations (stairs, easy mornings, beating younger coworkers) rather than imposing reasons to change. Change talk is repeatedly elicited and reflected back, culminating in the patient voicing his own desire to 'breathe easy.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The exchange operates as a genuine collaboration — the clinician proposes the two-week experiment, but the patient sets the terms, the start date, and the success criteria. The final agreement is explicitly co-constructed ('Sound fair?' / 'Fair enough — deal's a deal').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently invites and reinforces change talk through open questions ('what would be different,' 'what's your plan for getting back on track'). Sustained and commitment-oriented change talk emerges: 'Tomorrow I start,' 'two weeks, stairs, evidence.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician responds to sustain talk (burden of treatment, resistance to weekly visits) without confrontation, validating the concern and pivoting to simplicity and patient-defined limits ('no lectures, no stacking on extra stuff'). Resistance diminishes markedly over the session.
