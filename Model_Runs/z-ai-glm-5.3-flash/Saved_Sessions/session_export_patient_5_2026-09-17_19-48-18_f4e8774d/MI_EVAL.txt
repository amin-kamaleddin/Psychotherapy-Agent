# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Affirmations are frequent, genuine, and specific to Alessia's values, and both sides of her ambivalence are validated without judgment. No hint of disapproval or condescension appears, even when she reveals she only half-remembered the pharmacist's warnings.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, double-sided reflections capture emotional nuance precisely and mirror the client's exact language (e.g., commitment to doing things right AND worry about the medication-free gap). Reflective listening is consistent throughout the session.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains a clear focus on the safe transition from phenelzine to citalopram, with no topic drift. The session progresses logically through the client's agenda and closes with a concrete commitment to schedule the prescriber appointment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The work is framed as genuinely joint ('we can fill in the pieces together'), and the client's ideas materially shape the plan. Knowledge flows both directions, with the client's life-context expertise treated as essential.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Control is repeatedly handed back to the client, and safety-critical information is framed as supporting her informed choice. Medical decisions are appropriately deferred to Alessia and her prescriber, with no pressure or controlling language.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently draws motivation and solutions from within the client, eliciting powerful self-generated insights ('respect is different from fear'). The rating falls short of 5 only because safety-critical education necessarily requires substantial informing rather than evoking.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the expert on her own life, and her contributions demonstrably shape the session. The clinician necessarily retains most informational authority given safety-critical content, limiting the extent of power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled open questions tied to the client's motivations produce rich change talk ('I'll book that prescriber appointment this week'), which is reinforced effectively. The information-heavy educational context limits the proportion of evocative exchanges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (worry about the gap period, hesitation to commit) is acknowledged with a double-sided reflection and then pivoted toward the client's resources, without amplifying fears. The softening relies on a single reflection-plus-pivot rather than a repeated, deliberate pattern.
