### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is consistently accurate, including the double-sided reflection and the insightful reframe of the cigarette as a 'pause' that Linda confirmed shifted her view. However, emotional moments — the guilt about her son's question ('that hurt') and 'it's part of who I am' — were bypassed rather than explored, keeping understanding cognitive rather than affective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on Linda's smoking behavior without drifting, guiding each exchange toward exploring ambivalence and connecting her concern about her kids to concrete change. The session moves coherently from expressing no interest to a specific, achievable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Works alongside Linda rather than directing her, asking for her ideas about what could replace the cigarette and what would make the after-dinner plan work. Ideas for the plan (coffee ready, walk with kids, telling her husband) come primarily from Linda herself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly affirms Linda's right to choose: 'you enjoy smoking and that's completely okay,' 'you're not promising anything, and that's completely your call,' and 'you don't have to pick one today.' No pressure or subtle coercion appears at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses well-sequenced evocative questions targeting desire ('what kind of example do you wish they saw?'), vision ('kids being proud'), activation ('one small thing this week?'), and values ('what feels most important about telling your family?'). Draws out Linda's own motivations rather than imposing the clinician's reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Uses Linda's name and her exact words, and frames the work as a joint exploration rather than expert-driven advice. Decisions about pace and content of the change plan are shared, with Linda's input shaping each step.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The strongest dimension: change talk is evoked strategically and then amplified immediately ('I want to be that person for them, I really do' / 'that doesn't seem impossible'). Each emergence of desire, ability, and commitment language is reflected, strengthened, and built upon toward a concrete first step.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Handles sustain talk ('I have no plans to quit,' 'part of who I am,' 'it seems so hard') without arguing, countering, or correcting — accepting it and pivoting with curiosity instead. Doubt about ability is acknowledged as real ('both are real, and you don't have to pick one today'), keeping resistance from escalating.
