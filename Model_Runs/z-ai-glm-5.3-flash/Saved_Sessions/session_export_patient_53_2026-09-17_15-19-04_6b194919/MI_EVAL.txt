# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with validating affirmations throughout, though affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic, with less explicit warmth around her fear of failure and history of not following through.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections capture Isabella's core ambivalence and land well. However, some replies stack multiple questions, diluting empathic depth and limiting her room to fully explore her loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with each turn advancing toward a concrete, specific plan (Saturday 10:00, sit near the door for 20 minutes, coping moves, reminder text, follow-up). Exemplary MI direction tailored to a contemplation-stage client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine two-way exchange with the plan co-constructed and summarized back for confirmation, but the counselor drives the pacing and agenda, making the session somewhat more counselor-shaped than client-shaped.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly and repeatedly supported with opt-in choices at nearly every turn, and Isabella explicitly confirms this approach worked for her ('not pushing me too hard'). She declines telling someone without pressure and retains full ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation is genuinely elicited from the client (loneliness, her own reasons and plan), but evocation is intermittent as the counselor shifts quickly into supplying ideas and menus. Missed opportunities to deepen her hope and semi-commitment leave the motivation real but shallow.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Isabella's expertise is centered—she picks her own activity, timing, reward, and wording, with menus and consent before offering ideas. However, most option menus are supplied by the counselor rather than drawn from Isabella, keeping idea generation clinician-led.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk, progressing Isabella from vague openness to concrete planning with affirmations reinforcing her progress. Confidence isn't deeply explored and some commitment language ('I'll think about it') is left undeepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with neutral, validating reflections and quick pivots toward safer, small steps. The early question deliberately eliciting sustain talk is appropriate but slightly foregrounds reasons against change, and sustain talk isn't always strategically reframed toward her own motivations.
