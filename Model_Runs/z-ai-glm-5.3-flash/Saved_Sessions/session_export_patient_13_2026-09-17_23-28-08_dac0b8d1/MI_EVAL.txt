# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unwavering unconditional positive regard, explicitly stating 'there's no judgment here about how long it took' and 'It absolutely counts' when the client voices shame. Affirmations of her efforts (writing the script, asking for support, knowing her own pace) feel genuine and individualized, with no hint of disapproval, labeling, or impatience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and goes beneath surface content to emotional meaning, capturing that 'the fear isn't really about the appointment itself — it's about what finding out would mean.' Reflections on her environment-bound avoidance (the kitchen holding 'old patterns of putting things off') consistently land and are confirmed by the client's elaboration and growing openness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on the target behavior (making the medical appointment) while honoring the client's pace, progressing logically from fear to ambivalence to a small step, accountability plan, and conditions of readiness (timing, location). It culminates in a concrete, picturable first step — calling at the park during her daughter's swing time.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely co-constructed, with the plan's content (script, accountability style, location) coming entirely from the client's ideas while the psychiatrist checks in before each suggestion and negotiates every element. Full equality of partnership is evident in both language and outcome.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is actively fostered ('I'm not going to push you toward that door,' 'There's no deadline here') and the psychiatrist adopts the client's own rules of engagement (ask first, believe her answer). The client directly names the mechanism working: knowing she can say 'I'm not ready' without ruining everything makes her more likely to say yes.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never argues for change or supplies reasons; motivation is consistently drawn from within via the friend-reframe, the 'what's really stopping you today?' script, and invitations to define accountability. The client's answers grow increasingly concrete and self-motivated — she discovers she already knows what she needs.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client is explicitly and repeatedly positioned as the expert ('You're the expert on what environment helps you feel steady,' 'you're the one holding the pen'). Her contributions substantially shape the session, with each subsequent question built from her material — textbook power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is the standout strength: the 'beloved friend' question elicits powerful self-directed change talk ('she deserves to know, and waiting makes the worry bigger'), which is reflected back as her own words. The psychiatrist highlights her shift from 'impossible' to 'doesn't sound impossible,' culminating in 'That actually feels doable. I can picture it.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('maybe it'll just go away,' 'it seems overwhelming') is acknowledged without reinforcement and consistently pivoted toward change, with ambivalence normalized as making 'complete sense.' Her request for accountability is reframed as evidence of motivation, and sustain talk steadily diminishes across the session.
