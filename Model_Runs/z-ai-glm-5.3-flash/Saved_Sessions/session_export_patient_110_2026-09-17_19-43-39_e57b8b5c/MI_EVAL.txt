# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys warmth and respect through affirmations like 'You're the expert on your own life,' and normalizes Richard's ambivalence. However, affirmations occasionally feel formulaic, and the underlying emotional experience (e.g., fear about his health) is never named.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and consistent throughout, capturing Richard's ambivalence and the personal meaning behind his words. Each reflection prompts expanding disclosure, a strong indicator of empathic understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains unwavering focus on cholesterol management while progressing logically through exploration, values, planning, and rehearsal. There is no topic drift, and every exchange moves toward concrete, values-linked action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The entire plan—walking, mall walks, telling his wife—originates from Richard, with the psychiatrist facilitating rather than advising. The framing 'you're leaving with yours' perfectly captures the power-sharing dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are consistently framed as Richard's own, and his self-defined success criteria are honored without imposing clinical standards. The clinician avoids pressure even when Richard minimizes risk, allowing his values to generate motivation.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist draws out Richard's own motivations—retirement dreams, partnership with his wife, self-knowledge about accountability—rather than supplying reasons for change. Richard's closing commitment is entirely self-authored, the hallmark of effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Richard is explicitly positioned as the expert, and his contributions substantially shape the session's direction. Every question solicits his own knowledge and ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoke progressively stronger change talk, from tentative ideas to explicit commitment and rehearsal of the conversation with his wife. The clinician amplifies this language effectively.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Richard's minimization is handled with a balanced double-sided reflection followed by a pivot to change, and sustain talk is reframed around values rather than loss. Minor deductions for briefly echoing sustain talk content and a closing summary that doesn't contrast with reasons for staying the same.
