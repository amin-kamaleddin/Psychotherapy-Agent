# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently conveys unconditional positive regard, affirming the patient's courage and perspective without judgment. Empathic understanding is evident throughout, even when the patient expresses self-doubt.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture both the content and the emotional weight of the patient's experience (fear of judgment, past disappointments, self-criticism). The clinician normalizes ambivalence and validates fear without dismissing it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintains a clear trajectory from exploring barriers to brainstorming solutions to solidifying a specific, actionable plan. Each question builds purposefully on the patient's last statement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation unfolds as a genuine partnership, with the clinician building on the patient's ideas (sitting with friends, texting Maya) rather than imposing an agenda. The plan is co-constructed from the patient's own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly positions the patient as the expert ('You know your school best,' 'You're the expert on that pattern') and elicits her own solutions rather than directing. Choices are framed as hers to make, supporting self-efficacy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician draws out the patient's own reasons, strengths, and coping resources (music, drawing, her cousin) rather than suggesting them. The patient articulates her own motivation and self-insight ('I usually just assume the worst').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversational flow is highly responsive, with each reflection or question derived directly from the patient's preceding words. There is no evidence of a pre-set script; the dialogue feels naturally shared.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicits and shapes change talk, guiding the patient from 'maybe' to 'probably' to a concrete commitment ('I'll just send it right when I get out of here'). The transition from sustain talk ('I always chicken out') to commitment language is expertly handled.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (chickening out, overthinking, assuming the worst) is met with curiosity and reframing rather than argument. The clinician helps the patient identify and counter her own patterns, eliciting self-motivating statements instead of opposing her doubts.
