# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirms the patient's autonomy and perspective ('your plan,' 'your timing'), never judging her ambivalence. Her identity as a 'storyteller' and her fears are treated as valid and understandable.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections capture both the emotional core ('invisible work your friends never see') and the patient's own metaphors ('brain and stomach having two different conversations'). The patient explicitly feels heard: 'nobody's really asked me about this stuff before.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation stays focused on the patient's goal of disclosing diabetes to friends, with each exchange moving toward exploration of readiness and concrete next steps. The shift from fear to 'maybe soon-ish' shows clear forward movement.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All ideas originate from the patient; the psychiatrist frames her as the expert on her friends and explicitly notes 'this whole plan is yours.' Questions invite rather than direct.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly avoids pressure ('I'm not going to push you toward any timeline') and reinforces patient ownership. The closing 'not a promise, not pressure' captures this stance.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions draw out the patient's own reasons and strategies: what telling would look like, who to tell first, and what 'right moment' feels like. Change talk ('hiding feels worse than telling,' 'weirdly a relief') is elicited from the patient, not suggested.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue is a genuine back-and-forth; the clinician builds on each patient response ('You've actually just sketched out a way it could work') rather than steering. Advice-giving is absent.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully highlights and reflects patient-generated change talk ('a little bit possible,' 'maybe I don't have to keep this separate forever') without pushing her beyond her readiness, honoring 'probably. yeah.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fears ('what if they look at me different') are met with empathy and normalizing ('that's such a human place to be') rather than rebuttal. The clinician lets the patient resolve her own doubts through her knowledge of Marisol.
