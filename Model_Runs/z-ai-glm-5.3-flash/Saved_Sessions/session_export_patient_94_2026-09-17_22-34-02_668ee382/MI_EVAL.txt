# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, validating Maria's skepticism as a strength and never labeling or judging her defensiveness. Her gradual disclosure of never-before-shared fears reflects the safety this regard created.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, including reframing her stated time pressure as safety concerns and capturing her ambivalence precisely. The response to her 3 a.m. fear disclosure ('I'm glad you trusted me with it') was a powerful empathic moment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained steady focus on medication adherence, with each exchange moving toward a concrete outcome despite emotional detours. Strategic questions at each step gave the session clear momentum toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was a model of equal partnership: the plan was co-constructed from Maria's own criteria, and information was shared only when she asked. The final 'Deal?' sealed a genuinely two-sided agreement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly emphasized that decisions stayed in Maria's hands, honored her caveats like 'I can't promise anything' and 'I decide the pace' without pushback, and offered choices throughout. Her final commitment was freely chosen, making it far more likely to hold.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist drew motivation from within Maria through evocative questions about her confidence, criteria, and vision rather than persuading her. Maria articulated her own motivations and change plan on her own terms.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly honored Maria's expertise, offered primary sources for independent verification, and invited her to challenge the evidence. Her final 'I decide the pace' being accepted demonstrates genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully evoked and deepened change talk, highlighting Maria's initial 'maybe' and eliciting her decision criteria and vision of change. Her language progressed from pre-contemplation to a conditional, self-authored commitment ('I'll try it. Low dose, with the rinsing, on my terms.').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally treated as legitimate rather than resistance, with factual answers to fears and honest acknowledgment of negatives. However, the psychiatrist occasionally re-voiced sustain talk content (cousin's experience, side-effect fears), giving extra airtime to reasons against change.
