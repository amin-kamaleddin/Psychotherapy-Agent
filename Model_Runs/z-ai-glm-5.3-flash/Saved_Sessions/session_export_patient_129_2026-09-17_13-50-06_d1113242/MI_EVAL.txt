# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout despite Tyler's resistance and defensiveness. Falls short of 5 due to lack of explicit affirmations of Tyler's own strengths and values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections and validation of frustration, but reflective listening is thin. Responses frequently jump to advice and scripts, crowding out deeper exploration of Tyler's perspective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Session stays organized and goal-oriented, but the target drifts from substance use to mother-son conflict management. The reported weekend marijuana use is never revisited for risk or Tyler's own feelings about it.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tyler's choice of agenda option and input during role-play shaped the session, but the power balance is uneven. The counselor generates nearly all content while Tyler mostly rehearses and reacts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine menu-based choices offered, frequent check-ins, and Tyler's refusal to promise abstinence was respected and incorporated. At times the counselor dictates solutions rather than letting Tyler author his own approach.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is largely supplied by the counselor rather than elicited from Tyler — scripts and strategies replace evocative exploration of his values and concerns. Flickers of change talk ('I might actually try this') are not deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership in offering choices, but the middle session is highly advice-driven with counselor-supplied scripts and templates. Tyler's own insights are not explicitly highlighted or built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good moments like inviting a session goal and role-play that produced mild change talk ('doesn't sound that bad... I might actually try this'). However, change talk is never systematically evoked, deepened, or reinforced, and centers on managing his mother rather than his substance use.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk is mirrored and at times amplified — e.g., endorsing 'You're not the one seeing a problem, your mom is.' While the counselor avoids arguing for change, there are almost no double-sided reflections or strategic pivots toward self-examination.
