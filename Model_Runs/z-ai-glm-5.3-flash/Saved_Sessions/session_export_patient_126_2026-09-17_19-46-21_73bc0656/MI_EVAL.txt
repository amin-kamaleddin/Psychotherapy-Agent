# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unconditional positive regard throughout, never criticizing the patient's minimizing stance and affirming his track record instead of confronting. Tone stayed warm and respectful even when the patient deflected priorities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including the accurate double-sided opening and capturing the core tension between mates' nights and family Sundays. Missed opportunities to reflect deeper emotional nuances like the patient's guilt about 'counting the minutes till bedtime' and his vulnerability in 'I hear how that sounds.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus, progressively guiding from general minimization toward a concrete target behavior while gracefully following the patient's sleep agenda. Not a 5 because the session ended without a concrete follow-up commitment or fuller consolidation of the behavioral target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every question was genuinely open and built on the patient's answers, with the plan co-constructed entirely from his ideas. The patient's surprise at not being 'told off' underscores the effective collaborative stance.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, explicit autonomy support ('you don't have to pick one forever,' 'you're the expert on your own life') with no prescribing, pressuring, or moralizing. The patient's closing statement directly shows the approach strengthened his ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the patient through skillful open questions and the pivotal reflection on hearing himself connect heavy nights to missing his daughter. The patient's own words ('my own words catching up with me') are the hallmark of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently located expertise within the client, with the entire plan originating from the patient rather than the clinician. The patient explicitly confirmed this worked: 'my plan rather than something handed down.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited the patient's own reasons and solutions, producing strong change talk ('I could see trying that,' 'it's worth a go'). Deliberately amplified the pivotal moment when the patient connected heavy nights to missing his daughter.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided reinforcing sustain talk, meeting minimization with values-based reflections and reframes rather than argument. Minor deduction for occasionally re-voicing sustain talk in summaries without strategic reframing toward change.
