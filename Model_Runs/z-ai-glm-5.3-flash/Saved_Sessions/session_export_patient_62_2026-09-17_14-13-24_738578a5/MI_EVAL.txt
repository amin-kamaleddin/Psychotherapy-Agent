# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental; affirmed Jordan's efforts ("That was a real win") and normalized his wariness without labeling. Warmth was at times brief and procedural (quick transitions to option lists), and affirmations could have been more frequent and individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening throughout — captured both content and emotion ("frustrating and exhausting... would make anyone feel demoralized") and named the "heavy, empty exhaustion." Deeper exploration of the shame around the lead-job rejection and isolation of not wanting to burden friends was only lightly touched.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session focused on core concerns and translated vague hopelessness into a concrete target behavior (resume update), maintaining momentum through bullet creation, backup, and a scheduled next step. Deducted for early scattered option menus (coping, work, social) and a focus that was somewhat counselor-chosen rather than fully negotiated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (asking for details, offering choice, continued Tuesday support), but the work often became counselor-led instruction — pre-drafted bullets, template emails, and structured steps with Jordan mainly approving. True co-creation (e.g., having Jordan word his own bullet) was limited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choice and honored Jordan's preferences — respected his refusal of grounding exercises without pushback and his pace of small commitments ("not overcommitting"). Could be strengthened with explicit statements reinforcing Jordan's ownership ("this is your plan, your pace").

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation emerged indirectly through the small-task menu ("I'd like to not feel stuck anymore"), but most change talk was a byproduct of problem-solving rather than deliberate evocative questions. Jordan's own reasons, values, and hopes for change were never explicitly explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan was repeatedly given choice and his input shaped the plan, but the counselor often slid into an expert/directive role — providing pre-written bullets and templates rather than drawing out Jordan's own wording. The expertise balance tilted toward the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully channeled the initial spark ("I wish something would just go right") into action, eliciting meaningful self-affirming change talk ("I've done more than I gave myself credit for," "I showed up today, got something done"). Evocation was strong in the action-planning phase, but early on leaned toward offering solutions rather than drawing out Jordan's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Did not amplify or argue against Jordan's pessimism ("why bother," "my luck's garbage"); acknowledged briefly ("It's normal to feel wary after setbacks") and pivoted to achievable steps — an appropriate response. A double-sided reflection linking his doubt to his demonstrated capability would have more powerfully resolved ambivalence.
