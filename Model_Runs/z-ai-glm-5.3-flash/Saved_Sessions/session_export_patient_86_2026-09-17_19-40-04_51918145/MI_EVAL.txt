# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates warm, unconditional positive regard with specific affirmations throughout. Marco's defensiveness and pushback are received with respect, and there is no judgment about continued smoking despite major health events.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is excellent, including subtle cues like the dark humor in 'neither am I, apparently.' Emotions beneath the surface (shame, fear of failure) are accurately named and validated without amplifying sustain talk.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains steady focus on smoking cessation without ever becoming pushy, guiding via questions and honoring Marco's pace. Session ends with concrete, behaviorally specific next steps and a follow-up timeframe.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Behaves as a genuine equal partner, co-constructing a plan that originates entirely from Marco's ideas. Tone reflects two equals engaging in 'straight talk both ways.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding autonomy support: choice to try one step or sit with it, experiment framing as 'not a test you can fail,' and explicit attribution of all progress to Marco. The client's closing 'you didn't lecture me once' confirms he experienced genuine autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every motivation in the session is client-generated — grandkids, Charlie's story, the day-three plan — with the clinician never supplying reasons to quit. Skilled closing evocative question draws out Marco's deepest personal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Marco as the expert, and the entire quit plan originates from him with the clinician merely organizing and reflecting it back. Exemplary power-sharing for a client who hates being lectured.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open-ended questions evoke change talk that progresses from faint openness to detailed change planning (porch ritual, daughter contact, fridge drawing). Strategically amplifies change talk and elicits the deeply personal grandkids motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely avoids dwelling on sustain talk, and skillfully reframes past failure as useful data. Minor deduction for the opening reflection briefly echoing the entrenched nature of the habit before pivoting toward change.
