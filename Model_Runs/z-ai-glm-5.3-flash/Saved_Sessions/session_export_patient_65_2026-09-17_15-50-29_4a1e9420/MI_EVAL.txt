# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with affirmations like 'That's a solid, doable plan' and explicit permission ('No pressure—your choice'). However, warmth remained somewhat task-focused, with little explicit validation of Julian beyond the logistics problem.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections were accurate ('unsure and a bit resigned'), but reflections devolved into brief summaries followed by multiple-choice menus. Feelings of powerlessness, worry about being disbelieved, and forgetfulness were acknowledged but never explored in emotional depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on completing community service hours by month's end with minimal drift, progressing to concrete steps (texts, verification, reminders). Kept from 5 because focus stayed on logistics without linking to broader goals or other concerns.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing: Julian's knowledge and preferences (texting, casual tone, alarm) directly shaped the plan, built co-constructively on his own ideas. Option menus slightly limited true collaboration by steering choices.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice ('No pressure—your choice', 'Which step feels most helpful?') and honored all of Julian's selections without pushback. However, the multiple-choice format felt more like guided option-selection than open ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation remained entirely external (avoiding trouble with the judge), and questions were almost exclusively logistical. No evocative questions about values, hopes, confidence, or discrepancy — a significant missed opportunity given Julian's precontemplative, mandated status.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently collaborative, deferring to Julian's knowledge of his situation and letting his preferences determine the drafted scripts. Kept from 5 because repeated three-option menus subtly steered the direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Captured and affirmed emergent commitment language ('I'll do it today, probably') and used structured choices to move Julian toward concrete actions. However, never deliberately evoked deeper change talk about personal reasons or values, leaving motivation purely external.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and redirected gently toward action, but did so via solution menus rather than softening ambivalence itself. No double-sided reflections tying doubts to consequences; the 'stress stuff' was skipped past rather than explored.
