# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently showed unconditional positive regard, reframing David's resistance ('Sure. Fine.') as legitimate concerns rather than judgment. David explicitly confirmed his worries were treated like real questions, not excuses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, capturing David's underlying meanings such as his desire for control and dislike of passive compliance. David confirmed feeling genuinely heard: 'somebody actually listening, for one.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on medication adherence throughout, with every question advancing toward clarifying the decision. David moved from vague skepticism to a concrete plan (blood test, question list, decision after results).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operated between equals, with no unsolicited advice; David's own knowledge drove the agenda and the plan was co-constructed. David recognized this as a different experience from typical medical encounters: 'figuring out the plan myself? That was mine.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently reinforced, with decisions framed entirely as David's ('The pill decision comes after I see real numbers') and no pressure to commit. David confirmed: 'you didn't push me to promise anything.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor's strategy was almost entirely evocative, drawing out David's own motivations rather than supplying reasons for adherence. David's strong self-motivational statements ('all my excuses are maybe excuses') show motivation was elicited, not instilled.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** David was positioned as the expert and genuinely shaped the session—he generated the entire plan himself (blood test, questions, decision criteria). The counselor affirmed it as self-generated: 'That's you taking control on your own terms.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** David progressed from sustain talk ('I'm not even certain I need it') to strong commitment language ('I'll call the office this week... before I talk myself out of it'). Open questions progressively deepened his own motivation, culminating in 'I notice I'm talking myself toward it, not away from it.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally not amplified and David moved decisively away from reasons against change. However, a few reflections restated sustain talk verbatim (e.g., muscle weakness fears) rather than using double-sided reflections to actively resolve ambivalence.
