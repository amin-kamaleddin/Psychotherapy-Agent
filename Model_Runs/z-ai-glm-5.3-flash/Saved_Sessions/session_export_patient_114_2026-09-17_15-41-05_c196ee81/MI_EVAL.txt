# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with consistent validation, though affirmations are somewhat generic rather than individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captures Ryan's ambivalence and perspective, but emotional fears (academic panic, social rejection) could be explored more deeply before problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol-related risk, steering every exchange toward concrete strategies culminating in a specific commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ryan's preferences shaped key choices, but the middle of the session is advice-heavy, with most harm-reduction ideas originating from the counselor rather than the client.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frames change as Ryan's choice and honors his goal of still having fun 'on his terms,' though ownership could be reinforced more explicitly in moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited genuine motivation and Ryan's own decline line, but frequently supplied ideas via persuasion-by-menu rather than drawing solutions from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positions Ryan as the decision-maker and his contributions drove the plan, limited mainly by presenting pre-formed solution menus before eliciting his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and amplifies change talk around value discrepancies; would be stronger with more of Ryan's own reasons and commitment language rather than counselor-generated strategies.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged without argument and resistance is rolled with effectively, but strategic reframing of sustain talk to build discrepancy is used only occasionally.
