# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Nabil's anger and normalizing his rumination without stigma. Warmth occasionally feels procedural, with brief affirmations lacking deeper individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures both emotion and circumstance, including his skepticism about calm approaches. Missed opportunities to slow down after emotionally significant disclosures, such as his wife's fear of his reactions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, logical progression from validation to practical options to rehearsal and generalizing insight. Early tactical push slightly bypassed exploration, and repeated menu re-offers sometimes pulled focus from emergent reflections.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative, with the plan built from Nabil's own feedback and the psychiatrist role-playing so he could lead. Early problem-solving was counselor-generated (options A/B/C, drafted scripts) rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with consistent choice-offering, explicit permission to disengage, and no pressure or persuasion. Nabil's reluctant agreements were accepted without pushback, keeping ownership clearly with him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behavioral rehearsal effectively evoked client-generated insights like the 'quiet-then-explode' pattern. However, motivation was sometimes supplied via tool lists, with few open questions probing Nabil's own values, reasons, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Nabil was repeatedly positioned as decision-maker, with his choices genuinely shaping the session direction. Dense early option lists had an expert flavor, and his deeper disclosures were steered back to the rehearsal agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully moved Nabil from external blame to self-generated change talk ('actually doable') and affirmed his insight about his pattern. Could have deepened change talk further with evocative questions about what staying steady would mean for his family.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk but did limited active softening, often responding to doubts ('nothing changes,' 'people ignore you') with new solution menus rather than double-sided reflections. Drifted toward supplying counterarguments instead of helping Nabil resolve ambivalence himself.
