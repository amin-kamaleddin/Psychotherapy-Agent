# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations like 'Good questions — valid to want specifics.' However, affirmations were brief and procedural, lacking deeper validation of the emotional weight of being prescribed an unexplained medication.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed intermittent understanding ('Totally understandable to feel uneasy'), but reflections were minimal and responses quickly pivoted to information delivery. Key emotional statements from Mai, such as 'It was the not-knowing that was stressing me out,' were never reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on the target behavior of understanding and adhering to the statin, progressing logically from confusion to education, scripting, role-play, and a concrete action plan. No topic drift; Mai left with a fully specified plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Consulted Mai on activities and iterated scripts based on her feedback, but the session was counselor-led with dense expert-style bullet lists. Rarely asked for Mai's own ideas, limiting genuine co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered meaningful choices about activities, and Mai reported feeling 'in charge of it.' Content remained somewhat directive, however, with heavy reliance on recommendations and prescribed checklists.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Rarely drew motivation from within the client—no evocative questions, confidence rulers, or exploration of Mai's values and reasons. Motivation emerged through instruction and rehearsal rather than deliberate evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Gave Mai choices and treated her as capable of driving next steps, but the interaction was heavily expert-driven with the counselor dispensing checklists and scripts. Mai's contributions shaped the content, yet the counselor's rapid-fire information dominated the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively scaffolded conditions for Mai's own commitment statements ('I'll switch to orange juice,' 'I'll take it at night,' 'I'll call Dr. Ferris right away'). However, change talk was elicited implicitly through collaborative tasks rather than deliberate evocative questions and reinforcement.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mai's mild sustain talk ('I don't like taking medication I don't understand,' 'ready to just not fill it') resolved naturally through education, but the counselor did not engage with it at all. Missed opportunities for double-sided reflections or paired affirmations to actively pivot toward change language.
