# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, including affirmations of Kevin's honesty and autonomy with no judgment about his drinking. Warm, respectful tone throughout, ending with 'You did the work today.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent reflective listening, capturing both content and emotion, e.g., 'Both of those things can be true at once' and reflections on the fear of being 'the odd one out.' Accurate, emotionally attuned complex reflections tracked Kevin's evolving stance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, progressive focus from exploring ambivalence through envisioning change, defining success, and building concrete strategies with scheduled follow-up. Each turn moved the session forward toward a specific target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative, with the plan built entirely from Kevin's own ideas (pacing drinks, the joke-off strategy, recruiting Dave as an ally). The counselor acted as a partner rather than an expert directing solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, consistent autonomy support: 'choosing slow down rather than quit is completely yours to make' and 'you get to define what counts as success.' Change was framed as Kevin's own experiment ('one night, just to see') with no pressure or directives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was elicited from within rather than provided — Kevin moved from 'nothing really needs to change' to articulating his own reasons and a self-generated plan. The counselor consistently drew out Kevin's reasons and strategies through open questions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveyed that expertise resided with Kevin, whose contributions shaped the entire plan. Affirmations like 'you did the work today' reinforced the shared, patient-led process.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skilled evocation drew sustained change talk, from 'drinking less wouldn't hurt' and 'maybe trying it one weekend' to a full commitment with follow-up. Questions like 'What would make that first weekend feel like a win?' deepened Kevin's own momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (bad luck, mates' grief, 'not saying quit') was acknowledged without reinforcement and gently pivoted toward change. The counselor redirected consistently without arguing or confronting.
