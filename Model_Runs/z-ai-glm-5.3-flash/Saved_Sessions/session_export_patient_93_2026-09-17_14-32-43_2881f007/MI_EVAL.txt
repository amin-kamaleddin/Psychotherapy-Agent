# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with no criticism of her drinking. However, affirmations were sparse and generic, and strengths (her independence, social ties, willingness to try) were not specifically affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Facts were summarized accurately, but emotional reflections were minimal and shallow. Meaningful disclosures — the January fall, fear of 'looking like a child' with lemonade, teasing friends, her son's nagging — were not reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on reducing alcohol with no topic drift across the session. A 5 was prevented by pushing toward commitment and weekend limits before ambivalence was fully resolved.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret selected her strategy and weeknight limit, and follow-up timing was negotiated. But the plan's architecture was counselor-defined, with a dictated options menu, unilateral tracker, and checklist-style safety scripts.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated choices were offered, her refusal of abstinence and pub-night boundaries was respected, and the plan was framed as hers. Weakened somewhat by prescriptive risk framing, a mechanical decision-tree script, and the unfulfilled reminder call.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplied the reasons for change (BP, sleep, coordination) rather than eliciting Margaret's own values and concerns. No open questions exploring what mattered to her or developing her change talk into internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Margaret's input was invited and shaped the plan details, but the counselor often adopted an expert stance with didactic risk information and a pre-packaged menu. Her expertise about her routines was consulted but not genuinely centered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler and open questions did elicit change talk ('five and a half... if cutting down'; 'I'd rather not have another fall'). Opportunities to deepen it were missed — no elaboration questions or 'Why a five and not a two?' follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced, and the fall reframe effectively softened 'seven drinks isn't much.' But deflections ('not promising anything about the weekends') were passively accepted rather than addressed with double-sided reflections.
