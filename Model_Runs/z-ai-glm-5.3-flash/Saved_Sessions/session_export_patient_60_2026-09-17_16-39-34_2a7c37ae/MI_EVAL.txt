# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Jason's frustration throughout. However, affirmations are sparse and formulaic, praising compliance rather than personal strengths or values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections are accurate, but once the session shifts to drafting and role-play, the counselor moves into advice-and-coaching mode. Most notably, Jason's genuine emotional disclosure about being scared by his memory loss was skipped over entirely — a significant missed empathic opportunity.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on concrete goals: statement, witness, drink limit, and safe-ride plan. However, the focus is on managing the conduct process rather than the underlying behavior of alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is genuine back-and-forth on editing the statement and constructing the role-play. But the counselor supplies nearly all substantive content — statement text, scripted answers, drink limits — while Jason mostly approves or tweaks.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offers meaningful process choices and frames next steps as Jason's to determine. However, when Jason pushes back (refusing the beer pong commitment), the counselor steers content with scripted answers and suggested limits rather than honoring his judgment.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor predominantly supplies answers rather than drawing them out — scripted replies, a pre-written statement, and suggested limits, with virtually no exploration of Jason's own values or reasons for change. Even Jason's self-motivated moment about the memory loss scare passed without follow-up.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor uses inclusive language and incorporates Jason's edits, showing some responsiveness. But the counselor drives most of the agenda and content, making Jason more a consumer of a script than a partner and expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor does elicit some commitments about drink limits and next steps, and Jason responds with commitment language. However, change talk stays procedural (managing the conduct process) rather than exploring Jason's own motivations, and his spontaneously offered self-reflection was never deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor appropriately rolls with Jason's defensiveness without arguing or confronting. However, sustain talk ('I'm not an alcoholic,' 'everyone does it') is simply bypassed rather than strategically redirected — no double-sided reflections or discrepancy development were used.
