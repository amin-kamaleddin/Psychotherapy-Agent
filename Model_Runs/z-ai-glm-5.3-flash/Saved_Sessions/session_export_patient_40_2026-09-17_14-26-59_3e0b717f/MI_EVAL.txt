# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and reframes relapse and asking for help as protective steps. However, affirmations of Elias's own strengths—like his three months of sobriety—were sparse and never directly acknowledged.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections and validation, but empathy was often superficial and quickly overtaken by advice and task instructions. Emotionally rich disclosures ('I felt like I'd ruined everything,' identity doubts) were met with strategies rather than deep reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent focus on relapse prevention with a logical flow and a measurable two-week outcome. Slightly diminished by racing through multiple plans without pausing to consolidate around Elias's core values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias picked options, set timelines, and negotiated plan realism, showing collaborative elements. Yet the counselor frequently handed down fully-formed protocols and checklists in an expert-to-client style rather than co-creating strategies together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choices ('Which would you like to try?') and frames the plan as a test Elias controls, fostering strong autonomous language. Slightly limited by supplying pre-packaged scripts rather than letting Elias fully author his own message.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The internal/external motivation exercise and ownership rating genuinely drew out Elias's own reasons and self-assessed commitment. Much of the session relied on information-giving and scripts, missing opportunities like 'What would moving from a 6 to an 8 look like for you?'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited Elias's input and let him choose options that shaped the plan. However, the counselor frequently shifted into expert mode with drafted scripts and detailed protocols, making the agenda largely counselor-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The values/motivation exercise successfully elicited strong change talk (shame avoidance rated 9/10 'fully mine') and the daily intent journal reinforced autonomous commitment. Sometimes answered own questions with pre-packaged plans rather than deepening Elias's change talk through follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce Elias's doubt but also failed to actively soften it—no double-sided reflections or strategic pivoting toward his stated change reasons. The reframe of handing over pills as 'not a failure' was a good moment, but pervasive self-trust doubt went largely unaddressed.
