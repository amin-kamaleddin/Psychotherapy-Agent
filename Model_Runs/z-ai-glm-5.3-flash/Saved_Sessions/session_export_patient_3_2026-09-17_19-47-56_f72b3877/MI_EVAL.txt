# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicates consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, with phrases like 'no judgment either way' and 'You should feel good about where you're starting from.' No labeling, disapproval, or judgment appears anywhere, even when discussing past 'failures.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are highly accurate, capturing both emotional content and underlying meaning (e.g., fear of letting herself down, valuing immediate well-being over jeans). The counselor consistently reflects Aisha's shift from all-or-nothing thinking toward self-compassion.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, progressive focus on initiating exercise, moving from exploration to a concrete plan (Mon/Wed/Fri walks, calendar tonight, two-week check-in). The counselor guides the agenda without wandering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every plan element originates from the client (walking, timing, fallback, sister's support), with the counselor reflecting and organizing rather than prescribing. 'We'll figure out the next step together' frames the work as genuinely shared.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces the client's ownership, offering the check-in as an option and drawing success criteria from her own words. There is no advice-giving, persuasion, or pressure anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions ('What's different now?', 'What would be the first sign this is working?') consistently elicit the client's own reasons, values, and coping strategies. Aisha explicitly notes 'just saying this out loud to you is helping,' demonstrating successful evocation of sustainable change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly credits the client as the expert ('You've done the real thinking here; I just helped you say it out loud'), and the plan emerges entirely from her ideas. Scored 4 rather than 5 because occasional leading questions (e.g., 'Sometimes it's a set time, sometimes it's a person') slightly narrow open exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions like 'What's different now?' and 'What would be the first sign that tells you this is working?' skillfully elicit and deepen change talk, from 'I'm not chasing perfect anymore' to commitment language ('keeping my word to myself'). Each episode is reinforced with affirmations rather than left hanging.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never amplified; fears of quitting are briefly validated then immediately reframed toward client strengths and willingness. Discouragement and all-or-nothing thinking are consistently reframed as self-knowledge, with the counselor highlighting the client's own solutions ('something is better than nothing').
