# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, explicitly accepting Marisol's choice to keep the piercing and stating 'I'm not here to make you remove it.' However, warmth is sparse, with no explicit affirmations of her strengths or personal qualities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening reflection is the only substantial empathic statement; after that the counselor shifts almost entirely into information-giving and checklists. Marisol's feelings — her resentment of the dentist, pride in autonomy, and ambivalence — are rarely reflected or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on the piercing and related dental concerns, moving logically from aftercare information to a rehearsed plan and committed action. The main limitation is that the focus shifted to communication skills at the client's initiative without re-anchoring to the core health behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There is partial collaboration through offering a session menu and interactive roleplay, but the dominant pattern is unidirectional expert advice via long prescriptive lists. The counselor never asks what Marisol already knows or has considered before supplying answers.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with repeated affirmations of choice ('it's your choice,' 'My role is to support your choices') and no pressure to remove the piercing. A higher score would require more emphasis on Marisol's ownership of the change process and her capacity for self-directed care.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is assumed rather than elicited — no open-ended evocative questions, importance/confidence rulers, or exploration of the client's values. Progress like the titanium swap came from Marisol's own pragmatism rather than skilled evocation, making this the weakest domain.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor occasionally acknowledges client expertise and defers to Marisol's agenda choice, but advice delivery dominates. Her own ideas are incorporated only lightly through wording tweaks rather than genuine power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marisol spontaneously offers movement ('the shorter titanium bar I can do,' 'checking it myself isn't a big deal'), but these go unreflected and undeepened. No open questions target reasons, importance, or confidence for the health behavior itself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Strong sustain talk ('I'm not removing it over a maybe,' 'Tons of people have piercings and are fine') is met by avoiding argument, which prevents reinforcement but not strategic softening. No double-sided reflections, reframing, or summaries that weigh her ambivalence are used.
