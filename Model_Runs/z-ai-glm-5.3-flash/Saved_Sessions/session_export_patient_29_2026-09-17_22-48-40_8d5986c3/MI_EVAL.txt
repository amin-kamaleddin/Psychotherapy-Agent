# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, affirming David without judgment ('That takes real courage to name the shame'). Slips are normalized as 'a stumble, not a collapse,' with no criticism even around three failed attempts or fear of excuses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate throughout, capturing both content and emotional nuance—e.g., reflecting that 'the patio isn't really about cigarettes—it's about connection.' The clinician skillfully mirrors the shame dynamic and David's ambivalence between nervousness and readiness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation, moving purposefully from motivation exploration to concrete planning (medication, deadlines, social triggers, check-ins). Each response guides the conversation forward, culminating in a well-defined action plan without wandering off-target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely between equal partners, with inclusive framing like 'let's build on your ideas together.' Every plan element emerged from David and was jointly refined; the clinician never assumes an expert stance or dictates the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** David's ownership is regularly affirmed, positioning him as decision-maker ('What do you imagine those tools looking like for you?'). A point is deducted because the clinician occasionally gently steers toward preferred options, such as suggesting the Marco idea, rather than purely reflecting client-generated choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within David through evocative questions, producing deeply personal change talk about his father's death, retirement plans, and self-talk. The clinician never argues for change or supplies unrequested reasons, fully embodying the principle that motivation resides in the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is actively located within David ('you know your own patterns better than anyone'), and his contributions visibly shape the session. The Marco idea, wife-as-support plan, and slip protocol all originate from David and are adopted into the final plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open-ended evocative questions ('What matters most to you about making this attempt different?') reliably elicit rich desire, reason, need, and commitment language. Change talk is reinforced with affirmations like 'That's real momentum' and 'a plan made by someone who truly knows himself.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about stress, social triggers, and fear of failure is acknowledged briefly and redirected toward David's own solutions without being reinforced. A point is deducted because explicit double-sided reflections that strategically reframe sustain talk toward change are rare; redirection relies mostly on neutral acknowledgment followed by a question.
