### Acceptance

**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, affirming Brandon's honesty and normalizing ambivalence without judgment, even during deflections. No labels, disapproval, or pushback appeared at any point.

### Empathy

**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured both sides of Brandon's ambivalence and his social concerns. Reflections were often combined with questions or option lists, limiting exploration of the emotional core beneath his minimization.

### Direction

**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on weekend drinking was maintained throughout, progressing logically from ambivalence to a concrete experiment and troubleshooting. Every exchange advanced the change goal despite deflections.

### Collaboration

**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Brandon defining the nights, threshold, support person, refusal line, and success criteria. Permission-style questions built each step directly on his answers.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively supported from the opening through explicit choice of focus and experiment design, with reframing slips as 'info not failure.' Brandon's self-directed plan reflects ownership rather than clinician control.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within, with confidence rulers moving Brandon from 6 to 8 and self-generated solutions like the 'slip isn't failure' reframe. His shift from 'this was pointless' to 'I'll actually try this' demonstrates evoked change.

### Partnership

**Comments:** Brandon's own ideas formed the backbone of the plan, with the clinician repeatedly asking which options felt most doable. Some turns front-loaded multiple suggestions before seeking his input, slightly shifting the idea-generation burden.

**Comments:** Skillful evocation drew out change talk via cost-benefit exploration and confidence rulers that moved Brandon from 6 to 8, including planning, rehearsal, and self-defined success criteria. His closing reflection on going from 'pointless' to having a plan showcases excellent change talk cultivation.
