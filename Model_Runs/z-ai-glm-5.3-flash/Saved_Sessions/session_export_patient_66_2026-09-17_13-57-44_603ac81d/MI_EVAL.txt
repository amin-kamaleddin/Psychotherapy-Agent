# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, affirming the patient's autonomy needs without criticizing his smoking or resistance. However, affirmations were somewhat generic and warmth limited by quick pivots to prescriptive option lists.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections captured the patient's frustration and ambivalence, with the autonomy reflection especially effective in prompting deeper self-disclosure. Missed opportunities to deepen emotional moments, such as reflecting on the daughter's ashtray comment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed clearly focused on smoking cessation and the workplace context, ending with a concrete 7-day quit plan and start date. Direction was sometimes expressed as premature momentum toward solutions before change intentions emerged.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (permission-asking, option menus, co-shaping the final plan), but much of the session followed a counselor-driven format with bulleted advice. The patient had to actively push back twice to slow the pace.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeated choice-framing and non-shaming slip language helped the patient explicitly claim ownership of the change. Early delivery of detailed plans before the patient had decided to quit partially undermined autonomy, as the patient called out.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The pros/cons and values exercise elicited genuine self-motivational statements (daughter, money, control). However, the counselor often defaulted to providing reasons and solutions rather than asking evocative questions — the patient had to prompt the key question himself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice-offering and permission-asking were present, but the counselor repeatedly positioned expertise through prescriptive lists and scripts before agreement to change. True power-sharing emerged mainly late, driven largely by the patient's assertiveness.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful change talk emerged ('maybe it's all-or-nothing,' 'Seven days, I can do seven days'), partly through the values exercise. Change talk was rarely reflected back or amplified, and no commitment-strengthening or confidence questions were used.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally reflected without argument and reframed toward what mattered to the patient, never amplified or argued against. The pros/cons exploration of smoking's benefits was strategically used at the patient's request.
