# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard with a resistant adolescent ('I'm not here to pile on with your mom'), never judging the substance use or correcting Tyler's defensive framing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate complex reflections ('the good stuff about you is invisible to her'), though some reflections reframed toward the mother relationship rather than fully exploring feelings of being surveilled and labeled.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains focus on substance use and its relational context, progressing to a concrete testable step, though early relational venting was explored at some length.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change is framed as a joint, negotiated project ('what would your half of meeting her partway look like?'), with Tyler's resistance incorporated rather than overridden.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never pressures or prescribes; Tyler retains full control ('that's not happening') and the counselor accepts it, letting him define the size and pace of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent evocation for a forced, precontemplative client—Tyler's motivations and plan ('not lying so much... texting her back') were elicited from within rather than supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positions Tyler as the expert on his life, with every forward step built from his own answers and contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully amplified Tyler's own words ('wouldn't have to hide stuff') to build incremental change talk, moving him from 'it's on her' to articulating his half of a deal.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids amplifying sustain talk via strategic pivots toward Tyler's preferences, but mother-blame is sometimes restated without consistent redirection toward his agency.
