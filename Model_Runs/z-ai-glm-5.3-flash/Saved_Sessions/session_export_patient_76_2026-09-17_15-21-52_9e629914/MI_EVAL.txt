# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout despite high-risk content, with matter-of-fact safety screening and affirmations of her skepticism. Affirmations were somewhat generic and formulaic, limiting warmth beyond procedural validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections early on, but the session shifted toward checklists and strategy lists. Powerful statements like 'It's so I can keep going' and 'I don't want to get my hopes up' received only surface acknowledgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on self-harm as coping, moving from exploration to safety assessment to a concrete coping plan. Direction occasionally stalled in list-recitation, and repeated verbatim safety caveats padded the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely between equals — permission-seeking, co-constructed plan, accepted her negotiated one-week trial terms. Heavy reliance on pre-packaged option menus and scripted safety language gave parts of the exchange a protocol-driven feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed everything as optional and respected her limits, including declining the roommate text without pressure. Would be stronger with more explicit reinforcement of her ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** A few evocative questions drew out tentative preferences, but her motivations for reducing self-harm, values, and hopes were never elicited or developed. Motivation remained largely untapped given her precontemplative presentation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Liora as the expert; all coping strategy decisions were client-selected and the plan was ratified by her. Sometimes defaulted to pre-formatted options rather than drawing solutions from her own knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited modest change talk ('I could try the ice,' 'I'll try. For a week, maybe') and the option menu invited her choices effectively. These commitments were left underdeveloped — no elaboration, deepening, or reflection of her change statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not amplify her substantial sustain talk and used neutral reflections, but several responses merely restated sustain talk without pivoting toward change. No double-sided reflections linking her ambivalence were used; redirection was passive rather than strategic.
