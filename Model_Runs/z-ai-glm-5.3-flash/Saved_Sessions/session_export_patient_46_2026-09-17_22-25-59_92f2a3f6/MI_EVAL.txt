# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist shows unconditional positive regard throughout, never arguing or judging even when Ethan dismisses the referral as 'overkill.' Affirmations are specific and earned, and Ethan's boundary ('no forever promises') is immediately honored.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and emotionally attuned, including strong double-sided reflections and matching Ethan's casual register. Only minor critique: a few reflections lean formulaic, preventing a perfect match throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains a consistent focus on drinking and its consequences with a clear MI arc. Points withheld due to slightly redundant, circular questioning in the final exchanges that briefly stalled momentum instead of consolidating with a summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is a genuine partnership: all strategies (Gatorade, Danny, 'one weekend at a time,' follow-up format) originate with Ethan and are adopted without revision. The clinician's expertise enters only through questions Ethan answers himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is exceptional and explicit — disclaiming the referral's authority, affirming Ethan's ownership, and enthusiastically endorsing his 'no forever promises' boundary. There is zero pressure, coercion, or premature advice anywhere in the transcript.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every reason for change is spoken by Ethan, not the clinician, and evocation is sustained across the full arc — from values-linked prompts through barrier planning. A contemplation-stage adolescent articulating his own reasons, plan, and confidence by session's end is strong evidence of evocative skill.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly locates expertise within Ethan from the first turn and lets his contributions substantively shape the session's direction. Explicit power-sharing ('You get to steer this') is exercised and honored, including the closing 'Deal?'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout domain: desire, reason, ability, commitment, and values talk are systematically evoked and harvested, and all solutions are client-generated. The psychiatrist asks and then gets out of the way — textbook change-talk cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never argued with or reinforced, and double-sided reflections are excellent. Points withheld because some sustain talk is left hanging rather than actively pivoted into change-side material, and Ethan's closing 'no forever promises' is accommodated without gentle reframing.
