# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicated unconditional positive regard throughout — validated frustration about the double standard, explicitly disclaimed lecturing, and affirmed Maya as 'the good kid you actually are' with no judgment or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and progressively deeper, moving from surface concerns (fallout, parental scrutiny) to deeper meaning (college as 'my exit plan'); the client explicitly confirmed feeling understood ('You actually got it').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained focus on alcohol while skillfully linking it to Maya's own concerns (trust, college, parental conflict), though mid-session drifted toward parent/school dynamics before circling back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opened by asking what would make the conversation worth Maya's time, co-created the agenda from her answers, and asked rather than told throughout — her own ideas drove the intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support throughout ('you get to decide what this means for your life'), with an explicit refusal to lecture; the plan was fully self-generated and the counselor reinforced Maya's ownership of her choices.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from within, eliciting Maya's values, strategies, and reasons — marked by 'I've never actually said any of that out loud before' — though evocation relied on general open questions rather than rulers or deeper elaboration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Maya as the expert ('you know your life way better than I do'), and her contributions visibly shaped the session's direction toward trust, autonomy, and the mom conversation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk via strategic questions ('where does drinking fit for you?') and amplified autonomy language, ending with commitment ('I could try it this week'), but confidence/ability talk was not explicitly deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to minimizing and resistance with accepting, double-sided reflections that pivoted toward change without arguing, though some sustain talk (e.g., drinking as 'normal') was acknowledged neutrally rather than strategically reframed.
