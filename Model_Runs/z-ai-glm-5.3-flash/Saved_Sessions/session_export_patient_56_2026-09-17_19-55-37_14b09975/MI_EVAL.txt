# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with frequent, individualized affirmations ('that's a real insight, and it came straight from you'). The client is consistently treated with respect and positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is accurate and builds on the client's own metaphors (drawer, daughter's face, coming back to what she chose). However, reflections are largely confirmatory rather than deepening—the guilt she raised ('half-there') was passed over rather than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on aligning evening behavior with the value of family happiness, progressing logically from gap to plan to success criteria. Turn length is occasionally long with multi-part questions, which slightly dilutes momentum.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuine exchange between equals—the client's own words become the working material of the session, and her contributions shape the plan and agenda. The clinician asks rather than tells throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is actively reinforced: the plan, pacing, and success standards are entirely the client's, with explicit pressure removal ('there's nothing to fix here'). The clinician never advises or directs.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from the client herself—her reasons, anticipated rewards, resilience, and personal definitions of success. She ends the session self-motivated and self-affirming, evidence of effective evocation rather than suggestion.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client ('that's your own wisdom talking, not something I told you'), and her contributions demonstrably steer the session. Textbook power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's standout strength—the clinician systematically evokes change talk and reinforces Isolde's rich commitment language ('I feel ready,' 'I feel proud') without ever supplying content himself. Every element of the plan is client-generated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The small amount of sustain talk (guilt about being 'half-there') is handled effectively by pivoting to her strengths rather than dwelling on it. However, the approach is somewhat passive—no active double-sided reflection or strategic reframing was used.
