# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent respect and warmth with repeated affirmations ('You're being careful — good'), including neutral handling of risky St. John's Wort use. However, positive regard feels somewhat formulaic and does not deeply validate her emotional relief.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but largely cognitive and informational rather than emotional. The counselor never reflects Alessia's anxiety, relief, or fear, leaving the session dominated by medication facts rather than emotional attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on medication safety and concrete next steps throughout, culminating in a specific commitment to call the prescriber and a personalized safety checklist with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly seeks Alessia's input and co-constructs scripts and checklists tailored to her requests. Collaboration is somewhat superficial, though, as finished solutions are presented for approval rather than jointly brainstormed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent choice-offering ('role-play the call, draft an email, or a checklist?') and reinforcement that Alessia controls the process. Some advice is still delivered prescriptively (e.g., 'Stop St John's Wort now') without asking her thoughts first.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and open questions elicit some change language, but most of the session is information delivery by the counselor. There is no exploration of Alessia's values or the personal meaning behind her motivations beyond numeric ratings.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** While Alessia's questions shape content, the session is largely expert-driven information delivery with the counselor as authority. Her own expertise about her body and routines has little influence on the plan beyond format preferences.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective scaling questions tracked importance (7) and rising confidence (5 to 8), with commitment-fostering questions like 'When will you call?'. The counselor does not explore reasons behind the confidence increase or what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was minimal and was reframed as a strength ('You're right to be cautious — good'), with firm but respectful redirection on the St. John's Wort risk. Some reflections simply restate her worries without pivoting toward motivation.
