# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician communicated unconditional positive regard throughout — normalizing Malik's anger, never moralizing about cannabis use, and reinforcing respect when he asked not to be labeled a 'bad kid.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate and consistent, including the pivotal reflection of the loneliness beneath Malik's bravado that shifted him from defensiveness to openness ('Finally someone gets it').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician maintained a clear, progressive focus from validating frustration through eliciting change talk, developing discrepancy, and building a concrete plan — with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician behaved as a genuine equal partner, using curious rather than leading questions and deferring to Malik's expertise on his peer dynamics and home life. Malik's visible comfort ('better than I thought it'd be') confirms the power-sharing felt real.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was repeatedly reinforced ('this is your space,' 'no promises needed,' 'it's your call'), the plan was framed as Malik's own choice, and imperfect effort was explicitly accepted.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently elicited from within Malik rather than installed — no advice or persuasion was offered, and the session's outcome ('I got this,' 'I chose this') demonstrates genuine evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly positioned Malik as the expert, and the plan was entirely client-generated with repeated ownership credited ('That came from you, not me, not your mom').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully evoked spontaneous change talk from a precontemplative teen (foggy Saturdays, bad tryout, coach noticing) and deepened it into strong commitment language through strategic questions and affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections generally paired sustain talk with change talk effectively, but the reflection 'your friends wouldn't either' slightly restated peer minimization, and the clinician didn't always actively pivot away from 'it's not a big deal' statements.
