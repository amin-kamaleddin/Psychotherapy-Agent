# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with affirmations like 'Starting small is smart' and nonjudgmental handling of David's skepticism and humor. Falls short of 5 because affirmations are somewhat generic and don't individually value his honesty about ambivalence or past failed attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective summaries capture both content and ambivalence (e.g., 'You're torn—want to avoid meds but need proof diet works'). Reflections rarely go deeper emotionally, however—David's fear of judgment and hiding his lab results from his wife are acknowledged but not explored for underlying shame or fear.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary directional steering: every turn advances a tight behavioral arc from exploration to a concrete plan (6:15 alarm, shoes by door, 6:30 walk), barriers, backup plan, safety rule, and a scheduled Friday 2pm check-in. Maintains focus despite David's deflections without becoming coercive.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans largely emerge from David's own suggestions (dog walk, lunch loop, telling his wife in his own words), giving him visible ownership. Not a 5 because some exchanges are counselor-proposed and client-approved rather than genuinely negotiated, with advice bundles offered before inviting his ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength: consistent menu-based choices, explicit 'I won't push; I'll support your plan,' and medication framed as David's decision with diet as a legitimate trial. David's own closing ('The meds question isn't closed—just on pause') shows he owns the process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Elicits motivation from within—David's own success criteria ('If the numbers actually dropped') and core value of staying healthy for his family anchor the plan. Uneven at times, with key material (fear of judgment, concealment from his wife, history of abandoned diets) left unmined for meaning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats David as the expert on his life: solicits his agenda, offers menu-based choices, and defers when he rejects rehearsing. Stops short of 5 because some dense advice blocks arrive before inquiring into David's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked commitments ('Tonight, probably,' 'I'll aim for five walks') and the confidence scaling generated self-motivational statements ('Seven's not bad... the most confident I've felt'). Missed opportunities to deepen—not asking why confidence rose from 5 to 7 or what the walk would mean—keep it from a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently validates skepticism and barriers, then redirects to problem-solving, and reframes meds as 'a backup option' to soften the all-or-nothing stance. Some reflections simply restate sustain talk without double-sided reflections tying to his family values, and the medication ambivalence itself is parked rather than resolved.
