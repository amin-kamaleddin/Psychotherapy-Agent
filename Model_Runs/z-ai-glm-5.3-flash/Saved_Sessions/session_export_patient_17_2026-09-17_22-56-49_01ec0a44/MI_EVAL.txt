# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

**Patient:** Anisa
**Interviewer Role:** Psychiatrist
**Session Focus:** Smoking Cessation

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard maintained throughout. When Anisa voiced fear of slips and shame ("I've had enough shame about this to last a lifetime"), the counselor validated her grief ("you don't have to pretend otherwise") and framed slips as survivable. "We're not signing a contract, we're exploring" reflected unconditional positive regard without judgment or labeling.

---

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, consistent reflective listening. Early reflection—"part of you deeply wants this... and part of you wonders if you can actually do it. I hear both"—captured ambivalence precisely. Later reflections ("losing an old companion is real grief, even when it's poison"; naming her "you've blown it" voice) demonstrated deep understanding of her shame, fear of failure, and attachment to the habit.

---

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus on smoking cessation. Each exchange moved toward a concrete goal—from values, to the first step (delaying the morning cigarette), to implementation details (tea, breakfast, crossword, walk to school)—ending with a plan Anisa restated herself. No aimless drift; exploration always served the target behavior.

---

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative. Counselor invited co-creation ("would you like to think that through together?"); Anisa responded "Let's think it through together." Anisa proposed the delay strategy, the counselor helped problem-solve the delayed hour, and Anisa designed her own replacement routine. Decisions were genuinely co-constructed.

---

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Counselor consistently reinforced ownership and choice: "this is my pace" (noted by Anisa herself), "we're not signing a contract," "what would feel like a fair, kind first step for you?" Never prescriptive; the plan flexed when Anisa rejected journaling. Anisa confirmed: "this felt like a conversation, not a lecture."

---

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn from within the client via open evocative questions ("What matters most to you?", "What's the one piece... you feel most confident will stick?"). Anisa's motivation deepened visibly—from "I want to try" to "I'm worth it too" to vividly picturing a smoke-free morning ("Nice strange, though").

---

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located in Anisa: "You know yourself and this habit better than anyone"; "you're the expert on your own life." Her contributions directly shaped the session—her tea/breakfast idea became the core plan, built on her self-observation that she never smokes with a cup in her hand. Power sharing was genuine and impactful.

---

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Change talk skillfully evoked and reinforced: "There's real hope in what you just said—that'd mean something to me"; "'I want to try' is powerful, especially alongside 'I'm worth it.'" Anisa's change talk strengthened over the session ("I can picture it already... no ashtray on the table"), indicating effective cultivation.

---

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk consistently acknowledged without amplification, then pivoted toward change. The "old friend" metaphor was validated as grief but tied to her strengths ("your mother's stubbornness and your own tiredness of feeling gross are both on your side"). All-or-nothing thinking was reflected as her own insight for resilience; sustain talk was never reinforced.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score:** **5.0/5**

**Summary:** An exemplary MI session. The counselor maintained unconditional positive regard amid shame and fear of failure, used emotionally attuned reflections (including ambivalence and her "you've blown it" voice), skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk, and consistently framed Anisa as the expert designing her own plan. The session progressed from values exploration to a concrete, self-authored first step (delaying the morning cigarette with tea, breakfast, and a crossword), within a fully collaborative, autonomy-supportive relationship. Minor growth areas: deeper earlier exploration of the grief of letting go, and gently exploring how to involve her daughter in support. Overall, this models high-fidelity MI practice.
