# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with validating statements like 'That makes sense' and 'I respect you choosing what's doable.' Limited explicit affirmation of Rowan's strengths and somewhat formulaic warmth kept this from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered accurate reflections capturing irritation, ambivalence, and worry about forgetting, but often followed them immediately with questions. Deeper emotional content — fear that compliance means 'admitting everyone was right,' exhaustion, spite dynamic with his sister — was acknowledged briefly rather than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on the device-charging target with no topic drift, progressing logically from ambivalence to scaling to a concrete plan. Guided toward change consistently without forcing it.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely co-created the plan through brainstorming and letting Rowan select steps, scripts, and backups that fit him. Stacking multiple questions in one turn sometimes shifted the conversational balance and felt checklist-like.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength: 'It's your call,' 'Totally okay you're not promising,' and repeated offering of choices reinforced Rowan's ownership. Accepted his 'no promises' stance and framed the plan as an experiment he controls.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from Rowan through scaling questions and eliciting upsides, with his own words carrying the change rationale. Could have dug deeper into what change would mean for him personally beyond stopping the nagging.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Rowan as the expert, with his contributions substantially shaping the plan (bedtime plug-in, pillow placement, direct script). The rapid multiple-question format occasionally made the exchange feel more checklist-like than exploratory.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Scaling questions and elicitation of benefits moved confidence from 2 to 6–7, culminating in 'Tonight. I'll do it.' Reinforced commitment by having him specify concrete steps and practice the boundary script; deeper elaboration of his own reasons would elevate this further.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and used some reframing ('Trying it as an experiment is reasonable'), but eliciting 'benefits of not charging' and restating resistance gave the status quo extra airtime. Limited strategic pivoting or double-sided reflection to shift weight toward change.
