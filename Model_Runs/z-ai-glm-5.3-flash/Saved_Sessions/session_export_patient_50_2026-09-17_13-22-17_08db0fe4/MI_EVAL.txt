# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, but offered no affirmations of Hana's honesty, courage, or past successes. Her powerful disclosure about the work presentation that 'turned out great' was completely ignored — a clear missed opportunity.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was nearly absent; the psychiatrist relied on closed factual questions and responded to emotional disclosures with checklists rather than reflections. Hana's shame, fear of failure, and spontaneous hope ('right now I mostly feel kind of hopeful? That's new') went entirely unacknowledged.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained unwavering focus on the target behavior — preparing for and taking the certification exam — from the first exchange, with every turn advancing the goal. Arguably narrow (logistics-focused), but strong within this criterion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction was markedly unequal: the psychiatrist delivered lengthy expert monologues (12-topic curriculum, week-by-week schedules) while Hana mostly responded and approved. She received a plan rather than building one, and her relevant work experience was never explored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered genuine micro-choices (grounding vs. study plan, scheduling options), giving Hana some control. However, the overall structure was directive, with solutions offered before she asked and change presumed as the agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied on giving advice, schedules, and scripts rather than eliciting Hana's own motivations, values, or confidence. The one good evocative question about past success was never followed up to deepen her spontaneous motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Assumed the expert role for most of the session, delivering prescriptive strategies and detailed multi-week plans with little client input. Hana's contributions shaped details only after the framework was already imposed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Hana generated substantial change talk herself, but the psychiatrist made almost no deliberate effort to evoke it — no importance, confidence, or vision questions. The single effective evocative question's payoff (the praised presentation) was never reflected back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Hana repeatedly voiced sustain talk about failure and embarrassment, which was never addressed through reflection, reframing, or normalization. Her core fear-of-failure barrier remains unresolved beneath the logistical plan.
