# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with affirmations of Marco's effort and explicit acceptance of his conditions (no lectures, no overdone congratulations, being short on the phone). Warmth occasionally felt formulaic, and his resilience through major health issues was not explicitly affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflective statements like "You've tried a lot and it's worn you down" showed some understanding, but reflections were mostly content-level restatements. Little attention was paid to the emotional weight behind Marco's resignation after open-heart surgery, cancer, or his partner giving up nagging.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking reduction throughout, never wandering despite deflective humor. It concluded with a specific, actionable commitment: two cigarettes dropped daily and a Wednesday 2:00pm check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly solicited Marco's input on which cigarettes to drop, check-in timing, and what to focus on or avoid in the call. However, key plan elements (skipping three cigarettes, pack out of sight, swapping the routine) were counselor-proposed rather than co-generated from Marco's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: explicit acceptance of Marco's terms, "I'll support whatever you choose, no pressure," and framing slips as information rather than failure. The low-pressure experiment framing directly countered Marco's fear of coercion, which he acknowledged ("you're the first one in a while who hasn't tried to shove me").

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions were used early, but four were stacked at once, which Marco flagged. Motivation was evoked mainly through the experiment framing rather than exploring Marco's own reasons, values, or confidence, leaving his motivations more assumed than elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marco's preferences shaped the plan, including which cigarettes to drop, a Wednesday afternoon 2:00pm call, mobile contact, and avoiding feelings questions. It fell short of 5 because the core plan structure was counselor-proposed rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The one-week experiment framing effectively moved Marco from precontemplation ("I can't see myself changing") to concrete commitment ("mid-morning and after-dinner. Those I'll drop"). Confidence talk and self-efficacy were not deepened, leaving room for growth.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced; the sacred coffee cigarette was deferred rather than argued, and slips were reframed as "information, not failure." Some reluctance ("tired of lectures") was restated repeatedly without actively reframing to resolve the ambivalence.
