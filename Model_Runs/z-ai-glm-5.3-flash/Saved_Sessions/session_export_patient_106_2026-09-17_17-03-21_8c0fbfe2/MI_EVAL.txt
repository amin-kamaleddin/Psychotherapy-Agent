# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout ('Whatever you decide, I'm here to support you'; 'If you smoke, it's information, not failure') with a warm, nonjudgmental tone. The client explicitly valued this: 'Thanks for not lecturing me—that's why I keep coming back.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrates highly accurate reflective listening, capturing both the client's values and barriers ('your one sacred break after relentless days') and her fear of failure. Also responds skillfully to subtle cues and practical requests in a way that shows understanding of her exhausted, time-pressed life.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, progressive focus on smoking cessation with each exchange building toward a specific plan: rulers, barriers, a concrete experiment, a logging format, and scheduled follow-up. Incorporates the parenting concern as a motivational anchor without drifting off target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The experiment is fully co-constructed: the client modified the delay time, chose test nights around her real schedule, and negotiated her own backup plan. Phrases like 'try a tiny experiment together' reinforce equality and joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces client ownership and frames advice as options rather than directives, repeatedly offering choice ('Would that feel doable, or would you prefer a different tiny step?'). Respects the client's stated limits rather than pushing additional content.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Elicits motivation from within the client through open questions and rulers, drawing out her own reasons (protecting Danny, his comment about smelling like 'the outside'). The psychiatrist provides scaffolding but never argues for change herself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Actively positions the client as the expert by asking permission and deferring to her judgment at every step. The client's contributions shaped the session substantially, and her closing comment ('treating me like someone capable') confirms genuine power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk via rulers and follow-up probing ('What would need to happen to move your confidence even a couple points?'), leading the client to generate her own plan and intrinsic motivators. Her commitment language grew organically through the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoids amplifying sustain talk and redirects effectively toward change (affirming existing harm reduction, reframing relapse). Missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections when ambivalence emerged (e.g., the wine comparison, 'he doesn't have asthma'), which would have let the client hear the discrepancy herself.
