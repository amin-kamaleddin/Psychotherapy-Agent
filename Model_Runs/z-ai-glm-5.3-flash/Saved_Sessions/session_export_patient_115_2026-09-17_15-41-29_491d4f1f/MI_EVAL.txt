# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard with specific, genuine affirmations. Respectfully agreed to postpone smoking discussion with no disapproval of continued behaviors.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening and validation of partial progress, but deeper emotional reflections were limited. Could have explored fatigue, resignation ('that's just how I am'), and the meaning of 'having the win' more fully.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, progressive focus on asthma management throughout. Each turn moved logically from exploration to planning and accountability with no drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully egalitarian interaction with a co-constructed plan. Counselor supplied structure while the patient supplied nearly all content and solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support from the opening line and choice offered at every step. Respected patient's limits and explicitly honored his request to postpone the smoking discussion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation consistently drawn from within the client via confidence rulers and future-vision questions. Client-generated ideas dominated, and change talk grew from a 3 to a 7.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client expertise consistently centered, with the patient choosing triggers, solutions, and accountability style. The closing comment about not getting the expected lecture confirms the egalitarian stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation using confidence rulers, 'one point up' questions, and future-focused prompts. Change talk grew steadily and every step was reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never amplified; acknowledged neutrally and pivoted to doable steps. Supported harm reduction rather than arguing for abstinence, letting the client arrive at his own change.
