# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and never labels Jason a problem drinker, but offers no affirmations of his strengths (e.g., calling his roommate) and repeatedly jumps to problem-solving, limiting warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections are brief and formulaic, and the counselor misses repeated emotional cues (anxiety about the hearing, fear of his mother finding out, worry about seeming shady), pivoting quickly to scripts and logistics instead.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on the referral and hearing prep, but targets impression management rather than the underlying behavior; upcoming birthday drinking plans are never redirected toward alcohol use.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Surface-level collaboration exists—Jason's edits and preferences are incorporated—but the counselor supplies all content and strategy while Jason only approves and rehearses, keeping the frame expert-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor consistently offers genuine choices ('Which feels right?', 'Role-play now or tweak?'), but gives directive advice tied to gaming the hearing rather than supporting Jason's ownership of behavioral change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation is almost entirely supplied by the counselor's tactical advice; open questions about Jason's own values, reasons, or relationship with alcohol are virtually absent, and spontaneous change talk is never developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor adapts drafts to Jason's rejections and invites his input on options, but acts as expert strategist while Jason's own ideas about change are never sought.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No effort is made to evoke change talk; when Jason offers 'I'm not gonna be an idiot about it,' the counselor responds with logistics instead of deepening it, and goal–behavior discrepancy is never elicited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Substantial sustain talk ('this isn't even my fault,' 'not saying I have a problem') is sidestepped rather than worked with; there are no double-sided reflections or pivots toward change language.
