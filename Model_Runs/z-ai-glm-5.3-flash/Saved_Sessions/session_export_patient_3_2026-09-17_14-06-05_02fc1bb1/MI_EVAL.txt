# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, affirming Aisha's motivation and plan without labeling or disapproval. However, affirmations are brief and generic rather than deep, individualized reflections of her values and courage.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Empathy is limited to one early reflection that Aisha corrected; her disclosures about body-image distress, guilt, and self-consciousness receive no emotional reflection. Responses jump quickly to advice and procedural questions rather than understanding her inner experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, consistent focus on starting exercise from the opening question to the close, guiding the transition from motivation to a concrete, actionable plan. The session never drifts from the goal, appropriate for a preparation-stage client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor's contributions dominate through detailed plans, calendar formats, and pre-written scripts, with collaboration mostly superficial. Aisha's good ideas are accepted but not built upon or given real weight in shaping direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There are some autonomy-supportive elements, such as inviting Aisha to pick times and tweak message drafts. However, the overall style is highly prescriptive, offering choice at the margins rather than at the level of strategy, and her one correction of the counselor's framing went unacknowledged.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Beyond a few opening questions, virtually all content is counselor-generated advice rather than drawn from the client. Aisha's rich intrinsic motivations — feeling like herself, escaping guilt, self-kindness — are never explored or linked to the plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role, delivering prescriptive top-down lists and drafted scripts rather than acknowledging Aisha's expertise about her own life. There is little exploration of what she already knows works for her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Aisha arrives with abundant spontaneous change talk that the counselor partially reinforces, and the readiness scale is a reasonable evocative tool. However, the counselor never deepens or elaborates her change talk — no exploration of why this matters or what makes this time different — and her emerging excitement goes unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is minimal, and the counselor appropriately does not amplify it; the 10-minute fallback effectively lowers the stakes. However, barriers are addressed mainly via practical problem-solving tips rather than double-sided reflections helping Aisha resolve ambivalence in her own words.
