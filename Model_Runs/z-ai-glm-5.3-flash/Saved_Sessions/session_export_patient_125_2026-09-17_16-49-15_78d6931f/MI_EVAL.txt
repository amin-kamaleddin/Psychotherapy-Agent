# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Consistently respectful, non-judgmental stance; validated the unfairness narrative and affirmed the patient's record of compliance. Warm, encouraging tone throughout. Affirmations, however, were somewhat brief and procedural rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening capturing both emotional content and practical stakes (e.g., "I hear how much that call means... and how stuck you feel"); normalized voice cracking during role-play. Missed opportunities to explore deeper vulnerability (release anxiety, family separation, feeling singled out); reflections often pivoted quickly to planning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☐ 4 ☒ **5**
**Comments:** Exemplary focus. Session moved from venting to a fully operationalized plan — script, "no" response, appeal letter, and timing strategy — all tied directly to the patient's values (family connection, clean record, successful release). Role-play was directional and skill-specific.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Consistently operated between equals; co-constructed the script, rehearsed jointly, and decided on timing together. The clinician drafted scripts and delivery tips mostly unilaterally rather than co-authoring line by line.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice and ownership ("Which option feels most useful?", "Which do you prefer?"); patient's decisions genuinely shaped the session flow; no pressure or persuasion. Offerings were sometimes pre-packaged menus rather than open explorations of the patient's own ideas.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn out rather than instilled — patient's self-generated insight ("I guess arguing got my phone taken in the first place") demonstrates this. However, explicit evocative questions ("What matters most about getting home?") were largely absent; the clinician relied more on offering options and practice.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Patient positioned as the expert; his preferences (personal tone, practicing the "no" response first, requesting the appeal letter) drove the agenda. Clinician was somewhat quick to supply structure rather than letting the patient generate more content himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Strong elicitation of motivation — change talk emerged organically ("I don't wanna blow it over this"; "I can control me. Three weeks, keep my head down, get home to my mom"). Change talk was rarely explicitly reflected back or deepened when it occurred (e.g., missing elaboration questions).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐ 1 ☐ 2 ☐ 3 ☒ **4** ☐ 5
**Comments:** Never argued with or amplified the blame narrative; acknowledged the grievance once, then pivoted skillfully to agency-focused solutions. Allowed the patient to resolve it himself ("I can't control him. I can control me"). A 5 would require more deliberate reframing or double-sided reflections earlier in the session.

---

### Overall Score: 4.1 / 5

**Supervisor Summary:** A strong MI performance with a frustrated, guarded adolescent. Standout strengths include exceptional direction, effective handling of sustain talk, and robust autonomy support and partnership — resulting in visible client change talk and self-generated insight. To reach excellence: (1) deepen emotional reflections beyond surface frustration; (2) use more open evocative questions; (3) co-author content rather than supplying it; (4) slow down to reflect and deepen change talk before moving to logistics.
