# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard ('I'm not here to judge') with no disapproval despite the patient's defensiveness. Affirmations throughout convey warmth and respect.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including an accurate double-sided reflection and a precise final summary of the patient's values. Reflections occasionally affirm rather than deepen emotional nuance, such as fear of parental consequences.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear focus on alcohol use and harm reduction, with every question advancing the target toward a concrete, client-owned plan. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed from the patient's ideas, reinforcing equality. However, the counselor does not explicitly negotiate the agenda or address the patient's reluctance about the externally initiated referral.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces the patient's ownership of choice ('on your terms'), with no directives or pressure. Autonomy is affirmed even in moments of doubt.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Never provides reasons for change; all motivations emerge from the patient himself (reputation, parents, control). The patient moves from precontemplative minimization to self-generated commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly locates expertise within the patient, and the entire plan emerges from his own contributions. Exemplary power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes change talk through open questions, moving from hypothetical warning signs to concrete plans to explicit commitment language. Each instance is reinforced with affirmations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argues with sustain talk, using reframes and double-sided reflections to pivot toward the patient's goals. Some sustain talk (e.g., 'didn't need a whole meeting') is acknowledged rather than strategically reframed.
