# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor conveyed unconditional positive regard from the opening ('I'm here to listen without judgment') and validated Margaret's perspective throughout, never criticizing her drinking or reacting defensively to her dismissiveness. Warmth and respect were maintained even at the start when she minimized the concern as 'a fuss over nothing.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured both sides of her ambivalence well ('You sound torn—your pub nights mean a lot, and you don't want another fall'). Reflections occasionally stayed at the content level and could have explored deeper emotional meaning, such as her fear after the fall or the pub's role since retirement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a consistent focus on alcohol reduction and fall risk, with each exchange advancing toward a concrete plan. The session ended with a fully specified, measurable plan (Mon–Thu abstinence, coping strategies, safety steps, follow-up) with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was genuinely equal: the counselor asked permission before sharing information, offered menus rather than prescriptions, and invited Margaret to tailor refusal lines herself. Her closing remark that the session was 'more useful than I expected' and that she 'didn't need to quit' confirmed the plan felt like hers.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly affirmed Margaret's choice and fully accepted her refusal to quit pub weekends without pressure. Her explicit ownership ('I made that rule myself, so I'll keep it') is clear evidence of successful autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within Margaret using evocative questions and importance/confidence ratings, moving her from 'I don't see the problem myself' to self-generated commitment ('this is about staying on my feet'). The counselor never argued for change—Margaret's own words carried her there.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located within Margaret ('You're in charge of what we explore', 'pick one that feels natural'), and nearly every element of the plan—weekday rule, cordial, Jean phone call, refusal lines—was her idea. Information was positioned as an offer rather than instruction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked via importance/confidence scaling and menu-based brainstorming, yielding 'I'd say an eight' and 'I suppose I could cut down some.' Margaret then developed her own plan and commitment language, which the counselor consistently reinforced and built upon.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections acknowledged her attachment to pub nights while pivoting toward change, and 'I don't need to quit' was acknowledged without argument. A point is withheld because sustain talk ('seven drinks isn't much') was never explicitly reframed, though it was never reinforced and diminished over the session.
