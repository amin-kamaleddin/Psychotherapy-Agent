# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently affirmed the patient's perspective and worth, validating frustration with the mandated referral. Acceptance was slightly limited by not gently exploring the discrepancy between 'I'm not a big drinker' and the screening flag.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the patient's feelings of unfairness and being misunderstood throughout the session. Empathy was strongest early on and became somewhat more summary-focused as the session progressed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained a clear focus on the patient's priorities and how drinking fit alongside them. However, direction toward a concrete conversation about actual drinking behavior was never fully achieved.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly framed decisions as shared, asking what the patient wanted from the sessions and offering choices at the end. Collaboration could have been deepened by inviting more joint agenda-setting early in the conversation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support throughout — the clinician emphasized 'you're the expert on your own life,' took the patient at their word, and offered genuine choices about session format and focus.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions successfully evoked the patient's own warning signs, coping plans, and values around band and academics. Evocation of sustained self-motivation was strong, though the patient's ambivalence about the referral itself was never fully drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The tone was consistently side-by-side, with reflective listening and questions that positioned the patient as the decision-maker. The partnership was effective given a guarded, mandated adolescent, though some questions led rather than followed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The clinician skillfully elicited preparatory change talk — warning signs, planned responses, and priorities. However, change talk was not deepened toward commitment language or a concrete plan beyond vague intentions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'm not a big drinker,' 'the flag was overblown') was acknowledged and reflected without confrontation, and doubt ('maybe that's naive') was explored non-judgmentally. The clinician could have more actively rolled with this sustain talk to develop discrepancy.
