# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, with specific genuine affirmations ('That's not small, Derrick'). Explicitly honored both sides of Derrick's ambivalence without labeling or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate, emotionally attuned reflections, including the opening reflection capturing both minimization and the cost of past sobriety attempts. Reflections consistently landed, evidenced by Derrick escalating in honesty each turn.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior while remaining client-centered, moving progressively from ambivalence to concrete strategies and a follow-up commitment. Guided toward change without derailing or topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative: open-ended questions, agenda emerging from Derrick's own words, and a plan co-constructed from his ideas (cab money, basketball, Marcus). The closing 'Deal?' symbolized equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy reinforced at every turn ('Those are your plans, not mine'), with no directives and full client ownership of the plan and pace. Derrick explicitly noted he was never controlled or labeled.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn entirely from within the client through evocative questions, never supplying reasons to change. Derrick's self-persuasion ('you let me talk myself into it') represents the gold standard of MI evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise consistently located with the client ('You know your world better than I do'), and Derrick's ideas genuinely shaped the session direction. Power sharing confirmed by the client himself.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk evoked and developed masterfully via highlighting the client's own words, asking what already worked, and forward-looking questions. Elicited sophisticated commitment language ('I'll call Marcus... I'll be back next week').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided amplifying sustain talk, using double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk. Minor deduction for the final 'third strike' consequence framing, which leaned away from change language.
