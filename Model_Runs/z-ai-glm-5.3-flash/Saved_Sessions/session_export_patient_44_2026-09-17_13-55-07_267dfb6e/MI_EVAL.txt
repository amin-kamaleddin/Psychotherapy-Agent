# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations of progress and Erik feels safe disclosing vulnerabilities. Affirmations are brief and formulaic rather than deep, individualized validation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is largely absent; emotional disclosures about shame, comparison, and fear of failure were met with bullet-point strategies rather than reflections of the underlying feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays focused on target behaviors and progresses logically from barriers to plan to relapse prevention with a clear two-week outcome. Direction is driven by information delivery rather than collaborative goal clarification, and diet is dropped without explicit deferral.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely advisor-and-recipient, with all plans, logs, and scripts counselor-designed. Erik supplies the collaborative energy while the counselor never explores what Erik thinks would work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some respect for choice is shown through options and letting Erik decide on logging and sharing with his wife. However, the overall style is prescriptive with 'no negotiation' framing, and Erik's ownership of decisions is not actively reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The counselor makes almost no effort to elicit Erik's own motivations, reasons, or confidence; nearly all motivational content comes from Erik or is left unexplored. Questions like 'Why does this matter to you?' are never asked, implying solutions reside with the counselor.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, delivering prescriptive strategies while Erik's own good insights go undrawn upon. Occasional choice questions appear, but the substance of every response is counselor-supplied solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nearly all change talk is spontaneously generated by Erik and is never deliberately evoked, deepened, or reinforced. No scaling questions, amplifying reflections, or eliciting of commitment language occur.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Erik's substantial sustain talk about relapse, all-or-nothing thinking, and frustration is consistently skipped over in favor of more tips. No double-sided reflections, validation, or reframing are used to address the ambivalence.
