# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, even amid hostility and defensiveness, with validating statements like 'That resistance makes sense.' Falls short of top marks because affirmations were general rather than deeply personalized (e.g., his loyalty, 25-year marriage).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening captured Richard's ambivalence accurately (e.g., valuing his ritual while worrying about the marriage). Reflections were often brief, pivoting quickly to advice rather than deepening poignant moments like 'I don't want to lose her' and his fear of failure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the target behavior and marital strain, never allowing drift despite Richard's initial resistance. Every exchange built momentum toward a concrete, time-limited plan with a follow-up check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered options and incorporated some of Richard's suggestions (water glass, crossword). However, the counselor generated most solutions and agendas unilaterally, making Richard a compliant refiner rather than an equal co-creator.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and explicitly normalized not being ready, accepting caveat-laden commitments without pressure. Rapid-fire advice lists at times reduced genuine choice space, with little explicit reinforcement of Richard's ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The likes/dislikes exercise surfaced relational motivation and rehearsal drew out commitment language. Much of the session relied on counselor-supplied reasons and plans, missing opportunities to explore Richard's own values, confidence, or imagined outcomes (e.g., no scaling questions).

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices at key junctures, but much of the session shifted into directive, expert-driven coaching with scripts, tips, and alarms. Richard's own expertise about his routines was underutilized.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively moved Richard from 'there's no problem' to volunteering a two-week behavior change and possible self-initiated return. Change talk was elicited mainly through structured exercises rather than deeper evocative questions about importance and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently rolled with sustain talk without arguing, and reframes like 'this is about keeping the relationship intact' effectively pivoted away from quitting-as-identity. Some sustain framing (e.g., 'won't promise to quit') was accepted without gentle double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence.
