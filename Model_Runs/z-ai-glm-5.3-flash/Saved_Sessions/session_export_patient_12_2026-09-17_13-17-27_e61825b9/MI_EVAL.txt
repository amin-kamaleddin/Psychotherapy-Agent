# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgmental regard with respectful early safety screening ('Relapse doesn't erase the progress you made. You're not worthless for it'). Warmth is somewhat formulaic and checklist-like, lacking individualized affirmation of Amira as a person rather than a relapse case.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections (loneliness, exhaustion, 'distractions didn't help'), but frequent pivots to menus and plans cut short emotional processing. The core fear — 'I stop caring — that's the part that scares me' — was never deeply reflected or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed focused on the target behavior and its antecedents (loneliness, Friday nights), moving from relapse exploration to a concrete test and next-week follow-up. Direction was maintained via prescriptive structure rather than collaborative goal clarification, and emotional focus drifted into plan mechanics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative framing was present ('we'll figure out next steps together'), but the clinician supplied most of the substance — plan, script, reminder text — while Amira mostly accepted, modified, or declined. Bidirectional shaping of the session was limited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice was consistently offered with no pressure when Amira declined options, and she retained control ('No promises about the not-caring part'). However, specific interventions (5-4-3-2-1 grounding, alarm scripts) were delivered as recommendations rather than generated from her preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied by the clinician's reframes rather than drawn from Amira; her reasons for change, values, and past sobriety were never explored. Final commitment language ('I'll try it') appears compliance-driven rather than internalized.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices were repeatedly offered, but the session's structure was clinician-authored via pre-packaged scripts, alarm labels, and directives. Amira's own expertise about her Friday nights was never explicitly acknowledged or invited into plan design.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some change talk emerged ('A walk, maybe. That I could do,' 'I'll set the alarm'), and the clinician offered small steps Amira partially adopted. However, commitment language was extracted through yes/no plan selection rather than deepened with open questions about her own reasons and hopes.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently reframed, with the effective 'information, not failure' frame internalized by Amira. A deduction for missing double-sided reflections linking her doubt to her demonstrated resilience during her prior period of sobriety.
