# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, reframing relapses as common setbacks rather than failures. Affirmations like 'Nice' and 'You're doing solid work' added warmth, but were brief and formulaic rather than expressing deeper unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated good reflective listening, accurately capturing Marco's frustration and the fatigue-driven evening snacking pattern. Reflections stayed at a practical level and missed deeper emotional layers like guilt ('why bother') and fear of blowing it.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session tightly focused on evening snacking and the home food environment from exploration through concrete planning and defined follow-up metrics. A 5 would require a more deliberate progression through the client's own values and motivations rather than moving quickly into solutions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Co-constructed elements of the plan — Marco chose the short if-then, softened the wife script, and suggested Sunday prep, all incorporated. However, the flow was largely counselor-generated recommendations followed by Marco's approval, never eliciting Marco's own ideas about barriers or strategies first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and let Marco pick the follow-up time, plan components, and script style with no pressure or coercion. The high volume of unsolicited concrete advice leaned controlling in flavor; stronger support would frame options as Marco's to generate.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was treated as residing in the counselor's plan rather than drawn from the client — no exploration of why change matters, his values (kids, marriage), or follow-up on his volunteered confidence rating of six-to-seven. Marco's own key insight ('That's probably the biggest change here, honestly') went unexplored, with change talk emerging only incidentally from planning.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited input and incorporated Marco's corrections, showing some responsiveness to his expertise. The style remained predominantly advice-driven with prescriptive step lists generated by the counselor rather than elicited, leaving the expert stance dominant.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Positive momentum emerged incidentally — Marco raised his own confidence from six to seven and said the plan felt 'real' — but this was a byproduct of collaborative planning, not deliberate evocation. Key opportunities were missed, including exploring the confidence rating and deepening Marco's spontaneous insight about the biggest change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Responded reasonably to Marco's doubts ('it's gonna be really hard,' 'I still half-expect to blow it') without amplifying them, offering practical accommodations and a compassionate slip framing he embraced. However, sustain talk was never directly reflected or processed with double-sided reflections to help Marco resolve ambivalence.
