# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicated respect and nonjudgment toward the patient's autonomy, never criticizing her decision to keep the piercing. However, acceptance was expressed formulaically ('I respect that' repeated) rather than through individualized affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured the patient's stated positions but remained at the level of simple factual summaries. Missed opportunities to reflect emotional subtext, such as her desire for change, resentment of judgment, and emerging concern about tooth damage.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on piercing-related oral health throughout, with each turn ending in a question guiding toward the goal. Some information blocks and option menus slightly diffused focus rather than sharpening a single change target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asked permission, offered choices at every juncture, and built the plan from the patient's own preferences. The early risk-information block was delivered in an expert-lecture format rather than negotiated collaboratively.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly affirmed the patient's freedom of choice, explicitly respecting her refusal to remove the piercing and gracefully dropping rejected suggestions. Occasionally supplied long recommendation lists and a pre-drafted text, edging toward advice-giving.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective scaling and evocative questions produced client-generated commitments with 8/10 confidence, but motivation was drawn out only at a surface level. The counselor relied on supplying options rather than exploring the patient's own values and reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the patient as the decision-maker, and her preferences directly shaped the final plan. The dense early block of expert information shifted the interaction toward a directive-advisory mode.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully moved the patient from dismissiveness to concrete commitments (texting the piercer, confidence 8/10). Change talk was evoked but not deepened — motivation, values, and what success would look like were never explored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with sustain talk, instead pivoting to feasible options and keeping the closing summary focused on commitments. Double-sided reflections were never used, and one summary repeated her sustain position more than necessary.
