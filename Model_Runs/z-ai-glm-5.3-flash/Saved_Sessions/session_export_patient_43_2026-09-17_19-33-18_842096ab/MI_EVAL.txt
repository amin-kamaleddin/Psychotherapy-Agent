# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warmth and nonjudgmental regard are consistent, with genuine affirmations like 'you took her concern seriously enough to check it out' and the powerful acceptance statement 'Both of those things are true for you right now.' Affirmations are somewhat sparse given Eric's guardedness, and no closing affirmation of his autonomy was offered.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is a clear strength, with accurate complex reflections such as 'You'd rather carry the cough than carry her anxiety about you' that produced significant deepening. The score falls short due to the first-person reflection error where the psychiatrist voiced Eric's content as his own experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus stays tightly on smoking and its consequences, with a coherent, purposeful question sequence that moved a pre-contemplative client toward contemplation. However, the session never consolidates into a forward-looking next step or summary, ending on reflection rather than anchoring the 'in between' idea Eric raised.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is fully collaborative — the psychiatrist asks rather than tells, and Eric's contributions genuinely shape the session, with his ladder mention becoming the centerpiece reflection. Eric's closing question shows he treats the conversation as a shared enterprise, with no expert-driven stance at any point.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is exemplary — 'I'm not here to talk you out of either one' and 'not for your wife, but for yourself' reinforce ownership, and the clinician never advises, warns, or prescribes. Eric explicitly names this as the reason he opened up: 'You're not pushing, and that's... different.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn entirely from within — the clinician never educates about risks or cites the doctor's warnings as leverage, instead eliciting Eric's own values around livelihood, pride, and his wife's peace of mind. Eric's acknowledgment 'You're asking me questions I've never let myself sit with' is strong evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly and repeatedly locates expertise within the client ('You know yourself better than anyone here'), maintaining a genuinely egalitarian dynamic. This partnership is especially notable given Eric's guardedness, and it demonstrably enabled his honest self-reflection.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** This is the session's standout domain — strategic questions like 'What would be your first sign?' and 'what would it take for you to feel ready... for yourself?' elicited client-generated desire and ability statements. Eric's language shifted noticeably from 'nothing serious' to 'maybe there's something in between doing nothing and quitting cold turkey.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist largely avoids reinforcing Eric's heavy sustain talk, pivoting instead to affirmations and future-oriented hypotheticals, and 'I'm not here to talk you out of either one' defuses defensiveness well. Some reflections repeat sustain talk nearly verbatim at length, giving the 'giving up something I enjoy' rationalization extended airtime.
