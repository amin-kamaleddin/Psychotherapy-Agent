# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, validates Mateo's struggles, and explicitly frames potential setbacks as informative rather than failures. No judgmental or disapproving language appears anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are consistently accurate and emotionally attuned, naming Mateo's ambivalence precisely and validating both the physical pain and psychological response. Mateo's 'Yeah, that's it exactly' confirms the reflections landed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a clear, purposeful focus on rehab engagement with a logical arc from exploring manageable steps to a concrete, time-bound plan. There is no topic drift, and even reflections serve the directional goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session operates as genuine dialogue between equals, with every substantive step co-constructed and ideas sourced from the client (calendar, recording games, sleep timing). Mateo's closing comment—'you got me thinking about what I can actually do instead'—confirms he experienced it as collaborative.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor defers all decisions to the client, never imposes advice, and reinforces Mateo's ownership ('the pain might not be fully in your hands, but how you come at those mornings partly is'). Even potential failure is framed as acceptable, preserving choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All key content originates from Mateo—the sleep solution, pacing insight, calendar system, and slip planning—with the counselor never supplying reasons for change. Evocative questions target ability, need/reason, and commitment, honoring the client's own motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly and explicitly locates expertise within Mateo ('you know your body best'), and the calendar idea comes entirely from him. The closing affirmations make the power-sharing explicit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor systematically evokes change talk through open questions, exception questions, and values-based probes that build progressively stronger client language, culminating in 'I've got a plan... and it's mine.' Each turn follows Mateo's change talk with a question that deepens it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally uses double-sided reflections to pivot toward change and redirects pain talk toward exceptions. The score is 4 rather than 5 because a few early reflections lingered slightly on pain and hopelessness before pivoting; tightening these pivots would be even more efficient.
