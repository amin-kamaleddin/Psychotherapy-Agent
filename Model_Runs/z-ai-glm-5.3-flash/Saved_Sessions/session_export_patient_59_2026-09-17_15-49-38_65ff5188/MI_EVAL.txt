# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and non-judgmental throughout, but warmth was superficial. Agreeing to edit the note to 'look good' for the PO crossed into capitulation rather than genuine unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections were shallow and repetitive, focusing on logistics rather than meaning. Failed to reflect Jamal's expressed feelings of unfairness or humiliation, and repeated the identical closing script despite visible irritation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Opened with a clear MI agenda, but the session immediately devolved into administrative territory (note wording, DOB, safety checkboxes). The target behavior was never revisited after the first exchanges.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction resembled a clerk servicing a client rather than two partners working on a problem. Session content was entirely ceded to Jamal with no joint agenda-setting or shared problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed Jamal's choices and avoided pressure about use. However, allowing him to dictate session content and edit the note was acquiescence, not principled autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Beyond the unanswered importance ruler, no effort was made to elicit Jamal's motivations, values, or goals. Motivation was never drawn out; the counselor settled for documenting attendance.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Choice-offering was more accommodation than genuine power-sharing. Jamal's expertise was never engaged and his input did not shape any substantive session content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** A reasonable opening evocation and importance ruler were used, but the '2' answer was never explored (e.g., 'What would make it a 4?'). The one crumb of change talk was reflected only as compliance.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Jamal's sustain talk ('nobody cares,' 'half the parents smoke') went largely unaddressed. No double-sided reflections, reframes, or pivots toward change; accommodating a skip of substantive discussion reinforced the status quo.
