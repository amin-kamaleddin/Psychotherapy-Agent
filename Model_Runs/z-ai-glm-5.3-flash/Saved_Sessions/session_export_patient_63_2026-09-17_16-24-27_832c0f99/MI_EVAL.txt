# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 **☑4** ☐5
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout ("no judgment" stated at outset). Jordan's disclosures about wanting to shoplift and peer loyalty were validated without shaming. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic, and opportunities to affirm deeper strengths (loyalty to friends, honesty about freezing in the moment) were missed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 **☑3** ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Reflective listening present but thin. Early summary reflection was decent; later reflections became brief labels ("Good choice," "That makes sense"). Did not deeply reflect the grief/conflict of Jordan's attachment to high school friends or the poignant moment when he admitted "yeah, kind of, I gotta stop."

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 ☐4 **☑5**
**Comments:** Unfailing focus on the target behavior (shoplifting/recidivism) in every exchange. Session progressed logically from triggers → options → selection → rehearsal → handling pushback → concrete plan. No drift; clear strength.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 **☑3** ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Role-plays were genuinely interactive and Jordan's feedback shaped the plan. However, overall pattern was counselor-as-consultant delivering prescriptive lists of solutions before exploring Jordan's own thinking; Jordan's request "help me figure out what that sounds like" was answered rather than co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 ☐3 **☑4** ☐5
**Comments:** Consistently framed choices as Jordan's ("Which feels most doable?", "Pick one that sounds like you"); Jordan genuinely chose his exit excuse, line, and pacing, with confidence rulers reinforcing ownership. Not a 5 due to rapid-fire option menus and prescriptive scripting that sometimes controlled the "how."

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 **☑2** ☐3 ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions came predominantly from the counselor. Almost no open questions eliciting Jordan's own reasons, values, or desired future. Court-avoidant motivation ("I want this behind me") never deepened into intrinsic motivation. Jordan's own insights ("maybe that's the real answer eventually"; "I gotta stop") were met with tactics rather than evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 **☑3** ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Some power-sharing ("Which of those feels most doable?"), but the counselor repeatedly positioned himself as the expert by generating nearly all solutions — scripts, excuse rotations, alarm systems — while Jordan explicitly said "I don't really know what that even looks like." Never asked "What ideas do you have?" or "What's worked for you before?"

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 **☑3** ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Confidence rulers effectively escalated change talk (4 → 5.5 → 7 → 7.5), and Jordan produced meaningful change statements ("I can't keep doing this"). However, the counselor did not strategically deepen change talk after it occurred — e.g., pivoted to logistics after "maybe that's the real answer eventually." No evocative questions about values or life without shoplifting.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☐1 ☐2 **☑3** ☐4 ☐5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not amplified or reinforced — solution orientation is a strength. However, sustain talk was largely bypassed rather than skillfully softened. Jordan's peer-loyalty ambivalence ("that's what makes it hard," "that's the one that gets under my skin") was met with more strategies instead of double-sided reflections.

---

**Overall Global Rating:** **3.3/5**

**Summary:** Strong structure, direction, and skills-based rehearsal with effective confidence rulers (4 → ~7.5) and a concrete, feasible plan. Primary weaknesses are MI-spirit and evocation: advice-heavy, prescriptive approach; missed intrinsic motivation moments; and peer-loyalty ambivalence met with tactics rather than empathic, double-sided reflection.

**Recommendations:**
1. Ask more open questions inviting the client's own solutions ("What have you tried before? What do you think would work?").
2. Use complex and double-sided reflections to hold ambivalence around peer loyalty.
3. When change talk or insight emerges, reflect and explore it rather than moving to logistics.
4. Explore Jordan's values and a valued future direction to build intrinsic, not just court-avoidant, motivation.
