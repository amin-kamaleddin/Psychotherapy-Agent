# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, normalizing defensiveness and affirming Tyler's efforts without labeling or disapproval. However, positive regard was somewhat brief and formulaic, pivoting quickly to questions and tips rather than engaging with Tyler's individual values like his scholarship and friendships.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections captured Tyler's ambivalence well, but rapid-fire question batteries and advice lists left little room to reflect deeper emotional content. Vulnerable moments — fear of losing his scholarship, feeling 'the boring one' among friends — were not deeply reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, consistent focus on Tyler's alcohol use and moved steadily toward concrete planning with no topic drift. The limitation is that direction was heavily counselor-driven, narrowing prematurely to harm-reduction tactics without confirming Tyler's broader goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was a collaborative tone with genuine interactive role-play and check-ins, but the counselor dominated with advice menus and scripts. Tyler mostly selected from counselor-authored options rather than co-creating strategies from his own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offered meaningful choices that genuinely shaped the session's direction and activities. Score is limited because once tips were introduced, specifics like the drink limit of 3 and code word were prescribed rather than generated by Tyler, slightly undercutting ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers, open questions, and role-play elicited genuine change talk from Tyler, who ended with self-motivated commitment. However, most strategies and scripts were counselor-provided, and key opportunities to amplify the scholarship discrepancy were met with logistics instead of exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Tyler's input was requested at key points and his choices shaped the session, but the counselor frequently assumed the expert role with prescriptive tip lists and scripts. Tyler selected among counselor-generated options rather than contributing his own expertise about his social world.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor successfully evoked some meaningful change talk using rulers and open questions, including Tyler's fear of conduct meetings. However, these statements were not deepened or reinforced — the scholarship fear was met with a logistics list rather than exploration of what his education means to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not argue with sustain talk and occasionally reframed it reasonably, but most sustain talk about drinking as fun passed without strategic double-sided reflections. The harm-reduction framing implicitly accepted continued drinking without helping Tyler resolve his ambivalence between drinking and his educational values.
