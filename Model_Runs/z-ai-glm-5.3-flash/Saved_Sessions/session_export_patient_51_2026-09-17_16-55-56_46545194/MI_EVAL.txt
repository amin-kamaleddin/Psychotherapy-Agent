# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirmations like 'You did the right thing checking' and accommodation of Ingrid's time constraints. However, warmth feels somewhat procedural and missed the chance to explicitly validate her honesty about avoiding the bigger picture.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ('stretched thin,' 'that makes total sense'), but multiple emotional disclosures ('a bit worrying,' 'tired of drifting,' low mood and patchy sleep) went unreflected. Responses repeatedly pivoted back to logistics rather than the feelings underneath.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a fragmented, compressed session oriented toward concrete outcomes: safety parameters, medication clarification, and a booked follow-up with a prioritized agenda. Repetitive back-and-forth over appointment logistics consumed space that could have been used more strategically.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The follow-up agenda was genuinely co-constructed from Ingrid's stated priorities. Collaboration is weakened by the unequal, expert-driven delivery of medical safety information without checking her understanding.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered structured choices (slots, duration, format, agenda priority), and Ingrid's own decisions shaped the plan. The medication discussion, however, was delivered didactically without inviting her perspective.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine evocative questions drew motivation and self-reflection, but this was largely confined to the booking phase. Rich material — years of on-and-off antidepressant use, fear of the interaction, the cost of 'drifting' — surfaced but was never developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ingrid's input shaped the session agenda and priorities, and her preferences were solicited at key points. The interaction often felt transactional and expert-driven, with directive monologues and instruction lists rather than genuine two-way dialogue.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited strong change talk through scaling, 'most important change' questions, and the 'win' question, producing commitment language like 'I'm tired of drifting.' Could have reinforced these statements more explicitly with affirmations or elaborative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or pushing against Ingrid's ambivalence and time pressure, letting her resolve wavering herself ('twenty is fine to start'). Could have more actively reframed her avoidance statements rather than simply moving past them.
