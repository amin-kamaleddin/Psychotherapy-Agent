# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, using affirming responses like 'That makes sense' even when Ethan minimized. Warmth was occasionally brief/transactional, and there was little explicit affirmation of Ethan's honesty or willingness to attend.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early and mid-session reflections were accurate, including capturing that consequences—not drinking itself—bothered Ethan. However, the counselor shifted toward rapid-fire advice and checklist options, skipping deeper reflection on Ethan's ambivalence and social fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on reducing risky alcohol use, moving progressively from exploration to a concrete plan (texting Marco, soda, refusal line, ride plan). Focus drifted somewhat toward logistics and safety advice rather than deeper motivation work.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ethan shaped the plan (Marco, soda, refusal wording), but the counselor often delivered pre-packaged option lists and scripts before he could brainstorm, making the flow feel like guidance-delivery rather than joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choice ('which feels realistic?', 'or something else?') and respected Ethan's limits and wording without pushback. Some prescriptive list turns and 'Will you send the text tonight?' edged toward steering.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited meaningful intrinsic motivations (hating the puking, avoiding getting busted) and used a confidence ruler. But the counselor frequently supplied facts, options, and scripts instead of drawing out and expanding Ethan's own reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked for Ethan's input and let him select realistic steps and his own refusal line. A six-directive list delivered in one turn took on an expert/educational tone, consulting his expertise on selecting rather than generating options.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk ('maybe I could pace myself', 'texting Marco tonight') via open questions and a confidence ruler. Options were sometimes supplied too quickly, and commitment language wasn't consolidated at the close.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Ethan's sustain talk ('not quitting altogether', 'not gonna be some angel') was acknowledged without argument or confrontation. However, no double-sided reflections or strategic pivots were used—sustain talk was tolerated rather than actively redirected.
