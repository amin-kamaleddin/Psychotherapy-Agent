# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with genuine affirmations and explicit honoring of the patient's honesty. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic, and ambivalence/slips were normalized only briefly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured both sides of her ambivalence and validated the function of smoking. Deeper emotional nuances, such as guilt and fear behind the residue disclosure, were sometimes missed in favor of information and tactics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session consistently focused on smoking and its impact on her son, culminating in a concrete agreed-upon first step. Direction came more through option-listing than strategic summaries linking next steps to her stated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative elements existed, including co-developing the final one-line plan, but the session initially shifted into an expert-driven mode with advice lists the patient rejected. True partnership emerged only after she pushed back twice.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed her choice and ownership, accepted her boundaries without pressure, and avoided lecturing — which the patient explicitly valued. At times pre-selected options flooded the conversation rather than leaving direction fully open.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions drew out her own reasons for change, but the default mode was supplying information rather than eliciting. The pivotal 'hitting different' moment passed without deepening, and no confidence/importance exploration or scaling was used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Inclusive language and permission-seeking were present, but substantial psychoeducation and option lists dominated with little input from her. Her expertise shaped the plan only after she insisted on it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicited change talk and commitment to a first step, and invited reflection on what felt hard or surprising. Key opportunities to deepen emerging motivation were missed, with no scaling or expansion of her reasons tied to her son.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing against sustain talk and did not reinforce it, which is a strength. However, repeated risk re-explanations and option lists prompted sustain talk, and double-sided reflections linking her coping needs to her values were largely absent.
