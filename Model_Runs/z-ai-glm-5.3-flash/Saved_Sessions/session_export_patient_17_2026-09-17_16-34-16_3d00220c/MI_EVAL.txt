# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with no judgment despite past failures. Warm affirmations throughout, culminating in 'I'm proud of you,' which visibly moved the client.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening captured the emotional core (shame, fear of failure, family pressure). Reflections were sometimes compressed and vulnerable disclosures were met with quick pivots to planning rather than deeper emotional exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on smoking cessation from start to finish. Every turn advanced the goal logically from motivation to a concrete plan with a scheduled 48-hour follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary partnership: permission was sought before advising, options offered rather than directives, and the plan was literally co-authored with the client's own ideas. Repeated check-ins reinforced joint problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice consistently reinforced ('I'll support whatever you decide'), with a patch trial offered instead of a quit date to respect her comfort level. The client ended with internalized ownership ('I can do this').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective evocative questions early on elicited genuine motivation ('For me too') and strong commitment language. However, evocation tapered in the latter half, where the counselor supplied more content than was elicited.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise was consistently located within the client and the plan drew on her own solutions. Slightly reduced because the counselor at times supplied substantial advisory content rather than eliciting her ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was actively elicited and anchored into concrete commitments (patch trial, trigger strategies, doctor message, check-in). Some deepening was missed, e.g., her hope ('I can do two weeks... probably') was not amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk around fear of failure was handled respectfully but not strategically softened—no double-sided reflections or reframing of failure as learning. Planning pivots avoided sustain talk but left her fear of failure largely unexplored.
