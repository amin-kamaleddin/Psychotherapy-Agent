# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, affirming Laura's ideas ('That's smart, actually') and normalizing her inconsistency without any labeling or disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured Laura's ambivalence and motivational structure, but reflections sometimes summarized rather than deepened emotional nuance, such as her surprise that health topped the list.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained crystal-clear focus on value-congruent health behavior, progressing logically from values to barriers to concrete commitment with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction was fully collaborative between equals, with Laura's own insights (volunteer-commitment structure, revised sticky note) genuinely shaping the plan and decisions co-constructed rather than prescribed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Laura's choice was repeatedly honored and permission sought before suggestions, but some practical suggestions (specific alert timings, mat placement) became somewhat prescriptive, slightly constraining ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently drew motivation from within Laura, using confidence scaling (6→7→8) and envisioning questions that produced strong self-generated change talk like 'Saturday morning, I'm on that mat.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor treated Laura as the expert and visibly incorporated her contributions, but occasionally relied on rapid-fire multi-part questions that steered rather than purely followed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation through scaling questions and envisioning produced strong commitment language ('I'll send the text right now—no more putting it off') and progressively rising confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor generally pivoted away from sustain talk toward solutions, and the reframe landed ('It's not all-or-nothing anymore'), though the intentional question about derailers invited some obstacle-focused talk that could have been more tightly reframed.
