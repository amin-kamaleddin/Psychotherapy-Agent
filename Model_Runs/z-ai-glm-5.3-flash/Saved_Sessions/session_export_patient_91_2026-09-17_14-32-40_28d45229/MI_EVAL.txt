# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone with normalization and self-compassion reminders, but praise is generic. Missed opportunities for specific affirmations of Marcus's honesty, motivation, and self-insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Minimal reflective listening; emotional statements about nervousness, fear of failure, and self-doubt were repeatedly bypassed in favor of logistics and tips. Only one early validating reflection was present.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on blood-sugar monitoring with a concrete, actionable plan and defined follow-up. Direction occasionally tipped into premature focusing before exploring concerns and confidence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partial collaboration—incorporated Marcus's pill-routine idea, alarm label, and his proposed check-in. However, the session was largely counselor-delivered guidance with limited room to co-construct.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some procedural choice-points offered (alarm wording, log template), but the overall style was prescriptive with instructions. Marcus's ownership of the change process was accommodated rather than actively reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Little effort to draw out Marcus's own motivations, values, or confidence—no open questions about why managing diabetes matters or past successes. His motivation emerged from his own cooperativeness, not skillful elicitation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor adopted an expert stance for most of the session, prescribing plans and clinical tips unprompted. Marcus's contributions were accepted but did not substantially shape the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Partially leveraged Marcus's change talk by reinforcing his pill-routine idea and enabling commitment language through the small-step plan. However, change talk was not deliberately evoked or deepened, and key commitments went unreflected.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus voiced meaningful doubt about sustaining the habit and fear of failing, but the counselor skipped over it in favor of logistics. No double-sided reflections or affirmations were used, though negative talk was not amplified.
