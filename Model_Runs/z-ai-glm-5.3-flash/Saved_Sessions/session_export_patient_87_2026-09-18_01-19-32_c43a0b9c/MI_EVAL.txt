# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful stance with several good affirmations and non-judgmental validation of past failed attempts. Affirmations tend to praise the content of the patient's reasoning rather than express personal warmth or normalize the struggle.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate complex reflections including emotionally nuanced reflections the patient confirmed. Occasionally reads as rehearsed or quote-heavy, and deeper emotions like shame and repeated failure were touched but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behavior with progressive momentum toward a self-generated plan. The session closes with a contingency question rather than a consolidating summary, explicit commitment, or follow-up plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative: the entire plan originated from the patient's contributions, which the clinician took up and built on. Power-sharing was genuine, with the patient's ideas demonstrably determining the direction of the intervention.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding autonomy support—every strategic choice was returned to the patient, with flexible pacing and ownership of both success and setbacks affirmed. No coercive, persuasive, or expert-imposing language anywhere; the patient explicitly noted he didn't feel told what to do.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The approach was consistently evocative rather than prescriptive, with questions deliberately directing the patient to his own internal resources. The patient generated his own plan, motivation, and safeguards, and self-monitored his confidence shift from six to seven.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary partnership—expertise and ownership were explicitly located with the patient throughout, and all ideas originated from him. The patient directly confirmed the power-sharing worked.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The strongest domain: open questions systematically evoked and deepened change talk, including implementation-oriented and contingency questions. Results were visible in the patient's rising confidence and rich commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without dwelling, with effective double-sided reflections and reframing of past failure as data. Occasionally the clinician restated barrier imagery in ways that risked amplifying rather than softening it.
