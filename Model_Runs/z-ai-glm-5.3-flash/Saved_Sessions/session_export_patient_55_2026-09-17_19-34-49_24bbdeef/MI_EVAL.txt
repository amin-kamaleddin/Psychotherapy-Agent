# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard despite the client's guardedness and minimization; warm, nonjudgmental responses like 'Whatever feels right to share is enough' and genuine affirmations of her strengths. No labeling or disapproval anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections capturing both content and feeling (e.g., sensing her uncertainty about the non-voluntary visit). Loses a point as some reflections summarize rather than deepen the emotional weight of the alcohol-poisoning story or her fear of social judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Once alcohol use surfaces, the session maintains clear focus with logical progression from values to behavior to possible change. The opening third stays on background and the referral reason is only addressed after the client raises it.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative stance with agenda invitations and integration of client answers into follow-up questions. Slightly below top marks because collaboration is more clinician-framed than co-created, with choices offered within the clinician's structure.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of client choice, including 'if anything' language that explicitly leaves the door open to no change. No advice, persuasion, or pressure despite the client's clear minimization.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong eliciting questions ('what matters most to you...?') drew out values and an emerging change idea about pre-planning limits. Evocation is weaker in the opening fact-gathering phase and the change idea isn't developed with confidence/commitment exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly frames the client as the expert ('You're the expert on your own life') and her contributions shape the session. Her mention of band and IB anchors the value-based exploration, and her spontaneous ambivalence is picked up and deepened.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked genuine change talk ('maybe actually think about my limit before I go') from a precontemplative client using value-linked questions. Not a 5 because the emerging change talk about setting limits isn't deepened or reinforced.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never agrees with minimization; the double-sided reflection on the alcohol-poisoning story effectively opened ambivalence. A few neutral reflections ('It makes sense you'd see it that way') briefly restated sustain talk before redirecting.
