# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, explicitly reassuring the client ('I won't judge — deciding to try is progress') with no labeling or disapproval. However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic, and opportunities to affirm the client's self-awareness (e.g., her honest skepticism about timers) are underused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections acknowledge the phone's relaxing function while validating real consequences, but the counselor rarely reflects emotional states or ambivalence. Key feelings — nighttime anxiety ('my brain just keeps going'), fear of failure, and FOMO — receive no reflective acknowledgment before jumping to practical fixes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on the target behavior (nighttime phone use) with structured recaps each turn. Deducted for narrowing prematurely to logistics — the same checklist is recapped nearly verbatim four times without revisiting underlying ambivalence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session opens as making 'a small plan together,' and the client's input genuinely shaped it (30 instead of 60 minutes, mum's calls allowed). Still, the counselor generates most content and prescribes a new tool each turn, while client-generated ideas (charger across the room) are absorbed but not credited as her expertise.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Affirms client choice at multiple points ('Which sounds doable?', 'Ready to start tonight or another day?') and honors her self-negotiated 30-minute limit. However, directive advice-giving dominates, and skeptical pushback is answered with more suggestions rather than affirming her capacity to find her own solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is minimally drawn from the client — after two initial questions, the counselor shifts almost entirely to dispensing techniques. Her spontaneous change talk and confidence shift ('weirdly less stressed than when I walked in') are never explored; no open questions about reasons, confidence, or what success looks like.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client contributions shape some plan details, but the counselor occupies a strong expert role, delivering plans, scripts, and messages prescriptively with 'Quick tips' and 'Here's a plan.' Her question 'What do you think?' was deflected into a menu rather than collaborative exploration.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client offers meaningful change talk ('grades slipped last semester,' 'I'll actually try'), but the counselor never explicitly reinforces or deepens these statements. No follow-up questions evoke commitment language; change talk emerges spontaneously but is left unexploited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never amplified and is gently reframed initially ('it can still cause real consequences'), but subsequent sustain talk ('scrolling is my brain's off switch,' 'my urges don't really fade') is mostly met with more advice. Ambivalence is sidestepped rather than resolved through double-sided reflections.
