# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance with warmth and validation of Jonathan's overwhelm and resistance. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and not individualized to him as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate but brief, often pivoting quickly to advice. Jonathan's meaningful disclosure about unrecognized hypoglycemic episodes ('never connected those before') was acknowledged with only 'Good insight' rather than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on diabetes self-management, progressing from overwhelm to one concrete behavior with troubleshooting, fallback steps, and a 7-day follow-up. Minor drift into detailed medical information momentarily overwhelmed the motivational focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly checks in on feasibility and adapts the plan based on Jonathan's input, including switching from morning to evening checks. Some steps were handed over as completed plans rather than negotiated, limiting full power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong support for Jonathan's autonomy: respecting his refusal of his wife's reminders, offering menu options, and keeping change 'on his terms.' Falls short of a 5 because prescriptive plans are sometimes presented first and agreement sought afterward.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good use of the confidence ruler and open questions, with Jonathan generating his own insights. The counselor often supplied reasons directly rather than eliciting Jonathan's own values, leaving motivation largely external.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked Jonathan's preferences and permission before adding elements, honoring his expertise. Plans were often delivered as counselor-generated lists rather than co-constructed, with the counselor assuming an expert role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Evoked commitment language through the confidence ruler and plan-choice questions ('I'll give it a shot this week'). Change talk was rarely deepened or reflected back, and much of the session was counselor-directed advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Occasionally acknowledged and reframed sustain talk ('choosing one tiny habit is smart'), but rarely used double-sided reflections to pivot toward change. The glucose information dump even re-triggered sustain talk ('that's the huge amount of stuff I was talking about').
