# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and offers harm-reduction support without lecturing. However, there are no explicit affirmations of Diego's strengths, honesty, or readiness, and warmth is minimal and clinical.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session is dominated by question barrages and advice, with essentially no complex reflections. Emotionally significant disclosures — fear of testing, shame about telling Marcus, the pain heroin masks — are met with logistics rather than validation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent, consistent focus on harm reduction from assessment through planning, rehearsal, and follow-up. There is no topic drift, and the session progresses logically toward concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor honors Diego's preferences on sequencing and format, but defaults to prescriptive monologues without checking existing knowledge or jointly problem-solving. True collaboration drawing out Diego's own solutions is limited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regular choices are offered, and Diego's refusal to share his ZIP and preference to make calls himself are respected, which he explicitly values. The score falls short of 5 due to advice-heavy delivery and lack of explicit affirmation of his ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Virtually no effort to elicit Diego's own motivations, values, or reasons for change; questions are logistical. All motivation comes spontaneously from Diego rather than being evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role with instruction lists and stacked closed questions, offering minimal power-sharing. Diego's existing knowledge and skills are not built upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Diego offers strong change talk spontaneously, but the counselor rarely evokes or amplifies it through open or evocative questions. Support of self-generated plans sustains change talk only incidentally.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is tolerated without argument, reflecting a harm-reduction stance. However, ambivalence is not actively engaged — no double-sided reflections or reframing of Diego's desire to escape pain.
