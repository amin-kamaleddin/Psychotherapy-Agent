# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates respect and nonjudgment, meeting Timur's disclosures about crime and self-labeling with warmth rather than disapproval. However, there are few explicit affirmations of Timur as a person (his grit, honesty, identity), keeping this below a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate dual reflections captured both sides of his ambivalence, e.g., pride in 22 days versus the pull of the old life. Reflections are somewhat formulaic and rarely deepen into the emotional core—shame about prison, fear of failure, feeling unaccepted.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear, consistent focus on desistance and pro-social structure with no topic drift, moving logically from ambivalence exploration through readiness scaling, rehearsal, and a locked-in commitment. Every exchange moved toward change and action planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely collaborative, with Timur's ideas (cousin as support person, receipt script, Thursday timing) genuinely shaping the plan. The counselor sometimes supplied pre-packaged solutions (full script, checklist) before exploring Timur's own ideas, making some exchanges feel counselor-driven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions were regularly framed as Timur's, with choices offered and his 'maybe' and 'not yet' accepted without pressure. Some moments felt slightly leading, as the counselor supplied options (training program, probation resource, contacts) rather than first asking Timur to generate his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew motivation from within Timur, eliciting his own reasons for change ('Fiona's worth more than that,' 'this actually feels... doable'). Occasionally the counselor's own reasoning and example options substituted for evocation rather than letting Timur supply the content.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly conveyed that expertise resided with Timur, and his contributions directly shaped the plan. During the role-play the counselor momentarily slipped into answering as themselves, and provided a fairly prescriptive list of preparation items rather than eliciting Timur's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Active evocation via open questions and a readiness ruler successfully elicited strong change talk and a concrete commitment to the forklift course. Reinforcement was good, though reflections could more often amplify Timur's own motivation statements (e.g., 'once I make a decision, I stick to it' was not fed back as an affirmation of capacity).

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well through neutral dual reflections and effective redirection toward coping planning when the 'whisper' about quick money arose. Occasionally sustain talk was reflected back verbatim ('annoyed at lazy feelings') rather than reframed.
