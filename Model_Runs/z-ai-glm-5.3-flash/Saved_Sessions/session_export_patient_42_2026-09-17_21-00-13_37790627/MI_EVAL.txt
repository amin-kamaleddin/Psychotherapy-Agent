# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveys warmth and unconditional positive regard, affirming the client's honesty and normalizing her fears without any judgment or labeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and evolves with the client, as seen in the double-sided opening reflection and the reframing that 'the break can stay even if the cigarette goes.' The client repeatedly confirms with 'Yeah... exactly.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, progressive focus on smoking cessation, moving steadily from exploring ambivalence to a concrete one-week action plan without wandering off target.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session unfolds as a genuinely egalitarian dialogue, with ideas like the coffee break and telling Maria coming from the client and the final commitment co-constructed by both.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently reinforces client choice—'something manageable, on your terms'—and all plan ideas originate with the client, including framing slips as 'information, not failure.'

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within the client through open questions that surface deeply personal reasons—her daughter, self-respect, and proving hard things are doable—rather than counselor-supplied arguments.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor positions the client as the expert, and her contributions clearly shape the plan. It falls slightly short because evaluative framing like 'I love that' or 'That insight feels significant' occasionally centers the counselor's judgment rather than the client's ownership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully elicit strong client-generated change talk ('I think I could try that,' 'I've got this'), and each change talk moment is reflected and deepened rather than left unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced; the counselor validates fears briefly and then gently redirects toward change, reframing fear as 'information, not failure.'
