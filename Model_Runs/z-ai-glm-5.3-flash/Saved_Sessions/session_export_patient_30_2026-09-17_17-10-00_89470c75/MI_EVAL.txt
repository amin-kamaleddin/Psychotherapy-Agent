# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating David's concerns and autonomy (e.g., affirming his request for fair criteria). Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and skepticism was not deeply normalized, keeping this below a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate summary reflections early on showed good perspective-taking, but reflections were largely factual and later replaced by planning logistics. Little exploration of the emotional meaning behind David's distrust of medication or past plans that 'usually kill' his efforts.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on cholesterol management with a concrete 3-month plan and defined follow-up points (6-week text, 12-week labs). The later session became dominated by logistical details rather than deepening the behavioral change conversation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan was genuinely co-constructed around David's stated constraints and his own success criteria, with the counselor asking rather than telling at most decision points. Option menus constrained the range of plans rather than opening fully open-ended problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered options, asked permission before sharing information, and respected David's refusal to contact his mother. Rapid-fire option menus occasionally felt like steering toward a pre-structured plan, and explicit 'you're in charge' affirmations were limited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The opening evocative question about what matters most was never answered or revisited, and David's internal motivations were never developed. The counselor quickly moved to offering plans and logistics instead of drawing out the client's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly treated David's expertise as central, soliciting his food preferences, work schedule, and family involvement to shape the plan. Some exchanges felt like checklist-gathering rather than deep power-sharing, and David was not explicitly named as the owner of the plan's success.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change language ('I can try that for twelve weeks,' 'Send it, I'll start Monday') but rarely deepened it with strategic follow-ups. Most of David's engagement remained surface-level compliance ('Okay, sure. Fine.') without exploration of why health matters to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and initially reflected it neutrally, but provided limited active softening with few double-sided reflections linking concerns to values. The counselor accepted the 'no meds if numbers improve' framing without exploring ambivalence further.
