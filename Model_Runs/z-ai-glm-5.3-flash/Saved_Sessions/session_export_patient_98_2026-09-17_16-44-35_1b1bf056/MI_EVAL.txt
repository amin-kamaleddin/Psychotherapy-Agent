# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated warmth, respect, and nonjudgment—validating frustration, affirming persistence ('real grit'), and responding to Mateo's self-disclosures with acceptance rather than correction. No judgment or disapproval appeared at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening with an accurate opening reflection and solid summaries, but reflections were sometimes brief rather than emotionally deep. Mateo's fear of being dismissed and his pattern of 'going quiet' were not explored emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained unwavering focus on self-advocacy in rehab for pain management and activity progress, with a concrete timeline and secured follow-up. No topic drift occurred.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative—checking in before offering materials and co-constructing the plan with Mateo's input shaping each iteration. However, occasionally leading with pre-packaged solutions (scripts, stretches) tilted power toward the clinician.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Mateo's ownership through choice-offering and confirming his decisions rather than prescribing. His confidence rose organically from 5 to 8, indicating the plan was genuinely his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The approach was fundamentally evocative, drawing deeply personal motivations from Mateo himself—walking with Rosa, not folding, speaking up for the first time in months. His confidence climbed through his own elaboration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Mateo was positioned as the expert, with choices offered and self-generated solutions (he chose the therapist conversation himself). The score fell short of 5 because the psychiatrist's specific scripts and framings shaped the session more than Mateo's own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk via the opening question and importance/confidence rulers, driving incremental confidence growth from 5 to 8. Repeatedly deepened commitment language into a concrete plan Mateo owned.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and was gently redirected, with discouragement acknowledged before pivoting to affirmations. However, Mateo's fear of failure ('that's when I fold') was met with a backup script rather than first being reflected.
