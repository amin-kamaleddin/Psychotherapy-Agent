# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental throughout, validating ambivalence and skepticism without shaming. However, explicit affirmations of Selene's specific strengths (honesty, insight, self-awareness) were sparse.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate summaries and reflections consolidated her ambivalence well, but reflections sometimes served as lead-ins to advice. The emotional weight of her mother's illness and her pattern of fading enthusiasm went largely unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, unwavering focus on medication adherence from opening invitation to concrete commitment. Each exchange advanced the target progressively with no drift off-topic.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction: Selene selected, modified, and added her own elements (coffee pairing, cereal box, daughter as reminder, fridge calendar). Her comment about expecting a lecture but leaving with a real plan confirms the collaborative tone transformed engagement.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were consistently framed as Selene's ('Which of those feels easiest?', 'pick your reward now or check first?'), and she explicitly noted the non-lecturing approach motivated her. Later checklist-driven turns slightly diminished felt ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Importance and confidence rulers effectively elicited change talk, and confidence rose from 5 to 7. Occasionally shifted into advice-giving mode where the counselor's ideas rather than the client's own reasons carried momentum.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly invited Selene's expertise and her contributions genuinely shaped the plan. Fell short of 5 because the counselor supplied most of the menu of options rather than asking Selene to generate solutions first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk through rulers and self-designed planning, producing commitment language ('one week. Let's see', 'I'll move the pills tonight'). Evocation sometimes transitioned too quickly into counselor-generated solutions, reducing space for Selene to expand on her own motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and reframed all-or-nothing thinking as 'data, not failure.' However, sustain talk was largely left unaddressed rather than actively reframed, with few double-sided reflections or explicit discrepancy amplification.
