# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, directly reframing Oliver's self-labels ('pathetic,' 'weak') as strength. No judgment about his delays, humor, or ambivalence; the closing affirms his progress warmly and specifically.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate throughout, capturing ambivalence ('You care about him, and you also miss your own space') and mirroring Oliver's own words back. Oliver explicitly confirms feeling understood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear focus on the target behavior — the assertive conversation with the flatmate. Every counselor turn advances this goal, with no drift and a concrete follow-up commitment at the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interview proceeds as a genuine exchange between equals, with the counselor reflecting and building on Oliver's content. Even the gentle pushback on 'pathetic' is framed respectfully and invites Oliver's own response.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership are consistently reinforced ('that shift is already yours to keep'), with no prescriptive or coercive language. All strategies originate from Oliver's own answers, and the closing supports self-efficacy without pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within — Oliver's key insights (kind-but-firm, the first-minute strategy, saying the caring part out loud) were all elicited via open questions. One notable lapse: the counselor repeated Oliver's speech almost verbatim as his own words, briefly inverting roles.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positions Oliver as the expert, and every plan element (script, housing list, first-minute strategy) comes from Oliver. The counselor only mirrors and amplifies, explicitly disclaiming ownership of the change.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked with strategic questions and reinforced ('Scared, but ready — I want to sit with that'). Oliver responds with strong commitment language: 'saying it once and not undoing it,' 'I'm ready.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged but not reinforced, using double-sided reflections and constructive reframing of guilt. Occasionally the counselor explores fear of the flatmate's reaction without immediately pivoting to change, though these explorations ultimately serve change preparation.
