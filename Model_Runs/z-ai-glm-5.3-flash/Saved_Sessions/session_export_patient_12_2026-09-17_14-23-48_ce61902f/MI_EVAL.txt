# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated respect and warmth without judgment, including affirmations like 'You did something today — that matters.' Score falls short of 5 because affirmations were somewhat formulaic and missed moments like Amira's vulnerable admission that being checked on 'doesn't happen much.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections captured ambivalence, but reflections were brief and quickly pivoted to advice-giving and checklists. Deeper emotional content — loneliness, shame about the neighbor, and her relief at being checked on — was repeatedly missed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on the target behavior, converting ambivalence into concrete commitments (24-hour delay, 8 PM check-in, pharmacy visit). Direction was achieved through directive instruction rather than collaborative goal clarification, and emotional threads were dropped.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The plan was co-created to a degree — Amira picked the small step and set check-in timing — but the interaction often resembled an expert delivering a protocol with numbered lists and checklists. Her contributions rarely reshaped the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some choices were offered and her limits respected ('I'm not promising more than that'), but the session was heavily prescriptive regarding disposal, breathing, and distractions. Choice was acknowledged but not consistently fostered, with the counselor steering toward its own agenda.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied from outside rather than evoked from within, with few open questions exploring her hopes, values, or confidence. Amira's spontaneous motivations ('maybe showing up counts for something') emerged but were not drawn out or amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amira's contributions were partially incorporated — she chose the small step and shaped the plan details. However, the overall stance was expert-driven, with prescriptive instructions and closed-ended quizzes replacing engagement with her expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Change talk was rarely actively evoked; Amira offered some spontaneously but the counselor did not deepen these with open questions about her reasons or confidence. Instead of eliciting her motivations, the counselor supplied the rationale and steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not reinforce sustain talk, but also did not strategically soften it — doubts like 'what's the point?' were met with reassurance and options rather than double-sided reflections. Sustain talk was largely bypassed rather than worked with.
