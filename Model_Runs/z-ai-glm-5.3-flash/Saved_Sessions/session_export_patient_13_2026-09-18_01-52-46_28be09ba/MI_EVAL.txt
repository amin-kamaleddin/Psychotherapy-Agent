# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist unconditionally accepts the patient's ambivalence, fears, and self-doubt without any judgment, framing every concern as valid ('both of those feelings belong here'). The patient's pace is consistently respected, including her stated limits.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist accurately reflects deep emotional content — the chest tightness, fear of confirmation, and exhaustion of hiding — and mirrors the patient's own words ('my son deserves a mom who isn't hiding from this'). Responses consistently convey understanding beneath the surface of what is said.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation maintains a clear focus on moving toward health-seeking behavior, with a concrete outcome achieved: a specific commitment (telling her sister on Saturday) and a follow-up plan. Each response gently advances the patient toward change without derailing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist explicitly positions the patient as the expert ('that's your wisdom, not mine') and offers options rather than prescriptions. The final plan is jointly negotiated, with the patient defining what she needs (a check-in).

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly affirmed throughout: 'you get to set the pace,' 'your idea and your timeline,' 'the rest is mine to do.' The psychiatrist emphasizes the patient's ownership of all insights and decisions, and explicitly avoids promising or pushing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently draw out the patient's own motivations and solutions — who could accompany her, what the tightness protects, what would shift if she weren't alone. All key change ideas (sister as companion, mom babysitting, the sentence in her phone) originate from the patient.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The dialogue is genuinely collaborative with balanced turn-taking, shared exploration ('if we listed options together'), and joint planning. The psychiatrist acts as a companion in the process rather than an advisor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully reinforces and reflects change talk at every step ('I could maybe do that,' 'Saturday, I'll say it Saturday'), highlighting movement from ambivalence to commitment. Questions are designed to elicit the patient's own reasons and plans for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear, embarrassment, wanting to hide) is neither confronted nor ignored; it is validated, reflected, and gently explored ('what is it protecting you from — and is it also keeping something from you?'). This approach allows the patient herself to soften her resistance and find the other side of the what-if.
