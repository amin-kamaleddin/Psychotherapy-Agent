# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and affirms small steps; patient explicitly reported feeling heard. Affirmations are brief and generic, with little individualized recognition of the patient's strengths or values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections appear early, but empathy is inconsistent — shame about the birthday and the patient's vulnerable 'maybe there's something to what she's saying' moment went unreflected. Reflections tend toward brief acknowledgments rather than deeper emotional content.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stays well focused on alcohol use and moves purposefully from exploration to quantification and a concrete monitoring plan. Direction is action-oriented rather than motivational, rushing to a behavioral experiment before ambivalence was resolved.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early collaboration is good — permission-seeking, offering choices, and co-selecting the tracking experiment. The second half becomes clinician-directed, delivering a preset package (template, drink guide, pressure scripts) without negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames everything as optional, honors the patient's paper-tracking choice, and validates that tracking isn't quitting. Some autonomy language is formulaic, and advice-giving segments slightly undercut perceived choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The weakest domain — the counselor informs and instructs far more than elicits, with no open questions designed to draw out the patient's own reasons for change. The importance rating of 3 was never explored, and spontaneous change talk went undeveloped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission, offers options, and lets the patient pick his first step, reflecting some power-sharing. However, the counselor takes over as the expert, never asking what has worked for the patient before or eliciting his own ideas about handling pressure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The importance ruler successfully evoked change talk ('fewer mornings like that birthday weekend,' 'maybe there's something to what she's saying'). This change talk was never deepened — no follow-up on why a 3 and not lower, no confidence scaling, no exploration of discrepancy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoids arguing with or reinforcing sustain talk, responding neutrally to the 'nursing a water' objection and the football season joke. Missed opportunities for strategic double-sided reflections that would pivot sustain talk toward change.
