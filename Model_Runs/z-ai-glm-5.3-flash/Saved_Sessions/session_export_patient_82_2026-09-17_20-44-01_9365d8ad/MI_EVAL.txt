# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, with no judgment about the drinking. Ambivalence and peer pressure are treated with respect, and the closing 'you leading and me in your corner' reinforces a supportive stance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate and emotionally attuned throughout, capturing both content and emotional nuance. The counselor effectively mirrors Lukas's insights and subtle social fears.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on the target behavior (drinking in peer contexts) while remaining client-driven. Every turn advances from ambivalence toward a concrete, actionable plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is fully collaborative, with permission-style questions and each step built on the client's prior statements. The client's own comment that it 'feels like a real plan now' reflects genuine shared ownership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All choices are framed as Lukas's own, with no advice or directives given. Even the Saturday check-in and accountability style were designed by the client himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited almost entirely from the client, drawing out his own values (athletics, scholarship, self-respect). All strategies were self-generated, and the counselor consistently names this.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly and explicitly locates expertise in the client ('you're the expert on your friends'). Client contributions directly shaped every element of the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and reinforced, producing escalating commitment language ('this feels doable,' 'I think I'm set'). All specific plans emerged from the client.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged briefly and neutrally ('Both voices make sense') then consistently redirected toward change. Social fears are reframed using the client's own knowledge that most friends won't push.
