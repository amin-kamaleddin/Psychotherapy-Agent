# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, thanking Jordan for his candor and explicitly respecting his skepticism. Affirmations were somewhat generic and lacked deep, individualized warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated consistent reflective listening, accurately summarizing Jordan's perspective and showing good perspective-taking. Reflections occasionally stayed at surface level and did not deeply explore the emotional weight of being pressured or protecting his grandmother.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on the vaccination decision from opening question to a concrete plan, progressing logically from ambivalence to commitment with barrier planning. Excellent directional momentum without being pushy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Framed the session as joint problem-solving, and the final plan was co-constructed with Jordan contributing key elements. Information delivery occasionally shifted the session toward a counselor-led, expert posture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength — repeatedly stated 'I respect your choice' and offered menus of options. The client's own feedback ('thanks for not just lecturing me like my doctor did') confirmed he experienced genuine choice and freedom.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Jordan's own motivation, particularly his grandmother's vulnerability, and amplified it rather than supplying reasons himself. Relied heavily on structured choice menus rather than sustained open-ended exploration of his values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered Jordan choice among options, but often positioned himself as the information expert with lengthy didactic blocks. The client's own ideas did not substantially shape the session content, so genuine power-sharing was only moderate.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited and reinforced change talk, leading to clear commitment language ('I'll do it') with a concrete Saturday plan and proactive barrier exploration. Change talk was largely channeled through information-giving menus rather than deeper open-ended evocation of values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it, met it with neutral reflections, and avoided arguing. Some reflections restated sustain talk without a double-sided pivot toward change.
