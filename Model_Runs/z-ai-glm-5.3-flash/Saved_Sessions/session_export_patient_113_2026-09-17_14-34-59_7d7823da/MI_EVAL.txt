# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with unconditional regard and validation of autonomy despite the client's bluntness. Minor deduction as early screening questions felt scripted and warmth was occasionally formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflected Rowan's frustration, autonomy concerns, and feeling of being monitored, validating the emotional core of his resistance. Deducted because empathy was often followed by rapid pivots to problem-solving and option menus, limiting deeper exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Stayed tightly focused on the target behavior and produced concrete outcomes: a draft message, overnight charging trial, and boundary line with clear next steps. Some drift into logistical detail and the risk explanation was more educational than MI-consistent.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements like adjusting the boundary line to Rowan's voice and checking which options felt useful. However, the psychiatrist supplied most ideas, menus, and drafts while Rowan mainly approved or rejected them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support throughout, including explicit non-commitment framing, repeated offers of choice, and accepting Rowan's qualifiers and edits without pressure. Deducted for being quick to supply solutions and scripts, pre-empting the client's own decision-making.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The well-timed risk answer evoked genuine change talk and a self-generated nighttime plan. But overall relied on information delivery and menus rather than evocative questions drawing out the client's own reasons, values, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered client choice, but the multiple-choice menus had an expert-driven quality with the counselor generating all ideas. Rowan's own contributions were only briefly affirmed and the draft message was counselor-authored.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Capitalized well on spontaneous openings when Rowan asked about risks, evoking meaningful change talk and an initial plan. However, change talk was reactive rather than systematically evoked, with no confidence scaling and no deepening of his tentative commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk directly and delivered effective risk information when asked. However, repeatedly offering boundary lines and exit options subtly aligned with avoidance rather than change.
