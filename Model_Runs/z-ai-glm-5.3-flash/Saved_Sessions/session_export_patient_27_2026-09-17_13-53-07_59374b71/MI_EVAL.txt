# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Consistent respect and support ("Thanks for trusting me with that," "I'm proud you're thinking about this step"). No judgment about secrecy or the lie to Kayla. Affirmations somewhat generic; fear of stigma not explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections ("It makes sense you'd worry about changing how friends see you"), but often pivots quickly to scripts/tips. Guilt about lying to Kayla, near-tears about the "trust" line, and loneliness of hiding in the bathroom were not emotionally reflected or validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5/5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on disclosure to peers throughout — fears, scripts, rehearsal, follow-up prep, planning ("Kayla," "this week"), and a scheduled debrief. No drift; ends with concrete, client-owned plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Genuine interactivity via role-play and responsiveness to requests, but psychiatrist functions primarily as expert coach dispensing content while Daniela practices. Limited two-way knowledge exchange; Daniela's expertise about her friends underused.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Consistent choice points ("pick one or mix them," "Which would help most?", "Pick when you want to check in"). Pacing respected; "this week" is client-owned timeline. Pre-written scripts somewhat limit client authorship; autonomy could be more explicitly reinforced ("You're in charge of how much you share").

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Openers invited Daniela's perspective and some motivation emerged ("I control how much they get to know"), but bulk of session was counselor-supplied content. Her transformation from "I'd never tell anyone" to "I got this" occurred via rehearsal of counselor-generated material rather than evocation of intrinsic reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Invites choice, but psychiatrist supplies all content (scripts, replies, tips) with Daniela selecting among pre-built options. Client input influenced flow but material was never co-created in her own words; her insight about controlling disclosure went unused for power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4/5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and channeled change talk via options and rehearsal — from "I'd only tell them exactly the point" to "This week. Kayla. I got this." Could deepen with "What makes this feel possible now?" or confidence/change talk ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3/5
**Comments:** Sustain talk not reinforced, but also often sidestepped rather than softened with double-sided reflections ("I'm not ready for that conversation," "probably"). Fear of being "diabetes girl" was met with a script pivot rather than a reflection honoring the fear while amplifying her desire for honesty.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 3.6/5**
