# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, normalizing doubt and never judging past failed plans or avoidance. The patient's closing thanks for 'making it feel possible' reflects feeling fully accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening throughout, capturing both emotional and situational content (e.g., the chest-tightness after practice, the conflict about involving the roommate). Reflections repeatedly landed, evidenced by the patient's affirmations and increasing engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained unwavering focus on the target behavior, moving concretely from identifying avoidance onset to a specific actionable plan (micro-step, if-then plan, confidence scale, backup, and 7:30 accountability text). The clinician steered without rushing and avoided drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was largely built from the patient's own ideas (self-texting, notebook on desk, pen-touch backup), with suggestions offered lightly. However, the clinician proposed most option menus herself, so the patient mostly selected rather than co-generated solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly reinforced through repeated choice points ('Which backup do you want to try tonight?') and explicit pressure removal ('no pressure to continue'). The clinician respected the patient's choice to keep the plan minimal without adding more structure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently elicited rather than imposed, and the patient's own motivations surfaced powerfully, including the closing insight that 'doing it scared is the only way.' Missed the opportunity to explore that rich change-talk statement, instead closing with logistics.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient was repeatedly positioned as the decision-maker, choosing options and rejecting the roommate idea, and the clinician respected her decision to continue without more tools. Some multi-part, menu-style questions constrained space for the patient's own contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation through scaling questions, tiny-step framing, and success criteria; confidence measurably rose from 5 to 6 and change talk grew from 'no promises' to 'I'll try it tonight.' Slightly below expert level because the spontaneous 'doing it scared' statement wasn't deepened with reflective affirmation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent sustain talk ('I just end up avoiding it anyway') was acknowledged briefly and pivoted toward action, with the pen-touch backup reframing failure as nearly impossible. Double-sided reflections appropriately included doubt but occasionally maintained focus on sustain talk without strategic amplification of change.
