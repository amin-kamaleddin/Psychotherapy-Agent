# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, accepting the client's ambivalence and engaging helpfully with practical questions. However, explicit affirmations of strengths and autonomy-honoring statements of regard were sparse; warmth was understated and practical rather than deeply affirming.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made some accurate reflections early on showing decent perspective-taking, but reflections became formulaic ('I hear you' repeated across turns). Emotional nuances — fear of proving himself wrong, frustration at his sister's nagging, relief at avoiding a lecture — were mostly passed over rather than explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent, unwavering focus on coffee consumption and its health implications with no topic drift. The session progressed coherently from exploration to a concrete, client-approved two-week experiment with a scheduled follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely collaborative: options were offered as a menu, the client chose the decaf swap, flexible tracking was negotiated, and the next session agenda was co-planned. However, detailed advice blocks (BP protocols, brand recommendations) tilted the exchange toward expert delivery.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed the plan as optional and low-commitment, respected the client's preference for flexibility over strict homework, and did not push for abstinence. Yet he answered 'What would you start with?' with a direct recommendation rather than reinforcing the client's own authority, and much of the plan was counselor-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked some evocative questions (worry scale, 'What would you do with that reassurance?') but kept them superficial and brief, quickly pivoting to options and advice. Confidence and ability dimensions were never explored; session momentum came from the counselor's proposed experiment rather than the client's own intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked permission before offering options and checked in on doability, reflecting partnership spirit. However, he frequently assumed the expert role with unprompted protocols and recommendations, and answered the client's 'what would you do?' question directly instead of exploring the client's own ideas first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully moved the client toward tentative commitment ('That sounds manageable... I can do that') through low-effort experiments. This was achieved more by proposing options than by evoking the client's own motivation, missing chances to explore why the BP reading 'nags' him or what a healthier future would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided amplifying sustain talk, briefly acknowledging coffee's benefits and redirecting toward data-gathering without arguing or reinforcing status quo reasons. Some reflections restated both sides equally without gently pivoting toward the client's expressed concerns or developing discrepancy.
