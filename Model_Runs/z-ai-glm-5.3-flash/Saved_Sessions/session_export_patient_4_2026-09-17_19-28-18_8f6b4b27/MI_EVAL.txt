# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist conveys consistent unconditional positive regard with warm, individualized affirmations ('That's real strength and self-advocacy') and validates the client's fear of being dismissed. No judgment or disapproval appears, even when the client insists on biomedical explanations and distrusts her GP.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate, with complex reflections capturing emotional and situational nuance. Key reflections ('losing a piece of yourself') are confirmed by the client, and the psychiatrist precisely validates her fear that her pain would be labeled psychological.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on functioning and moves progressively toward a concrete, time-bound plan (call neighbor, walk around the block, written questions, return visit). Minor deductions for briefly amplifying the testing agenda at the close and lengthy early symptom exploration.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan is co-constructed entirely from the client's own ideas (neighbor's company, bench-and-breathe plan, written questions), with genuine power-sharing. The client's contributions demonstrably shape the session's course, and the follow-up offer is relational rather than directive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client choice is consistently reinforced ('on your own terms,' 'What feels most important to you?') with no pressure toward psychological framing. The client's closing statement that she felt heard rather than told to relax confirms autonomy was protected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn almost entirely from the client via Socratic questioning, eliciting her pattern-recognition, her own memories of what helps, and her own rationale ('For my son, for my husband. For me'). Strong self-motivated change talk ('I can do one block,' 'I will call my neighbor tomorrow') demonstrates successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client ('That's your own insight, not mine'), and her observations and conditions define the plan. Not a 5 because option-framing questions are occasionally somewhat leading rather than fully open agenda-setting.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is evoked skillfully through open questions about patterns, first steps, and safety conditions. The client produces strong self-motivated statements ('If I wait until I'm not scared, I'll wait forever'), which the psychiatrist affirms rather than adding her own arguments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (cancer fears, distrust of Dr. Brown) is handled without argument by joining the client's agenda rather than opposing it. Slight deductions for briefly echoing the testing agenda at the close and reframing the cancer fear without actively exploring it toward resolution.
