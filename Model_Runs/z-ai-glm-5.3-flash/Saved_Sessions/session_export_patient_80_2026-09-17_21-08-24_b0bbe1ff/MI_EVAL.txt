# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, warm, nonjudgmental tone with specific, strengths-based affirmations ('you're building your own toolkit,' 'that honesty about lying before takes courage'). Not a 5 because some affirmations feel formulaic and slightly repetitive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Complex reflections accurately capture both content and underlying meaning, e.g., 'the real battle isn't just the drink—it's that old belief that struggling alone is the man's way.' A significant lapse occurs when the counselor inexplicably speaks in the client's first-person voice, breaking the reflective stance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays tightly focused on target behaviors (alcohol use, aggression, coping) with coherent momentum from urges to relapse planning. The first-person mirroring turn momentarily derailed direction, and the 'urge' theme is revisited several times before advancing.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is largely between equals, with the plan co-constructed from Luka's own ideas and shared problem-solving invited ('help us figure out how to make it easier'). The odd first-person turn temporarily collapses the collaborative frame, and the counselor never explicitly checks whether the emerging plan matches what Luka wants from the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are consistently framed as belonging to the client ('you know your rhythms better than anyone'), and the plan emerges entirely from Luka's ideas. No explicit acknowledgment of the client's freedom to choose or decline is offered, and alternatives to the proactive-call suggestion aren't fully explored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is expertly drawn from within the client: questions like 'what's that like afterwards?' elicited powerful change statements ('better than any drink ever made me feel'), and planning/obstacle questions generated progressively sophisticated client-authored solutions. The counselor builds on the client's own metaphors ('making the good reasons louder').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise is repeatedly located in the client ('You know your rhythms better than anyone'), and the plan emerges from Luka's own ideas. The counselor never explicitly negotiates the agenda and subtly pre-selects focus areas; the confusing first-person turn also disrupted the shared-power dynamic.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully and consistently evoked, with questions that drew out powerful commitment language ('no hiding, ring instead of waiting until Monday') and detailed planning. The final question about obstacles deepened preparation into concrete relapse-prevention commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well without amplification, using double-sided reflections ('part of you is already thinking like someone who's protecting those good mornings, AND part of you knows the drink voice gets loud'). The counselor redirects toward strengths, though some obstacle-focused questions briefly center failure scenarios.
