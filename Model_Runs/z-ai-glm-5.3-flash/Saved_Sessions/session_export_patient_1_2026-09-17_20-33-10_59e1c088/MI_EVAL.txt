# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout — validated Ahmed's reasoning and honesty without labeling or disapproval. Even his minimizations were met with respect and affirmation.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple accurate complex reflections captured both content and underlying emotion, including naming Ahmed's lost voice among competing others. Reflections consistently landed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on cigar use, purposefully moving from ambivalence to values, to a concrete commitment, to relapse planning. No topic drift; each exchange built toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — asked permission, offered to relay the dentist request contingent on Ahmed's wishes, and co-constructed the plan around his own conditions. Ahmed's ideas genuinely shaped the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Ahmed's ownership ('that's you taking charge of this, not the dentist, not your wife, not me'). Information about risks was offered only after Ahmed requested it.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within — the pivotal 'pause vs. smoke' question produced Ahmed's own insight and self-generated change talk. The counselor never lectured or supplied reasons first.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned Ahmed as the expert ('Nobody's asking you to promise anything today'). His response — 'Nobody's actually asked me that before' — demonstrates the counselor elicited wisdom others had not.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation with open questions and strategic reinforcement of change talk, e.g., highlighting 'No weekdays — that much I can tell you now' as Ahmed taking charge. Also prompted concrete planning for high-risk moments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally avoided reinforcing sustain talk with neutral, validating reflections of ambivalence. Minor weakness: a few reflections briefly dwelled on sustain talk (e.g., 'quitting completely feels like a lot') without always pivoting sharply toward change.
