# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental stance toward Diego's heroin use and handled his guardedness without defensiveness. Affirmations were somewhat generic; more personalized recognition of Diego's strengths would strengthen this.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening consistently captured Diego's ambivalence and validated the function of his use. Deeper emotional content (shame about the housemate conversation, underlying avoidance) was acknowledged but never reflected at an emotional level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on risk reduction, with each step building logically toward concrete commitments. The information-dense risk segment briefly felt like content delivery, and no closing summary tied the steps together.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction read as two equals problem-solving, with Diego negotiating script wording and driving session content. The overdose education was delivered as expert instruction, and some of Diego's ideas were re-edited rather than simply endorsed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly supported through menu options, permission-seeking, and accepting Diego's 'no' to using alone by pivoting to his own compromise. The permission question was immediately followed by an unsolicited information dump, slightly undercutting the gesture.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions, confidence rulers, and role-play successfully drew out Diego's own solutions and commitment language. Evocation was reactive rather than strategic — importance rulers, future-oriented questions, and the escape function went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego substantially shaped the session — his script edits, agenda, and harm-reduction framing were all adopted. The risk-education segment was a fairly long expert monologue that briefly shifted power toward the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was actively reinforced, with confidence rulers and role-play effectively converting intention into rehearsal and eliciting strong commitment (9/10 confidence). The psychiatrist responded to client-generated change talk rather than strategically deepening it with evocative questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego's sustain talk was acknowledged without argument and gently redirected toward his own harm-reduction goals. Redirects were sometimes abrupt; double-sided reflections and revisiting the escape function as potential change motivation were missed.
