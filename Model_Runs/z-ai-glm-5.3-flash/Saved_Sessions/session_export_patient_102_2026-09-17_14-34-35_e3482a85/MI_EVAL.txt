# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a non-judgmental, respectful tone, but warmth felt formulaic and few genuine affirmations of Milan were offered; missed chances to validate that experimentation at 17 is common while still caring about safety.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflections were sparse and shallow, with rapid-fire multi-question turns prioritizing information-gathering over understanding. Core emotional drivers — fear of parental discovery, identity concerns, and ambivalence — were never meaningfully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept the session focused on alcohol use and guided it toward concrete outcomes (drink limit, refusal line, check-in plan) with no aimless drifting. Score limited by the fact that focus was maintained through rapid questioning rather than collaborative agenda-setting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Milan selected the text wording, the two-drink limit, and shaped the follow-up plan, showing some co-creation. However, the counselor dictated the plan structure and safer-party tactics, incorporating Milan's ideas only at the margins.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered options and Milan freely chose his limit and peer-support approach. Yet frequent advice-giving and pre-packaged plans subtly steered toward the counselor's preferred solution, with no explicit reinforcement of Milan's ownership of the change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied on providing information and ready-made solutions rather than drawing motivation from Milan. Key evocative questions about values, worries, and the meaning of drinking were missing, and the opening to explore his statement about never saying a number out loud was passed over.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumed the expert role, supplying scripts, tactics, and plans rather than eliciting Milan's own ideas. Client contributions shaped the session in spite of, not because of, deliberate power-sharing; Milan himself sensed a lingering lecture flavor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully elicited movement from minimization to a two-drink commitment, a text to Luka, a rehearsed refusal line, and a check-in agreement. Missed opportunities to deepen the change talk by exploring what made Milan willing to try or reflecting his commitment language back.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with or moralizing against Milan's substantial sustain talk, which is a strength. However, no deliberate softening techniques were used — no double-sided reflections, autonomy affirmations, or reframing — as the counselor shifted past ambivalence to a safety plan.
