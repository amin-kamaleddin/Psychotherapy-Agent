# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance with effective normalization and reframing of setbacks ('data, not failure'). Praise was somewhat generic and formulaic, lacking deeper, individualized affirmations of the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated solid reflective listening that accurately captured situational triggers and emotional concerns. Reflections occasionally stayed summary-level, and emotional nuances (loss of the smoking ritual, fear of repeating past failures) weren't deeply explored before pivoting to solutions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus on the target behavior, with every exchange tied to triggers, coping tools, if-then planning, and concrete cue placement. The session ended with a specific, measurable plan and a forward-looking goal of five cigarettes per day.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely a joint problem-solving enterprise, with the client's ideas (drawer card, 2:30 reminder) integrated into the final plan and his script wording adopted verbatim. Occasional rapid-fire option menus and advice-giving tilted slightly toward the counselor supplying answers rather than co-generating them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A clear strength—every step was framed as the client's choice, options were offered as menus never prescriptions, and rejected options were dropped without persuasion. The client's closing statement ('this feels more doable than last time') reflects genuine self-directed ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently drew out the client's own reasons, strategies, and language, with client-authored self-talk and rehearsal particularly strong at building self-efficacy. Would reach 5 with more systematic exploration of deeper values and use of confidence/importance rulers.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the client as the expert ('Which of these feels most doable right now?'), and his contributions genuinely shaped the plan. At times slipped into suggestion-giving mode, listing six intervention options at once, which leaned slightly toward expert-driven menu provision.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively built on the client's motivation through open questions and having him author his own coping script, with commitment language growing to 'I'll commit to both' and an emerging new goal. Could have used confidence/importance scaling to further consolidate motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk (past failed plans, rejected gum and breathing options) without reinforcement—letting rejected options drop and effectively defusing lapse fears with the 'data, not failure' reframe. Sustain talk was occasionally echoed in summaries before redirecting, which is acceptable but not maximally skillful pivoting.
