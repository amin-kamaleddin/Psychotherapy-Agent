# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently warm and nonjudgmental with well-timed affirmations of Liyana's persistence and courage; never labels or criticizes her behaviors. Affirmations occasionally feel formulaic, and there is limited explicit normalization of the plateau and adolescent body-image struggles that would deepen unconditional regard for her 'maybe I'm just broken' self-narrative.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurate throughout, with effective complex and double-sided reflections (exhaustion, carrying the secret) that reliably elicit elaboration. Reflections sometimes paraphrase rather than reach for deeper emotional meaning, e.g., the shame beneath her mother's comments and the 'broken' self-criticism are not fully unpacked.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on the target behavior with a well-sequenced progression from frustration to a specific change and support plan, ending near an actionable commitment. Lacks a closing summary or explicit commitment/confidence check, leaving the momentum slightly implicit rather than deliberately steered to a landing point.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Collaboration is explicit and genuine throughout ('I'd love to figure that out together'), with a flat power dynamic and no expert pronouncements or advice-giving. Liyana's own observations and reasoning steer each next question and carry the insight.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is consistently reinforced, and every plan element is generated and owned by Liyana, with explicit affirmation of her expertise on her own body and situation. The autonomy framing is sometimes rhetorical (presumed direction) and there is no explicit statement that she can change her mind or decline, which would fully cement ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation of Liyana's reasons, values, solutions, and strategies, with her language growing progressively more committed from 'maybe that counts for something' to 'I think I could actually do that this week.' The counselor never supplies reasons for change; every motivation comes from Liyana herself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session explicitly establishes Liyana as the expert ('You're the expert on your own life') and genuinely builds on her contributions. Her ideas drive the entire direction, and the plan elements—adding lunch, walking with Aina—come from her, not the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematic evocation and development of change talk at every turn, with rich desired-outcome talk ('I'd feel like I'm not broken... I fixed it') and commitment-adjacent language ('I think I could actually do that this week'). Change talk is consistently reflected and amplified while sustain talk is kept brief.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (feeling broken, fear of gaining, 'everyone says eating less is the answer') is handled well without amplification, using skillful double-sided reflections that pair it with change talk. Occasionally restates negative framing, and the fear of weight gain is revisited multiple times without a reframe toward her capacity to monitor and adjust.
