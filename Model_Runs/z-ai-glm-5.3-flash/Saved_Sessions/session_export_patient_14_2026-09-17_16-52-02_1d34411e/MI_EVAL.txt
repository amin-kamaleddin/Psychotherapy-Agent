# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating Andrei's frustration about the wait and affirming his practical planning. Affirmations were somewhat formulaic and the initial anger about waiting time was not deeply validated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening, capturing Andrei's core tension between billables and health and validating his self-assessment. Reflections sometimes skimmed the emotional surface, missing opportunities to explore deeper frustration or anxiety about his health.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior from opening question through concrete, specified commitments with timing, monitoring, and fallback planning. Each exchange narrowed the plan further — a genuine strength given the client's time pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were co-constructed with Andrei's practical input shaping the session, and permission was checked before advising. The pace and format were at times counselor-driven, with rapid-fire option lists and stacked closing questions reducing genuine two-way negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choice and allowed Andrei to design his own check system, reminder wording, and pace, leaving him with clear ownership ('Two things, that's it'). Pre-selected option menus sometimes channeled him toward the counselor's framing rather than his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within via rulers, success markers, and Andrei's own insights ('I'm better at systems than willpower'). The deeper personal 'why' behind weight control — health fears, values, longevity — was never evoked, leaving motivation resting mostly on efficiency logic.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Andrei's knowledge as central, building the plan from his own solutions and endorsing his systems-thinking insight. The counselor still supplied menus of ideas rather than drawing solutions out first, keeping the expert role active.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Systematically evoked change talk through importance/confidence rulers and specific commitments, generating strong commitment statements ('Tomorrow, actually'). Relied heavily on structured scaling rather than open exploration of Andrei's own deeper reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce Andrei's sustain talk and pivoted toward actionable options, but handling was directive — offering solution lists instead of double-sided reflections. When Andrei said 'knowing isn't my issue,' the counselor jumped to options rather than exploring the ambivalence.
