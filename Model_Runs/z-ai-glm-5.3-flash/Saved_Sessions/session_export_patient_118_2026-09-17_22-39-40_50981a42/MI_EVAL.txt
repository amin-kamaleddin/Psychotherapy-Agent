# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently communicates unconditional positive regard and respect, never judging or scolding the patient despite his defensiveness. The patient's own words ('you treated me like I had a brain, not like a fella needing a scolding') confirm this acceptance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and consistent throughout, with reflections that visibly disarm defensiveness and are repeatedly confirmed by the patient ('that's a fair question,' 'someone who actually listened for once'). The double-sided reflection about smoking being part of his life while still talking about it was particularly effective.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains focus on smoking cessation while gracefully honoring the patient's demand to discuss dental work, pairing each transition with door-opening statements. A point is deducted because patient redirections occasionally suspend the change focus for extended exchanges.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The tone is egalitarian and non-hierarchical, with next steps co-constructed from the patient's own ideas (gradual cut-back, wife's support, counting cigarettes). Slightly counselor-steered question sequences at moments when the patient asked to move on keep this from a 5.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership are consistently reinforced ('No signing up today'; 'the door stays open, on your terms only'), with explicit attribution of change to the client. The patient's response ('doing it my way, my timing... I'd need to want it myself') confirms the effectiveness of this autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently elicited from within the client rather than supplied, with no advice-giving anywhere in the session. Every probe draws out the patient's own reasons and solutions, which he explicitly names as the turning point: 'Nobody's asked me the how before.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently located within the client ('That picture came from you'; 'That shift happened because of you, not me'). This textbook power-sharing visibly changed the patient's engagement: 'Makes it feel like it'd be my project, not theirs.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoke substantial client-generated change talk, from ability ('I could maybe manage that much') to desire and a concrete self-generated plan (counting cigarettes). Change talk is reinforced by reflecting it back to the patient.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is generally acknowledged briefly and pivoted toward change rather than amplified. A point is deducted for occasionally restating sustain language ('expect to fail') without immediate reframing, though the net effect remained positive.
