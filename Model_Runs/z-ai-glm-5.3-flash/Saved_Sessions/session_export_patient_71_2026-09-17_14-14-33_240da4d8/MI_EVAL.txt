# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, including compassionate framing around missed days. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and missed chances to directly affirm Laura's stated strengths and values, such as her insight into all-or-nothing thinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured Laura's ambivalence about health slipping despite its importance. Reflections were sparse, however, and the emotional layers—self-criticism and fear of the 'old pattern'—were touched but not deeply explored before moving to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on Laura's target behavior—post-work movement/breathing practice—was maintained throughout. Every exchange moved purposefully from goal definition to implementation intention, backup plan, check-in, and reminder script with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-created: Laura selected the intervention, negotiated the check-in time, and personalized the reminder script. The score falls short of a 5 because the counselor sometimes presented structured solutions (implementation intentions, tracking) before eliciting Laura's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent choice-offering ('Pick one or tweak it,' 'Sound good or want a different day/time?') positioned Laura as the expert. Her choices visibly shaped the plan, including reminder wording and lead time.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura's motivations (feeling grounded, self-care rather than punishment, values-alignment) emerged largely unprompted and were not systematically drawn out. Few open questions explored her reasons or confidence; the session leaned toward action-planning rather than deliberate elicitation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong partnership framing with Laura meaningfully shaping the final plan, though the psychoeducational content about common barriers leaned slightly expert-driven before eliciting her own theory of her barriers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura generated strong change talk ('I want this... I'd like my actual life to match that,' 'most hopeful I've felt'), but the counselor did not deliberately evoke, reflect, or deepen it. Missed opportunities for elaboration requests and value-linking summaries kept momentum driven by plan logistics rather than change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Laura voiced doubts ('too small to count,' 'let's see how long this lasts') that the counselor briefly normalized but never actively reflected or reframed. Laura did the work of softening her own sustain talk; the counselor missed chances to amplify that pivot with double-sided reflections.
