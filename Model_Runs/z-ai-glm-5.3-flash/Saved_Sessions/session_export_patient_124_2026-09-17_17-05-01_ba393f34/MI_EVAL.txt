# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with affirmations like 'no judgment, just interested in what you learn' and a warm 'I've got your back.' Falling short of 5, unconditional regard could be more explicit, such as affirming Sofia's worth independent of outcomes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captured Sofia's ambivalence around relief versus worry about money, parents, and long-term benefit. However, reflections were sometimes recycled and deeper emotional content (test anxiety, family expectations) was acknowledged but not explored at an emotional level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on a specific, testable target — a drug-free Saturday with a concrete coping sequence. Slight drift via strategy menus, and Sofia's flagged hardest situations (weeknights) were deferred, leaving some direction untapped.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-authored, with Sofia's ideas (texting Maya, Saturday morning lock-in) directly shaping it. Reliance on pre-packaged option menus and the rejected in-session text draft kept it from a 5, though recovery was graceful.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was consistently supported ('I'll support whatever you decide') and Sofia's pace was respected without pressure. Missed opportunities included affirming her capacity for self-direction during moments of doubt and eliciting rather than summarizing the closing plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rulers, open questions, and 'what would bump that to a 7 or 8?' effectively drew out Sofia's own motivations and a meaningful experiment framing. Deeper values exploration (family expectations, identity) was touched but not developed into extended change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sofia was framed as the expert, permission was sought before offering options, and her limits were respected. Occasional multiple-choice menus and somewhat instructional delivery of the breathing exercise fell short of full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of open questions, scaling, and follow-ups produced genuine change talk and concrete commitments (Saturday, texting Maya, 7pm reminder). A 5 would involve deeper elaboration of her insights and commitment language beyond 'baby steps.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Double-sided reflections were used appropriately, but the counselor often repeated the sustain side across summaries and let minimizing statements ('it's not a problem') pass without strategic reframing. Adequate but not strategic.
