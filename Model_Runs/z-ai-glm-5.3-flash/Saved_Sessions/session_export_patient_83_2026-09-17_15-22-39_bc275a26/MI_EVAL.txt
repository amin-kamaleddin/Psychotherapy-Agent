# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, with validation of Mai's frustration and skepticism. However, affirmations were somewhat generic and formulaic, lacking deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured Mai's ambivalence and practical mindset (e.g., 'annoyed but practical'). Occasionally leaned toward information delivery and missed chances to explore deeper emotions like her anxiety about her mother's heart problems.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on understanding and safely starting the statin, progressing logically from confusion to a complete, actionable plan with no topic drift. The closing adherence question kept direction pointed toward sustained behavior change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative overall, with the final plan co-constructed and Mai's input shaping decisions. Information-giving segments were somewhat lecture-like, without consistently asking what Mai already knew first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently offered menus of options and presented alternatives without prescribing, letting Mai construct her own plan (orange juice, evening dosing). No coercive or pressuring language appeared at any point.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some commitment language via the closing adherence question, but readiness developed largely through education rather than deliberate evocation. Never explored Mai's own values, importance, or confidence regarding change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered some choices and options, but much of the session followed an expert-driven educational format with multi-part information blocks. Mai's expertise in her own routines was not explicitly leveraged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk emerged naturally ('I could switch to orange juice,' 'I'd rather get ahead of this now') and was reinforced with affirmations. However, it arose mainly from education rather than skillful evocation techniques like scaling questions or confidence rulers.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledged Mai's skepticism and grievances without amplifying them, providing balanced information about need. Slightly repetitive in validating the complaint about Dr. Ferris at the end, marginally redirecting focus toward a barrier.
