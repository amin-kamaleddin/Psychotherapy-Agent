# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and supportive tone with validating affirmations ('Confidence 6 is honest and useful'). However, praise is somewhat formulaic and misses chances to affirm Marcus's self-knowledge, e.g., his 'I'd skip it by Thursday' insight.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capture both content and values, such as linking family walks to what matters to Marcus. Reflections are sometimes brief, with quick pivots to questions rather than exploring the emotional weight of repeated failures.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout, opening with a clear agenda and moving systematically to a specific, measurable plan with timeline and homework. Every turn advances toward the target behavior, culminating in a structured recap and commitment elicitation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed from Marcus's preferences (walking, family format, dancing). Collaboration is somewhat undermined by rapid expert-generated option lists rather than joint brainstorming.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choice and frames advice as optional, respecting Marcus's flexible weekends without pushback. Pre-selected menu options constrain his choices, and there is no explicit acknowledgment that Marcus owns the outcome.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of confidence rulers, barrier planning, and success indicators that draw out Marcus's own reasons and solutions. Less strong at evoking deeper personal values or exploring why past attempts failed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session is frequently menu-driven, with the counselor supplying most ideas rather than inviting Marcus to generate solutions first. Marcus's own contributions (backup walk, dancing) do substantially shape the plan, which is a strength.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes and reinforces change talk via rulers and 'What would help raise that number,' with Marcus progressing from tentative interest to 'I'm kind of excited about it' and concrete commitments. Missed opportunity to explore why this feels different from past attempts.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced; discouragement is met with empathic reflection and practical redirection, and barriers like doubting home workouts are pivoted around skillfully. A 5 would require reframing sustain talk as information rather than simply moving past it.
