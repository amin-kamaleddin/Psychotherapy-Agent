# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist honors the patient's autonomy throughout, especially around disclosing the cards secret ('Only you can decide when or whether to share it'), and consistently seeks her permission implicitly while guiding toward her own priorities.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections repeatedly capture both surface and ambivalent content ('I hear you want to fix it quietly yourself AND you know that hasn't worked'), with the therapist accurately naming shame, fear, and self-awareness without judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation is clearly anchored to the patient's stated concern (gambling and debt) and pulls toward change, from the pub break to the fridge chart to the Sunday call to her sister.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All ideas originate from the patient (Egypt fund, fridge chart, accountability partner), with the therapist positioning as a curious guide rather than an expert, asking 'what do you think would need to change?'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist frames every decision as the patient's own ('you came up with two ideas yourself,' 'you built this yourself'), reinforcing her competence and choice at each step.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is consistently elicited through open questions about the patient's own reasons, capabilities, and commitment ('what would make it even slightly easier?'), and is then reflected and strengthened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The therapist maintains a strong collaborative spirit, treating the patient as the expert on her own life and building on each of her contributions rather than redirecting them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is actively deepened—desire ('Egypt as motivation'), ability ('two weeks to show her'), reasons ('enough easy months'), and commitment ('this weekend')—with the therapist skillfully amplifying commitment language once it emerges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (delaying, keeping the secret, 'wine courage') is met with reflective acceptance rather than confrontation, allowing the patient to voice and then counter her own ambivalence, though one direct question ('is that just another excuse to delay?') slightly sharpens the discord.
