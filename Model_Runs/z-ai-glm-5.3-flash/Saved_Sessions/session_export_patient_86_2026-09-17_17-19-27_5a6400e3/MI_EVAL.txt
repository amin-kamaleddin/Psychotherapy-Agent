# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently communicated respect and unconditional positive regard — acknowledged Marco's autonomy, normalized slips ('no drama'), and stayed non-judgmental despite heavy sustain talk. Fell short of 5 because warmth was somewhat formulaic, with few individualized affirmations of Marco's strengths and candor.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening early on, accurately capturing both the functional role of smoking and Marco's resistance to lectures. Deeper emotional content — his fatalism, smoking's identity-laden meaning ('the one thing nobody's taken off me yet'), and embarrassment over past failures — was only acknowledged at surface level and never reflected in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking from the first utterance through to a concrete commitment (Mon/Wed/Fri walks, craving plan, check-in, and weather/bowls contingencies). Exemplary MI direction with an appropriate target behavior and logical progression from exploration to specific plan and verification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Options were offered as a menu, Marco negotiated terms (three nights, no gum, toothpicks), and joint decision-making was invited throughout. Fell short because the psychiatrist sometimes presented fairly complete, pre-formed plans for approval rather than co-constructing each element from Marco's ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Outstanding autonomy support: asked permission before advising, offered genuine choices, accepted Marco's conditions without pressure (no gum, morning smoke off-limits, secrecy from his wife), and framed slips non-punitively. Marco — who entered saying 'I don't like lectures' — explicitly praised the approach and agreed to a plan, confirming his sense of choice was protected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from the client rather than lecturing, eliciting Marco's own reasons (dental stains, partner's comments, dog walk routine) and commitment ('I'll give it a burl'). Not maximal because deeper values and his broader 'why' were never explored, and motivation wasn't developed further once initial change talk appeared.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely positioned Marco's expertise as central — the dog-walk idea came from his own routine, and his contributions substantially shaped the plan. Not a 5 because the structured menu of options and detailed plan arrived somewhat prematurely, slightly reducing how fully the client's agenda directed the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled open questions ('what would have to be different?', 'what would feel like a win this week?') elicited genuine change talk from a precontemplative client, including a concrete three-night commitment. Fell short by not deepening confidence/ability talk (e.g., a 0-10 confidence ruler) or elaborating Marco's reasons once they emerged.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided amplifying reasons against change, responding to 'I can't see myself changing' and past failed attempts with neutral, non-reinforcing reflections and accommodation of Marco's conditions. No deliberate double-sided reflections were used to pair sustain talk with change talk, and some responses sidestepped ambivalence rather than actively resolving it.
