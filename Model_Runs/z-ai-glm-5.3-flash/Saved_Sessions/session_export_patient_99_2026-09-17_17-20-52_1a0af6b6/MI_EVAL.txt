# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, non-judgmental stance and repeatedly affirms support for Matteo's choices, but few explicit affirmations of his personal strengths (e.g., his honesty, self-awareness) limit deeper positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately captures ambivalence and validates his core concerns, but deeper emotional reflections are limited — Matteo's surprise at the binge comparison is not explored at an emotional level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on alcohol use from opening through goal-setting and follow-up scheduling. Each response builds progress toward a concrete, measurable plan anchored in Matteo's own goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative: asks permission before sharing guidance, invites preferences at every decision point, and co-constructs the plan from Matteo's ideas. Matteo's own comment that it wasn't a lecture confirms the collaborative experience.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout strength — choice is explicitly and repeatedly reinforced, options are presented as menus, and Matteo's harm-reduction goal is fully honored. Slips are framed as information rather than failure, reinforcing ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Draws motivation largely from Matteo, who generated most of his own strategies and commitment language. Open evocative questions about values and personal reasons for change are sometimes bypassed in favor of menus and information.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positions Matteo as the expert, and his own ideas substantially shape the plan (script, Danny as backup, cue to leave). Minor deduction for delivering the low-risk guidance in an information block before checking whether he wanted it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evokes change talk ('I've skipped a couple honestly,' 'that's kind of a wake-up,' 'I can probably hold that line') and reinforces commitments. Relies somewhat heavily on option menus rather than open evocative questions about why change matters.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk without amplifying and uses effective double-sided reflections honoring the social value of Wednesday nights. Conversation consistently moves toward risk-reducing actions with no strategy talk anchored to sustained drinking.
