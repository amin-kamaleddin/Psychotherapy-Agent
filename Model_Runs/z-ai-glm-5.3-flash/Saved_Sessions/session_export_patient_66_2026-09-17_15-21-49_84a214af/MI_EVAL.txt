# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, with the client moving from guardedness to open disclosure. Affirmations were somewhat sparse and generic, missing opportunities to explicitly affirm his honesty and past success at rationing.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured both sides of ambivalence, frustration, and self-doubt. Reflections sometimes leaned toward summaries and probing rather than deep emotional mirroring of the 20-year habit, fear of failure, or significance of telling his wife.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear, consistent focus on smoking behavior, progressing logically from ambivalence to a concrete, measurable plan (12/day, car storage, phone tally, start date, one-week review). No drifting from the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was co-constructed with the client contributing most of its content, and permission was asked before advising. Some exchanges resembled a consultant presenting pre-packaged options, with stacked multiple questions giving the counselor a slightly leading role.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly and repeatedly supported through frequent choice-offering and explicit framing of change as the client's decision. The counselor respected stated limits, such as not pressing when the client declined rehearsal.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew motivation from within via open questions and confidence scaling, evidenced by the client's shift from 'I'm not gonna quit' to 'Didn't walk in here planning to say any of that.' Occasionally supplied ideas (substitutes, tracking tips) rather than persistently drawing everything from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treated the client's expertise as central, eliciting his own proven strategies which substantially shaped the final plan. Rapid-fire multi-question turns and a pre-packaged suggestion list slightly limited the client's influence on session structure.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk through open questions and confidence scaling, producing substantial commitment language and rising confidence (6 to 7). Evocation sometimes shifted quickly into advice-giving, and pivotal statements like 'proof I still control this thing' went unreflected.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoided amplifying sustain talk, reflecting without arguing and reframing resistance as understandable. A dense list of practical suggestions including quitting-adjacent options briefly risked pushing change rather than letting sustain talk resolve naturally.
