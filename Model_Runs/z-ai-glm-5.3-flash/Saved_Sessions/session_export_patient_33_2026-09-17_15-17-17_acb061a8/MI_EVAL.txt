# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations ('That's huge') and acceptance of Diego's refusal to quit, though warmth is somewhat formulaic and Diego's guardedness at intake wasn't explicitly normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections are accurate but stay at surface level; the counselor never explores what Diego is escaping from or the emotional weight of shame and fear of judgment.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on harm reduction (Narcan disclosure, test strips, safer injecting), progressing logically from exploration to rehearsal to a concrete plan for tonight.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission is asked and options offered, but long instructional blocks position the counselor as the expert with Diego in a mostly reactive role rather than eliciting his own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Diego's limits and choice not to quit are honored without pressure, and choices are repeatedly offered, but steps are largely prescribed rather than developed from his preferences and autonomy affirmations stay implicit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Readiness/confidence scaling effectively drew out commitment language, but much of the session's content (scripts, instructions) was counselor-provided and deeper motivations were left unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and inviting Diego's input reflect some partnership, but the session becomes counselor-driven with long advice blocks and limited elicitation of Diego's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Open questions and scaling effectively evoked commitments ('Maybe this week'), readiness ratings (10/10), and confidence ratings (8–9), though change talk wasn't deepened beyond survival.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('the one thing that keeps me sane,' 'quitting? That's a different number') is acknowledged neutrally or passed over without double-sided reflections or reframing to resolve ambivalence.
