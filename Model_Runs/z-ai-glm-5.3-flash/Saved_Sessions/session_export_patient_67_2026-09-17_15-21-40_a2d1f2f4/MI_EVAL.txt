# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with normalized uncertainty, but affirmations were somewhat generic and transactional checklists diluted the felt sense of genuine regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Solid reflective listening accurately captured both sides of Kenji's ambivalence, but missed opportunities to fully reflect the emotional weight of the 47-drink shock and the fear of 'giving something up'.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, progressive focus moving logically from exploration to quantified assessment, safety screening, and a concrete client-selected first step, with tangents gently absorbed into the focus.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative — the plan emerged from Kenji's own selection — but multi-question blocks and expert-provided guideline data tilted some exchanges toward information delivery rather than shared agenda-shaping.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support through permission-asking, menu-based options, and honoring Kenji's choice of delaying; however, the counselor moved quickly to steps when Kenji expressed fear rather than reinforcing his ownership of the pace.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation was elicited (importance ruler, counting exercise, self-selected step), but evocation was often superseded by psychoeducation, with no confidence ruler or exploration of Kenji's own reasons and values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kenji was positioned as the source of data and chooser of direction, with the collaborative counting exercise embodying power-sharing; however, the clinician still led the structure heavily and rarely affirmed Kenji's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change language through the importance ruler and counting exercise, producing pivotal moments like 'I don't like that number'; however, these change-talk threads were left underdeveloped without elaboration questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged via double-sided reflections that softened without arguing; minor issues included a reflection that could unintentionally validate continuing as-is and missed pivots from dislike of the number toward commitment language.
