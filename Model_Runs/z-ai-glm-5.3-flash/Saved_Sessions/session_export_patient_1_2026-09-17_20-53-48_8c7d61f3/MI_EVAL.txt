# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental affirmations throughout ('that takes real character', 'You're not someone who buries his head in the sand') honored Ahmed's worth regardless of his smoking. Sustain talk about the uncle and minimization was received without disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including nuanced reflections of minimization and ambivalence ('part of you took it seriously enough to tell your wife'). Precise echoing of the patient's most meaningful words ('won't be sitting there just nodding anymore').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, purposeful focus on the dental findings with a progressive build from concern to plan to obstacles. Falls short of 5 because the target behavior (cigar use) was only lightly touched rather than progressively clarified.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan (wife accountability, calendar reminder, asking the dentist) was entirely client-generated with no unsolicited directives. The patient's own words — 'the plan feels like mine, not something handed to me' — confirm full collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit autonomy support was exceptional: 'It's your call... Nobody here is going to pressure you' and 'your wife, your calendar, your call.' The patient directly confirmed it helped him engage rather than shut down.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within via questions like 'what would it take for this to feel worth looking into more closely?' Kept at 4 because evocation stayed at the monitoring level; values around long-term health and the meaning of the smoking ritual went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned the patient as the expert ('You're the expert on your own body and your life'), and his contributions visibly shaped the final plan. The patient confirmed: 'You actually asked what I think.'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked change talk ('I'd want to know, even if the answer isn't fun', 'I can do that much') and a concrete self-generated plan. Kept at 4 because change talk centered on monitoring rather than the cigar use itself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was met with double-sided reflections rather than argument, keeping it from dominating and redirecting to the patient's own conditions for action. Scored 4 because some sustain talk ('not going to quit over maybe') remained unworked, though appropriately for a precontemplative client.
