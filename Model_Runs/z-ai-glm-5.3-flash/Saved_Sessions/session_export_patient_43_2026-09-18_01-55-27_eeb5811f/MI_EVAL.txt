# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental stance with genuine affirmations and graceful ownership of errors; fell short of a 5 due to occasional performance-flavored warmth and limited explicit validation of Eric's more vulnerable disclosures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflections reusing Eric's exact words, plus a skilled reframe about his wife's worry; early reflections occasionally leaned toward interpretation and emotional content was only lightly explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus and built steady momentum toward a concrete plan; the session opened on a mismatched focus (exhaustion) and the target behavior of smoking was never directly named.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative — the plan was built from Eric's own ideas and the clinician co-constructed contingency planning; at times a chain of leading questions made it feel like a guided agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support: choices were explicitly framed as Eric's (including permission not to change), his limits were accepted without pressure, and the plan was entirely self-generated.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary evocation with no persuasion or advice-giving; Eric's own reasons, confidence strategies, and contingency plans were drawn out, shifting him from 'I feel fine' to 'I'd probably have to listen then.'

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Eric was explicitly positioned as the expert and his contributions genuinely shaped the plan; his feedback that questioning felt pressured ('you don't let up') suggests the balance occasionally tilted toward the clinician's agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The standout strength: change talk was systematically evoked from a pre-contemplative client and developed into commitment, concrete planning, and taking-steps language within one session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was mostly met with double-sided reflections and pivots rather than argument; however, it was sometimes repeated back at length and left unsoftened at key points (e.g., 'no promises after the results').
