# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a consistently respectful, nonjudgmental tone, affirming Luis's reading efforts and desire for independence. Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, missing opportunities for more individualized expressions of unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Delivered accurate complex reflections capturing both content and emotion (frustration, relief, wariness). Reflections sometimes stayed at the surface of practical concerns without deepening into the emotional layer, e.g., fear implied by nighttime symptoms or the sting of learning his technique was wrong after ten years.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained exceptionally clear focus on asthma self-management while advancing the agenda every turn, folding tangential worries back into the plan. Textbook arc from precontemplation to a specific, agreed action plan without being pushy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative; the final plan was co-constructed from Luis's constraints and the counselor's expertise. The counselor often supplied the full option menu himself rather than eliciting Luis's own solution ideas first, making power-sharing more reactive than proactive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support was outstanding and explicit: choices were consistently offered, refusals honored (skipping the peak-flow gadget), and no added visits imposed without agreement. Luis's closing trust ('You've been straight with me on that') reflects genuinely felt autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully drew out Luis's own reasons and self-generated commitments (puff counting, pharmacy skills check) through open questions about burden and safety. Often moved quickly to providing information and options, and the strongest change talk ('worth something') was left unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Luis's contributions as driving the session, and his preferences (phone calls, pharmacy check, no gadget) directly shaped the final plan. Early option menus were fairly pre-packaged rather than explicitly inviting Luis's own ideas about what would work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Created conditions for substantial commitment language ('I'll do the little counting thing,' 'that'd be... worth something') and reinforced it with concrete, low-burden options Luis actively shaped. Rarely paused to deepen or reflect the strongest change talk back to Luis, missing chances to amplify commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided arguing with minimization and resistance, instead pivoting to simplicity and control, with warnings delivered factually only when Luis asked. Some responses quietly accommodated sustain talk (e.g., accepting 'no machine' without exploring the concern) rather than strategically reframing ambivalence.
