# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist communicates consistent warm, nonjudgmental regard, with specific, individualized affirmations (e.g., 'you're actually solving the mystery') and no disapproval even around the heavy juice habit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate throughout, with complex reflections that capture both content and emotional meaning, such as the 'gut punch' of the juice discovery and the shift in the client's relationship with the scale.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains unwavering focus on the target behavior, with purposeful, sequentially ordered questions that build a complete change plan without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely two-way, with the client's ideas driving the session content. However, the clinician-led question sequence and lack of transparent agenda-sharing keep it from the highest level.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly reinforced: expertise is located with the client, every plan element originates from Marco, and ownership is explicitly attributed to him ('it's your plan, not her job').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently elicited from within, and the strongest change content comes from the client's own words. However, the clinician did not actively test the client's stated confidence of 10 or use evocative techniques like rulers or ambivalence exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located with the client, and his contributions shape the session. Some reflections do interpretive work for the client, and the session remains clinician-structured, which keeps it from a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk is skillfully evoked and reinforced, resulting in strong commitment language from the client. However, evocation was somewhat opportunistic, lacking deliberate techniques like importance/confidence rulers or explicit elicitation of commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk was handled with a light touch via double-sided reflection and a quick pivot toward change. The softening relied largely on redirection rather than active, deliberate techniques like reframing or agreeing with a twist.
