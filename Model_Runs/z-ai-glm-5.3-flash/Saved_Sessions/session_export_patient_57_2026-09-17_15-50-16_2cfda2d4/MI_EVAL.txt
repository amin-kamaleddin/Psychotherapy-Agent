# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with specific affirmations and no shaming despite openings to moralize. Affirmations were brief and missed normalizing the difficulty of resisting peer pressure in team culture.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured Jamal's ambivalence and subtle self-doubt well. Reflections sometimes pivoted too quickly to question menus, leaving less space to sit with his emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Relentless, purposeful focus on managing party drinking under peer pressure with excellent momentum from ambivalence to goals to rehearsal and contingency planning. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed from Jamal's needs, and role-play was initiated collaboratively. Counselor-generated option lists occasionally tilted the power balance toward the counselor.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: explicit transfer of control from the opening, options framed as choices, and full acceptance of Jamal's rejections. When Jamal doubted follow-through, ownership was reinforced rather than lectured.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out Jamal's own reasons and self-persuasion, evident in his commitment language. Missed opportunities to explore his deeper values (athlete identity, team belonging) as intrinsic motivation, and some information was delivered rather than drawn out.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed that expertise resides with Jamal, whose contributions genuinely shaped the session. Pre-packaged option menus slightly crowded out his own idea generation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicited and developed desire, need, and commitment language, and behavioral rehearsal converted change talk into skill practice. Could have deepened with values-based evocative questions and explicit confidence/importance queries.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced but also not strategically softened—no double-sided reflections or pivots back to Jamal's voiced change talk. Direct reassurance responses were informative rather than MI-consistent.
