# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with repeated affirmations of David's caution, even in the face of dismissiveness and refusal to commit. Affirmations become somewhat repetitive and formulaic, with warmth that feels generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that accurately links David's skepticism to his values (work, family). Reflections lack emotional depth — themes like feeling lectured by others and his guardedness were never reflected back, and the muscle-weakness reflection was repeated without deepening.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior throughout, progressing from skepticism to a concrete plan (baseline labs, 10-minute walk, phone script, scheduled follow-up). Pacing is partly driven by repeatedly re-offering the same options, and stacked questions dilute momentum toward commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strongly collaborative stance with a menu of options, co-constructed plans, and practical supports offered rather than imposed. The opening rapid-fire question turn feels pre-scripted, and solutions are sometimes offered before exploring David's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces David's ownership of every decision without pressure toward medication, explicitly accepting his non-commitment. David directly names this as valuable ('I appreciate you not pushing the pills down my throat'), providing clear client evidence of successful autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine efforts to elicit values (grandkids, wife, work), but evocation is superficial — no elaboration questions, importance/confidence rulers, or exploration of past successes. Momentum comes more from the counselor's menu of options than from developing David's intrinsic motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Treats David as the expert by offering choices and letting his decisions drive the plan (blood test, walks, follow-up schedule). Does not explicitly acknowledge David's expertise, and early multi-question turns suggest an agenda-driven script.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively evokes change talk through values questions and small-step invitations, successfully drawing out David's own motivations and tentative openness. Does not deepen commitment language with scaling or elaboration questions, leaving change talk largely preparatory.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently avoids arguing with resistance, briefly acknowledging concerns and pivoting to values and actionable steps. Occasionally restates the sustain theme via repeated muscle-worry reflections, and misses opportunities for double-sided reflections contrasting sustain talk with David's emerging motivations.
