# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental, with warm validation of the patient's shame and discouragement. However, affirmations are sparse and generic — the two-week quit effort and her insight into triggers went unaffirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections like the double-sided summary of her ambivalence were accurate, but deep emotional material (the 'leper' metaphor, 'I feel gross', her daughter's rejection) was never explored or reflected. Multiple questions were often stacked in a single turn.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional, unwavering focus on smoking cessation throughout, with a logical arc from rulers to barriers to a concrete written plan, triggers, and accountability. No drift into unrelated topics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was genuinely co-constructed — tick boxes, daughter's initials, reward jar, break plan, and reminder phrase all originated from the patient. The patient explicitly noted the lack of lecturing made her want to try harder.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Explicit choice language ('support whatever you choose'), permission-seeking, and a menu of options respected her autonomy. However, the checklist and some tips were largely counselor-delivered rather than elicited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Readiness rulers and the 'what helped you get through those two weeks' question effectively drew out her own reasons, pride, and solutions. Some emotional material was met with solutions rather than further evocation of her strengths and hopes.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned the patient as the expert by asking permission and inviting her input, and her ideas genuinely shaped the plan. Falls short of 5 because plan structure and advice were provided fairly rapidly rather than drawn from her.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Readiness rulers with 'why that number' and the success-memory question reliably evoked strong change talk and a concrete step. Change talk was rarely deepened once elicited — e.g., confidence 4 was not explored for what would raise it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently pivoted from barriers toward solutions and used shame-softening framings like 'slips as info, not failure.' Some sustain talk (fear of failure) was voiced back without reframing toward her capacity or past success.
