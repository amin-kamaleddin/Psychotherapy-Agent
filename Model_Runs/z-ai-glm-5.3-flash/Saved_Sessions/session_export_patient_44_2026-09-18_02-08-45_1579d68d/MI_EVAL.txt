# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently honored the patient's autonomy, framing all decisions as the patient's own ('You're the expert on your own experience'). Discomfort about comparisons and diet struggles was acknowledged without judgment.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Demonstrated deep understanding through reflective statements like 'there's a real pull between two things' and accurately naming the patient's ambivalence about comparisons and self-patience. The patient repeatedly felt heard and validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained a clear focus on the patient's stated goal of consistent gym attendance and motivation, skillfully steering the conversation toward the patient's own reasons for change. The diet struggle was acknowledged but appropriately kept secondary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions consistently invited the patient's expertise: 'What do you think would help...?' and 'Which of those feels more doable to you right now?' The plan (bag by the door, two visits) came entirely from the patient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reinforced self-directed change throughout: 'That patience is a skill,' 'it came from your own thinking, not mine.' Emphasized the patient's ownership ('something that belongs entirely to you').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Successfully drew out change talk, including strong expressions of desire and ability ('I want to feel like that more often,' 'I'm done pretending I can't change anything'). Used the treadmill math and back-pain memory as evocative anchors.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session felt like a genuine dialogue, with the psychiatrist building on each patient contribution rather than directing. Active collaboration in defining success criteria and concrete next steps.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertly recognized and amplified the patient's own change statements, such as highlighting 'I actually want to see what happens' and reflecting it back as hope and curiosity. Change talk increased markedly by the end.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (dreading the gym, comparisons to younger men, candy bar temptations) was met with reflection rather than confrontation, and the patient progressively restructured it into motivation ('my own race to run').
