# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirmed the patient's perspective, normalized her ambivalence ('it makes sense you'd want the whole picture'), and reframed self-criticism as self-protection ('that's you looking out for your future self, not being stubborn').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Deep, accurate empathy was shown throughout, with reflections that captured both sides of the patient's ambivalence ('you're holding both of these at once') and even named her surprise at noticing new insights. The patient explicitly confirmed feeling understood ('exhausting is the right word').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation had a clear therapeutic direction toward exploring the patient's medication concerns, with skilled use of her own stated goals (understanding the inhaler, prioritizing the practical mixing-up issue first) to guide the path forward.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician worked in a strong partnership stance, offering choices ('bring it to your doctor, or explore it more here with me first?'), using 'we' language ('let's figure it out together'), and explicitly deferring to the patient's decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's autonomy was emphasized repeatedly ('you get to decide,' 'wherever you land, you get to decide'), which the patient directly identified as helpful in contrast to her husband's pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently drew out the patient's own motivations and ideas rather than persuading, eliciting her proposal for a 'middle spot,' her self-generated questions, and her reasoning about prioritizing the fixable problem first.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A genuine partnership was evident, including a shared commitment ('I'll hold that promise with you: we will come back to it') and joint problem framing ('whichever we start with, we start together').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was well-evoked (willingness to learn, 'I don't want to end up in an ER,' planning to prep questions for her doctor), though the clinician could have paused more explicitly to reflect and reinforce some of these statements rather than moving on quickly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fear of dependence, faith in trigger-avoidance) was consistently reflected and diffused non-confrontationally, allowing the patient to talk herself toward change ('maybe skipping the daily one is why I need the rescue one so much... I never put those two together before') without argument.
