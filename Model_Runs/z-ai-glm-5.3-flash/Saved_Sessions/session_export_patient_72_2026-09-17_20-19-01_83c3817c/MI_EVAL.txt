# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with no judgment about inactivity or past failed attempts. The closing explicitly honors the client's timeline and ownership ('starts don't have to be flawless', 'it's yours').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that often captures deeper meaning ('I just want to feel like myself again'). Missed opportunities include unaddressed emotional content, such as Leila's stated embarrassment about her step count, and some reflections that remain simple restatements.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tight focus on re-establishing exercise with no topic drift. Each response builds momentum from imagining benefits to a concrete, client-generated plan (two walks, YMCA visit) appropriate to her stage of change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Substantive two-way exchange with all ideas originating from the client. However, late-session turns blur roles—the counselor sounds like the one making commitments ('I'll text Maya')—temporarily distorting the equal-partner dynamic.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Leila chooses her own activities, accountability, and support preferences, all affirmed without pressure or advice-giving. Permission-oriented questions ('What would be most helpful from me?') and the closing reinforce full client ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Nearly every turn is designed to draw out the client's own motivations, solutions, and insights, with no advice or persuasion. Evocation goes beyond surface reasons to deeper values like strength, confidence, and 'feeling like herself again'.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Leila is consistently positioned as the expert ('you came up with it yourself', 'you've done the hard part already'). The late-session role reversal, where the counselor's voice sounds like the client's commitments, muddies the partnership frame in the closing portion.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions elicit rich desire, ability, and commitment language, reinforced by reflections like highlighting the 'really different voice'. The session is saturated with client-generated change talk culminating in concrete commitments.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk is handled well by redirecting to the client's own solutions without amplifying barriers. A double-sided reflection was never used, though the low level of ambivalence makes this a minor gap.
