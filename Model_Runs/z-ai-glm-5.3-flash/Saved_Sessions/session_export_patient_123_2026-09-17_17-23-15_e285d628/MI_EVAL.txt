# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, normalizing Sofia's view that the screening wasn't a big deal and avoiding lecturing or labeling. Warmth was consistent but somewhat generic and formulaic, with repeated scripted-sounding affirmations.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections accurately summarized surface content (school, friends, grades, science test) but stayed factual. Sofia's underlying feelings—her defensiveness, awkwardness about refusal lines, and desire to leave—were acknowledged only superficially, with no complex reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening four-question barrage scattered focus into generic background-taking, and the session later drifted toward study stress and class logistics. Alcohol risk remained the nominal focus, but no specific collaborative behavior change was developed.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menu-based choices and permission-seeking showed some equal footing, but the core intervention was delivered as expert psychoeducation—six safety tips offered without first eliciting Sofia's existing knowledge or strategies. Sofia remained largely a passive recipient.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offered choices (safety tips vs. study support, practicing a line vs. keeping it simple) and respected Sofia's decisions without pressure. However, the options were counselor-constructed rather than tied to Sofia's own ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was assumed rather than elicited—the counselor never asked Sofia what she thought of the screening results or what staying safe would mean for her goals. Her natural hook ('my friends look out for each other') was affirmed but not developed into client-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some partnership was present through menu options and agenda questions, but when Sofia expressed uncertainty the counselor quickly shifted into expert mode, delivering feedback and safety tips. Sofia's contributions were incorporated mainly as validation of pre-selected counselor content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** There was minimal effort to evoke change talk—Sofia never articulated desire, ability, reasons, or need for change. The only mild elicitation ('which two feel doable?') was not paired with open questions about her own reasons, values, or vision for staying safe.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sofia's clear sustain talk ('it's not a big deal,' 'my friends look out for each other,' 'this all seems a bit much') was mirrored back rather than met with double-sided reflections, inadvertently reinforcing minimization. Redirection toward change was weak and replaced mostly by advice-giving.
