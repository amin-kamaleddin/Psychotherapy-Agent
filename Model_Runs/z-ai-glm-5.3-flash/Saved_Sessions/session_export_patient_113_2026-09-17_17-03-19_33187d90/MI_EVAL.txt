# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance, validating Rowan's constraints and honoring his boundaries (no texts, no praise) without defensiveness. However, affirmations were brief and warmth felt somewhat transactional rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were reasonable but stayed at the content level, with the session dominated by rapid, multi-part questions. Rowan's underlying feelings — annoyance at being written off, quiet pride, fear of failure — were never explicitly explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior and ended with a concrete, specific experiment (charger in key bowl, three alarms, coiled cord). The opening triple-barreled question muddied focus, and the low importance rating (3/10) — the core barrier — was never revisited.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Interaction was largely between equals, with Rowan's preferences and rules genuinely shaping the plan. However, most concrete solutions (power bank, Velcro strap, reminder phrase) were counselor-generated rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: framed the plan as Rowan's experiment to judge for himself, honored his refusal of texts and praise, and consistently offered options rather than directives. Rowan's control over pace and terms was reinforced throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and some open questions drew out numbers and criteria, but the counselor pivoted quickly to supplying solutions rather than continuing to evoke motivation. Rowan's emerging intrinsic reasons (not feeling different when skipping, resentment at low expectations) were never amplified.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Rowan as the decision-maker, asking his preferences on alarms, phrasing, and check-in style, and following his lead. Some advice (Velcro strap, reminder phrase examples) remained counselor-generated, keeping it from a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used importance/confidence rulers and following-the-lead questions to consolidate commitments ('I'll do the keys thing and see'). Missed the strategic opportunity to explore why importance was only a 3 to evoke desire/need talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rolled with resistance by reframing persistence as 'a solid experiment' and acknowledged Rowan's right to judge after trying, without arguing. Limited use of double-sided reflections to resolve ambivalence, but no reinforcement of the status quo.
