# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance with consistent validation and no moralizing about drinking or the incident. Affirmations were somewhat generic and lacked individualized depth or normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening accurately captured Tyler's ambivalence about drinking and his record. However, reflections rarely touched the emotional core (fear about scholarship, peer judgment) and often moved too quickly to menu options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use and the conduct incident, progressing logically from email to advisor meeting to party strategies. Focus occasionally scattered with multiple option lists, and the drinking discussion stayed surface-level without linking to deeper values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked as a partner through role-play and joint script development, incorporating Tyler's own ideas into the final plan. Still led with prepared lists and drafts at times, making the power balance uneven.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed decisions as Tyler's ('Which feels truer to you?', 'Which feels most doable?') with no coercive language. However, pre-packaged option menus narrowed space for self-directed solutions.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened with a good evocative question and drew out Tyler's own motivations, but the work stayed transactional and logistical. Never explored why education matters to Tyler, future vision, or confidence, leaving motivation driven more by the counselor's framing than the client's values.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Incorporated Tyler's input and followed his preferences, but frequently supplied ready-made scripts, drafts, and option lists rather than eliciting Tyler's own solutions first, tipping toward a consultant role.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk ('pacing myself', committing to send the email tonight) but failed to strategically deepen or consolidate it, missing chances to reinforce the commitment or link it to Tyler's stated values.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided reinforcing sustain talk and effectively redirected it with brief acknowledgment. However, missed opportunities for double-sided reflections pairing sustain talk with change talk, and some option lists risked inviting negotiation rather than resolving ambivalence.
