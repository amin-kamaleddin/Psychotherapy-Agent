# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Opens with warmth and unconditional regard, positioning the client as the expert with no judgment or labeling. Excellent demonstrated acceptance throughout the visible transcript.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Counselor demonstrates consistent reflective listening, accurately reflecting content ('health is what mattered most,' 'failure weighs on you, but drift you can work with') and emotion (hope, guilt, self-compassion). Complex reflections track and deepen Laura's meaning throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on aligning daily actions with health values through values clarification and targeted follow-up questions. Sustained guidance through drift and ambivalence is evident throughout the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Positions the session as joint exploration and consistently builds on Laura's own ideas and solutions. The counselor visibly integrates and extends her contributions across multiple turns.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongly supports Laura's autonomy with non-directive questions and explicit ownership framing. Client confirms feeling supported rather than told what to change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within the client—her own reasons, strategies, and reframings ('friendship with myself'). Counselor skillfully evokes rather than supplies solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary partnership language ('you're the expert on your own life, I'm along for the exploration') and questions that consistently return ownership to the client ('what feels like the right first step for you?').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Evocative questions successfully elicit extensive change talk: 'I want that version of my week,' 'that feels doable,' 'I feel more ready than I have in a while.' Counselor deepens this talk through reflections and affirmations rather than merely eliciting it once.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Counselor actively addresses sustain talk ('it's just one night,' 'you're too tired') using double-sided reflections that acknowledge ambivalence while pivoting toward change ('part of you remembers the drift, and part of you is genuinely hopeful').
