# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, with normalizing statements like 'notice without judgment.' However, affirmations are generic and formulaic ('you've got this' repeated), lacking individualized recognition of Farid's strengths and history of dropped plans.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal—one early empathic reflection, then mostly advice-giving. Farid's emotionally meaningful disclosures (self-doubt, past failures, discouragement) receive no reflective or validating responses.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on increasing exercise with movement toward a concrete action step. However, the direction is premature and narrow—locking onto one micro-plan within the second exchange without exploring values or readiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely counselor-driven; the implementation intention and alarm label come entirely from the counselor, with Farid only ratifying them. A collaborative approach would co-construct the plan from Farid's knowledge of his schedule and history.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine choice is offered ('Want to try one of these?'), and Farid selects the backup plan. But repetitive prescriptive recaps have a controlling, checklist quality that undermines ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is supplied by the counselor through cheerleading rather than drawn from Farid. Open-ended evocative questions about why exercise matters, hopes, or confidence are almost entirely absent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, presenting pre-packaged options and a finished plan for approval. Little inquiry into what Farid thinks would work, despite his own useful idea about renaming the alarm.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Farid spontaneously offers change talk ('I've been thinking about working out more,' 'I actually feel okay about this one'), but the counselor rarely evokes or deepens it. No open questions explore Farid's own reasons, confidence, or meaning of success.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk about laziness and past dropped plans is quickly dismissed ('You're not lazy') rather than strategically explored. This avoids reinforcement but misses the chance to resolve ambivalence; no double-sided reflections are used.
