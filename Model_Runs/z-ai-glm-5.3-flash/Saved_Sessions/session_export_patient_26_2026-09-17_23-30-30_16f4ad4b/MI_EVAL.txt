# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard maintained throughout—no judgment about smoking, drinking, or distrust of family. Self-doubt was validated warmly, and Daniela's growing openness suggests she felt fully accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately echoed her language and emotional core (fear of being a burden, carrying everything alone). Fell short of 5 by not deeply exploring the pain beneath 'I haven't felt like I could count on myself lately.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent focus on reliable childcare, progressing logically from options to rehearsal to timing and Head Start planning. Each question tightened focus without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine back-and-forth, including offering to rehearse the text together, with the client's ideas driving the plan. Fell slightly short because reflections occasionally led ahead of Daniela before she confirmed next steps.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice repeatedly reinforced: 'that's your call' on money, 'what feels most comfortable to you.' Ownership of the plan was consistently handed back to Daniela.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every meaningful motivation—pride in self-reliance, her son's future, finishing school—came from Daniela's own words, drawn out through skilled open questions. Motivation was elicited, never supplied.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise clearly placed within the client ('You already know your own answers here'), leading directly to her generating her own solutions. Her contributions shaped the session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength—skillful evocation of options, meaning, and commitment ('I want to walk in here and say I did both things. Not I meant to — I did'). Rich commitment language and a concrete plan were all client-generated.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Doubts about asking and the 'eight o'clock version of me' were softened by reflecting and pivoting to her strengths and past successes. Relied more on reassurance than strategic double-sided reflections, sometimes reassuring too quickly.
