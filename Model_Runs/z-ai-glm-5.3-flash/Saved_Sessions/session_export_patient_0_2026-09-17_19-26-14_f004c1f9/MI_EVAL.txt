# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and non-judgment throughout, explicitly stating 'there's no judgment in the room' and repeatedly affirming Adrian's honesty. No labeling, arguing, or disapproval even during defensive moments.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening, including complex and double-sided reflections that captured minimization and hidden emotional content. Reflections consistently prompted Adrian to open up further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on meth use and its risks from the opening through a concrete plan (testing kits, weekend boundaries). Momentum was guided through pivots built on Adrian's own words without being pushy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative: the psychiatrist asked rather than told and co-constructed the plan from Adrian's own ideas. Adrian's input visibly redirected the session toward a harm-reduction frame he proposed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit and repeated support of choice, affirming self-defined goals like 'figuring out what staying safe looks like for you is a legitimate goal.' No pressure or coercive framing; Adrian set the pace (e.g., deferring the brother conversation).

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out Adrian's own motivations—avoiding another infection, being someone his brother doesn't worry about, fearing loss of control. Change talk developed visibly across the session, including the spontaneous Wednesday-use disclosure.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicitly positioned Adrian as the expert on his own life, with his contributions ('being smarter about it,' testing kits, written rules) becoming the session's agenda and plan. Full power-sharing with client-driven direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic open questions reliably evoked change talk ('I don't want to do that again'), which the psychiatrist then reinforced and developed into a concrete commitment. Strong desire, commitment, and vision talk elicited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Generally used double-sided reflections and reframing to soften sustain talk without amplifying it. Minor deduction for the closing self-disclosure ('I've never been great at rules I make for myself'), which briefly bordered on validating sustain talk before recovery.
