# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, and nonjudgmental with specific affirmations; the patient confirmed feeling taken seriously. Deduction for formulaic, recycled affirmations (the 'glad you asked' praise repeated ~4 times) making warmth feel templated.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate reflections on her need for clarity and a written plan, but a major miss on her repeatedly voiced time pressure, which was acknowledged only glancingly. Reflections recycled the same two themes rather than tracking in-the-moment emotional nuance.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong topical focus: the safety-critical answer was delivered directly and accurately, and deferring exact washout dates to the prescriber was appropriate. Deductions for poor pacing — the session was extended well past its natural endpoint and reopened after a goodbye instead of consolidating with a summary.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed (patient generated the phone-note system and doctor questions), with inclusive language. But the clinician unilaterally controlled session length and rhythm, creating partnership in content but not in process.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Verbal autonomy support was present, but behaviorally the patient's explicit, repeated choice to end the session was overridden at least five times — including reopening with a new evocative question after a goodbye. This inconsistency in honoring the client's actual choices is the decisive failure of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Frequent open questions drew out authentic client-generated material and self-knowledge ('I'm disciplined once something's on the list'). However, evocation stayed at the logistics level, never touched values or confidence, and became repetitive and extractive when pressed onto a client who had stated her need and time limit.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good partnership behaviors: agenda-setting at the start, tentative suggestion framing, and building on the patient's own contributions. But her explicit feedback ('I just need the facts') was reflected yet never behaviorally incorporated, as the clinician's agenda repeatedly superseded hers.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine evocation of commitment language ('I won't start anything on my own,' the phone-note implementation intention), which the clinician reinforced. Deductions for mechanical repetition and continued elicitation after the change goal was already secured and after the patient signaled she needed to leave; no exploration of importance or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Appropriately, no sustain talk was amplified — the patient was fully aligned with the safety plan. The only counter-change material (time-constraint barrier talk) was never skillfully engaged, just overridden, landing the performance at neutral-adequate.
