# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently conveys unconditional positive regard — validates the piercing as her choice ('it was your call to make, period') and never judges or labels it a mistake. Warm, respectful, and free of disapproval throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate, validating reflections capture her dismissive-but-rational stance and her critique of vague warnings. Slightly short of a 5 because reflections mostly paraphrase content without exploring emotional nuance, e.g., her feelings about her grandmother's dental decline.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on the target behavior with a concrete progression — from dismissal to identifying warning signs to a self-check plan and follow-up steps — while still feeling client-paced rather than forced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Asks permission before informing and co-constructs the plan around her stated preferences, though one information-delivery stretch (warning signs list) is counselor-led without explicit invitation for the client to add her own knowledge or negotiate the agenda.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is affirmed explicitly and often ('it's yours to make either way,' 'no promises beyond what feels right'), with genuine choices offered and her preference for no scheduled homework accepted without pressure. No coercive language about removal anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Draws motivation from the client, with the values-based question about her grandmother eliciting the session's most meaningful change talk. Some follow-ups are somewhat leading and evocation is limited to a single values probe.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently locates expertise in the client ('you know your own mouth and habits better than anyone'), offers information only after asking permission, and lets the client's preferences shape the session's direction — a hallmark of strong partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and reinforces the client's own change language ('if my mouth starts telling me something's wrong, I'll listen then,' 'catching it early beats pretending it's nothing'), well-tailored to a pre-contemplative client. Falls short of a 5 only because desire/ability language is not developed more deeply.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Never argues against sustain talk, instead pivoting to affirm the workable part ('the piercing stays... now you're protecting it on your own terms'). Sustain talk is sidestepped rather than battled, but double-sided reflections are not used to actively shift the balance toward change.
