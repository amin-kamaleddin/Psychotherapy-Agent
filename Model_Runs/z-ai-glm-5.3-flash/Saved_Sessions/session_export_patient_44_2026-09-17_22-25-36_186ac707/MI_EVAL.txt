# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, with affirmations like 'You've got real insight there' and reframing self-blame respectfully. No judgment, labeling, or disapproval anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every response opens with an accurate reflection capturing both emotional and content layers of Erik's speech. Reflections are varied (simple, complex, double-meaning) and never inaccurate or repetitive.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on the target behaviors (gym consistency, diet, self-comparison) with a logical progression from problem exploration to concrete commitment. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Interview conducted between equal partners, with no advice or psychoeducation. Erik explicitly values the collaborative stance ('it feels better when I figure these things out myself').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions consistently emphasize Erik's choice and self-defined standards ('keeping the bar low enough to clear, on your own terms'). Zero advice-giving or pressure; all strategies originate from Erik.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is systematically drawn from within Erik via strategic open questions, surfacing values like back pain, his wife, and the hiking trip. Change talk is deepened progressively from desire ('I could try') to commitment ('The bag gets packed tonight').

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is consistently positioned within the client, and Erik explicitly recognizes this ('Nobody's asked me that before'). Every plan element originates from Erik, whom the counselor credits as the problem-solver.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions deliberately elicit client-generated change talk ('I could start tonight, actually'; 'I'm willing to try'), which is then reinforced through reflection. Values are evoked to deepen motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently acknowledged then pivoted toward change without dismissal—e.g., reflecting comparison discouragement once before redirecting to progress. Barriers are reframed as foresight ('you're seeing the traps before they spring') rather than amplified.
