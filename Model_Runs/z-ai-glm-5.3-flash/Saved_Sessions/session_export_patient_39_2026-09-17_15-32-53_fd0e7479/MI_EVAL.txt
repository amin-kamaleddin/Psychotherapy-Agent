# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a warm, nonjudgmental stance and validates Elias's discouragement throughout. However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic and there is no explicit normalization of his fear of looking foolish.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong, accurate reflective listening captures both the meaning of the outing and his shame. Reflections occasionally stay surface-level, not fully exploring the loneliness of 'four walls' or the fear of looking foolish.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus with no topic drift — every exchange ties back to returning to the activity group. The session progresses logically from values, to one small change, to a confidence ruler, to a concrete, rehearsed commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor frames the work jointly, checks in before moving forward, and Elias's contributions substantially shape the plan. Collaboration weakens somewhat when the counselor supplies multiple pre-written script options rather than developing Elias's own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear autonomy support through option-offering and affirming that choices are Elias's decision, and Elias owns the final plan. The counselor becomes somewhat prescriptive when offering scripted language, reducing Elias's sense of authorship.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within — Elias generates his own reasons, coping plan (tap signal), and assertive replies, and the confidence ruler is well used. The script-writing segment tips toward counselor-supplied content rather than eliciting Elias's phrasing.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias's input is invited early, but the counselor increasingly takes the lead with scripts, suggested replies, and role-play steering. Little explicit acknowledgment that Elias holds the expertise on his own life and marriage.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skilled evocation through future-oriented questions, a small-change focus, and a confidence ruler elicits genuine change talk and a concrete commitment. Could be deepened by reflecting Elias's change statements back and exploring his values ('a man making a plan').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced; brief acknowledgment is followed by pivots to change, and a double-sided reflection is used effectively. Occasionally ambivalence is mirrored without an immediate pivot, slightly diluting momentum.
