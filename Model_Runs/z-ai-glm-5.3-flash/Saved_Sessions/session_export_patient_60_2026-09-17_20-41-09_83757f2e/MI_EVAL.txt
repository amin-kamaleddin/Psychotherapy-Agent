# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard was maintained throughout despite the patient's dismissive stance, with no judgment or disapproval about the drinking. Frustration was normalized and the patient's thinking was affirmed even during minimization and deflection.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was highly accurate, precisely mirroring the patient's externalization of blame and later his own realizations. Repeatedly capturing his values (fun, friendships, not being 'Davis') visibly deepened self-reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus on the target behavior was maintained with a coherent arc from validation through exploration to planning and follow-up. A point is lost for not directly addressing the recurring 'I'll be 21 in a month' minimization, which slightly blunted focus near the end.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was fully collaborative, with the counselor's questions genuinely inviting the patient's ideas and his answers visibly redirecting the conversation. The follow-up plan was co-constructed on the patient's own terms.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was strongly reinforced ('You'd be running the show'), with no imposed advice and the check-in framed entirely as the patient's choice. All ideas came from the patient or his roommate, with zero pressure or persuasion.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within rather than supplied, with questions eliciting the patient's own values (graduation, career, not being 'Davis') and connecting them to behavior. A textbook evocation arc from external blame to self-motivated commitment.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient was explicitly positioned as the expert ('You'd know better than anyone,' 'You'd be running the show'), and his own contributions shaped the entire plan. His expertise clearly drove the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked via strategic questions ('what would need to happen for the partying side and that bigger picture to fit together?'), progressing the patient from 'just vibes' to a concrete self-generated plan. Each change statement was reinforced with reflections.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally not reinforced, with resistance rolled effectively (e.g., honoring 'I don't want to quit going out'). However, the 'one party, come on' and 'birthday solves it' rationalizations were left unaddressed at the close, missing a chance to gently reframe them.
