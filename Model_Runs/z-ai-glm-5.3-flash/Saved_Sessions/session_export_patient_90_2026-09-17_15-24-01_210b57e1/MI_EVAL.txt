# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated warm, nonjudgmental regard, exemplified by the respectful handling of the dog boundary ('dog is family, no questions') and the opening 'I'm here to listen without judgment.' Marcus's closing comment ('Thanks for not piling more on me') confirms the supportive, affirming tone throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured Marcus's core distress and ambivalence, including the double-sided reflection 'You're torn—asthma scares you, but extra appointments and lost work feel impossible.' Deducted for limited deepening of emotional exploration, such as the fear behind nighttime wheezing or the meaning of the dog in his life.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained clear focus on asthma management throughout, with every response tying back to the target behavior and culminating in a concrete, actionable plan. Minor deduction for the early phase briefly surveying all stressors before narrowing, though this served as necessary agenda-setting rather than aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely a joint enterprise, with Marcus identifying priorities, choosing among options, setting his own timing, and contributing the wife's role himself. Deducted one point because the inhaler technique and breathing exercise segments were delivered as expert instruction rather than interactive problem-solving.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently strong autonomy support with repeated emphasis on choice, offering menus of options, and explicitly honoring the dog boundary ('no questions') without any pressuring language. Marcus left with self-selected, self-generated commitments—the word 'maybe' about future appointments reflecting genuine ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively drew motivation from within, using open-ended questions, self-selected first steps, and confidence scaling that elicited strong change talk ('maybe I'm not as stuck as I thought'). Evocation tapered in the second half, shifting to giving tips rather than continuing to elicit and consolidate Marcus's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor conveyed that expertise resides with Marcus ('You're in charge of what we focus on'), offered menus of options, and the entire plan was built from Marcus's own preferences and ideas. A point was deducted because the inhaler technique instruction was delivered as a fairly unidirectional teaching block rather than co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of evocative questions, including asking Marcus to pick 'one small, doable thing' and confidence scaling with follow-up about what would raise his confidence from 5–6 to 7–8, which produced substantial change talk. Slight deduction because evocative questions were somewhat generic and the strongest change statements were not further deepened or affirmed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Barriers like finances and the dog boundary were acknowledged respectfully without argument, and the counselor pivoted to dog-free, low-cost options. However, softening relied largely on avoidance rather than skillful double-sided reflections, leaving ambivalence about the pet and appointments unresolved and likely to resurface.
