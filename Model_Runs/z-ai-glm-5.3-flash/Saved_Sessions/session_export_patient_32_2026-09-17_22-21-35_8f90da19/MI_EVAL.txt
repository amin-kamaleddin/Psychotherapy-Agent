# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor communicates unconditional positive regard, offering individualized affirmations ('That's you finding your own answer') and honoring Derrick's loyalty to his friends as real. The relapse and minimization are never judged, and struggle is normalized without condoning the drinking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex reflections land precisely, e.g., capturing that sobriety failed not from not-drinking but from the empty social space. The double-sided reflection holding both loyalty to his friends and his clear-eyed plan accurately captures his ambivalence.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains clear focus on the drinking/DUI concern while progressing coherently from filling the social void to a concrete commitment (signing up before next session). There is no aimless wandering; each exchange ties back to the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is fully between equal partners: every plan element (basketball, wallet card, Saturday excuse) is Derrick's invention, and the counselor seeks his evaluation at each step. The counselor reflects and supports rather than redirects.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Derrick's ownership is consistently reinforced — the written plan is offered as a question and adapted to his style (wallet card rather than journaling). Self-generated motivation ('it's gotta be mine') is explicitly affirmed rather than met with external pressure.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions are masterfully designed to elicit Derrick's own resources, producing his insight that his 'why' must be internal ('It's gotta be mine'). Confidence talk is evoked by connecting him to his prior six-month success.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly locates expertise within the client and never offers advice; every plan element originated with Derrick. Questions consistently defer to Derrick's self-knowledge, making his contributions the driving force of the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open, evocative questions ('Which one feels most alive to you?') consistently draw out rich change talk, including commitment language ('I'll sign up for the league before our next talk'). The counselor reinforces and deepens this language with affirmations and forward-looking questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk and fear of being 'the guy who's too good for everybody' are acknowledged briefly through double-sided reflections, then redirected toward Derrick's own change language. No session time amplifies reasons against change.
