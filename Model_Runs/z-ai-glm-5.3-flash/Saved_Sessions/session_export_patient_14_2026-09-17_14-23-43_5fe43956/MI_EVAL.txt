# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a neutral, nonjudgmental stance and apologized appropriately for the wait. However, repeated safety check-ins felt formulaic despite the patient's clarifications, and there were no affirmations of the patient's strengths or genuine warmth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal; the counselor acknowledged frustration briefly but pivoted quickly to solutions and logistics. The patient's emotional content—self-criticism about failed plans and fear of weight 'getting a hold of' him—went largely unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior and efficiently produced a concrete plan with specific days, fallbacks, tracking, and follow-up. Focus was maintained through efficiency rather than strategic MI direction tied to the patient's values, preventing a higher score.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There was partial collaboration—the patient's preferences shaped the schedule, tracking method, and follow-up frequency. However, the core intervention was delivered in an expert-driven, list-like manner, and the patient's statement 'I know all of it' was a missed cue to elicit his own solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offered meaningful choices throughout (topic order, behavior selection, tracking method, follow-up interval) and adapted when the patient rejected weekly check-ins. Initial suggestions were nonetheless presented as a done-deal plan rather than a menu the patient helped generate.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was supplied rather than evoked; the counselor provided reasons, structure, and solutions. The patient's spontaneous change talk ('I'd like to get a hold of that') was never elicited, reflected, or expanded—no open questions explored his own reasons, hopes, or confidence.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor positioned itself as the source of solutions, answering the patient's request for ideas with a prescriptive two-item plan. The patient's contributions shaped the plan more through his own assertiveness than through deliberate power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The patient spontaneously offered strong change statements about the stairs and weight, but the counselor responded with logistics rather than reflecting, affirming, or amplifying them. No questions explored why change matters, what success would look like, or how confident he felt.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not argue with sustain talk ('I really haven't got time', 'food is just fuel') and the 'data, not failure' reframe effectively softened the patient's all-or-nothing pattern. However, there were no double-sided reflections linking barriers to stated desires, and food-prep doubt was bypassed rather than explored.
