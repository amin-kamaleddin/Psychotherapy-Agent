# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing ambivalence and thanking the client for sharing. Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, with limited validation of the emotional difficulty of confronting the habit.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of Kenji's routine, stress-relief drinking, and history of a failed cutback attempt. Missed opportunities to explore deeper emotional content, such as his hint at loneliness ('I don't really have relationships to mess up').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use with thorough assessment, safety screening, a concrete tracking plan, and a scheduled follow-up. Some delivery was dense and checklist-like, and focus stayed on tracking rather than building motivation toward the reduction goal itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered choices at multiple decision points and accommodated Kenji's preferences and doubts. Planning was somewhat one-directional — the log template was counselor-defined rather than co-created, and final turns were repetitive monologues without checking in.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Regularly affirmed choice and pace, accepting Kenji's selection of tracking over an alcohol-free day without pressure. Deducted for prescriptive, repeated safety messaging and counselor-defined plan details.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some client insights emerged ('eye-opening,' 'I drink the third beer just because it's there'), but these arose from structured menus rather than deliberate evocative questioning. No open exploration of Kenji's values, importance/confidence, or deepening of his change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Positioned Kenji as decision-maker through choice-offering and incorporated his suggestions into the plan; his 'thanks for not making this a big lecture thing' confirms the stance landed. Fell short of explicitly acknowledging Kenji as expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was elicited indirectly through menu options ('I can do that,' proposing to delay drinks) but was never deepened or reinforced. Lacked open questions about Kenji's own reasons, confidence, or values for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responded to ambivalence and resistance with normalization rather than argument, and let Kenji choose the lower-barrier option when an alcohol-free day 'sounds hard.' Slight deduction because the structure occasionally kept attention on barriers rather than pivoting toward change language.
