# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Communicates unconditional positive regard throughout, including honoring the confidentiality request ('Your secret's safe with me') and affirming Timur's honesty about fear and temptation. Warmth is genuine and individualized, with no judgment about his criminal history or self-labels.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate, capturing phrases like 'boring and mine' and subtle emotional shifts such as Timur's fear of rejection and his humor. The counselor tracks and mirrors his feelings throughout, including his reluctance to show vulnerability.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session follows a clear arc from envisioning a pro-social life, to a concrete step (visiting the training place), to a coping plan and follow-up agreement. Each exchange moves momentum forward, with periodic summaries reinforcing direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session functions as genuine joint problem-solving, with the counselor asking rather than telling and co-constructing the plan from Timur's own resources (the training place, Fiona's support). Timur explicitly notes the contrast with past experiences: 'You actually listened.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice is repeatedly and explicitly reinforced ('Either way, it's your call,' 'nothing signed away, it's just asking a question'), and disclosure to Fiona is left entirely to Timur's timing. The counselor never pressures and accepts his pacing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every major insight is client-generated ('a no's a no, not the end of me,' 'you've done harder than this'), and the counselor deliberately avoids providing answers ('it came from you, not me'). The result is strong commitment language and a fully self-authored plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Timur is explicitly positioned as the expert ('let's use your expertise here,' 'it came from you, not me'), and all plan elements originate from him. The counselor builds each step from the client's own material rather than prescribing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions ('What would a life worth staying clean for look like?') elicit progressively stronger change talk, from 'someone gets them, right?' to a concrete commitment ('Tuesday'). The counselor reflects change talk back and ends by reinforcing the identity shift.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is handled well through reframing and double-sided reflections ('Both can be true') without argument or reinforcement. A point short of 5 because fear of rejection and lingering temptation toward easy money are explored at length rather than consistently pivoted toward change.
