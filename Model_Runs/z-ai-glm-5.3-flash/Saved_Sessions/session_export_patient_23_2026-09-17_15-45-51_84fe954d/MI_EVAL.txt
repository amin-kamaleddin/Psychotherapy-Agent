# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warm respect throughout, with affirmations ('that's a real strength') and acceptance of Claudia's modest two-drink goal without judgment. Warmth was somewhat brief and formulaic, with little normalization of slips or validation of the emotional weight of her depression.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured her ambivalence well, e.g., 'wine helps you unwind but sometimes leaves you feeling worse.' Reflections stayed at the content level and did not explore the low mood and automaticity driving her drinking.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every exchange tied back to reducing alcohol use, progressing logically from quantifying the pattern to a specific, dated plan with slip planning and social support. No meaningful topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction was largely joint, with the counselor asking before advising and checking fit ('Does that feel right or would you like it tweaked?'). Some tools (slip protocol, text script) were delivered as pre-formed packages rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently honored her choices, accepting her two-drinks goal over clinical guidelines and letting her discard the notepad and alarm. Detailed step prescriptions (pause-name-rate-jot, scripted texts) occasionally edged toward directing rather than facilitating.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from within via open questions, scaling rationale probes, and prompts for past successes. Claudia generated her own reasons, capabilities, and support person, showing motivation was evoked, not installed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Claudia was repeatedly framed as the expert and her contributions visibly shaped the plan, including adapting tools to her preferences. At times the counselor supplied structured solutions (scripts, step lists) that led the process rather than jointly generating them.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematic evocation of importance, confidence, and commitment through scaling questions that visibly moved Claudia from confidence 5 to 7–8. She produced clear change talk and a concrete, client-generated commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally but frequently amplified by the 'why not a 10' question, which invited elaboration on reasons to keep drinking. No double-sided reflections were used to pair sustain talk with change talk.
