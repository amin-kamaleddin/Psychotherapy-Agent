# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental tone throughout ('Relapse is common and doesn't mean you've failed'). Affirmations remain somewhat generic and unconditional regard isn't fully conveyed during repeated expressions of hopelessness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine understanding ('that sounds really painful and exhausting'), but reflections are sparse and shallow. Key disclosures — Friday-night isolation, self-hatred, deep loneliness ('No one wants to help me') — are never explicitly reflected back.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on stimulant use and ends with a specific actionable plan (telehealth search at 8pm, sending a text). Points lost for drifting into generic tool checklists rather than a progressive exploration toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong collaboration in the final third (co-creating scripts and the concrete plan), but earlier portions are largely unilateral, with the psychiatrist presenting menus of techniques from an expert stance while Amira remains passive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are regularly offered and preferences (text over calls, telehealth, no promises) are respected without pressure. Advice-giving is still fairly prescriptive, and statements like 'Yes — I do think you can get something out of outpatient care' gently steer her.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some internal motivation is drawn out, and the concrete plan is largely Amira's own articulation. However, key evocative opportunities are missed — why she wants change, what success would look like — and the session mostly supplies external resources.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choices and preference adjustments foster some power sharing, but large blocks of expert advice are delivered with minimal exploration of Amira's own ideas, so her contributions shape logistics more than substance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk is captured and concretized ('that's a meaningful step,' setting a specific time tonight), but key statements like 'I shouldn't have let it get this far' pass unreflected. Evocation is real but inconsistent.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Hopelessness is not argued against or reinforced, which is good, but there is little active reframing. No double-sided reflections linking her regret with her despair; sustain talk is absorbed into practical planning rather than skillfully softened.
