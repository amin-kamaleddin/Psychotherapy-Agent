# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicates warmth and unconditional positive regard—normalizing reluctance, affirming Anya's thoughtfulness, and praising her existing safety behaviors—without any judgmental language, even when she minimizes risk.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening captured ambivalence well (e.g., school referral plus willingness to talk), but reflections were occasionally brief and missed deeper emotional nuance, such as the underlying fear of seeming 'boring' to peers.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on alcohol-related safety with logical progression toward concrete commitments. Slight drift into party anecdotes and a focus on harm reduction rather than the target drinking behavior itself reduced the score.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed, with Anya's edits incorporated (adding 'I'll owe you one,' rejecting the PSA-style line). Pre-packaged option lists and scripts gave parts of the brainstorming a slightly counselor-initiated feel.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was consistently and explicitly supported ('Answer what feels comfortable,' option menus), and Anya's decisions were honored without pushback. Her ownership of the plan is evident in her self-generated commitments.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did elicit some of Anya's own ideas, but much content was counselor-supplied (safety steps, scripts, refusal lines). Motivation around alcohol itself—values, future goals, identity—was never evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Anya was positioned as the expert and her input directly shaped the plan (sister as backup, own refusal phrasing). More explicit acknowledgment of her expertise and less reliance on pre-formatted menus would strengthen this.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some preparatory change talk was evoked ('That's pretty doable,' 'I'll probably ask her tonight'), but it was not deepened or reinforced with confidence/importance questions. Change talk centered on safety planning rather than motivation about alcohol itself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'm totally fine with it,' 'everyone does it,' 'my mom drinks wine') was not reinforced, but opportunities for double-sided reflections to develop discrepancy were missed. Asking what she enjoyed about party nights amplified status quo reasons without balancing exploration.
