# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently conveyed unconditional positive regard with warm, nonjudgmental responses throughout—even when the patient explicitly declined to discuss smoking.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflections captured the patient's frustration and priorities, but they stayed cognitive and practical; deeper emotions (embarrassment, past medication failure, provider fatigue) were never mirrored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Narrowing to dental concerns built strong alliance with a precontemplative client, but the smoking target was almost entirely abandoned—even when the patient opened the door—and much of the session was dental logistics.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Agendas were genuinely negotiated and the counselor adapted to the patient's pushback. Falls short of excellent because information delivery came as expert-driven lists rather than fully co-constructed exchanges.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: the patient was framed as the decision-maker, deferrals were accepted gracefully, and no pressure was applied at any point. The patient explicitly rewarded this approach with trust.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session leaned heavily on providing information rather than eliciting the patient's own motivations; the one good evocative question was never followed up, and the motivational shift was self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The patient's expertise was centered and his choices visibly shaped the session and pacing. Slightly limited by dense informational blocks offered without checking fit.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged organically ('that's a bit mad, isn't it?', 'stuck with me a bit') but the counselor never deepened it with evocative questions and passively accepted the deferral instead.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully avoided lecturing and used the re-staining timeline as a clever indirect strategy letting the patient draw his own conclusions. However, sustain talk was left unaddressed rather than actively softened with double-sided reflections.
