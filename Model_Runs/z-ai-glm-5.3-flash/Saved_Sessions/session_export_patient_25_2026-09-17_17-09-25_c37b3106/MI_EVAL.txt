# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with no shaming even when the patient discloses drinking beyond his intentions. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic, with the 'quit-smoking muscle' framing recycled repeatedly.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening, including an accurate complex reflection capturing Daniel's ambivalence about control. Reflections could go deeper into emotional content, such as the shame implied by 'my wife noticed' and the fear of being the 'stick-in-the-mud'.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on the target behavior from opening to close, with each turn advancing concrete behavioral planning. The session progresses from exploration to a specific, time-bound action plan with a scheduled Tuesday check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan is genuinely co-constructed from Daniel's own ideas (delay trick, Mike as support, refusal line) with an egalitarian, playful tone. Several unsolicited tip-lists still shape the agenda before the patient's own solutions are fully explored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices are consistently framed as Daniel's, with advice offered as options and the patient selecting strategies and follow-up timing. Some advice-heavy lists prescribe solutions before Daniel generates his own.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently draws motivation from within the client rather than lecturing, eliciting his own reasons ('hungover Dad is no fun') and self-efficacy. The counselor affirms and builds on this self-generated motivation, moving Daniel from vague ambivalence to a confident, self-owned plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Daniel's contributions form the backbone of the plan, and his preferences are explicitly invited at decision points. Dense advice lists lean slightly expert-driven, and the patient's expert status is more implied than stated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent use of open questions, confidence scaling, and commitment-oriented questions that consistently generate the patient's own motivation and commitment language. Skillfully builds desire, ability, reasons, and commitment throughout.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoids dwelling on sustain talk, using a double-sided reflection to reframe minimization toward change and offering practical solutions for social pressure. A few tip-focused responses momentarily shift attention to problem details rather than redirecting ambivalence.
