# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently conveyed warmth and unconditional positive regard, explicitly normalizing slips ('info, not failure') and affirming Margaret's choice to keep pub nights without judgment. No labeling or criticism appeared at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was strong and accurate, capturing both sides of Margaret's ambivalence and linking her values to behavior. Reflections occasionally lacked emotional depth — her fear of ending up in a home or being found at the bottom of the stairs was not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus was maintained on reducing alcohol to protect independence and prevent falls throughout. Every exchange advanced toward a concrete plan with obstacles anticipated and safety-netting arranged.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session was largely co-constructed, with Margaret's ideas shaping the plan (high shelf, cordial, note, Thursday appointment). Occasional counselor-suggested strategies slightly limited full equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently offered choices, asked permission, and reinforced Margaret's ownership of the plan. Her refusal to quit entirely and desire to keep pub nights were fully respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from Margaret rather than lectured, using rulers, open questions, and value-linking. She generated her own reasons, plan components, and coping strategies, culminating in her articulating the discrepancy herself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Margaret was consistently positioned as the expert, choosing strategies and shaping the plan. A few tips were offered as menus rather than elicited from her, keeping it just below full partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation throughout — open questions, readiness and confidence rulers, and implementation prompts elicited strong, specific change talk. Margaret's own reasons (independence, avoiding falls) were repeatedly affirmed and deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally handled well by acknowledging it while pivoting to values, and pub nights were supported rather than challenged. Some sustain talk ('I don't need to quit') was occasionally left unaddressed rather than actively reframed.
