# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and screens for postpartum safety in a caring way. However, reassurance is formulaic and delivered prematurely, with few individualized affirmations of Nadine's strengths as a mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; the counselor jumps from brief acknowledgment into advice lists. The client's deeper feelings — guilt about asking for help, fear of being a 'bad mom,' sensitivity to her mother's comparisons — are rarely reflected back or explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays well-focused on infant sleep, maternal exhaustion, and support-seeking, progressing logically to a concrete three-step plan and role-play. Mild drift into the mother's critical comments remains acceptably tied to coping.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The overall stance is expert-driven, with rapid-fire recommendations, drafted messages, and scripted replies. Nadine's contributions are incorporated only lukewarmly, with the role-play offer being the exception rather than the pattern.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offers some genuine choice about how to proceed (brainstorm, one-liners, role-play). However, directive advice-giving dominates and there is little explicit reinforcement of Nadine's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** There is virtually no effort to elicit motivation from within the client; the counselor supplies reasons, solutions, and scripts. Nadine generates the entire plan herself, but the counselor fails to explore what this success would mean or to draw out confidence and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor largely assumes the expert role, delivering prescriptive lists and pre-packaged scripts rather than inviting the client's expertise. Nadine steers the session effectively, but this happens despite — not because of — the counselor's power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Most change talk originates from Nadine herself rather than from counselor evocation, and is never deliberately elicited, reflected, or deepened. Affirmations of her readiness are generic rather than tied to her own stated motivations.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk appears repeatedly ('I don't know how much longer I can do this,' 'I hate asking for help,' 'backing down is my whole problem') but is never strategically reflected, reframed, or softened. The counselor instead pivots immediately to problem-solving scripts, missing chances to help Nadine resolve her ambivalence.
