# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful and nonjudgmental throughout, but affirmations remain generic rather than reflecting Isolde's specific strengths (e.g., her weekly values reviews, phone-away-at-meals effort).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Understanding is implied but rarely demonstrated—virtually no complex reflections, and emotionally meaningful content like her guilt about emails went unreflected.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus from value identification to a concrete behavior change with a scheduled 7-day follow-up; every exchange advances the target behavior.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client made key decisions, but the counselor supplied most structure and solutions; Isolde mostly selected from counselor-generated options rather than co-creating them.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine choices offered and Isolde's preference to pick her own replacement activity was respected; however, directive instruction phrasing undercuts the invitational spirit.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged largely from the exercise structure rather than skillful MI; few open questions explored why the value matters or amplified her motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Client input shaped the plan, but the counselor's prescriptive, expert-driven style (instruction lists, pre-written scripts) dominated the session's pace and direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Well-designed exercises elicited strong commitment language ('I'll start Monday,' 'puts that into practice'), but the counselor missed chances to reflect and amplify it (e.g., 'It fits everything I believe in').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Minimal sustain talk given the maintenance-stage client; her worry about urgent emails was handled constructively via the emergency channel, though the emotional guilt behind it went unaddressed.
