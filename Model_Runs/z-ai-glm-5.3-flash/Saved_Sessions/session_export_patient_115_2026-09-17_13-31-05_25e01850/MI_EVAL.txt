# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations like 'You're doing a lot to manage this.' However, affirmations are somewhat formulaic and task-focused, missing chances to affirm Samir's emotional courage (e.g., confronting the ER experience and family fears).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens empathically but most responses pivot quickly to checklists and advice. Emotionally significant disclosures—his daughter crying in the ER, the cigarette being 'the one thing that's mine'—receive minimal reflective acknowledgment, with reflection staying mostly cognitive rather than emotional.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps consistent focus on asthma management and related behaviors (smoking, drinking, adherence, appointment prep) with concrete next steps. Direction is slightly medical-preparation-heavy rather than centered on resolving ambivalence about quitting itself.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invites Samir's agreement before activities, but the flow is largely counselor-generated content followed by client assent. Samir's own expertise (taxi rank triggers, hand-to-mouth habit, work stress) is acknowledged but not substantially integrated into the plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently frames change as Samir's choice, offering options as menus rather than directives. His own conclusions ('one thing at a time,' 'not today, but soon') show autonomy was preserved, though explicit reinforcement of his ownership would elevate this to a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relies heavily on providing information, scripts, and advice rather than drawing out Samir's own motivations. Powerful motivation (his daughter's tears, his fear, 'quitting would help my asthma') surfaced spontaneously but was neither explored nor reflected back to expand it.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly asks for input, supporting a collaborative stance, but the overall posture remains expert-driven with question lists, scripts, and plans supplied wholesale. Samir mostly receives and rehearses rather than co-creates.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Conditions for change talk were created and Samir produced meaningful statements ('I'm ready to try quitting properly'), but the counselor did not actively deepen them with open questions like 'What makes this feel possible now?' Change talk emerged largely from the client's own momentum rather than deliberate evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor did not amplify Samir's sustain talk ('I can't just give up everything,' 'cutting down should be enough') but neither actively softened it with double-sided reflections or strategic pivots. Ambivalence was left largely unresolved, resolved only by Samir himself.
