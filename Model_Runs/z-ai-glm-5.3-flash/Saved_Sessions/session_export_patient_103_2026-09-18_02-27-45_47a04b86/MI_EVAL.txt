# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained consistent respect and warmth despite the patient's hostility, validating his frustration and affirming his efforts. Fell short of 5 as acceptance occasionally served steering, and affirmations were somewhat formulaic at the close.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was excellent, with deep, accurate reflections capturing vulnerable emotions (e.g., anger masking powerlessness) and somatic experience. Reflections consistently advanced the conversation rather than merely restating.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session progressed clearly from exploring feelings beneath anger to a concrete plan and rehearsal, with no drift. Every question advanced toward the target behavior of anger in medical settings.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Operated as a genuine partnership of equals, co-constructing plans and letting the patient's answers shape the session. His shift from 'waste of time' to 'where do we start?' evidences the collaborative stance working.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and ownership were actively fostered, with options offered rather than prescribed and no pressure toward specific strategies. All plan elements originated from the patient himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was consistently drawn from the patient's own experiences and values, even deflecting his direct advice-seeking back to his own experience. Every plan element and hopeful statement was self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was repeatedly and explicitly located with the client, with session direction shaped almost entirely by his contributions. Power sharing at the highest level of the scale.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and deepened, from the exception question eliciting his own plan to explicit crediting ('That's your idea, not mine'). His closing rehearsal of the plan is strong evidence of cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was neither argued with nor amplified, instead acknowledged and paired double-sidedly with change talk. Could have pivoted more quickly at moments, and one reflection restated sustain talk.
