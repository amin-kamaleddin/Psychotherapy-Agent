# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent nonjudgmental warmth with specific, individualized affirmations, including unconditional regard for the patient's resistant honesty. The near-constant density of affirmations risks feeling formulaic, and the patient's guardedness was never normalized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate double-sided reflections demonstrate strong perspective-taking, but reflections stay cognitive and strategic rather than emotionally deep. The vulnerable disclosure that the wife 'weighs on me more than I let on' went unexplored emotionally.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Focus never drifts from the cigars and throat, progressing logically through exploration to plan. However, the target behavior was deferred via the mask test, and the core misconception about mini-cigar harm was never addressed despite natural openings.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative throughout: the plan was co-constructed entirely from the patient's own ideas, terms were negotiated, and there was virtually no unsolicited advice. The only critique is that the counselor's medical expertise was never offered even when invited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook autonomy support with explicit, repeated statements locating choice with the patient ('your words, your pace, your call'). Zero pressure or directive language, and boundary tests from the patient were honored and built upon rather than deflected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew out the patient's own reasons and leveraged his past energy-drink success as a template for change. However, motivation was never deepened into commitment language, and the emotional driver (the wife) plus the discrepancy between the patient's self-image and minimization were left unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary power-sharing: the entire action plan originated from the patient, and the psychiatrist explicitly recognized his ownership ('you just laid out your own experiment'). Consistently located expertise within the client and framed the work as joint ('compare notes,' 'figuring this out together').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent, well-crafted evocative questions successfully drew preparatory change talk across the session. The talk never deepened into commitment language (no scaling or consolidation tools), and weak final change talk was over-translated as 'already ahead of the plan.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally acknowledged and elegantly reframed into self-efficacy, with double-sided reflections ending on the change side. One significant lapse: the closing reflection ('if that scratchy throat clears, the cigars aren't hurting you') echoed and slightly endorsed the patient's minimization.
