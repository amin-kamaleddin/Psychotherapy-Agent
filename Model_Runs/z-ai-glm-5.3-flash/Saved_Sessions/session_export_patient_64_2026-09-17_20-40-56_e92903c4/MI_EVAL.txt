# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard: affirming Jordan's honesty, normalizing his deflection, and pre-emptively normalizing setbacks. No labeling, shaming, or pressure at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections like 'being young and healthy doesn't stop you from carrying it to them.' Occasionally more cognitive than emotional — the rich feeling moment 'I'd feel terrible if I got sick and passed it to them' was left under-processed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on flu vaccination throughout, moving from questions to ambivalence to motivation to a concrete plan with no topic drift. Structured without being pushy.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine equal exchange: agenda prioritization sought from Jordan, and the final plan (Thursday walk-in, texting mom) was co-constructed from his own suggestions. Information offered as a resource, not a lecture.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Explicit autonomy reinforcement ('you're the expert on your life,' 'you get to decide what pace feels right') with decisions repeatedly returned to Jordan. No pressure or control language anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary evocation: strategic questions like 'where would a flu land hardest if you brought it home?' let Jordan generate the family-protection motivation himself. Every motivation and plan element originated with the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong power sharing — 'you're the expert on your life' and Jordan's own contributions (Sunday dinners, grandpa's heart, pharmacy preference) drove the session. Client confirmed the non-hierarchical experience.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Textbook change talk cultivation: Jordan shifted from 'I don't really need it' to 'I'd want to get the shot, honestly' with a concrete plan. Strategic questions and reflective reinforcement of change talk led to genuine commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk never argued with or amplified; effective double-sided reflection and redirection toward family protection. Ingredient concerns were acknowledged but not deeply engaged or strategically reframed.
