# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with specific, genuine affirmations ('that's real self-knowledge', 'that took courage') and explicit acceptance of imperfection. No judgment or labeling anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally attuned, often articulating meaning beyond the client's words (the beer 'holding a seat' for the tinkerer, habit 'wired into every shift'). Skillfully captured ambivalence from the opening reflection onward.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear progression from target selection to concrete steps and support planning, with focus maintained when Erik offered to defer the food topic. Held back from 5 because emotional material (father, grandchildren) wasn't explicitly tied back to the action plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue between equals — every plan element (garage, bin, chips, telling his wife) came from Erik and the counselor never prescribed. The closing 'Deal?' was a mutual agreement with terms defined by Erik.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support: 'which one, if any', 'you're the expert on your pace', 'only you can say if that's doable'. Erik explicitly recognized the difference from being told what to do ('choosing it myself, that's different').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation drawn entirely from Erik via open questions that elicited increasingly deep reasons — from practical steps to identity (his father's workshop) and values (grandchildren). The session's arc from defensiveness to self-authored commitment demonstrates masterful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Erik positioned as the expert throughout ('you know your evenings better than I ever will'), and his ideas shaped the entire plan. The counselor's role was purely facilitative, ending with Erik setting his own meeting terms.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and amplified change talk ('maybe try', 'weeknights I could do this', 'choosing it myself', 'I'd like to be around for that'), strategically reflecting it back and escalating it. The final evocation about Erik's father produced the deepest motivational material of the session.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was generally met with acceptance and reframing rather than reinforcement ('planning for real life, not a perfect one'). A point held back because doubts (Saturday-night drinking, fear the chips would win) were sometimes accepted or pivoted from without deliberate double-sided reflections exploring the ambivalence.
