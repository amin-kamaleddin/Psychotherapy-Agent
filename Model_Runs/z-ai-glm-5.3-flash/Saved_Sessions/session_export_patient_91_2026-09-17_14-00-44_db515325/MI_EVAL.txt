# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with brief praise, but warmth is sparse and embedded in task instructions. Marcus's self-criticism and anxiety are never normalized or affirmed at a deeper level.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens with an accurate reflection of feeling overwhelmed, but the session quickly becomes procedural. Key emotional statements—fear of the lancet, self-blame, and worry about quitting—receive no reflective response.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps consistent, productive focus on blood glucose monitoring from concern identification through live practice and a testing plan. The direction is counselor-imposed and task-focused, bypassing emotionally relevant content tied to adherence.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Includes permission-seeking and menu-based choices, but the counselor designs the goal, schedule, and questions with minimal elicitation of Marcus's own ideas. His contributions surface only late and are not woven into the plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some genuine choices and asks permission before walk-throughs, but the overall structure is prescriptive with pre-packaged SMART goals and scripts. Little explicit reinforcement of Marcus's ownership or capacity to self-direct.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Virtually no questions designed to elicit Marcus's reasons, values, confidence, or hoped-for outcomes. Motivation is supplied externally through praise and strategies rather than drawn from the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session shifts quickly into a directive, expert-driven format with Marcus primarily receiving and complying. His own ideas (wife's support, going blank at appointments) appear only at the end and never shape the plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus volunteers meaningful change talk ('I want to try it', 'maybe knowing the number is better'), but the counselor makes almost no deliberate effort to evoke or deepen it. No open questions about his reasons or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not argue with or amplify Marcus's ambivalence, which is a strength, but sustain talk is largely ignored rather than strategically softened. No double-sided reflections or reframing to pivot ambivalence toward change.
