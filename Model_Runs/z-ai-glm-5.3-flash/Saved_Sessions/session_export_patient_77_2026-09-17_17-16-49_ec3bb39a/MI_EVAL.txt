# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental tone with validation of Liyana's feelings and efforts. Would reach 5 with more explicit affirmations of her strengths, such as her two months of consistent exercise and courage in texting a friend.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections of frustration and loneliness that helped Liyana keep opening up. Reflections are at times formulaic ('I hear you') and the counselor pivots to problem-solving too quickly, especially around her fear of getting her hopes up.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed on target and ended with a concrete, feasible plan (water bottle, casual text, Thursday check-in). Minor drift into family/loneliness topics, and a consolidating summary would have strengthened direction.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Worked as an active partner—co-editing the text, negotiating check-in logistics, and asking what she wants. Collaboration was somewhat menu-driven, with pre-formulated choices limiting open co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced Liyana's choice through menus, honoring her rejection of the food log and her confidentiality request, with no pressure or directives. Liyana set the pace, contact method, and tone of the text.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some motivation elicited (wanting to try, choosing a trusted friend), but the counselor frequently supplied ideas, wording, and structure rather than drawing them from the client. More open questions about her reasons and confidence would locate motivation within her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently offered choices and asked for Liyana's input, honoring her preferences. The counselor tended to generate most ideas rather than eliciting her own solutions first, so power-sharing was good but not fully client-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some commitments (water bottle, texting) and Liyana expressed wanting to try. However, the counselor missed chances to deepen her own change talk—e.g., responding with logistics when she said 'I want to actually try this. For once it feels like a plan.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged hopelessness ('What's the point?') with neutral reflections and normalized the plateau. Limited strategic pivoting back toward change—counselor often re-offered the same options instead of reframing sustain talk toward her agency and values.
