# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard and warmth throughout, even when Nabil was snappy and hostile. Explicitly normalized uncertainty and affirmed his efforts rather than addressing rudeness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Demonstrated accurate reflective listening that captured both emotional content and ambivalence (e.g., 'being disregarded feels infuriating, yet you're seeing the cost'). Some reflections were formulaic ('I hear you') and deeper emotional exploration (shame, fear of being labeled 'difficult') was limited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained exceptionally clear focus on managing anger in response to appointment delays, progressively building a concrete plan (trigger identification, script, 20-minute cutoff, breaths, exit plan, wife message). No aimless drift despite the client's initially resistant, venting presentation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions were largely co-constructed, with Nabil's ideas substantially shaping the final plan (timer cue, script personalization). The counselor's frequent use of pre-written scripts and numbered plans created some unevenness in power-sharing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly affirmed choice ('Which sounds most helpful right now?', 'If you're open') and normalized uncertainty, backing off when Nabil dismissed breathing as 'soft.' Autonomy support was somewhat constrained by a menu-driven format with pre-packaged options presented before buy-in.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within via confidence rulers (6 → 6.5 → 7) and tapped the client's own motivations (protecting his job and relationship with his wife). Deeper values and meaning of change were rarely explored, with early motivation exploration thin.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently treated Nabil's contributions as shaping the session—his choices, tweaks, and expertise were actively incorporated. At times the counselor drove the agenda with structured menus (A/B/C options, numbered plans), limiting client influence.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked change talk through repeated confidence scaling (escalating from 'Six, maybe' to 'Seven, honestly') and commitment questions ('Which will you try first?'). Evocation relied more on options and questions than deeper exploration of the client's own reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was substantial ('I don't think I need to change—I need people to respect my time'), and the counselor neither reinforced nor actively softened much of it. Beyond one good early double-sided reflection, blame externalization ('the delays are the problem') was left unaddressed or pivoted to menus rather than strategically reframed.
