# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, supportive tone with affirmations of progress and non-judgmental safety screening. However, affirmations are brief and generic rather than reflecting specific understanding of Erik's effort and history of failure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal; Erik's core emotional pain about comparison and self-criticism is met with a quick label followed by tactics lists. Discouragement and fear of quitting receive no deep reflection or emotional validation.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps clear focus on gym consistency, comparison/discouragement, and diet, with efficient transitions and a concrete recap. Brisk pacing means emotional obstacles receive only tactical gloss-overs rather than being worked through.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session resembles brief advice-giving with bullet-pointed plans and finished scripts rather than co-constructed planning. Erik's meaningful contributions are not integrated, making power-sharing superficial.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik is offered some choice between options and receives supportive closing language, but the stance is largely prescriptive with counselor-designed menus, scripts, and texts to send verbatim. His own strategies (treadmill math, back-pain reframe) are not reinforced as valid alternatives.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Nearly all strategies and reframes are counselor-supplied, with virtually no open questions exploring Erik's own reasons, confidence, or values. His self-generated motivation (the sundae/treadmill math) is a missed opportunity for evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor positions itself as the plan-maker, handing over pre-written goals, scripts, and texts. Erik's contributions do not substantively shape the plan, and his back-pain reframe suggestion is ignored in the final scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Erik generates rich spontaneous change talk (back pain improvement, treadmill math, 'progress, not perfection'), but the counselor does almost nothing to deepen or affirm it. Key insights like 'these young guys aren't fixing a bad back. I am.' go unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reframes address Erik's 'why bother' voice, but they are counselor-supplied rather than elicited. Repeated dwelling on slip/failure scenarios and absence of double-sided reflections keep focus on relapse logistics.
