# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with affirmations like 'Totally fair' and 'Good question,' and resistance is accepted without arguing. However, affirmations are brief and formulaic, and repeated rapid-fire advice lists slightly undercut unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections are present, but the session's most emotionally significant moment — Adrian's admission that being high contributed to his injury — receives no reflection, with the counselor jumping to a checklist. Shame about hiding use from friends is also never validated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stays loosely connected to substance use and harm reduction but moves through topic persistence rather than purposeful MI movement. Much of the time is spent delivering safety information, and the session ends without a summary connecting back to Adrian's health scare.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor checks in and accepts Adrian's edits to the plan, but much of the session is counselor-led advice delivery, including seven intake questions at once that Adrian experienced as 'a lot at once.' True joint problem-solving appears only late in the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor's strongest area — explicit permission-giving ('you don't have to set a goal,' 'You don't have to tell friends') and accepting refusals without pressure. The main limitation is that options are presented as pre-packaged menus rather than elicited from Adrian.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is rarely drawn from the client; instead the counselor supplies reasons and solutions. Adrian's motivational material (contamination fears, injury connection, skating, boredom) surfaces on his own initiative, with no exploration of his own reasons, values, or confidence for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invites input and defers to Adrian's decisions, but large unrequested advice blocks position the counselor as expert rather than drawing on Adrian's expertise. Adrian's best ideas (Dave, skating, gym) come from him, not counselor curiosity.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Open questions invite meaningful change talk ('that's crossed my mind,' 'being smarter about what's actually in it makes sense'), but the counselor never deepens or reinforces it. Missed evocation opportunities include Adrian's own motivation and his 'I didn't expect to agree to anything' remark.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly engages with sustain talk, meeting minimizations and the 'overdose talk feels dramatic' pushback with more instructions rather than reframing toward Adrian's own concerns. On the positive side, there is no arguing and points are conceded gracefully.
