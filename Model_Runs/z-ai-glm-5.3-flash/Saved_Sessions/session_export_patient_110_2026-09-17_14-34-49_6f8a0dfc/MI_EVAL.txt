# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations, though these were somewhat generic and minimization was not validated as a legitimate feeling.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections of Richard's values were brief and quickly followed by advice or question lists; deeper emotional content like fear of his father's heart trouble was never explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on cholesterol management with concrete goals and a specific end plan, though focus occasionally drifted into checklist and medical-info delivery.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were genuine collaborative moments like co-deciding start days, but much information was delivered in expert-to-patient blocks without checking what Richard already knew or wanted.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor offered meaningful choices and respected Richard's control, though some prescriptive meal swaps slightly reduced his ownership of the plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Motivation was acknowledged but relied on practical questions rather than open evocative questions that would actively elicit and develop Richard's own reasons for change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Richard's choices shaped the plan, but the counselor frequently assumed the expert role with detailed pros/cons and checklists, making power-sharing only partial.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor effectively elicited and reinforced Richard's change talk ('Monday we start walking', 'I'll get that prescription sorted'), though deepening was sometimes replaced by practical advice.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged as normal ambivalence, but double-sided reflections were not used and the session moved quickly to action planning, leaving ambivalence largely unexplored.
