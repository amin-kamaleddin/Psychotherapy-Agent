# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard despite Richard's defensiveness, powerfully affirming his dignity with 'That's yours to decide, always.' No judgment or disapproval appeared at any point.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captured Richard's ambivalence, including the grief and protection themes beneath his smoking. However, repeating key questions nearly verbatim (which Richard noticed) slightly diluted the sense of attunement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on tobacco cessation with no topic drift, systematically moving Richard from external pressure toward choice language and first steps. Repeated prompts and a closing question that circled back to earlier material rather than consolidating a plan held this below top marks.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuinely two-sided interaction with Richard's contributions driving the session, and the counselor adopted his language ('reading pack,' 'one thing,' 'choosing vs. giving up'). Occasionally worked 'on' Richard with elegant but counselor-authored interpretations rather than joint exploration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support — explicitly framed change as Richard's choice ('That's yours to decide, always') and even validated his resistance to external pressure. No coercive or directive language appeared, and Richard's response ('If it's gonna happen, it's gotta be mine') shows this landed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Masterfully elicited motivation from within — every significant reason to change (protecting his wife, grandkids, winded stairs, self-respect) came from Richard's own mouth in response to open questions. Never argued for quitting or supplied education.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Richard as the expert ('You're the only one who knows how to hold both of those') and never prescribed solutions — all ideas (patches, car first, morning walks) were client-generated. Explicitly disclaimed taking control throughout.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strategic questions evoked rich, client-driven change language, moving Richard from 'maybe I should at least think about it' to 'it's gotta be mine' and 'maybe I look at those patches.' Change talk was reflected and amplified effectively across a clear arc from pre-contemplation to tentative action.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Rolled with resistance effectively, responding to spiteful smoking and 'my one thing' with neutral, empathic reflections rather than challenge. At times sustain talk was re-reflected, holding ambivalence in the room slightly longer than necessary.
