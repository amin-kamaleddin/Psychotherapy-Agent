# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Jordan's reluctance and honoring his privacy request. Deeper, more individualized affirmations of his character would elevate the score.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflections capturing Jordan's ambivalence, though often summary-style rather than emotionally deep. Missed opportunities to reflect underlying family loyalty and guilt about 'dragging something home.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, unwavering focus on flu vaccination throughout, with each response advancing toward a decision. Would benefit from more explicit link-backs to Jordan's stated values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Menu-based options and client steering were strong, but long expert monologues and pre-made lists reduced co-creation. Coping strategies ideally would have been brainstormed together.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with choice-based menu options, no pressure to decide, and respect for privacy. Could be strengthened by explicitly affirming Jordan's ownership of the decision and his capacity to follow through.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jordan's own motivation (protecting grandparents) was elicited, but much of the session relied on information provision rather than drawing out his reasons and values. The counselor could have deliberately amplified the family-protection theme and built confidence language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Power-sharing questions were present, but the middle of the session shifted into expert mode with extensive facts and advice lists. The counselor ended by drafting scripts for Jordan rather than letting him author his own plan.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew out change talk, culminating in a concrete commitment ('I'm gonna call the doctor's office this week'). The evocation of which pros/cons mattered most was skilled, though confidence language was not explicitly evoked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without reinforcement, but often restated in summaries rather than strategically pivoted toward change. No double-sided reflections foregrounding change elements were used.
