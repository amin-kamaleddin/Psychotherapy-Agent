# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental despite Amina's hostility ('I respect that boundary,' honoring her documentation request). Deducted for somewhat formulaic warmth and missed opportunities to affirm her strengths explicitly (e.g., her courage in sharing about her mother).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offered several accurate reflections (feeling 'unfair and like everyone blames you,' 'blamed for leaving a fight') that contributed to Amina's notable opening up. Reflections sometimes repeated similar themes without deepening into her emotional core, and the pivot to safety questions felt abrupt.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a loose focus on home conflict, and Amina progressed from one-word answers to meaningful disclosure. However, constant option menus fragmented focus and stalled momentum, and the counselor never consolidated gains with a summary or concrete next steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaved as an equal partner—negotiating documentation, asking permission, and honoring Amina's input (rejecting 'coping talk,' choosing the music). Collaboration stayed at the level of offering choices rather than true joint problem-solving, delivered in a somewhat scripted way.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary throughout: transparent confidentiality limits, honoring the request to only document that they met, consistent 'your call' language, and adapting to her request to pick the music for her. No pressure or persuasion, a high-level demonstration with an autonomy-sensitive adolescent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited meaningful material (her wish that her parents listen, thoughts of leaving at 18) through open questions, real progress with a nearly non-communicative client. Evocation was inconsistent—follow-ups rarely deepened her expressed desires, and 'I don't know why I'm telling you this' went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Amina as the expert ('I care because you're the expert on your life') and let her direct the session. Her pushback ('Just pick for me for once') suggests the choice-offering became rigid, and the counselor only partially adapted to her request for more initiative.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully evoked meaningful client language ('I just want them to actually listen,' leaving at 18) via the question about what she wishes her parents knew. Missed chances to strategically deepen this change talk—exploring what being listened to would look like or what independence at 18 would mean.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Avoided arguing or dwelling on sustain talk ('You're all the same anyway') and immediately pivoted when Amina rejected the coping-skill framework. Sometimes restated sustain talk verbatim ('you expect them to make a big deal'), mildly reinforcing the negative framing without pivoting toward change.
