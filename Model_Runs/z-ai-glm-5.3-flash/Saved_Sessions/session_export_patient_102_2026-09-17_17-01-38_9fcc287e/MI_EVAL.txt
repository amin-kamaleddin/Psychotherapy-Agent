# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Milan's perspective and honesty without moralizing. Falls short of 5 due to somewhat transactional warmth and few affirmations of Milan's strengths or values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers accurate reflections of Milan's ambivalence, resistance, and skepticism. Reflections occasionally merge into advice-giving and emotional undercurrents like fear of parental reaction are only lightly explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear, consistent focus on alcohol use with a logical progression from exploration to a fully specified harm-reduction plan. Each turn steers back to the target behavior despite deflections.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative elements exist (permission-seeking, Milan selecting plan details), but the pattern is largely counselor-generated menus followed by client selection. Milan's own ideas rarely originate strategies, and question stacking compresses dialogue.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly frames participation as Milan's choice, honors his one-off request, and accepts his refusal to tell a friend. Falls short of 5 due to subtle steering pressure in safety planning and rapid-fire option lists.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Opening questions show intent to elicit motivation, but evocation is superficial and quickly abandoned for counselor-provided solutions. External motivation ('proving adults wrong') is never explored or transformed into values-based change talk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission and solicits Milan's input, which shapes the plan ($20, Sunday 3pm, Cole). However, the counselor generates nearly all solution options, so ideas feel counselor-driven rather than co-discovered.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Attempts to evoke change talk with likes/worries and values questions, and consolidates Milan's commitment-adjacent language into concrete planning. Misses opportunities to deepen Milan's own reasons for change or what the experiment might mean to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Generally avoids reinforcing sustain talk and uses double-sided reflections, but sometimes restates the sustain position without pivoting toward change. The 'group bottle' rationalization is acknowledged but only partially explored as a risk.
