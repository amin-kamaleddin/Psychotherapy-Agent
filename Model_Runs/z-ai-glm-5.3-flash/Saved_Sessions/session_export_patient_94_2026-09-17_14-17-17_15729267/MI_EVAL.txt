# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains basic respect and avoids judgment or labeling, but warmth and affirmation are largely absent. Maria's honesty and self-research efforts go unacknowledged, making the tone transactional rather than supportive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal, with brief acknowledgments quickly pivoting to advice-giving. Significant emotional disclosures, such as feeling ganged up on by her husband and doctor, are ignored in favor of instructions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays firmly anchored to inhaler adherence and asthma management with concrete steps established (tracking, spirometry, portal message, spacer/rinsing, safety plan). However, direction is achieved through checklist-driven advice rather than collaborative goal clarification tied to Maria's values.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is predominantly expert-driven, with the counselor supplying drafts, checklists, and plans without first eliciting Maria's ideas. There is no shared agenda-setting or negotiation, and Maria's knowledge is treated as secondary.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine autonomy acknowledgments exist (option menus, honoring her refusal of the husband text, accepting her two-week limit). However, the overall style is prescriptive with rapid-fire instructions, and autonomy is accommodated reactively rather than proactively fostered.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation is supplied via information rather than elicited—there are no open questions about her reasons for managing asthma, no confidence rulers, and no exploration of her hopes. Change-relevant statements she volunteers are never developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role with risk lectures, drafted messages, and a six-point checklist. Maria's lived experience and preferences are acknowledged only superficially, and session direction is consistently set by the counselor's pre-formed plans.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Commitments emerged largely from counselor checklists rather than being evoked from Maria. When she volunteered change talk ('something I could actually send'), the counselor failed to reflect, affirm, or deepen it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Abundant sustain talk about bone loss and daily medication is met with neutral acceptance followed by advice, with no double-sided reflections or reframing. The sustained emphasis on medication instructions arguably kept attention on the contested behavior rather than resolving ambivalence.
