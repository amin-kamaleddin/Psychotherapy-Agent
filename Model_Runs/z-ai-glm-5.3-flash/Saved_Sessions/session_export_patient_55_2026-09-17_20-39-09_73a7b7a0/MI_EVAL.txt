# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard, including affirming her four-year commitment, validating her honesty, and normalizing her protective minimization without any judgment. No criticism or disapproval even when the client downplays stress.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening that progressively deepens, from reflecting her protective 'pushing through' style to capturing her core insight about living by an untested feeling. Reflections consistently advanced the client's self-understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Clear focus on stress, self-care, and the guilt-driven overwork pattern, moving steadily toward a concrete experiment and success criteria with no aimless wandering. A 5 would require more explicit linking of the focus to the referral context.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor invited the client's input at every turn and built on her answers rather than imposing an agenda. The plan emerged jointly, with the client's ideas clearly steering the direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership was consistently reinforced — accepting her 'no promises' stance, letting her choose the break activity, and framing outcomes as 'yours to learn from.' The counselor never prescribed content.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation was drawn from within the client, with no advice or imposed reasons; open questions elicited her own experiment idea, success markers, and commitment language. The client's closing remark that she expected a lecture but felt 'actually listened' to is direct evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor consistently positioned Isabelle as the expert ('let me ask you as the expert on you') and explicitly credited her Sunday sax plan as 'your idea, and it's a good one.' The client's contributions substantially shaped the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and deepened change talk — from 'maybe actually taking breaks' to 'it's worth a shot during exams' and finally 'I'll give it a real shot.' The counselor amplified, celebrated the shift, and elicited implementation details and success criteria.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was handled well via non-argumentative, double-sided reflections that pivoted toward the client's own concerns. Doubt-based statements like 'I'm not promising anything' could have been more explicitly affirmed and redirected.
