# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental regard with frequent, specific affirmations. Past failures and fears are met with understanding, never disapproval.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening accurately captures ambivalence and normalizes fear of cravings. However, deeper emotional content (shame, feeling of letting her daughter down) is acknowledged but not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustained focus on delaying the first morning cigarette, progressing logically from exploration to a concrete if-then plan, start date, and check-in. Every exchange advances toward change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully co-constructed session with inclusive language and joint rehearsal and planning. The client's ideas directly determine the final plan.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy explicitly reinforced; the client makes every key decision herself. Nicotine replacement is framed as a topic to discuss with her doctor rather than prescribed.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is elicited from the client, whose confidence rises from 5 to 6.5 and who generates her own commitment language. Occasionally the counselor supplies option lists and pre-written scripts instead of evoking all content from her.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client is positioned as the expert, and her contributions substantially shape the plan. Frequent counselor-supplied option menus somewhat pre-structure her choices.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful evocation via prior-success exploration, repeated confidence scaling, and eliciting her own strategies and scripts; change talk grows measurably. Sometimes the counselor supplies content rather than consistently evoking it.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is met with normalization and reframing, pivoting to strengths and co-created recovery phrases. Relies more on reframing than explicit double-sided reflections holding both sides of ambivalence.
