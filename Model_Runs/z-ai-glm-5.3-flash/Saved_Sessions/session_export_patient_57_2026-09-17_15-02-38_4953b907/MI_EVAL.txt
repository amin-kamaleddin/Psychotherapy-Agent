# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with consistent affirmations ('good thinking,' 'real progress') and no criticism of the underage drinking. However, praise is brief and formulaic rather than deeply individualized, with no explicit affirmations of Jamal's courage in facing peer pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurately reflects the core ambivalence between belonging and worry multiple times. Empathy is limited by quick pivots to problem-solving without deeper exploration of fears of social exclusion or loneliness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent, consistent focus on navigating post-game drinking situations, progressing logically from goal clarification to strategies, rehearsal, and planning. Session ends with a concrete, actionable plan for Friday with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative, with the final plan genuinely co-constructed and roleplay highly interactive. Raw material (strategies, scripts) predominantly originates from the counselor, making power-sharing uneven at times.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choice through menus and acknowledges Jamal's stated limits without pressure, with Jamal retaining ownership of the plan. Frequent advice-giving and script-provision somewhat structure the choices within counselor-generated options.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Uses some evocative techniques (open questions, confidence rulers with follow-up), but the approach is skill-building and advice-driven rather than grounded in Jamal's own motivations. Never explores why cutting back matters to him (athletic identity, values), so motivation is partially assumed rather than evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission before advising and lets Jamal choose options and roleplay scenarios, conveying some power-sharing. However, the counselor quickly assumes the expert role, generating most strategies, scripts, and comebacks rather than drawing out Jamal's own ideas or explicitly positioning him as the expert on his team dynamics.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes change talk through open questions and twice-used confidence rulers with 'what would boost that number,' drawing out substantial client statements and concrete plans. Falls short because Jamal's change talk is rarely reflected back to amplify it, and his own reasons for change beyond behavioral confidence are not elicited.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Jamal offers considerable sustain talk ('easier to just play along,' 'I'm not saying I'll never drink with them again'), which is neither reinforced nor strategically softened. No double-sided reflections are used; sustain talk is largely left standing while the counselor moves to solutions, missing opportunities to resolve ambivalence.
