# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard without lecturing—the client's stated trigger. The client's closing comment ('That's more than I usually give doctors') shows she felt genuinely accepted despite initial defensiveness.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate, deep reflections throughout, including reframing smoking as 'something completely yours' and a double-sided reflection capturing ambivalence after her relapse disclosure. Each reflection visibly advanced her self-exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stayed clearly focused on smoking, using tangents (divorce, daughter) as material rather than drifting. It concluded with a specific, measurable next step achieved without pressure.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Questions offered genuine choice ('Or is just noticing enough for today?'), and plans were co-constructed as equals, with the final 'deal' negotiated on the client's terms.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was explicitly reinforced ('I'm not pushing you anywhere with that,' 'let's keep it yours'), including support for small, self-defined steps and the no-lecturing agreement. The client experienced full ownership of her change plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No persuasive arguments about risks were offered; the client's own reasons emerged (daughter's reaction, coughing, cigarettes only 'kind of' working). She generated her own rationale, experiment, and accountability structure.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('You're the expert on your own life,' 'That's your blueprint, not mine'), and the session's key ideas—the walking, the 'test' framing—originated with her. Her comment about 'homework I gave myself' confirms effective power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoked change talk that evolved from vague ('maybe not feeling like I have to defend it') to specific commitment ('Wednesday lunch, first walk,' 'like a test'). The psychiatrist highlighted and deepened client-generated change language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (mother's longevity, past miserable quit, 'don't expect miracles') was briefly acknowledged and pivoted toward change, e.g., the textbook strategic pivot after the mother example. Nothing in the session reinforced or dwelled on arguments against change.
