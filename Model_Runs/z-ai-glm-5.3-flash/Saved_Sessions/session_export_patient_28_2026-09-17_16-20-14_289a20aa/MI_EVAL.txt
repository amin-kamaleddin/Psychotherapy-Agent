# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5/5**
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout — opening with "Hi Dante — good to see you," affirming "I'm proud of you for doing this," and normalizing setbacks ("If plans change, that's okay; this is about small steps and progress, not perfection"). No judgment or disapproval anywhere; client clearly felt fully accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **4/5**
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening (e.g., "gym-at-work made it easy... importance 7, confidence 4 — that makes sense") and reflection of emotional content (worry about overthinking). Falls short of 5 because reflections were often bundled into long multi-question turns rather than offered standalone; the emotional significance of the client's father's health problems was noted but never explored reflectively.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5/5**
**Comments:** Steady focus on re-establishing exercise in every turn; logical progression from ratings → specific day/time/place → if-then plan → prep steps → accountability → scheduled check-in. No topic drift; values exploration consistently tied back to concrete action. Exemplary directional steering.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5/5**
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction: counselor proposed if-then format while client supplied content; Wednesday plan adjusted to evening walk at Emma's practice following client's lead. Check-in criteria came from the client. Client's feedback — "less like being lectured and more like figuring it out myself" — confirms collaborative tone.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5/5**
**Comments:** Choice language throughout ("Which one feels most helpful?", "Do you want Wednesday at noon... or a different time/place?"). Advice offered as optional options (shoes in car), never imposed; explicit permission for imperfection reduced external pressure. All plan elements were freely chosen by the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5/5**
**Comments:** Motivation consistently elicited from within: open values questions drew out deeply personal motivators (kids, father's health); confidence rulers tracked self-efficacy from 4 → 7. Client-generated solutions (planning Wednesday, accountability, "Anyone can do one minute") demonstrate motivation was evoked, not installed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5/5**
**Comments:** Expertise clearly positioned within the client — lunch walk, parking lot loop, Emma's practice walk, wife as accountability, and calendar label all came from Dante. Counselor contributions were additive refinements (backup plan, one-minute rule), and the closing question handed the client authority over follow-up. Power-sharing was felt by the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **5/5**
**Comments:** Change talk evoked from the opening through the close: "maybe I could walk at lunch" → "if it's a pattern" → "this feels doable. First time in a while I've said that about exercise." Confidence rulers used effectively (4→5→6→7); intrinsic motivation successfully mobilized via values connection ("Walk. For the kids").

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** ☑ **4/5**
**Comments:** All-or-nothing pattern effectively neutralized via 10-minute backup plan and reframing imperfection; one-minute rule offered as counter to overthinking. Falls short of 5 only because some counselor turns restated barriers ("commute, kids, and fatigue get in the way"), briefly amplifying sustain talk before pivoting to solutions.

---

**Overall Global Ratings Score: 4.8/5** — An exemplary MI session with seamless integration of evocation, collaboration, and direction; confidence rose from 4 to 7 through skillful ruler use, culminating in a client-generated, values-driven plan. Recommendation: offer more standalone complex reflections and fewer stacked questions to deepen empathy and conversational pacing.
