# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental with a client expecting criticism; validated defenses and affirmed past successes. Some affirmations felt formulaic and repeated (e.g., 'slips are normal and useful').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that captured Erik's defensiveness and priorities, though reflections were sometimes brief and bundled with questions. Deeper emotional content like his fear of his father's diabetic complications was acknowledged but not explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on target behaviors throughout, progressing logically to a specific written plan and two scheduled follow-ups. Every exchange tied back to concrete action.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely worked as an equal, co-constructing the plan from Erik's own ideas and letting him set the pace. Occasionally gave way to instructional blocks (meal examples, tactic lists) with limited negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and respected Erik's limits, culminating in his own words: 'a plan I actually chose myself.' Heavy use of pre-packaged advice lists at times bordered on directing rather than supporting.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong evocative opening questions elicited Erik's core motivations (father's complications, daughter, job). However, evocation gave way to advice menus, and his 'daughter moment' change talk was never deepened with importance or confidence exploration.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Conveyed Erik's expertise by asking about his patterns and past successes, with his contributions visibly shaping the plan. Frequently defaulted to providing tactic lists, which shifted the expert role back to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elicited some change talk and concrete commitments (three-pint limit, snack swaps), but much momentum came from the counselor's menu of suggestions rather than organically evoked motivation. Commitment language was never deepened with confidence or importance scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively avoided arguing with sustain talk, pivoting instead to Erik's own realistic framing and respecting his limits. Missed opportunities to use double-sided reflections to actively resolve his minimization of the referral.
