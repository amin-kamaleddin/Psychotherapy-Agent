# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone with affirmations of Daniel's smoking cessation and capabilities; Daniel explicitly appreciated not being lectured. Affirmations were somewhat generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was minimal; the counselor jumped to advice lists instead of reflecting Daniel's ambivalence about social belonging vs. health goals. The one empathic statement was quickly buried under prescriptive suggestions.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on alcohol use and its links to sleep and parenting, narrowing the agenda appropriately and building a concrete Saturday plan with follow-up structure. Early direction was imposed via advice rather than established collaboratively.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Daniel's own ideas (alternating with water, texting his wife) became central to the plan and the counselor offered drafting help. However, bulleted instruction lists and solution-offering before exploration made it feel more like a coach giving a game plan than equal partners.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice-based language and reframing slips as data rather than failure supported autonomy. The volume of unsolicited prescriptive advice tipped toward a controlling, expert stance, and permission before advising was never sought.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely presumed rather than evoked; beyond the single readiness ruler there were no evocative questions. Daniel's own motivators surfaced spontaneously but were affirmed rather than explored and developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited Daniel's input on which steps to try and which night, and Daniel shaped the plan. The overall stance remained expert-driven, with rapid-fire recommendations positioning the counselor as the expert rather than drawing out Daniel's own expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The readiness ruler evoked change talk ('maybe a five') and Daniel committed to concrete steps. The counselor never deepened the readiness score (e.g., 'why a 5 and not a 3?') and missed Daniel's spontaneous link to his smoking success.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mild sustain talk ('it's my release,' 'the guys dragged me into one more') was acknowledged without argument, but no double-sided reflections or reframes were used to resolve ambivalence. Responses defaulted to new advice lists rather than strategic softening.
