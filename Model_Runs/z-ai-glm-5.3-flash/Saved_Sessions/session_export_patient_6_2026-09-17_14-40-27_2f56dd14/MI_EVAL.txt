# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, non-judgmental opening and a consistently respectful, non-shaming stance toward the patient's minimization. Affirmations were sparse and generic, with little explicit validation of the patient's strengths beyond restating his concerns.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections were consistently accurate and on target, such as identifying the patient's worry about his mother over the drinking itself. Empathy tended to be brief and instrumental, pivoting quickly to menu options rather than deepening the emotional experience.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed anchored to alcohol but drifted early toward logistical concerns (records, confidentiality, doctor scripts) tangential to the target behavior. Clear direction toward the drinking behavior only emerged mid-session when option (c) was offered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly offered choices and role-played at the patient's request, but much of the session consisted of dispensing expert content (question lists, scripts, refusal lines). True power-sharing was partial rather than sustained.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently and explicitly supported the patient's choice ('I'll stick with whatever you choose') with no pressure or persuasion. The patient's genuine ownership of the plan reflects a clear strength of the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The offer of option (c) successfully drew out values-based change talk ('Drinking just isn't worth messing that up'). However, the counselor relied heavily on providing information and options rather than open questions that elicit the patient's own motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor invited the patient's preferences and honored his scenario choice, but the expert stance was prominent with ready-made scripts and supplied refusal lines. The substance of the plan was largely counselor-generated rather than co-created from the patient's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk was elicited ('I don't wanna become addicted') but only late in the session and largely indirectly. There were no explicit evocative questions or requests for elaboration when change talk emerged; the patient mostly generated it himself.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('it's not a habit') was acknowledged without reinforcement, which is adequate. The counselor did not strategically redirect it using double-sided reflections or reframes tied to the patient's goals, instead moving to logistical options.
