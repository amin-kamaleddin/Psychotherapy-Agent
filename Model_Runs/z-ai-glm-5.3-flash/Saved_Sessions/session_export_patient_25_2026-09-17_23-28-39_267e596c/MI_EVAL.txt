# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates warmth and unconditional positive regard, with specific genuine affirmations ('You've just done something powerful'). Daniel's minimizing ('Which is dumb, right?') is met with validation rather than judgment, and slips are normalized without undoing the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Complex, accurate reflections throughout, including a double-sided reflection capturing both fitting in with friends and concern about coming home buzzed. Tracks subtle emotional content well, e.g., 'your son is your compass — the crayon moment, the fuzzy memories of your own dad.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on social alcohol use while leveraging the smoking-cessation success as a resource. Each turn advances toward a concrete testable plan (Saturday barbecue, two beers, driving) with follow-up built in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Fully collaborative — all plan elements originate from Daniel, and the counselor frames follow-up as mutual ('we compare notes'). Daniel confirms the teamwork ('Feels good having someone actually tracking this with me').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Repeatedly attributes ownership to the client ('it's all yours, not something I'm imposing'), permits imperfection, and asks permission for follow-up. Daniel's closing statements reflect full ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillfully draws out Daniel's own values, motivations, and solutions rather than supplying them, then develops the material (turning the crayon incident into a coping strategy Daniel endorses). Commitment language grows from 'maybe' to concrete self-generated plans.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Power sharing is exemplary — no advice is given at any point, and the counselor explicitly places Daniel in the expert role ('those are your ideas, drawn from something you've already proven you can do'). Every plan component comes from the client.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Systematically evokes and deepens change talk through open questions linking to values (son, family), then reinforces it ('You're sounding like someone who believes it'). Daniel's language progresses measurably from ambivalence to commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (fitting in, drinking not feeling like a 'problem') is acknowledged without being argued with or reinforced, using double-sided reflections that pair it with change talk. The focus remains consistently on change.
