# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm and nonjudgmental throughout, with affirmations of Anya's courage and progress. However, affirmations are brief and the counselor moves quickly to scripts and tasks rather than individualizing praise.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections capture ambivalence well, but later reflections become formulaic and repetitive. Emotionally significant moments—shame, anxiety, and the weight of admitting the 'p-word'—receive minimal reflective exploration.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptional focus on reducing gambling and protecting finances with no topic drift. Every exchange advances toward a concrete, fully specified action plan culminating in successful role-play rehearsal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anya's ideas (phone wallet removal, Priya's help, hard-version role-play) are incorporated into the plan. However, the counselor supplies most content—scripts, comebacks, checklists—making the exchange more coach-driven than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some respect for choice through options and permission-seeking, but heavy scaffolding subtly takes ownership of the change process from Anya. Few explicit statements reinforcing that the decision and plan are hers.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Rulers and open questions elicit reasons and commitment language effectively. However, motivation is often given rather than drawn out—the reasons script is written for Anya to recite, and deeper exploration of what change would mean for her dreams is missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumes the expert role with verbatim scripts, comebacks, and checklists. Anya even comments on the prescriptive style ('You really want details, don't you?'), and her own solutions are drawn out only incidentally.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session's greatest strength—readiness and confidence rulers, specific next-step questions, and role-play rehearsal systematically evoked commitment language. Anya progressed from 'maybe a five' to confidence 8 and strong self-authored commitment statements.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not reinforced but is largely sidestepped rather than strategically softened. Moments like 'it's just a bit of plastic' and 'I haven't managed it to this point' receive options and questions instead of double-sided reflections.
