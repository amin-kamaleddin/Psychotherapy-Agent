# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout ('Be kind to yourself — one setback isn't failure'), and the client explicitly noted not feeling judged. Affirmations were somewhat sparse and formulaic, with few individualized affirmations of the client's strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were some accurate reflections ('That sounds really frightening'), but the counselor repeatedly pivoted quickly from emotion to checklists and facts. Deeper emotional experiences — fear of family history, 'different person takes over' feeling — were largely underexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus maintained throughout on the two interlinked targets of alcohol reduction and contraceptive reliability. The session stayed concrete and relevant, closing with a coherent action plan with no topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative moments existed (asking which step feels doable, agreeing on follow-up), but the interaction often resembled expert consultation with information dumps. Plans were largely constructed and delivered by the counselor rather than co-created.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered choice at points ('Which of these feels most doable?'), and the client made her own decisions. However, the heavily prescriptive format — long bulleted directives before exploring her preferences — undermined perceived choice, and explicit ownership statements were absent.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was minimally elicited; most change talk was client-initiated and unprompted. There were no evocative questions ('What makes this important to you?'), no scaling follow-ups on her importance 8/confidence 4 ratings, and little developing of discrepancy — the counselor provided reasons and facts instead.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor repeatedly assumed the expert role with lengthy prescriptive lists and pre-written scripts delivered before exploring the client's ideas. Her self-generated strategies (sober buddy, Planned Parenthood for cost) received little acknowledgment or building upon.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged largely from the client herself, and the counselor acknowledged progress ('confidence rise to a five is real progress'). However, there was no strategic evocation — no elaborating on stated motivations or open questions exploring reasons for change, and the confidence scaling was never followed up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The client's doubts ('drunk me ignores it', judgment going 'out the window') were not reinforced but also not actively softened or reframed. The counselor jumped to concrete tips rather than helping resolve ambivalence or reframing her self-doubt into the strength of recognizing that external accountability works for her.
