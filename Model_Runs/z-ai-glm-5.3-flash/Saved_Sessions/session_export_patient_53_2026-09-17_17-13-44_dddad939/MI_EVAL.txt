# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, normalizing avoidance and affirming Isabella's honesty. However, praise is somewhat formulaic and repeated, lacking individualized depth.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections were present, but responses often moved quickly to offering options and drafting materials. Missed opportunities to explore her shame, isolation history, and pattern of not following through.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus throughout on the target behavior, moving from vague ambivalence to a precise, concrete plan with backup and accountability. Every response advanced the goal without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Isabella made some choices, but the pattern was largely counselor-proposes, client-approves. The counselor drafted nearly all materials, limiting true co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choices were consistently offered without pressure, and Isabella set key parameters herself. Heavy counselor drafting of materials slightly blurred ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions and open prompts elicited some client motivation, but the counselor often shifted to providing solutions instead of drawing out her own. Key self-generated insights were not developed further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Menus of options supported some power sharing, but the counselor dominated content by drafting all scripts and messages. Isabella's comment 'You already basically wrote them' reflects this imbalance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions elicited useful change talk, including 'less room to hide' and 'that part's maybe a little louder.' These statements were not strategically reflected or amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoidance was reframed neutrally without dwelling on sustain talk. However, expressed doubts were not paired with change talk via double-sided reflections, missing chances to resolve ambivalence.
