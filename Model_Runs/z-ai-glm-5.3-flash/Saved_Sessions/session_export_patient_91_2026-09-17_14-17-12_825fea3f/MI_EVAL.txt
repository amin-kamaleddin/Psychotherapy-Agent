# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, normalizing Marcus's worries. However, affirmations are generic ('Nice—great step') rather than specific to his own strengths.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening largely absent; emotional disclosures (fear of doing it alone, shame, catastrophic thinking) were skipped in favor of instructions. Almost no complex reflections were offered.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent focus on diabetes self-management — blood sugar monitoring and the nurse appointment. On track throughout, though direction was driven by the counselor's task-completion agenda rather than collaboratively set goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative elements (asking what feels overwhelming, offering options, role-play at Marcus's request), but the counselor supplied nearly all content. Marcus's own plan shaped the outcome more than the counselor's suggestions, yet was never explicitly co-constructed.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choices (checklist vs. script, role-play or text) and accepted Marcus's limits ('No promises yet'). But most turns ended in prescriptive instructions that subtly pressured him toward the counselor's plan without affirming his ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Assumed Marcus needed information and tools rather than eliciting his own reasons, confidence, or values. His self-efficacy emerged spontaneously ('I think I can do this') but was never drawn out or developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Counselor functioned primarily as an advice-dispenser — scripts, checklists, and instructions dominated. Marcus's ideas (bringing his wife, calling the nurse himself) were accepted but never credited as his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Marcus spontaneously offered meaningful change talk ('Maybe I'll call them this week', 'I think I can do this'), but the counselor never deepened or reinforced it. Responses to change talk were met with more task assignments instead of exploration or affirmation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk and effectively reframed panic fears ('adjust and keep going, not panic'). However, ambivalence like 'I'm not great at asking for help' was skipped rather than reflected or reframed into self-efficacy.
