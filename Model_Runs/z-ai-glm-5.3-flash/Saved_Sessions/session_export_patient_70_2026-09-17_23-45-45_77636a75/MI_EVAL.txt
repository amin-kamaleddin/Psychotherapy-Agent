# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Affirmations were genuine, specific, and frequent, with nonjudgmental handling of Lars's literacy struggles, offending history, and ambivalence. Warmth felt individualized, honoring his strengths rather than formulaic praise.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening was consistently accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing mixed feelings about release and shame about literacy without judgment. Reflections were complex, often rephrasing Lars's words into deeper meaning and tracking subtle shifts in his self-perception.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session maintained clear focus on recidivism reduction and re-entry with natural progression from forms to Friday plans to life vision. Emotional/relational material was productive but could have been tied to the target behavior slightly more explicitly at moments.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session was fully collaborative, opening by deferring to the client's agenda with every plan element client-generated and merely organized by the clinician. Choice-oriented questions signaled equal partnership, with no prescribing, correcting, or taking over direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was continuously reinforced with ownership language throughout ('That's a big realization, and it came from you'). No advice or directives were given; even risk-related questions were framed to leave answers entirely to the client.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from the client, with questions consistently eliciting his own aspirations and values, producing a rich vision statement. Escalating commitment language ('now or never,' 'properly want it') demonstrates highly successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treated Lars as the expert on his own life, with every plan element originating from him. Self-generated coping strategies were explicitly credited back, exemplifying power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions consistently invited desire, reason, and commitment language, with change talk escalating from 'I don't want to end up back inside' to 'I actually want it. Properly want it.' Key change-talk statements were selectively reflected and strategically sequenced toward commitment and action planning.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently softened without reinforcement, using dual-sided or validating reflections before pivoting to what would feel doable. Reflections occasionally paraphrased the worry side before pivoting, but never amplified reasons against change.
