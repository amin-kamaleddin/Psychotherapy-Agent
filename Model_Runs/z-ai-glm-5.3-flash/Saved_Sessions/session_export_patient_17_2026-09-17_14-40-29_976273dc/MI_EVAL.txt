# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, consistently validating Anisa's feelings without labeling or disapproval even amid disclosures of failure and shame. However, affirmations were brief and formulaic, missing chances to affirm her specific strengths — her insight into triggers, honesty about excuses, and courage in setting a date.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Decent reflective listening early on, but the counselor rarely reflected the emotional core of Anisa's disclosures — her shame, tears about her daughter, and fear of disbelief were met with practical summaries rather than deep empathic reflection. Empathy was present but often overtaken by the problem-solving agenda.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on smoking cessation throughout, with every exchange advancing the target behavior — assessment, medication, quit date, morning routines, costs, daughter involvement, and slip planning. No topic drift, and the session progressed logically from motivation to concrete, actionable steps.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative gestures appeared (co-selecting the date, Anisa revising the script), but the interaction skewed toward the counselor's pre-structured bullet-point plans and scripts, with Anisa mostly reacting and approving. The overall texture was closer to a well-informed advice session than an exchange between equal partners.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed everything as choice, gated advice with permission, and repeatedly asked Anisa to select options. However, autonomy support came largely through menu-offering within counselor-defined boundaries rather than genuine open exploration or deeper reinforcement of her ownership.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Drew little motivation out from within the client — no open questions about her reasons for change, hope, or confidence, and no scaling questions. Anisa's spontaneous change talk ('I'm worth this') was met with logistics, and the session's momentum came from the counselor's structure rather than elicited client motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked which options felt best and offered to co-create, but substantially assumed the expert role, delivering pre-packaged plans, scripts, and checklists each turn. Anisa's own contributions were welcomed but not substantially shaped or developed by the counselor.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Anisa generated substantial change talk herself, but the counselor did little to actively evoke or deepen it — questions were mostly logistical, with no strategic evocative questions, confidence scaling, or elaboration of her 'I'm worth this' moment. Change talk was reinforced indirectly through plan-building rather than MI technique.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk acceptably without amplifying it, and the slip plan reframing ('one cigarette ≠ failure') effectively softened Anisa's all-or-nothing thinking. However, redirections relied on concrete problem-solving rather than MI skills — no double-sided reflections, and ambivalence was occasionally glided past.
