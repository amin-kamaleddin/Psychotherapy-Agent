# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful of Richard despite hostility; validated frustration and offered choice early. Lacked explicit affirmations of his courage or honesty, and warmth felt somewhat formulaic.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured ambivalence, but later turned generic as advice-giving took over. Missed deeper emotional content like shame after the drywall incident and the loneliness of Susan's cold goodbyes.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained steady focus on smoking despite deflections and steered toward a concrete, realistic target (one smoke-free evening). Final turns drifted into repetitive procedural recap rather than consolidating direction or linking to deeper motivations.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plan was largely co-constructed from Richard's own preferences (mug, candle, worry beads). Counselor occasionally slid into prescriptive mode with technique lists before asking what Richard thought might work.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongest domain: repeated choice menus, 'I won't push,' and 'experiment, not a verdict' framing reinforced ownership. Richard's autonomous decision-making ('one night, then we'll see') reflected genuine internal choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Drew out relational motivation ('I don't want to lose her') and emergent hope ('part of me wants to see if I even can do it'). Relied too heavily on supplying options rather than eliciting Richard's own reasons, confidence, or solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly asked which options felt most useful, and Richard's ideas shaped the plan. Counselor still occupied a quasi-expert role by supplying all strategy ideas rather than eliciting Richard's own first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Modest change talk emerged late via the experiment framing ('wants to see if I even can do it'). Missed the key opening when Richard hinted at wanting to keep the routine without Susan's look—jumped to techniques instead of deepening his own hope and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Acknowledged sustain talk without arguing and gently reframed the failed quit attempt. Did not consistently use double-sided reflections pivoting toward change, and one reflection arguably reinforced the status quo framing.
