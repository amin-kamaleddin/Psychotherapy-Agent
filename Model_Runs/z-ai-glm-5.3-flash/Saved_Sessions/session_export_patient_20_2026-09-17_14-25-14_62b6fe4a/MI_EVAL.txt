# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone and validates Brandon's perspective without labeling or pushing abstinence. However, affirmations of his honesty and strengths are sparse, keeping warmth somewhat transactional.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections appear, but reflective listening largely disappears after the opening turns. Emotional content—like hating Sunday hangovers and sister pressure—gets bypassed in favor of delivering tactics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor maintains consistent focus on alcohol use throughout, moving from quantifying consumption to a concrete cut-back plan with a scheduled check-in. The weakness is that direction is maintained through directive list-giving rather than collaborative goal clarification.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Brandon does shape the plan (choosing alcohol-free days, limits, check-in), but the counselor dominates with long prescriptive lists and scripts, making the session feel like dispensing a program rather than genuine co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Offers meaningful menu-based choices throughout (plan elements, limits, check-in timing) and explicitly honors Brandon's autonomy, normalizing slips. Choices remain within counselor-generated options, and his 'no promises' limit isn't explicitly reinforced as his right.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Almost no questions elicit Brandon's own reasons, values, or ideas; questions are screening and logistical. His motivations emerge spontaneously but are never explored or amplified—the counselor supplies tactics instead of drawing out his own solutions.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks permission to question and allows some choice, but the overall stance is expert-driven, with prescriptive tactics and monitoring requirements delivered without first eliciting Brandon's own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Brandon spontaneously offers meaningful change talk ('maybe there's some middle ground,' 'I'll give it a real shot'), but the counselor rarely reflects or deepens it and asks no open questions to evoke motivation. His commitment language goes unaffirmed and unexplored.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoids arguing with sustain talk and reframes consequences non-judgmentally, but does little active softening—no double-sided reflections or strategic use of sustain talk. Brandon's weekend drinking disclaimer goes entirely unreflected.
