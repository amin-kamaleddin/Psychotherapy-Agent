# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout ('I'll be nonjudgmental — bring the truth, good or bad'), but affirmations are sparse and generic. Warmth is functional rather than deeply personalizing — strengths and honesty are acknowledged only once, near the close.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate reflections ('the office candy bar is the real snag') but they are infrequent and quickly pivot to options or plans. The emotional core of Erik's discouragement — shame comparing himself to younger men — is reflected once but never revisited.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps consistent focus on target behaviors (exercise, self-comparison, diet) and closes with a clear, specific recap and follow-up plan. Loses a point for splintering across many micro-goals without checking whether the volume risks overwhelming Erik, which he himself flags.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks for feasibility input and incorporates Erik's modifications, but plans, templates, and tips are predominantly counselor-generated. The interaction resembles counselor-proposing-client-approving rather than equal co-construction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers menus of options, frames change as non-perfectionistic, and honors Erik's right to decline (nuts idea dropped after pushback). Falls short of a 5 by emphasizing counselor-designed systems over Erik's own agency and capacity for self-direction.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation and solutions come mostly from the counselor rather than from Erik; the promising opening evocative question is quickly abandoned for pre-packaged approaches. No open questions explore Erik's reasons, values, or confidence, and his spontaneous insights are not developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite collaborative-sounding questions, the counselor assumes the expert role with detailed tips, templates, and structured plans. Erik's own ideas (his 3pm habit cue, asking about the candy bar first) are absorbed into the plan but not credited or built upon as his expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Erik volunteers substantial change talk and the counselor elicits some via choice questions, but key opportunities to deepen it are missed — his 'trying over perfection' insight passes without reinforcement. No confidence scaling or exploration of why change matters to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoids amplifying sustain talk and responds acceptably by dropping options after pushback, but sustain talk is left unaddressed rather than strategically softened. Statements like 'I'd probably have it anyway' receive no double-sided reflection or reframing toward his own motivation.
