# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warm unconditional regard from start to finish, including a prompt apology for the 45-minute wait and non-judgmental reframing of eating at the desk as what the day allows. No labeling, disapproval, or conditional regard observed despite the client's initial hostility.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were consistently accurate and deepened disclosure, e.g., noting that 'not ideal' about the stairs carried hidden weight tied to his father's heart troubles. The counselor correctly decoded 'food is just fuel' and reframed self-criticism as useful information.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The focus on weight, diet, exercise, and blood pressure was maintained throughout despite the client's initial refusal to discuss weight. Every question advanced the concrete behavioral agenda, closing with a specific 3-week follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Agenda, plan, and follow-up structure were fully co-constructed, with the psychiatrist deferring to the client's knowledge and operating style throughout. Genuine power-sharing was evident in the client's comfortable negotiation of the next appointment, which was honored.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was consistently positioned as the decision-maker: 'this is your space,' 'you're the one setting the size,' and control over accountability explicitly handed back to him. The counselor offered options rather than prescriptions and let the client select.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from the client himself — his father's heart history, breathlessness, and preference for his wife's cooking — via open evocative questions. The counselor avoided lecturing and left the verdict on the experiment with the client.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client's expertise was actively central ('you're the expert on your life'), and the plan was built entirely from his own operating style. He recognized ownership of the plan and requested ongoing accountability, a sign of mutual trust.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was explicitly amplified ('I would like to get hold of this is you talking, not the doctor') and linked to personal stakes. The counselor elicited the client's blueprint, producing strong commitment language around diary appointments, Sunday batch cooking, and follow-up reports.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('no time,' 'walking won't fix the eating') was briefly acknowledged and pivoted to problem-solving without argument — e.g., 'which of those sounds least impossible.' When asked 'am I just making excuses?', the counselor normalized rather than disputed or reinforced.
