# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and non-judgmental, with warm affirmations and normalization that circumstances—not capability—caused the setback. Affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic; deeper individualized regard (e.g., her confidence of 10) was underused.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Showed adequate early empathy and validation of embarrassment, but reflections were sparse and the session shifted quickly to problem-solving. Rarely explored underlying emotions like fear of missing another interview or worry about sounding desperate.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear, consistent focus on employment barriers tied to childcare, with a concrete action plan that never drifted. Falls short of 5 because the agenda was prescriptive rather than collaboratively negotiated.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine collaborative moments including co-editing the script and role-playing together, but the structure was largely counselor-designed. Amina selected from counselor-generated options rather than jointly building strategies from her own knowledge.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered choice menus and asked which steps she preferred, respecting autonomy procedurally. However, the advice was highly directive in content, with few statements reinforcing her ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Operated from an advice-giving model—scripts, swap ideas, and coping techniques came from the counselor, not from Amina. Strong internal resources like her confidence rating of 10 and her certainty about a next interview were largely unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina's preferences shaped the session (casual script, role-play order), but because the counselor offered menus rather than locating expertise in the client. Overall stance leaned expert-driven.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Amina generated substantial change talk herself and the counselor responded supportively, but rarely deliberately evoked or deepened it. Missed opportunities to amplify, e.g., 'What makes it a 10 rather than a 7?'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Did not reinforce sustain talk, but also did not actively soften it—responses to fears about embarrassment and sitters jumped straight to reassurance and lists. Little use of double-sided reflections or reframing toward change.
