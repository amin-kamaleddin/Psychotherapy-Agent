# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, with affirmations of Hana's courage and honesty, and she explicitly reported not feeling judged. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and repetitive, lacking deeper individualized positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were brief and quickly pivoted to questions or planning; key emotional content like years of avoidance and fear of others' judgment was never deeply explored. Responses were largely procedural, and Hana supplied most of the emotional depth herself.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stayed tightly focused on the target behavior, with questions consistently advancing toward a concrete goal and landing on a specific, measurable plan. A 5 was withheld because direction was achieved through rapid action-planning rather than strategic MI sequencing (discrepancy, importance/confidence).

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The tone was friendly and joint, and Hana's contributions (tea reward, note idea) were incorporated. However, the counselor proposed solutions faster than exploring them, recited checklists, and offered advice bundles without permission, limiting true two-way shaping.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some autonomy-supportive elements (realistic time choice, check-in options, information-not-verdict framing), but the counselor leaned prescriptive — dictating the plan, texts, and reminders. Hana's compliance suggests agreement to the counselor's scaffolding rather than a self-generated plan.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Strong evocative opening questions drew out Hana's presentation success story and self-generated motivation. However, evocation was front-loaded — the session shifted to counselor-provided tools without exploring the personal meaning of the certification or systematically eliciting confidence and commitment language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some power sharing occurred (time choice, check-in format), but the counselor slipped into an expert stance, supplying the full plan largely unilaterally. Hana's own ideas emerged despite rather than because of explicit invitations.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively elicited change talk early ('stop feeling this constant weight,' 'I think I'm ready to try'), connected it to past success. However, change talk was not strategically deepened or reflected in the moment, such as after 'Maybe that's progress though — knowing the voice is coming.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced and occasional reframing occurred ('Booking again shows courage'), but ambivalence received only brief acknowledgment with few double-sided reflections. Softening was done implicitly by rushing to action planning rather than skillfully resolving ambivalence.
