# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Counselor is polite and nonjudgmental, with a warm opening ('I'm here to listen and help') and closing ('you've got this'). However, regard is generic rather than tailored — no affirmations of Leila's specific strengths beyond a brief 'well done for deciding to try.'

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is nearly absent; Leila's emotional material (shock at 2,000 steps, frustration, body-image concerns, loss of her active identity) is never reflected back. The counselor responds to rich disclosures with rapid-fire factual questions and pivots quickly to delivering a plan.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains strong, consistent focus on establishing an exercise routine with no topic drift; every exchange ties back to concrete action (the 4-week plan, YMCA visit, walking goal, sales script). Direction sometimes outruns collaboration, however, rushing to prescription before fully exploring.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction follows an expert-delivers/client-executes pattern, with a complete 4-week program, checklists, and scripts offered without inviting Leila's input. The one genuinely collaborative moment (tweaking the sister text) was client-initiated.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor asks permission before advising and offers some options (checklist vs. role-play), but the plan arrives as a package of directives with repeated reminder lists that feel instructive. Leila's sense of ownership ('because I chose it') is self-generated and never explicitly affirmed by the counselor.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Leila's strong motivation is never actively drawn out — no scaling questions, values exploration, or elicitation of her own solutions ('What has worked for you before?'). The counselor supplies all the answers: the plan, scripts, breathing technique, and reward ideas.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor quickly assumes the expert role, delivering a fully prescriptive multi-week program without ever asking what Leila thinks would work or acknowledging her as the expert on her own life. Her contributions shape the plan because she is unusually proactive, not because the counselor deliberately cedes expertise.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Leila generates abundant change talk spontaneously ('I want to feel stronger,' 'I feel more confident when I'm active'), but the counselor does nothing to evoke or deepen it — no open questions about her reasons for change or strategic reflections amplifying desire. The change talk occurred despite, not because of, counselor technique.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Leila's barriers (contract fears, sales pressure, weather, home distraction) are not amplified — the counselor pragmatically designs around them with month-to-month gyms, scripts, and indoor alternatives. This is practical problem-solving rather than MI-consistent softening, with no double-sided reflections or reframing of ambivalence.
