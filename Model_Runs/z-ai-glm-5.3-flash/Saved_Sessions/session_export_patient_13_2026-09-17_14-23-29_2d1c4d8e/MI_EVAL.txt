# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm and nonjudgmental throughout; Anara explicitly felt unshamed. Falls short of 5 because affirmations are formulaic and tied to task completion rather than unconditional regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate early reflections, but deeper fears (bad news, 'the not knowing') are never explicitly validated. The counselor pivots quickly to scripts and coping techniques without exploring feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent focus sustained from first turn to last, with a logical progression to a concrete, time-bound plan (Tuesday call). The session's strongest domain.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Friendly and cooperative, but the counselor hands down fully-formed solutions and checklists while Anara executes the plan. Collaboration is procedural rather than equal partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine choices offered (phone vs. portal, role-play role), but much of the session is prescriptive — counselor assigns the day, reward, check-frequency, and techniques. Choices are logistical, not substantive.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation is supplied externally via reassurance and accountability rather than drawn out. The counselor never asks why change matters to Anara or explores her confidence and hope.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, delivering scripts, checklists, and instructions nearly every turn. Little curiosity about Anara's own ideas for what would work.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Anara generates commitment-adjacent language ('maybe I could actually book it,' 'not this time'), aided by role-play and scripting. However, this came through task prescription rather than deliberate MI evocation of her reasons and confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I'll find reasons to put it off,' 'chicken out') is not amplified, but also not actively softened with double-sided reflections or affirmations. The counselor jumps past ambivalence to tasks — neutral, not skillful.
