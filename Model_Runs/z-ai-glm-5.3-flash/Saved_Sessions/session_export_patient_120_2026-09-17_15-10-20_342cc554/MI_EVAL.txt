# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains  a  respectful,  nonjudgmental  tone  and  normalizes  Sofia's  disclosure  without  shaming,  in  contrast  to  the  referring  doctor.  However,  warmth  is  brief  and  affirmations  are  sparse  relative  to  the  volume  of  advice  given.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Complex  reflections  accurately  capture  Sofia's  guilt,  fear,  and  ambivalence,  including  the  attuned  reflection  on  telling  a  friend.  Deeper  emotional  content  (identity  grief,  fear  of  isolation)  is  sometimes  passed  over  in  favor  of  menu  options.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains  a  clear,  consistent  focus  on  reducing  alcohol  use  during  pregnancy  with  no  topic  drift.  The  session  progresses  logically  from  refusal  skills  to  medical  disclosure  to  social  support,  with  each  transition  offered  back  to  the  client.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Genuine  collaboration  appears  in  the  role-plays  with  check-ins  and  client  evaluation,  but  monologue-style  advice  blocks  (scripts,  checklists)  dominate.  Sofia's  own  ideas  are  received  but  not  deeply  integrated  into  the  plans.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Actively  supports  autonomy  by  letting  Sofia  choose  directions  and  respecting  her  disclosure  boundaries.  Falls  short  of  5  because  menus  of  options  are  counselor-generated  and  ownership  of  outcomes  isn't  explicitly  reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Meaningful  motivation  and  self-efficacy  do  emerge  from  Sofia  and  are  well  affirmed.  However,  few  open,  evocative  questions  explore  her  own  reasons,  hopes,  or  confidence  —  motivation  is  affirmed  more  than  evoked.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Explicitly  positions  expertise  with  Sofia  and  repeatedly  asks  her  to  choose  directions,  but  repeatedly  slips  into  an  expert  stance  with  pre-packaged  scripts  and  tips.  Sofia's  own  strategies  (the  soda  trick,  Jess's  Friday-night  text)  were  often  more  personalized  than  the  counselor's  contributions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates  conditions  for  strong  change  talk  ('I  owe  the  kid  at  least  that  much,'  'like  I'm  already  being  the  mom  I  want  to  be')  and  links  it  to  her  values.  Rarely  deepens  change  talk  with  follow-up  questions  or  elicits  confidence/commitment  language  directly.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers  a  decent  double-sided  reflection  and  does  not  reinforce  sustain  talk.  However,  sustain  talk  is  redirected  into  action-planning  menus  rather  than  explored  and  resolved  through  emotional  reflection.
