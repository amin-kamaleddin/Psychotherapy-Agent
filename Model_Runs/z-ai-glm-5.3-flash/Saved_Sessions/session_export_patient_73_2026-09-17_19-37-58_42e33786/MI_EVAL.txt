# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Warmth, respect, and unconditional positive regard were consistent throughout, with explicit welcome of honesty about failure. No judgment or disapproval was present anywhere in the session.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Complex, accurate reflections captured both sides of ambivalence and the client's fear of failure and desire for self-proof. Deeper reflection of the emotional weight of 38 years of failed attempts would have strengthened empathy further.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on tobacco cessation from the opening question through to a specific, written, dated plan. Every exchange advanced the target behavior without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong collaboration — the plan was client-generated and the clinician asked permission before writing. Slightly more explicit negotiation of commitment would have made power-sharing fully equal.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy was actively fostered: the client chose the day and plan, and the clinician affirmed her ideas as drawing on her own learning. Nothing was imposed, and honesty about failure was welcomed as her choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation came from the client via skilled open questions, with her determination emerging and strengthening through hearing her own words reflected. The clinician never argued for change or supplied reasons.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The client was treated as the expert, with questions consistently eliciting her ideas, which shaped the entire plan. Asking permission to write things down reinforced power sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful, consistent evocation of desire, ability, and commitment language through open questions, with change talk reflected and amplified. This led directly to a concrete client-owned plan — textbook change talk cultivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged via double-sided reflections and effectively redirected toward change. Weaknesses: the summary repeated sustain talk content at length, and the clinician momentarily adopted the client's negative self-talk ('I've made enough excuses'), risking reinforcement.
