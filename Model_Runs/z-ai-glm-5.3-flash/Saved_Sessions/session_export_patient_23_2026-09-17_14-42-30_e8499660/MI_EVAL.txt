# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout, validating the client's honesty and moderate goal without labeling or disapproval. Affirmations are somewhat brief and generic rather than deeply individualized.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture the client's ambivalence and meaning, including her insight that 'the ritual matters more than the actual drink.' However, reflections are often immediately followed by bundled questions, limiting exploration of her emotional self-criticism and low mood.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains a clear, consistent focus on reducing alcohol intake from the opening question through a concrete, testable plan. Every turn advances the target behavior with no topic drift, progressing logically from exploration to planning.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine joint problem-solving with permission-seeking, co-constructed if-then plans, and client input at every decision point. Occasional multi-item advisory suggestion lists introduce a mild expert asymmetry.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly reinforced through repeated offering of choices, explicit permission before sharing ideas, and acceptance of refusals without pressure. Client ownership is evident in her self-generated plans and spoken confidence.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently elicits motivation from within using rulers, open questions, and client-generated strategies, with strong internal reasoning emerging from the client. Frequent offering of ready-made ideas occasionally substitutes provision for evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The client generated most plan elements herself, with counselor suggestions serving as a menu she selected from. Some pre-packaged idea lists slightly reduce the extent to which the client's own ideas drive the content.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillful use of importance/confidence rulers and open questions drove confidence from 5 to 8 and elicited concrete commitment language ('I'd start tonight, honestly. Why wait?'). Some change talk statements were not deepened with follow-up evocative questions before moving on.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Responds to sustain talk by reflecting the client's chosen realistic alternatives rather than arguing, and affirms declined options as 'doable and private' without pressing. Few deliberate double-sided reflections or explicit reframes of her doubts.
