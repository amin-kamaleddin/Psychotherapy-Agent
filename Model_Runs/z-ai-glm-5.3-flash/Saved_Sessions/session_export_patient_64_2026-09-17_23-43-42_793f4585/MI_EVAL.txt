# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional regard throughout, with genuine, specific affirmations and immediate validation of the patient's boundaries ('That's a completely fair boundary'). No judgment or disapproval appears anywhere, even when the patient dismisses the data.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurate, with double-sided reflections that capture ambivalence well and emotional reflections deepening around the grandparents. Falls short of a 5 because some reflections restate rather than probe deeper meaning, and one ('they confirmed what you expected') could have carried a subtle edge.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintains a clear, consistent focus on the flu vaccine decision, with every exchange advancing the target behavior from values through practical logistics. It ends with a concrete, client-generated action step ('I could swing by a pharmacy this week').

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session content is co-constructed at every juncture, with the patient's skepticism, family concern, and practical questions actively shaping the direction. Information is shared conversationally with check-ins ('How does that land?') rather than lecturing.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary autonomy support with repeated explicit statements like 'You're the expert on your own body' and 'It is your call, Jordan, completely.' Information is offered only after the patient invites it, and genuine choice points are provided throughout.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is consistently drawn from within the client via values exploration, the family-dinner projection, and the 'what would need to be true' question. The key insight ('it's not really about me') comes entirely from the patient, and closing commitment language is fully client-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician explicitly positions the patient as the expert, and his contributions substantially shape the session's agenda, emotional centerpiece, and closing plan. Choice-offering is authentic rather than token.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation elicits values and uses evocative questions while noticing and reinforcing early change talk. This yields a clear progression of client-owned change talk, from 'that'd be on me' to 'I could swing by a pharmacy this week.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is never reinforced or debated, and it is cleverly reframed into change-relevant material ('What would need to be true...?'). Not a 5 because sustain talk is occasionally reflected back neutrally with equal airtime and without a strategic pivot, though it does diminish over the session.
