# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains warm, nonjudgmental regard throughout, validating Malik's irritation without moralizing and using affirmations like 'That's a really honest thing to say' to convey genuine respect. No defensiveness even when the patient pushes back.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is highly accurate and emotionally attuned, capturing feelings of being singled out and the bind between family stress and peer refuge. The psychiatrist also skillfully rolls with the patient's correction without losing rapport.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session keeps clear focus on cannabis use and advances toward concrete behavioral steps, despite brief drift into family dynamics. Falls short of a 5 due to the absence of a closing summary consolidating change talk.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist positions himself as curious rather than expert, with each question building directly from Malik's words. All plans and framing emerge from the patient, with no unilateral steering.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy support is explicit and consistent ('I'm not here to lecture,' 'I'm not here to push you either way'), with no advice offered and all steps originating from the patient. Malik's conditions are accepted without challenge.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist systematically elicits Malik's own motivations, and his reasons for change surface organically, culminating in his own commitment language ('It could work. Worth a shot, anyway.'). Discrepancy development is excellent.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Every plan element originates from Malik, and affirmations explicitly locate expertise with the client. Falls short only because there is no explicit invitation for Malik to set the session agenda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is systematically elicited and developed through questions about small steps, fair deals, and timing, progressing Malik from sustain talk to concrete plans and preparatory language. Reinforced with affirmations like 'that's you choosing to do something.'

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk is consistently reframed productively, including a textbook double-sided reflection about risk versus staying stuck. The main limitation is occasionally reflecting sustain talk at length before pivoting.
