# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintains unconditional positive regard throughout, explicitly disarms the coercive context, and never judges the substance use or peer group. Tyler is consistently validated, including when deflecting blame onto his mother.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections are complex and emotionally attuned, capturing both content and feeling (e.g., 'it's all for her, never for you'). Tyler's progression from one-word defensiveness to vulnerable self-disclosure shows the reflections consistently land.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session follows a coherent, progressive arc toward the target behavior without lecturing. The deduction reflects the somewhat circuitous early-middle phase, where substantial time is spent on the mother relationship and school before substance use is addressed directly.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction is genuine joint inquiry ('help me understand,' 'what's your gut say'), with Tyler's answers shaping the session's direction at every step. The clinician steers only through reflections and questions, never expert-driven redirection.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is explicitly reinforced throughout: coercion is removed at the start, Tyler's choices are protected ('your friends are yours'), and ownership is repeatedly placed with Tyler ('that has to come from you'). No advice-giving or pressure appears anywhere in the session.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist never supplies reasons for change but draws out Tyler's own, which build from 'responsible stuff works' to 'it adds less than the guys do' and finally 'I just never let myself actually go through with it.' Tyler's evolution from reluctance to tentative commitment is direct evidence of successful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tyler is repeatedly positioned as the expert ('you know your crew better than anyone'; 'you know her better than I do'). Every key question hands the agenda to Tyler, and his contributions visibly drive the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open, strategic questions ('what role would you want the smoking to play?', 'what would make that feel safe?') elicit Tyler's self-generated reasons rather than supplied ones. Tyler's change talk builds progressively toward near-commitment language by session's end.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk is acknowledged briefly and consistently pivoted toward change without argument — e.g., 'she's not gonna change' is reflected as double-sided ambivalence, and fear of peer rejection is met with Tyler's own disconfirming evidence (Marcus). The minimization that 'everyone does it' is used as a bridge rather than reinforced.
