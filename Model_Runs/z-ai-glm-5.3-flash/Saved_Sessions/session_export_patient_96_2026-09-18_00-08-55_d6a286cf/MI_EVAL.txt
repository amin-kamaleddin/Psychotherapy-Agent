# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently affirms patient autonomy, explicitly stating that keeping the piercing is their call and that downsizing involves no commitment beyond asking. The patient's independence is repeatedly reinforced, even when they express reluctance.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflective listening is used throughout to accurately capture the patient's perspective, including ambivalence ('a bit of both, but mostly want') and the desire for self-direction. The patient feels deeply understood, as evidenced by their increasingly open engagement.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Every response maintains focus on the target behavior of protecting gums/teeth while keeping the piercing, with no drift into unrelated topics. The conversation moves coherently from exploration toward a concrete, patient-generated plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician consistently frames the process as collaborative, using phrases like 'Maybe we put those together' and repeatedly asking the patient for their thoughts and ideas. The plan emerges entirely from the patient's own reasoning rather than from directives.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is strongly supported through explicit statements that choices belong to the patient ('that's your call, and you get to make it') and by highlighting that changes are self-determined. The clinician reframes even the patient's resistance ('I don't like being told what to do') as self-direction to be honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician draws out the patient's own motivations and solutions through questions like 'what do you think could work?' and 'what would a good outcome look like to you?'. The patient identifies the gum-rubbing concern, the downsizing idea, the photo baseline, and warning signs independently, which the clinician affirms as self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician positions themselves as a partner with expertise to combine ('You know what the piercing means to you, and your dentist knows teeth'), never claiming authority over the outcome. The final summary explicitly credits the patient: 'Nothing here was my plan — you figured out...'

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk is actively elicited and reinforced through questions about experimenting, priorities, and next steps, yielding strong commitments ('I'll text her, actually... This week'). The clinician deepens momentum by asking about desired outcomes and specific plans, producing robust preparatory and mobilizing change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk (resistance to removal, 'no promises,' 'it's just a piercing') is met with acceptance and reframed toward autonomy rather than challenged. The clinician skillfully redirects defensive energy into choice language, transforming 'not being told what to do' into motivation for action on the patient's own terms.
