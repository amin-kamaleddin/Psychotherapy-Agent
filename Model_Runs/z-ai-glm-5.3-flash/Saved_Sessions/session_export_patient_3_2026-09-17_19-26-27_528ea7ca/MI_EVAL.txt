# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Respect and warmth are consistent, with no judgment and effective normalization of ambivalence. Affirmations are somewhat formulaic and the emotional weight of feeling stuck is not explicitly validated, keeping this just below the highest level.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening is strong and accurate, including a nuanced double-sided reflection capturing both excitement and dread. However, emotional content like the client's shame ('feeling rubbish about myself') is handled cognitively rather than reflected at the affective level.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session stays laser-focused on initiating exercise, moving from options to a concrete, measurable plan (three walks, calendar, sister accountability, Monday start, Wednesday risk plan). No drift occurs; every exchange serves the change goal.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The entire plan—walks, fridge calendar, sister check-ins—is the client's own creation, built on her ideas and past experience. Input is sought at every step, and the client experiences the collaboration as making change 'doable.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions are consistently framed as the client's, with options offered rather than prescriptions and no pressure or direction. The score falls short of 5 only because ownership of the change process is implied rather than explicitly reinforced.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation is drawn from within at every step—never argued for or supplied. The client generates her own insights, contingency plan, and readiness statement, with the clinician purely evoking, reflecting, and structuring.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise is explicitly located in the client from the opening question, and every follow-up builds on her contributions rather than steering. She functions as the source of solutions, driving the session's direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** A standout domain: open questions like 'which one feels most doable?' and 'what would make that first week feel like a success?' consistently elicit the client's own commitment, confidence, and self-efficacy language. Change talk is reflected and amplified rather than supplied by the clinician.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Doubt is handled well—sustain talk is held without amplification and past failure is reframed productively. One double-sided reflection gives some airtime to the 'old pattern,' though it is effectively balanced.
