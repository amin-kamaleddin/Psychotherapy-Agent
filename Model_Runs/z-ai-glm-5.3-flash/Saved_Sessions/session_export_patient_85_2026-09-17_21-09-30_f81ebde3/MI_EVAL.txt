# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance with genuine, specific affirmations ('That's real self-awareness, Marco'). Falls short of a 5 due to occasional formulaic praise and no explicit normalization of setbacks.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including an excellent summary ('from feeling blindsided by the scale to having a plan, a partner, and a deadline'). Significantly undermined by the counselor speaking as the patient in first person, breaking the empathic frame.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains unwavering focus on the target behavior with progressively sequenced questions toward commitment and action, ending with concrete next steps. No topic drift anywhere in the session.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Marco's own ideas clearly shape the plan and the counselor builds on them with shared-investment language. The first-person role-confusion passage breaks the two-partner structure by having the counselor occupy both roles.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforces Marco's ownership—defers to his expertise, frames choices as his ('what feels right to start?'), and hands the follow-up agenda to the client. Excellent framing of imperfection ('one glass isn't six glasses').

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequently and skillfully draws motivation from within Marco, yielding strong client-generated reasons and confidence ('A 10'). Falls short only because the counselor briefly voiced motivation on Marco's behalf during the role-confusion passage.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some good client-expertise statements, but the counselor repeatedly speaks for Marco rather than handing the microphone back. When asked 'should I go gradual or rip the band-aid off?', he answered as the patient instead of offering information or reflecting the question back.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evokes and deepens change talk, eliciting rich commitment language ('I'm ready for this. Genuinely ready'). Serious flaw: the counselor generated change talk himself while role-playing the patient ('I think the juice has got to go').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Does not amplify sustain talk ('Boy, do I love that OJ though') but also does not use double-sided reflections or reframes to address it strategically. The counselor even voiced the ambivalence himself in the problematic first-person passage.
