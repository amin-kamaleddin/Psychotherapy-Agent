# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a neutral, non-judgmental stance with affirming phrases like 'That makes sense,' but missed a key opportunity to affirm Maya's strengths when she worried about being seen as a 'problem kid.' Warmth was generic rather than individualized, with no explicit unconditional positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offered several accurate reflections showing genuine understanding, but frequently followed them with multiple questions or option menus that diluted the sense of being deeply understood. Maya's fear of her father's disappointment was touched but not explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The session stayed loosely focused on the weekend incident and stress but never directly named alcohol use as the target behavior or asked about drinking patterns despite multiple natural openings. Focus drifted among stress, self-criticism, and coping without a consistent thread toward the referral concern.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided an authoritarian stance and asked for Maya's input, but relied on pre-defined option menus rather than co-constructing the session. Maya's own ideas were accommodated briefly before returning to structured techniques, with little genuine back-and-forth negotiation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently honored Maya's preferences, including when she declined texting her mom or setting boundaries, and reinforced her choices without pressure. The constant menus, while autonomy-supportive in form, felt somewhat formulaic and did not explicitly reinforce her ownership of change.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Relied heavily on psychoeducation, reframing techniques, and menu options rather than eliciting Maya's own motivations, values, and reasons for change. No open questions were used to draw out what matters to her, reflecting a telling rather than evocative orientation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices, conveying some respect for Maya's input, but often through pre-formatted menus rather than genuine co-construction. Her expertise about her own life was not explicitly honored as valuable knowledge.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Made little deliberate effort to evoke Maya's own change talk, focusing questions on processing the negative experience rather than eliciting hopes, goals, or reasons for change. Maya's spontaneous change-adjacent statements ('I know better') went unreflected and unamplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Did not reinforce Maya's sustain talk ('everyone goes out,' 'Jess drinks way more and she's fine'), which is positive, but also made no active effort to soften it through double-sided reflections or strategic pivots. The ambivalence about drinking was largely left unaddressed.
