# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently respected Kareem's stance ('I respect that' after 'I'm not gonna quit'), with no judgment or pressure toward cessation. Held below 5 due to somewhat formulaic warmth and limited explicit normalization of ambivalence.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflections accurately captured content and ambivalence, e.g., 'breaks that feel restful, not rushed or sneaky.' Emotional layers like fear of 'failing at not smoking' and dread of caving were not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintained clear focus and pulled consistently toward concrete action with implementation supports (timing, backups, scaling, barriers). Direction was firm on the proximal sub-goal (Monday walk) while avoiding the core target of smoking.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kareem negotiated and modified the plan (delay-10-minutes, indoor laps backup) and set his own success criteria. Collaboration was uneven at times, with the counselor proposing rather than eliciting and consolidating the plan in her own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was repeatedly affirmed ('you're in charge,' 'keeps control with you') and Kareem defined his own success criteria. Rapid-fire option menus and detailed plan prescriptions occasionally edged toward directing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong evocation of client-generated reasons and commitment language ('fixing my mornings, which I can get behind,' 'Fine. I'll do it.') via open questions, confidence scaling, and success-criteria elicitation. Deeper values related to broader change were not probed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opened with strong power-sharing, but the counselor increasingly supplied solutions — listing options, prescribing walk details, drafting texts — within a pre-set menu rather than eliciting them first.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effective use of evocative questions, confidence ruler, and success indicators produced genuine change talk ('maybe trying a walk wouldn't kill me'). Evocation focused on the workplace experiment rather than broader cessation change talk.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing and never reinforced sustain talk, but softening was largely by avoidance and validation. Double-sided reflections or strategic pivots to resolve ambivalence about quitting were not used.
