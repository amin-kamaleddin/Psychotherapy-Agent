# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout, honoring Daniel's frame and explicitly agreeing to 'no lecture' if the data show no change. Affirmations were brief and lacked deeper statements of unconditional regard beyond the plan.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate double-sided reflections captured both his view of drinking as normal and his motivation to train well, plus his beer-as-day's-done ritual. Reflections sometimes stayed at the content/plan level rather than deepening the emotional meaning of his nervousness about skipping beer.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on alcohol and its link to marathon training, with no topic drift. Every turn moved toward a concrete experiment: specific metrics, success criteria, start date, and one-week check-in.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-constructed: Daniel chose the experiment, defined success, picked swaps and start time, and specified check-in focus. The closing—reviewing notes together with curiosity and letting results guide next steps—epitomized equal-partner inquiry.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent, explicit choices throughout (focus, approach, start time, check-in timing), with a genuine concession to drop the topic if results show no change. No pressure or control language anywhere.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The experiment format drew out Daniel's own stakes and spontaneous insights, including that his nervousness about skipping two beers 'tells me something.' Evocation was partly incidental to the experiment design; no direct evocative questions about importance, confidence, or reasons were used.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong power-sharing: offered choices, asked Daniel to define success, start timing, and what to notice at check-in. The psychiatrist still provided the tracking list and research points quickly, and never explicitly named Daniel as the expert on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The self-experiment setup evoked notable change talk, including 'saying that number out loud' and his self-generated insight about nervousness. However, change talk was evoked indirectly rather than through direct evocative questions about reasons, importance, or confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged without amplification and neutralized by framing the experiment as low-stakes self-verification ('if not, we let it go'). Some reflections restated sustain talk without a strategic double-sided pivot each time.
