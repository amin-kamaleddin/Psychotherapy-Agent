# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a warm, nonjudgmental tone throughout, affirming sitting as valid participation. However, warmth feels somewhat formulaic, with repeated checklist recitations crowding out deeper individualized regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections show good empathy, but when Elias revealed his core shame about being 'the old man in the chair,' the psychiatrist responded with scripts rather than reflecting the shame and dignity theme. Responses to emotional disclosures often pivoted quickly to problem-solving.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session stays clearly focused on a concrete, measurable goal (Thursday group, 10 minutes) with confidence ratings tracking progress. Deduction for quietly abandoning the medication adherence component and repetitive end-of-session checklists.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some collaborative moments (offering options, incorporating Elias's ideas like the front seat), but the interaction was dominated by structured lists and scripts for Elias to execute. Closing checklist repetitions reduced Elias to a compliant recipient rather than a co-designer.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Choice is regularly supported, including flexible fallback plans and no coercion around activity or medications. Deduction because the plan was structured primarily by the clinician's checklist rather than by Elias, and autonomy wasn't explicitly reinforced around the medication decision.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effective use of confidence rulers and a barrier question that elicited Elias's self-generated insight about choice versus failure. However, the psychiatrist relied heavily on providing solutions and scripts rather than drawing out Elias's own resources, and motivational content (hope, Marisol, dignity) went unexplored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Elias's input was sought and his choices shaped the plan, but pre-packaged problem-solving lists and a somewhat expert-driven stance tempered true power sharing. When Elias named the shame barrier, the psychiatrist supplied scripts rather than co-creating a solution.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was well evoked and reinforced, with Elias moving from 'maybe that counts' to 'maybe I'm a 6' to expressed hopefulness. Minor deduction for not deepening value-based change talk around the Marisol memory.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without argument but largely sidestepped rather than actively softened—no double-sided reflections, and the medication 'lost cause' resistance was simply dropped. Reframing sitting as full participation partially defused the main barrier.
