# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warmth and unconditional regard maintained even through Maria's anger and skepticism, with genuine, well-timed affirmations. Score held at 4 because some affirmations feel slightly generic and the clinician occasionally frames things before Maria articulates them herself.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening, including an excellent validation-first response to her feeling unheard, and accurate reflection of ambivalence. Some reflections are slight reframes rather than pure reflections, and moments of intense despair were sometimes moved past rather than fully absorbed.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, clinically appropriate focus on surviving tonight and reconnecting next week, with every turn tied to that target. Exemplary directionality for a high-acuity suicidality session, producing a concrete, client-owned plan.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The interaction is genuinely two-way, with Maria's ideas (memes, nightstand, small texts) directly shaping the safety plan. Held at 4 because the clinician introduced the safety-planning agenda themselves rather than fully co-creating it from the start.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy is consistently reinforced — 'no pressure,' 'thinking about it is a real answer, and it's yours' — with terms of reach-out negotiated on her conditions and nothing coercive. Maria's own words ('It's small. But it's mine') confirm the clinician's success.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation is effectively drawn from within, eliciting her brother's meaning, her deal-keeping value, and her own criteria for reaching out. Not a 5 because several evocative questions are variations of the same prompt, and deeper exploration of reasons for living beyond her brother was missed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maria is consistently treated as the expert, and her contributions substantially shape the safety plan — a hallmark of power sharing. The clinician occasionally pre-shapes reflections and introduces the agenda rather than fully co-developing it.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully spots and amplifies sparse change talk (the 'tiny wish,' her brother), and Maria's commitments grow organically into a self-generated three-point plan. Evocation is effective but partly reactive; more strategic exploration of her reasons before offering safety planning would reach a 5.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('Nothing works,' 'I can't take this much longer') is met with validation and gentle redirection toward her ambivalence without dismissing despair. Redirections sometimes come quickly, and a brief double-sided reflection acknowledging both sides verbatim could have deepened resolution.
