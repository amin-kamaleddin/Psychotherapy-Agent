# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** 1. A consistently respectful and supportive tone was maintained with no judgment of Richard's past failed attempts or minimization. 2. However, affirmations were somewhat formulaic and lacked individually tailored acknowledgment of Richard's strengths, such as his self-awareness about making excuses.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** 1. Accurate reflective listening captured Richard's ambivalence well, particularly his retirement motivation and doubts about pills and diet. 2. Reflections occasionally stayed at surface content and missed opportunities to deepen emotional nuance, such as his fear of a heart attack derailing plans or shame about downplaying symptoms at doctor visits.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** 1. Clear, consistent focus was maintained on medication adherence and lifestyle change throughout, with no topic drift. 2. Every turn advanced the agenda and closed with a forward-moving question that guided toward change while remaining client-centered.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** 1. The session was largely a joint endeavor, with menus of options offered and plans co-developed, including the walking schedule and doctor script. 2. At times the counselor supplied most of the plan content, functioning more as a knowledgeable advisor than fully eliciting Richard's own solutions first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** 1. Choice was consistently affirmed, with options offered rather than directives, and Richard's ownership was evident. 2. Some responses bundled strong advice with only brief invitations, slightly narrowing Richard's perceived latitude to choose differently.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** 1. Motivation was skillfully drawn from Richard's own values, with the opening question eliciting the retirement vision that anchored the entire session. 2. Mid-session opportunities to deepen emerging confidence, such as Richard's remark that falling lab numbers would keep him going, were not fully explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** 1. Richard was consistently positioned as the decision-maker, and his contributions substantially shaped the session, including the script style and walking schedule. 2. Occasional drift toward prescriptive solutions, such as specific times and backup plans, slightly undermined the fully equal partnership.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** 1. Open questions and choice invitations successfully elicited strong commitment language, including 'I'll take the medicine' and 'First pill Tuesday morning.' 2. Reinforcement of change talk relied more on information and advice than deliberate MI strategies such as scaling questions or elaboration of confidence.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** 1. Sustain talk was never amplified, but minimization and excuses were rarely met with strategic double-sided reflections that pivoted toward change. 2. Redirection occurred implicitly through forward-moving questions rather than explicit softening techniques.
