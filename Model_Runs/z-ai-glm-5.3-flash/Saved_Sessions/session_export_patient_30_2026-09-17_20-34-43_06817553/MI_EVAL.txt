# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with no judgment despite the client's skepticism; warm affirmations like 'that's a real shift, David' and 'I'm rooting for you' made him feel fully accepted.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate reflective listening captured both sides of ambivalence, the gym-failure fear, and his autonomy needs; the client confirmed reflections repeatedly ('you just said my whole plan back to me better than I did').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, purposeful focus on cholesterol management throughout, building progressively from exploring necessity to a concrete self-generated plan with no meaningful drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely collaborative with all plan elements originating from the client, but the counselor's occasional rhetorical framing and long summaries slightly limited two-way exchange.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and personal agency were reinforced constantly, never pressuring toward medication; the client's closing 'That feels like my call, not just the doctor's' directly evidences perceived autonomy.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn entirely from the client through open questions with no prescriptive advice; his own words ('Guess I just talked myself into a plan without noticing') demonstrate effective evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor explicitly positioned the client as the expert and credited him with his own solutions ('You just solved your own doctor problem'), with all plan elements emerging from his own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked and amplified, shifting dramatically from 'Okay, sure. Fine' to concrete commitments like booking the appointment and the comeback rule.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged via double-sided reflections and pivots to agency, though residual medication fears ('I still hate that') were not explicitly reframed beyond deferring to the retest.
