# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and offered genuine, specific affirmations with a defensive, mandated teen. However, warmth felt somewhat formulaic and Milan's strengths (grades, soccer, honesty) were never explicitly affirmed.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captured Milan's core grievance about being singled out and unfairly distrusted. Reflections tended to be summary-style and cognitive, rarely touching the hurt beneath his defensiveness.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** After a competent alcohol assessment, the counselor fully followed Milan's reframing of the problem as his dad, and the referral concern was effectively lost. The dad work was never linked back to drinking or bridged to future alcohol-focused discussion.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Genuine co-construction was evident—permission-seeking, option-offering, and Milan's contributions shaping the final script. The advice-heavy middle section, where the counselor generated most content, held this back.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offered choice, avoided pressure, and Milan visibly engaged—even negotiating script content himself. Fell short because the counselor never explicitly reinforced Milan's ownership or self-direction capacity.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked Milan's motivations about the father relationship (trust, fairness, practical agreements). Motivation for the clinical target of reducing alcohol risk was never evoked, nor was the discrepancy with his stated values explored.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered Milan choices but over-compensated by supplying expert content—scripts, frameworks, closing lines—with Milan in the student role. A stronger partnership would have drawn out Milan's own ideas before providing scripts.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Effectively evoked Milan's proactive language about the father relationship, but change talk around alcohol itself was minimal. After his 2/10 concern rating, the drinking topic was dropped rather than developed using rulers or discrepancy questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor never reinforced Milan's sustain talk ('it's not that bad,' 'I'm not worried'), but also did not soften, reframe, or use double-sided reflections to address it. Abandoning the drinking topic after the low concern rating effectively let sustain talk stand unexamined.
