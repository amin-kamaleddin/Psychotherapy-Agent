# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, validating Isabelle's frustration about being flagged. However, affirmations were sparse and somewhat formulaic, missing chances to affirm deeper qualities like her honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate but stayed at surface level and grew repetitive. Deeper emotional content—fear of her mother's reaction, feeling punished for honesty—was acknowledged but never explored for underlying meaning.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session drifted from the alcohol-use referral into logistics about the school's flagging, email drafting, and parent notification. Disclosures about drinking were followed up once then dropped, with menu options left unpursued.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early collaboration was genuine, with Isabelle shaping the agenda. Later, the counselor took over by producing email drafts and scripts rather than building from Isabelle's own words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor's strongest domain—choice was explicitly reinforced throughout ('You're in charge'), refusals were honored, and every turn offered options with permission to decline.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Ideas and motivation came almost entirely from the counselor via scripts and menu options. No questions evoked Isabelle's own values, concerns, or motivations regarding alcohol or any change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Collaborative framing and follow-the-client pacing were present early, but the counselor slipped into an expert/script-writer role in the second half, under-recognizing Isabelle's expertise about her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** No effort to evoke change talk about the target behavior of alcohol use—no questions about her goals, concerns, or desire regarding drinking. The only 'change' discussed was communication logistics with the school.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('I know my limits,' 'I'm not stupid about it') was not only unaddressed but actively reinforced—e.g., affirming she was 'responsible' and 'careful at parties'—without any double-sided reflections or gentle pivots toward the flagged behavior.
