# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistent warmth, non-judgment, and respectful confidentiality handling, with affirmations like 'Thanks for telling me — that takes courage.' Affirmations were somewhat generic and not individualized to Marcus's deeper values.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening that captured Marcus's perspective and validated his skepticism. Reflections occasionally stayed surface-level, missing emotional content in the DUI story and peer-approval pressure.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Session maintained consistent focus on concrete harm-reduction behaviors, redirecting past sarcasm without lecturing. Closed with a clear summary that produced explicit commitment ('I'll try the ride thing this weekend').

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Largely equal footing: menus offered rather than directives, and Marcus shaped the plan's specifics. Clinician-driven question barrages (four in the opening) and pre-packed suggestion lists tilted the power balance somewhat.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Standout strength: explicit choice support ('I'm here to support whatever choice you make'), acceptance of minimal commitment, and willingness to drop tools Marcus disliked. Marcus's closing 'way less preachy than I thought' confirms genuine choice.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from Marcus's own safety reasoning, plan structure, and confidence rating. Evocation centered on harm-reduction logistics rather than exploring deeper values or personal 'why' language.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was built almost entirely from Marcus's own ideas (ride check, walk fallback, cousin backup), honoring his expertise. Periodic unsolicited suggestion lists temporarily shifted the expert role back to the clinician.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evoked and reinforced Marcus's change language (confidence 8, 'Yeah, I can do that') and reflected his self-generated plan. Focus stayed on preparation/action talk rather than deeper personal reasons for change.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged but not strategically softened or pivoted — suggestion lists implicitly accepted the 'it's working' framing, and 'you sound in control' risked reinforcing minimization. More double-sided reflections tied to Marcus's values (e.g., the DUI story) were needed.
