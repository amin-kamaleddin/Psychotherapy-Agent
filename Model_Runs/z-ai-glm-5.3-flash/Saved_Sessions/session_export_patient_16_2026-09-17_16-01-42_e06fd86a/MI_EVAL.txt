# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance with affirmations ('Taking this step already shows strength') and normalization of slips without shame. Some affirmations felt formulaic and the opening was slightly assumptive.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate complex reflections captured Anika's ambivalence well and validated her perspective. Reflections sometimes shifted quickly into questions, and stress, family conflict, and perfectionism were not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent, unwavering focus on the target behavior with no topic drift. Session progressed coherently from assessment through readiness, planning, relapse prevention, and follow-up scheduling.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Options were offered, Anika chose her goal, and her wording was incorporated into the plan. However, the dynamic was often counselor-proposes/client-picks rather than jointly generating strategies, keeping Anika somewhat reactive.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offered choices and respected Anika's preferences without pressure or shaming. Autonomy was occasionally undercut by prescriptive plans, techniques, and the early 'avoid getting caught' framing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently drew motivation from Anika via rulers, her own alternatives, and refined refusal lines, with visible strengthening of commitment. At times supplied expert solutions instead of further eliciting her ideas for managing stress.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Invited Anika's input and built the plan from her own words. However, often slipped into an expert, prescriptive mode with ready-made plans and techniques before Anika generated her own solutions.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively used readiness and confidence rulers, open questions, and refusal practice to elicit strong change talk ('I want to play well,' 'Seven feels more like an eight now'). Relied heavily on structured scaling and menus rather than organic values exploration.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged without endorsement, but given considerable airtime. The early offer to 'avoid getting caught' reinforced minimization, and redirection toward change was only partial and inconsistent.
