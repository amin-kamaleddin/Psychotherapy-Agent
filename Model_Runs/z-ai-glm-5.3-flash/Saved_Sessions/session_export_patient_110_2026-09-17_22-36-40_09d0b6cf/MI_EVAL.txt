# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent warmth and unconditional positive regard throughout, with no judgment of Richard's minimization. The closing invitation to author his own change sentence communicates deep respect for him as the driver of his own change.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Highly accurate complex reflections captured both sides of ambivalence and the emotional core of his concerns about side effects. The counselor also reflected Richard's own word 'lighter,' showing deep attunement to his emotional process.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on starting cholesterol medication through a deliberate arc from ambivalence to concrete planning and commitment consolidation. Each turn advanced the trajectory without drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuine dialogue between equals, with the plan co-created from Richard's own ideas rather than imposed. His comment 'You let me get there myself' directly evidences experienced collaboration.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Autonomy consistently reinforced ('You're the one who knows your body and your life best'), with choices offered and no persuading or pressuring. Richard explicitly confirmed his decision was his own, not fear-driven.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from within — retirement, hiking with his wife, 'too much left I want to do' — through open questions and affirmation. The session culminated in a self-authored, values-based commitment sentence, textbook evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise explicitly located with the client, and the plan (doctor first, phone notes, wife's involvement) originated entirely from Richard. His thanks for 'not just telling me what to do' confirms power-sharing was experienced.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Open questions skillfully evoked commitment language ('Yeah, I'll take the medicine'), which was then amplified and developed into a self-generated commitment statement. Richard's own reflection that 'it's sticking' confirms effectiveness.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged non-judgmentally and handled well with double-sided reflections, without reinforcement. The one area for refinement is giving sustain talk fairly full reflective airtime rather than pivoting toward change earlier.
