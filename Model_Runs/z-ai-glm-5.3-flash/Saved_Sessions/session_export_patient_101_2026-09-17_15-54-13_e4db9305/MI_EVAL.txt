# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and affirming ('nice work,' 'good call'), with a warm, judgment-free opening. Affirmations are somewhat brief and formulaic, lacking deeper unconditional regard for Mei as a person.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some accurate reflections (importance 8/confidence 4, IUD rationale), but reflections are thin and quickly replaced by advice lists. Deeper emotions—fear of becoming like family, shame about 'drunk-me,' social anxiety—go unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains clear focus on alcohol reduction and contraceptive reliability, steering toward concrete steps (specific nights, decline line, IUD plan). Minor drift into detailed contraceptive risk counseling.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans are largely co-created, with Mei's choices (Dana as check-in, exit time, decline line) shaping the plan. Advice-heavy segments lean expert-driven, but the psychiatrist invites Mei's selection at nearly every turn.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently offers choice and positions Mei as the decision-maker ('Which would you like to focus on first?', 'Pick one or tell me how to tweak'). More explicit reinforcement of her ownership of the change process would earn a 5.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Importance/confidence scaling drew out Mei's own motivations, but much of the session consists of supplied ideas and scripts rather than elicited ones. Confidence building is largely absent despite her rating of 4.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Mei is asked which options she prefers, but the interaction frequently shifts into an expert stance with unsolicited bulleted advice lists. Mei's own knowledge is rarely positioned as the primary resource.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions evoked change talk ('I really don't want to go down that path') and commitment-adjacent language. Little deepening occurs—no elaboration questions or reflection of Mei's hopefulness and emerging momentum.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is not amplified and doubts are met with supportive pivots to tactics rather than argument. However, sustain talk is bypassed rather than strategically softened with double-sided reflections, and confidence is never revisited.
