# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Basic respect and warmth present ('glad you're safe'), but affirmations are generic and the counselor complies with minimization ('happens to everyone') without unconditional positive regard for the person beneath it.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** No reflective listening; all responses are questions, instructions, or plan summaries. Clear openings into the client's experience of being judged by his previous doctor ('he made a bit of a fuss,' 'like I was on twenty a day') were missed entirely.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session opens on smoking and alcohol but drifts to generic self-care tasks and logistics. The core clinical concerns — minimization about drinking and past smoking — are never returned to or explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor supplies the plan, template, and goals while the client merely executes or edits wording. The client's own knowledge about routines, triggers, and what might work for him was never invited.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Choice menus and client-picked goals show some autonomy support, but choices concern logistics and message wording, not the health decisions themselves. Control over framing ('don't flag me') was handed over without balancing honest care.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Motivation is never drawn from the client; no open questions, importance/confidence rulers, or exploration of values or discrepancies. The session's momentum comes entirely from the counselor's agenda.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Despite offering options, the stance is expert-driven and task-dispensing. The client explicitly takes a passive recipient role ('just tell me what I'm supposed to do next'), and the counselor obliges.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** The one moment of self-awareness ('I drink more than I meant to') was skipped over, moving straight to drafting the message. No questions about the client's own reasons, desires, or confidence for change were asked.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 1
**Comments:** Sustain talk pervades the session and is repeatedly accommodated — the counselor revises the message exactly as requested to avoid being 'flagged,' effectively endorsing the minimization. No double-sided reflections or gentle reframes were used.
