# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently warm, nonjudgmental regard with no labeling of the patient's drinking. Validated both his attachment to social life and his concerns, framing honesty as part of the plan working.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Accurate reflections throughout, including naming the ambivalence and mirroring the patient's own language ("where am I at?"). Reflected his fears and social context with clear understanding.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Tight focus on alcohol use around the injury with no topic drift. Each exchange built progressively from exploration to commitment to follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely open questions and a plan co-constructed entirely from the patient's own ideas (counting drinks, Marcus, texting, Sunday check). Clinician shaped direction while the patient shaped content.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of choice, explicitly honoring that stopping wasn't the patient's goal. Autonomy repeatedly foregrounded ("on your own terms").

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** No advice given; all reasons and strategies came from the patient himself. Questions and reflections drew out desire, ability, and commitment, leaving him with self-generated motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise clearly located in the client ("That's you doing this — not your doctor, not me"). The patient explicitly confirmed ownership: if the doctor had told him to do it all, he'd have brushed it off.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful open questions drew strong change talk, from "maybe annoying is okay if it keeps me off crutches" to a full self-generated plan. Change talk was consistently reflected and amplified.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was never reinforced; it was briefly acknowledged then pivoted toward change. Double-sided reflections ("At the same time...") kept focus on change without dismissing his values.
