# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance throughout, handling the confidentiality question honestly without shaming and thanking Lukas for his disclosure. However, affirmations were generic and rarely affirmed deeper personal qualities like his loyalty or honesty.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Efforts to understand were present but relied heavily on questions, checklists, and advice rather than reflective listening. Emotional disclosures such as fear of peer judgment and loyalty to friends were met with practical steps instead of reflections, leaving understanding implied rather than demonstrated.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent, consistent focus on the target behavior, linking drinking to athletic and academic performance throughout the session. The session never wandered and ended with a clear, time-bound plan including a one-week follow-up.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Good collaborative moments existed, such as jointly filling out the log entry and letting Lukas choose the experiment, but the counselor often proposed batches of solutions for Lukas to accept or reject. The power balance tilted toward the counselor until Lukas asserted himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Autonomy was regularly supported by offering options, honoring Lukas's choices (phone notes, rejecting refusal scripts and driver duty), and accepting his boundary of no promises past three weeks. A dense menu of tactics and a pre-scripted reminder message slightly diluted full ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The data-driven self-discovery approach was well-matched to this skeptical teen and produced authentic change talk, but evocative questioning was sparse. The counselor rarely asked open questions about Lukas's values, motivations, or confidence, so motivation emerged largely despite rather than because of skillful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor positioned Lukas's own data and judgment as central, which Lukas responded well to. However, frequent defaults to advice-list formats edged into expert-driven territory, and the approach only became genuinely client-led after Lukas pushed back.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Moderately supported change talk, notably through the data-tracking experiment that let Lukas discover his own motivation and his self-motivated goals around season and GPA. Missed opportunities to deepen commitment language included elaborating on what the season meant to him and using confidence/importance scaling.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor avoided arguing and did not reinforce Lukas's sustain talk about not being addicted or bailing on friends. However, no double-sided reflections or affirmations were used to soften it, with redirection being passive rather than strategically skillful.
