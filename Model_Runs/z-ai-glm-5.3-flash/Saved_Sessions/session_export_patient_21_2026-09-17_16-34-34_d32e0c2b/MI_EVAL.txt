# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and warm with no judgment or labeling; however, affirmations were somewhat generic and could have more specifically recognized Camila's individual strengths (e.g., her self-awareness and consideration for her partner).

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening and accurate summaries that captured Camila's goals and key points. Missed opportunities to deepen emotional reflection, such as the meaning of shared time with her partner or her fear of a 'no' derailing the plan.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exceptionally clear focus on increasing physical activity throughout, with every exchange tied to the concrete plan (days, timing, duration, backup, support, follow-up signals). No topic drift; each turn progressed toward a specific, achievable change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Plans were genuinely co-constructed with Camila's input shaping days, backups, wording, and celebrations. The counselor occasionally supplied content she hadn't asked for (scripted texts, reward tips), slightly reducing pure co-creation.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently reinforced ownership through choices and fully respected Camila's decisions, including declining role-play. Camila's closing statement that the plan 'feels like mine' is the strongest evidence of effective autonomy support.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively drew motivation from within, with Camila generating her own reasons and self-affirming ('I'll believe that 8 is possible'). Deductions for rapid question clusters and occasionally supplying ideas before she fully explored her own.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Camila was consistently positioned as the expert, and her choices substantively shaped the plan — 'you let me find my own way to this' directly evidences power sharing. Occasional leading suggestions (three scripted texts) kept this from a 5.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and reinforced change talk through values exploration, scaling questions, and 'what would move the number up' prompts, culminating in Camila saying 'now I actually want to try this.' Somewhat mechanical reliance on scaling questions.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflected sustain talk neutrally and pivoted to strengths ('you're not starting from zero'), with the low-energy backup plan effectively softening barriers. Some pivots were quick without fully acknowledging the legitimacy of the struggle.
