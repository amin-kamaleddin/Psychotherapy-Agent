# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintained a nonjudgmental, neutral stance and never labeled or criticized Jason's drinking. However, warmth was minimal and there were no affirmations of his strengths or honest self-disclosures.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Early reflections accurately captured Jason's frustration and dual narrative of feeling singled out while wanting to seem responsible. Empathy grew shallow once the session shifted to hearing rehearsal, with embarrassment and his admission of heavy drinking left unexplored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The session stayed narrowly focused but on the wrong target: conduct-hearing impression management rather than high-risk drinking. The counselor complied when Jason asked to be done with the alcohol topic, so the focus served legal self-presentation, not therapeutic change.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Surface features of collaboration were present through option menus and preference checks. Yet the counselor supplied the agenda and scripts while Jason merely selected among them, with no genuine co-construction around his drinking or goals.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor consistently offered choices and avoided coercive language about drinking. However, autonomy support was undermined by orienting guidance toward minimizing consequences rather than supporting self-determined decision-making about alcohol.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was treated as residing in the counselor's scripts rather than within Jason. The counselor never asked about his values, goals, or how drinking fit his desired identity, and his admission of getting 'pretty hammered' was met with another script instead of an evocative question.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Repeated menu-based choices conveyed some power-sharing, but the counselor assumed the expert role by supplying scripted statements and delivery tips. Jason's own expertise about his drinking and social life was never leveraged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor made almost no effort to evoke or reinforce change talk, leaving change-relevant content largely patient-initiated. The rare moment of openness about heavy weekend drinking was not explored or reflected, and questions targeted hearing logistics rather than motivation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor frequently accommodated and even amplified sustain talk, scripting denials like 'I don't believe I have a substance problem' as impression management. There were no double-sided reflections or strategic pivots toward change, and the counselor complied when Jason deflected the alcohol topic.
