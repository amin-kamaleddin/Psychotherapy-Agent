# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unconditional positive regard was consistent throughout, with normalizing statements ('it's not dumb — it's human') and no judgment despite the compliance-oriented referral context. Acceptance was conveyed independent of outcomes.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were accurate and emotionally attuned, including double-sided reflections ('You're torn between your team and your game, and both make sense'). The counselor also caught and amplified the client's own pivotal statement ('why am I even messing with that?').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Unwavering focus on the target behavior while honoring belonging concerns; each exchange advanced a concrete agenda (high-risk moment, driving duty, scripted responses, recruiting Marcus, follow-up). No aimless drifting.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session functioned as genuine joint problem-solving, with the relapse-prevention plan built interactively from the client's own ideas and a client-initiated follow-up agreed upon. No prescriptions were offered.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy language throughout ('if it were totally up to you', 'your plan'); all strategies originated from the client, with no advice-giving or pressure. The client's choice to defer involving his mother was respected.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn almost entirely from within the client via open questions eliciting his own reasons, strategies, and anticipated self-image, with change talk reflected back rather than supplied. The client left with self-generated motivation and a plan.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Expertise was explicitly located in the client ('You're the expert on your team'), with the plan repeatedly credited to him ('you built this whole thing yourself'). Client contributions directly shaped session direction.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Skillful evocation via questions about teammates who sidestepped games, worth ('what would it be worth to keep both?'), and anticipated outcomes, producing strong client change talk ('It's worth a lot', 'I'd feel good', 'saying it out loud makes it feel more doable').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk was not reinforced, and effective double-sided reflections held ambivalence without amplifying the against-change side. Minor deduction: early probes somewhat directly invited sustain talk (e.g., 'it feels normal') before pivoting.
