# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently treated Linda with respect and warmth, affirming her persistence ('You've shown persistence trying patches, gum, cold turkey') with no judgmental language even around past failures. Warmth was occasionally formulaic and affirmations not deeply individualized, keeping it from a 5.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening throughout, accurately capturing Linda's ambivalence and normalizing her reluctance ('It's normal to feel nervous and to protect yourself from another big promise'). Missed opportunities to reflect more deeply on the grief of losing a thirty-year social ritual and the emotional toll of repeated failures limited the score.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, consistent focus on smoking cessation from the opening question through the closing plan, with every exchange tied back to the target behavior. There was no meaningful topic drift, and the session built momentum toward a specific, measurable commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The session felt like joint problem-solving with shared agenda decisions and a plan co-constructed from Linda's own idea ('Maybe a smaller step first?'). The initial menu of interventions and scripts was counselor-generated rather than co-created, which prevented a 5.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with repeated permission-asking, choice menus, and acceptance of Linda's pace, including protecting her from pressure ('If you don't say it or step back, that's still useful'). A slight deduction because multi-item advice lists (medications, strategies) leaned expert-directed rather than purely choice-focused.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Motivation was drawn from Linda herself through rulers, questions about past successes, and a self-generated commitment, producing change talk like 'maybe I can do this.' The score fell short because the psychiatrist periodically supplied reasons and solutions rather than consistently evoking and expanding Linda's own.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The psychiatrist asked permission before offering options and let Linda choose scripts and strategies, supporting partnership. However, much of the session was expert-driven content delivery, with scripts provided rather than elicited and little explicit acknowledgment that expertise resides within Linda.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Change talk was actively elicited via importance/confidence rulers and evocative questions, leading Linda to voice motivation and a concrete commitment, with wins reinforced to strengthen self-efficacy. The psychiatrist sometimes answered his own evocative questions by moving quickly to options, limiting deeper development of Linda's own reasons.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk was acknowledged neutrally without reinforcement and pivots toward small steps were made, but there was little use of double-sided reflections or strategic reframing. The six-month timeline was never explicitly examined or gently challenged toward a nearer step — Linda raised the smaller step herself.
