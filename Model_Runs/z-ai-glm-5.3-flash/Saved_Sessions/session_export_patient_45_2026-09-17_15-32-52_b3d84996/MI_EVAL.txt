# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicated unconditional positive regard, warmly reframing the mandated visit and avoiding defensiveness when tested. Affirmed Erik's autonomy throughout, including accepting his tentative stance on alcohol without pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Good reflective listening captured nuances like ambivalence about pills and the barrier of shift work. Reflections occasionally stayed at content level, e.g., the emotional sting of being labelled 'a sick person' was not deeply explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintained excellent focus, offering a clear choice question to narrow a diffuse opening and building progressively to a specific, locked-in commitment with if-then planning. The session moved purposefully toward change throughout.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Behaved largely as an equal partner, co-constructing plans around Erik's schedule and ideas. However, occasionally slipped into expert advisory mode, such as the dense lifestyle bullet list that drew pushback ('Blimey, that's a lot at once').

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Offered choices at every decision point and accommodated Erik's preferences, such as rejecting formal tracking and not writing the alcohol change in stone. Framing around the pills directly restored Erik's sense of agency.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully drew motivation from within, using open questions, a confidence ruler, and obstacle planning to elicit Erik's own reasons and strategies. Sometimes answered direct questions with information rather than first exploring Erik's own knowledge or the personal 'why' behind change.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positioned Erik as the expert, and he explicitly noted the power-sharing felt genuine ('Didn't expect you to actually ask what I want'). Score limited by occasional pre-packaged option menus rather than open elicitation of his own ideas.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully evoked and developed change talk through choice questions and confidence scaling, producing self-generated commitments (walking, tea swap, weekend-only beers). A 5 would require deeper elaboration of the change talk, e.g., why the walking mattered to him.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Handled sustain talk effectively without argument, acknowledging Erik's minimization neutrally and pivoting to his chosen focus. Minor weakness: briefly listing alcohol's effects in the advice step risked triggering resistance, though Erik responded well.
