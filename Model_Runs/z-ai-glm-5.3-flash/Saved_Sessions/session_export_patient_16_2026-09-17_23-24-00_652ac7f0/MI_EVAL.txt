# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist maintained unconditional positive regard from the start ('this space is yours, and there's nothing you have to perform here'), disclaimed lecturing, and met every sustain statement with warmth and curiosity rather than judgment or correction.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections were highly accurate, capturing the frustration with the referral, the function of vaping, ambivalence via a double-sided reflection, and process-level shifts ('You've moved from "I could pause it" to "this is an actual decision"').

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session kept a clear, purposeful focus on vaping and progressed logically from exploration through change talk to a concrete commitment plan, without topic drift or pushiness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The counselor asked permission, built on the client's ideas, and co-constructed next steps through questions rather than telling; the client's answers demonstrably shaped the session's direction.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Client ownership was explicitly reinforced throughout ('That's your plan, not mine,' 'You're the expert'), with permission sought before exploring and no plan content suggested by the counselor.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** All motivation came from the client — her own reasons, plan, confidence rating, barrier, and solution — with no unsolicited advice or persuasive arguments from the psychiatrist.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist repeatedly located expertise in the client ('Since you're the expert on what actually chills you out'), and the entire plan originated from the client and was built upon rather than prescribed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Change talk was skillfully evoked through future visualization, weighing questions, and confidence scaling ('what would make the difference between a seven and a nine?'), each deepening commitment, ability, and taking-stock language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk was consistently acknowledged briefly and redirected toward the client's own change-oriented reasoning, never argued with; the client's language steadily shifted toward commitment.
