# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent unconditional positive regard with affirmations like 'that's strong' and 'you've done the hard work,' no judgment about violence history or drinking, and warm, respectful tone including 'Kia ora' throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Opening reflections accurately captured core ambivalence about change versus independence and the Tristan/whānau tension. However, reflections were somewhat repetitive and deeper emotional layers like shame and fear of his mother's judgment were touched but not fully explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained clear, consistent focus on alcohol reduction, violence prevention, and housing, with every turn advancing the agenda toward a concrete written plan and commitment. No topic drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor framed work as joint ('draft it together') and the plan genuinely incorporated Wiremu's own ideas, but much content arrived as pre-formed counselor solutions he adopted rather than co-created from scratch.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice was reinforced throughout via menu-based questions ('What would you most like to focus on?'), the counselor declined to call Mum for him, and honored his decision to stop rehearsing and speak straight.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Scaling questions effectively elicited his reasons (kids as anchor), but much of the session was advice-giving, with scripts, slip protocol, and checklist content supplied by the counselor rather than drawn from the client. His own reasons for sobriety and solutions deserved more evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Wiremu shaped plan details (board amount, chores, safe destination) and noted 'You helped me do it my way,' but the counselor frequently slipped into expert mode with prescriptive scripts and step-by-step plans delivered with limited negotiation.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Frequent use of readiness/confidence scaling and hypotheticals evoked strong change talk ('I'd do anything to get them back,' confidence rising to eight). However, evoked talk centered mostly on logistics; desire/need talk linking his children-values to his goals could have been deepened.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk about independence and Tristan was acknowledged without amplification and reframed constructively via the trial-stay option that protected autonomy. Minor issue: the pros/cons suggestion risked reinforcing cons, but was quickly pivoted to a solution-oriented trial.
