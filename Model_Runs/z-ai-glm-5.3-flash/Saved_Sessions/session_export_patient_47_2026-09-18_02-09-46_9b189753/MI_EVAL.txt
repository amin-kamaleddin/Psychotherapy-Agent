# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently took the patient's perspective seriously, exploring her views about her drinking without judgment and validating her autonomy throughout.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Multiple accurate reflective statements demonstrated deep understanding of the patient's social connections, fears about isolation, and concern for her son.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation maintained a clear focus on reducing alcohol consumption, with the psychiatrist gently steering away from minimization while honoring the patient's goals.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The plan emerged entirely from the patient's own ideas, with the psychiatrist asking open questions rather than prescribing solutions, exemplifying true partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently emphasized 'your own terms,' 'your own plan,' and supported the patient's self-directed strategies including the handbag note and check-in format.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient's own motivations (her son's worry, her fall, staying steady) were drawn out through open questions rather than imposed, with the change talk clearly self-generated.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The patient explicitly framed the relationship as 'a partner, not a headmaster,' and the psychiatrist responded in kind, reinforcing a non-authoritarian stance.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The psychiatrist skillfully reinforced change talk by reflecting commitments ('that's you finding your own middle ground') and eliciting specifics about upcoming challenges.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (minimization, 'not a big drinker') was acknowledged and reflected rather than confronted, allowing the patient to move through ambivalence at her own pace.
