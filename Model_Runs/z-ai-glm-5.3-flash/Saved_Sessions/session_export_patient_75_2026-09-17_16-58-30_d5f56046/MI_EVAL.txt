# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone, validating Linda's fears without criticism ('That worry makes total sense, Linda — not dumb at all'). Warmth is somewhat functional and checklist-like, quickly pivoting to scripts and tasks rather than dwelling on her emotional experience.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens with a strong accurate complex reflection capturing Linda's ambivalence between freedom and worry. Later disclosures—her history of quitting alone, feeling dismissed by doctors, and exhaustion—receive minimal reflective response, with information delivery replacing deeper emotional reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session maintains clear focus on consistent inhaler use and preparing for a medical appointment, with every exchange tying back to the target behavior ending in a concrete behavioral plan. Slight drift into extensive script detail and option lists adds procedural bulk without advancing motivation.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Action plan is genuinely co-constructed from Linda's own ideas (test run, toothbrush cue, bringing her husband), and her input visibly shapes the session. The counselor sometimes slides into advice-dispensing with pre-formed scripts and checklists rather than fully co-creating wording.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strongest domain—choice is explicitly handed to Linda at nearly every step, and medication use is framed as a client-chosen 'test run' with a reassessment window rather than a permanent obligation. The final plan is assembled entirely from Linda's own stated preferences.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Opens well by eliciting Linda's values-based motivations (walking Max, hiking with her husband). The second half relies on providing information and scripts rather than drawing out motivation—her powerful statements of readiness and self-awareness are affirmed but never explored or deepened.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently treats Linda as the expert, repeatedly asking what she'd like to focus on and following her choices each time. Some exchanges lean toward dispensing pre-packaged scripts rather than co-creating language, though Linda adapts them into her own words.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Creates multiple opportunities for change talk and responds well when it emerges, reflecting and building on Linda's 'exit ramp' statement and affirming her spoken commitment ('I want to try this', 'test run'). Could have deepened commitment language further through confidence scaling or exploring what success would look like.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Reframes dependency fears effectively with factual information and highlights uncontrolled asthma as the greater risk, prompting Linda's 'I didn't realize' shift. Her repeated self-defeating pattern of quitting on her own is glossed rather than explored, and more double-sided reflections linking her worries to her values would have softened ambivalence more strategically.
