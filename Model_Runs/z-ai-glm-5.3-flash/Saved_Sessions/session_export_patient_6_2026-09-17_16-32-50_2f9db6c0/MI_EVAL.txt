# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, nonjudgmental stance throughout; never argues with Alexei's minimization and affirms his responsibility. Regard is somewhat brief and formulaic, limiting depth of positive regard.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurate early reflections, but the counselor shifts quickly to coaching and skips opportunities to deepen emotional content (fear of disappointing his mom, anxiety about the incident). Later turns often jump to options without reflection.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Clear, consistent focus on preventing underage drinking in service of soccer and scholarship goals. Every turn ties back to the target behavior with progressive skill-building toward commitment.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Coach-like collaborative tone with role-play and genuine incorporation of Alexei's ideas. The counselor still generates most options and scripts rather than co-creating them from Alexei's words.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistent reinforcement of choice ('pick one you'd use', 'which do you want to try first?'), and Alexei explicitly owns his decisions ('it's my choice'). No coercive or directive language appears.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively draws on Alexei's own values (soccer, scholarships, mom's trust) via rulers and open questions. Somewhat narrow, leaning on prescriptive option lists rather than sustained exploration of deeper motivations.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly hands decision-making to Alexei, whose contributions genuinely shape the session and plan. Counselor-led option menus limit full power-sharing.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reinforcement of change talk via confidence rulers and goal framing, eliciting commitment-adjacent statements ('Soccer's worth more than one night of beers'). Bursts of change talk are rarely deepened with follow-up reflection.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Neutral reframing of 'this stuff isn't for me' without arguing, consistently pivoting back to goals. Some early questions invite fear-based rather than value-based motivation, and minimization is mostly passed over rather than actively reframed.
