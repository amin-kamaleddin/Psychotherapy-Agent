# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental, using affirmations ('that honesty matters') and avoiding labels. Warmth was somewhat formulaic and did not deeply normalize Brandon's ambivalence or fear.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Accurate reflective listening throughout, capturing ambivalence ('hopeful and worried at once'). Reflections occasionally stayed shallow, not exploring the emotional weight of the memory gaps, driving incident, or fear of disappointing his sister.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed well-focused on alcohol use and its consequences with a concrete follow-up plan. At times drifted into logistical detail (reminder timing, log format) rather than consolidating the deeper motivational thread.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently framed the work as joint and sought consent before questions. Heavy reliance on option menus was respectful but did less to genuinely draw out and build on Brandon's own problem-solving ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong autonomy support with multiple options at every decision point, accepting refusal of sober days without pressure, and echoing 'you chose this yourself.' The client's ownership of the plan was the session's clear strength.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some indirect evocation through exploring the birthday gaps, but little deliberate eliciting of Brandon's own reasons, values, or confidence. Much of the strongest change talk arose spontaneously rather than from skillful evocation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly solicited Brandon's preferences and followed his redirections, such as pivoting from tracking to the birthday memory. Options remained menu-driven and counselor-framed rather than openly co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerged largely from the client and was met supportively but not actively deepened or amplified. Missed opportunities to explore what cutting back would mean to Brandon or his hopes and commitment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively acknowledged sustain talk without reinforcing it and immediately accepted Brandon's refusal of sober days, redirecting to an acceptable alternative. Limited effort to actively reframe sustain talk toward change.
