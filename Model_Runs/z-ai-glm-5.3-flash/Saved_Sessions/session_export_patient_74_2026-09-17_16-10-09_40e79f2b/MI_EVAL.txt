# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently communicates unconditional positive regard, warmly validating Linda's honesty about enjoying smoking without any judgment or pressure. Genuine, specific affirmations throughout ('That's honest and strong').

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Strong reflective listening that accurately captures Linda's ambivalence, including her fear of sounding hypocritical. Misses deeper emotional material, such as her fear of being 'a failure on top of everything.'

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains excellent focus on the negotiated target of protecting Linda's daughter, explicitly respecting that quitting is not her goal. Every exchange produces concrete momentum: car rule chosen, script rehearsed, spouse conversation practiced.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Genuinely collaborative — Linda chose the focus, the car rule, and the role-plays, and the counselor functions as a coach and rehearsal partner rather than an authority. A model of two-way partnership.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly offers choices and explicitly respects Linda's pace ('We can focus on protecting her without forcing you into change'). Loses a point for supplying scripts and options before asking Linda what she thinks would work.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asks good open questions but doesn't systematically develop Linda's seeds of change, which are met with option menus instead of exploratory follow-ups. Much change content comes from counselor-provided scripts rather than from Linda herself.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently invites Linda's input ('Which feels most doable?') and her choices genuinely shape the session. Slightly limited by the counselor tending to supply content rather than positioning Linda as the expert on her own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Change talk emerges mostly spontaneously from Linda ('maybe someday,' 'it plants a little seed') rather than through deliberate evocation. Key moments pass without deepening (e.g., 'What would have to change for someday to become now?').

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk neutrally without amplifying or debating it, explicitly redirecting to her stated concern (protecting her daughter). Handles Linda's enjoyment of smoking without confrontation.
