# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Maintains a respectful, nonjudgmental tone throughout with no labeling or criticism. However, warmth is formulaic ('I hear you' repeated verbatim) and there are no genuine affirmations of her strengths as a mother despite repeated openings for them.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening is minimal — vulnerable disclosures like 'Maybe I'm part of the problem too' and her heartbreak go unexplored. The session is dominated by advice-giving rather than understanding of her feelings.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Keeps consistent focus on the target behavior — reducing co-parenting conflict and its impact on the son — with no drift. However, direction leans toward premature action, moving to concrete tactics before ambivalence and values are fully explored.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction is largely counselor-driven, with scripts, messages, and practice agendas proposed without first asking what Amina thinks would work. She reacts to and approves the counselor's products rather than co-creating solutions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Offers some menu choices about session activities ('Want me to tailor a message?', 'role-play or comeback texts?'), but the substance is prescriptive — dictating exact wording, timing, and follow-up actions. There is no explicit statement that the decisions and plan are hers to accept, modify, or reject.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Conveys that answers reside with the counselor rather than the client — motivation and solutions are supplied, not drawn out. Virtually no open questions after the initial one, and her strong intrinsic motivation as a mother went largely untapped.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The counselor assumes the expert role, delivering pre-packaged scripts, texts, and checklists rather than drawing on Amina's own expertise. Her demonstrated knowledge of her son and ex-partner is never explicitly acknowledged or incorporated.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The opening question successfully invites change talk, and Amina produces strong value-based motivation ('I just want to figure out how to do right by him'). However, the counselor never deliberately evokes, reflects, or deepens it — the change talk emerges spontaneously rather than through skillful evocation.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk is appropriately not amplified or reinforced, but it is also not actively softened. There are no double-sided reflections linking her doubts to her motivations, and her fear ('What if he still says no?') is met with scripted tactics rather than exploration.
