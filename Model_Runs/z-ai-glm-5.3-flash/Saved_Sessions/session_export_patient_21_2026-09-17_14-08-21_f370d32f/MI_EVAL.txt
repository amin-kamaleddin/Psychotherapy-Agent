# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Respectful and supportive tone throughout, but warmth was formulaic and Camila's existing successes (walking most days, stretching, insight) were never affirmed. Scripted safety messaging after she said she was in a good place further reduced attunement.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Reflective listening was largely absent; the counselor jumped to prescriptive plans rather than reflecting ambivalence and ignored Camila's emotional disclosure about being hard on herself. The single acknowledgment about energy variability was superficial.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Session stayed consistently focused on increasing physical activity, with concrete next steps established (10-minute walk after breakfast, tracking, accountability buddy, one-week check-in). Only limitation was that the direction was counselor-imposed rather than collaboratively shaped.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Interaction was largely counselor-led, with multi-part question batteries and pre-drafted schedules, tips, and texts delivered with minimal joint deliberation. Camila's adjustments were reactive refinements rather than co-designed decisions.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some genuine choice-offering ('Which would you prefer?') and nonjudgmental framing of lapses, but the plan and its components were largely presented as a package to adopt. Instructions like 'Set a daily repeating alarm' directed rather than invited.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation came almost entirely from Camila spontaneously; the counselor never explored her reasons, desires, or confidence for change. External scaffolding (rewards, alarms, buddies) was supplied instead of evoking internal motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Expert-driven stance predominated; the detailed plan came from clinical expertise rather than Camila's own knowledge. Her contributions were client-initiated accommodations, and her existing expertise about what works for her was never acknowledged.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Camila spontaneously offered meaningful change talk ('I feel more hopeful than I expected', 'I'll aim for all seven') but the counselor never reflected, reinforced, or deepened it. Her hopeful statement was met with logistics instead of evocative follow-up.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Sustain talk about losing steam and fear of failure was glossed over with reassurance or ignored, rather than met with double-sided reflections or reframes. The counselor did not actively reinforce it, but also missed repeated opportunities to resolve ambivalence.
