# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained a respectful, nonjudgmental stance and honored Jonathan's boundaries without labeling or disapproval. However, warmth was somewhat formulaic and explicit affirmations of his strengths were scarce.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Made reasonable reflective attempts but reflections were often surface-level paraphrases. Deeper emotional content — the fear connected to his father's deterioration and resentment toward his wife's nagging — was named but never explored with emotional depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Kept a clear focus on medication adherence and drove the session toward a concrete, time-bound plan. Slightly narrowed focus by entirely avoiding the food topic rather than keeping a door ajar for broader diabetes self-management.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The plan was genuinely co-created with Jonathan negotiating check-in format and terms. Fell short because the counselor occasionally steered with pre-set option lists rather than inviting Jonathan to generate his own ideas first.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Consistently affirmed choice, fully accepted Jonathan's boundary around food talk, and worked to shift the pill habit toward internal ownership. Jonathan set the agenda, terms, and check-in format, all of which the counselor honored.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Successfully evoked Jonathan's most meaningful motivation — avoiding his father's fate — and his commitment language. However, the father reference wasn't deepened, and repeated logistical questions generated little new motivation instead of probing values further.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently positioned Jonathan as the expert through menu offerings and by letting his preferences shape the plan. Slightly short of 5 because some questions were offered as lists to select from rather than co-constructed.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully elicited genuine change talk, including the father motivation and the commitment statement 'a week on the pills... that I can actually do.' Change talk wasn't always deepened — the counselor pivoted quickly to menu options after the father reference.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Used double-sided reflections and gently pivoted to what was doable without amplifying Jonathan's frequent sustain talk. Occasionally echoed sustain talk without reframing and avoided food talk entirely rather than helping incremental movement toward it.
