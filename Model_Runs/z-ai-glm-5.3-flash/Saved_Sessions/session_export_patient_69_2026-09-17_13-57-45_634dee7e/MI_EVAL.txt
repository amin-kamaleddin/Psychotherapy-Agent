# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor maintained a nonjudgmental, respectful tone without labeling or pressure. However, affirmations were generic and there was no explicit normalization of Kieran's ambivalence or unconditional valuing of his honesty and autonomy.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Some accurate reflections captured his fatigue and resentment of control, but most were surface-level restatements. A key moment — Kieran noting he wasn't thinking about the loop — was missed rather than reflected for its deeper meaning of agency and relief.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor kept a clear focus on one small behavioral change and followed through with a structured check-in, which Kieran completed. Points lost because the direction was pursued rigidly rather than collaboratively and was never linked back to Kieran's broader concern about the 'loop.'

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** The interaction was counselor-directed: when Kieran repeatedly said 'You can pick,' the counselor selected the activity, prescribed exact steps, and scripted the check-in rather than exploring his passivity. There was no joint agenda-setting, and Kieran's input never shaped the session.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor offered frequent choice language and 'no pressure' statements, but these felt performative since the plan was already decided and follow-up questions functioned as instructions. Kieran's statement that 'people tell me what to do' was not met with genuine power-sharing.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation came from the counselor's plan rather than being evoked from Kieran; questions were mostly procedural. The counselor never explored what matters to him or amplified the one evocative moment — his comment that the loop quieted during the walk.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** After Kieran explicitly described his life as others telling him what to do, the counselor chose the walk himself — directly reproducing that dynamic. Instructions came in checklist form rather than being co-created.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Kieran produced modest change talk ('a little less wound up,' 'wasn't thinking about the loop') after completing the walk, but the counselor failed to deepen or amplify it. No confidence questions, exploration of what made it possible, or commitment language for next steps.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Sustain talk ('not making any promises,' 'not sure it's gonna fix anything') was acknowledged but never reframed or pivoted with double-sided reflections. Repeated 'if you don't do it, that's okay' statements risked giving sustain talk more airtime than necessary.
