# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor consistently communicated respect and positive regard, using affirmations like 'I'm glad you reached out — that takes courage' and responding nonjudgmentally to admissions of potentially skipping days. However, affirmations were somewhat brief and formulaic, lacking deeper individualized validation of the emotional weight of the new diagnosis.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Reflective listening was accurate, effectively capturing Marcus's ambivalence ('pills feel doable but checking blood sugar feels overwhelming') and his emotional shift from 'drowning' to manageable routine. Reflections remained at surface level, missing opportunities to explore anxiety about failure and family dynamics.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The session maintained exemplary, consistent focus on the target behavior of blood glucose monitoring with no meaningful drift. The counselor moved purposefully from exploring barriers to scaling, hands-on demo, goal-setting, and preparing clinician questions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** There were genuine collaborative elements, including permission-seeking and building the plan around Marcus's stated preferences (once daily, phone logging, morning timing). However, during the demo and educational segments, the counselor shifted into an instructive, expert-delivery mode, tilting the power balance toward himself.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The counselor regularly offered choices rather than directives, and Marcus's preferences meaningfully shaped the final plan. Autonomy support was imperfect, with somewhat prescriptive clinical guidance (specific thresholds, log structure) that didn't explicitly emphasize Marcus's ownership of the change process.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The counselor successfully evoked motivation through importance/confidence rulers, eliciting a measurable rise in confidence from 4 to 7. However, he frequently supplied information and solutions (finger-stick instructions, thresholds, log templates) rather than drawing them from the client, missing chances to deepen motivation.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Permission-seeking and offering choices supported initial power sharing, and Marcus's own ideas influenced the plan. The mid-session was largely psychoeducational and directive, positioning the counselor as the expert without explicitly acknowledging Marcus as the authority on his own life.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** The 0–10 scaling questions successfully evoked meaningful change talk, with Marcus's confidence rising from 4 to 7 and statements like 'I think I can handle once a day.' The counselor did not consistently reinforce or deepen this change talk with reflections, pivoting to new tasks rather than consolidating growing self-efficacy.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** When Marcus expressed hesitancy about skipping days, the counselor responded with a normalizing, momentum-oriented reframe rather than dwelling on barriers. These pivots were largely implicit, lacking deliberate double-sided reflections or strategic reframing toward change.
