# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The psychiatrist consistently honors the patient's autonomy, explicitly affirming his right to try things his own way and keeping the medical advice within an invitational framework. Only minor deduction as the re-test framing subtly steers toward the doctor's agenda.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Reflections consistently capture both surface content and underlying meaning, e.g., naming the tension between 'doctor knows best' and walking out unsure, and the job-focused fear of muscle weakness. The patient repeatedly feels understood and builds on the reflections.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** The conversation is skillfully steered toward clarifying values, concrete next steps, and a self-generated plan, which the patient clearly owns. A small deduction because some reflections ('That sounds like it matters a lot to you') lean slightly toward pre-chosen conclusions.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The clinician repeatedly positions the patient as the expert ('you know your life best'), asks permission implicitly through open questions, and the entire plan originates from the patient's own ideas. The patient explicitly notes leaving with 'a say in things.'

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choices are consistently offered back to the patient ('call this week or sort food with your wife first?'), and self-persuasion is evoked rather than imposed. Even the commitment to reconsider medication is framed as the patient's own deal with himself.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Excellent use of open questions to draw out the patient's own motivations ('what would worth it look like?', 'what would help you get started?'). The patient generates his own reasons, safeguards, and contingency plan rather than adopting the clinician's.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction operates as genuine collaboration, with the clinician following the patient's lead and seeking his input on every step, including troubleshooting his own barriers. Power dynamics are explicitly flattened ('you're the one who said you'd think harder about the medicine').

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Strong sustain talk about medication is acknowledged without confrontation, then mobilized into rich change talk (diet plan, ten pounds, re-test date, 'Monday, first thing'). Commitment language escalates naturally, culminating in a firm verbal contract the patient himself proposed.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Sustain talk (muscle weakness fears, 'pills are forever', distrust of the one number) is consistently reflected and reframed rather than argued against, and even serves as useful information for the plan. Slight deduction as the clinician briefly pre-answers the patient's medical questions rather than fully deferring them to the doctor visit.
