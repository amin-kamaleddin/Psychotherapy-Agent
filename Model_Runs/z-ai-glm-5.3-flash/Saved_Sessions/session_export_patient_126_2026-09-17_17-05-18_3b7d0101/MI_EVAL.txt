# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently respectful and nonjudgmental despite Thomas's sarcasm and minimization. However, warmth felt somewhat formulaic with little explicit affirmation of Thomas beyond his choices.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Accurately tracked Thomas's minimization with reasonable reflections, but often pivoted quickly to questions or suggestions. Deeper emotional content (fear of judgment, wife's criticism, social dynamics) was acknowledged but not explored in depth.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Maintained clear focus on drinking behavior with logical progression from exploration to a concrete plan and check-in date. At times pushy with rapid-fire multiple questions, but focus was well maintained.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Asked Thomas to pick between options and check feasibility, but the counselor supplied the options, plan, tactics, and metrics while Thomas remained largely reactive. The plan did not emerge from Thomas's own ideas.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly framed the plan as Thomas's choice ('Which, if any, sounds doable?', 'you're keeping control') and honored the time-limited framing. Structured menus and tactics slightly limited genuine open choice, with little emphasis on Thomas's own capacity.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Motivation was largely supplied by the counselor rather than drawn from Thomas; evocative questions were deflected and abandoned in favor of providing solutions and pros/cons lists. Thomas's own values (mornings with his child, wife's approval) surfaced incidentally and were never developed.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 2
**Comments:** Frequently assumed the expert role with pre-packaged options, ready-made plans, and scripts — Thomas himself noted 'you've got options ready-made, haven't you?'. Asking for input on which option felt doable didn't compensate for Thomas's ideas not shaping the session.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Successfully elicited modest change talk (commitment to a three-drink experiment, the motivator of tired mornings, confidence 7–8), but this emerged late and reluctantly. Change talk was captured but never amplified or deepened into stronger commitment language.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 3
**Comments:** Avoided arguing with Thomas's sustain talk and did not reinforce minimization. However, sustain talk was sometimes echoed neutrally, and balanced pros/cons lists gave equal airtime to cons of change, inadvertently amplifying it.
