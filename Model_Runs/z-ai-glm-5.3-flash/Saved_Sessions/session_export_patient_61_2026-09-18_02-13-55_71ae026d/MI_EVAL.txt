# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer consistently respected the patient's autonomy, explicitly acknowledging that 'nobody can force this' and that change must come from him. Even hesitant language like 'wouldn't kill me' and 'don't hold me to it' was met with acceptance rather than pressure.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer demonstrated strong empathic understanding through reflective statements like 'that image of diabetes as a second job you didn't apply for really says a lot,' capturing the patient's sense of being overwhelmed. Reflections were consistently accurate and emotionally attuned throughout.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The conversation was clearly and skillfully channeled toward exploring a feasible change step, moving from broad overwhelm to a specific, concrete plan. Each response built deliberately on the patient's own small signals of openness.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer positioned the patient as the designer of his own plan ('you designed something that fits the way you actually live') and explicitly avoided re-handing the doctor's list. Questions invited the patient's judgment on what could work for him.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Choice and self-direction were emphasized repeatedly — 'you're the expert on your own schedule,' 'the plan is yours,' and 'no pressure, just a maybe.' The interviewer framed success in the patient's own terms.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer elicited the patient's own reasons and capabilities, drawing out his ideas about testing before coffee, weekday-only trials, and enlisting his wife. Change talk emerged naturally from the patient rather than being supplied by the interviewer.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interaction functioned as a genuine collaboration, with the interviewer asking for the patient's input at each juncture ('what feels least doable,' 'what would need to change,' 'is that a version you could see yourself trying?'). Both parties shaped the plan together.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** The interviewer skillfully noticed and amplified minimal change talk ('wouldn't kill me,' 'maybe starting with just one thing') and used open questions and confidence scaling to grow it. The patient's language evolved from 'maybe' to 'I could see trying that' to a concrete, self-designed experiment.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Sustain talk like 'all of it, honestly' and 'I'm not promising anything' was met with non-confrontational reflection rather than argument, allowing the patient to move past it himself. The interviewer acknowledged ambivalence while keeping space for change.
