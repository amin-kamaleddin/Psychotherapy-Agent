# Motivational Interviewing Clinical Evaluation – MI

---

### Acceptance
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Warm, respectful, nonjudgmental stance with frequent, specific affirmations and validation of the fear of failure. Falls short of 5 due to occasionally formulaic affirmations and limited explicit normalization of past failed quit attempts.

### Empathy
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Consistently accurate reflective listening, including attuned double-sided reflections and a strong reflection on the fear of failure. Reflections sometimes pivot quickly to questions, and deeper emotional content (father's story, awkwardness of urges) is not always explored.

### Direction
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Maintains an unwavering focus on smoking cessation across every turn, with questions consistently guiding toward goals and concrete next steps. No aimless drift.

### Collaboration
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Decisions consistently framed as joint ('decide together,' 'we'd review together') and the patient's input visibly shapes the plan. Occasionally the counselor presents information blocks before re-inviting collaboration rather than co-constructing throughout.

### Autonomy Support
#### **Score (1–5):** 5
**Comments:** Exemplary, explicit autonomy support ('This is your call to make,' 'always your call') with options offered without pressure. The patient's choices emerge entirely from him.

### Evocation
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Skillfully draws motivation from within the patient, eliciting his own reasons ('Think of Dad,' wife-centered reasons) and success criteria. A few informational turns (medication pros/cons) miss opportunities to keep eliciting within education.

### Partnership
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Repeatedly positions the patient as the expert and lets his contributions shape the session. A few transitions lean slightly toward counselor steering, with limited explicit acknowledgment that he knows his own life best.

### Cultivating Change Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Effectively evokes the patient's own change language and commitment-adjacent talk through well-crafted questions. Missed opportunities to explicitly elicit commitment language or confidence ratings.

### Softening Sustain Talk
#### **Score (1–5):** 4
**Comments:** Acknowledges sustain talk (coping worries, fear of failure) without amplifying it and reframes failure into evidence of change. Would need more consistent double-sided reflections pivoting toward change talk to reach a 5.
